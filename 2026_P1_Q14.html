Polynomial Division & Repeated Roots

Question

When the polynomial \(g(x)\) is divided by \(x^2+2x+a\), the quotient and the remainder are \(x+13\) and \(b(x-3)\) respectively, where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. It is given that \(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x-3\).

(a)   Find \(a\). (3 marks)

(b)   Is there only one possible value of \(b\) such that at least two roots of the equation \(g(x)=0\) are equal? Explain your answer. (5 marks)

Solution

STEP 1 — write g(x) using the division identity
Division Identity \(g(x)=q(x)(x+13)+r(x)\) The stated division writes \(g(x)=(x^{2}+2x+a)(x+13)+b(x-3)\).
\[ g(x)=(x^2+2x+a)(x+13)+b(x-3) \]
PART (a) — use g(3) = 0
Factor Theorem \((x-c)\ \text{is a factor of}\ g(x)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ g(c)=0\) Divisibility by \(x-3\) gives \(g(3)=16(a+15)=0\), so \(a=-15\).
Since \(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x-3\), \(g(3)=0\): \[ g(3)=(9+6+a)(16)+b(0)=16(a+15)=0 \] \[ a=-15 \]
PART (b) — factor g(x) with a = −15
\[ x^2+2x-15=(x+5)(x-3) \] \[ g(x)=(x+5)(x-3)(x+13)+b(x-3) \] \[ =\,(x-3)\big[(x+5)(x+13)+b\big]=(x-3)\big(x^2+18x+65+b\big) \]
PART (b) — Case 1: the quadratic has a repeated root
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For a repeated root, \(18^{2}-4(65+b)=0\), so \(b=16\).
Discriminant \(=0\): \[ 18^2-4(65+b)=0 \implies 324-260-4b=0 \implies b=16 \] Then \(g(x)=(x-3)(x+9)^2\): the roots are \(3,\,-9,\,-9\) ✓
PART (b) — Case 2: the quadratic shares the root x = 3
\[ 3^2+18(3)+65+b=0 \implies 9+54+65+b=0 \implies b=-128 \] Then \(g(x)=(x-3)^2(x+53)\): the roots are \(3,\,3,\,-53\) ✓
PART (b) — conclusion
Both \(b=16\) and \(b=-128\) make at least two roots equal, so the answer is no — there are two possible values of \(b\).
(a) \(a=-15\)    (b) No — there are two values: \(b=16\) (double root \(-9\)) and \(b=-128\) (double root \(3\)).

Question. It is given that \(g(x)=(x^{2}+x+a)(x+11)+b(x-2)\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants.
(a) If \(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x-2\), find \(a\).
(b) If the equation \(g(x)=0\) has a repeated root, find all possible values of \(b\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(g(2)=(4+2+a)(13)+b(0)=13(a+6)=0\), so \(a=-6\).
Step 2. Then \(x^{2}+x-6=(x+3)(x-2)\), so \(g(x)=(x-2)\left[(x+3)(x+11)+b\right]=(x-2)(x^{2}+14x+33+b)\).
Step 3. Repeated root of the quadratic: \(14^{2}-4(33+b)=0\), so \(196=132+4b\) and \(b=16\); then \(x^{2}+14x+49=(x+7)^{2}\).
Step 4. Second case, the quadratic also vanishes at \(x=2\): \(4+28+33+b=0\), so \(b=-65\); then \(x^{2}+14x-32=(x+16)(x-2)\).
Step 5. Both values give equal roots, so \(b=16\) or \(b=-65\): there is more than one possible value.
Answer: (a) \(a=-6\) (b) \(b=16\) or \(b=-65\)
