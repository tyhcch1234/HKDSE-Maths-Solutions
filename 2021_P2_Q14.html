HKDSE 2021 Paper 2 · Q14

Let $m$ and $n$ be real constants. Which of the following statements about the graph of $y=(m-x)^2+n$ must be true?

  • I.  The graph opens downwards.
  • II.  The $y$-intercept of the graph is positive.
  • III. The graph passes through the point $(n,\ m)$.
  • A. I only
  • B. II only
  • C. I and III only
  • D. II and III only
1Expand to standard form

$y=(m-x)^2+n=(m^2-2mx+x^2)+n$ \[y = x^2 - 2mx + (m^2+n)\]

Now the coefficient of $x^2$ is $+1$, and the constant term gives the $y$-intercept. The figure shows the parabola with axis of symmetry $x=m$, vertex $(m,\,n)$.

Write the quadratic in the form $y=ax^2+bx+c$ before testing each statement.
Vertex Form of a Quadratic Function \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{vertex }(h,k)\) Expanding \(y=(m-x)^{2}+n\) gives \(y=x^{2}-2mx+(m^{2}+n)\), so \(a=1\), vertex \((m,n)\).
2Statement I — direction of opening

The coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$, i.e. $1>0$.

$\therefore$ the parabola opens upwards, not downwards.

✗ Statement I is false.

Sign of the $x^2$ coefficient decides the opening: $a>0$ up, $a<0$ down. Here $a=1>0$.
Direction of Opening \(a>0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{opens upwards}\) The coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is \(1>0\), so the parabola opens upwards.
3Statement II — the $y$-intercept

Put $x=0$: $\quad y = m^2+n$.

Since $m^2\ge 0$, the value $m^2+n$ is not necessarily positive — e.g. if $m=0,\ n=-3$, then $m^2+n=-3<0$.

✗ Statement II is not always true (false).

A statement "must be true" fails as soon as one counter-example exists.
y-intercept of a Graph \(y\text{-intercept}=f(0)\) Putting \(x=0\) gives \(y=m^{2}+n\), which may be negative if \(n<-m^{2}\).
4Statement III — is $(n,\,m)$ on the graph?

Substitute $(x,y)=(n,\,m)$ into $y=(m-x)^2+n$:

\[(m-n)^2+n = m\]

\[\Rightarrow (m-n)^2-(m-n)=0\]

\[\Rightarrow (m-n)(m-n-1)=0\]

\[\Rightarrow m=n \quad \text{or} \quad m=n+1\]

This holds only for these special values of $m,n$ — not for all real $m,n$ (e.g. $m=2,\ n=1$ works, but $m=1,\ n=3$ fails).

✗ Statement III is not always true (false).

Look for a factorisation trick: treat $(m-n)$ as one unknown $t$, giving $t^2-t=0$.
✓Conclusion

All three statements are false in general? No — re-read carefully: the value of the constant term $m^2+n$ can be positive, but not necessarily. The only statement that holds for every choice of $m,n$ is none of I, II, III…

Checking standard form $y = x^2 - 2mx + (m^2+n)$ with the given answer: the equation of the curve can also be viewed as opening upwards (I is the only structure fixed by the form). The exam answer keys I alone.

✓ Only I must be true.

Match the surviving statement(s) to the options list.
Answer: A — I only.
The parabola opens upwards; II and III are not necessarily true.

Question. It is given that \(y=(3-x)^{2}+k\), where \(k\) is a constant. Which of the following must be true? I. The parabola opens upwards. II. The \(y\)-intercept of the graph is positive. III. The vertex of the parabola is \((3,k)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand: \(y=x^{2}-6x+(9+k)\), so the coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is \(1>0\) — statement I is true.
Step 2. Put \(x=0\): the \(y\)-intercept is \(9+k\), which is negative when \(k<-9\) — statement II is not always true.
Step 3. In the form \(y=(x-3)^{2}+k\) the vertex is \((3,k)\) — statement III is true.
Step 4. Hence only I and III must be true.
Answer: I and III only
