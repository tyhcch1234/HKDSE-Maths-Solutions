Rate & Ratio Weighted Average Mixture Problems

📋 Question

The cost of flour of brand \(X\) is \(\$42/\text{kg}\). If \(3\text{ kg}\) of flour of brand \(X\) and \(2\text{ kg}\) of flour of brand \(Y\) are mixed so that the cost of the mixture is \(\$36/\text{kg}\), find the cost of flour of brand \(Y\).

A. \(\$27/\text{kg}\)
B. \(\$30/\text{kg}\)
C. \(\$32/\text{kg}\)
D. \(\$39/\text{kg}\)

Answer: A  ($27/kg)

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning.

Let \(y\) be the cost of flour of brand \(Y\) in dollars per kg.

💡 Hint: We are told the mixture costs \(\$36/\text{kg}\), so \(y\) must be less than \(\$36\) because brand \(X\) costs \(\$42\).

The total cost of the mixture is the sum of the costs of the two brands:

Total cost \(= 3 \times 42 + 2 \times y = 126 + 2y\)
🔑 Key idea: Multiply the mass (kg) by the unit price ($/kg) for each brand.

Simply add the masses together:

Total mass \(= 3 + 2 = 5\text{ kg}\)

The cost per kg of the mixture is the total cost divided by the total mass, which equals \(\$36\):

\[ \frac{42 \cdot 3 + y \cdot 2}{3 + 2} = 36 \]
💡 Hint: This is a weighted average problem. The weights are the masses (3 kg and 2 kg).

Multiply both sides by 5:

\(42 \cdot 3 + 2y = 36 \times 5\)

\(126 + 2y = 180\)

\(2y = 180 - 126 = 54\)

\(y = 27\)

✅ Conclusion: The cost of flour of brand \(Y\) is \(\$27/\text{kg}\). The correct answer is A.

🧠 Quick Check

Try another mixture to test your understanding.

Suppose \(4\text{ kg}\) of brand \(X\) (cost \(\$42/\text{kg}\)) are mixed with \(1\text{ kg}\) of brand \(Z\) (cost \(\$z/\text{kg}\)). If the mixture costs \(\$38/\text{kg}\), find \(z\).

\(\dfrac{4 \times 42 + 1 \times z}{5} = 38\)

\(168 + z = 190 \quad\Rightarrow\quad z = 22\)

So brand \(Z\) costs \(\$22/\text{kg}\).
