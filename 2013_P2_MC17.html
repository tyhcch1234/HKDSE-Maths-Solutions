Mensuration — Surface Area of a Composite Solid (Cone + Hemisphere)

Right Circular Cone Hemisphere Slant Height Curved Surface Area Pythagoras

Question Full Question

In the figure, the solid consists of a right circular cone and a hemisphere with a common base. The base radius and the height of the circular cone are \(3\text{ cm}\) and \(4\text{ cm}\) respectively. Find the total surface area of the solid.

A. \(30\pi\text{ cm}^2\)    B. \(33\pi\text{ cm}^2\)    C. \(48\pi\text{ cm}^2\)    D. \(51\pi\text{ cm}^2\)

(Multiple-choice · 1 mark for the correct option)

Original Figure

Part (a) Find the Slant Height of the Cone

The curved surface of the cone requires the slant height \(\ell\). The radius \(r=3\) and the vertical height \(h=4\) form a right triangle with \(\ell\).

r = 3.0 cm h = 4.0 cm ℓ = √(r²+h²) = 5.00 cm
0 / 2
11MIdentify the right triangle

The axial cross-section through the apex gives a right triangle with height \(h = 4\text{ cm}\) and base \(r = 3\text{ cm}\). The slant height \(\ell\) is the hypotenuse.

Hint: The slant height is the distance from the apex to any point on the base circle — it is NOT the vertical height.
21AApply Pythagoras' Theorem

\[ \ell = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} = \sqrt{9+16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ cm} \]

Hint: \(3,4,5\) is a Pythagorean triple — recognise it instantly and save time in the MCQ.
Slant height \(\ell = 5\text{ cm}\)

Part (b) Curved Surface Area of the Cone

The cone's curved surface is a sector of radius \(\ell\) that wraps around the base circle of radius \(r\).

Unrolled sector radius = ℓ = 5.0 cm Arc of sector = 2πr = 18.85 cm Curved area = πrℓ = 47.12 cm²
0 / 2
11MFormula for the cone's curved surface

The curved surface area of a right circular cone is

\[ \text{CSA}_{\text{cone}} = \pi r \ell \]
Hint: The base circle is glued to the hemisphere, so it is not part of the exposed surface area.
21ASubstitute the values

\[ \text{CSA}_{\text{cone}} = \pi (3)(5) = 15\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]

Hint: \(3 \times 5 = 15\). Keep the answer in the form \(k\pi\) to compare directly with the options.
Curved surface area of cone \(= 15\pi\text{ cm}^2\)

Part (c) Curved Surface Area of the Hemisphere

The hemisphere has the same radius \(r = 3\text{ cm}\). Only its curved surface is exposed.

r = 3.0 cm Sphere surface = 4πr² = 113.10 cm² Hemisphere CSA = 2πr² = 56.55 cm²
0 / 2
11MHalf of a sphere's surface area

A full sphere has surface area \(4\pi r^2\). A hemisphere exposes only half of that curved surface:

\[ \text{CSA}_{\text{hemisphere}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(4\pi r^2) = 2\pi r^2 \]
Hint: The flat circular face is glued to the cone, so it is internal and hidden — do NOT add it.
21ASubstitute r = 3

\[ \text{CSA}_{\text{hemisphere}} = 2\pi (3)^2 = 2\pi(9) = 18\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]

Hint: A common mistake is \(4\pi r^2 = 36\pi\); that is the whole sphere. Halve it!
Curved surface area of hemisphere \(= 18\pi\text{ cm}^2\)

Part (d) Total Surface Area of the Solid

The total exposed surface is the sum of the cone's curved surface and the hemisphere's curved surface (the common base circle cancels out — it is internal).

Cone CSA = 15π Hemisphere CSA = 18π Total = 15π + 18π = 33π cm²
0 / 2
11MAdd the two curved surfaces

\[ \text{Total} = \text{CSA}_{\text{cone}} + \text{CSA}_{\text{hemisphere}} \]

\[ \text{Total} = 15\pi + 18\pi \]
Hint: Only exposed surfaces count. The common base circle is shared internally, so it is excluded.
21AFinal answer

\[ \text{Total surface area} = 33\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]

Total surface area \(= 33\pi\text{ cm}^2\) → Option B

Summary Final Answers

  • Slant height of cone: \(\ell = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} = \mathbf{5\,cm}\)
  • Curved surface area of cone: \(\pi r\ell = \mathbf{15\pi\,cm^2}\)
  • Curved surface area of hemisphere: \(2\pi r^2 = \mathbf{18\pi\,cm^2}\)
  • Total surface area of solid: \(15\pi + 18\pi = \mathbf{33\pi\,cm^2}\)
  • Correct option: B
