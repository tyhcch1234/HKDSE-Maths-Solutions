Coordinate Geometry — Equation of a Circle from a Chord
Circle Equation Perpendicular from Centre Pythagoras Mid-point of Chord

Question MC

The coordinates of the centre of the circle \(C\) are \((7,-5)\). If \(C\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at the points \(P\) and \(Q\) such that \(PQ=24\), then the equation of \(C\) is

  • A. \(x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0\)
  • B. \(x^{2}+y^{2}+14x-10y-95=0\)
  • C. \(x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-119=0\)
  • D. \(x^{2}+y^{2}+14x-10y-119=0\)

Part (a) — Set up the configuration Geometry

Since \(C\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(P\) and \(Q\), the chord \(PQ\) lies on the \(x\)-axis. The centre is \(G(7,-5)\). Because the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord, the mid-point \(M\) of \(PQ\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(G\).

r = 13
Step 11M

The perpendicular from the centre \(G\) to the chord \(PQ\) bisects \(PQ\). Since \(PQ\) lies on the \(x\)-axis, the perpendicular is vertical, so \(M\) has the same \(x\)-coordinate as \(G\):

\[M=(7,0)\]

The distance from \(G\) down to the \(x\)-axis is

\[MG = |{-5}| = 5\]
Hint: The vertical distance is just the absolute value of the \(y\)-coordinate of the centre, since the chord is on \(y=0\).
Step 21M

\(M\) is the mid-point of \(PQ\), so

\[PM = MQ = \frac{PQ}{2} = \frac{24}{2} = 12\]
Hint: "Perpendicular from centre bisects the chord" is the key circle theorem used here.
Step 31M

Triangle \(PMG\) is right-angled at \(M\). By Pythagoras' theorem, the radius \(r=PG\) is

\[r=\sqrt{MG^{2}+PM^{2}}=\sqrt{5^{2}+12^{2}}=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13\]
Radius: \(r=13\)
Hint: Recognise the 5-12-13 Pythagorean triple — no calculator needed!

Part (b) — Write the equation of \(C\) Equation

A circle with centre \((h,k)\) and radius \(r\) has equation \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\). Expand it to match one of the four options.

Step 11M

Substitute centre \((7,-5)\) and \(r=13\):

\[(x-7)^{2}+(y-(-5))^{2}=13^{2}\] \[(x-7)^{2}+(y+5)^{2}=169\]
Hint: Mind the signs: \(y-(-5)=y+5\).
Step 21M

Expand the squares:

\[x^{2}-14x+49 + y^{2}+10y+25 = 169\] \[x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y+74-169=0\] \[x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0\]
Hint: \(49+25=74\), and \(74-169=-95\).
Step 31A

The general form is \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\) with

\[D=-14,\quad E=10,\quad F=-95\]

Comparing with the options, this matches A.

Answer: \(\boxed{\text{A}}\;\) \(x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0\)
Hint (check): The \(x\)-intercepts satisfy \(x^{2}-14x-95=0\Rightarrow x=19\) or \(x=-5\), so \(PQ=19-(-5)=24\). ✓
