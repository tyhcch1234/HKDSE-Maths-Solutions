Formulas and Sequences — Public Housing Flats

The development of public housing in a city is under study.

It is given that the total floor area of all public housing flats at the end of the 1st year is $9\times10^{6}\ \text{m}^2$ and in subsequent years, the total floor area of public housing flats built each year is $r\%$ of the total floor area of all public housing flats at the end of the previous year, where $r$ is a constant, and the total floor area of public housing flats pulled down each year is $3\times10^{5}\ \text{m}^2$. It is found that the total floor area of all public housing flats at the end of the 3rd year is $1.026\times10^{7}\ \text{m}^2$.

(a)(i) Express, in terms of $r$, the total floor area of all public housing flats at the end of the 2nd year.
(a)(ii) Find $r$. [4 marks]
(b)(i) Express, in terms of $n$, the total floor area of all public housing flats at the end of the $n$th year.
(b)(ii) At the end of which year will the total floor area of all public housing flats first exceed $4\times10^{7}\ \text{m}^2$? [5 marks]
(c) It is assumed that the total floor area of public housing flats needed at the end of the $n$th year is $\left(a(1.21)^{n}+b\right)\text{m}^2$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. Some research results reveal the following information:
$n$Total floor area of public housing flats needed at the end of the $n$th year (m²)
1$1\times10^{7}$
2$1.063\times10^{7}$

A research assistant claims that based on the above assumption, the total floor area of all public housing flats will be greater than the total floor area of public housing flats needed at the end of a certain year. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Model the recurrence for $A_n$.
Recurrence Relation \(A_{n}=A_{n-1}(1+r\%)-3\times10^{5}\) Each year the area is multiplied by \((1+r\%)\) and then reduced by the \(3 imes10^{5}\ ext{m}^{2}\) pulled down.

Let $A_n$ be the total floor area (in m²) at the end of the $n$th year. Each year the area is multiplied by $(1+r\%)$, then reduced by $3\times10^{5}\,\text{m}^2$:

$$A_{1}=9\times10^{6},\qquad A_{n}=A_{n-1}(1+r\%)-3\times10^{5}.$$ Hint: Write the $r\%$ increase as a decimal multiplier $(1+r\%)$ and treat this as a recurrence relation.
Step 2(a)(i) End of the 2nd year. $$A_{2}=9\times10^{6}(1+r\%)-3\times10^{5}=(870+90r)\times10^{4}\ \text{m}^2$$ $$=9\times10^{6}+9\times10^{5}r-3\times10^{5}.$$ Hint: Apply the recurrence once with $A_1=9\times10^{6}$; keep the answer in terms of $r$.
Step 3(a)(ii) Use the 3rd-year data to find $r$.

Apply the recurrence twice, given $A_{3}=1.026\times10^{7}$:

$$\left(9\times10^{6}(1+r\%)-3\times10^{5}\right)(1+r\%)-3\times10^{5}=1.026\times10^{7}.$$

Divide by $10^{5}$ and let $u=1+r\%$:

$$150u^{2}-5u-176=0.$$ Hint: Substitute $u=1+r\%$ to turn the recurrence into a quadratic in $u$.
Step 4Solve for $r$. $$u=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25+4(150)(176)}}{2(150)}=\frac{5\pm325}{300} \Rightarrow u=\frac{11}{10}\ \text{or}\ u=-\frac{16}{15}.$$

Reject $u=-\tfrac{16}{15}$ (a negative multiplier is impossible). So $1+r\%=1.1$.

$$\boxed{r=10}$$ Hint: Only the positive root above $1$ is meaningful since $A_n$ grows.
Step 5(b)(i) Closed form for $A_n$.
Sum of a Geometric Series \(S_{n}=\dfrac{a(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}\) The unrolled pull-down terms form a geometric series with ratio \(1.1\) and \(n-1\) terms.

With $r=10$, $A_{n}=1.1A_{n-1}-3\times10^{5}$. Unroll the recurrence:

$$A_{n}=9\times10^{6}(1.1)^{\,n-1}-3\times10^{5}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(1.1)^{k}.$$

Using the geometric sum $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(1.1)^{k}=\frac{(1.1)^{n-1}-1}{1.1-1}$:

$$A_{n}=9\times10^{6}(1.1)^{\,n-1}-3\times10^{6}\left((1.1)^{\,n-1}-1\right) =\left(6(1.1)^{\,n-1}+3\right)\times10^{6}\ \text{m}^2.$$ Hint: Each "pull-down" term contributes a geometric series — sum it, then simplify.
Step 6(b)(ii) First year exceeding $4\times10^{7}$.

Require $A_n>4\times10^{7}$:

$$\left(6(1.1)^{\,n-1}+3\right)\times10^{6}>4\times10^{7} \Rightarrow (1.1)^{\,n-1}>\frac{37}{6}.$$

Take logarithms (base $10$):

$$(n-1)\log 1.1>\log\frac{37}{6} \Rightarrow n-1>\frac{\log\frac{37}{6}}{\log 1.1}>20.0867.$$

So $n>21.0867$, giving the smallest integer $n=22$.

Hint: Always round up for "first exceeds" — the answer is the smallest integer satisfying the inequality.
Step 7Answer (b)(ii). $$\boxed{\text{At the end of the 22nd year}}$$ Hint: Check: $A_{21}\approx3.94\times10^{7}$ and $A_{22}\approx4.04\times10^{7}$, so the 22nd year is the first to exceed $4\times10^7$.
Step 8(c) Find $a$ and $b$.

Given needed area $N_n=a(1.21)^{n}+b$ with $N_1=1\times10^{7}$ and $N_2=1.063\times10^{7}$:

$$1.21a+b=10^{7},\qquad (1.21)^{2}a+b=1.063\times10^{7}.$$

Subtract: $\;a\!\left(1.4641-1.21\right)=0.063\times10^{7} \Rightarrow a=\dfrac{0.063\times10^{7}}{0.2541}=\dfrac{300}{121}\times10^{6}.$

Then $b=10^{7}-1.21a=7\times10^{6}.$

Hint: Two data points give two simultaneous linear equations in $a$ and $b$.
Step 9Set up the comparison (*).

Compare $A_n$ with $N_n$:

$$\left(6(1.1)^{\,n-1}+3\right)\times10^{6}>\left(\frac{300}{121}(1.21)^{n}+7\right)\times10^{6}\quad\cdots(*).$$

Rearrange:

$$-\frac{300}{121}(1.21)^{n}-7+6(1.1)^{\,n-1}+3>0.$$ Hint: Divide through by $10^{6}$ first, then move all terms to one side.
Step 10Show (*) has no solution via the discriminant.
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For \(75x^{2}-165x+121\), \(\Delta=-9075<0\), so the quadratic is always positive.

Note $(1.21)^{n}=(1.1)^{2n}=\left[(1.1)^{n}\right]^{2}$ and $6(1.1)^{\,n-1}=6\cdot\dfrac{(1.1)^{n}}{1.1}=\dfrac{60}{11}(1.1)^{n}$.

Multiplying (*) by $\dfrac{121}{60}$ (positive) with $x=(1.1)^{n}>0$ gives:

$$75x^{2}-165x+121<0.$$

Discriminant: $\Delta=(-165)^{2}-4(75)(121)=-9075<0$, and the coefficient $75>0$, so the quadratic is always positive.

Hint: A negative discriminant with a positive leading coefficient means the quadratic never changes sign — here it is always $>0$, so $(*)$ is never satisfied.
Step 11Conclusion.

Since $75x^{2}-165x+121>0$ for all real $x>0$, inequality $(*)$ has no solution. Thus $A_n$ is never greater than $N_n$ for any year $n$.

Hint: "No solution for $(*)$" $\Rightarrow$ the claim is incorrect.
Answer (a)(i) $(870+90r)\times10^{4}\ \text{m}^2$  ·  (a)(ii) $r=10$
(b)(i) $A_n=\left(6(1.1)^{\,n-1}+3\right)\times10^{6}\ \text{m}^2$  ·  (b)(ii) At the end of the 22nd year
(c) $a=\dfrac{300}{121}\times10^{6},\; b=7\times10^{6}$; inequality $(*)$ reduces to $75x^2-165x+121<0$ with $\Delta=-9075<0$, which has no solution. Hence the claim is incorrect.

Question. The number of trees in a plantation at the end of the 1st year is \(5\times10^{5}\). Each year the number increases by \(r\%\) of the number at the end of the previous year, and \(2\times10^{4}\) trees are cut down. At the end of the 3rd year the number is \(6.76\times10^{5}\).
(a) Find \(r\).
(b) Find the number of trees at the end of the \(n\)th year.
(c) Find the first year in which the number exceeds \(10^{6}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(A_{n}\) be the number at the end of the \(n\)th year: \(A_{1}=5\times10^{5}\) and \(A_{n}=A_{n-1}(1+r\%)-2\times10^{4}\).
Step 2. With \(u=1+r\%\): \(A_{3}=5\times10^{5}u^{2}-2\times10^{4}u-2\times10^{4}=6.76\times10^{5}\), which simplifies to \(125u^{2}-5u-174=0\).
Step 3. \(u=\dfrac{5\pm\sqrt{25+87000}}{250}=\dfrac{5\pm295}{250}\), so \(u=1.2\) (the negative root is rejected) and \(r=20\).
Step 4. Unrolling the recurrence: \(A_{n}=5\times10^{5}(1.2)^{n-1}-2\times10^{4}\times\dfrac{(1.2)^{n-1}-1}{0.2}=\left(4(1.2)^{n-1}+1\right)\times10^{5}\).
Step 5. Require \(4(1.2)^{n-1}+1>10\), i.e. \((1.2)^{n-1}>2.25\). Taking logarithms, \(n-1>4.448\), so \(n>5.448\) and the first such year is \(n=6\).
Answer: (a) \(r=20\) (b) \(\left(4(1.2)^{n-1}+1\right)\times10^{5}\) (c) the 6th year
