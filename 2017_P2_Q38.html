Finding a length in a rectangle using the cosine formula

Pythagoras' Theorem Cosine Formula Rectangle Diagonal Trigonometric Ratio

QQuestion

[2017MC#38] In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rectangle. If \(E\) is a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(AE = 30\text{ cm}\), then \(DE =\)

A\(3\sqrt{65}\) cm
B\(5\sqrt{29}\) cm
C\(\sqrt{641}\) cm
D\(\sqrt{697}\) cm

Given: \(AB = 28\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 21\text{ cm}\), \(E \in AC\) with \(AE = 30\text{ cm}\).

Original Figure

1Explore the Rectangle & the Point \(E\)

Drag the slider to slide the point \(E\) along the diagonal \(AC\). Watch how the length \(DE\) changes — at \(AE = 30\) cm we can read off the answer.

\(AC = 35\) cm (fixed)
\(AE = 30.0\) cm
\(EC = 5.0\) cm
\(DE = 24.187\) cm
0 / 4
Step 1

In the right-angled triangle \(ABC\), \(AB = 28\) cm and \(BC = 21\) cm. The diagonal \(AC\) is the hypotenuse, so apply Pythagoras' Theorem:

\[ AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{28^2 + 21^2} = \sqrt{784 + 441} = \sqrt{1225} = 35\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(7^2 = 49\), so \(28 = 4\times 7\) and \(21 = 3\times 7\). Thus \(AC = 7\sqrt{4^2+3^2} = 7\times 5 = 35\). Look for the "3–4–5" pattern!
\(AC = 35\) cm
Step 2

We need the angle \(\angle DAE\) (the angle between \(AD\) and \(AC\)). In triangle \(ACD\) (right-angled at \(D\), with \(AD = BC = 21\) cm and \(AC = 35\) cm):

\[ \cos \angle CAD = \frac{AD}{AC} = \frac{21}{35} = \frac{3}{5} \]
Hint: Since \(\angle DAE = \angle CAD\) (they are the same angle — \(A\), \(E\), \(C\) are collinear), this value will be substituted directly into the cosine formula.
\(\cos \angle DAE = \dfrac{3}{5}\)
Step 3

Now look at \(\triangle ADE\), where we know two sides and the included angle:

\[ AD = 21 \text{ cm}, \quad AE = 30 \text{ cm}, \quad \angle DAE \text{ with } \cos\angle DAE = \tfrac{3}{5} \]

Apply the cosine formula to find \(DE\):

\[ DE = \sqrt{AD^2 + AE^2 - 2\cdot AD \cdot AE \cdot \cos \angle DAE} \]
Hint: The cosine formula is just Pythagoras with a correction term \(-2ab\cos C\). If the angle were \(90^\circ\), the correction vanishes.
Cosine formula set up correctly.
Step 4

Substitute and simplify:

\[ \begin{aligned} DE &= \sqrt{21^2 + 30^2 - 2(21)(30)\left(\tfrac{3}{5}\right)} \\ &= \sqrt{441 + 900 - 756} \\ &= \sqrt{585} \\ &= \sqrt{9 \times 65} = 3\sqrt{65} \text{ cm} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: \(\sqrt{585}\) is not in simplest form — always look for the largest perfect square factor: \(585 = 9\times 65\).
\(DE = 3\sqrt{65}\) cm  →  Answer A

2Focus on \(\triangle ADE\) — the Cosine Formula

Isolate \(\triangle ADE\). Drag the slider to change \(AE\) (from 0 to 35 cm) and see how \(DE\), computed by the cosine formula, behaves. The marker shows the position \(AE = 30\) cm from the question.

\(\cos \angle DAE = 0.6000\) (constant)
\(\angle DAE = 53.13^\circ\)
\(DE = \sqrt{441 + AE^2 - 36\,AE} = 24.187\) cm
\(DE^2 = 585.0\) cm²
0 / 3
Step 1Observation

Because \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, \(\triangle ACD\) is right-angled at \(D\). The diagonal \(AC\) splits the rectangle into two congruent right-angled triangles. Hence:

\[ \angle DAE = \angle DAC, \qquad \cos\angle DAE = \frac{AD}{AC} = \frac{21}{35} = 0.6 \]
Hint: \(\angle DAE\) is constant no matter where \(E\) sits on \(AC\) — only the side \(AE\) changes.
\(\cos \angle DAE = 0.6\), so \(\angle DAE \approx 53.13^\circ\)
Step 2

In \(\triangle ADE\), the two known sides are \(AD = 21\) cm and \(AE = 30\) cm, and they form the included angle \(\angle DAE\). The cosine formula gives:

\[ DE^2 = AD^2 + AE^2 - 2(AD)(AE)\cos\angle DAE \] \[ DE^2 = 21^2 + 30^2 - 2(21)(30)(0.6) \]
Hint: Use \(DE^2\) first, then take the square root at the end — this avoids rounding errors.
\(DE^2 = 441 + 900 - 756 = 585\)
Step 3

Take the positive square root (a length is positive) and simplify the surd:

\[ DE = \sqrt{585} = \sqrt{9\cdot 65} = 3\sqrt{65}\text{ cm} \approx 24.19\text{ cm} \]
Hint: Check against the options: \(3\sqrt{65} \approx 24.19\), \(5\sqrt{29} \approx 26.93\), \(\sqrt{641}\approx 25.32\), \(\sqrt{697}\approx 26.40\). Only A matches.
\(DE = 3\sqrt{65}\) cm  →  A

Summary of the Solution

  • Diagonal: \(AC = \sqrt{28^2+21^2} = 35\) cm (Pythagoras, 3–4–5 ratio).
  • Angle: \(\cos \angle DAE = \dfrac{AD}{AC} = \dfrac{21}{35} = \dfrac{3}{5}\).
  • Cosine formula in \(\triangle ADE\) with \(AD=21\), \(AE=30\): \[ DE = \sqrt{21^2+30^2-2(21)(30)\left(\tfrac35\right)} = \sqrt{585} \]
  • Simplified: \(DE = 3\sqrt{65}\) cm \(\approx 24.19\) cm.
  • Correct option: A (\(3\sqrt{65}\) cm).
