Simplify the expression

Simplify $$ \frac{x^{-8}\,y}{(x^{7}y^{9})^{-6}} $$ and express your answer with positive indices.

Step-by-step solution

Step 1: Apply power of a product rule to the denominator ▼

We have (x⁷ y⁹)⁻⁶. Distribute the exponent −6 to each factor inside the parentheses.

$$ (x^{7}y^{9})^{-6} = (x^{7})^{-6} \cdot (y^{9})^{-6} $$
📌 Hint: Rule: $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$. Here $a = x^7$, $b = y^9$, $n = -6$.
Step 2: Simplify powers of powers ▼

Multiply the exponents: $(x^{7})^{-6} = x^{7 \times (-6)} = x^{-42}$, and $(y^{9})^{-6} = y^{9 \times (-6)} = y^{-54}$.

$$ (x^{7}y^{9})^{-6} = x^{-42}\, y^{-54} $$
📌 Hint: Power of a power rule: $(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$.
Step 3: Rewrite the whole expression as a single fraction ▼

Substitute the denominator back:

$$ \frac{x^{-8}\,y}{x^{-42}\,y^{-54}} $$

Now apply the quotient rule for each variable: $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$.

Step 4: Apply quotient rule to x and y separately ▼

For \(x\): exponent = \(-8 - (-42) = -8 + 42 = 34\).

For \(y\): exponent = \(1 - (-54) = 1 + 54 = 55\).

$$ = x^{\,34}\, y^{\,55} $$
📌 Hint: Remember \(y = y^1\). So for \(y\), the exponent in the numerator is \(1\). Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: \(1 - (-54) = 1 + 54\).
✅ Step 5: Final answer with positive indices ▼

Both exponents \(34\) and \(55\) are positive, so the expression is already in the required form.

$$ \frac{x^{-8}\,y}{(x^{7}y^{9})^{-6}} = x^{34}\, y^{55} $$

✅ All indices are positive — no further changes needed.

🧠 Key indices rules used

  • Power of a product: $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$
  • Power of a power: $(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$
  • Quotient rule: $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$
  • Positive indices: final answer should have no negative exponents
