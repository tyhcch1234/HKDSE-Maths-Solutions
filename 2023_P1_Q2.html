HKDSE 2023 Paper 1 — Question 2

[2023 #2]

Simplify \(\dfrac{x^{-8}y}{\left(x^{7}y^{9}\right)^{-6}}\) and express your answer with positive indices. [3 marks]

1 Remove the outer power

Apply the power-of-a-product rule  \((ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}\)  to the denominator.

\[(x^{7}y^{9})^{-6}=x^{7\times(-6)}\,y^{9\times(-6)}=x^{-42}y^{-54}\]

Multiply the small exponents \(7\) and \(9\) by \(-6\). Keep the signs!

2 Rewrite as a single fraction

Substitute the denominator back:

\[\frac{x^{-8}y}{x^{-42}y^{-54}}\]

Now it is a quotient of like bases — use \(\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{m-n}\) on each letter separately.

3 Subtract the exponents

For the \(x\)-powers: \(\;-8-(-42)=-8+42=34\)

For the \(y\)-powers: \(\;1-(-54)=1+54=55\)

\[=x^{\,34}y^{\,55}\]

Remember the hidden exponent on \(y\), i.e. \(y=y^{1}\). Subtracting a negative adds.

4 Check the indices are positive

Both exponents \(34\) and \(55\) are positive, so no further rewriting (e.g. into \(\frac{1}{x^{k}}\)) is needed.

The question demands positive indices — this final check earns the last mark.

Answer:

\[\frac{x^{-8}y}{\left(x^{7}y^{9}\right)^{-6}}=x^{34}y^{55}\]
