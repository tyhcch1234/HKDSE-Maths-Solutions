Trigonometry — Amplitude, period and phase shift of \(y=\sin(kx^\circ+\theta)\)

Sine Graph Period & Frequency \(k\) Phase Shift \(\theta\) Horizontal Scaling

QUESTION The Problem

Let \(k\) be a positive constant and \(-180^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ\). If the figure shows the graph of \(y=\sin(kx^\circ+\theta)\), then
A. \(\;k=\dfrac{1}{2}\) and \(\theta=-30^\circ\)
B. \(\;k=\dfrac{1}{2}\) and \(\theta=30^\circ\)
C. \(\;k=2\) and \(\theta=-30^\circ\)
D. \(\;k=2\) and \(\theta=30^\circ\)
Read the figure: The curve crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=75\) going downward (it had been rising), and rises again through the \(x\)-axis at \(x=165\). Two consecutive "rising" axis-crossings are one full period apart.

STEP GROUP 1 Finding the frequency \(k\) from the period

Period = 180° k = 2 x-intercepts: 75°, 165°
0 / 3
1Identify one complete period from the graph
The curve descends through \((75,0)\) and rises again through \((165,0)\). Between two successive crossings where the graph goes the same way is exactly one period. \[ T = 165^\circ - 75^\circ = 90^\circ \]
Hint: The points \(x=75\) and \(x=165\) are both "rising" (negative→positive) axis-crossings, so they are exactly one period apart. (Equivalently, the maximum at \(x=120\) and the minimum at \(x=30\) are half a period apart: \(120-30=90\).)
2Relate the period to \(k\)
For \(y=\sin(kx^\circ+\theta)\) the period is \[ T = \frac{360^\circ}{k}. \] Substituting \(T=90^\circ\): \[ \frac{360^\circ}{k}=90^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac{360}{90}=4. \] But wait — check the scaling direction. Here the graph reaches a full cycle in \(90^\circ\), which is shorter than the standard \(360^\circ\), so the graph is compressed, meaning \(k>1\). Let us re-verify with the reference curve.
Hint: \(k\) is the number of complete cycles fitted into \(360^\circ\). A period of \(90^\circ\) means 4 cycles in \(360^\circ\)… so we must be careful — the figure's labelled crossings are \(75\) and \(165\), but is the distance between them a full period? Two identical-type crossings (both descending) are one period apart. Use the descending crossings at \(x=75\) and the next descending crossing.
3Correct reading of the period
The labelled crossings are \(x=75\) (descending) and \(x=165\) (ascending) — that is only half a period, because the graph goes from "crossing down" to "crossing up". \[ \frac{T}{2}=165^\circ-75^\circ=90^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=180^\circ. \] Hence \[ k=\frac{360^\circ}{180^\circ}=2. \]
✔ \(\;k=2\)
Hint: A quick sanity check — the peak is at \(x=120^\circ\) and the trough at \(x=30^\circ\) (reading the figure). Peak-to-trough is half a period: \(120-30=90=\tfrac{T}{2}\), so \(T=180^\circ\).

STEP GROUP 2 Finding the phase shift \(\theta\)

θ = 30° sin(kx+θ)=0 at x = 75° k=2, θ=30°
0 / 3
1Use a known point on the curve
We already found \(k=2\), so \(y=\sin(2x^\circ+\theta)\). The point \((75,0)\) lies on the graph: \[ \sin(2(75)^\circ+\theta)=0 \] \[ \sin(150^\circ+\theta)=0. \]
Hint: The zeros of \(\sin u\) occur at \(u=0^\circ,180^\circ,-180^\circ,\dots\) So \(150^\circ+\theta\) must be a multiple of \(180^\circ\).
2Solve for the possible values of \(\theta\)
\[ 150^\circ+\theta = 0^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; \theta=-150^\circ \] \[ 150^\circ+\theta = 180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; \theta=30^\circ \] Both satisfy \(-180^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\). Use the second crossing or the gradient to decide which one is correct.
Hint: We must also match the terminal direction: at \(x=75\) the curve is going downward (slope negative). Compute \(\tfrac{dy}{dx}=2\cos(2x+\theta)\cdot\tfrac{\pi}{180}\) and test each candidate.
3Test the slope at \(x=75^\circ\)
Try \(\theta=30^\circ\): \(\;2(75)+30=180^\circ\), and \(\cos 180^\circ = -1 <0\) — descending. ✓
Try \(\theta=-150^\circ\): \(\;2(75)-150=0^\circ\), and \(\cos 0^\circ = +1 >0\) — ascending. ✗
So we must have \[ \theta = 30^\circ. \]
✔ \(\;k=2\) and \(\theta=30^\circ\) — Answer D
Hint (elegant check): \(y=\sin(2x^\circ+30^\circ)=\sin(2(x+15)^\circ)\). So the graph of \(y=\sin(2x^\circ)\) is shifted left by \(15^\circ\). Its first zeros after the shift move from \(0^\circ,90^\circ,\ldots\) to \(-15^\circ,75^\circ,165^\circ,\ldots\) — exactly matching the figure.

DEEP DIVE How \(y=\sin x^\circ\) becomes \(y=\sin(2x^\circ+30^\circ)\) concept

A: y = sin x B: y = sin 2x C: y = sin(2x + 30°)
Read this diagram: First the period is halved (\(k=2\)), giving \(y=\sin 2x^\circ\) with zeros at \(0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ,\ldots\) Then the whole curve is translated left by \(15^\circ\), moving those zeros to \(-15^\circ, 75^\circ, 165^\circ,\ldots\) — precisely the two labelled crossings in the question.

📋  Final Answer at a Glance

  • Period of the graph: \(\;\dfrac{T}{2}=165^\circ-75^\circ=90^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; T=180^\circ\)
  • Frequency: \(\;k=\dfrac{360^\circ}{T}=\dfrac{360^\circ}{180^\circ}=\mathbf{2}\)
  • Substitute \((75,0)\): \(\;\sin(150^\circ+\theta)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta=-150^\circ\) or \(\theta=30^\circ\)
  • Slope test at \(x=75^\circ\) (descending) rules out \(\theta=-150^\circ\)
  • Final: \(\;\boxed{\,k=2,\quad \theta=30^\circ\,}\)
  • Correct option: D
  • Equivalently: \(\;y=\sin(2x^\circ+30^\circ)=\sin\!\big(2(x+15)^\circ\big)\) — shift left by \(15^\circ\)
