HKDSE 2013 P2 Q37 Inequalities & Linear Programming

Consider the following system of inequalities:

$$x\ge 2,\qquad y\ge 0,\qquad 4x-y\le 20,\qquad x+4y\le 22.$$

Let $D$ be the region which represents the solution of the above system of inequalities. If $(x,y)$ is a point lying in $D$, then the greatest value of $3y-4x+15$ is

A. 3 B. 17 C. 22 D. 30
1 Read off the constraints and shade $D$

The four constraints are

$$x\ge 2,\qquad y\ge 0,\qquad 4x-y\le 20,\qquad x+4y\le 22.$$

$D$ is the bounded polygon cut out on the right by the two slanting lines $4x-y=20$ and $x+4y=22$, bounded on the left by the vertical $x=2$ and below by $y=0$.

Hint: the vertices of $D$ come from intersecting pairs of these boundary lines.
2 Find the four corner points

$x=2$ with $y=0$:   $(x,y)=(2,0)$.

$y=0$ with $4x-y=20$ gives $4x=20\Rightarrow x=5$:   $(x,y)=(5,0)$.

$4x-y=20$ with $x+4y=22$. From the first, $y=4x-20$. Substitute: $$x+4(4x-20)=22 \Rightarrow 17x=102 \Rightarrow x=6,\; y=4.$$ So $(x,y)=(6,4)$.

$x+4y=22$ with $x=2$:   $2+4y=22\Rightarrow y=5$:   $(x,y)=(2,5)$.

Hint: there are 4 vertices, so 4 candidate points.
Method of Substitution \(y=mx+k\ \Longrightarrow\ dx+e(mx+k)=f\) Writing \\(y=4x-20\\) from \\(4x-y=20\\) and substituting into \\(x+4y=22\\) gives the corner \\((6,4)\\) at once.
3 Use the "corner-point" (vertex) principle

The objective $3y-4x+15$ is linear in $x$ and $y$. A linear function on a bounded polygonal region always attains its greatest (and least) value at a corner point of the region.

So it suffices to test the four corner points found in Step 2.

Hint: no need to test interior points — the extreme value sits on a vertex.
Corner-Point Principle \(\max_{D}\,(ax+by+c)=\max_{\text{vertices}}\,(ax+by+c)\) A linear objective on the bounded region \\(D\\) is greatest at a vertex, so only the four corners need testing.
4 Evaluate $3y-4x+15$ at each corner
$(x,y)$$3y-4x+15$
$(2,0)$$3(0)-4(2)+15=7$
$(5,0)$$3(0)-4(5)+15=-5$
$(6,4)$$3(4)-4(6)+15=3$
$(2,5)$$3(5)-4(2)+15=22$
Hint: the largest value appears at $(2,5)$, the top-left corner.
5 Conclusion

The greatest value of $3y-4x+15$ over $D$ is $22$.

(Consistency check: as $x$ decreases and $y$ increases, $-4x+3y$ grows — direction $(-4,3)$ points towards the top-left corner $(2,5)$, the maximum.)

Answer: C. 22 Greatest value of $3y-4x+15$ on $D$ occurs at $(x,y)=(2,5)$.

Question. A region \\(D\\) is defined by \\(x\ge1\\), \\(y\ge0\\), \\(3x-y\le12\\) and \\(x+y\le8\\). If \\((x,y)\\) lies in \\(D\\), find the greatest value of \\(5y-2x+6\\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The corner points come from intersecting pairs of boundary lines. \\(x=1\\) with \\(y=0\\) gives \\((1,0)\\); \\(y=0\\) with \\(3x-y=12\\) gives \\((4,0)\\); \\(x=1\\) with \\(x+y=8\\) gives \\((1,7)\\).
Step 2. The fourth corner is where \\(3x-y=12\\) meets \\(x+y=8\\): adding gives \\(4x=20\\), so \\(x=5\\) and \\(y=3\\).
Step 3. Evaluate \\(5y-2x+6\\): at \\((1,0)\\) it is \\(4\\); at \\((4,0)\\), \\(-2\\); at \\((5,3)\\), \\(11\\); at \\((1,7)\\), \\(39\\).
Step 4. By the corner-point principle the greatest value over the bounded region is \\(39\\), attained at \\((1,7)\\).
Answer: \(39\) (at \\((x,y)=(1,7)\\))
