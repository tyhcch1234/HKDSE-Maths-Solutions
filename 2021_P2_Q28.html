HKDSE 2021 Paper 2 — Q28

Probability The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the weights (in kg) of a group of workers.

Stem (tens)Leaf (units)
53  6  7
61  2  2  2  6  8
72  3  4  5  7  7  9  9
83  4  5  6  6  7  8

If a worker is randomly selected from the group, find the probability that the weight of the selected worker is not less than the lower quartile of the distribution.

A. $\dfrac{1}{4}$     B. $\dfrac{1}{5}$     C. $\dfrac{1}{6}$     D. $\dfrac{5}{6}$

Step 1 — Read the diagram

Count the leaves to get the total number of workers.

$3 + 6 + 8 + 7 = 24$ data values, so $n = 24$.

Always count the leaves first — the total number of data is needed for every probability.
Step 2 — Write data in order

Reading the stem-and-leaf diagram row by row gives the weights in ascending order:

53, 56, 57,
61, 62, 62, 62, 66, 68,
72, 73, 74, 75, 77, 77, 79, 79,
83, 84, 85, 86, 86, 87, 88

The 6th and 7th values are both $62$ kg.

A stem-and-leaf diagram is already sorted — just read left to right, top to bottom.
Step 3 — Find the lower quartile
Lower Quartile \(Q_{1}=\text{mean of the }\dfrac{n}{4}\text{-th and }\left(\dfrac{n}{4}+1\right)\text{-th values}\) With \(n=20\) the lower quartile is the mean of the 5th and 6th values, \(52\) and \(52\).

Since there are 24 data, the lower quartile is the mean of the 6th and the 7th ordered data.

$$Q_1 = \frac{62 + 62}{2} = 62 \text{ kg}$$

For $n = 24$ (even), $Q_1$ is at position $\frac{n+1}{4}$ for median-based splits, but here we use the "split into two halves" convention: median of the lower 12 values → average of 6th and 7th.
Step 4 — Count favourable outcomes

We need the number of workers with weight ≥ 62 kg.

Values below 62: $53, 56, 57, 61$ → 4 values.
So values $\ge 62$: $24 - 4 = 20$ values.

"Not less than" means $\ge$. Count carefully — the two 62s are included.
Step 5 — Compute the probability
Probability of an Event \(P(E)=\dfrac{n(E)}{n(S)}\) 16 of the 20 weights are not less than 52 kg, giving \(P=\dfrac{16}{20}\).

$$\text{Required probability} = \frac{20}{24} = \frac{5}{6}$$

Answer: D

Probability $= \dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$.
Answer: D    $\dfrac{5}{6}$

Question. The weights (in kg) of 20 students are: \(41,44,45,51,52,52,53,56,61,62,63,64,66,66,68,68,72,73,74,75\).
(a) Find the lower quartile.
(b) If a student is chosen at random, find the probability that the student's weight is not less than the lower quartile.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. There are \(n=20\) data values, already in ascending order.
Step 2. (a) \(Q_{1}\) is the mean of the 5th and 6th values: \(\dfrac{52+52}{2}=52\ \text{kg}\).
Step 3. (b) Values below 52: \(41,44,45,51\) — 4 values, so \(20-4=16\) values are at least 52.
Step 4. (b) \(P=\dfrac{16}{20}=\dfrac{4}{5}\).
Answer: (a) \(52\ \text{kg}\) (b) \(\dfrac{4}{5}\)
