HKDSE 2021 Paper 2 Q28 Probability & Statistics

Stem-and-Leaf Diagram & Lower Quartile

Find the probability that a randomly selected worker weighs not less than the lower quartile.

Question

The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the weights (in kg) of a group of workers.

Stem (tens) Leaf (units)
5 3 6 7
6 1 2 2 2 6 8
7 2 3 4 5 7 7 9 9
8 3 4 5 6 6 7 8

If a worker is randomly selected from the group, find the probability that the weight of the selected worker is not less than the lower quartile of the distribution.

A. \(\frac{1}{4}\)
B. \(\frac{1}{5}\)
C. \(\frac{1}{6}\)
D. \(\frac{5}{6}\)

Weight Distribution (Frequency)

Each bar represents the number of workers (frequency) at that weight. The highlighted bar (at 65 kg) is the median of the lower half – the lower quartile.

53 kg 66 kg 78 kg
Lower Quartile = 62 kg

Step-by-Step Solution

Count the leaves (each leaf represents one worker):

  • Stem 5: 3 leaves (53, 56, 57 kg)
  • Stem 6: 6 leaves (61, 62, 62, 62, 66, 68 kg)
  • Stem 7: 8 leaves (72, 73, 74, 75, 77, 77, 79, 79 kg)
  • Stem 8: 7 leaves (83, 84, 85, 86, 86, 87, 88 kg)

Total number of workers \( n = 3 + 6 + 8 + 7 = 24 \).

Hint: The total number of data values is the sum of all leaves. Here \(n = 24\).

With \( n = 24 \) data values, the lower quartile \(Q_1\) is the median of the lower half of the data.

The lower half consists of the first \( \frac{24}{2} = 12 \) ordered values.

Since there are 12 values, the median of the lower half is the average of the 6th and 7th values in the ordered data.

Hint: For an even number of data, the lower quartile is the mean of the two middle values of the lower half. Here, positions 6 and 7.

List the data in ascending order (already sorted in the stem-and-leaf diagram):

53 56 57 61 62 62 62 66 68 72 73 74 75 77 77 79 79 83 84 85 86 86 87 88

The 6th value is 62 kg, and the 7th value is also 62 kg.

Hint: Ensure the data is ordered from smallest to largest before locating positions.

Lower quartile \( Q_1 = \frac{62 + 62}{2} = 62 \) kg.

So, the lower quartile weight is 62 kg.

Hint: The average of 62 and 62 is simply 62. No change to the value.

We need workers with weight \( \geq 62 \) kg.

From the ordered list, the workers with weight \( \geq 62 \) kg are:

62, 62, 62, 66, 68, 72, 73, 74, 75, 77, 77, 79, 79, 83, 84, 85, 86, 86, 87, 88

Count them: \( 3 + 6 + 8 + 7 = 24 \) total, but we exclude the first two values (53 and 56) and one 57? Wait, let's count directly.

Values below 62: 53, 56, 57, 61 → 4 values.

Total values = 24. So values \( \geq 62 \) = \(24 - 4 = 20\).

Hint: "Not less than" means \( \geq \). Count all data from 62 kg upwards.

Probability \( = \frac{\text{number of workers with weight} \geq 62}{\text{total number of workers}} \)

\( = \frac{20}{24} = \frac{5}{6} \).

✅ Correct answer: D. \( \frac{5}{6} \)

Key Concepts

  • Stem-and-leaf diagram: A way to display data where the last digit is the leaf and the preceding digits form the stem.
  • Lower quartile (\(Q_1\)): The median of the lower half of the data. For \(n\) data, it's the median of the first \(n/2\) values when \(n\) is even.
  • Probability: \( P = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} \).
  • "Not less than" means \( \geq \).
