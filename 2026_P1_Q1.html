Making the Subject of a Formula

Question

Make \(m\) the subject of the formula \[ \frac{m}{2}-1=\frac{5m-n}{3} . \] (3 marks)

Solution

STEP 1
Clear the fractions: multiply both sides by 6
Clearing Fractions (L.C.M.) \(\text{multiply every term by the L.C.M.}\) Multiplying by \(6\) gives \(3m-6=2(5m-n)\), removing both denominators.
(the LCM of 2 and 3). \[ 6\left(\frac{m}{2}-1\right)=6\cdot\frac{5m-n}{3} \implies 3m-6=2(5m-n) \]
STEP 2
Expand the right-hand side. \[ 3m-6=10m-2n \]
STEP 3
Collecting Like Terms \(2n-6=7m\) Subtracting \(3m\) and adding \(6\) leaves \(7m\) alone on the right.
Collect the m-terms on one side.
Subtract 3m from both sides and add 6: \[ 2n-6=10m-3m \implies 2n-6=7m \]
STEP 4
Divide by 7 to isolate m. \[ m=\frac{2n-6}{7} \]
Check: substituting back, \(\frac{m}{2}-1=\frac{2n-6-14}{14}=\frac{n-10}{7}\) and \(\frac{5m-n}{3}=\frac{5(2n-6)-7n}{21}=\frac{n-10}{7}\). Both sides agree. ✓
\( m=\dfrac{2n-6}{7} \)
