Coordinate Geometry — Equation of a Locus of a Moving Point

Locus Perpendicular Bisector Mid-point Formula Slope & Perpendicular Lines Multiple Choice

QQuestion MC · 5 marks

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((2,\,5)\) and \((4,\,-1)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(AP = BP\). Find the equation of the locus of \(P\).

  • A.   \(x - 3y + 3 = 0\)
  • B.   \(x - 3y - 7 = 0\)
  • C.   \(x - 3y + 13 = 0\)
  • D.   \(3x + y - 11 = 0\)

1Part (a) — Identify the locus of \(P\) 1M

Since \(AP = BP\), the moving point \(P\) is always equidistant from the two fixed points \(A\) and \(B\). The locus of all such points is the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(AB\).

The locus of \(P\) is the set of points \(\{(x,y): AP = BP\}\), which is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
\(A(2,5)\) \(B(4,-1)\) \(M(3,2)\) \(AP = 3.20\) \(BP = 3.20\)
0 / 3
Step 11M
Interpret the condition \(AP = BP\): the locus is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
Hint: "Equidistant from two fixed points" is the classic definition of a perpendicular bisector — not a circle, not a straight line through \(A\) and \(B\).
Locus of \(P\) = perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
Step 2Reasoning
The perpendicular bisector passes through the mid-point \(M\) of \(AB\), and is perpendicular to \(AB\). So any point \(P\) on it satisfies \(AP = BP\) and \(\angle AMP = 90^\circ\).
Hint: Drag the slider — as \(P\) moves, the two dashed lengths stay equal. That visual equality is the locus condition.
Key geometric facts: through \(M\), perpendicular to \(AB\).
Step 3Result
Substituting \(A(2,5)\), \(B(4,-1)\) into \(AP^2 = BP^2\): \[ (x-2)^2 + (y-5)^2 = (x-4)^2 + (y+1)^2 \] which will be simplified in Part (b) to give the required line.
Hint: "Squaring the distances" is quicker than finding the mid-point and slope, and avoids fraction slopes.
\(AP^2 = BP^2\) is the algebraic version of the locus condition.

2Part (b)(i) — Mid-point of \(AB\) 1M

Use the mid-point formula to find the co-ordinates of the mid-point \(M\) of \(AB\).

\(M = \left(\dfrac{2+4}{2},\ \dfrac{5+(-1)}{2}\right)\) \(M(3,\,2)\)
0 / 2
Step 11M
\[ M = \left(\frac{2+4}{2},\ \frac{5+(-1)}{2}\right) = (3,\ 2) \]
Hint: Mid-point formula: add the \(x\)-coordinates and halve; add the \(y\)-coordinates and halve.
\(M(3,\,2)\)
Step 2Check
Indeed \(AM = MB\) and \(M\) lies halfway along the segment — see the diagram: \(M\) is inside the green midpoint marker between \(A\) and \(B\).
Hint: The perpendicular bisector must pass through this exact point. Any other line through \(A\) or \(B\) is wrong.
The locus passes through \(M(3,2)\).

3Part (b)(ii) — Slope of the locus 1M

Find the slope of \(AB\), then use the perpendicular condition to get the slope of the locus.

\(m_{AB} = \dfrac{-1-5}{4-2} = -3\) \(m_{\text{locus}} \cdot m_{AB} = -1\) \(m_{\text{locus}} = \dfrac{1}{3}\)
0 / 2
Step 11M
\[ m_{AB} = \frac{5-(-1)}{2-4} = \frac{6}{-2} = -3 \]
Hint: Slope formula \(m = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\). Be careful with signs: \(5-(-1)=6\) and \(2-4=-2\).
\(m_{AB} = -3\)
Step 21M
Since the locus is perpendicular to \(AB\): \[ m_{\text{locus}} \times (-3) = -1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m_{\text{locus}} = \frac{1}{3} \]
Hint: Perpendicular lines multiply to \(-1\): \(m_1 m_2 = -1\). The negative reciprocal of \(-3\) is \(+\frac{1}{3}\) (positive!).
Slope of the locus \(= \dfrac{1}{3}\)

4Part (b)(iii) — Equation of the locus 1A

Combine the point \(M(3,2)\) and the slope \(\frac{1}{3}\) to write the equation of the locus.

\(y - 2 = \frac{1}{3}(x-3)\) \(x - 3y + 3 = 0\) \(AP = BP = 4.12\)
0 / 3
Step 11M
Point–slope form with \(M(3,2)\) and slope \(\frac{1}{3}\): \[ y - 2 = \frac{1}{3}(x - 3) \]
Hint: Use \(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\). Make sure you take the point that lies on the line: \(M(3,2)\), not \(A\) or \(B\).
\(y - 2 = \frac{1}{3}(x-3)\)
Step 21A
Multiply both sides by \(3\): \[ 3(y-2) = x - 3 \;\Rightarrow\; 3y - 6 = x - 3 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 3y + 3 = 0 \]
Hint: Rearrange so all terms on one side. Watch the signs when moving terms across the equals sign — a common trap is getting \(-3y\) instead of \(+3\).
\(x - 3y + 3 = 0\)  →  Option A
Step 3Check
Alternative (often faster in MC): square the distances directly. \[ (x-2)^2+(y-5)^2 = (x-4)^2+(y+1)^2 \] \[ x^2-4x+4+y^2-10y+25 = x^2-8x+16+y^2+2y+1 \] \[ 4x - 12y + 12 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 3y + 3 = 0 \]
Hint: Squaring removes the square roots immediately — no need for a diagram or slope at all. Ideal for multiple-choice under time pressure.
Both methods agree: \(x - 3y + 3 = 0\), i.e. Option A.

Summary of Answers

  • Locus: perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) (all points equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\)).
  • Mid-point \(M\): \(\left(\frac{2+4}{2},\frac{5+(-1)}{2}\right) = (3,\,2)\).
  • Slope of \(AB\): \(-3\)  ⟹  slope of locus: \(\frac{1}{3}\) (since \(m_1 m_2 = -1\)).
  • Equation: \(y - 2 = \frac{1}{3}(x-3)\)  ⟹  \(\class{hl}{x - 3y + 3 = 0}\).
  • Correct option: A
