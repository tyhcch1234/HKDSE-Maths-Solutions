Mensuration — Sector Arc Length
Arc Length Sector Percentage Change Proportion

QUESTION The Problem

The angle of a sector is decreased by \(60\%\) but its radius is increased by \(k\%\). If the arc length of the sector remains unchanged, find the value of \(k\).

  • A. \(40\)
  • B. \(60\)
  • C. \(67\)
  • D. \(150\)

PART A Understanding the Sector

An arc length \(l\) of a sector is given by \[ l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \] where \(r\) is the radius and \(\theta\) is the angle in degrees. Move the sliders below to see how changing \(r\) and \(\theta\) affects the arc length.

Arc length \(l\) = —
Step 1

Write down the arc length formula for a sector:

\[ l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \]
Hint: This formula comes from taking the fraction \(\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\) of the full circumference \(2\pi r\).
Step 2

Rearrange to isolate the product \(r\theta\):

\[ l = \frac{\pi r \theta}{180} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r\theta = \frac{180\,l}{\pi} = \text{constant} \]
Hint: Since \(l\) is unchanged, the product \(r\theta\) must remain constant.
Key relation: \(r\theta = \text{constant}\)

PART B Finding \(k\)

The angle is decreased by \(60\%\), so the new angle is \((1 - 60\%)\theta = 0.4\theta\). The radius is increased by \(k\%\), so the new radius is \((1 + k\%)r\). Since the arc length is unchanged, the product \(r\theta\) stays the same. Use the slider to see how the new sector compares to the original.

New arc length / Original arc length = —
Step 3

Express the new angle and new radius:

\[ \theta_{\text{new}} = (1 - 60\%)\theta = 0.4\theta \] \[ r_{\text{new}} = (1 + k\%)r \]
Hint: A \(60\%\) decrease means multiplying by \(0.4\). An increase by \(k\%\) means multiplying by \(1 + \frac{k}{100}\).
Step 4

Since the arc length is unchanged, equate the old and new arc lengths:

\[ (1 + k\%)r \times (1 - 60\%)\theta = r\theta \] \[ (1 + k\%) \times 0.4\,\theta r = \theta r \]
Hint: The \(r\theta\) cancels from both sides because it is non-zero.
Step 5

Solve for \(k\):

\[ 0.4(1 + k\%) = 1 \] \[ 1 + k\% = \frac{1}{0.4} = 2.5 \] \[ k\% = 1.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = 150 \]
Hint: \(k\% = 150\%\) means the radius is increased by \(150\%\).
Answer: \(k = 150\) (Option D)

Summary

  • Arc length formula: \(l = 2\pi r \times \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\)
  • Key invariant: Since \(l\) is constant, \(r\theta = \text{constant}\)
  • Equation: \((1 + k\%)(0.4\theta)(r) = \theta r \Rightarrow 0.4(1 + k\%) = 1\)
  • Final answer: \(k = 150\) — Option D
