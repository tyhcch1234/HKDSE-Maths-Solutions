HKDSE Math 2025 Paper 1 Q3

Ratio & Simultaneous Equations

Topic 2 — Rate, Ratio and Variations  ·  3 marks

Question

Let \(m\) and \(n\) be two numbers. The sum of \(2m\) and \(3n\) is \(999\) while the ratio of \(m\) to \(n\) is \(8:7\). Find \(n\).

Ratio Simultaneous Equations Substitution Word Problem

What does the ratio \(m:n = 8:7\) mean?

Split both numbers into equal "parts". \(m\) takes 8 parts, \(n\) takes 7 parts — so \[ \frac{m}{n}=\frac{8}{7} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 7m = 8n \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=\frac{8}{7}\,n. \] Drag the slider to resize the parts and watch the ratio stay locked at \(8:7\).

Step-by-Step Solution

The sentence "the sum of \(2m\) and \(3n\) is 999" becomes an equation:

\[ 2m + 3n = 999 \]

The phrase "the ratio of \(m\) to \(n\) is \(8:7\)" becomes:

\[ \frac{m}{n}=\frac{8}{7} \]
Hint — Ratio \(\to\) equation: the ratio \(a:b\) always means \(\dfrac{\text{first}}{\text{second}}=\dfrac{a}{b}\). Here \(m:n = 8:7\) so \(\dfrac{m}{n}=\dfrac{8}{7}\). Cross-multiplying gives \(7m = 8n\).

From \(\dfrac{m}{n}=\dfrac{8}{7}\), multiply both sides by \(n\):

\[ m = \frac{8}{7}\,n \tag{1} \]

Equivalently, cross-multiplying gives \(7m = 8n\). Both forms are correct — we choose the one that lets us substitute easily.

Hint — Why make \(m\) the subject? We are asked for \(n\). If we replace \(m\) by an expression in \(n\), the equation \(2m+3n=999\) will contain only \(n\) and become solvable in one line.

Replace every \(m\) in \(2m+3n=999\) with \(\dfrac{8}{7}n\):

\[ 2\left(\frac{8}{7}n\right)+3n = 999 \]

Simplify the first term: \(2 \times \dfrac{8}{7}n = \dfrac{16}{7}n\).

\[ \frac{16}{7}n + 3n = 999 \]
Hint — Substitution method: one equation gives one variable in terms of the other (Step 2); "plug it in" to the other equation so only ONE unknown remains. This earns the M mark.

Write \(3n\) as \(\dfrac{21}{7}n\) so both terms share the denominator 7:

\[ \frac{16}{7}n + \frac{21}{7}n = 999 \]

Add the numerators:

\[ \frac{37}{7}n = 999 \]
Hint — Adding fractions: \(\dfrac{16}{7}+3 = \dfrac{16}{7}+\dfrac{21}{7}=\dfrac{37}{7}\). The number of "\(n\)'s" is \(\frac{16}{7} + \frac{21}{7} = \frac{37}{7}\).

Multiply both sides by \(7\), then divide by \(37\):

\[ 37n = 999 \times 7 = 6993 \] \[ n = \frac{6993}{37} = 189 \]

So \(\boxed{n = 189}\).

Hint — Quick check of divisibility: \(6993 = 37 \times 189\), and indeed \(37 \times 189 = 37(200-11)=7400-407=6993\). ✓

If \(n = 189\), then from \(m=\dfrac{8}{7}n\):

\[ m = \frac{8}{7}\times 189 = 8 \times 27 = 216 \]

Now verify the first condition:

\[ 2m + 3n = 2(216) + 3(189) = 432 + 567 = 999 \;\checkmark \]

And the ratio: \(\dfrac{m}{n}=\dfrac{216}{189}=\dfrac{24}{21}=\dfrac{8}{7}\) ✓

Everything matches — the answer \(n = 189\) is correct.

✅ Final Answer: \(n = 189\)

Try It Yourself — Find the "magic" part size

Why does this work? The condition \(2m+3n=999\) picks out exactly ONE value of \(k\): \(2(8k)+3(7k)=16k+21k=37k=999 \Rightarrow k=27\), giving \(n = 7 \times 27 = 189\).
