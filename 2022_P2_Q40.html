Angles between planes in a cube — 3D trigonometry

3D Geometry Angle between Planes Cube / Square Perpendicularity MC Technique

QUESTION 2022MC#40

In the figure, \(ABCDEFGH\) is a cube. Let \(\alpha\) be the angle between \(\triangle AFG\) and \(\triangle AFH\) while \(\beta\) be the angle between \(\triangle AFH\) and \(\triangle FGH\). Which of the following is true?

  • A. \(\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta\)
  • B. \(\alpha < \beta < 60^\circ\)
  • C. \(60^\circ < \alpha < \beta\)
  • D. \(60^\circ < \beta < \alpha\)

Cube labelling convention: \(ABCD\) is the bottom face, \(EFGH\) the top face, with \(A\) above \(E\), \(B\) above \(F\), \(C\) above \(G\), \(D\) above \(H\).

PART (a) Finding \(\alpha\) — angle between \(\triangle AFG\) and \(\triangle AFH\)

Tip: rotate until \(AF\) appears edge-on — then the two triangles collapse to lines and \(\alpha\) is measured in the plane \(\perp AF\).
Unit cube, \(FG = 1\), \(\angle FGH = 90^\circ\), \(FG = GH = 1\)
\(\alpha = \angle GFH =\) 45.0°
0 / 4
Step 1 Identify the planes and their common line

The two planes are the plane containing \(\triangle AFG\) and the plane containing \(\triangle AFH\). Their intersection line is the common side \(AF\).

To measure the angle between two planes, we take a point on the intersection line and drop perpendiculars in each plane to that line. The angle between these two perpendiculars is the required angle.

Hint: Notice \(AF\) is a vertical edge of the cube and \(F\) lies on the line \(AF\), so \(F\) is a convenient point on the intersection line.
Step 2 Construct the perpendiculars at \(F\)

In the plane \(AFG\), the line through \(F\) perpendicular to \(AF\) is \(FG\) (since \(AEFB\) is a square face, \(FG\) lies in the top face and is perpendicular to the vertical edge \(AF\)).

In the plane \(AFH\), the line through \(F\) perpendicular to \(AF\) is \(FH\) (since \(EFGH\) is a square top face and \(FH\) is perpendicular to the vertical edge \(AF\) at \(F\)).

\[ AF \perp FG \quad\text{and}\quad AF \perp FH \]
Hint: This is the key observation — \(AF\) is perpendicular to the whole top face \(EFGH\), so \(AF \perp FG\) and \(AF \perp FH\).
Therefore \(\alpha = \angle GFH\), the angle in the top square face \(EFGH\).
Step 3 Evaluate \(\angle GFH\) in the square \(EFGH\)

\(EFGH\) is a square (a face of the cube). In a square, each diagonal bisects the right angles. Hence the diagonal \(FH\) bisects the \(90^\circ\) angle at \(F=\angle EFG\), giving

\[ \angle GFH = \tfrac12 \times 90^\circ = 45^\circ \]

Alternatively, in the right isosceles triangle \(FGH\) with \(FG = GH = 1\):

\[ \tan\angle GFH = \frac{GH}{FG} = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \angle GFH = 45^\circ \]
Hint: Both the properties of a square and a right-isosceles-triangle computation agree, so the result is reliable.
Step 4 Conclude the value of \(\alpha\)
\[ \alpha = \angle GFH = 45^\circ \]
\(\alpha = 45^\circ\)

PART (b) Finding \(\beta\) — angle between \(\triangle AFH\) and \(\triangle FGH\)

Move the slider to view the configuration from “almost front-on” to “almost edge-on”. The 90° relation persists.
Plane \(AFH\) contains the vertical edge \(AF\),
which is \(\perp\) to the top face \(EFGH\).
\(\beta =\) 90.0°
0 / 3
Step 1 Recognise \(AF \perp\) top face \(EFGH\)

In the cube \(AF\) is a vertical lateral edge. Since \(EFGH\) is the top face of the cube, the edge \(AF\) is perpendicular to the entire plane \(EFGH\).

Consequently, \(AF\) is perpendicular to every line in the top face through \(F\), in particular \(FG\) and \(FH\):

\[ AF \perp FG,\qquad AF \perp FH \]
Hint: This was already used in Part (a). It is the crucial geometric fact for both parts.
Step 2 Show plane \(AFH \perp\) plane \(EFGH\)

The plane \(AFH\) contains the line \(AF\). A plane is perpendicular to another plane if it contains a line perpendicular to that other plane. Since

\[ AF \perp \text{plane } EFGH, \]

and \(AF\) lies in the plane \(AFH\), we conclude

\[ \text{plane } AFH \perp \text{plane } EFGH. \]

But plane \(FGH\) is exactly the top face \(EFGH\). Hence

\[ \text{plane } AFH \perp \text{plane } FGH. \]
Hint: The “line in one plane perpendicular to the other plane” criterion is the standard test for perpendicular planes.
Step 3 Conclude \(\beta = 90^\circ\)

The angle between two perpendicular planes is a right angle:

\[ \beta = 90^\circ \]
\(\beta = 90^\circ\)

CHOICE Compare \(\alpha\), \(\beta\) and \(60^\circ\)

We have obtained:

\[ \alpha = 45^\circ,\qquad \beta = 90^\circ \]

Therefore:

\[ 45^\circ < 60^\circ < 90^\circ \] \[ \boxed{\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta} \]
Correct option: A   (\(\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta\))

Option A matches our computation exactly. Options B, C, D all contradict \(\alpha=45^\circ\) and/or \(\beta=90^\circ\).

SUMMARY Answers at a glance

  • \(\alpha\) = angle between \(\triangle AFG\) and \(\triangle AFH\) = \(\angle GFH\) = \(45^\circ\)
  • \(\beta\) = angle between \(\triangle AFH\) and \(\triangle FGH\) = \(90^\circ\) (perpendicular planes)
  • Since \(AF \perp\) plane \(EFGH\), both results follow from: \(AF \perp FG\), \(AF \perp FH\).
  • Comparison: \(\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta\)
  • Correct option: A
