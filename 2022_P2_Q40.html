Question 40 · Trigonometry 2022 MC #40

Question. In the figure, $ABCDEFGH$ is a cube. Let $\alpha$ be the angle between $\triangle AFG$ and $\triangle AFH$ while $\beta$ be the angle between $\triangle AFH$ and $\triangle FGH$. Which of the following is true?

  1. $\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta$
  2. $\alpha < \beta < 60^\circ$
  3. $60^\circ < \alpha < \beta$
  4. $60^\circ < \beta < \alpha$
Step 1 · Set up the cube

Take the cube with side length $1$. Place it with $A$ at the top-back-right, as in the figure. A convenient coordinate assignment is:

$$A=(1,1,1),\qquad F=(0,1,0),\qquad G=(0,0,0),\qquad H=(1,0,0).$$

Here $EFGH$ is the bottom square and $A$ is the vertex of the top square directly above $F$. The key geometric fact is that $AF$ is perpendicular to the whole base plane $EFGH$ (since $AF$ is vertical and $EFGH$ is horizontal).

Step 2 · Angle $\alpha$ between $\triangle AFG$ and $\triangle AFH$

Both triangles share the edge $AF$. The angle between two triangles is measured along the line perpendicular to their common edge, i.e. it is the angle between $FG$ and $FH$ in the base plane:

$$\alpha=\angle GFH.$$

$EFGH$ is a square, so $\angle GFH$ is half of $90^\circ$ (a diagonal splits a square's corner):

$$\alpha=\angle GFH=45^\circ.$$

Step 3 · Angle $\beta$ between $\triangle AFH$ and $\triangle FGH$

Now consider the plane $AFH$ and the base plane containing $\triangle FGH$ (which is the plane $EFGH$). Since $AF\perp$ plane $EFGH$, the plane $AFH$ contains a line perpendicular to plane $EFGH$.

A plane containing a line perpendicular to another plane is perpendicular to that plane:

$$\text{plane } AFH \perp \text{plane } EFGH \;(=\text{plane } FGH).$$

Hence the dihedral angle between $\triangle AFH$ and $\triangle FGH$ is a right angle:

$$\beta=90^\circ.$$

Step 4 · Compare and choose

We found $\alpha=45^\circ$ and $\beta=90^\circ$. Therefore

$$\alpha=45^\circ<60^\circ<90^\circ=\beta.$$

This matches option A.

Answer: A  —  $\alpha = 45^\circ$ and $\beta = 90^\circ$, so $\alpha < 60^\circ < \beta$.
