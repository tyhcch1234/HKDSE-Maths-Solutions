QQuestion

Let \(G\), \(H\), \(I\) and \(J\) be the centroid, the orthocentre, the in-centre and the circumcentre of \(\triangle PQR\) respectively. If \(\angle QPR = 136^\circ\), which of the following are true?
  1. \(G\) lies inside \(\triangle PQR\).
  2. \(I\) lies outside \(\triangle PQR\).
  3. \(G\), \(H\) and \(J\) are collinear.

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

aPart (a) — Analyse the type of triangle

Step 1
Since \(\angle QPR = 136^\circ > 90^\circ\), the triangle is obtuse-angled at \(P\). For the standard HKDSE isosceles model, the other two angles are equal: \[ \angle PQR = \angle PRQ = \frac{180^\circ - 136^\circ}{2} = 22^\circ. \]
Hint: An obtuse angle at a vertex means the orthocentre \(H\) lies outside the triangle, on the opposite side of the longest side.
Step 2
The centroid \(G\) is the intersection of the three medians. Every median lies inside the triangle, so \[ G \text{ always lies inside } \triangle PQR. \] This is true for any triangle, obtuse or not.
Hint: The centroid is the "average" of the vertices: \(G=\frac{P+Q+R}{3}\), always inside.
So statement I is true.
Step 3
The incentre \(I\) is the intersection of the three internal angle bisectors. Each bisector lies inside the triangle, so \[ I \text{ always lies inside } \triangle PQR. \]
Hint: Only the excentres lie outside. The incentre is always inside — it is the centre of the incircle, which is tangent to all three sides.
So statement II is false (it says \(I\) lies outside).

bPart (b) — Collinearity of \(G\), \(H\) and \(J\)

The centroid \(G\), orthocentre \(H\) and circumcentre \(J\) always lie on one straight line — the Euler line. Toggle the checkbox and drag the triangle to see the line update live.

0 / 3
Step 1
Use position vectors with any origin. Let the vertices be \(P, Q, R\).
\[ G = \frac{P+Q+R}{3} \]
Hint: The centroid is the average of the vertex position vectors — this works for any triangle, in any coordinate system.
Step 2
Take \(J\) as the origin (the circumcentre). Then the circumradius is constant: \[ |P| = |Q| = |R| = R_c. \]
The orthocentre satisfies \[ H = P + Q + R \quad (\text{with } J \text{ as origin}). \]
Hint: Why? The altitude from \(P\) is perpendicular to \(QR\). The vector \(Q+R\) satisfies \((Q+R)\cdot (Q-R) = |Q|^2 - |R|^2 = 0\), so \(Q+R\) is perpendicular to \(QR\) — i.e. the altitude from \(P\) passes through \(P+Q+R\). By symmetry, all three altitudes meet at \(P+Q+R\).
Step 3
With \(J\) as origin: \[ H = P+Q+R, \qquad G = \frac{P+Q+R}{3} = \frac{H}{3}. \] Both \(G\) and \(H\) are scalar multiples of \(H\) from \(J\), so \[ \overrightarrow{JG} = \frac{1}{3}\overrightarrow{JH} \implies HJ : JG = 3 : 1. \] Hence \(G\), \(H\), \(J\) are collinear, on the Euler line.
So statement III is true.

ΣFinal Answer at a Glance

  • I. \(G\) lies inside \(\triangle PQR\) — TRUE (centroid always inside)
  • II. \(I\) lies outside \(\triangle PQR\) — FALSE (incentre always inside)
  • III. \(G\), \(H\), \(J\) are collinear — TRUE (Euler line, \(HG:GJ = 2:1\))
  • Correct option: B. I and III only
