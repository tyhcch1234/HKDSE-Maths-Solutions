Circle $C$ Line $x-y+k=0$ Centre $(-1,2)$ Intersection points Radius $=3\sqrt2$

Question

Find the range of values of $k$ such that the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-4y-13=0$ and the straight line $x-y+k=0$ intersect at two distinct points.

A. $-9
B. $-3
C. $k<-9$ or $k>3$
D. $k<-3$ or $k>9$
Step 1 · Find the circle's centre & radius

Complete the square for the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-4y-13=0$:

$$(x+1)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=13+1+4=18$$

So centre $=(-1,\,2)$ and radius $r=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt{2}\approx 4.24$.

The diagram shows this circle in blue with its centre marked in purple.
Step 2 · Make the line the subject

From $x-y+k=0$ we get $y=x+k$: a family of lines with slope $1$ whose intercept is $k$.

Varying $k$ slides this line up and down (drag the slider on the left to see it move).

Step 3 · Substitute into the circle

Put $y=x+k$ into $x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-4y-13=0$:

$$x^{2}+(x+k)^{2}+2x-4(x+k)-13=0$$

$$2x^{2}+2(k-1)x+(k^{2}-4k-13)=0\quad\cdots(*)$$

Step 4 · Two distinct points ⟺ Δ > 0

A quadratic has two distinct real roots exactly when its discriminant is positive:

$$\Delta=[2(k-1)]^{2}-4(2)\bigl(k^{2}-4k-13\bigr)>0$$

$$4(k^{2}-2k+1)-8(k^{2}-4k-13)>0$$

Step 5 · Simplify & factorise

$$-4k^{2}+24k+108>0$$

Divide by $-4$ (reverse the inequality):

$$k^{2}-6k-27<0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (k+3)(k-9)<0$$
