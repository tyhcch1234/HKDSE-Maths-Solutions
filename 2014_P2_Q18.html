[2014MC#18]  ·  13. Trigonometry

In the figure, $\angle ABD = \angle ADC = \angle BCD = 90^\circ$. If $AB = \ell$, then $CD =$

  • A. $\ \ell\sin\theta$
  • B. $\ \ell\cos\theta$
  • C. $\ \ell\sin\theta\tan\theta$
  • D. $\ \dfrac{\ell\tan\theta}{\cos\theta}$

Step-by-step solution

Step 1 · Right triangle ABD

Triangle $ABD$ is right-angled at $B$. With the known side $AB=\ell$ and the angle at $A$ equal to $\theta$, we find the common side $BD$:

\[ \tan\theta=\frac{BD}{AB}=\frac{BD}{\ell} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{BD=\ell\tan\theta} \]
This is the key linking side — both right triangles share it.
Tangent Ratio \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\) In the right triangle \(ABD\), \(BD\) is opposite \(\theta\) and \(AB=\ell\) is adjacent to it.
Step 2 · Angle sum in ABD and ADC

In $\triangle ABD$ (right angle at $B$):

\[ \angle ADB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \theta = 90^\circ - \theta \]

In $\triangle ADC$ the angle at $D$ is $90^\circ$, and the ray $DB$ splits it:

\[ \angle BDC = \angle ADC - \angle ADB = 90^\circ - (90^\circ-\theta) = \theta \]
Step 3 · Right triangle BCD

Now $\triangle BCD$ is right-angled at $C$ with the hypotenuse $BD$ and angle $\angle BDC=\theta$. Resolve the side $CD$ along $BD$:

\[ \cos\angle BDC=\frac{CD}{BD} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad CD = BD\cos\theta = (\ell\tan\theta)\cos\theta \]
Cosine Ratio \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In the right triangle \(BCD\) the angle \(\angle BDC=\theta\) has adjacent side \(CD\) and hypotenuse \(BD\).
Step 4 · Simplify

Use $\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$:

\[ CD=\ell\cdot\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\cdot\cos\theta=\ell\sin\theta \]
Quotient Identity \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\) Replacing \(\tan\theta\) in \(CD=\ell\tan\theta\cos\theta\) lets the \(\cos\theta\) cancel, leaving \(\ell\sin\theta\).
Step 5 · Answer

Comparing with the choices, the answer is A.

FINAL ANSWER
$CD=\ell\sin\theta$  (Option A)
Memory tip: the two right triangles “share” the hypotenuse of one as the hypotenuse of the other, so $CD = (\ell\tan\theta)\cos\theta = \ell\sin\theta$.
