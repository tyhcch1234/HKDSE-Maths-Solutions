Question

Let \(k\) be a constant. Solve the equation

\[ x^2 + 4x = k^2 - 2k - 3 \]
A. \(x = k - 3\) or \(x = -k - 1\)
B. \(x = k - 3\) or \(x = k + 1\)
C. \(x = k + 3\) or \(x = -k + 1\)
D. \(x = k + 3\) or \(x = k - 1\)

1 Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Step 1

Rearrange into standard quadratic form

Move all terms to one side so the equation looks like \(ax^2+bx+c=0\):

\[ x^2 + 4x - (k^2 - 2k - 3) = 0 \] \[ x^2 + 4x - k^2 + 2k + 3 = 0 \]
💡 Hint: The coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(1\), which makes the cross method convenient. The constant term is \(-(k^2 - 2k - 3)\).

Step 2

Factorise the constant term

Notice that \(k^2 - 2k - 3\) can itself be factorised:

\[ k^2 - 2k - 3 = (k - 3)(k + 1) \]

Check: \((k-3)(k+1) = k^2 + k - 3k - 3 = k^2 - 2k - 3\) ✓

Step 3

Apply the cross method

The equation becomes \(x^2 + 4x - (k-3)(k+1) = 0\). We split the constant term into two factors \((k+1)\) and \(-(k-3)\) so that their sum gives the middle coefficient \(4\):

\((k+1) + [-(k-3)] = k+1-k+3 = 4\) ✓

x k + 1 x −(k − 3)
\[ (x + (k+1))\,(x - (k-3)) = 0 \] \[ [\,x + (k+1)\,]\,[\,x - (k-3)\,] = 0 \]
💡 Hint: A quadratic of the form \(x^2 + bx + c\) factorises as \((x + p)(x + q)\) where \(p+q=b\) and \(pq=c\).

Step 4

Solve for x

For a product to equal zero, at least one factor must be zero:

\[ x + (k+1) = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x - (k-3) = 0 \] \[ \boxed{x = -k - 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = k - 3} \]

✅ Therefore the answer is A.

Alternative

Quadratic Formula

💡 Hint (Formula): \(x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\)  with \(a = 1,\; b = 4,\; c = -(k^2 - 2k - 3)\).
\[ x = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4^2 - 4(1)(-(k^2-2k-3))}}{2(1)} \] \[ = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 4k^2 - 8k - 12}}{2} \] \[ = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4k^2 - 8k + 4}}{2} \] \[ = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4(k^2 - 2k + 1)}}{2} \] \[ = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4(k-1)^2}}{2} \] \[ = \frac{-4 \pm 2(k-1)}{2} \] \[ = -2 \pm (k-1) \]

Case \(+\):  \(x = -2 + k - 1 = k - 3\)

Case \(-\):  \(x = -2 - (k - 1) = -k - 1\)

Same answer: \(x = k - 3\) or \(x = -k - 1\) → Option A ✓
