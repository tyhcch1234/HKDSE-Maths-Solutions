HKDSE 2023 · Paper 2 · Q4

Factorise

$$2x^2+xy-y^2+4x+4y =$$
A.   $(x+y)(2x+y-4)$
B.   $(x+y)(2x-y+4)$
C.   $(x-y)(2x+y-4)$
D.   $(x-y)(2x-y+4)$

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 — Write down the expression & look for groups $$2x^2+xy-y^2+4x+4y$$
Hint: split into the quadratic part $(2x^2+xy-y^2)$ and the linear part $(4x+4y)$.
Step 2 — Factorise the quadratic part We look for two numbers multiplying to $2\times(-1)=-2$ and adding to $+1$.
Cross Method \(acx^{2}+(ad+bc)xy+bdy^{2}=(ax+by)(cx+dy)\) For \(2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}\) the pair \(2\) and \(-1\) splits the middle term into \(2xy-xy\).
These are $+2$ and $-1$: $$2x^2+xy-y^2 = 2x^2+2xy-xy-y^2 = 2x(x+y)-y(x+y)=(x+y)(2x-y)$$
Hint: the quadratic part is a homogeneous quadratic — factor it by splitting the middle term.
Step 3 — Factorise the linear part $$4x+4y = 4(x+y)$$
Hint: a common factor of $4$ can be taken out of the last two terms.
Step 4 — Spot the common factor $(x+y)$ Combining Steps 2 and 3: $$2x^2+xy-y^2+4x+4y=(x+y)(2x-y)+4(x+y)$$
Hint: a common bracket $(x+y)$ now appears in both terms — this is the key grouping trick.
Step 5 — Take out the common factor
Common Factor Law \(AB+AC=A(B+C)\) After grouping, both terms contain the bracket \((x+y)\), which is taken out.
$$=(x+y)\big[(2x-y)+4\big]=(x+y)(2x-y+4)$$
Hint: factor out $(x+y)$ and simplify the remaining bracket.
Step 6 — Check the answer Expand to verify: $$(x+y)(2x-y+4)=2x^2-xy+4x+2xy-y^2+4y=2x^2+xy-y^2+4x+4y \;\checkmark$$
Hint: expanding always confirms the factorisation is correct.
Answer: B  —  $(x+y)(2x-y+4)$

Question. Factorise \(3x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}+2x+2y\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Split the expression into \(3x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\) and \(2x+2y\).
Step 2. The quadratic part factorises as \(3x^{2}+3xy-2xy-2y^{2}=3x(x+y)-2y(x+y)=(x+y)(3x-2y)\).
Step 3. The linear part is \(2x+2y=2(x+y)\).
Step 4. So the expression is \((x+y)(3x-2y)+2(x+y)=(x+y)(3x-2y+2)\).
Step 5. Check: \((x+y)(3x-2y+2)=3x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}+2x+2y\) ✓
Answer: \((x+y)(3x-2y+2)\)
