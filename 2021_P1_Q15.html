A queue is randomly formed by 7 teachers and 3 students.

  1. How many different queues can be formed?  [1 mark]
  2. Find the probability that no students are next to each other in the queue.  [3 marks]
STEP 1

Understand the situation

There are $7$ teachers (T) and $3$ students (S) — all $10$ people are distinct individuals. A "queue" means an arrangement in a line, so order matters.
Hint: "randomly formed queue" → every permutation of the 10 people is equally likely.
STEP 2

(a) Count all possible queues

Arrange all $10$ distinct people in a row: \[ P_{10}^{10}=10!=3\,628\,800 \]
1 mark: state $10!$ (or $P_{10}^{10}$) and the value $3\,628\,800$.
STEP 3

(b) Start from the teachers

To keep students apart, first arrange the $7$ teachers: \[ 7! \] These $7$ teachers create $7+1=8$ gaps (including the two ends) where students may stand: \[\_\;T\;\_\;T\;\_\;T\;\_\;T\;\_\;T\;\_\;T\;\_\]
Hint: with $n$ objects in a row there are $n+1$ gaps — ends count too.
STEP 4

Choose gaps for the students

Choose $3$ of the $8$ gaps and place the $3$ students there (one per gap, so they are never adjacent): \[ C_3^{8}\times 3! \] Hence the number of favourable queues is \[ 7!\;C_3^{8}\;3!=5040\times 56\times 6=1\,693\,440 \]
1M for $7!$, 1M for $C_3^{8}3!$ (choosing 3 gaps × ordering the students).
STEP 5

Form the probability

\[ \frac{7!\,C_3^{8}\,3!}{3\,628\,800} =\frac{1\,693\,440}{3\,628\,800} =\frac{7}{15} \]
1A: must give the fraction in lowest terms, $\dfrac{7}{15}$ (≈ 0.4667).

Answers:

(a)  $10!=3\,628\,800$ different queues.

(b)  $P(\text{no two students adjacent})=\dfrac{7!\,C_3^{8}\,3!}{10!}=\dfrac{7}{15}$.
