Arithmetic Sequence & Logarithms

Question

Let \( A(n) \) be the \( n \)th term of an arithmetic sequence. It is given that \( A(5)=26 \) and \( A(12)=61 \).

  1. Find \( A(1) \). (2 marks)
  2. Suppose that \( \log_{2}G(n)=A(n) \) for any positive integer \( n \). Find the greatest value of \( k \) such that \( \log_{8}\!\big(G(1)G(2)G(3)\cdots G(k)\big) < 999 \). (5 marks)

Solution

STEP 1 · Part (a) — find the common difference
nth Term of an Arithmetic Sequence \(A(n)=A(1)+(n-1)d\) \(A(12)-A(5)=7d=35\), so \(d=5\).
If \( d \) is the common difference, \[ A(12)-A(5) = 7d = 61-26 = 35 \implies d = 5. \]
STEP 2 · Part (a) — the first term \[ A(1) = A(5)-4d = 26-20 = 6. \] Hence \( A(n) = 6+5(n-1) = 5n+1 \).
STEP 3 · Express \( G(n) \) From \( \log_{2}G(n)=A(n)=5n+1 \), \[ G(n) = 2^{5n+1}. \]
STEP 4 · Part (b) — the product
Sum of an Arithmetic Sequence \(\sum_{n=1}^{k}(5n+1)=\dfrac{k}{2}\left[2(6)+5(k-1)\right]=\dfrac{k(5k+7)}{2}\) The indices \(A(n)=5n+1\) form an arithmetic sequence with first term \(6\) and common difference \(5\).
\[ G(1)G(2)\cdots G(k) = 2^{\sum_{n=1}^{k}(5n+1)} = 2^{\frac{5k(k+1)}{2}+k} = 2^{\frac{k(5k+7)}{2}}. \]
STEP 5 · Apply the logarithm
Change of Base of Logarithms \(\log_{a^{m}}x=\dfrac{1}{m}\log_{a}x\) Since \(8=2^{3}\), \(\log_{8}x=\dfrac{1}{3}\log_{2}x\).
Since \( \log_{8}x = \tfrac{1}{3}\log_{2}x \), \[ \log_{8}\!\big(G(1)\cdots G(k)\big) = \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{k(5k+7)}{2} = \frac{k(5k+7)}{6}. \] The condition becomes \[ \frac{k(5k+7)}{6} < 999 \implies 5k^{2}+7k-5994 < 0. \]
STEP 6 · Solve the quadratic inequality The positive root of \( 5k^{2}+7k-5994=0 \) is \[ k = \frac{-7+\sqrt{49+119880}}{10} = \frac{-7+\sqrt{119929}}{10} \approx \frac{-7+346.31}{10} \approx 33.93. \] So \( k \le 33.93 \), and the greatest integer value is \( k=33 \).
STEP 7 · Check \[ k=33:\ \frac{33\times172}{6} = 946 < 999 \ \checkmark \qquad k=34:\ \frac{34\times177}{6} = 1003 > 999 \ \times \]
Final Answer: (a) \( A(1) = 6 \)  |  (b) \( k = 33 \)

Question. It is given that \(A(n)\) is the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence, \(A(3)=20\), \(A(9)=74\), and \(\log_{2}G(n)=A(n)\) for all positive integers \(n\).  [7 marks]
(a) Find \(A(1)\) and the common difference of the sequence.
(b) Find the greatest integer \(k\) such that \(\log_{8}\big(G(1)G(2)\cdots G(k)\big)<999\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(A(9)-A(3)=6d=74-20=54\), so \(d=9\). Then \(A(1)=A(3)-2d=20-18=2\).
Step 2. \(A(n)=2+9(n-1)=9n-7\), so \(\log_{2}G(n)=9n-7\) and \(G(n)=2^{9n-7}\).
Step 3. \(\log_{2}\big(G(1)\cdots G(k)\big)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}(9n-7)=\dfrac{k}{2}\left[2(2)+9(k-1)\right]=\dfrac{9k^{2}-5k}{2}\).
Step 4. \(\log_{8}\big(G(1)\cdots G(k)\big)=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{9k^{2}-5k}{2}=\dfrac{9k^{2}-5k}{6}<999\), so \(9k^{2}-5k-5994<0\).
Step 5. The positive root is \(\dfrac{5+\sqrt{215\,809}}{18}\approx26.09\), so \(k\le26\). Check: \(k=26\) gives \(992.3<999\) and \(k=27\) gives \(1071>999\).
Answer: (a) \(A(1)=2\), \(d=9\)  (b) \(k=26\)
