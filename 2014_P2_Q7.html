Question. The solution of $\dfrac{x-3}{x} > 0$ is
  • A.   $x > 0$
  • B.   $x > 3$
  • C.   $x < 0$ or $x > 3$
  • D.   $0 < x < 3$
1
Identify the critical points The expression $\dfrac{x-3}{x}$ can only change sign where the numerator or the denominator is zero.
Numerator $=0 \Rightarrow x=3$;   Denominator $=0 \Rightarrow x=0$ (this value is not allowed).
2
Mark the points on a number line The two critical points $x=0$ and $x=3$ split the real line into three intervals: \[ (-\infty,\,0),\quad (0,\,3),\quad (3,\,\infty) \]
Hollow dots at $0$ and $3$: both are excluded (0 makes the denominator zero, 3 makes the expression 0, not $>0$).
Critical Values of a Rational Expression \(\dfrac{A}{B}=0\iff A=0\quad (B\ne 0)\) So \(\dfrac{x-3}{x}\) can change sign only at \(x=3\) (numerator zero) and \(x=0\) (not allowed).
3
Test the sign in each interval

A “$+$ over $-$” or “$-$ over $+$” gives a negative value — only the same-sign rows give $+$.
4
Select the intervals where the expression is positive From the table, $\dfrac{x-3}{x}>0$ is satisfied on \[ x<0 \quad \text{or} \quad x>3. \]
Both critical points are excluded, so the solution is a strictly open union of intervals.
Sign of a Quotient \(\dfrac{A}{B}>0\iff AB>0\quad (B\ne 0)\) Here \((x-3)x>0\) exactly when the two factors have the same sign, i.e. \(x<0\) or \(x>3\).
5
Match with the options Comparing with the choices, $x<0$ or $x>3$ is exactly option C.
Quick sanity check: $x=-1$ works, $x=1$ fails, $x=4$ works. ✔
Answer: C  —  $x<0$ or $x>3$  (both critical points excluded)
