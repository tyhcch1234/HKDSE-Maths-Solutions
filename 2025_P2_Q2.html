Step 1 — the given expression $36-(3m+4n)^2$ is a difference of two squares.

HKDSE 2025 · Paper 2 · Q2

Topic 4 · Polynomials

Question   [2025MC#2]

$36-(3m+4n)^2=\;?$

  • A. $(6+3m+4n)(6-3m+4n)$
  • B. $(6+3m+4n)(6-3m-4n)$
  • C. $(6+3m-4n)(6-3m+4n)$
  • D. $(6+3m-4n)(6-3m-4n)$

Step-by-step solution

1Recognise the difference of two squares

Write 36 as a square and identify the pattern $A^2-B^2$:

$$36-(3m+4n)^2 \;=\; 6^{2}-(3m+4n)^{2}$$

Hint: a difference of two squares has the form (something)² − (something)² . Here $A=6$ and $B=3m+4n$.
2Apply the identity $A^2-B^2=(A+B)(A-B)$

Substitute $A=6$, $B=3m+4n$:

$$6^{2}-(3m+4n)^{2} \;=\; \big[\,6+(3m+4n)\,\big]\big[\,6-(3m+4n)\,\big]$$

Hint: this identity works for any expressions A and B — they do not need to be single terms.
Difference of Two Squares \(A^{2}-B^{2}=(A+B)(A-B)\) Taking \(A=6\) and \(B=3m+4n\) turns the expression into a product of two brackets.
3Remove the inner brackets carefully

The first bracket is a sum; in the second bracket the minus sign multiplies both $3m$ and $4n$:

$$=\;(6+3m+4n)\,(6-3m-4n)$$

Hint: the common trap is $6-(3m+4n)=6-3m+4n$ — wrong! It must be $6-3m-4n$.
Removing Brackets with a Negative Sign \(-(a+b)=-a-b\) \(6-(3m+4n)=6-3m-4n\), so both signs inside the bracket change.
4Check by expanding

Expand the answer to confirm it returns to the original expression:

$$(6+3m+4n)(6-3m-4n)=36-(3m+4n)^2 \;\checkmark$$

Hint: quick check — compare each option's second factor. Only option B gives $6-3m-4n$.
Answer: B  —  $(6+3m+4n)(6-3m-4n)$
