Question

In the figure, $ABCD$ is a parallelogram. $E$ and $F$ are points lying on $AB$ and $CD$ respectively. $AD$ produced meet at $G$. It is given that $DF:FC = 3:4$ and $AD:DG = 1:1$. If the area of $\triangle DFG$ is $3\text{ cm}^2$, then the area of the parallelogram $ABCD$ is
A. $12\text{ cm}^2$
B. $14\text{ cm}^2$
C. $18\text{ cm}^2$
D. $21\text{ cm}^2$
STEP 1 · Link △ADF and △DFG

$\triangle ADF$ and $\triangle DFG$ share the same height (measured from line $AG$ to $D$),
so their areas are in the ratio of their bases along $AG$,
i.e. $AD:DG$.

$$\frac{\text{area of }\triangle ADF}{\text{area of }\triangle DFG}=\frac{AD}{DG}=\frac{1}{1}$$

Hence $$\text{area of }\triangle ADF=\text{area of }\triangle DFG=3\text{ cm}^2 \qquad (1)$$

Tip: triangles with a common vertex and collinear bases — compare base lengths.
STEP 2 · Link △ACD and △ADF
Triangles on a Common Base \(\dfrac{\text{area of }\triangle ADF}{\text{area of }\triangle DFG}=\dfrac{AD}{DG}\) Both triangles share the same height from \(F\), so areas follow the bases.

$\triangle ACD$ and $\triangle ADF$ share the same height (from $A$ down to line $DC$),
so their areas are in the ratio of their bases $CD$ and $DF$ along the line $DC$.

$$\frac{\text{area of }\triangle ACD}{\text{area of }\triangle ADF}=\frac{CD}{DF} =\frac{DF+FC}{DF}=1+\frac{FC}{DF}=1+\frac{4}{3}=\frac{7}{3}$$

Therefore $$\text{area of }\triangle ACD=\frac{7}{3}\times\text{area of }\triangle ADF \qquad (2)$$

STEP 3 · Double for the parallelogram
Area Ratio with a Common Vertex \(\dfrac{\text{area of }\triangle ADF}{\text{area of }\triangle ACF}=\dfrac{DF}{FC}\) Sharing vertex \(A\), the areas are in the ratio \(3:4\).

Parallelogram $ABCD$ and $\triangle ACD$ share the same base $CD$ and the same heights,
so the parallelogram is twice the triangle.

$$\text{area of }ABCD = 2\times \text{area of }\triangle ACD \qquad (3)$$
Why? A diagonal splits a parallelogram into two congruent triangles.
STEP 4 · Combine and compute
Area of a Parallelogram \([\text{parallelogram}]=2\times\text{area of }\triangle ACD\) A diagonal halves the parallelogram, so \(ABCD=2 imes 7\).

Substituting (1) and (2) into (3):

$$\text{area of }ABCD = 2\times\frac{7}{3}\times\text{area of }\triangle ADF$$ $$=2\times\frac{7}{3}\times 3 = 14\text{ cm}^2$$

So the area of the parallelogram is $14\text{ cm}^2$.

Answer
B. $14\text{ cm}^2$

Question. In parallelogram \(ABCD\), \(E\) lies on \(AD\) with \(AE:ED=2:1\) and \(F\) lies on \(DC\) with \(DF:FC=1:1\). If the area of \(\triangle DEF\) is \(4\text{ cm}^{2}\), find the area of \(ABCD\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\triangle DEF\) and \(\triangle AEF\) share height from \(F\), so \(\dfrac{\text{area of }AEF}{\text{area of }DEF}=\dfrac{AE}{ED}=\dfrac{2}{1}\), giving \(\text{area of }AEF=8\).
Step 2. Then \(\text{area of }ADF=8+4=12\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Step 3. \(\triangle ADF\) and \(\triangle ACF\) share height from \(A\), so \(\text{area of }ACF=12\times\dfrac{FC}{DF}=12\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Step 4. \(\text{area of }ACD=12+12=24\text{ cm}^{2}\), and the parallelogram is twice that: \(ABCD=48\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Answer: \(48\text{ cm}^{2}\)
