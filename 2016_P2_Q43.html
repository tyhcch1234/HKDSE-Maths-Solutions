Question [2016MC#43]

There are 20 boys and 15 girls in a class. If 6 students are selected from the class to form a committee consisting of at most 2 girls, how many different committees can be formed?

💡 Key Concept: Combination Formula

When choosing $r$ items from $n$ items where order does not matter, we use the combination formula:

$$C_r^n = C^{n}_{r} = \frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$$

\(C_r^n\) counts the number of ways to choose a group of \(r\) objects from \(n\) distinct objects. The order of selection inside the committee is irrelevant.

📌 "At most 2 girls" means:

The committee can have 0 girls, 1 girl, or 2 girls. We must count each case separately and add them together.

Since the committee must have exactly 6 members, the number of boys is 6 − (number of girls).

⚠️ Common mistake: Don't forget the "0 girls" case! "At most 2" includes 0, 1, and 2.

We select 6 boys from the 20 boys, and 0 girls from the 15 girls.

$$C^{20}_{6} \times C^{15}_{0} = 38\,760 \times 1 = 38\,760$$

Recall: \(C^{n}_{0} = 1\) for any \(n\).

We select 5 boys from the 20 boys, and 1 girl from the 15 girls.

$$C^{20}_{5} \times C^{15}_{1} = 15\,504 \times 15 = 232\,560$$

\(C^{20}_{5} = 15\,504\) and \(C^{15}_{1} = 15\).

We select 4 boys from the 20 boys, and 2 girls from the 15 girls.

$$C^{20}_{4} \times C^{15}_{2} = 4\,845 \times 105 = 508\,725$$

\(C^{20}_{4} = 4\,845\) and \(C^{15}_{2} = 105\).

Since the three cases are mutually exclusive, we add them:

$$C^{20}_{6} + C^{20}_{5}C^{15}_{1} + C^{20}_{4}C^{15}_{2}$$ $$= 38\,760 + 232\,560 + 508\,725$$ $$= \mathbf{780\,045}$$

Answer

D. 780 045

🎯 Exam Tip

  • ▸ Whenever you see "at most" or "at least", break the problem into separate cases and add them.
  • ▸ Check that the total number of people in each case is always the same (here: 6).
  • ▸ Use the complement method as a check: total committees minus committees with 3, 4, 5, or 6 girls.
