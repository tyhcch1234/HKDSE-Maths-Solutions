2016 P2 Q4314. Probability / Counting

There are 20 boys and 15 girls in a class. If 6 students are selected from the class to form a committee consisting of at most 2 girls, how many different committees can be formed?

  • A. 271 320
  • B. 324 415
  • C. 508 725
  • D. 780 045

Step-by-step solution

1 Interpret "at most 2 girls" A committee of 6 with at most 2 girls means the number of girls $g$ can be $$g = 0,\;1,\;\text{or }2.$$ Each case is counted separately, then added (mutually exclusive cases). Hint: "at most" = ≤, so list the allowed numbers of girls.
2 Case g = 0: all 6 are boys Choose 6 boys from 20, no girls: $$C^{20}_{6} = \binom{20}{6} = 38\,760.$$ Hint: $\binom{n}{r}$ counts unordered selections of $r$ objects from $n$.
3 Case g = 1: 5 boys + 1 girl Choose 5 boys from 20 and 1 girl from 15: $$C^{20}_{5}\cdot C^{15}_{1} = 15\,504 \times 15 = 232\,560.$$ Hint: multiply when the choices are made together (product rule).
4 Case g = 2: 4 boys + 2 girls Choose 4 boys from 20 and 2 girls from 15: $$C^{20}_{4}\cdot C^{15}_{2} = 4\,845 \times 105 = 508\,725.$$ Hint: $\binom{15}{2}=\frac{15\times14}{2}=105$.
5 Add the three cases $$\binom{20}{6}+\binom{20}{5}\binom{15}{1}+\binom{20}{4}\binom{15}{2}$$ $$= 38\,760 + 232\,560 + 508\,725 = 780\,045.$$ Hint: "or" in counting means adding the disjoint cases.
Answer: D. 780 045 $\binom{20}{6}+\binom{20}{5}\binom{15}{1}+\binom{20}{4}\binom{15}{2} = 780\,045$
