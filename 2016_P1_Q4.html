Topic 4: Polynomials — Factorization

Polynomials Factorization Common Factor Cross Method

Question

Factorize

(a)   \(5m - 10n\),

(b)   \(m^2 + mn - 6n^2\),

(c)   \(m^2 + mn - 6n^2 - 5m + 10n\).   [4 marks]

💡 Key Insight: Part (c) is designed so that parts (a) and (b) can be reused. Notice that \(5m - 10n = 5(m-2n)\) and \(m^2+mn-6n^2 = (m+3n)(m-2n)\). The common factor \((m-2n)\) appears in both pieces — that is the whole trick!

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Hint: Look for the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of the coefficients \(5\) and \(10\). Both are divisible by \(5\). Also check if any variable \(m\) or \(n\) is common — here there is none.

Factor out \(5\) from both terms:

\[ 5m - 10n = 5(m - 2n) \]

✓ This matches answer (a) — 1 mark (1A).

Hint (Cross Method): This is a quadratic in \(m\) with coefficients involving \(n\).
We want \((m + \_)(m + \_n)\) such that the product of the two "\(n\)" numbers is \(-6\) and their sum is \(+1\).
Numbers: \(+3\) and \(-2\) (since \(3 \times -2 = -6\) and \(3 + (-2) = 1\)).

Apply the cross method:

\[ m^2 + mn - 6n^2 = (m + 3n)(m - 2n) \]

✓ This matches answer (b) — 1 mark (1A).

Hint: Split part (c) into two groups that match (a) and (b).
\(m^2 + mn - 6n^2 \;\; \underbrace{-\;5m + 10n}_{= -(5m-10n)}\)

Notice that the last two terms are the negative of part (a):

\[ m^2 + mn - 6n^2 - 5m + 10n = \underbrace{(m^2 + mn - 6n^2)}_{\text{part (b)}} - \underbrace{(5m - 10n)}_{\text{part (a)}} \]

This rearrangement is the key trick to reveal the common factor.

Replace each bracket with its factorized form:

\[ = (m + 3n)(m - 2n) - 5(m - 2n) \]

🎯 Now \((m - 2n)\) appears in both terms — this is the common factor! Awarded 1M (method mark) for this step.

Hint: Treat \((m-2n)\) as a single block. It is like \(X \cdot (m+3n) - 5X = X \cdot (m+3n-5)\).

Take out the common factor \((m - 2n)\):

\[ = (m - 2n)\big[(m + 3n) - 5\big] \] \[ = (m - 2n)(m + 3n - 5) \]

✓ Final answer for (c) — 1 mark (1A).

✅ Final Answers

(a)   \(5(m - 2n)\)

(b)   \((m + 3n)(m - 2n)\)

(c)   \((m - 2n)(m + 3n - 5)\)

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Always tackle (a) and (b) first — they are stepping stones for (c).
  • Look out for the common factor after factorizing each part. In (c) both parts shared \((m-2n)\).
  • For the cross method, remember: product of the two numbers = constant term, sum = coefficient of the middle term.
  • Factoring \(-1\) out of a group (like \(-5m + 10n = -(5m-10n)\)) is a powerful way to reveal hidden common factors.
