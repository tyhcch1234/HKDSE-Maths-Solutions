Mensuration — arc length & area of a sector: find the angle

Sector Area Arc Length Simultaneous Equations Radians vs Degrees

Q Question

If the arc length and the area of a sector are \(8\pi\text{ cm}\) and \(80\pi\text{ cm}^2\) respectively, then the angle of the sector is

A. \(36^\circ\) B. \(45^\circ\) C. \(60^\circ\) D. \(72^\circ\)

A sector of a circle has two given measurements: the curved arc length and the enclosed area. Both depend on the radius \(r\) and the angle \(\theta\) — so we can form two equations and solve them.

(Multiple-choice question — full working shown below.)

1 Set up the two equations

Introduce the two unknowns and write down the standard sector formulae.

72°
10 cm
\(r = \) 10 cm
\(\theta = \) 72°
\(L = 2\pi r\frac{\theta}{360} =\) 4.0π cm
\(A = \pi r^2\frac{\theta}{360} =\) 20.0π cm²
Step 1 Name the unknowns

Let \(r\text{ cm}\) be the radius and \(\theta^\circ\) be the angle of the sector.

Hint: There are two unknown quantities but we have two pieces of given information — so two equations will determine them uniquely.
Step 2 Equation for the area

Area of a sector \(= \pi r^2 \times \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\). Given area \(= 80\pi\text{ cm}^2\):

\[ \pi r^2 \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = 80\pi \quad\cdots(1)\]
Hint: Divide both sides by \(\pi\) to simplify immediately: \(r^2\theta = 80 \times 360\).
Step 3 Equation for the arc length

Arc length \(= 2\pi r \times \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\). Given arc length \(= 8\pi\text{ cm}\):

\[ 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} = 8\pi \quad\cdots(2)\]
Hint: The same factor \(\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\) appears in both formulae — this is the key to solving quickly by division.
Answer: The system is \(\;\pi r^2\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}=80\pi\;\) and \(\;2\pi r\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}=8\pi\).

2 Solve the system for \(r\) and \(\theta\)

Divide equation (1) by equation (2) — the \(\theta\) cancels and only \(r\) remains.

From (1) ÷ (2): \(\dfrac{\pi r^2\theta/360}{2\pi r\,\theta/360}=\dfrac{80\pi}{8\pi}\)
\(\Rightarrow \dfrac{r}{2}=10 \Rightarrow r=20\) cm
Step 1 Divide (1) by (2)

\[ \frac{\pi r^2 \cdot \frac{\theta}{360}}{2\pi r \cdot \frac{\theta}{360}} = \frac{80\pi}{8\pi} \]

The factor \(\pi\) and \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\) cancel completely:

\[ \frac{r}{2} = 10 \]
Hint: Dividing removes \(\theta\) entirely — a neat trick when both the area and the arc length are known. This ratio \(\dfrac{A}{L}=\dfrac{r}{2}\) is worth remembering.
Step 2 Obtain \(r\)

\[ r = 20\text{ cm} \]

Hint: For any sector, area ÷ arc length always equals \(\dfrac{r}{2}\), independent of the angle. Here \(80\pi \div 8\pi = 10\), so \(r=20\).
Answer: \(r = 20\text{ cm}\).
Step 3 Substitute back to find \(\theta\)

Put \(r=20\) into equation (2):

\[ 2\pi(20)\times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}=8\pi \] \[ 40\pi \times \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}=8\pi \] \[ \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}=\frac{8\pi}{40\pi}=\frac{1}{5} \]
Hint: You may equally use equation (1) with \(r=20\): \(\pi(400)\frac{\theta}{360}=80\pi\) gives the same \(\frac{\theta}{360}=\frac15\).
Step 4 Compute the angle

\[ \theta = \frac{1}{5}\times 360^\circ = 72^\circ \]

Hint: Check: with \(r=20,\ \theta=72^\circ\), arc \(=2\pi(20)\cdot\frac15=8\pi\) ✓ and area \(=\pi(400)\cdot\frac15=80\pi\) ✓
Answer: \(\theta = 72^\circ\) — option D.

3 Verify: test each option against the data

With \(r=20\) fixed, only one of the four options reproduces both the given arc length and the given area. Move the slider to sweep through the options and watch the checkmarks.

72°
Arc length \(= 2\pi(20)\frac{\theta}{360}=\) 8.0π cm
Area \(= \pi(20)^2\frac{\theta}{360}=\) 80.0π cm²
✓ Both match — correct!
Step 1 Fix \(r=20\) and test A–D

Using \(L=2\pi(20)\frac{\theta}{360}=\frac{40\pi\theta}{360}=\frac{\pi\theta}{9}\):

  • \(\theta=36^\circ \Rightarrow L=4\pi \ne 8\pi\) ✗
  • \(\theta=45^\circ \Rightarrow L=5\pi \ne 8\pi\) ✗
  • \(\theta=60^\circ \Rightarrow L=\frac{20\pi}{3}\ne 8\pi\) ✗
  • \(\theta=72^\circ \Rightarrow L=\frac{72\pi}{9}=8\pi\) ✓ and \(A=\pi(400)\frac{72}{360}=80\pi\) ✓
Hint: Only D satisfies both conditions at once. Note that the area alone would not be enough — many angles paired with different radii could give \(80\pi\); it is the simultaneous use of both data that pins down the answer.
Final answer: \(r=20\text{ cm},\ \theta=72^\circ\) → option D.
