Question Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(ABCDE\) is a circle. \(AC\) and \(BE\) are diameters of the circle. Let \(P\) be the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\). If \(\angle ABE = 46^\circ\) and \(\angle DBE = 16^\circ\), then \[ \angle APD = \]
A. \(\;58^\circ\)     B. \(\;74^\circ\)     C. \(\;86^\circ\)     D. \(\;104^\circ\)
5 marks

Part 1 — Identify the Centre \(O\) Setup

Since \(AC\) and \(BE\) are both diameters, they must intersect at the centre of the circle. That point is \(O\), and \(O\) lies on \(AC\). Therefore \(OA = OB = OC = OE = \text{radius}\).

r = 1.00

Step 11M

Mark the intersection of \(AC\) and \(BE\) as \(O\). Because both are diameters, this intersection is the centre of the circle.

Hint: Any two diameters of a circle always meet at the centre.
\(O\) is the centre of circle \(ABCDE\).
Step 21A

Since \(OA\) and \(OB\) are radii of the same circle, \(\triangle OAB\) is isosceles: \[ OA = OB. \]

Hint: Equal radii → isosceles triangle → equal base angles.
Step 31M

Use base angles of the isosceles triangle \(\triangle OAB\): \[ \angle OAB = \angle OBA = 46^\circ. \]

Hint: The given \(\angle ABE\) is exactly \(\angle OBA\) since \(O\) lies on \(BE\).
Step 41A

In \(\triangle OAB\), the exterior angle at \(O\) equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles: \[ \angle POB = \angle OAB + \angle OBA = 46^\circ + 46^\circ = 92^\circ. \]

Hint: Exterior angle of a triangle = sum of the two opposite interior angles.
\(\angle POB = 92^\circ\)

Part 2 — Find \(\angle APD\) Key Step

Now look at \(\triangle PBD\). The angle \(\angle DBE = 16^\circ\) is the same as \(\angle PBD\), and \(\angle POB\) is an exterior angle of this triangle at vertex \(P\). We can therefore apply the exterior-angle theorem a second time.

\(\angle APD\) = 108°

Step 11M

In \(\triangle PBD\), the point \(O\) lies on the line \(PB\) extended (since \(E,O,B\) are collinear). Thus \(\angle POB\) is an exterior angle of \(\triangle PBD\) at \(P\), and \(\angle DBE = \angle PBD = 16^\circ\).

Hint: \(B, O, E\) are collinear because \(BE\) is a diameter through the centre \(O\).
Step 21M

Apply the exterior-angle theorem again: \[ \angle APD = \angle POB + \angle DBE = 92^\circ + 16^\circ. \] (Note \(\angle APD = \angle BPC\) by vertically opposite angles.)

Hint: The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
Step 31A

Compute: \[ \angle APD = 92^\circ + 16^\circ = 108^\circ. \]

\(\angle APD = 108^\circ\)  →  Answer: D

Summary of Answers

  • ●\(O\), the intersection of the two diameters, is the centre of the circle.
  • ●\(\triangle OAB\) is isosceles: \(\angle OAB = \angle OBA = 46^\circ\).
  • ●Exterior angle: \(\angle POB = 46^\circ + 46^\circ = 92^\circ\).
  • ●Again exterior angle: \(\angle APD = \angle POB + \angle DBE = 92^\circ + 16^\circ\).
  • ●Final answer: \(\angle APD = 108^\circ\) (Option D)
