HKDSE 2019 · Paper 2 · Q10

Which of the following statements about the graph of $y=(3-x)(x+2)+6$ is/are true?

I.  The graph opens downwards.

II.  The graph passes through the point $(1,\,10)$.

III.  The $x$-intercepts of the graph are $-2$ and $3$.

  • A. I only
  • B. II only
  • C. I and III only
  • D. II and III only
1Expand into general form.
Expanding a Product of Two Binomials \((a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd\) Expanding \((3-x)(x+2)+6\) gives \(-x^{2}+x+12\).
$y=(3-x)(x+2)+6 = 3x+6-x^{2}-2x+6 = -x^{2}+x+12$
Expanding gives the standard form $y=ax^2+bx+c$ with $a=-1,\;b=1,\;c=12$.
2Check statement I — opening direction.
Direction of Opening of a Parabola \(y=ax^{2}+bx+c:\ a<0 \Rightarrow \text{opens downwards}\) Here \(a=-1<0\), so statement I is true.
The coefficient of $x^{2}$ is $a=-1<0$, so the parabola opens downwards.
$a<0 \Rightarrow$ opens downwards; $a>0 \Rightarrow$ opens upwards. Statement I is TRUE.
3Check statement II — the point $(1,10)$.
Substitute $x=1$: \[ y = -(1)^{2}+(1)+12 = -1+1+12 = 12 \neq 10. \]
The graph passes through $(1,12)$, not $(1,10)$. Statement II is FALSE.
4Check statement III — the $x$-intercepts.
x-intercepts of a Quadratic Graph \(\text{set } y=0 \text{, then factorise}\) \(-x^{2}+x+12=0\) becomes \((4-x)(x+3)=0\), so the intercepts are \(-3\) and \(4\).
Set $y=0$: \[ -x^{2}+x+12=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{2}-x-12=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (x-4)(x+3)=0. \] Hence $x=-3$ or $x=4$.
The $x$-intercepts are $-3$ and $4$, not $-2$ and $3$. Statement III is FALSE.
5Alternative check of III at a glance.
The factor form $(3-x)(x+2)+6$ is not a full factorisation — the $+\,6$ shifts the curve. A quick test at $x=3$: \[ y=(3-3)(3+2)+6=6\neq 0, \] so $3$ cannot be an $x$-intercept (similarly $x=-2$ gives $y=6\neq 0$).
A common trap: assuming the factors $(3-x)$ and $(x+2)$ give the roots. They do not, because of the trailing $+6$.
Answer: A  (I only)

Question. The graph of \(y=(6-x)(x+2)+3\) is drawn. Decide whether each statement is true: I. The graph opens downwards. II. Its vertex is \((2,19)\). III. Its \(x\)-intercepts are \(-2\) and \(6\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand: \((6-x)(x+2)+3=6x+12-x^{2}-2x+3=-x^{2}+4x+15\).
Step 2. I. The coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is \(-1<0\), so the graph opens downwards. True.
Step 3. II. Complete the square: \(-x^{2}+4x+15=-(x-2)^{2}+19\), so the vertex is \((2,19)\). True.
Step 4. III. At \(x=-2\): \(y=0+3=3\neq 0\);
at \(x=6\): \(y=0+3=3\neq 0\). The \(+3\) shifts the curve, so these are not intercepts. False.
Step 5. So only I and II are true.
Answer: I and II only
