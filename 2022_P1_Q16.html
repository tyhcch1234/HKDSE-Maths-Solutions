Completing the Square & the Section Formula on a Parabola

Completing the Square Vertex of a Parabola Transformation \(y=2g(-x)\) Section Formula (Area Ratio)

Question

Let \(\;g(x)=3x^{2}+12kx+16k^{2}+8\;\), where \(k\) is a non-zero real constant.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\). 2 marks

(b) On the same rectangular coordinate system, denote the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) and the vertex of the graph of \(y=2g(-x)\) by \(A\) and \(B\) respectively. Let \(M\) be a point lying on \(AB\) such that the area of \(\triangle OBM\) is the triple of the area of \(\triangle OAM\), where \(O\) is the origin. Express, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of \(M\). 3 marks

Part (a) — Completing the square

2 marks

1.0
Step 11M
\[ g(x)=3x^{2}+12kx+16k^{2}+8 \]
\[ =3\bigl(x^{2}+4kx\bigr)+16k^{2}+8 \]
\[ =3\bigl(x^{2}+4kx+4k^{2}\bigr)-12k^{2}+16k^{2}+8 \]
\[ =3(x+2k)^{2}+4k^{2}+8 \]
Hint: Take the coefficient of \(x^{2}\) (here \(3\)) out of the first two terms only. Half of \(4k\) is \(2k\), so we add and subtract \((2k)^{2}=4k^{2}\) inside the bracket — remember it gets multiplied by \(3\).
Step 21A
The completed-square form is
\[ y=3(x+2k)^{2}+4k^{2}+8 \]
Compare with \(\;y=a(x-h)^{2}+c\;\), so \(\;h=-2k\;\) and \(\;c=4k^{2}+8\).
Vertex of \(y=g(x)\) is \(\;A\bigl(-2k,\;4k^{2}+8\bigr)\).
Hint: The vertex is at the minimum of the parabola. Since the bracket is squared, its smallest value is \(0\), attained at \(x=-2k\); the \(y\)-value is then the constant \(4k^{2}+8\). Note \(4k^{2}+8\ge 8>0\) always — the vertex never touches the \(x\)-axis.
0 / 2

Part (b) — Locating \(M\) on \(AB\)

3 marks

1.0
Step 11M
From part (a), \(\;A\bigl(-2k,\;4k^{2}+8\bigr)\).
Now \(y=2g(-x)=2\Bigl[3(-x)^{2}+12k(-x)+16k^{2}+8\Bigr]\)
\[ =2\bigl(3x^{2}-12kx+16k^{2}+8\bigr)=6x^{2}-24kx+32k^{2}+16 \]
\[ =6(x-2k)^{2}+8k^{2}+16 \]
So \(\;B\bigl(2k,\;8k^{2}+16\bigr)\).
Hint: Replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) reflects the graph in the \(y\)-axis, so the vertex moves from \(-2k\) to \(+2k\). Multiplying by \(2\) then stretches vertically: the \(y\)-coordinate doubles to \(2(4k^{2}+8)=8k^{2}+16\).
Step 21M
Both triangles \(\triangle OAM\) and \(\triangle OBM\) share the same altitude from \(O\) to the line \(AB\) (since \(A\), \(M\), \(B\) are collinear), and the same base line. Their bases along \(AB\) are \(AM\) and \(MB\).
\[ \frac{[OBM]}{[OAM]}=\frac{MB}{AM}=3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad AM:MB=1:3 \]
Thus \(M\) divides \(AB\) internally in the ratio \(1:3\), i.e. \(M\) is one quarter of the way from \(A\) to \(B\).
Hint: The area-ratio trick: two triangles with the same height have areas in the ratio of their bases. Here \(\triangle OAM\) and \(\triangle OBM\) do have the same height (drop a perpendicular from \(O\) onto \(AB\)), so the given area condition is exactly a statement about the lengths \(AM\) and \(MB\).
Step 31A
Using the section formula with \(AM:MB=1:3\):
\[ x_M=\frac{3(-2k)+1(2k)}{1+3}=\frac{-6k+2k}{4}=\frac{-4k}{4}=-k \]
\[ y_M=\frac{3(4k^{2}+8)+1(8k^{2}+16)}{1+3}=\frac{12k^{2}+24+8k^{2}+16}{4}=\frac{20k^{2}+40}{4}=5k^{2}+10 \]
Therefore \(\;M\bigl(-k,\;5k^{2}+10\bigr)\).
Hint: In the section formula \(\dfrac{mB+nA}{m+n}\) the ratio is \(AM:MB=m:n\) — the weights are "crossed". Here \(m=1\), \(n=3\), so \(B\) gets weight \(1\) and \(A\) gets weight \(3\). Also note the neat check: \(x_M=\tfrac{x_A+x_B}{2}=-k+\ldots\) — in fact \(M\)'s \(x\)-coordinate is the midpoint of \(-2k\) and \(2k\) shifted, i.e. \(-k\), halfway between the axis \(-2k\) and the origin.
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Vertex of \(y=g(x)\): \(\;A\bigl(-2k,\;4k^{2}+8\bigr)\)
  • (b) Vertex of \(y=2g(-x)\): \(\;B\bigl(2k,\;8k^{2}+16\bigr)\)
  • (b) Area ratio \([OBM]:[OAM]=3:1\;\Rightarrow\;AM:MB=1:3\)
  • (b) \(\;M\Bigl(-k,\;5k^{2}+10\Bigr)\)
