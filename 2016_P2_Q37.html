Topic 13 · Trigonometry — graph of $y=a\cos 2x^\circ$

Interactive Graph

Question

Let $a$ and $b$ be constants. If the figure shows the graph of $y=a\cos 2x^\circ$, then
A.  $a=-2$ and $b=90$
B.  $a=-2$ and $b=360$
C.  $a=2$ and $b=90$
D.  $a=2$ and $b=360$

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Read the graph
Amplitude of a Cosine Curve \(y=A\cos kx^{\circ}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{amplitude}=A\ (A>0)\ \text{or}\ -A\ (A<0)\) The curve reaches \(y=2\), so \(a=2\) or \(a=-2\); the sign is fixed by the value at \(x=0\).

At $x=0$ the curve passes through the origin level, rises to a maximum $y=2$ at $x=b$,
then returns.
So the curve is a cosine-type wave sitting on the $x$-axis with maximum value $+2$ at $x=b$.

From the figure: the maximum is reached at $x=b$,
and the first $x$-intercept after that is at $x=2b$.

Hint 1: The graph is upside-down relative to $y=\cos x^\circ$? No. It starts low at the $y$-axis and rises, so the standard cosine is flipped (or shifted). Check the value at $x=0$.
STEP 2 · Find $b$ — the position of the maximum
Period of a Cosine Curve \(y=\cos kx^{\circ}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{period}=\dfrac{360^{\circ}}{k}\) With \(k=2\) the period is \(180^{\circ}\), so the first zero after the peak comes at \(x=2b=180^{\circ}\).

For $y=\cos 2x^\circ$, the maximum value $+1$ occurs when $2x^\circ=0^\circ,\,360^\circ,\dots$,
i.e. $x=0,\,180^\circ,\dots$

But this graph attains its maximum at $x=b$ and the curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x=2b$ (the first intercept). Since the full wave has period $180^\circ$,
the first minimum/zero pattern gives

$$2b = 180 \quad\Rightarrow\quad b=90$$

Hint 2: One complete cycle of $\cos 2x^\circ$ has period $360^\circ/2 = 180^\circ$. The peak is halfway, at $90^\circ$.
STEP 3 · Find $a$ — the amplitude and sign
Cosine of Special Angles \(\cos 180^{\circ}=-1,\qquad \cos 0^{\circ}=1\) Substituting \((90,\,2)\) into \(y=a\cos 2x^{\circ}\) gives \(2=a\cos 180^{\circ}=-a\), so \(a=-2\).

Substitute the peak point $(x,y)=(b,\,2)=(90,\,2)$ into $y=a\cos 2x^\circ$:

$$2 = a\cos(2\cdot 90^\circ)=a\cos 180^\circ = -a$$

Hence $a=-2$.

Check: at $x=0$, $y=a\cos 0^\circ = a = -2$,
which matches the graph (the curve at the $y$-axis is below the $x$-axis).

Hint 3: $\cos 180^\circ=-1$, so a negative $a$ is needed to produce a positive peak at $x=90^\circ$.
STEP 4 · Verify the shape

With $a=-2,\;b=90$: $y=-2\cos 2x^\circ$.

Period $=180^\circ$. Maximum $=+2$ at $x=90^\circ,\,270^\circ,\dots$ Minimum $=-2$ at $x=0^\circ,\,180^\circ,\dots$ $x$-intercepts at $x=45^\circ,\,135^\circ,\dots$ — exactly the wave drawn.

The graph correctly shows maximum $2$ (so $a=2$ or $a=-2$) and the first peak at $x=90$ (so $b=90$).
Answer: C  —  $a=-2$ and $b=90$

Question. The figure shows the graph of \(y=a\sin 3x^{\circ}\), where \(a\) is a constant. The graph attains its maximum value \(5\) at \(x=b\), where \(0A. \(a=5\) and \(b=30\)    B. \(a=-5\) and \(b=30\)    C. \(a=5\) and \(b=60\)    D. \(a=-5\) and \(b=60\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The maximum value of the curve is \(5\), and the peak is above the \(x\)-axis,
so \(a=5\).
Step 2. For \(y=\sin 3x^{\circ}\) the period is \(\dfrac{360^{\circ}}{3}=120^{\circ}\), and the first maximum occurs at \(3x=90^{\circ}\),
i.e. \(x=30^{\circ}\).
Step 3. Hence \(b=30\), and the answer is option A.
Answer: A. \(a=5\) and \(b=30\)
