QUESTION 8 · 5 MARKS

In Figure 1, $E$ is the point of intersection of $AC$ and $BD$. It is given that $\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 90^\circ$ and $BC = AD$.

(a) Prove that $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle BAD$.

(b) If $AD = 12\text{ cm}$ and $DE = 9\text{ cm}$, find the area of the pentagon $ADBCE$.

STEP 1 · Set up the two right-angled triangles

From the given condition $\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 90^\circ$,
both $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle BAD$ are right-angled triangles:

  • $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled at $C$ (so $AB$ is its hypotenuse);
  • $\triangle BAD$ is right-angled at $D$ (so $AB$ is its hypotenuse).

They share the common side $AB$,
and $BC$ and $AD$ are given equal in length.

Hint: list the three pieces of information you need — a right angle,
the hypotenuse, and one leg.

STEP 2 · Prove the congruence (RHS)
RHS Congruence Condition \(\angle=\angle 90^{\circ},\ \text{hypotenuse}=\text{hypotenuse},\ \text{leg}=\text{leg}\) \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle BAD\) have right angles at \(C\) and \(D\), the common hypotenuse \(AB\) and \(BC=AD\).

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle BAD$:

  • $\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 90^\circ$  
    (given)
  • $AB = AB$  
    (common side)
  • $BC = AD$  
    (given)

Therefore $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle BAD$  
RHS

$$\therefore \angle ABE = \angle BAE \quad \text{and} \quad AC = BD.$$

STEP 3 · Find $AE$ using right-angled $\triangle ADE$
Pythagoras' Theorem \(AE^{2}=AD^{2}+DE^{2}\) With \(AD=12\) and \(DE=9\), \(AE=\sqrt{144+81}=15\text{ cm}\).

In right-angled $\triangle ADE$ (right angle at $D$), with $AD = 12$ and $DE = 9$:

$$AE = \sqrt{AD^2 + DE^2} = \sqrt{12^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{144 + 81} = 15\text{ cm}$$

This is the Pythagorean theorem in the green triangle $ADE$.

STEP 4 · Show $BE = AE$ and find $CE$
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle \(\angle ABE=\angle BAE\Rightarrow AE=BE\) Equal base angles in \(\triangle ABE\) give \(BE=AE=15\), so \(BD=15+9=24\).

From Step 2, $\angle ABE = \angle BAE$. In $\triangle ABE$, equal angles at $A$ and $B$ mean the opposite sides are equal:

$$AE = BE = 15\text{ cm}$$

Now $BD = BE + DE = 15 + 9 = 24\text{ cm}$,
and since $AC = BD$ (from the congruence),

$$AC = 24\text{ cm}$$

Finally, $CE = AC - AE = 24 - 15 = 9\text{ cm}$. (Note $CE = DE = 9\text{ cm}$.)

STEP 5 · Split the pentagon into two triangles

The pentagon $ADBCE$ is the union of $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle BCE$ (they meet along $BD$ at $E$).

So the required area is:

$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}(AD)(BD) + \tfrac{1}{2}(BC)(CE)$$

Each base is a leg of a right-angled triangle,
so the heights are easy to identify.

STEP 6 · Substitute and compute

We have $AD = 12$, $BD = 24$, $BC = 12$ (given), $CE = 9$:

$$ \begin{aligned} \text{Area} &= \tfrac{1}{2}(12)(24) + \tfrac{1}{2}(12)(9) \\[4pt] &= 144 + 54 \\[4pt] &= 198\text{ cm}^2 \end{aligned} $$

FINAL ANSWER

(a) $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle BAD$ (RHS)

(b) Area of pentagon $ADBCE = 198\text{ cm}^2$

Question. In the figure, \(\angle ACB=\angle ADB=90^{\circ}\), \(BC=AD=16\text{ cm}\), \(AD\perp DE\), \(DE=12\text{ cm}\) and \(E\) lies on \(BD\) and \(AC\).
(a) Prove that \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle BAD\).
(b) Find the area of the pentagon \(ADBCE\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) Both triangles are right-angled (at \(C\) and at \(D\)), \(AB\) is a common hypotenuse and \(BC=AD\) is given.
By the RHS condition, \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle BAD\).
Step 2. Hence \(\angle ABE=\angle BAE\), so \(\triangle ABE\) is isosceles and \(AE=BE\).
Step 3. In right-angled \(\triangle ADE\), \(AE=\sqrt{16^{2}+12^{2}}=\sqrt{400}=20\text{ cm}\), so \(BE=20\) and \(BD=20+12=32\text{ cm}\).
Step 4. From the congruence \(AC=BD=32\), so \(CE=AC-AE=32-20=12\text{ cm}\).
Step 5. The pentagon splits into \(\triangle ADB\) and \(\triangle BCE\): area \(=\tfrac12(16)(32)+\tfrac12(16)(12)=256+96=352\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Answer: (a) RHS; (b) \(352\text{ cm}^{2}\)
