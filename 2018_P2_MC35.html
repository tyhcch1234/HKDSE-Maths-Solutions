HKDSE Math 2018 · Paper 2 · Q35

Formulas and Sequences

Sequence Sum of first n terms $S_n$ General term $a_n$ Geometric sequence

QQuestion

If the sum of the first $n$ terms of a sequence is $6n^{2}-n$, which of the following is/are true?

  1. $22$ is a term of the sequence.
  2. The 1st term of the sequence is $5$.
  3. The sequence is a geometric sequence.
A.I only
B.II only
C.I and III only
D.II and III only

1Step-by-Step Guided Solution

1 The key idea: how $a_n$ is related to $S_n$ ▼

We are told the sum of the first $n$ terms, not the terms themselves. So we must first convert the sum into the general term $a_n$.

$$S_n=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{n-1}+a_n$$
$$S_{n-1}=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{n-1}$$

Subtracting the second line from the first gives the standard relation:

$$a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\quad \text{for } n\ge 2$$

Also note the special first term: $a_1=S_1$.

Formula: $S_n-S_{n-1}=a_n$   (for $n\ge 2$)
Meaning of each symbol:
• $S_n$ — the sum of the first $n$ terms
• $S_{n-1}$ — the sum of the first $n-1$ terms
• $a_n$ — the $n$-th term
Their difference is exactly the single term that was added last.
2 Derive the general term $a_n$ ▼

Substitute $S_n=6n^2-n$ into the relation. To get $S_{n-1}$, replace every $n$ in the formula by $n-1$.

$$\begin{aligned} a_n&=S_n-S_{n-1}\\ &=(6n^{2}-n)-\left[6(n-1)^{2}-(n-1)\right]\\ &=6\left[n^{2}-(n-1)^{2}\right]-\left[n-(n-1)\right]\\ &=6(2n-1)-1\\ &=12n-7 \end{aligned}$$

Now check the formula also works for the first term:

$$a_1=S_1=6(1)^2-1=5,\qquad 12(1)-7=5 \;\checkmark$$
$$\boxed{\,a_n=12n-7\,}\quad\text{for every positive integer }n$$
Formula used: $(n-1)^2=n^2-2n+1$, so $n^2-(n-1)^2=2n-1$.
Why check $n=1$? The relation $a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$ needs $S_0$, which is not defined. So we verify separately that $a_1=S_1$ and confirm the same formula $12n-7$ holds. It does — so we may use it for all $n$.
3 Statement I: Is 22 a term of the sequence? ▼

A term of the sequence must equal $a_n=12n-7$ for some positive integer $n$. Set up the equation:

$$12n-7=22\;\Longrightarrow\;12n=29\;\Longrightarrow\;n=\frac{29}{12}$$

But $\dfrac{29}{12}$ is not a positive integer, so there is no such $n$.

✗Statement I is false — 22 is not a term of the sequence.
$n$$a_n=12n-7$
$a_n=$ 5
Key checking rule: the index $n$ of a sequence must be a positive integer. If solving $a_n = k$ gives a non-integer (or a non-positive value) of $n$, then $k$ is NOT a term.

Look at the table: terms jump $5,\,17,\,29,\,41,\dots$ — the value $22$ is skipped.
4 Statement II: Is the 1st term 5? ▼

By definition, the sum of the first $1$ term is just the first term itself: $S_1=a_1$.

$$a_1=S_1=6(1)^{2}-1=6-1=5$$

This matches the general formula too: $a_1=12(1)-7=5$.

Statement II is true.
Formula: $a_1=S_1$
Meaning: $S_1$ means "sum of the first 1 term" — there is only one term to add, so the sum equals that term.
5 Statement III: Is it a geometric sequence? ▼

A geometric sequence requires a constant common ratio $r=\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ for all $n$. Test it:

$$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{12(n+1)-7}{12n-7}=\frac{12n+5}{12n-7}=1+\frac{12}{12n-7}$$

This ratio depends on $n$, so it is not a constant. For example:

$$\frac{a_2}{a_1}=\frac{17}{5}=3.4,\qquad \frac{a_3}{a_2}=\frac{29}{17}\approx 1.706$$
✗Statement III is false — the ratio of consecutive terms is not constant, so the sequence is not geometric.
Definition: Geometric sequence $\iff \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=r$ where $r$ is a constant independent of $n$.
Common trap: $a_n=12n-7$ is linear in $n$, so the sequence is arithmetic with common difference $12$ — not geometric.

Indeed $a_{n+1}-a_n=12$ for every $n$.
6 Conclusion and answer ▼
✗I. 22 is a term of the sequence — False
II. The 1st term is 5 — True
✗III. The sequence is geometric — False

Only statement II is true.

ANSWER: B

Only II is true.

★Key Takeaways

1. Convert sum to term: $a_n=S_n-S_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$, and $a_1=S_1$.
2. To test whether a value $k$ is a term, solve $a_n=k$ — the index $n$ must be a positive integer.
3. Geometric $\iff \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ is constant. Here $a_n=12n-7$ is linear in $n$, so it is arithmetic, with common difference $12$.
