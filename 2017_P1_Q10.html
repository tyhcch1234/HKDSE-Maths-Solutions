Mensuration — Congruent Triangles & the Area of a Sector
Congruent Triangles (SSS) Angle at Centre = 2 × Angle at Circumference Sector Area Proof with Reasons

Q The Question

In Figure 1, \(OPQR\) is a quadrilateral such that \(OP = OQ = OR\). \(OQ\) and \(PR\) intersect at the point \(S\). \(S\) is the mid-point of \(PR\).

(a) Prove that \(\triangle OPS \cong \triangle ORS\).  2 marks

(b) It is given that \(O\) is the centre of the circle which passes through \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\). If \(OQ = 6\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle PRQ = 10^\circ\), find the area of the sector \(OPQR\) in terms of \(\pi\).  4 marks

Modelled in the diagrams below: \(O\) sits at the origin, the circle of radius \(6\text{ cm}\) passes through \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\), and \(S\) is the mid-point of chord \(PR\).

STEP 11M
State the three pairs of equal sides, using the given information. \[ \begin{aligned} OP &= OR && \text{(given)}\\ PS &= RS && \text{(}S\text{ is the mid-point of }PR\text{)}\\ OS &= OS && \text{(common side)} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Every congruence proof needs the reasons. “Given”, “mid-point” and “common side” are the three standard reasons used here.
STEP 21A
Therefore the two triangles are congruent by the SSS condition: \[ \boxed{\;\triangle OPS \cong \triangle ORS \quad (\text{SSS})\;} \]
Hint: All three pairs of corresponding sides are equal, so the triangles are congruent. Once congruent, corresponding angles are equal — this is what makes \(OS \perp PR\) and is used in part (b).
\(\triangle OPS \cong \triangle ORS\) (SSS) ✔

b Area of sector \(OPQR\) 4 marks

Now \(O\) is the centre of the circle through \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\), with radius \(OQ = 6\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle PRQ = 10^\circ\). The same diagram is used: \(S\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(O\) to chord \(PR\), and \(Q\) lies on the circle on the same side of \(PR\) as \(O\).

Radius r = 6.00 cm ∠PRQ = 10.0° ∠POR = 40.0° Sector area = 4.00π cm²
STEP 11M
Find \(\angle POQ\). Since \(\triangle OPS \cong \triangle ORS\) (part (a)), \(OS\) bisects \(\angle POR\). Also \(\triangle OPQ\) is isosceles with \(OP = OQ\), so \(\angle POQ = 2\,\angle PRQ\) (angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference subtended by the same arc \(PQ\)): \[ \angle POQ = 2\angle PRQ = 2(10^\circ) = 20^\circ \]
Hint: Both \(\angle POQ\) and \(\angle PRQ\) subtend the same arc \(PQ\) of the circle. The one at the centre is exactly twice the one at the circumference.
STEP 21M
Since \(OS\) bisects \(\angle POR\), we get \(\angle POQ = \angle QOR\): \[ \begin{aligned} \angle POR &= \angle POQ + \angle QOR\\ &= \angle POQ + \angle POQ \quad (\text{by part (a)})\\ &= 2\angle POQ\\ &= 2(20^\circ) = 40^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Hint: This is the key use of part (a). The congruence makes \(OS\) the perpendicular bisector of \(PR\), so it also bisects the central angle \(\angle POR\).
\(\angle POR = 40^\circ\), so the sector angle is \(40^\circ\).
STEP 31M
Apply the sector area formula with \(r = 6\text{ cm}\) and sector angle \(40^\circ\): \[ \text{Area} = \frac{40^\circ}{360^\circ}\,\pi r^2 = \frac{40}{360}\,\pi (6)^2 \]
Hint: A sector is a fraction \(\dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ}\) of the whole circle. Write the fraction before substituting \(r = 6\).
STEP 41A
Simplify: \[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{9}\,\pi(36) = 4\pi\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint: The question says “in terms of \(\pi\)”, so keep \(\pi\) in the answer — do not evaluate it numerically.
Area of sector \(OPQR = 4\pi\ \text{cm}^2\) ✔
0 / 4

Σ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(OP = OR\) (given), \(PS = RS\) (mid-point of \(PR\)), \(OS = OS\) (common side) \(\Rightarrow \triangle OPS \cong \triangle ORS\) (SSS).
  • (b) \(\angle POQ = 2\angle PRQ = 2(10^\circ) = \mathbf{20^\circ}\).
  • \(\angle POR = \angle POQ + \angle QOR = 2\angle POQ = \mathbf{40^\circ}\) (since \(OS\) bisects \(\angle POR\) by part (a)).
  • Area of sector \(OPQR = \dfrac{40}{360}\,\pi (6)^2 = \dfrac{1}{9}\pi(36) = \mathbf{4\pi\ \text{cm}^2}\).
