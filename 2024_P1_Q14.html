HKDSE 2024 Paper 1 — Question 14

Let $F(x)=\left(6x^{2}+x+p\right)\left(qx^{2}+rx-10\right)$, where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are constants. The constant term of $F(x)$ is $40$.

(a) Write down the value of $p$. (1 mark)
(b) When $F(x)$ is divided by $x+1$, the remainder is $-12$. It is given that $x-2$ is a factor of $F(x)$.
(i) Find $q$ and $r$. (2 marks)
(ii) How many irrational roots does the equation $F(x)=0$ have? Explain your answer. (5 marks)
1(a) Constant term of $F(x)$

The constant term of a product comes from multiplying the two constant terms:

$F(x)=(\dots+p)(\dots-10)\;\Rightarrow\;\text{constant term}=p\times(-10)=-10p$.

Given the constant term is $40$:

$-10p=40\;\Rightarrow\;p=-4$.

Hint: only the last terms matter for the constant term — the $x$ terms never contribute.
2(b)(i) Use the remainder theorem
Remainder Theorem \(\text{remainder}=F(-1)\) Division by \(x+1\) leaves remainder \(F(-1)=-12\), and \(x-2\) being a factor means \(F(2)=0\).

With $p=-4$: $\;F(x)=(6x^{2}+x-4)(qx^{2}+rx-10)$.

Divided by $x+1$ gives remainder $-12$,
so $F(-1)=-12$. Also $x-2$ is a factor, so $F(2)=0$.

Substitute $x=-1$: the first bracket is $6(1)+(-1)-4=1$,
so $F(-1)=1\cdot(q-r-10)=q-r-10$.

Hence $q-r-10=-12\;\Rightarrow\;q-r=-2.\quad\cdots(1)$

Hint: $F(-1)=$ (remainder) and $F(2)=0$ are the two equations you need.
3(b)(i) Use the factor theorem
Factor Theorem \(F(2)=0\iff x-2\text{ is a factor}\) At \(x=2\) the first bracket is \(22\), so the second must vanish: \(4q+2r-10=0\), i.e. \(2q+r=5\).

Substitute $x=2$: the first bracket is $6(4)+2-4=22$, so $F(2)=22\,(4q+2r-10)$.

$F(2)=0\;\Rightarrow\;4q+2r-10=0\;\Rightarrow\;2q+r=5.\quad\cdots(2)$

Solve $\begin{cases}q-r=-2\\ 2q+r=5\end{cases}$: adding gives $3q=3$,
so $q=1$, and then $r=q+2=3$.

Check: $q-r=1-3=-2$ ✓ and $2q+r=2+3=5$ ✓.

Hint: the bracket $22\ne 0$,
so the second bracket must vanish.
4(b)(ii) Factorise completely

Now $\;F(x)=(6x^{2}+x-4)(x^{2}+3x-10)$.

$6x^{2}+x-4=(2x-1)(3x+4)$  
and  $x^{2}+3x-10=(x-2)(x+5)$.

So $F(x)=(6x^{2}+x-4)(x-2)(x+5)=0$ gives

$6x^{2}+x-4=0$, $\;x-2=0\,$ or $\;x+5=0$.

Hint: use the result of part (b)(i) to write the second factor,
then factorise each quadratic.
5(b)(ii) Solve each factor

From $x-2=0$: $\;x=2$ (rational).
From $x+5=0$: $\;x=-5$ (rational).

For $6x^{2}+x-4=0$, use the quadratic formula with $a=6,b=1,c=-4$:

$x=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4(6)(-4)}}{12}=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{97}}{12}$.

Hint: $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}$ — keep the surd form to judge irrationality.
6(b)(ii) Count the irrational roots
Irrational Roots from the Discriminant \(\sqrt{\Delta}\ \text{not a perfect square}\Rightarrow\text{irrational roots}\) Since \(9^{2}=81<97<100=10^{2}\), \(\sqrt{97}\) is irrational, so \(\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{97}}{12}\) are the two irrational roots.

$\sqrt{97}$ is not a perfect square (since $9^{2}=81<97<100=10^{2}$), so $\sqrt{97}$ is irrational.

Hence $\dfrac{-1-\sqrt{97}}{12}$ and $\dfrac{-1+\sqrt{97}}{12}$ are irrational.

Meanwhile $2$ and $-5$ are integers,
hence rational (not irrational).

Therefore the equation has exactly 2 irrational roots.

Hint: a root of $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$ is irrational exactly when the discriminant is not a perfect square.
Answer: (a) $p=-4$.   (b)(i) $q=1$, $r=3$.   (b)(ii) The equation $F(x)=0$ has 2 irrational roots, namely $\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt{97}}{12}$.

Question. Let \(F(x)=(4x^{2}+2x+p)(2x^{2}+qx-8)\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are constants. The constant term of \(F(x)\) is \(48\). When \(F(x)\) is divided by \(x+1\), the remainder is \(12\).
(a) Find \(p\).
(b) Find \(q\).
(c) Solve the equation \(F(x)=0\) and state the number of irrational roots.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) The constant term is \(p\times(-8)=-8p=48\), so \(p=-6\).
Step 2. (b) \(F(-1)=(4-2-6)(2-q-8)=(-4)(-6-q)=24+4q\), so \(24+4q=12\) and \(q=-3\).
Step 3. Hence \(F(x)=(4x^{2}+2x-6)(2x^{2}-3x-8)=2(2x+3)(x-1)(2x^{2}-3x-8)\).
Step 4. \(F(x)=0\) gives \(x=-\dfrac{3}{2}\), \(x=1\), or \(2x^{2}-3x-8=0\).
Step 5. For \(2x^{2}-3x-8=0\): \(x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{9+64}}{4}=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{73}}{4}\).
Step 6. \(\sqrt{73}\) is not an integer because \(8^{2}=64<73<81=9^{2}\), so these two roots are irrational: \(F(x)=0\) has exactly \(2\) irrational roots.
Answer: (a) \(p=-6\); (b) \(q=-3\); (c) \(x=-\dfrac{3}{2},\,1,\,\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{73}}{4}\), with \(2\) irrational roots
