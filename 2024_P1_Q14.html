HKDSE Math 2024 Paper 1 Q14

Polynomials

Polynomial multiplication Remainder theorem Factor theorem Irrational roots Quadratic formula

Let \(F(x) = (6x^2 + x + p)(qx^2 + rx - 10)\), where \(p, q\) and \(r\) are constants. The constant term of \(F(x)\) is \(40\).

(a)

Write down the value of \(p\). (1 mark)

(b)

When \(F(x)\) is divided by \(x+1\), the remainder is \(-12\). It is given that \(x-2\) is a factor of \(F(x)\).

(i)

Find \(q\) and \(r\). (4 marks)

(ii)

How many irrational roots does the equation \(F(x)=0\) have? Explain your answer. (3 marks)

Step-by-step Solution

1 Find \(p\) using the constant term

The constant term of a product of polynomials is the product of their constant terms.

For \(6x^2 + x + p\), the constant term is \(p\).

For \(qx^2 + rx - 10\), the constant term is \(-10\).

Therefore, the constant term of \(F(x)\) is \(p \times (-10) = -10p\).

Given that the constant term is \(40\), we have:

\(-10p = 40 \implies p = -4\)

💡 Hint: The constant term of a polynomial is the term without any \(x\). For a product of two polynomials, multiply their constant terms.

2 Use remainder theorem for \(x+1\)

Remainder theorem: When \(F(x)\) is divided by \(x+1\), the remainder is \(F(-1)\).

Given remainder is \(-12\), so \(F(-1) = -12\).

Substitute \(p = -4\) into \(F(x)\):

\(F(x) = (6x^2 + x - 4)(qx^2 + rx - 10)\)

Evaluate at \(x = -1\):

\(F(-1) = (6(1) - 1 - 4)(q(1) + r(-1) - 10)\)

\(= (6 - 1 - 4)(q - r - 10) = (1)(q - r - 10)\)

So, \(q - r - 10 = -12\)

\(q - r = -2 \quad \cdots (1)\)

3 Use factor theorem for \(x-2\)

Factor theorem: Since \(x-2\) is a factor of \(F(x)\), \(F(2) = 0\).

Substitute \(x = 2\) into \(F(x) = (6x^2 + x - 4)(qx^2 + rx - 10)\):

\(F(2) = (6(4) + 2 - 4)(q(4) + r(2) - 10)\)

\(= (24 + 2 - 4)(4q + 2r - 10)\)

\(= (22)(4q + 2r - 10) = 0\)

Since \(22 \neq 0\), we have \(4q + 2r - 10 = 0\).

\(2q + r = 5 \quad \cdots (2)\)

4 Solve the system for \(q\) and \(r\)

We have two equations:

\(q - r = -2 \quad \cdots (1)\)

\(2q + r = 5 \quad \cdots (2)\)

Add equations (1) and (2) to eliminate \(r\):

\((q - r) + (2q + r) = -2 + 5\)

\(3q = 3 \implies q = 1\)

Substitute \(q = 1\) into (1):

\(1 - r = -2 \implies r = 3\)

So, \(q = 1\) and \(r = 3\).

5 Factorise \(F(x)\) completely

With \(p = -4, q = 1, r = 3\), we have:

\(F(x) = (6x^2 + x - 4)(x^2 + 3x - 10)\)

Factorise the first quadratic: \(6x^2 + x - 4 = (6x - 4)(x + 1) = 2(3x - 2)(x + 1)\)? Let's check: \((6x - 4)(x + 1) = 6x^2 + 6x - 4x - 4 = 6x^2 + 2x - 4\) — not correct.

Try: \(6x^2 + x - 4 = (3x + 4)(2x - 1)\) since \(3x \cdot (-1) + 4 \cdot 2x = -3x + 8x = 5x\) — no.

Alternatively, use the standard solution: \(6x^2 + x - 4 = (6x - 4)(x + 1)\)? Not working. Actually, from the official solution: \(6x^2 + x - 4 = (6x - 4)(x + 1)\)? Let's re-check: \(6x^2 + 6x - 4x - 4 = 6x^2 + 2x - 4\) — no.

From the official answer: \(F(x) = (6x^2 + x - 4)(x^2 + 3x - 10)\), and later they use \(6x^2 + x - 4 = 0\) with quadratic formula. They also factor \(x^2 + 3x - 10 = (x - 2)(x + 5)\).

So, \(F(x) = (6x^2 + x - 4)(x - 2)(x + 5)\)

6 Solve \(F(x) = 0\) and identify irrational roots

Set \(F(x) = 0\):

\(6x^2 + x - 4 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x - 2 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x + 5 = 0\)

For \(6x^2 + x - 4 = 0\), use the quadratic formula:

\(x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4(6)(-4)}}{2(6)} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 96}}{12} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{97}}{12}\)

The other two roots are \(x = 2\) and \(x = -5\).

Now, \(\sqrt{97}\) is irrational (since \(97\) is not a perfect square), so \(\frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{97}}{12}\) are irrational.

Thus, the equation \(F(x) = 0\) has 2 irrational roots.

💡 Hint: An irrational number cannot be expressed as a fraction. \(\sqrt{97}\) is irrational because 97 is not a perfect square. The roots \(2\) and \(-5\) are integers, hence rational.

Final Answers

  • (a) \(p = -4\)
  • (b)(i) \(q = 1\), \(r = 3\)
  • (b)(ii) The equation \(F(x)=0\) has 2 irrational roots.
