Geometry — Angle chasing with parallel lines & isosceles triangles

Parallel Lines Corresponding Angles Isosceles Triangle Angle Sum of Triangle Vertically Opposite Angles

Q Question

In Figure 1, \(B\) and \(D\) are points lying on \(AC\) and \(AE\) respectively. \(BE\) and \(CD\) intersect at the point \(F\). It is given that \(AB=BE\), \(BD /\!/ CE\), \(\angle CAE = 30^\circ\) and \(\angle ADB = 42^\circ\).

(a) Find \(\angle BEC\). 3 marks

(b) Let \(\angle BDC = \theta\). Express \(\angle CFE\) in terms of \(\theta\). 5 marks

Given: \(AB = BE\) (so \(\triangle ABE\) is isosceles), \(BD /\!/ CE\), \(\angle CAE = 30^\circ\), \(\angle ADB = 42^\circ\).

∠AEB = 30° ∠ADB = 42° ∠BEC = 12°

Drag the slider to distort the figure — note that the angle relationships stay the same, which is exactly why angle-chasing works.

Step 11M
Since \(BD /\!/ CE\), the corresponding angles give \[ \angle AEC = \angle ADB = 42^\circ. \]
Hint: Look at the transversal \(AE\) cutting the parallel lines \(BD\) and \(CE\). The angle at \(D\) and the angle at \(E\) occupy the same relative position — so they are equal (corresponding angles, \(BD /\!/ CE\)).
Step 21M
In \(\triangle ABE\), \(AB = BE\), so \(\triangle ABE\) is isosceles with base \(AE\). Hence the base angles are equal: \[ \angle AEB = \angle BAE = \angle CAE = 30^\circ. \]
Hint: Because \(B\) lies on \(AC\), the angle \(\angle BAE\) is the same as the given \(\angle CAE = 30^\circ\). Equal sides \(AB = BE\) → equal opposite angles.
Step 31A
At point \(E\), the ray \(BE\) splits \(\angle AEC\): \[ \angle BEC = \angle AEC - \angle AEB = 42^\circ - 30^\circ. \]
\[ \therefore \angle BEC = 12^\circ \]
Hint: Always check your figure: \(\angle AEB\) sits inside \(\angle AEC\), so subtract to isolate \(\angle BEC\).

Move the slider — the value of \(\angle CFE\) changes with \(\theta\), confirming the linear relationship \(168^\circ - \theta\).

Step 11M
Since \(BD /\!/ CE\), the alternate angles on the transversal \(CD\) are equal: \[ \angle DCE = \angle BDC = \theta. \]
Hint: \(BD /\!/ CE\) and \(CD\) is a transversal. The angle \(\angle BDC\) (at \(D\)) and the angle \(\angle DCE\) (at \(C\)) are alternate angles and therefore equal.
Step 21A
Consider \(\triangle CFE\). Its three interior angles are: \[ \angle CEF = \angle BEC = 12^\circ,\quad \angle ECF = \angle DCE = \theta,\quad \angle CFE = ? \]
Hint: At \(E\), the ray \(EF\) is the same as \(EB\), so \(\angle CEF = \angle BEC = 12^\circ\). At \(C\), the ray \(CF\) is the same as \(CD\), so \(\angle ECF = \angle DCE = \theta\). Now apply the angle sum of a triangle.
Step 31A
Angle sum of \(\triangle CFE\): \[ \angle CFE = 180^\circ - \angle BEC - \angle DCE = 180^\circ - 12^\circ - \theta. \]
\[ \therefore \angle CFE = 168^\circ - \theta \]
Hint: Watch the size of the answer: since \(\theta\) is one angle of the triangle, we need \(0^\circ < \theta < 168^\circ\), which is consistent with the figure.

✓ Final Answers

  • (a) \(\angle BEC = 42^\circ - 30^\circ = 12^\circ\)
  • (b) \(\angle CFE = 180^\circ - 12^\circ - \theta = 168^\circ - \theta\)
  • Key ideas: corresponding angles (\(BD /\!/ CE\)), isosceles \(\triangle ABE\), alternate angles, and the angle sum of a triangle.
