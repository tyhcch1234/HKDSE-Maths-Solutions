📋 Question Overview

(a) In Figure 7, the equation of the straight line \(L_1\) is \(6x + 7y = 900\) and the \(x\)-intercept of the straight line \(L_2\) is \(180\). \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) intersect at the point \((45, 90)\). The shaded region (including the boundary) represents the solution of a system of inequalities. Find the system of inequalities. [4 marks]

(b) A factory produces two types of wardrobes, \(X\) and \(Y\). Each wardrobe \(X\) requires 6 man-hours for assembly and 2 man-hours for packing while each wardrobe \(Y\) requires 7 man-hours for assembly and 3 man-hours for packing. In a certain month, the factory has 900 man-hours available for assembly and 360 man-hours available for packing. The profits for producing a wardrobe \(X\) and a wardrobe \(Y\) are \(\$440\) and \(\$665\) respectively. A worker claims that the total profit can exceed \(\$80\,000\) that month. Do you agree? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

We are given two points on \(L_2\): the \(x\)-intercept \((180, 0)\) and the intersection point \((45, 90)\).

Slope of \(L_2\):

\[ m = \frac{90 - 0}{45 - 180} = \frac{90}{-135} = -\frac{2}{3} \]

Equation of \(L_2\) (using point-slope form):

\[ y - 90 = -\frac{2}{3}(x - 45) \]

Multiply both sides by 3:

\[ 3y - 270 = -2x + 90 \]

\[ 2x + 3y = 360 \]

Hint: The slope formula is \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\). Here, substituting the coordinates gives \(-\frac{2}{3}\). This slope means for every 2 units down, we move 3 units right.

The shaded region is below both lines and in the first quadrant. We identify each boundary:

  • Boundary \(L_1\): \(6x + 7y = 900\) \(\Rightarrow\) Shaded region: \(6x + 7y \leq 900\)
  • Boundary \(L_2\): \(2x + 3y = 360\) \(\Rightarrow\) Shaded region: \(2x + 3y \leq 360\)
  • \(x\)-axis boundary: \(y = 0\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(y \geq 0\)
  • \(y\)-axis boundary: \(x = 0\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(x \geq 0\)

Thus, the system of inequalities is:

\[ \begin{cases} 6x + 7y \leq 900 \\ 2x + 3y \leq 360 \\ x \geq 0 \\ y \geq 0 \end{cases} \]

Hint: To determine the inequality sign, pick a test point inside the shaded region, e.g., \((0,0)\). Substitute into \(6x + 7y\): \(0 \leq 900\) is true, so the inequality is \(\leq\).

Let \(x\) and \(y\) be the number of wardrobes \(X\) and \(Y\) produced respectively.

Constraints:

  • Assembly: \(6x + 7y \leq 900\)
  • Packing: \(2x + 3y \leq 360\)
  • Non-negativity: \(x \geq 0, y \geq 0\) and \(x, y\) are integers.

Profit function:

\[ P = 440x + 665y \]

Hint: The profit per wardrobe \(X\) is \$440 and per wardrobe \(Y\) is \$665. The total profit is a linear combination of the quantities produced.

The vertices of the shaded feasible region (from Figure 7) are:

\((0, 0),\ (0, 120),\ (45, 90),\ (150, 0)\)

We evaluate the profit function \(P = 440x + 665y\) at each vertex:

Vertex Calculation Profit \(P\)
\((0,0)\) \(440(0) + 665(0)\) \$0
\((0,120)\) \(440(0) + 665(120)\) \$79\,800
\((45,90)\) \(440(45) + 665(90)\) \$79\,650
\((150,0)\) \(440(150) + 665(0)\) \$66\,000

Hint: For a linear programming problem, the maximum or minimum value of a linear objective function occurs at one of the vertices of the feasible region.

The greatest possible profit is \(\$79\,800\) (attained when \(x = 0\) and \(y = 120\)).

Since \(\$79\,800 < \$80\,000\), the worker's claim that the total profit can exceed \(\$80\,000\) is not valid.

Therefore, the claim is disagreed.

Hint: The maximum profit is obtained at the vertex \((0, 120)\). This means the factory should produce 0 wardrobes of type \(X\) and 120 of type \(Y\) to maximise profit.
