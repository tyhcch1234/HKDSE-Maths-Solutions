Question. If $m(m-a)=a(1-m)$, then $a=$

  • A. $m$
  • B. $2m$
  • C. $m^2$
  • D. $\dfrac{m^2+m}{2}$

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1
Start with the given equation:
$$m(m-a)=a(1-m)$$
Goal: isolate the unknown $a$ on one side.
STEP 2
Distributive Law \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) \(m(m-a)=m^{2}-ma\) and \(a(1-m)=a-am\).
Expand both sides:
$$m^2 - ma = a - am$$
Left: $m(m-a)=m^2-ma$. Right: $a(1-m)=a-am$. Note $-ma=-am$.
STEP 3
Addition Property of Equality \(A=B\ \Rightarrow\ A+C=B+C\) Adding \(ma\) to both sides cancels the \(a\)-terms, leaving \(m^{2}=a\).
Add $ma$ (i.e. $am$) to both sides so the $a$-terms cancel on the left:
$$m^2 - ma + ma = a - am + ma$$
$$m^2 = a$$
Since $-am+ma=0$, the $a$-terms on the right vanish and the left simplifies.
STEP 4
Therefore:
$$a = m^2$$
Comparing with the options, this matches choice C.
CHECK
Substitute $a=m^2$ back:
LHS $=m(m-m^2)=m^2-m^3$; RHS $=m^2(1-m)=m^2-m^3$. ✓
Both sides are identical, so the answer is verified.
Answer: C  ( $a=m^2$ )

Question. If \(p(p+q)=q(3-p)\), then \(q=\)
A. \(p\)    B. \(\dfrac{p^{2}}{3}\)    C. \(\dfrac{p^{2}}{3-2p}\)    D. \(\dfrac{p^{2}+p}{3}\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand both sides: \(p^{2}+pq=3q-pq\).
Step 2. Collect the \(q\)-terms on one side: \(p^{2}=3q-pq-pq=3q-2pq=q(3-2p)\).
Step 3. Divide both sides by \(3-2p\) (with \(p\ne\dfrac{3}{2}\)): \(q=\dfrac{p^{2}}{3-2p}\), option C.
Answer: C. \(q=\dfrac{p^{2}}{3-2p}\)
