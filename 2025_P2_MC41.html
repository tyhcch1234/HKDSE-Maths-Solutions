QThe Question

Denote the origin by \(O\). The coordinates of the point \(U\) are \((20,\,0)\). Let \(V\) be a point lying on the positive \(y\)-axis such that the \(x\)-coordinate of the in-centre of \(\triangle OUV\) is \(6\). Find the area of \(\triangle OUV\).

A. 70 B. 87 C. 210 ✓ D. 250

Step 11M

Since \(OU\) lies on the \(x\)-axis and \(OV\) lies on the \(y\)-axis, the two legs of \(\triangle OUV\) are perpendicular to each other. The inscribed circle touches both axes.

Because the circle is tangent to the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis, its centre \(I\) is at an equal distance \(r\) from each axis, i.e. the in-centre is \((r,\,r)\).

Hint: A circle tangent to both coordinate axes has its centre on the line \(y=x\). So if \(I=(6,6)\) then \(r=6\) — the in-radius is \(6\).
Step 21M

Given the \(x\)-coordinate of the in-centre is \(6\), we get

\[ I=(6,\,6)\qquad\text{and}\qquad r=6 .\]

So the distance from the in-centre \(I\) to each of the two legs is \(6\).

Hint: The in-radius equals the perpendicular distance from the in-centre to any side. Against the two axes this distance is just the \(x\)- and \(y\)-coordinate of \(I\).
Step 31A

The in-centre of a right triangle sits at \((\,\tfrac{r}{\text{no}}\)… but more simply: for a right triangle with legs \(p,\ q\) and hypotenuse \(c\),

\[ r=\frac{p+q-c}{2}. \]

Here \(p=OU=20,\ q=OV=k,\ c=UV\). With \(r=6\):

\[ 6=\frac{20+k-UV}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad UV=k+8. \]
Answer: \(r=6\), so \(UV = k+8\).

Step 11M

Let the inscribed circle touch \(OU,OV,UV\) at \(C,A,B\) respectively, so \(A,B,C\) are the tangent points. By the equal tangent lengths property:

\[ OA=OC=r=6,\qquad VA=VB=k,\qquad UB=UC. \]

Along \(OU=20\): \(\;OC+UC=20\Rightarrow UC=20-6=14\), hence \(UB=14\).

Hint: Tangents from the same external point to a circle are equal. That's why \(VA=VB\) and \(UB=UC\).
Answer: \(UB=UC=14\).
Step 21M

Look at the right-angled triangle \(OUV\): \(\;OU=20,\;OV=k,\;UV=VB+UB=k+14\).

Applying Pythagoras' Theorem:

\[ OU^2+OV^2=UV^2 \] \[ 20^2+k^2=(k+14)^2 \]
Hint: The same relation also came from the in-radius formula, but here we go directly with \(UV=VB+UB=k+14\).
Step 31A

Expand and solve:

\[ 400+k^2=k^2+28k+196 \] \[ 400-196=28k \] \[ 204=28k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac{204}{28}=7.28\ldots \]

Wait — check the in-radius relation \(r=\dfrac{p+q-c}{2}\) with \(r=6\): \(UV=k+8\), not \(k+14\). Re-deriving correctly:

Using the in-centre \((6,6)\), the touch point on \(OU\) is at \(C=(6,0)\) so \(UC=OU-OC=20-6=14\). And the tangent point on \(OV\) is \(A=(0,6)\) so \(VA=OV-OA=k-6\). Hence \(UV=VB+UB=VA+UC=(k-6)+14=k+8\).

\[ 20^2+k^2=(k+8)^2 \] \[ 400+k^2=k^2+16k+64 \] \[ 400-64=16k \;\Longrightarrow\; k=21 . \]
Hint: \(OA\) is also \(6\), so \(VA = k-6\), and \(UV=(k-6)+14=k+8\). Always subtract the radius from the leg before adding up the hypotenuse.
Answer: \(k=21\), so \(OV=21\) and \(UV=29\).

Step 11M

We now know \(O=(0,0)\), \(U=(20,0)\), \(V=(0,21)\). Since \(OU\perp OV\), the triangle is right-angled at \(O\).

Take base \(OU=20\) and the corresponding height \(OV=21\).

Hint: A right-angled triangle is the easiest case: the two legs are automatically a base and the matching height.
Step 21A
\[ \text{Area}=\frac12\times OU\times OV=\frac12\times 20\times 21=210. \]
Answer: Area \(=210\) → Option C.

📘 Summary of Answers

  • In-centre of \(\triangle OUV\) is \(\;\; I=(6,\,6)\)  ⟹  in-radius \(r=6\).
  • Tangent lengths: \(\;OC=OA=6,\; UC=14,\; UB=14\).
  • Hypotenuse: \(\;UV=VB+UB=VA+UC=(k-6)+14=k+8\).
  • Pythagoras: \(\;20^2+k^2=(k+8)^2 \Rightarrow \boxed{k=21}\).
  • Triangle legs: \(\;OU=20,\; OV=21\); area \(\;=\tfrac12\times 20\times 21=\boxed{210}\).
  • Correct choice: C. 210.
