Circle Geometry — Tangent, Angle at Centre & Alternate Segment

Question

In the figure, \(O\) is the centre of the circle \(ABC\). \(DE\) is the tangent to the circle at \(A\). If \(AB\) is the angle bisector of \(\angle CAE\), then \(\angle ACO =\)

A   \(28^\circ\)
B   \(26^\circ\)
C   \(31^\circ\)
D   \(34^\circ\) ✓

Also given in the figure: \(\angle BOC = 124^\circ\).

Interactive Figure

Drag the sliders to move the points and watch how the angles respond. The relation \(\angle ACO = \angle ACB - \angle OCB\) always holds as long as \(AB\) bisects \(\angle CAE\).

\(\angle BAC = \frac{1}{2}\angle BOC =\) 62° \(\angle ACB = \angle BAE =\) 62° \(\angle ACO =\) 34°

Part (a) — Find \(\angle BAC\) from the angle at the centre

Step 1
\[\angle BOC = 2\angle BAC \quad (\angle \text{ at centre} = 2\times \angle \text{ at circumference})\]
Hint: \(\angle BOC\) is the angle at the centre subtended by arc \(BC\); \(\angle BAC\) is the angle at the circumference on the same arc.
Step 2
\[\angle BAC = \frac{124^\circ}{2} = 62^\circ\]
Answer: \(\angle BAC = 62^\circ\)
0 / 2

Part (b) — Use the angle bisector and alternate segment theorem

Step 1
Since \(AB\) bisects \(\angle CAE\),
\[\angle BAE = \angle BAC = 62^\circ\]
Hint: An angle bisector splits an angle into two equal parts.
Step 2
By the alternate segment theorem (tangent \(DE\) at \(A\)):
\[\angle ACB = \angle BAE = 62^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in alt. segment})\]
Hint: The angle between the tangent and the chord \(AB\) equals the angle in the alternate segment subtended by \(AB\).
So \(\angle ACB = 62^\circ\)
0 / 2

Part (c) — Work in the isosceles triangle \(BOC\)

Step 1
\(OB = OC\) (radii), so \(\triangle BOC\) is isosceles and the base angles are equal:
\[\angle OCB = \angle OBC\]
Hint: Two radii always give an isosceles triangle.
Step 2
Use the angle sum of \(\triangle BOC\):
\[\angle OCB = \frac{180^\circ - \angle BOC}{2} = \frac{180^\circ - 124^\circ}{2} = \frac{56^\circ}{2} = 28^\circ\]
So \(\angle OCB = 28^\circ\)
0 / 2

Part (d) — Combine everything to get \(\angle ACO\)

\(\angle ACB\) \(\angle OCB\) \(\angle ACO = \angle ACB - \angle OCB\)
Step 1
In the figure, \(CO\) lies inside \(\angle ACB\), so:
\[\angle ACO = \angle ACB - \angle OCB\]
Hint: Split the larger angle into the two parts separated by the radius \(OC\).
Step 2
\[\angle ACO = 62^\circ - 28^\circ = 34^\circ\]
Answer: \(\angle ACO = 34^\circ\) → Option D
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle BAC = \dfrac{124^\circ}{2} = \mathbf{62^\circ}\)  (\(\angle\) at centre \(=2\times\angle\) at circumference)
  • \(\angle BAE = \angle BAC = \mathbf{62^\circ}\)  (\(AB\) bisects \(\angle CAE\))
  • \(\angle ACB = \angle BAE = \mathbf{62^\circ}\)  (\(\angle\) in alternate segment)
  • \(\angle OCB = \dfrac{180^\circ-124^\circ}{2} = \mathbf{28^\circ}\)  (base \(\angle\)s, isosceles \(\triangle BOC\))
  • \(\angle ACO = 62^\circ - 28^\circ = \mathbf{34^\circ}\)  → Answer: D
