Mensuration — areas of triangles sharing a common height

Area of Triangle Perpendicular Height Linear Equations Pythagoras' Theorem

QUESTION Full Question

In the figure, \(D\) is a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(BD\) is perpendicular to \(AC\). It is given that \(AC = 14\text{ cm}\) and \(BD = 12\text{ cm}\). If the area of \(\triangle ABD\) is greater than the area of \(\triangle BCD\) by \(24\text{ cm}^2\), then the perimeter of \(\triangle ABC\) is

  • A. 30 cm
  • B. 42 cm
  • C. 54 cm
  • D. 84 cm

Note: \(AC = 14\) cm is a fixed length. Both triangles \(\triangle ABD\) and \(\triangle BCD\) share the same height \(BD = 12\) cm, so the difference of their areas is controlled by the base lengths \(AD\) and \(CD\) only.

Original Figure

PART (a) Set up the area equation to find \(AD\)

4.0 cm
\(AD\) = 4.0 cm
\(CD = 14 - AD\) = 10.0 cm
Area \(\triangle ABD\) = \(\tfrac12(12)AD\) = 24.0 cm²
Area \(\triangle BCD\) = \(\tfrac12(12)CD\) = 60.0 cm²
Difference (A₁−A₂) = −36.0 cm²

Drag the slider: the target is a difference of \(\mathbf{+24\ cm^2}\). Move \(AD\) until the readout turns positive 24.

STEP 1
Both triangles have the same perpendicular height \(BD = 12\) cm. Therefore \[ \text{Area of } \triangle ABD = \tfrac12 \cdot AD \cdot BD = \tfrac12 (12)AD = 6\,AD \] \[ \text{Area of } \triangle BCD = \tfrac12 \cdot CD \cdot BD = \tfrac12 (12)(14-AD) = 6(14-AD) \]
Hint: The two areas only differ because the bases \(AD\) and \(CD\) differ — the height \(12\) cm is common to both.
STEP 2
The area of \(\triangle ABD\) exceeds that of \(\triangle BCD\) by \(24\text{ cm}^2\): \[ 6\,AD - 6(14-AD) = 24 \] \[ 6AD - 84 + 6AD = 24 \] \[ 12\,AD = 108 \]
Hint: Expand carefully — the minus sign before \(6(14-AD)\) flips the sign of both terms inside.
STEP 3
\[ AD = 9\text{ cm} \] (Consistent with the model diagram: \(\tfrac12(9)(12) - \tfrac12(5)(12) = 54 - 30 = 24\ \checkmark\))
Answer: \(AD = 9\text{ cm}\)
0 / 3

PART (b) Find \(CD\), then \(AB\) and \(BC\)

\(CD = 14 - 9\) = 5 cm
\(AB=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}\) = 15 cm
\(BC=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}\) = 13 cm
Perimeter \(= 15+13+14\) = 42 cm
STEP 1
Since \(D\) lies on \(AC\) and \(AC = 14\) cm: \[ CD = AC - AD = 14 - 9 = 5\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(D\) splits \(AC\) into two segments that must add to 14.
STEP 2
In right-angled \(\triangle ABD\) (right angle at \(D\), since \(BD \perp AC\)): \[ AB = \sqrt{AD^2 + BD^2} = \sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{81+144} = \sqrt{225} = 15\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \((9,12,15)\) is a scaled \(3\text{-}4\text{-}5\) Pythagorean triple — scale factor 3.
STEP 3
In right-angled \(\triangle BCD\) (right angle at \(D\)): \[ BC = \sqrt{CD^2 + BD^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25+144} = \sqrt{169} = 13\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \((5,12,13)\) is a primitive Pythagorean triple.
STEP 4
Perimeter of \(\triangle ABC\): \[ AB + BC + AC = 15 + 13 + 14 = 42\text{ cm} \]
Answer: Perimeter \(= 42\text{ cm}\)  →  Option B
0 / 4

Summary of Answers

  • Use the common height \(BD=12\): \(\text{Area}=\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times 12\).
  • Area difference: \(\;6\,AD - 6(14 - AD) = 24\)
  • \(12\,AD = 108 \;\Rightarrow\; AD = \mathbf{9\ \text{cm}}\)
  • \(CD = 14 - 9 = \mathbf{5\ \text{cm}}\)
  • \(AB = \sqrt{9^2+12^2} = \mathbf{15\ \text{cm}}\)
  • \(BC = \sqrt{5^2+12^2} = \mathbf{13\ \text{cm}}\)
  • Perimeter \(= 15 + 13 + 14 = \mathbf{42\ \text{cm}}\)
  • Correct choice: Option B — 42 cm
