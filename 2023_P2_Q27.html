Question 2023MC#27 Multiple Choice

The equations of the circles \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are

\[ x^{2}+y^{2}+7x-4y+15=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 2x^{2}+2y^{2}-2x-16y-17=0 \]

respectively. Let \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) be the centres of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) respectively. Denote the origin by \(O\). Which of the following are true?

  1. \(\Delta OG_1G_2\) is an equilateral triangle.
  2. The line segment \(OG_1\) lies inside \(C_2\).
  3. \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) intersect at two distinct points.
    4. >
    A.  I and II only
    B.  I and III only
    C.  II and III only
    D.  I, II and III

Part A Finding the centres & radii of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\)

Step 1Centre of \(C_1\)

For a circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\), the centre is \(\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)\).

\[ C_1:\; x^{2}+y^{2}+7x-4y+15=0 \] \[ G_1=\left(-\frac{7}{2},\,-\frac{-4}{2}\right)=\left(-\frac72,\,2\right) \]
Hint: Take the coefficient of \(x\) (here \(7\)) and halve it with a minus sign — that gives the \(x\)-coordinate of the centre. Do the same with the coefficient of \(y\) (\(-4\)).
Step 2Radius of \(C_1\)

Radius: \(r=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\dfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}\).

\[ r_1=\sqrt{\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{-4}{2}\right)^{2}-15} =\sqrt{\frac{49}{4}+4-15} =\sqrt{\frac{49}{4}-11} =\sqrt{\frac{5}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt5}{2} \]
Hint: Convert everything to quarters: \(\frac{49}{4}-\frac{44}{4}=\frac54\). Always simplify to a single fraction before taking the square root.
\(G_1=\left(-\dfrac72,\,2\right)\) and \(r_1=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}\approx 1.118\)
Step 3Simplify \(C_2\) first

The leading coefficients of \(C_2\) are both \(2\), so divide the whole equation by \(2\):

\[ 2x^{2}+2y^{2}-2x-16y-17=0 \] \[ \Rightarrow\; x^{2}+y^{2}-x-8y-\frac{17}{2}=0 \]
Hint: The circle formula needs coefficient \(1\) on \(x^2\) and \(y^2\). Forgetting this division is the most common mistake here — it would give a wrong centre and radius.
Step 4Centre & radius of \(C_2\)
\[ G_2=\left(-\frac{-1}{2},\,-\frac{-8}{2}\right)=\left(\frac12,\,4\right) \] \[ r_2=\sqrt{\left(\frac{-1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{-8}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(-\frac{17}{2}\right)} =\sqrt{\frac14+16+\frac{17}{2}} =\sqrt{\frac{1+64+34}{4}} =\sqrt{\frac{99}{4}}=\frac{3\sqrt{11}}{2} \]
Hint: \(F=-\frac{17}{2}\), so \(-F=+\frac{17}{2}\). Watch the double negative!
\(G_2=\left(\dfrac12,\,4\right)\) and \(r_2=\dfrac{\sqrt{99}}{2}=\dfrac{3\sqrt{11}}{2}\approx 4.975\)

Part B Statement I ✗ — Is \(\Delta OG_1G_2\) equilateral?

Step 1Length \(OG_1\)

Use the distance formula from \(O(0,0)\) to \(G_1\!\left(-\frac72,2\right)\):

\[ OG_1=\sqrt{\left(-\frac72-0\right)^{2}+(2-0)^{2}} =\sqrt{\frac{49}{4}+4}=\sqrt{\frac{65}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031 \]
Hint: Squaring removes the minus sign — \(OG_1\) is a length, so it must be positive.
Step 2Length \(G_1G_2\)
\[ G_1G_2=\sqrt{\left(\frac12-\left(-\frac72\right)\right)^{2}+(4-2)^{2}} =\sqrt{4^{2}+2^{2}}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\approx 4.472 \]
Hint: The horizontal gap is \(\frac12+\frac72=4\); the vertical gap is \(4-2=2\). Form a right triangle: \(\sqrt{4^2+2^2}\).
Step 3Compare the sides I is FALSE ✗
\[ OG_1=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031, \qquad G_1G_2=\sqrt{20}\approx 4.472 \]

Note that \(OG_1\neq G_1G_2\).

\(\therefore \Delta OG_1G_2\) is not an equilateral triangle, so statement I is false.
Hint: For an equilateral triangle you must check that all three sides are equal. Finding one unequal pair is already enough to reject it.

Part C Statement II ✓ — Does \(OG_1\) lie inside \(C_2\)?

Step 1Distance \(OG_2\)
\[ OG_2=\sqrt{\left(\frac12-0\right)^{2}+(4-0)^{2}} =\sqrt{\frac14+16}=\sqrt{\frac{65}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2} \]
Hint: Interesting — \(OG_2\) has the same value as \(OG_1\)! That's because \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) lie on the same circle centred at \(O\).
Step 2Compare with \(r_2\)

From Part A: \(r_2=\dfrac{\sqrt{99}}{2}\approx 4.975\).

\[ OG_2=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031 \quad\text{and}\quad G_1G_2=\sqrt{20}\approx 4.472 \] \[ \frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}<\frac{\sqrt{99}}{2}, \qquad \sqrt{20}<\frac{\sqrt{99}}{2} \]

Note that \(OG_2\) and \(G_1G_2\) are both smaller than the radius of \(C_2\).

Hint: Compare squares to avoid decimals: \(65<99\) and \(20<\frac{99}{4}\) because \(80<99\).
Step 3Conclusion II is TRUE

Both \(O\) and \(G_1\) lie inside \(C_2\).

\(\therefore\) The line segment \(OG_1\) lies inside \(C_2\), so statement II is true.
Hint: A circle is a convex region — if both endpoints of a segment are inside the circle, then the entire segment is inside it. So checking the two endpoints is enough.

Part D Statement III ✓ — Do \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) meet at two points?

Step 1Distance between the centres
\[ G_1G_2=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\approx 4.472 \]
Hint: We already computed this in Statement I. Reuse previous results — in an MC question, time is precious.
Step 2Difference & sum of radii
\[ r_2-r_1=\frac{\sqrt{99}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt5}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{99}-\sqrt5}{2}\approx 3.8569 \] \[ r_1+r_2=\frac{\sqrt5}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{99}}{2}=\frac{\sqrt5+\sqrt{99}}{2}\approx 6.0930 \]
Hint: Since \(\sqrt5\approx 2.236\) and \(\sqrt{99}\approx 9.950\), we get \(r_1\approx1.118\) and \(r_2\approx4.975\).
Step 3Apply the intersection test III is TRUE

Note that \(G_1G_2\) is greater than the difference of the radii of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) and is smaller than the sum of the radii of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\):

\[ 3.8569 \;<\; 4.4721 \;<\; 6.0930 \] \[ \Rightarrow\; |r_2-r_1| < G_1G_2 < r_1+r_2 \]
\(\therefore\; C_1\) and \(C_2\) intersect at two distinct points, so statement III is true.
Hint: Remember the three cases for two circles: \(d>r_1+r_2\) → separate; \(|r_1-r_2|two intersections; \(d<|r_1-r_2|\) → one inside the other.

Summary Answers at a glance

  • Centre of \(C_1\): \(G_1=\left(-\dfrac72,\,2\right)\)  ·  Radius: \(r_1=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}\approx 1.118\)
  • Centre of \(C_2\): \(G_2=\left(\dfrac12,\,4\right)\)  ·  Radius: \(r_2=\dfrac{\sqrt{99}}{2}\approx 4.975\)
  • Statement I: \(OG_1=\dfrac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\neq\sqrt{20}=G_1G_2\) → NOT equilateral ✗
  • Statement II: \(OG_2=\dfrac{\sqrt{65}}{2}segment \(OG_1\) inside \(C_2\)
  • Statement III: \(|r_2-r_1|\approx 3.857 < G_1G_2\approx 4.472 < r_1+r_2\approx 6.093\) → two intersection points
  • ∴ Only statements II and III are true → the answer is C.
