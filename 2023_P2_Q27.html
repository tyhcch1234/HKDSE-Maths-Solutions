Question

The equations of the circles $C_1$ and $C_2$ are

$$x^{2}+y^{2}+7x-4y+15=0 \quad\text{and}\quad 2x^{2}+2y^{2}-2x-16y-17=0$$

respectively. Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be the centres of $C_1$ and $C_2$ respectively. Denote the origin by $O$. Which of the following are true?

I. $\Delta OG_1G_2$ is an equilateral triangle.
II. The line segment $OG_1$ lies inside $C_2$.
III. $C_1$ and $C_2$ intersect at two distinct points.

  • A. I and II only
  • B. I and III only
  • C. II and III only
  • D. I, II and III
Step 1 · Centre & radius of $C_1$

Complete the square for $C_1$

$$x^{2}+y^{2}+7x-4y+15=0$$ $$\left(x+\tfrac{7}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y-2\right)^{2}=\left(\tfrac{7}{2}\right)^{2}+(-2)^{2}-15=\tfrac{5}{4}$$

So the centre is $G_1=\left(-\dfrac{7}{2},\,2\right)$ and the radius is $r_1=\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{4}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

The diagram shows $C_1$ (blue) centred at $G_1$ with its small radius.

Centre and Radius of a Circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\ \to\ \left(x+\tfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y+\tfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}=\tfrac{D^{2}+E^{2}}{4}-F\) Completing the square turns the general equation into centre and radius at once.
Step 2 · Centre & radius of $C_2$

Divide $C_2$ by 2, then complete the square

$$2x^{2}+2y^{2}-2x-16y-17=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{2}+y^{2}-x-8y-\tfrac{17}{2}=0$$

$$\left(x-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y-4\right)^{2} =\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+4^{2}+\tfrac{17}{2}=\tfrac{99}{4}$$

So the centre is $G_2=\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\,4\right)$ and the radius is $r_2=\sqrt{\dfrac{99}{4}}=\dfrac{3\sqrt{11}}{2}$.

$C_2$ (red) is the large circle passing near the origin.

Step 3 · Statement I — equilateral?

Compare the three side lengths of $\Delta OG_1G_2$

$$OG_1=\sqrt{\left(-\tfrac{7}{2}\right)^{2}+2^{2}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{49}{4}+4}=\sqrt{\tfrac{65}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031$$

$$OG_2=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+4^{2}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}+16}=\sqrt{\tfrac{65}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031$$

$$G_1G_2=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{7}{2}\right)^{2}+(4-2)^{2}}=\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{20}\approx 4.472$$

Since $G_1G_2\neq OG_1$, the triangle is isosceles but not equilateral. I is FALSE

Distance Formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) The three side lengths \(OG_{1}\), \(OG_{2}\), \(G_{1}G_{2}\) decide whether the triangle is equilateral.
Step 4 · Statement II — $OG_1$ inside $C_2$?

Check the distances from $G_2$ to $O$ and to $G_1$

$$OG_2=\frac{\sqrt{65}}{2}\approx 4.031 \;<\; r_2=\frac{3\sqrt{11}}{2}\approx 4.975$$

$$G_1G_2=\sqrt{20}\approx 4.472 \;<\; r_2\approx 4.975$$

Both $O$ and $G_1$ lie inside $C_2$. Since $C_2$ is a disc (convex), the whole segment $OG_1$ lies inside $C_2$. II is TRUE

Step 5 · Statement III — two intersections?

Compare $G_1G_2$ with $r_2-r_1$ and $r_2+r_1$

$$r_1=\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\approx 1.118,\qquad r_2=\frac{3\sqrt{11}}{2}\approx 4.975$$

$$r_2-r_1=\frac{3\sqrt{11}-\sqrt5}{2}\approx 3.857 \;<\; G_1G_2=\sqrt{20}\approx 4.472 \;<\; r_2+r_1=\frac{3\sqrt{11}+\sqrt5}{2}\approx 6.093$$

Since $r_2-r_1two distinct points. III is TRUE

The two cyan points on the diagram are the intersection points of $C_1$ and $C_2$.

Condition for Two Circles to Meet \(|r_{1}-r_{2}| Two distinct intersections occur exactly when the centre distance lies between the difference and the sum of the radii.
Step 6 · Conclusion

Select the correct option

I is false, II is true, III is true.

Final Answer
C  (II and III only)
