Statistics: Variance, Median, Mode

Question

Consider the following integers:
\(4,\ 7,\ 7,\ 7,\ 8,\ 11,\ 11,\ 13,\ x,\ y\)
Let \(u\), \(v\) and \(w\) be the variance, the median and the mode of the above integers respectively. If both the mean and the range of the above integers are \(9\), which of the following must be true?

I. \(u<7\)
II. \(v>7\)
III. \(w=7\)

  • A. I only
  • B. II only
  • C. I and III only
  • D. II and III only

Solution

Mean
Mean of a Data Set \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\text{sum of all data}}{\text{number of data}}\) With ten data values and mean \(9\), the total is \(9\times 10=90\), so the two unknown values satisfy \(x+y=90-68=22\).
Mean \(=9\Rightarrow\) sum \(=90\Rightarrow x+y=90-68=22\).
Range
Range of a Data Set \(\text{range}=\text{maximum}-\text{minimum}\) A range of \(9\) with \(4\) and \(13\) both present forces every data value into the interval \([4,13]\), which is why only \(\{9,13\},\{10,12\},\{11,11\}\) remain.
Range \(=9\) with \(4,13\) present \(\Rightarrow\) all values in \([4,13]\). So \(\{x,y\}=\{9,13\},\{10,12\}\) or \(\{11,11\}\).
Median v \(\{9,13\}\!\to 8.5\), \(\{10,12\}\!\to 9\), \(\{11,11\}\!\to 9.5\). Always \(v>7\). (II true)
Mode w \(7\) occurs 3 times, but \(\{11,11\}\) makes \(11\) occur 4 times (mode \(11\)). So \(w=7\) is not guaranteed.
(III false)
Variance u
Population Variance \(u=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_i-\bar{x}\right)^{2}\) The three candidate pairs give \(u=7.8\), \(7.2\) and \(7.0\) — all at least \(7\) but not always greater than \(7\), so statement I is false.
\(u=\tfrac{\sum(x-9)^2}{10}\): \(\{9,13\}\!\to 7.8\), \(\{10,12\}\!\to 7.2\), \(\{11,11\}\!\to 7.0\). Always \(u\ge 7\). (I false)
Conclusion Only II must be true.
Answer: B — II only

Question. The mean of the nine numbers \(4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 9,\ 9,\ 12,\ 13,\ x,\ y\) is \(9\), where \(x\le y\) are positive integers. The data have minimum \(4\), maximum \(13\) and range \(9\).
(a) Find \(x+y\).
(b) List the possible unordered pairs \(\{x,y\}\).
(c) Show that the median of the data is always \(9\). (3 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Mean \(=9\) with \(9\) data values, so the total is \(9\times 9=81\).
Step 2. The seven known values add up to \(61\), hence \(x+y=81-61=20\).
Step 3. Range \(=9\) with minimum \(4\) and maximum \(13\) means every value lies in \([4,13]\). The pairs with \(x\le y\) summing to \(20\) in that interval are \(\{7,13\}\), \(\{8,12\}\), \(\{9,11\}\) and \(\{10,10\}\).
Step 4. Sort each data set of \(9\) values;
the median is the \(5^{\text{th}}\) value.
Each case gives \(4,6,8,\dots,13\) with \(9\) in the middle, so the median is \(9\) in every case.
Step 5. The median is therefore always \(9\) — it does not depend on which pair occurs.
Answer: (a) \(x+y=20\); (b) \(\{7,13\},\ \{8,12\},\ \{9,11\},\ \{10,10\}\); (c) the \(5^{\text{th}}\) of the \(9\) ordered values is \(9\) in each case, so the median is always \(9\)
