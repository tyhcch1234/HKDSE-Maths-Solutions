2017 MC #18

In the figure, $AB = BC$ and $D$ is a point lying on $AE$ such that $AC = AD$. If $AE \parallel BC$, then $\angle ABC =$

A. $44^\circ$
B. $56^\circ$
C. $62^\circ$
D. $68^\circ$
STEP 1 · Angles on a straight line at D

$A$, $D$, $E$ are collinear, and $\angle CDE = 124^\circ$.

So at the point $D$ on the straight line $AE$:

$$\angle ADC = 180^\circ - \angle CDE = 180^\circ - 124^\circ = 56^\circ$$

(adj. $\angle$s on st. line)

STEP 2 · Isosceles $\triangle ACD$  AC = AD

In $\triangle ACD$, we are given $AC = AD$, so the base angles are equal:

$$\angle ACD = \angle ADC = 56^\circ$$

(base $\angle$s, isos. $\triangle$)

STEP 3 · Corresponding angles (AE ∥ BC)

Since $AE \parallel BC$, the angle $\angle CDE$ at $D$ equals the angle $\angle BCD$ at $C$ (both measured on the same side, with $CD$ as transversal):

$$\angle BCD = \angle CDE = 124^\circ$$

(corr. $\angle$s, $BC \parallel AE$)

STEP 4 · Find $\angle ACB$ at vertex C

The ray $CA$ lies inside the angle $\angle BCD$, so subtract:

$$\angle ACB = \angle BCD - \angle ACD = 124^\circ - 56^\circ = 68^\circ$$

(angle addition / subtraction at a point)

STEP 5 · Isosceles $\triangle ABC$  AB = BC

In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = BC$, so the base angles at $A$ and $C$ are equal:

$$\angle CAB = \angle ACB = 68^\circ$$

(base $\angle$s, isos. $\triangle$)

💡 Tip: The equal sides $AB$ and $BC$ meet at $B$, so the base is $AC$ — the equal angles are the ones at $A$ and $C$.
STEP 6 · Angle sum of $\triangle ABC$

$$\angle ABC = 180^\circ - \angle ACB - \angle CAB$$

$$= 180^\circ - 68^\circ - 68^\circ = 44^\circ$$

($\angle$ sum of $\triangle$)

Answer: A  ·  44°
