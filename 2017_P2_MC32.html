HKDSE 2017 · Paper 2 · MC32

Powers of 2 and Base 16

Question

\(8^{3} + 8^{19} =\)

  1. \(10000000000010_{16}\)
  2. \(20000000000020_{16}\)
  3. \(100000000000100_{16}\)
  4. \(200000000000200_{16}\)

Solution

Step 1 · Write in powers of 2 \[ 8^{3} = (2^{3})^{3} = 2^{9}, \qquad 8^{19} = (2^{3})^{19} = 2^{57}. \]
Step 2 · Convert \(2^{9}\) to base 16

Since \(16 = 2^{4}\),

\[ 2^{9} = 2 \times 2^{8} = 2 \times 16^{2}, \]

which is \(200_{16}\) — a digit \(2\) in the \(16^{2}\) place.

Step 3 · Convert \(2^{57}\) to base 16 \[ 2^{57} = 2 \times 2^{56} = 2 \times 16^{14}, \]

which in base 16 is a single digit \(2\) followed by \(14\) zeros (the \(2\) sits in the \(16^{14}\) place).

Step 4 · Add \[ 8^{3} + 8^{19} = 2\times16^{14} + 2\times16^{2}. \]

So the base-16 numeral has a digit \(2\) in the \(16^{14}\) place and a digit \(2\) in the \(16^{2}\) place, zeros elsewhere:

\[ 200000000000200_{16}. \]
Why not the other options? Options A and C have leading digit \(1\) (but both terms are even multiples, \(2\times16^{k}\)). Options A and B end in a single trailing zero, i.e. a \(16^{1}\) place, but \(2^{9}=2\times16^{2}\) ends in two zeros in base 16.
Final Answer: D. \(200000000000200_{16}\)
