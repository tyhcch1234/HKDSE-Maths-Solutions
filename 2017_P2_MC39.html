Trigonometry · Angle between a Line and a Plane
3-D Geometry Pythagoras' Theorem Projection Angle between Line & Plane

Question 4 marks

In the figure, \(AD\) is a vertical pole standing on the horizontal ground \(BCD\). If \(AB = 25\text{ m}\), \(AD = 15\text{ m}\), \(BC = 29\text{ m}\) and \(CD = 21\text{ m}\), find the angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\) correct to the nearest degree.
Options:   A. \(53^\circ\)    B. \(54^\circ\)    C. \(69^\circ\)    D. \(70^\circ\)

Below, the solution is broken into steps. Two interactive diagrams let you explore the vertical triangle \(ABD\) and the horizontal ground triangle \(BCD\).

Step 1 — Find \(BD\) in the vertical triangle \(ABD\) Working

\(AD\) = 15.0 m
\(AB\) = 25.0 m
\(BD\) = 20.0 m
\(\angle BAD\) = 36.9°
0 / 3
Step 11M
\(AD\) is a vertical pole, so \(AD \perp\) the ground plane and \(AD \perp BD\). Hence \(\triangle ABD\) is right-angled at \(D\). \[BD^2 = AB^2 - AD^2 = 25^2 - 15^2 = 625 - 225 = 400\] \[BD = 20\text{ m}\]
Hint: A vertical pole is perpendicular to every line on the horizontal ground passing through its foot \(D\).
\(BD = \mathbf{20\text{ m}}\)
Step 21A
We now have all three sides of the vertical right triangle: \[AD = 15,\quad AB = 25,\quad BD = 20.\]
Hint: Keep these for the next step — we need \(D\), the foot of the perpendicular from \(B\) to the plane.
Triangle \(ABD\): \(15\)–\(20\)–\(25\) (a \(3\)–\(4\)–\(5\) triangle scaled by 5).
Step 3—
The diagram on the right shows the vertical cross-section \(ABD\). Drag the slider to see how \(\angle BAD\) changes if \(AB\) changes, while keeping \(AD = 15\).
Hint: \(\cos\angle BAD = \dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{15}{25}\) gives \(\angle BAD \approx 53.1^\circ\) — this is the answer we are looking for, but we must first justify that \(D\) is indeed the projection of \(B\).
Vertical triangle established: \(AD \perp BD\).

Step 2 — Prove \(D\) is the projection of \(B\) onto plane \(ACD\) Key Idea

\(BD\) = 20.0 m
\(CD\) = 21.0 m
\(BC\) = 29.0 m
\(\angle BDC\) = 90.0°
0 / 3
Step 11M
In the horizontal triangle \(BCD\): \[BD^2 + CD^2 = 20^2 + 21^2 = 400 + 441 = 841\] \[BC^2 = 29^2 = 841\]
Hint: Compute both quantities separately before comparing.
\(BD^2 + CD^2 = BC^2 = 841\)
Step 21M
Since \(BD^2 + CD^2 = BC^2\), by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem, \(\angle BDC = 90^\circ\).
Hint: The largest side \(BC\) is opposite the largest angle, so \(\angle BDC\) is the right angle.
\(\angle BDC = \mathbf{90^\circ}\)
Step 31A
Now \(BD \perp CD\) and \(BD \perp AD\) (pole vertical). Also \(AD\) and \(CD\) are two intersecting lines in the plane \(ACD\). Therefore \(BD \perp\) plane \(ACD\). Hence \(D\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(B\) to the plane \(ACD\), and \(AD\) is the projection of \(AB\) onto the plane \(ACD\). \(\angle BAD\) is the angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\).
Hint: A line perpendicular to two intersecting lines in a plane is perpendicular to the whole plane.
The required angle is \(\angle BAD\).

Step 3 — Compute \(\angle BAD\) Final

\(AD\) = 15.0 m
\(AB\) = 25.0 m
\(\cos\angle BAD\) = 0.600
\(\angle BAD\) = 53.13° → 53°
0 / 2
Step 11M
In the right triangle \(ABD\) (right angle at \(D\)): \[\cos\angle BAD = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{15}{25} = 0.6\]
Hint: \(\angle BAD\) is between the hypotenuse \(AB\) and the leg \(AD\), so use \(\cos = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}\).
\(\cos\angle BAD = 0.6\)
Step 21A
\[\angle BAD = \cos^{-1}(0.6) \approx 53.13010235^\circ\] Rounded to the nearest degree: \[\angle BAD \approx 53^\circ\]
Hint: \(0.1301\ldots < 0.5\), so we round down to \(53^\circ\).
\(\angle BAD = \mathbf{53^\circ}\)  →  Answer A

Summary of Answers

  • Vertical triangle \(ABD\): \(BD = \sqrt{25^2 - 15^2} = \mathbf{20\text{ m}}\)
  • Horizontal triangle \(BCD\): \(20^2 + 21^2 = 29^2 \Rightarrow \angle BDC = \mathbf{90^\circ}\)
  • \(BD \perp AD\) and \(BD \perp CD\), so \(BD \perp\) plane \(ACD\); \(D\) is the projection of \(B\).
  • Required angle \(= \angle BAD = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{15}{25}\right) \approx \mathbf{53^\circ}\)
  • Correct option: A. \(53^\circ\)
