Quadratic Functions & Graphs — Discriminant, Roots and Intercepts
Quadratic Graph Discriminant x-intercepts y-intercept Factor Form

Q Question

Let \(h\) and \(k\) be real constants such that \(hk < 0\). Which of the following statements about the graph of \(y=(h-x)(k-x)\) are true?

I.  The graph opens upwards.
II. The graph has two \(x\)-intercepts.
III. The \(y\)-intercept of the graph is positive.

A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

Multiple Choice

I Statement I  —  The graph opens upwards

Interactive graph of \(y=(h-x)(k-x)\). Use sliders to change \(h\) and \(k\) (with \(hk<0\)).
2.0
-1.5
Step 1 1M

Expand the given expression:

\[ y = (h-x)(k-x) = hk - hx - kx + x^2 = x^2 - (h+k)x + hk \]
Hint: Rearrange into the standard form \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\). Identify the coefficients of \(x^2\), \(x\), and the constant term.
Step 2 1A

The coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(a = 1\).

Since \(1 > 0\), the parabola opens upwards.

Hint: For \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\): if \(a > 0\) the graph opens upwards (a “U” shape); if \(a < 0\) it opens downwards (a “∩” shape).
Statement I is TRUE. The graph opens upwards.

II Statement II  —  The graph has two \(x\)-intercepts

The same parabola, highlighting the \(x\)-intercepts at \(x = h\) and \(x = k\).
Step 1 1M

The \(x\)-intercepts occur where \(y = 0\):

\[ (h-x)(k-x) = 0 \implies x = h \ \text{ or } \ x = k \]
Hint: The factored form \((h-x)(k-x)\) immediately gives the roots — set each factor to zero.
Step 2 1M

Alternatively, consider the discriminant of \(x^2-(h+k)x+hk=0\):

\[ \Delta = [-(h+k)]^2 - 4(1)(hk) = (h+k)^2 - 4hk \]
Hint: The discriminant \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\). For two distinct real roots, we need \(\Delta > 0\).
Step 3 1M

Since \(hk < 0\), we have \(-4hk > 0\), therefore:

\[ \Delta = (h+k)^2 - 4hk > (h+k)^2 \ge 0 \]

Thus \(\Delta > 0\).

Hint: A perfect square \((h+k)^2\) is always \(\ge 0\). Adding a positive quantity \(-4hk\) makes \(\Delta\) strictly positive.
Step 4 1A

Since the discriminant is positive, the quadratic equation has two distinct real roots.

Hint: \(\Delta > 0 \Rightarrow\) two distinct \(x\)-intercepts; \(\Delta = 0 \Rightarrow\) one \(x\)-intercept (tangent); \(\Delta < 0 \Rightarrow\) no \(x\)-intercept.
Statement II is TRUE. The graph has two \(x\)-intercepts (\(x=h\) and \(x=k\)).
0 / 4

III Statement III  —  The \(y\)-intercept is positive

The \(y\)-intercept is the point \((0,\,hk)\), which lies below the \(x\)-axis since \(hk < 0\).
Step 1 1M

Put \(x = 0\) into \(y = (h-x)(k-x)\):

\[ y = (h-0)(k-0) = hk \]
Hint: The \(y\)-intercept is always found by substituting \(x = 0\).
Step 2 1A

Since it is given that \(hk < 0\), the \(y\)-intercept \(hk\) is negative — not positive.

Hint: A product of two non-zero numbers is negative only when the numbers have opposite signs. So one of \(h, k\) is positive and the other is negative.
Statement III is FALSE. The \(y\)-intercept is \(hk < 0\), which is negative.
0 / 2

📋 Summary of Results

  • Statement I is TRUE — coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(1 > 0\), so the graph opens upwards.
  • Statement II is TRUE — discriminant \(\Delta = (h+k)^2 - 4hk > 0\) since \(hk < 0\), so there are two distinct \(x\)-intercepts (at \(x = h\) and \(x = k\)).
  • Statement III is FALSE — the \(y\)-intercept is \(hk < 0\), which is negative.
✅ The correct option is  A. I and II only
