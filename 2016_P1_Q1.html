[2016 #1]

Simplify $\dfrac{(x^{8}y^{7})^{2}}{x^{5}y^{-6}}$ and express your answer with positive indices. [3 marks]

1 Expand the numerator — power of a power
$$(x^{8}y^{7})^{2} = x^{8\times 2}\,y^{7\times 2} = x^{16}y^{14}$$
Multiply the indices: (xᵃ)ᵇ = x^(a·b). This earns the first M mark.
2 Write the fraction with the numerator expanded
$$\frac{x^{16}y^{14}}{x^{5}y^{-6}} = x^{16-5}\,y^{14-(-6)}$$
Quotient law: divide powers of the same base by subtracting the index of the denominator.
3 Simplify each index carefully
$$x^{16-5}=x^{11}, \qquad y^{14-(-6)} = y^{14+6}=y^{20}$$
Watch the double negative: subtracting a negative index adds it. This is the second M mark.
4 State the final answer with positive indices
$$x^{11}y^{20}$$
Both indices 11 and 20 are positive, so no negative-index rewriting is needed. 1A mark.
Answer $$\frac{(x^{8}y^{7})^{2}}{x^{5}y^{-6}} = x^{11}y^{20}$$
