Question

The rectangular coordinates of the point \( Q \) are \( \left(4\sqrt{3},\,-4\right) \). If \( Q \) is rotated clockwise about the origin through \( 90^{\circ} \), then the polar coordinates of its image are

  • A \( \left(8,\,60^{\circ}\right) \)
  • B \( \left(8,\,120^{\circ}\right) \)
  • C \( \left(8,\,210^{\circ}\right) \)
  • D \( \left(8,\,240^{\circ}\right) \)

Solution

Step 1 · Plot Q

In the coordinate plane \( Q = (4\sqrt3,\,-4) \approx (6.93,\,-4) \). It lies in the fourth quadrant.

\[ r = \sqrt{(4\sqrt{3})^{2}+(-4)^{2}} = \sqrt{48+16} = 8. \]

Modulus of a Point \(r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\) For \(Q(4\sqrt{3},-4)\): \(r=\sqrt{48+16}=8\); a rotation leaves this distance unchanged.
Step 2 · Polar form of Q

With \( Q \) in the fourth quadrant and \( \tan\theta = \dfrac{-4}{4\sqrt{3}} = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \),

\[ \theta = 360^{\circ}-30^{\circ} = 330^{\circ}. \]

So \( Q = \left(8,\,330^{\circ}\right) \) in polar form.

Polar Coordinates of a Point \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}\ \text{with the quadrant of }(x,y)\) \(\tan\theta=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\) in the fourth quadrant gives \(\theta=360^{\circ}-30^{\circ}=330^{\circ}\).
Step 3 · Rotate clockwise by \(90^{\circ}\)

A clockwise rotation subtracts from the polar angle:

\[ 330^{\circ} - 90^{\circ} = 240^{\circ}. \]

The distance from the origin is unchanged, so the image is at \( \left(8,\,240^{\circ}\right) \), in the third quadrant.

Rotation about the Pole \((r,\theta)\ \text{rotated by }\alpha\ \text{gives}\ (r,\ \theta\pm\alpha)\) A clockwise quarter-turn subtracts \(90^{\circ}\): \(330^{\circ}-90^{\circ}=240^{\circ}\), with \(r=8\) kept.
Step 4 · Check the image

\[ Q' = \left(8\cos 240^{\circ},\, 8\sin 240^{\circ}\right) = \left(-4,\,-4\sqrt{3}\right)\approx(-4,\,-6.93). \]

This matches a clockwise quarter-turn of \( (4\sqrt3,\,-4) \): \( (x,y)\mapsto(y,-x) \).

Hint: In polar coordinates a rotation about the origin changes only the angle — the modulus \(r\) stays the same. Clockwise \(\Rightarrow\) subtract; anticlockwise \(\Rightarrow\) add.
Final Answer: D  \( \left(8,\,240^{\circ}\right) \)

Question. In a polar coordinate system, \(O\) is the pole. The rectangular coordinates of \(Q\) are \(\left(6\sqrt{3},\,-6\right)\). If \(Q\) is rotated clockwise about \(O\) through \(120^{\circ}\) to \(Q^{\prime}\), the polar coordinates of \(Q^{\prime}\) are A. \(\left(12,\,150^{\circ}\right)\) B. \(\left(12,\,210^{\circ}\right)\) C. \(\left(12,\,240^{\circ}\right)\) D. \(\left(12,\,330^{\circ}\right)\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(r=\sqrt{\left(6\sqrt{3}\right)^{2}+(-6)^{2}}=\sqrt{108+36}=12\).
Step 2. \(Q\) lies in the fourth quadrant and \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{-6}{6\sqrt{3}}=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\), so \(\theta=360^{\circ}-30^{\circ}=330^{\circ}\).
Step 3. A clockwise rotation subtracts from the polar angle: \(330^{\circ}-120^{\circ}=210^{\circ}\); the distance \(12\) is unchanged.
Step 4. Check: \(Q^{\prime}=\left(12\cos210^{\circ},\,12\sin210^{\circ}\right)=\left(-6\sqrt{3},\,-6\right)\), a half-turn image of \(Q\) about the pole \u2713.
Answer: B. \(\left(12,\,210^{\circ}\right)\)
