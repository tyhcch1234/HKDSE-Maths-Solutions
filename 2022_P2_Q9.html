[2022MC#9]
Let $g(x)=x^2+ax+b$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. If $g(x)$ is divisible by $x+2a$, find the remainder when $g(x)$ is divided by $x-2a$.
  • A. $-2a^2$
  • B. $0$
  • C. $2a^2$
  • D. $4a^2$
STEP 1 · Given information By the Remainder Theorem, $g(x)$ divisible by $x+2a$ means the remainder is $0$ when $x=-2a$: \[ g(-2a)=(-2a)^2+a(-2a)+b=4a^2-2a^2+b=2a^2+b=0. \] So we obtain the key relation \[ \boxed{\,b=-2a^{2}\,}. \]
Hint: Divisibility by $x+2a$ ⟺ $g(-2a)=0$ (put $x=-2a$).
STEP 2 · Set up the unknown remainder Let $r$ be the required remainder. Again by the Remainder Theorem, when $g(x)$ is divided by $x-2a$, the remainder is the value at $x=2a$: \[ r=g(2a)=4a^{2}+2a^{2}+b=6a^{2}+b. \]
Hint: The remainder on division by $x-2a$ is just $g(2a)$ — no long division needed.
STEP 3 · Use the symmetry trick The $x^2$ and constant terms cancel in $g(x)-g(-x)$, leaving only the odd part: \[ g(x)-g(-x)=\bigl(x^{2}+ax+b\bigr)-\bigl((-x)^{2}+a(-x)+b\bigr)=2ax. \] Now substitute $x=2a$: \[ g(2a)-g(-2a)=2a(2a)=4a^{2}. \]
Hint: $g(x)-g(-x)=2ax$, so $g(2a)=g(-2a)+4a^{2}$.
STEP 4 · Evaluate the remainder Putting the pieces together, with $g(-2a)=0$: \[ r-0=g(2a)-g(-2a)=4a^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=4a^{2}. \]
Hint: The constant $b$ cancels out, so the answer depends only on $a$.
STEP 5 · Check with a concrete value Take $a=1$: then $b=-2$, so $g(x)=x^{2}+x-2=(x+2)(x-1)$, which is indeed divisible by $x+2$. Dividing by $x-2$: $\;g(2)=4+2-2=4=4a^{2}$. ✓   This is option D.
Hint: Always verify with a numerical example when the answer has no $b$.
Answer: D  ·  the remainder is $4a^{2}$.
