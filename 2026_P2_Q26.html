Locus: Equal Distances to Two Lines

Question

The equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \(3x+5y=6\) and \(6x-10y=13\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_1\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_2\). The locus of \(P\) is

  • A. a point.
  • B. a circle.
  • C. a square.
  • D. a pair of straight lines.

Solution

Distance formula \(d_1=\dfrac{|3x+5y-6|}{\sqrt{34}},\quad d_2=\dfrac{|6x-10y-13|}{\sqrt{136}}=\dfrac{|6x-10y-13|}{2\sqrt{34}}\).
Set equal \(|3x+5y-6|=\tfrac12|6x-10y-13|\), i.e. \(2|3x+5y-6|=|6x-10y-13|\).
Resolve Removing the modulus gives two linear equations (the angle bisectors): \(y=-\tfrac1{20}\) and \(x=\tfrac{25}{12}\).
Conclusion The locus is a pair of straight lines.
Answer: D — a pair of straight lines
