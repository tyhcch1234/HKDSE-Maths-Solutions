QUESTION

The equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \(3x+5y=6\) and \(6x-10y=13\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_1\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_2\). The locus of \(P\) is

STEP 1 · DISTANCE FORMULA The perpendicular distance from \(P(x,y)\) to \(ax+by+c=0\) is \(\dfrac{|ax+by+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\). \[ d_1=\frac{|3x+5y-6|}{\sqrt{3^2+5^2}}=\frac{|3x+5y-6|}{\sqrt{34}}, \] \[ d_2=\frac{|6x-10y-13|}{\sqrt{6^2+(-10)^2}} =\frac{|6x-10y-13|}{\sqrt{136}} =\frac{|6x-10y-13|}{2\sqrt{34}}. \]
Distance from a Point to a Line \(d=\dfrac{|ax+by+c|}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}\) For \(3x+5y-6=0\) the distance is \(\dfrac{|3x+5y-6|}{\sqrt{34}}\).
STEP 2 · SET THE DISTANCES EQUAL Since \(d_1=d_2\): \[ \frac{|3x+5y-6|}{\sqrt{34}}=\frac{|6x-10y-13|}{2\sqrt{34}} \] \[ 2\,|3x+5y-6|=\bigl|6x-10y-13\bigr| . \]
STEP 3 · REMOVE THE MODULUS For \(|A|=|B|\) we get \(A=B\) or \(A=-B\). So \[ 2(3x+5y-6)=6x-10y-13 \;\;\text{or}\;\; 2(3x+5y-6)=-(6x-10y-13). \] First: \(6x+10y-12=6x-10y-13 \Rightarrow 20y=-1 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{1}{20}\).
Second: \(6x+10y-12=-6x+10y+13 \Rightarrow 12x=25 \Rightarrow x=\tfrac{25}{12}\).
Solving an Absolute-Value Equation \(|A|=|B|\Rightarrow A=B\ \text{or}\ A=-B\) The two cases give \(20y=-1\) and \(12x=25\).
STEP 4 · CONCLUSION The two equations \[ y=-\tfrac{1}{20} \qquad\text{and}\qquad x=\tfrac{25}{12} \] are both straight lines — they are the two angle bisectors of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\). Hence the locus of \(P\) is a pair of straight lines.
Locus Equidistant from Two Lines \(d_{1}=d_{2}\Rightarrow\text{two angle bisectors}\) Two distinct straight lines appear, so the locus of \(P\) is a pair of lines.
💡 Check the slopes: \(m_1=-\tfrac{3}{5}\) and \(m_2=\tfrac{6}{10}=\tfrac{3}{5}\). Since \(m_1m_2=-\tfrac{9}{25}\neq-1\), the lines are not perpendicular, so the two bisectors are not the same line — confirming two distinct straight lines.
✅ Answer: D — a pair of straight lines

Question. \(P\) is a moving point such that its perpendicular distance to \(L_{1}:4x-3y+5=0\) equals its perpendicular distance to \(L_{2}:12x+5y-10=0\). Describe the locus of \(P\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(d_{1}=\dfrac{|4x-3y+5|}{\sqrt{4^{2}+3^{2}}}=\dfrac{|4x-3y+5|}{5}\) and \(d_{2}=\dfrac{|12x+5y-10|}{\sqrt{12^{2}+5^{2}}}=\dfrac{|12x+5y-10|}{13}\).
Step 2. \(d_{1}=d_{2}\) gives \(13|4x-3y+5|=5|12x+5y-10|\).
Step 3. First case: \(13(4x-3y+5)=5(12x+5y-10)\), i.e. \(52x-39y+65=60x+25y-50\), so \(8x+64y-115=0\).
Step 4. Second case: \(13(4x-3y+5)=-5(12x+5y-10)\), i.e. \(52x-39y+65=-60x-25y+50\), so \(112x-14y+15=0\).
Step 5. Both are straight lines (the two angle bisectors), so the locus is the pair of lines \(8x+64y-115=0\) and \(112x-14y+15=0\).
Answer: the pair of straight lines \(8x+64y-115=0\) and \(112x-14y+15=0\)
