Linear relation between \(x^3\) and \(\sqrt{y}\) — finding \(y\) from a straight-line graph

Coordinate Geometry Linear Relation Two-Point Form Intercepts Multiple Choice

Q The Question

[2020 MC #34]

The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between \(x^{3}\) and \(\sqrt{y}\). If \(x=2\), then \(y=\)

  • A. \(3\)
  • B. \(8\)
  • C. \(9\)
  • D. \(33\)

The graph is a straight line with horizontal intercept \(-4\) (on the \(x^3\)-axis) and vertical intercept \(1\) (on the \(\sqrt{y}\)-axis).

Original Figure

A Reading the Intercepts from the Graph

First we identify the two key points marked on the axes of the given linear graph:

  • On the horizontal axis (labelled \(x^3\)): the line cuts at \(-4\).
  • On the vertical axis (labelled \(\sqrt{y}\)): the line cuts at \(1\).
0.25
Linear graph Intercept points Axes

Read‑outs: horizontal intercept \(=-4\), vertical intercept \(=1\).

Step 1

Read the two intercepts from the graph:

\[ \text{horizontal intercept}: \; x^3=-4, \qquad \sqrt{y}=0 \] \[ \text{vertical intercept}: \; x^3=0, \qquad \sqrt{y}=1 \]
Hint: On a \(x^3\)–\(\sqrt{y}\) graph, the horizontal coordinate is \(x^3\) (not \(x\)), and the vertical coordinate is \(\sqrt{y}\) (not \(y\)).
Intercepts Horizontal intercept at \((-4,\,0)\); vertical intercept at \((0,\,1)\).
0 / 1

B Form the Equation of the Line

With the two intercepts we can find the slope and form the equation of the straight line.

8.00
Line \(\sqrt{y}=0.25x^3+1\) Moving point Intercepts

Drag the slider: the moving point shows the corresponding \(\sqrt{y}\) value. At \(x^3=8\), \(\sqrt{y}=3\).

Step 1

Compute the slope using the two intercepts \((-4,\,0)\) and \((0,\,1)\):

\[ m=\frac{1-0}{0-(-4)}=\frac{1}{4}=0.25 \]
Hint: Slope \(=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}\). Here the line rises 1 unit while running 4 units to the right.
Result Slope \(m=\dfrac{1}{4}\).
Step 2

Using slope‑intercept form with vertical intercept \(1\):

\[ \sqrt{y}=\frac{1}{4}\,x^3+1 \]

Equivalently, multiply by 4:

\[ 4\sqrt{y}=x^3+4 \]
Hint: The general form is \(\sqrt{y}=m\,x^3+c\) where \(c\) is the vertical intercept.
Equation of the Line \[ \sqrt{y}=\tfrac{1}{4}x^3+1 \]

C Substitute \(x=2\) and Solve for \(y\)

Now substitute \(x=2\) into the linear relation and solve for \(y\).

2.0
Line \(\sqrt{y}=0.25x^3+1\) Point at \(x=2\) \(\sqrt{y}=3\) level

Live read‑out: when \(x=2\), \(x^3=8\), \(\sqrt{y}=0.25(8)+1=3\), so \(y=3^2=9\).

Step 1

Substitute \(x=2\) into the equation \(\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{4}x^3+1\):

\[ \sqrt{y}=\frac{1}{4}(2)^3+1 \] \[ \sqrt{y}=\frac{1}{4}(8)+1=2+1=3 \]
Hint: Compute \(x^3\) first: \(2^3=8\). Then apply the slope \(1/4\) and add the intercept \(1\).
Intermediate \(\sqrt{y}=3\)
Step 2

Square both sides to obtain \(y\):

\[ y = (\sqrt{y})^2 = 3^2 = 9 \]
Hint: Remember \(\sqrt{y}\) is the vertical coordinate, so to recover \(y\) we square it.
Answer \[ \boxed{y=9} \] Therefore the correct choice is C. \(9\).

Summary of Answers

  • A Horizontal intercept: \((-4,\,0)\)  ·  Vertical intercept: \((0,\,1)\)
  • B Slope \(m=\dfrac{1}{4}\)  ·  Equation \(\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{4}x^3+1\)
  • C At \(x=2\): \(\sqrt{y}=3\)  \(\Rightarrow\)  \(\boxed{y=9}\)

Correct option: C. \(9\)
