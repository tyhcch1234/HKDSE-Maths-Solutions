12. Coordinate Geometry

The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between $x^3$ and $\sqrt{y}$. If $x = 2$, then $y =$

A. 3
B. 8
C. 9
D. 33
STEP 1 · Read the intercepts

From the graph, the straight line cuts the horizontal axis at $x^3 = -4$ and the vertical axis at $\sqrt{y} = 1$.

Hence the two points on the line are:

$$A(-4,\ 0)\quad\text{and}\quad B(0,\ 1)$$

💡 Hint: The horizontal axis represents $x^3$ (not $x$) and the vertical axis represents $\sqrt{y}$ (not $y$).
STEP 2 · Find the slope
Slope-Intercept Form \(Y=mX+c\) Here the variables are \(X=x^{3}\) and \(Y=\sqrt{y}\), giving \(\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{4}x^{3}+1\).
Slope Formula \(m=\dfrac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\) With the two intercepts \(A(-4,0)\) and \(B(0,1)\): \(m=\dfrac{1}{4}\).

The slope of the line is

$$m=\dfrac{1-0}{0-(-4)}=\dfrac{1}{4}$$

So the equation of the line is

$$\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{4}\,x^3+1$$

STEP 3 · Substitute $x = 2$
Removing a Square Root \(\left(\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}=y\quad(y\ge 0)\) Once \(\sqrt{y}=3\), squaring gives \(y=9\).

Substitute $x=2$:

$$\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{4}(2)^3+1=\dfrac{8}{4}+1=2+1=3$$

Now square both sides to obtain $y$:

$$y=3^2=9$$

STEP 4 · Conclusion

Therefore when $x=2$, we get $y=9$.

Answer: C — 9

Question. The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between \(x^{3}\) and \(\sqrt{y}\). The straight line cuts the horizontal axis at \(x^{3}=-9\) and the vertical axis at \(\sqrt{y}=3\). If \(x=3\), then \(y=\) A. \(36\) B. \(81\) C. \(144\) D. \(169\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. From the intercepts the two points on the line are \((-9,\,0)\) and \((0,\,3)\); note the axes are \(x^{3}\) and \(\sqrt{y}\).
Step 2. Slope \(m=\dfrac{3-0}{0-(-9)}=\dfrac{1}{3}\), so the line is \(\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{3}x^{3}+3\).
Step 3. Put \(x=3\): \(\sqrt{y}=\dfrac{1}{3}(27)+3=9+3=12\).
Step 4. Square both sides: \(y=12^{2}=144\).
Answer: C. \(144\)
