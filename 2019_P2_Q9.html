Question
Let $k$ be a constant such that $2x^{4}+kx^{3}-4x-16$ is divisible by $2x+k$. Find $k$.
A. $-2$
B. $2$
C. $4$
D. $8$
1Identify the given information

Let $f(x)=2x^{4}+kx^{3}-4x-16$. The divisor is $2x+k$.

"Divisible by $2x+k$" means the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $2x+k$ is $0$.

Hint: The Factor Theorem says $f(a)=0$ whenever $(x-a)$ is a factor. Here the factor is linear but not monic.
2Find the zero of the divisor

Set the divisor to zero: $2x+k=0$

$\Rightarrow\; 2x=-k \;\Rightarrow\; x=-\dfrac{k}{2}$.

Hint: This value $-\frac{k}{2}$ is the root we substitute into $f(x)$.
Zero of a Linear Divisor \(ax+b=0\Rightarrow x=-\dfrac{b}{a}\) The divisor \(2x+k\) is zero at \(x=-\dfrac{k}{2}\), the value to substitute.
3Apply the Factor / Remainder Theorem

Since $2x+k$ is a factor, $f\!\left(-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)=0$:

$$2\left(-\frac{k}{2}\right)^{4}+k\left(-\frac{k}{2}\right)^{3}-4\left(-\frac{k}{2}\right)-16=0$$

Simplify each term:

$$2\cdot\frac{k^{4}}{16}+k\left(-\frac{k^{3}}{8}\right)+2k-16=0$$

$$\frac{k^{4}}{8}-\frac{k^{4}}{8}+2k-16=0$$

Hint: Notice the two $k^4$ terms cancel — that's the key simplification!
Factor Theorem \((ax+b)\text{ is a factor of } f(x)\iff f\!\left(-\dfrac{b}{a}\right)=0\) Divisibility by \(2x+k\) forces \(f\!\left(-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)=0\), where the \(k^{4}\) terms cancel.
4Solve for $k$

The quartic terms cancel, leaving:

$$2k-16=0$$

$$2k=16 \;\Rightarrow\; k=8$$

Hint: Always check your answer back in the original to confirm the remainder is exactly zero.
Answer: D. $k=8$

Question. Let \(k\) be a constant such that \(3x^{4}+kx^{3}-6x-24\) is divisible by \(3x+k\). Find \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Set the divisor to zero: \(3x+k=0\) gives \(x=-\dfrac{k}{3}\).
Step 2. By the Factor Theorem, \(f\!\left(-\dfrac{k}{3}\right)=0\) for \(f(x)=3x^{4}+kx^{3}-6x-24\).
Step 3. Substitute: \(3\cdot\dfrac{k^{4}}{81}+k\left(-\dfrac{k^{3}}{27}\right)-6\left(-\dfrac{k}{3}\right)-24=0\), i.e. \(\dfrac{k^{4}}{27}-\dfrac{k^{4}}{27}+2k-24=0\).
Step 4. The quartic terms cancel, leaving \(2k=24\), so \(k=12\).
Answer: \(k=12\)
