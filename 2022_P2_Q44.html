Question [2022MC#44]

In a test, the median of the test scores of a class of students is 30 marks. All the students fail in the test, so the test score of each student is adjusted such that each score is increased by 50% and then extra 8 marks are added. Let $x$ marks be the median of the test scores of the class of students after the score adjustment. In the test, the standard score of a student before the score adjustment is $-2$.

Denote the standard score of this student after the score adjustment by $z$. Find $x$ and $z$.

  • A. $x=45$ and $z=-2$
  • B. $x=45$ and $z=-1$
  • C. $x=53$ and $z=-2$
  • D. $x=53$ and $z=-1$
1Effect of the transformation on the median
Median under a Linear Transformation \(y=ax+b\ \Rightarrow\ \text{median}(y)=a\cdot\text{median}(x)+b\quad(a>0)\) Every score, including the median, is multiplied by \(1.5\) then increased by \(8\): \(30\times1.5+8=53\).

Every score is multiplied by $(1+50\%)$ and then 8 is added, so every value (including the median) is transformed the same way:

$$x = 30 \times (1+50\%) + 8 = 30 \times 1.5 + 8 = 45 + 8 = 53.$$

Hint: the median is just another data value under a uniform transformation — apply the same map to it.

2Setup: mean and standard deviation
Standard Score \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\) A standard score of \(-2\) means the raw score equals \(\mu-2\sigma\).

Let $\mu$ and $\sigma$ be the mean and the standard deviation of the test scores before the adjustment. The student's raw score is then

$$\frac{\text{score}-\mu}{\sigma}=-2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{score}=\mu-2\sigma.$$

Hint: standard score $z=\dfrac{\text{value}-\text{mean}}{\text{standard deviation}}$.

3New mean and new standard deviation
Effect of a Linear Transformation on Mean and Standard Deviation \(\mu^{\prime}=a\mu+b,\qquad \sigma^{\prime}=a\,\sigma\quad(a>0)\) Scaling by \(1.5\) multiplies both the mean and the standard deviation by \(1.5\), while adding \(8\) shifts only the mean.

Multiplying every score by $1+50\%$ multiplies both $\mu$ and $\sigma$ by $(1+50\%)$. Adding 8 to every score adds 8 to the mean but leaves the standard deviation unchanged.

$$\mu' = \mu(1+50\%)+8,\qquad \sigma' = \sigma(1+50\%).$$

Hint: shifts do not change spread, but scaling does.

4Compute the new standard score $z$

The student's adjusted score is $(\mu-2\sigma)(1+50\%)+8$, so

$$z=\frac{[(\mu-2\sigma)(1+50\%)+8]-\mu'}{\sigma'}.$$

Substitute $\mu'=\mu(1+50\%)+8$ and $\sigma'=\sigma(1+50\%)$:

$$z=\frac{[(\mu-2\sigma)(1+50\%)+8]-[\mu(1+50\%)+8]}{\sigma(1+50\%)} =\frac{-2\sigma(1+50\%)}{\sigma(1+50\%)}=-2.$$

Hint: the $+8$'s cancel, and the factor $(1+50\%)$ cancels top and bottom — so the standard score is unchanged.

5Conclusion

The median becomes $x=53$ and the student's standard score remains $z=-2$.

Hint: a linear transformation $y=ax+b$ with $a>0$ maps every standard score to itself.

Answer: $x = 53$ and $z = -2$ → Option C.

Question. In a test, every score is increased by \(20\%\) and then \(5\) marks are added. If the median score before the adjustment is \(40\) and a student has standard score \(-1.5\) before the adjustment, then the new median and the student\u2019s new standard score are A. \(48\) and \(-1.5\) B. \(53\) and \(-1.5\) C. \(53\) and \(-1.8\) D. \(45\) and \(-1.5\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The median is a data value,
so it is transformed the same way: \(40\times(1+20\%)+5=48+5=53\).
Step 2. New mean \(\mu^{\prime}=1.2\mu+5\), and new standard deviation \(\sigma^{\prime}=1.2\sigma\) because adding \(5\) does not change the spread.
Step 3. New standard score \(z^{\prime}=\dfrac{(1.2x+5)-(1.2\mu+5)}{1.2\sigma}=\dfrac{1.2(x-\mu)}{1.2\sigma}=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\).
Step 4. So \(z^{\prime}=-1.5\) is unchanged: scaling by \(1.2\) and shifting by \(5\) leave every standard score as it was.
Answer: B. new median \(53\), new standard score \(-1.5\)
