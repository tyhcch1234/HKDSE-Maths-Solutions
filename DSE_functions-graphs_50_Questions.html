Type 1

Function Notation, Values, Domain and Range · 7 questions

Evaluating f(x) and stating where it is defined

Core idea: \(f(x)\) is a machine: put a number (or an expression) in place of every \(x\) and evaluate. The domain is the set of allowed inputs — exclude values making a denominator zero, and for \(\sqrt{A}\) require \(A\ge 0\). The range is the set of outputs, best found from the vertex for a quadratic.

\(f(a)\): replace every \(x\) by \(a\)\(\frac{1}{g(x)}:\ g(x)\ne 0\)\(\sqrt{g(x)}:\ g(x)\ge 0\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q9 ↗

Let \(f(x)=3x^{2}-2x+1\). Find \(f(2)\).

Answer\(9\)
  1. Replace every \(x\) by 2: \(f(2)=3(2)^{2}-2(2)+1\).
  2. Evaluate the square first: \(3(4)-4+1\).
  3. \(=12-4+1=9\).
  4. Check: the terms are \(12\), \(-4\) and \(+1\), which total 9.
Q2★☆☆☆3 marks

Let \(g(x)=\frac{2x-5}{x+1}\). Find (a) \(g(3)\), (b) \(g(0)\).

Answer(a) \(\frac{1}{4}\)　(b) \(-5\)
  1. (a) \(g(3)=\frac{2(3)-5}{3+1}=\frac{6-5}{4}\).
  2. \(=\frac{1}{4}\).
  3. (b) \(g(0)=\frac{2(0)-5}{0+1}=\frac{-5}{1}=-5\).
  4. Check (a): \(4\times\frac{1}{4}=1\) and \(2(3)-5=1\). ✓
Common mistake

The denominator \(x+1\) makes \(x=-1\) illegal. Substituting \(x=-1\) would give division by zero, so \(g(-1)\) is undefined.

Q3★★☆☆4 marks

It is given that \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\), where \(f(1)=5\) and \(f(2)=14\). Find \(a\) and \(b\).

Answer\(a=2,\ b=3\)
  1. \(f(1)=a(1)^{2}+b(1)=a+b\), so \(a+b=5\).
  2. \(f(2)=a(2)^{2}+b(2)=4a+2b\), so \(4a+2b=14\), i.e. \(2a+b=7\).
  3. Subtract the first equation from this: \((2a+b)-(a+b)=7-5\), so \(a=2\).
  4. Then \(b=5-2=3\).
  5. Check: \(f(x)=2x^{2}+3x\) gives \(f(1)=5\) and \(f(2)=8+6=14\). ✓
Q4★★☆☆3 marks

Find the domain of each function.
(a) \(f(x)=\sqrt{x-3}\)
(b) \(g(x)=\frac{x+2}{x^{2}-9}\)

Answer(a) \(x\ge 3\)　(b) all real \(x\) except \(x=3\) and \(x=-3\)
  1. (a) A square root needs a non-negative argument: \(x-3\ge 0\), so \(x\ge 3\).
  2. (b) The denominator must not be zero: \(x^{2}-9\ne 0\), i.e. \((x-3)(x+3)\ne 0\).
  3. So \(x\ne 3\) and \(x\ne -3\).
  4. Every other real number is allowed, so the domain is all real \(x\) except \(\pm 3\).
Common mistake

In (a) the condition is \(\ge\), not \(\gt \): \(f(3)=\sqrt{0}=0\) is perfectly valid. In (b) both \(+3\) and \(-3\) must be excluded.

Q5★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

Let \(f(x)=x^{2}-4x+7\), where \(x\) is any real number. Find the range of \(f(x)\).

Answer\(f(x)\ge 3\), i.e. the range is \([3,\infty)\)
  1. Complete the square: \(x^{2}-4x+7=(x^{2}-4x+4)+3\).
  2. \(=(x-2)^{2}+3\).
  3. Since \((x-2)^{2}\ge 0\) for every real \(x\), the smallest value is 3 (at \(x=2\)).
  4. Hence \(f(x)\ge 3\) for all real \(x\); the range is \([3,\infty)\).
  5. Check: \(f(2)=4-8+7=3\) and \(f(0)=7\gt 3\). ✓
Common mistake

The range is not 'all real numbers'. A quadratic with a positive leading coefficient has a minimum, so its range is bounded below.

Q6★★★☆5 marks

Let \(f(x)=2x+3\) and \(g(x)=x^{2}-1\).
(a) Find \(f(g(x))\), simplifying your answer.
(b) Hence solve \(f(g(x))=19\).

Answer(a) \(2x^{2}+1\)　(b) \(x=3\ \text{or}\ x=-3\)
  1. (a) Put \(g(x)\) into \(f\): \(f(g(x))=2(g(x))+3=2(x^{2}-1)+3\).
  2. Expand: \(2x^{2}-2+3=2x^{2}+1\).
  3. (b) Set \(2x^{2}+1=19\): \(2x^{2}=18\), so \(x^{2}=9\).
  4. Hence \(x=3\) or \(x=-3\).
  5. Check: \(g(3)=8\), \(f(8)=19\). ✓
Common mistake

Do not expand \(f(g(x))\) as \(f(x)\times g(x)\). Composition means 'g first, then f'. And \(x^{2}=9\) has two solutions.

Q7★★★☆5 marks

Let \(f(x)=x^{2}+kx+3\), where \(k\) is a constant. If \(f(x+1)-f(x)=2x+5\) for all values of \(x\), find \(k\).

Answer\(k=4\)
  1. First \(f(x+1)=(x+1)^{2}+k(x+1)+3=x^{2}+2x+1+kx+k+3\).
  2. Subtract \(f(x)=x^{2}+kx+3\): the \(x^{2}\), \(kx\) and \(3\) terms cancel, leaving \(2x+1+k\).
  3. So \(2x+1+k=2x+5\) for all \(x\).
  4. Comparing constant terms: \(1+k=5\), hence \(k=4\).
  5. Check with \(k=4\): \(f(x)=x^{2}+4x+3\), \(f(x+1)-f(x)=(x^{2}+6x+8)-(x^{2}+4x+3)=2x+5\). ✓

Type 2

Transformations of Graphs · 7 questions

Translation, reflection and stretching of y = f(x)

Core idea: Every transformation is a statement about how each point moves. Inside the bracket means horizontal and works the 'wrong way round': \(f(x-h)\) shifts right by \(h\), \(f(x)+k\) shifts up by \(k\). A minus in front reflects in the \(x\)-axis; \(f(2x)\) squeezes horizontally by a factor 2.

\(y=f(x-h)+k\): right \(h\), up \(k\)\(y=-f(x)\): reflect in x-axis\(y=f(2x)\): halve every x-coordinate
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

The graph of \(y=x^{2}\) is transformed into the graph of \(y=(x-3)^{2}+2\). Describe the transformation.

AnswerTranslation to the right by 3 units and upwards by 2 units; the new vertex is \((3,2)\).
  1. Compare with the pattern \(y=f(x-h)+k\), where \(f(x)=x^{2}\).
  2. Here \(h=3\), which shifts the graph right by 3 units.
  3. Here \(k=2\), which shifts the graph up by 2 units.
  4. The vertex moves from \((0,0)\) to \((3,2)\).
Common mistake

\((x-3)\) means right, not left. The shift inside the bracket is opposite in sign to the number shown.

Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

The point \((2,5)\) lies on the graph of \(y=f(x)\). Find the coordinates of its image on
(a) \(y=f(x)+3\),
(b) \(y=f(x-4)\).

Answer(a) \((2,8)\)　(b) \((6,5)\)
  1. (a) Adding 3 outside the function moves every point up by 3: \((2,\ 5+3)=(2,8)\).
  2. (b) \(f(x-4)\) moves every point right by 4: \((2+4,\ 5)=(6,5)\).
  3. The shape and the \(y\)-value are unchanged by a horizontal shift.
  4. Check: the point that was at \(x=2\) is now at \(x=6\). ✓
Q10★★☆☆3 marks

The point \((3,-4)\) lies on the graph of \(y=f(x)\). Find the coordinates of its image on
(a) \(y=-f(x)\),
(b) \(y=f(2x)\).

Answer(a) \((3,4)\)　(b) \(\left(\frac{3}{2},-4\right)\)
  1. (a) A minus outside changes the sign of \(y\): \((3,-4)\to(3,4)\).
  2. (b) \(f(2x)\) halves every \(x\)-coordinate: \(x=\frac{3}{2}\), while \(y\) stays \(-4\).
  3. So the image is \(\left(\frac{3}{2},-4\right)\).
  4. Check: in \(y=f(2x)\), the input needed to reproduce \(f(3)\) is \(2x=3\), i.e. \(x=1.5\). ✓
Common mistake

\(f(2x)\) stretches by a factor \(\frac{1}{2}\) in the \(x\)-direction — it is a squeeze, not a stretch, and the \(y\)-coordinate is untouched.

Q11★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2026 Paper 2 Q7 ↗

The graph of \(y=x^{2}\) is first reflected in the \(x\)-axis, then translated 2 units to the left and 1 unit upwards. Find the equation of the resulting graph and its vertex.

Answer\(y=-(x+2)^{2}+1\), vertex \((-2,1)\)
  1. Reflection in the \(x\)-axis: \(y=-x^{2}\).
  2. Translate 2 units left: replace \(x\) by \((x+2)\), giving \(y=-(x+2)^{2}\).
  3. Translate 1 unit up: add 1, giving \(y=-(x+2)^{2}+1\).
  4. The vertex \((0,0)\) moves to \((-2,1)\) (left 2, up 1).
  5. Check: at \(x=-2\), \(y=1\); at \(x=0\), \(y=-4+1=-3\), which is below the vertex as expected for a downward parabola. ✓
Common mistake

Apply the operations in the order given. A reflection after a shift would give \(y=-\left[(x+2)^{2}+1\right]\), a different curve.

Q12★★☆☆4 marks

A parabola has vertex \((1,-3)\) and passes through the point \((0,-1)\). Find its equation in the form \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\).

Answer\(y=2(x-1)^{2}-3\)
  1. With vertex \((1,-3)\) the equation has the form \(y=a(x-1)^{2}-3\).
  2. Substitute the known point \((0,-1)\): \(-1=a(0-1)^{2}-3\).
  3. So \(-1=a-3\), giving \(a=2\).
  4. Hence \(y=2(x-1)^{2}-3\).
  5. Check: \(y=2(x^{2}-2x+1)-3=2x^{2}-4x-1\), and at \(x=0\), \(y=-1\). ✓
Q13★★★☆5 marks

The graph of \(y=3^{x}\) is transformed into the graph of \(y=3^{x+2}-1\).
(a) Describe the transformation.
(b) Find the \(y\)-intercept and the equation of the horizontal asymptote of the new graph.
(c) Solve \(3^{x+2}-1=26\).

Answer(a) left 2, down 1　(b) \(y\)-intercept 8, asymptote \(y=-1\)　(c) \(x=1\)
  1. (a) \(3^{x+2}=3^{x-(-2)}\), so the curve moves 2 units left; the \(-1\) moves it 1 unit down.
  2. (b) Put \(x=0\): \(y=3^{2}-1=9-1=8\), so the \(y\)-intercept is 8.
  3. The asymptote \(y=0\) of \(y=3^{x}\) also drops by 1, giving \(y=-1\).
  4. (c) \(3^{x+2}-1=26\Rightarrow 3^{x+2}=27=3^{3}\).
  5. Equal bases give equal exponents: \(x+2=3\), so \(x=1\). Check: \(3^{3}-1=26\). ✓
Common mistake

The horizontal asymptote moves with a vertical shift but the \(y\)-intercept must be recomputed. Do not claim the asymptote is still \(y=0\).

Q14★★★☆6 marks

Let \(f(x)=x^{2}-4x\). The graph of \(y=f(x)\) is transformed into \(y=f(2x)-3\).
(a) Find the equation of the new graph in the form \(y=ax^{2}+bx+c\).
(b) Find the coordinates of its vertex and hence its minimum value.

Answer(a) \(y=4x^{2}-8x-3\)　(b) vertex \((1,-7)\), minimum \(-7\)
  1. (a) \(f(2x)=(2x)^{2}-4(2x)=4x^{2}-8x\).
  2. Then subtract 3: \(y=4x^{2}-8x-3\).
  3. (b) Complete the square: \(4x^{2}-8x-3=4(x^{2}-2x)-3\).
  4. \(=4\left[(x-1)^{2}-1\right]-3=4(x-1)^{2}-4-3=4(x-1)^{2}-7\).
  5. So the vertex is \((1,-7)\) and, since the coefficient 4 is positive, the minimum value is \(-7\).
Common mistake

\(f(2x)\) is not \(2f(x)\): \(f(2x)\) squares the 2 (giving \(4x^{2}\)), whereas \(2f(x)\) would only double the whole expression.

Type 3

Linear Functions: Slope, Intercepts, Equations · 7 questions

Reading a line and reconstructing its equation

Core idea: A straight line is completely determined by its slope and one point. Slope \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\) measures the rise per unit run; the \(y\)-intercept is where \(x=0\) and the \(x\)-intercept is where \(y=0\). Parallel lines share \(m\); perpendicular lines have \(m_1m_2=-1\).

\(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)\(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\)\(m_1m_2=-1\) for perpendicular lines
Q15★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 2 Q8 ↗

Find the slope of the straight line joining \(A(1,3)\) and \(B(5,11)\).

Answer\(2\)
  1. Use \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\) with \((x_1,y_1)=(1,3)\) and \((x_2,y_2)=(5,11)\).
  2. \(m=\frac{11-3}{5-1}=\frac{8}{4}\).
  3. \(m=2\).
  4. Check: starting at \(A\), going right 4 and up 8 lands exactly on \(B\). ✓
Common mistake

Keep the points in the same order in the numerator and denominator. Mixing \((11-3)\) with \((1-5)\) gives the wrong sign.

Q16★☆☆☆3 marks

For the line \(y=3x-7\), state (a) the slope, (b) the \(y\)-intercept, (c) the \(x\)-intercept.

Answer(a) \(3\)　(b) \(-7\)　(c) \(\frac{7}{3}\)
  1. (a) In \(y=mx+c\), the coefficient of \(x\) is the slope: \(m=3\).
  2. (b) The constant term is the \(y\)-intercept: put \(x=0\), \(y=-7\).
  3. (c) Put \(y=0\): \(0=3x-7\), so \(3x=7\) and \(x=\frac{7}{3}\).
  4. Check: the point \(\left(\frac{7}{3},0\right)\) satisfies \(y=3x-7\). ✓
Q17★★☆☆3 marks

Find the equation of the straight line with slope \(-3\) passing through \((2,-1)\), giving your answer in the form \(y=mx+c\).

Answer\(y=-3x+5\)
  1. Use the point–slope form \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) with \(m=-3\), \((x_1,y_1)=(2,-1)\).
  2. \(y-(-1)=-3(x-2)\), i.e. \(y+1=-3x+6\).
  3. Subtract 1: \(y=-3x+5\).
  4. Check: at \(x=2\), \(y=-6+5=-1\). ✓
Q18★★☆☆4 marks

Find the equation of the straight line passing through \((-2,5)\) and \((4,1)\), giving your answer in the form \(Ax+By=C\) with integers \(A,B,C\).

Answer\(2x+3y=11\)
  1. Slope: \(m=\frac{1-5}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-4}{6}=-\frac{2}{3}\).
  2. Point–slope with \((4,1)\): \(y-1=-\frac{2}{3}(x-4)\).
  3. Multiply by 3: \(3y-3=-2x+8\).
  4. Rearrange: \(2x+3y=11\).
  5. Check both points: \(2(-2)+3(5)=-4+15=11\) and \(2(4)+3(1)=11\). ✓
Common mistake

\(\frac{-4}{6}\) simplifies to \(-\frac{2}{3}\), not \(-\frac{4}{6}\) left unsimplified — and the sign must be carried into the point–slope form.

Q19★★☆☆4 marks

A straight line cuts the \(x\)-axis at 6 and the \(y\)-axis at \(-4\).
(a) Find the equation of the line.
(b) Hence find the value of \(y\) when \(x=3\).

Answer(a) \(2x-3y=12\)　(b) \(y=-2\)
  1. (a) Use the intercept form \(\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1\) with \(a=6\), \(b=-4\): \(\frac{x}{6}+\frac{y}{-4}=1\).
  2. Multiply every term by 12: \(2x-3y=12\).
  3. (b) Put \(x=3\): \(6-3y=12\).
  4. Then \(-3y=6\), so \(y=-2\).
  5. Check: the point \((3,-2)\) satisfies \(2(3)-3(-2)=6+6=12\). ✓
Q20★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2023 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

Find the equation of the straight line passing through \((3,1)\) and perpendicular to the line \(2x-5y+10=0\), giving your answer in the form \(Ax+By=C\).

Answer\(5x+2y=17\)
  1. Rewrite the given line in slope form: \(5y=2x+10\), so \(y=\frac{2}{5}x+2\) and its slope is \(\frac{2}{5}\).
  2. Perpendicular slopes multiply to \(-1\): required slope \(=-\frac{1}{2/5}=-\frac{5}{2}\).
  3. Point–slope through \((3,1)\): \(y-1=-\frac{5}{2}(x-3)\).
  4. Multiply by 2: \(2y-2=-5x+15\), so \(5x+2y=17\).
  5. Check: \(5(3)+2(1)=17\). ✓ Also \(\frac{2}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)=-1\). ✓
Common mistake

The slope of \(2x-5y+10=0\) is \(\frac{2}{5}\), not \(\frac{2}{-5}\): first make \(y\) the subject. And a perpendicular slope is the negative reciprocal.

Q21★★★☆5 marks

The line \(L\) passes through \((2,5)\) and is parallel to the line \(3x-y=4\).
(a) Find the equation of \(L\).
(b) Find the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\).

Answer(a) \(y=3x-1\)　(b) \(\frac{1}{3}\)
  1. (a) From \(3x-y=4\) we get \(y=3x-4\), so the slope is 3. Parallel lines share the slope.
  2. Through \((2,5)\): \(y-5=3(x-2)=3x-6\).
  3. So \(y=3x-1\).
  4. (b) Put \(y=0\): \(0=3x-1\), so \(x=\frac{1}{3}\).
  5. Check: \(L\) passes \((2,5)\): \(3(2)-1=5\). ✓

Type 4

Quadratic Functions: Vertex and Maximum/Minimum · 7 questions

Completing the square and reading off the turning point

Core idea: Writing \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\) reveals everything: the vertex is \((h,k)\), the axis of symmetry is \(x=h\), and \(k\) is the maximum (if \(a\lt 0\)) or minimum (if \(a\gt 0\)) value. Completing the square is the reliable way to get there; the vertex also sits at \(x=-\frac{b}{2a}\).

\(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\)vertex \(\left(-\frac{b}{2a},\ c-\frac{b^{2}}{4a}\right)\)\(a\gt 0\): minimum; \(a\lt 0\): maximum
Q22★☆☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2018 Paper 2 Q7 ↗

By completing the square, write \(y=x^{2}-6x+5\) in the form \(y=(x-h)^{2}+k\) and state the coordinates of the vertex.

Answer\(y=(x-3)^{2}-4\), vertex \((3,-4)\)
  1. Take half of the coefficient of \(x\): \(\frac{-6}{2}=-3\), and square it: 9.
  2. Write \(x^{2}-6x+5=(x^{2}-6x+9)-9+5\).
  3. \(=(x-3)^{2}-4\).
  4. So \(h=3\), \(k=-4\) and the vertex is \((3,-4)\).
  5. Check: expand \((x-3)^{2}-4=x^{2}-6x+9-4=x^{2}-6x+5\). ✓
Q23★☆☆☆3 marks

For the curve \(y=(x+2)^{2}-9\), state (a) the coordinates of the vertex, (b) the axis of symmetry, (c) the minimum value of \(y\).

Answer(a) \((-2,-9)\)　(b) \(x=-2\)　(c) \(-9\)
  1. (a) Compare with \(y=(x-h)^{2}+k\) where \(x+2=x-(-2)\): \(h=-2\), \(k=-9\), so the vertex is \((-2,-9)\).
  2. (b) The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex: \(x=-2\).
  3. (c) Since \((x+2)^{2}\ge 0\), the smallest \(y\) occurs when \(x=-2\), giving \(y=-9\).
  4. Check: \(y=(0+2)^{2}-9=-5\gt -9\). ✓
Common mistake

\(y=(x+2)^{2}-9\) has vertex \(x=-2\), not \(+2\). Inside the bracket, the sign flips.

Q24★★☆☆4 marks

By completing the square, find the minimum value of \(y=2x^{2}+8x+3\) and the value of \(x\) at which it occurs.

AnswerMinimum \(-5\) at \(x=-2\)
  1. Factor out the coefficient of \(x^{2}\) first: \(y=2(x^{2}+4x)+3\).
  2. Complete the square inside: \(x^{2}+4x=(x+2)^{2}-4\).
  3. So \(y=2\left[(x+2)^{2}-4\right]+3=2(x+2)^{2}-8+3\).
  4. \(=2(x+2)^{2}-5\).
  5. Since \(2(x+2)^{2}\ge 0\), the minimum is \(-5\), occurring when \(x+2=0\), i.e. \(x=-2\).
Common mistake

The \(-4\) inside the bracket is multiplied by 2, giving \(-8\). Forgetting that factor of 2 gives the wrong minimum.

Q25★★☆☆4 marks

For the curve \(y=-x^{2}+4x+1\), find the maximum value of \(y\) and the corresponding value of \(x\).

AnswerMaximum \(5\) at \(x=2\)
  1. Factor out the \(-1\): \(y=-(x^{2}-4x)+1\).
  2. Complete the square: \(x^{2}-4x=(x-2)^{2}-4\).
  3. \(y=-\left[(x-2)^{2}-4\right]+1=-(x-2)^{2}+4+1\).
  4. \(=-(x-2)^{2}+5\).
  5. Because \(-(x-2)^{2}\le 0\), the largest \(y\) is 5, at \(x=2\).
Common mistake

With \(a\lt 0\) the parabola opens downwards, so the turning point is a maximum. Do not call it a minimum just because it is a turning point.

Q26★★☆☆4 marks

Let \(f(x)=3x^{2}-12x+7\).
(a) Write \(f(x)\) in the form \(a(x-h)^{2}+k\).
(b) Hence write down the range of \(f(x)\).

Answer(a) \(f(x)=3(x-2)^{2}-5\)　(b) \(f(x)\ge -5\)
  1. (a) \(f(x)=3(x^{2}-4x)+7\).
  2. \(x^{2}-4x=(x-2)^{2}-4\), so \(f(x)=3\left[(x-2)^{2}-4\right]+7\).
  3. \(=3(x-2)^{2}-12+7=3(x-2)^{2}-5\).
  4. (b) Since \(3(x-2)^{2}\ge 0\), we have \(f(x)\ge -5\) for all real \(x\).
  5. Check: \(f(2)=12-24+7=-5\). ✓
Q27★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 2 Q8 ↗

The curve \(y=x^{2}+bx+c\) has its vertex at \((4,-1)\).
(a) Find \(b\) and \(c\).
(b) Find the \(x\)-intercepts of the curve.

Answer(a) \(b=-8,\ c=15\)　(b) \((3,0)\) and \((5,0)\)
  1. (a) With vertex \((4,-1)\): \(y=(x-4)^{2}-1\).

Growth, decay, y-intercept and asymptote

Core idea: \(y=a\,b^{x}\) with \(a\gt 0,\ b\gt 1\) grows without limit and has \(y\)-intercept \(a\); with \(0\lt b\lt 1\) it decays towards 0. In both cases the curve passes through \((0,a)\), stays above the \(x\)-axis, and has the \(x\)-axis as a horizontal asymptote. Solve exponential equations by writing both sides as powers of the same base.

\(y=ab^{x},\ y(0)=a\)\(b=1+r\): growth rate \(r\)\(b^{m}=b^{n}\Rightarrow m=n\)

  5. So \(t=0\) (start) or \(t=4\); the ball lands at \(t=4\) s.
  6. (e) \(h\ge 9\): \(-3(t-2)^{2}+12\ge 9\Rightarrow (t-2)^{2}\le 1\).
  7. Hence \(-1\le t-2\le 1\), i.e. \(1\le t\le 3\), a duration of 2 seconds.
  8. Check: \(h(1)=9\), \(h(3)=36-27=9\). ✓
Common mistake

In (e) the inequality \((t-2)^{2}\le 1\) gives a single interval \(1\le t\le 3\), not two separate rays. Also give the duration (2 s), not just the interval.

Q50★★★★8 marks

The curve \(y=x^{2}+bx+c\) passes through \(A(1,0)\), and its axis of symmetry is the line \(x=4\).
(a) Find \(b\) and \(c\).
(b) Find the vertex of the curve.
(c) Find the \(x\)-intercepts.
(d) The line \(y=x+7\) is drawn on the same diagram. Find the coordinates of its intersections with the curve.

Answer(a) \(b=-8,\ c=7\)　(b) \((4,-9)\)　(c) \((1,0)\) and \((7,0)\)　(d) \((0,7)\) and \((9,16)\)
  1. (a) The axis of symmetry is \(x=-\frac{b}{2}=4\), so \(b=-8\).
  2. Since \(A(1,0)\) lies on the curve: \(1-8+c=0\), so \(c=7\).
  3. Hence \(y=x^{2}-8x+7\).
  4. (b) Complete the square: \(y=(x-4)^{2}-16+7=(x-4)^{2}-9\), so the vertex is \((4,-9)\).
  5. (c) Put \(y=0\): \((x-4)^{2}=9\Rightarrow x-4=\pm 3\), giving \(x=1\) or \(x=7\).
  6. So the intercepts are \((1,0)\) and \((7,0)\).
  7. (d) Set \(x^{2}-8x+7=x+7\): \(x^{2}-9x=0\).
  8. \(x(x-9)=0\Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=9\).
  9. Then \(y=x+7\): at \(x=0\), \(y=7\); at \(x=9\), \(y=16\). The points are \((0,7)\) and \((9,16)\).
  10. Check \(x=9\) on the curve: \(81-72+7=16\). ✓
Common mistake

In (d) the line and the curve share the point \((0,7)\), the \(y\)-intercept; that is one of the answers, not an error. Do not divide by \(x\) to lose the root \(x=0\).
