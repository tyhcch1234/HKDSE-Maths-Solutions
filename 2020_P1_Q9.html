Statistics · Mean, Median & Standard Deviation of a Discrete Distribution

Frequency Table Mean Median Standard Deviation Effect of a New Data Point

Question 5 marks

The table below shows the distribution of the numbers of subjects taken by a class of students.

Number of subjects taken\(4\)\(5\)\(6\)\(7\)
Number of students\(8\)\(12\)\(16\)\(4\)

(a) Write down the mean, the median and the standard deviation of the above distribution.

(b) A new student now joins the class. The number of subjects taken by the new student is \(5\). Find the change in the median of the distribution due to the joining of this student.

Part (a) Mean, Median and Standard Deviation 3A

1
Student no. 1 → 4 subjects Mean = 5.4 Median = 5.5
1Find the total number of students and total subjects1M

There are \(8+12+16+4=40\) students in the class.

\[ \text{Total subjects} = 4(8)+5(12)+6(16)+7(4)=32+60+96+28=216 \]
Hint: Add the frequencies first — the total \(40\) is an even number, which is exactly why the median will be the average of the 20th and 21st values.
2Compute the mean1A
\[ \text{Mean} = \frac{216}{40} = 5.4 \]
Hint: "Write down" still requires a clean final value. The mean need not be an integer — that's expected for grouped/weighted data.
Mean = 5.4
3Locate and compute the median1A

With \(40\) students, the median is the average of the \(20^{\text{th}}\) and \(21^{\text{st}}\) ordered values.

Cumulative frequencies: \(8,\;20,\;36,\;40\). So the \(20^{\text{th}}\) value is \(5\) and the \(21^{\text{st}}\) value is \(6\).

\[ \text{Median} = \frac{5+6}{2} = 5.5 \]
Hint: Because the data are already summarised in a table, the "ordered list" is implicit — build the cumulative-frequency column \(8,20,36,40\) to see where positions 20 and 21 land.
Median = 5.5
4Compute the standard deviation1A
\[ \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum f x^{2}}{n}-\bar x^{2}} \] \[ \sum f x^{2}=4^{2}(8)+5^{2}(12)+6^{2}(16)+7^{2}(4)=128+300+576+196=1200 \] \[ \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{1200}{40}-5.4^{2}}=\sqrt{30-29.16}=\sqrt{0.84}\approx 0.917 \]
Hint: Use the "calculator formula" \(\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum f x^{2}}{n}-\bar x^{2}}\); it avoids a second pass through the data. Keep enough decimal places: \(0.9165\ldots \approx 0.917\).
Standard deviation \(\approx 0.917\) (3 s.f.)
0 / 4

Part (b) Change in the Median when a New Student Joins 1M 1A

5
n = 41 (odd) New median = 5 Change = 0.5 (decrease)
1New total number of students—

After the new student joins: \(40+1=41\) students, an odd number.

Hint: When \(n\) is odd the median is a single data value (the \(\frac{n+1}{2}\)-th), not an average. That single change is what makes the median move.
2Find the new median1M

With \(41\) values, the median is the \(\frac{41+1}{2}=21^{\text{st}}\) value in ascending order.

Since the new student took \(5\) subjects, the counts become \(8,13,16,4\) with cumulative frequencies \(8,21,37,41\).

Hence the \(21^{\text{st}}\) value is \(5\).

New median = 5
Hint: The \(5\)'s now occupy positions \(9\) to \(21\) — the 21st value still falls in the block of \(5\)'s, so the median drops from \(5.5\) to \(5\).
3Compute the change in the median1A
\[ \text{Decrease in median}=5.5-5=0.5 \]
Hint: The question asks for the change, so state the direction (decrease) and the amount \(0.5\).
The median decreases by \(0.5\).
0 / 3
