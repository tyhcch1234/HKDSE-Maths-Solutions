Probability — No Two Boys Are Next to Each Other

If 4 boys and 5 girls randomly form a queue, find the probability that no boys are next to each other in the queue.
1Total number of arrangements (the denominator).
All 9 people are distinct, so they can be arranged in \[ (4+5)! = 9! = 362\,880 \text{ ways.} \] The whole queue is random $\Rightarrow$ every one of the $9!$ orders is equally likely.
Permutations of Distinct Objects \(n!\) All \\(9\\) people are different, so the queue can be formed in \\(9!=362\\,880\\) equally likely ways.
2First place the 5 girls.
The 5 girls can be arranged in their own line in \[ 5! = 120 \text{ ways.} \] This creates 6 gaps (including the two ends) between and around them: \[ \_\,G\,\_\,G\,\_\,G\,\_\,G\,\_\,G\,\_ \] To keep boys apart, each boy must go into a different gap — never two boys in one gap.
Gap Method (no two together) \(n\ \text{objects create}\ n+1\ \text{gaps}\) The \\(5\\) girls create \\(6\\) gaps (ends included), and the \\(4\\) boys must occupy four different gaps.
3Choose the gaps for the 4 boys, then arrange them.
Choose 4 of the 6 gaps: $\;C_4^6$. Arrange the 4 boys in them: $\;4!$.
Number of favourable arrangements: \[ C_4^6 \times 4! \times 5! = 15 \times 24 \times 120 = 43\,200. \] $C_4^6$ fixes which gaps get a boy; $4!$ fixes who stands where.
4Divide favourable outcomes by total outcomes.
\[ P=\frac{C_4^6\,4!\,5!}{(4+5)!} =\frac{43\,200}{362\,880} =\frac{5}{42}. \] Both the numerator and the denominator count equally likely orderings, so the ratio is the probability.
Probability of Equally Likely Outcomes \(P(E)=\dfrac{n(E)}{n(S)}\) Both \\(43\\,200\\) and \\(362\\,880\\) count equally likely orders, so their ratio \\(\\frac{5}{42}\\) is the probability.
Answer: $\displaystyle \frac{5}{42}$  (≈ 0.1190)
