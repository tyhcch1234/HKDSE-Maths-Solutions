2016 Paper 1 Q15

Probability Arrangements No Adjacent Combinatorics

Question

3 marks

If 4 boys and 5 girls randomly form a queue, find the probability that no boys are next to each other in the queue.

Key Idea

First arrange the 5 girls to create 6 "gaps". Then choose 4 of these gaps for the 4 boys. Count favourable arrangements ÷ total arrangements.

Progress 0 / 5
1

Understand the total number of arrangements

There are \(4 + 5 = 9\) distinct people in total. The total number of possible queues is:

\[ \text{Total} = 9! = 362\,880 \]
Hint: Since all 9 people are distinct, arranging them in a row gives \(9!\) equally likely outcomes.
2

Arrange the 5 girls first

To keep the boys separated, first place the 5 girls in a row. They can be arranged in:

\[ 5! \]
Hint: Arranging the girls first creates "gaps" where boys can be placed later.
3

Identify the gaps for the boys

With 5 girls placed, there are 6 gaps around and between them:

Gaps: _ G _ G _ G _ G _ G _

We need to choose 4 of these 6 gaps to place the 4 boys, ensuring no two boys share a gap.

\[ C^{6}_{4} \]
Hint: Choosing gaps ensures boys are never adjacent. At most one boy per gap.
4

Arrange the 4 boys in the chosen gaps

The 4 boys can be arranged in the 4 selected gaps in:

\[ 4! \]

So the number of favourable arrangements is:

\[ C^{6}_{4} \cdot 4! \cdot 5! \]
Hint: \(C^{6}_{4}\) chooses the gap positions, \(4!\) orders the boys, and \(5!\) orders the girls.
5

Compute the required probability

Divide the favourable arrangements by the total arrangements:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Required Probability} &= \frac{C^{6}_{4} \cdot 4! \cdot 5!}{(4+5)!} \\[8pt] &= \frac{C^{6}_{4} \cdot 4! \cdot 5!}{9!} \\[8pt] &= \frac{43\,200}{362\,880} \\[8pt] &= \frac{5}{42} \end{aligned} \]
Final answer: \(\displaystyle \frac{5}{42}\)
