Trigonometry — Sine Formula & the Angle between Two Planes

Sine Formula Ambiguous Case Angle between Planes 3-D Geometry

QUESTION Full Question

(a) It is given that \(WXY\) is a triangle, where \(WX = 6\text{ cm}\), \(XY = 5\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle WYX = 70^\circ\). Find \(\angle XWY\). [2 marks]

(b) Figure 3 shows the pyramid \(WXYZ\), where \(WZ = XZ = YZ\). The base of this pyramid is the triangle \(WXY\) described in (a).

0°
\(WX = \) 6.00 cm \(XY = \) 5.00 cm \(\angle WYX = \) 70.0° \(\angle XWY \approx \) 51.5° \(WP = PX = PY = \) 3.06 cm

It is given that the angle between \(WZ\) and the triangle \(WXY\) is \(30^\circ\). Does the angle between the triangles \(WXY\) and \(XYZ\) exceed \(45^\circ\)? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

Part (a) — Finding \(\angle XWY\)2 marks

5.0 cm
\(\angle XWY \approx\) 51.5° \(\angle XW'Y \approx\) 128.5° Sum check \(W'+X+Y\): 180.0°
Step 11M

Apply the sine formula to \(\triangle WXY\). The known angle \(\angle WYX = 70^\circ\) faces the side \(WX = 6\), while the required angle \(\angle XWY\) faces the side \(XY = 5\):

\[ \frac{\sin\angle XWY}{XY} = \frac{\sin\angle WYX}{WX} \]
Hint: Match each angle with the side opposite to it. Angles come from the same vertex, sides from the opposite endpoints.
Step 21M

Substitute the given values:

\[ \frac{\sin\angle XWY}{5} = \frac{\sin 70^\circ}{6} \] \[ \sin\angle XWY = \frac{5\sin 70^\circ}{6} \approx 0.7830 \]
Hint: Evaluate \(\dfrac{5\sin 70^\circ}{6}\) carefully — keep at least 4 significant figures before taking the inverse.
Step 31A

Take the inverse sine:

\[ \angle XWY \approx 51.54318937^\circ \ \text{ or } \ 128.4568106^\circ \]

Reject the obtuse value: \(\angle XWY + 70^\circ = 128.4568^\circ + 70^\circ = 198.5^\circ > 180^\circ\), which is impossible in a triangle.

Hint (ambiguous case): \(\sin\theta = k\) gives two solutions in \((0^\circ,180^\circ)\). Test \(\theta + 70^\circ < 180^\circ\) to eliminate the invalid one.
Answer (a): \(\angle XWY \approx 51.5^\circ\) (correct to 3 significant figures).

Part (b) — Angle between Planes \(WXY\) and \(XYZ\)4 marks

30°
\(\angle PMZ \approx\) 42.9° \(\tan\angle PMZ \approx\) 0.928 Compare with: 1  →   Does NOT exceed 45°
Step 11M

Let \(P\) be the projection of \(Z\) on the plane \(WXY\). Since \(WZ = XZ = YZ\), the apex \(Z\) is vertically above the point equidistant from \(W, X, Y\), i.e. \(P\) is the circumcentre of \(\triangle WXY\).

\[ PW = PX = PY \]

Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(XY\). The angle between the planes \(WXY\) and \(XYZ\) is the inclination \(\angle PMZ\).

Hint: The dihedral angle between two planes is measured in a plane perpendicular to their line of intersection \(XY\). Choosing \(M\) (the midpoint) makes \(MX \perp XY\) and \(MZ \perp XY\).
Identified dihedral angle: \(\angle PMZ\).
Step 21M

In the isosceles triangle \(PXY\) (\(PX = PY\)), the median \(PM\) bisects the apex angle:

\[ \angle MPX = \tfrac{1}{2}\angle XPY \]

\(P\) is the centre of the circle through \(W, X, Y\), so the inscribed angle \(\angle XWY\) subtends arc \(XY\), and the central angle is twice it:

\[ \angle XPY = 2\angle XWY \ \Longrightarrow \ \angle MPX = \angle XWY \]

In right-angled triangle \(PMX\):

\[ MP = PX\cos\angle MPX = PW\cos\angle XWY \]
Hint: Inscribed-angle theorem: the angle at the centre is twice the inscribed angle standing on the same arc.
Step 31M

The angle between \(WZ\) and the plane \(WXY\) is \(\angle PWZ = 30^\circ\). In right-angled triangle \(PWZ\):

\[ \tan 30^\circ = \frac{PZ}{PW} \ \Longrightarrow \ PZ = PW\tan 30^\circ \]

In right-angled triangle \(PMZ\):

\[ \tan\angle PMZ = \frac{PZ}{MP} = \frac{PW\tan 30^\circ}{PW\cos\angle XWY} = \frac{\tan 30^\circ}{\cos\angle XWY} \]
Hint: \(PW\) cancels nicely. The result depends only on the base angle found in part (a).
Step 41A

Substitute \(\angle XWY \approx 51.54318937^\circ\) from part (a):

\[ \tan\angle PMZ = \frac{\tan 30^\circ}{\cos 51.54318937^\circ} \approx 0.928328501 \]

Since \(\tan\angle PMZ \approx 0.9283 < 1 = \tan 45^\circ\), and \(\tan\) is increasing on \((0^\circ, 90^\circ)\):

\[ \angle PMZ < 45^\circ \]
Hint: You don't need the exact value of \(\angle PMZ\) — the question only asks whether it exceeds \(45^\circ\). Comparing \(\tan\angle PMZ\) with \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\) is enough.
Answer (b): \(\tan\angle PMZ \approx 0.9283 < 1\), hence \(\angle PMZ < 45^\circ\). The angle between the triangles \(WXY\) and \(XYZ\) does NOT exceed \(45^\circ\).

📌 Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\angle XWY \approx 51.5^\circ\) (3 s.f.). The obtuse solution \(128.5^\circ\) is rejected since \(\angle XWY + \angle WYX = 198.5^\circ > 180^\circ\).
  • (b) \(P\) = projection of \(Z\) on \(WXY\) is the circumcentre of \(\triangle WXY\); \(M\) = mid-point of \(XY\).
  • (b) Angle between planes \(WXY\) and \(XYZ\) is \(\angle PMZ\), and \(\tan\angle PMZ = \dfrac{\tan 30^\circ}{\cos\angle XWY} \approx 0.9283\).
  • (b) Since \(0.9283 < 1\), we get \(\angle PMZ < 45^\circ\). The angle does NOT exceed \(45^\circ\).
