Coordinate Geometry — Intersection of a Circle and a Straight Line
Circle Equation Line–Circle Intersection Discriminant Δ = 0 Tangency Condition

QQuestion

Find the constant \(k\) such that the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-6y+k=0\) and the straight line \(x+y+4=0\) intersect at only one point.

A.  −16 B.  −8 C.  8 D.  16

Full marks: 5 · One point of intersection ⇔ the line is a tangent to the circle.

1Centre & Radius of the Circle 2M

Rewrite the circle by completing the square to find its centre and radius (in terms of \(k\)).

Centre \(C = (\;\)(-1, 3)\(\;)\)
Radius \(r =\) 4.00
\(k = r^{2}-10 =\) 6.00

Drag the slider: the circle grows/shrinks about its fixed centre \(C(-1,3)\). When \(k=-8\), \(r=\sqrt{(1)^2+(-3)^2-(-8)}=\sqrt{18}\) and the line just touches the circle.

Step 11M

Complete the square in \(x\) and in \(y\):

\[x^{2}+2x+y^{2}-6y+k=0\] \[(x^{2}+2x+1)+(y^{2}-6y+9)+k-10=0\] \[(x+1)^{2}+(y-3)^{2}=10-k\]
Hint: The circle has the standard form \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k_0)^{2}=r^{2}\), so the right-hand side \(10-k\) must equal \(r^{2}\).
Step 21A

Read off the centre and the radius:

\[\text{Centre } C=(-1,\;3),\qquad r=\sqrt{10-k}\]
Answer: centre \((-1,3)\), radius \(\sqrt{10-k}\) (requires \(k<10\)).
Hint: In \((x-h)^2+(y-k_0)^2=r^2\) the signs of \(h,k_0\) are flipped from the brackets — a very common slip.

2Substitute the Line into the Circle 2M

From \(x+y+4=0\) we get \(y=-x-4\). Substitute into the circle equation.

Substituted equation: \(2x^{2}+16x+(k+40)=0\)
\(\Delta = 96-8k =\) 80.0
Number of intersections: 2 (secant)

Move \(k\): the circle's radius changes. Watch the line \(x+y+4=0\) (in red) — when the circle shrinks until the line only grazes it, that is tangency (\(k=-8\)).

Step 11M

Make \(y\) the subject of the line equation:

\[x+y+4=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=-x-4 \quad\cdots(1)\]
Hint: Either variable may be eliminated. Eliminating \(y\) keeps the algebra tidy; eliminating \(x\) gives a similar result.
Step 21M

Put (1) into the circle equation:

\[x^{2}+(-x-4)^{2}+2x-6(-x-4)+k=0\] \[x^{2}+(x^{2}+8x+16)+2x+6x+24+k=0\] \[2x^{2}+16x+(k+40)=0 \quad\cdots(2)\]
Hint: A single quadratic in \(x\) now describes all intersection points. Check: \((-x-4)^2 = x^2+8x+16\), \(-6(-x-4)=6x+24\).
Result: \(2x^{2}+16x+(k+40)=0\).

3Apply the Tangency Condition \(\Delta=0\) 1M+1A

One point of intersection ⇔ equation (2) has exactly one real root ⇔ its discriminant is zero.

\(a=2,\; b=16,\; c=k+40\)
\(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac=256-8(k+40)=96-8k\)
Set \(\Delta=0 \Rightarrow k=\) −8
Step 11M

For only one point of intersection, (2) has one real root:

\[\Delta=0\] \[(16)^{2}-4(2)(k+40)=0\] \[256-8(k+40)=0\] \[256-8k-320=0\] \[-8k-64=0\]
Hint: Here \(a=2,\;b=16,\;c=k+40\). Watch the signs: \(-4(2)(k+40) = -8(k+40)\).
Step 21A

Solve for \(k\):

\[-8k=64 \;\Longrightarrow\; k=-8\]
Final answer: \(k=-8\)  →  Option B
Check: with \(k=-8\), \(r=\sqrt{10-(-8)}=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2\), and the perpendicular distance from \(C(-1,3)\) to \(x+y+4=0\) is \(\dfrac{|-1+3+4|}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt2}=3\sqrt2=r\). ✓ The line is indeed tangent.

Summary of Answers

  • Circle rewritten: \((x+1)^{2}+(y-3)^{2}=10-k\)
  • Centre \(C(-1,\,3)\), radius \(r=\sqrt{10-k}\)
  • Line \(x+y+4=0\Rightarrow y=-x-4\), substituted gives \(2x^{2}+16x+(k+40)=0\)
  • Tangency condition: \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac=96-8k=0\)
  • \(k=-8\)  (Option B)
  • Geometric check: distance from \(C\) to the line \(=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt2}=3\sqrt2=\) radius
