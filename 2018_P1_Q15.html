Question

An eight-digit phone number is formed by a permutation of $2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8$ and $9$.

  1. How many different eight-digit phone numbers can be formed? 1 mark
  2. If the first digit and the last digit of an eight-digit number are odd numbers, how many different eight-digit phone numbers can be formed? 2 marks
1Part (a) — Understand the set-up

There are $8$ distinct digits:

$\{2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8,\ 9\}$

Every phone number is an arrangement that uses all eight digits, once each — so it is a permutation of $8$ distinct objects into $8$ positions.

Hint: no repetition is allowed, and order matters, so use permutations $P_8^8$.
2Part (a) — Count the arrangements
Permutation \(P^{n}_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!}\) Arranging 8 distinct digits in 8 positions gives \(P^{8}_{8}=8!\).

Number of arrangements $=8!$ (equivalently $P_8^8$):

$P_8^8 = 8! = 8\times7\times6\times5\times4\times3\times2\times1 = 40\,320$

Each arrangement is a different eight-digit phone number.

Hint: multiply the choices position by position: $8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1$.
3Part (b) — Identify the odd digits

The odd digits in the set are:

$\{3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9\}$ — that is $4$ odd digits.

The first digit and the last digit must each be chosen from these $4$ odd digits, and they must be different digits.

Hint: the two end positions are special; handle them before the interior.
4Part (b) — Choose the two end digits
Multiplication Principle \(m\times n\ \text{ways for two independent choices}\) The two ends give \(4\times3=12\) ways, and the middle then gives \(6!=720\) ways.

Choose and arrange an odd digit for the 1st and the 8th position:

$P_2^{4}=\dfrac{4!}{(4-2)!}=\dfrac{4!}{2!}=12$

Multiplication principle: $4$ choices for the first digit $\times\ 3$ choices for the last digit $=12$.

Hint: after fixing the ends, $2$ digits are used up, leaving $6$ digits.
5Part (b) — Fill the six middle positions

The remaining $6$ digits (some odd, some even) are arranged in the $6$ middle positions:

$P_6^{6}=6!=720$

By the multiplication principle:

$\bigl(P_2^{4}\bigr)\bigl(P_6^{6}\bigr)=12\times720=8\,640$
Hint: independent choices multiply: (ways to arrange the ends) × (ways to arrange the middle).
Answer

(a) $P_8^8 = 8! = 40\,320$

(b) $\bigl(P_2^{4}\bigr)\bigl(P_6^{6}\bigr) = 12\times720 = 8\,640$

Question. A seven-digit phone number is formed by a permutation of \(1,2,3,4,5,6,7\).
(a) How many different phone numbers can be formed?
(b) If both the first and the last digits are odd numbers, how many different phone numbers can be formed?

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) There are 7 distinct digits and all are used once, so the number of arrangements is \(7!=5040\).
Step 2. (b) The odd digits are \(1,3,5,7\), i.e. 4 of them; the two ends can be filled in \(4\times3=12\) ways.
Step 3. (b) The remaining 5 digits fill the 5 middle positions in \(5!=120\) ways.
Step 4. (b) Hence the number of such phone numbers is \(12\times120=1440\).
Answer: (a) \(5040\) (b) \(1440\)
