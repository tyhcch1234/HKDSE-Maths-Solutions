2018 MC #4 Visual Model

Common denominator: (3x + 7)(3x − 7)

HKDSE 2018 · Paper 2 · Question 4 (Topic 4: Polynomials)

$\dfrac{1}{3x+7}-\dfrac{1}{3x-7}=$
  • A. $\dfrac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}$
  • B. $\dfrac{14}{\,9x^{2}-49\,}$
  • C. $\dfrac{6x}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}$
  • D. $\dfrac{6x}{\,9x^{2}-49\,}$

Step-by-step Solution

1 Identify the two denominators (a difference of two fractions) The expression is a subtraction of two algebraic fractions: $\dfrac{1}{3x+7}-\dfrac{1}{3x-7}$.
Two denominators: $3x+7$ and $3x-7$. They are different, so we cannot subtract directly.
2
Difference of Two Squares\((a+b)(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}\)The common denominator \((3x+7)(3x-7)\) simplifies to \(9x^{2}-49\) with \(a=3x\), \(b=7\).
Find the common denominator Multiply the two denominators: \[ (3x+7)(3x-7) \] This is a difference of two squares,
so it simplifies to $9x^{2}-49$.
The LCM of $3x+7$ and $3x-7$ is their product, since neither divides the other.
3 Rewrite each fraction with the common denominator \[ \frac{1}{3x+7}=\frac{3x-7}{(3x+7)(3x-7)},\qquad \frac{1}{3x-7}=\frac{3x+7}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} \]
Multiply numerator and denominator of each fraction by the missing factor.
4
Adding Algebraic Fractions\(\dfrac{a}{b}-\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ad-bc}{bd}\)One common denominator lets the numerators be written as the single bracket \((3x-7)-(3x+7)\).
Subtract the numerators — keep the bracket! \[ \frac{1}{3x+7}-\frac{1}{3x-7} =\frac{(3x-7)-(3x+7)}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} \]
Because of the minus sign in front, the whole numerator $3x+7$ must be subtracted in brackets.
5
Distributive Law for a Minus Sign\(-(a+b)=-a-b\)The minus in front of \((3x+7)\) changes both signs, so the numerator is \(3x-7-3x-7=-14\).
Expand and simplify the numerator \[ (3x-7)-(3x+7)=3x-7-3x-7=-14 \] The $3x$ terms cancel,
leaving $-14$.
Watch out: $-(3x+7)=-3x-7$, not $-3x+7$.
6 Simplify the denominator and combine \[ (3x+7)(3x-7)=9x^{2}-49 \] So \[ \frac{-14}{9x^{2}-49} \]
Use the identity $(a+b)(a-b)=a^{2}-b^{2}$ with $a=3x$, $b=7$.
7 Move the minus sign into the denominator to match the options \[ \frac{-14}{\,9x^{2}-49\,}=\frac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,} \] The options are written with $49-9x^{2}$ in the denominator,
which matches option A.
$\dfrac{-a}{b}=\dfrac{a}{-b}$ — flipping the sign of one part flips the whole fraction.
Answer: A  —  $\dfrac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}$

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{1}{2x+5}-\dfrac{1}{2x-5}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The denominators \(2x+5\) and \(2x-5\) share no factor, so the L.C.M. is \((2x+5)(2x-5)\).
Step 2. Put over the common denominator: \(\dfrac{(2x-5)-(2x+5)}{(2x+5)(2x-5)}\).
Step 3. Remove the bracket: \(2x-5-2x-5=-10\).
Step 4. \((2x+5)(2x-5)=4x^{2}-25\), so the answer is \(\dfrac{-10}{4x^{2}-25}=\dfrac{10}{25-4x^{2}}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{10}{25-4x^{2}}\)

Quick check: substitute $x=0$ into the original → $\frac{1}{7}-\frac{1}{-7}=\frac{2}{7}$; option A gives $\frac{14}{49}=\frac{2}{7}$ ✓.
