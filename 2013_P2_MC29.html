Step 11M

Read the stem-and-leaf diagram. The stem is the tens digit and the leaf is the units digit, so every entry is a two-digit number.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Stem }4 &: 40,42,42,42,44,44,44,47\\ \text{Stem }5 &: 50,50,51,52,52,56,58,59\\ \text{Stem }6 &: 63,65,65,67\\ \text{Stem }7 &: 70\\ \text{Stem }8 &: 82,86\\ \text{Stem }9 &: 95 \end{aligned} \]

There are \(8+8+4+1+2+1 = 24\) data values in total.

Hint: Always count the number of leaves carefully — the number of data values will be needed for locating the median and quartiles.
Step 21M

The smallest datum and the largest datum are read directly from the ends of the diagram:

\[ \text{Smallest} = 40, \qquad \text{Largest} = 95 \]
Hint: The smallest value is the first leaf of the first stem, and the largest value is the last leaf of the last stem.
Step 31M

There are \(24\) data values (even), so the median is the average of the \(12^{\text{th}}\) and \(13^{\text{th}}\) values.

Counting in order: the \(12^{\text{th}}\) value is \(52\) and the \(13^{\text{th}}\) value is also \(52\). Hence

\[ \text{Median} = \frac{52+52}{2} = 52 \]
Hint: When \(n\) is even, \(\text{Median} = \dfrac{x_{(n/2)}+x_{(n/2+1)}}{2}\).
Step 41M

For the lower quartile \(Q_1\) we take the median of the lower half (the first \(12\) values), i.e. the average of the \(6^{\text{th}}\) and \(7^{\text{th}}\) values:

\[ Q_1 = \frac{44+44}{2} = 44 \]

For the upper quartile \(Q_3\) we take the median of the upper half (the last \(12\) values), i.e. the average of the \(18^{\text{th}}\) and \(19^{\text{th}}\) values:

\[ Q_3 = \frac{65+65}{2} = 65 \]
Hint: The five-number summary is \(x_{\min},\; Q_1,\; \text{Median},\; Q_3,\; x_{\max}\). Notice how the gaps between consecutive numbers grow: \(40\to44\to52\to65\to95\).
Five-number summary: \(40,\ 44,\ 52,\ 65,\ 95\)

bPart (b) — Match the correct box-and-whisker diagram

Choosing the answer
Toggle the options to compare. The correct diagram must have the five-number summary \(40,\ 44,\ 52,\ 65,\ 95\).
1.0
Step 11M

From part (a), the correct diagram must show:

\[ \text{Min}=40,\quad Q_1=44,\quad Q_2=52,\quad Q_3=65,\quad \text{Max}=95 \]

So the left whisker spans \(40\)–\(44\), the box spans \(44\)–\(65\) (with a line at \(52\)), and the right whisker spans \(65\)–\(95\).

Hint: A box-and-whisker diagram is completely determined by its five-number summary.
Step 21M

Compute the lengths of the segments in the correct diagram:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Left whisker} &: 44-40 = 4 \\ Q_1 \to Q_2 &: 52-44 = 8 \\ Q_2 \to Q_3 &: 65-52 = 13 \\ \text{Right whisker} &: 95-65 = 30 \end{aligned} \]

The segment lengths clearly increase from left to right: \(4 < 8 < 13 < 30\).

Hint: The right whisker should be much longer than each of the other three segments — a strongly right-skewed distribution.
Step 31M

Compare with the four options:

  • A — the whiskers are short and the box is wide: doesn't match.
  • B — median sits in the middle with two short whiskers: doesn't match.
  • C — a roughly symmetric box with a very short right whisker: doesn't match.
  • D — a short left whisker and a long right whisker, with median nearer the left: this fits the increasing-separation pattern.
Hint: In a right-skewed distribution the right whisker is the longest; in a left-skewed one the left whisker is the longest.
Answer: Option D.
