2023 Paper 1 · Question 14

Mensuration — Cone & Frustum, Volume & Surface Area
Right Circular Cone Frustum Volume Ratio Similar Solids Sphere

Question

The base radius and the curved surface area of a solid metal right circular cone are \(14\text{ cm}\) and \(700\pi\text{ cm}^2\) respectively.

(a) Find the height of the circular cone. 3 marks

(b) The circular cone is divided into a right circular cone \(X\) and a frustum \(Y\) by a plane which is parallel to its base. The curved surface area of \(Y\) is \(15\) times the curved surface area of \(X\).

(i) Express the volume of \(Y\) in terms of \(\pi\).
(ii) If \(Y\) is melted and recast into \(2\) identical solid spheres, find the diameter of each sphere. 5 marks

Part (a) Finding the height 3 marks

Given \(r=14\) and curved surface area \(=700\pi\). Let \(l\) be the slant height.

Radius \(r\) = 14 cm
Slant \(l\) = 50 cm
Height \(h=\sqrt{l^2-14^2}\) = 48.00 cm
Curved S.A. \(=\pi r l\) = 2199.1 cm²
Step 11M

Use the formula for curved surface area of a cone, with \(r=14\):

\[ \pi(14)(l) = 700\pi \] \[ \Rightarrow \; 14l = 700 \]
Hint: The \(\pi\) cancels on both sides — never drop the given \(700\pi\), just divide throughout by \(\pi\).
Slant height \(\; l = 50\text{ cm}\)
Step 21M

In the right-angled triangle formed by the height \(h\), radius \(r\) and slant height \(l\):

\[ h = \sqrt{l^2 - r^2} = \sqrt{50^2 - 14^2} = \sqrt{2500 - 196} \]
Hint: The slant height is the hypotenuse. Use Pythagoras: \(h^2 + r^2 = l^2\).
Step 31A
\[ h = \sqrt{2304} = 48 \]
Hint: \(2304 = 48^2\), a perfect square — so the answer is exact.
Height of the circular cone \(= 48\text{ cm}\)
0 / 3

Part (b)(i) Volume of frustum \(Y\) 3 marks

Let \(k\) be the linear scale factor of the small cone \(X\) relative to the whole cone.

Curved S.A. of \(Y = 15 \times\) curved S.A. of \(X\).

CSA ratio \(Y:X\) = 15.0
Height of \(X\) = 17.3 cm
Height of \(Y\) = 30.7 cm
Volume \(Y\) = 3087 π cm³
Step 11M

Cone \(X\) is similar to the whole cone. Let the linear scale factor be \(k\): \(h_X = 48k\), \(r_X = 14k\), \(l_X = 50k\). Then

\[ \text{CSA}_X = \pi(14k)(50k) = 700\pi k^2,\qquad \text{CSA}_Y = 700\pi(1-k^2) \]
Hint: Area scales as the square of the linear scale factor. The CSA of the whole cone is \(700\pi\).
Step 21M

Given \(\text{CSA}_Y = 15\,\text{CSA}_X\):

\[ 700\pi(1-k^2) = 15\cdot 700\pi k^2 \;\Rightarrow\; 1-k^2 = 15k^2 \;\Rightarrow\; 16k^2 = 1 \] \[ k^2 = \frac{1}{16} \qquad\qquad k = \frac14 \]
Hint: \(\dfrac{\text{CSA}_Y}{\text{CSA}_X} = \dfrac{1-k^2}{k^2} = 15\). Solving gives \(k = \tfrac14\) exactly (so \(r_X = 3.5\) cm).
\(k^2 = \dfrac{1}{16}\)
Step 31A

Volume of frustum \(Y\) = Volume of whole cone − Volume of cone \(X\). Volume scales as \(k^3\):

\[ V_Y = \frac{1}{3}\pi(14)^2(48)\left(1 - \left(\tfrac14\right)^3\right) = 3136\pi\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{64}\right) \] \[ = 3136\pi \times \frac{63}{64} = 49\pi \times 63 = 3087\pi \]
Hint: \(k^3 = \left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right)^3 = \tfrac{1}{64}\), so the frustum keeps \(\tfrac{63}{64}\) of the volume.
Volume of \(Y = 3087\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
0 / 3

Part (b)(ii) Diameter of each sphere 2 marks

\(Y\) is melted and recast into \(2\) identical solid spheres. Volume is conserved.

Frustum volume \(V_Y\) = 3087 π cm³
Volume per sphere \(=V_Y/2\) = 1543.5 π cm³
Diameter \(d\) = 21.00 cm
Step 11M

Let \(d\) cm be the diameter of each sphere. Each sphere has radius \(d/2\). Volume of two spheres = volume of \(Y\):

\[ 2 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^3 = 3087\pi \]
Hint: Equate total volume of the two spheres to the frustum's volume. The \(\pi\) cancels.
Step 21A
\[ \frac{8}{3}\pi \cdot \frac{d^3}{8} = 3087\pi \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\pi d^3}{3} = 3087\pi \] \[ d^3 = 9261 \;\Rightarrow\; d = 21 \]
Hint: \(9261 = 21^3\). Cube-root carefully: \(21^3 = 21\times21\times21\).
Diameter of each sphere \(= 21\text{ cm}\)
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Height of the circular cone \(= 48\text{ cm}\)
  • (b)(i) Volume of frustum \(Y = 3087\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
  • (b)(ii) Diameter of each sphere \(= 21\text{ cm}\) (radius \(10.5\text{ cm}\))
