Q14The base radius and the curved surface area of a solid metal right circular cone are 14 cm and $700\pi\ \text{cm}^2$ respectively.

(a) Find the height of the circular cone. [3 marks]

(b) The circular cone is divided into a right circular cone $X$ and a frustum $Y$ by a plane which is parallel to its base. The curved surface area of $Y$ is 15 times the curved surface area of $X$.

(i) Express the volume of $Y$ in terms of $\pi$.

(ii) If $Y$ is melted and recast into 2 identical solid spheres, find the diameter of each sphere.

[5 marks]

Step 1 · Part (a) — find the slant height
Curved Surface Area of a Cone \(A=\pi r l\) With \(r=10\) and area \(260\pi\), the slant height follows from \(\pi(10)l=260\pi\).

Let $\ell$ cm be the slant height of the cone. The curved surface area of a cone is $\pi r \ell$ with $r=14$.

$$\pi(14)(\ell)=700\pi$$ $$\ell=\frac{700\pi}{14\pi}=50$$
Hint: curved surface area of a cone $=\pi r \ell$.
Step 2 · Part (a) — find the height
Pythagoras Theorem \(l^{2}=r^{2}+h^{2}\) The height, radius and slant height form a right-angled triangle, so \(h=\sqrt{26^{2}-10^{2}}\).

The vertical height, the radius and the slant height form a right-angled triangle, so by Pythagoras:

$$\text{height}=\sqrt{\ell^{2}-14^{2}}=\sqrt{50^{2}-14^{2}}$$ $$=\sqrt{2500-196}=\sqrt{2304}=48\ \text{cm}$$
Answer (a): the height of the cone is 48 cm.
Step 3 · Part (b)(i) — ratio of the two cones
Area Ratio of Similar Solids \(\dfrac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}=k^{2}\) The curved surface of the frustum \(Y\) is \(3\) times that of \(X\), so the whole cone is \(4\) times \(X\).

Cone $X$ (top) is similar to the whole cone. For the curved surfaces: the curved surface of $Y$ $=15\times$ curved surface of $X$, so

$$\text{C.S.A. of whole cone}=16\times(\text{C.S.A. of }X)$$

For similar solids, area ratio $=k^{2}$ where $k$ is the linear ratio:

$$k^{2}=\frac{1}{16}\;\Rightarrow\;k=\frac{1}{4}$$

So every length of $X$ is $\tfrac14$ of the corresponding length of the whole cone; the scale factor from $X$ to the whole cone is $4$.

Area ratio $=k^2$ and volume ratio $=k^3$ (where $k$ is the linear scale factor).
Step 4 · Part (b)(i) — volume of frustum $Y$
Volume Ratio of Similar Solids \(\dfrac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=k^{3}\) With \(k=\dfrac{1}{2}\) the small cone is \(\dfrac{1}{8}\) of the whole, leaving \(\dfrac{7}{8}\) for \(Y\).

Volume ratio from $X$ to the whole cone is $k^{3}=4^{3}=64$, so

$$\frac{V_X}{V_{\text{whole}}}=\frac{1}{64}\quad\Rightarrow\quad V_Y=\frac{63}{64}V_{\text{whole}}$$

The whole cone has $r=14$, $h=48$:

$$V_Y=\frac{1}{3}\pi(14)^{2}(48)\left(1-\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{3}\right)$$ $$=\frac{1}{3}\pi(196)(48)\left(\frac{63}{64}\right)=3087\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}$$
Answer (b)(i): $V_Y=3087\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}$
Step 5 · Part (b)(ii) — recast into two spheres

Let $d$ cm be the diameter of each sphere. The two identical spheres have total volume $V_Y$.

$$2\times\frac{4}{3}\pi\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{3}=3087\pi$$ $$\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{3}=1543.5 \;\Rightarrow\; \left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{3}=1157.625$$ $$\frac{d}{2}=10.5 \;\Rightarrow\; d=21$$
Answer (b)(ii): the diameter of each sphere is 21 cm.

Question. A cone has base radius \(10\text{ cm}\) and curved surface area \(260\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\). (a) Find its slant height and its height. (b) The cone is cut by a plane parallel to the base; the curved surface of the frustum \(Y\) below the cut is \(3\) times the curved surface of the small cone \(X\) above it. Find the volume of \(Y\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\pi(10)l=260\pi\), so \(l=26\text{ cm}\).
Step 2. \(h=\sqrt{26^{2}-10^{2}}=\sqrt{676-100}=\sqrt{576}=24\text{ cm}\).
Step 3. \(X+Y=4X\), so \(k^{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}\) and \(k=\dfrac{1}{2}\) from \(X\) to the whole cone.
Step 4. \(V_{\text{whole}}=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(10)^{2}(24)=800\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Step 5. \(V_{Y}=800\pi\left(1-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{3}\right)=800\pi\times\dfrac{7}{8}=700\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Answer: (a) \(l=26\text{ cm}\), \(h=24\text{ cm}\) (b) \(700\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)
