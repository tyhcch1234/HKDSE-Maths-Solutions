🔍 Step-by-step solution

Follow the guided steps below. The key idea: for each base, take the highest power that appears in any expression.

The L.C.M. (Least Common Multiple) of algebraic expressions is the smallest expression that is divisible by each of them.

Think of each expression as a product of powers of its prime (or variable) factors. The L.C.M. must contain every factor with the greatest exponent seen among the expressions.

📌 Hint: Treat each variable (\(a, b, c\)) separately—just like numbers with prime factors.
\[ E_1 = a^4 b^2 c^1,\quad E_2 = a^3 b^4 c^1,\quad E_3 = a^2 b^5 c^2 \]

Notice that we can write each variable with its exponent, even if it is \(1\) (e.g. \(c = c^1\)).

For each variable, pick the largest exponent that appears in any expression.

Expression \(a\) \(b\) \(c\)
\(a^4 b^2 c\) 4 2 1
\(a^3 b^4 c\) 3 4 1
\(a^2 b^5 c^2\) 2 5 2

* The highlighted numbers are the highest power for each variable.

Multiply the highest powers together:

\[ \text{L.C.M.} = a^{\max(4,3,2)} \cdot b^{\max(2,4,5)} \cdot c^{\max(1,1,2)} \] \[ = a^4 \cdot b^5 \cdot c^2 \]
✅ \(\,a^4b^5c^2\,\)

Therefore the correct answer is Option D.
