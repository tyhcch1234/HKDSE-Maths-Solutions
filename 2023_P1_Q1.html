Make $h$ the subject of the formula \[ \frac{5}{h+k}=\frac{k}{h-3}. \]
(3 marks)
Step 1Clear the fractions — cross-multiply.
\[ \frac{5}{h+k}=\frac{k}{h-3}\ \Longrightarrow\ 5(h-3)=k(h+k) \]
Multiply each side by $(h+k)(h-3)$ (the common denominator).
Step 2Expand both brackets.
\[ 5h-15 = hk + k^{2} \]
$5(h-3)=5h-15$  and  $k(h+k)=hk+k^{2}$.
Step 3Collect all terms containing $h$ on one side.
\[ 5h - hk = 15 + k^{2} \]
Move $hk$ to the left and $-15$ to the right; then factor out $h$: \[ h(5-k)=15+k^{2}. \]
Step 4Divide by the coefficient of $h$.
\[ h=\frac{15+k^{2}}{5-k} \]
Divide both sides by $(5-k)$ — remember $5-k\neq0$.
Answer: $\;h=\dfrac{15+k^{2}}{5-k}$
