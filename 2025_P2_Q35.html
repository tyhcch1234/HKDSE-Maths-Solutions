Powers of $i$ on the Unit Circle

Cycle of period 4: $i,\,-1,\,-i,\,1$

$$i^{9}+i^{10}+i^{11}+\cdots+i^{999}$$

Step-by-step solution

Geometric series with ratio $i$ and $991$ terms.

1

Recognise the pattern of $i^n$

Powers of $i$ repeat every $4$ steps: $$i^1=i,\quad i^2=-1,\quad i^3=-i,\quad i^4=1.$$ Hence $i^{n+4}=i^n$ for every integer $n$.
Hint: the powers cycle $i \to -1 \to -i \to 1$ around the unit circle.
Cycle of Powers of i \(i^{1}=i,\ i^{2}=-1,\ i^{3}=-i,\ i^{4}=1\) The powers repeat every \(4\), so \(i^{9}=i\) and \(i^{991}=i^{3}=-i\).
2

Count the number of terms

The exponents run from $9$ to $999$ inclusive, so $$N=999-9+1=991\ \text{terms.}$$
Hint: “last $-$ first $+ 1$” avoids the classic off-by-one error.
3

Use the geometric series formula

With first term $a=i^{9}$ and common ratio $r=i$ ($r\neq 1$), $$S=\frac{a(1-r^{N})}{1-r}=\frac{i^{9}\left(1-i^{991}\right)}{1-i}.$$
Hint: $S=a\dfrac{1-r^N}{1-r}$ — here $N=991$.
Sum of a Geometric Series \(S=\dfrac{a(1-r^{N})}{1-r}\quad(r\neq1)\) There are \(991\) terms with first term \(i^{9}=i\) and ratio \(i\).
4

Reduce the powers using period 4

Since $9 \equiv 1 \pmod 4$: $$i^{9}=i.$$ Since $991=4\times 247+3$, i.e. $991 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$: $$i^{991}=i^{3}=-i.$$ Therefore $$\frac{i\left[1-(-i)\right]}{1-i}=\frac{i(1+i)}{1-i}.$$
Hint: only the remainder of the exponent modulo $4$ matters.
5

Rationalise the denominator

Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate $1+i$: $$\frac{i(1+i)}{1-i}\cdot\frac{1+i}{1+i} =\frac{i(1+i)^{2}}{(1-i)(1+i)} =\frac{i\left(1+2i+i^{2}\right)}{1-i^{2}}.$$
Hint: the conjugate makes the denominator real.
6

Evaluate and get the answer

Using $i^{2}=-1$: $$1-i^{2}=1-(-1)=2,\qquad 1+2i+i^{2}=1+2i-1=2i.$$ So $$S=\frac{i\cdot 2i}{2}=\frac{2i^{2}}{2}=i^{2}=-1.$$
Hint: a real answer is expected — the imaginary parts cancel.
ANSWER $$i^{9}+i^{10}+i^{11}+\cdots+i^{999}=-1\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\textbf{A}$$

Question. Find the value of \(i^{7}+i^{8}+i^{9}+\cdots+i^{97}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The exponents run from \(7\) to \(97\), so there are \(97-7+1=91\) terms.
Step 2. First term \(i^{7}=i^{3}=-i\) and common ratio \(i\), so \(S=\dfrac{-i\left(1-i^{91}\right)}{1-i}\).
Step 3. \(91=4\times22+3\), so \(i^{91}=i^{3}=-i\) and \(1-i^{91}=1+i\).
Step 4. \(S=\dfrac{-i(1+i)}{1-i}=\dfrac{-i(1+i)^{2}}{2}=\dfrac{-i(2i)}{2}=1\).
Answer: \(1\)
