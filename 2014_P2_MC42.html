Coordinate Geometry — Intersection of a Line and a Circle

Circle Equation Line & Circle Intersection Relations of Roots Mid-point of a Chord

Q Question Multiple Choice

If the straight line \(x-y=k\) and the circle \[x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-4y-1=0\] intersect at \(A\) and \(B\), then the \(x\)-coordinate of the mid-point of \(AB\) is

A. \(1+k\)
B. \(1-k\)
C. \(\dfrac{1+k}{2}\) ✓
D. \(\dfrac{1-k}{2}\)

A Part (a) — Rearrange the line & set up the intersection

From \(x-y=k\) we get the subject of the line:

\[ y = x - k \]
0 / 3
Step 1

Make \(y\) the subject of the straight line \(x-y=k\):

\[ x-y=k \;\Longrightarrow\; y = x-k \]
Hint: Everything hinges on substituting this into the circle. Always isolate one variable first — here \(y\) is the easiest.
Step 2

Substitute \(y=x-k\) into the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-4y-1=0\):

\[ x^{2}+(x-k)^{2}+2x-4(x-k)-1=0 \]

Expand:

\[ x^{2}+x^{2}-2kx+k^{2}+2x-4x+4k-1=0 \] \[ 2x^{2}+(-2k-2)x+(k^{2}+4k-1)=0 \quad \cdots(\ast) \]
Hint: Collect terms by powers of \(x\). The coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(2\) and the linear coefficient is \((-2k-2)\) — these feed directly into the sum-of-roots formula next.
Step 3

Since \(A\) and \(B\) lie on the line, their \(x\)-coordinates are exactly the two roots of \((\ast)\).

\[ 2x^{2}+(-2k-2)x+(k^{2}+4k-1)=0 \]
Hint: Each intersection point \((x,y)\) must satisfy both equations, so its \(x\) value solves the quadratic \((\ast)\). That is the bridge from geometry to algebra.
The \(x\)-coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\) are the roots \(a,b\) of \(2x^{2}+(-2k-2)x+(k^{2}+4k-1)=0\).

B Part (b) — Use relations of roots to find the mid-point

Let \(a\) and \(b\) be the \(x\)-coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\). Then \(a,b\) are the roots of \((\ast)\).

0 / 3
Step 1

Apply the sum-of-roots relation to \((\ast)\):

\[ 2x^{2}+(-2k-2)x+(k^{2}+4k-1)=0 \] \[ a+b = -\frac{\text{coefficient of } x}{\text{coefficient of } x^{2}} = -\frac{-2k-2}{2} = k+1 \]
Hint: Two minus signs cancel: \(-\dfrac{-2k-2}{2}=+\dfrac{2k+2}{2}=k+1\). Watch out for this classic sign slip.
Step 2

The \(x\)-coordinate of the mid-point \(M\) of \(AB\) is the average of the two \(x\)-coordinates:

\[ x_M = \frac{a+b}{2} \]
Hint: The mid-point of a segment always averages the two endpoints — this works for the \(x\)-coordinates and the \(y\)-coordinates separately.
Step 3

Substitute \(a+b=k+1\):

\[ x_M = \frac{a+b}{2} = \frac{1+k}{2} \]
Hint: The answer \(\dfrac{1+k}{2}\) grows with \(k\) — drag the slider in the diagram above to verify that \(M\) shifts right as \(k\) increases.
\(x\)-coordinate of the mid-point of \(AB\) \( = \dfrac{1+k}{2}\)  →  Answer C

Summary of Answers

  • Line rearranged: \(y = x-k\)
  • Intersection quadratic: \(2x^{2}+(-2k-2)x+(k^{2}+4k-1)=0\)
  • Sum of roots: \(a+b = k+1\)
  • Mid-point \(x\)-coordinate: \(\dfrac{a+b}{2} = \dfrac{1+k}{2}\)
  • Correct option: C  (\(\dfrac{1+k}{2}\))
