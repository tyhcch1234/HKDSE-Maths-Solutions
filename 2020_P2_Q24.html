Question

The point \( P \) is translated leftwards by 4 units to the point \( Q \). If the coordinates of the reflection image of \( Q \) with respect to the \( y \)-axis are \( (5,-1) \), then the polar coordinates of \( P \) are

  • A. \(\left(1,45^{\circ}\right)\)
  • B. \(\left(1,225^{\circ}\right)\)
  • C. \(\left(\sqrt{2},45^{\circ}\right)\)
  • D. \(\left(\sqrt{2},225^{\circ}\right)\)

Solution

Step 1 · Undo the reflection
Reflection in the y-axis \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,\,y)\) The image is \((5,-1)\), so undoing the reflection gives \(Q=(-5,-1)\).

Reflection in the \(y\)-axis sends \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)\). Since the image of \(Q\) is \((5,-1)\),

\[ Q = (-5,-1). \]
Step 2 · Undo the translation
Translation \((x,y)\mapsto(x+a,\,y+b)\) \(P\) moved \(4\) units left, so \(P=Q+(4,0)=(-1,-1)\).

\(P\) moved 4 units left to become \(Q\), so \(Q = P + (-4,0)\), hence

\[ P = Q + (4,0) = (-5+4,\,-1) = (-1,-1). \]
Step 3 · Convert to polar coordinates
Polar Coordinates \(r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}},\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}\) \(P=(-1,-1)\) gives \(r=\sqrt2\) and \(\theta=180^\circ+45^\circ=225^\circ\).
\[ r = \sqrt{(-1)^{2}+(-1)^{2}} = \sqrt{2}. \]

\(P=(-1,-1)\) lies in the third quadrant with reference angle \(45^{\circ}\),
so

\[ \theta = 180^{\circ}+45^{\circ} = 225^{\circ}. \]

Question. \(P\) is translated \(2\) units to the right to \(Q\), and \(Q\) is then reflected in the \(y\)-axis to give \((2,-4)\). Find the polar coordinates of \(P\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Undo the reflection: \(Q=(-2,-4)\).
Step 2. Undo the translation: \(P=Q-(2,0)=(-4,-4)\).
Step 3. \(r=\sqrt{(-4)^{2}+(-4)^{2}}=\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt2\approx5.66\).
Step 4. \(P\) is in the third quadrant with reference angle \(45^\circ\),
so \(\theta=180^\circ+45^\circ=225^\circ\).
Answer: \(\left(4\sqrt2,\ 225^\circ\right)\)
Final Answer: D. \(\left(\sqrt{2},\,225^{\circ}\right)\)
💡 Hint: Always undo the transformations in reverse order — first the reflection, then the translation. Draw the point to check its quadrant before deciding the polar angle.
