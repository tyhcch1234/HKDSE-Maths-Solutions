Geometry · Tangent to a Circle, Alternate Segment & Exterior Angle of a Triangle

Tangent Angle in Alternate Segment Exterior Angle of △ Cyclic Quadrilateral

QQuestion

In the figure, \(TAD\) is the tangent to the circle \(ABCDE\) at the point \(A\). \(PD\) produced and \(PC\) produced meet at the point \(P\); in fact \(P\), \(D\), \(E\) are collinear and \(P\), \(C\), \(B\) are collinear. It is given that \(\angle DAT = 55^\circ\) and \(\angle BPD = 29^\circ\). Also \(\angle ACB = 43^\circ\). Then \(\angle CBE = \)

A. \(26^\circ\)
B. \(69^\circ\)  ✓
C. \(72^\circ\)
D. \(82^\circ\)

(1 mark for the correct option — reasoning shown below)

config angle = 55°

1Step 1 — Use the Tangent–Chord Angle (Alternate Segment)

∠ACD = 55°
Step 1Key Fact

The tangent \(TA\) at \(A\) and the chord \(AC\) give us the angle in the alternate segment. By the tangent–chord angle (angle in alternate segment) theorem,

\[ \angle ACD = \angle DAT = 55^\circ \]
Hint: The angle between the tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment subtended by that chord. Here the chord is \(AC\), so \(\angle DAT = \angle ACD\).
So \(\angle ACD = 55^\circ\).

2Step 2 — Exterior Angle in \(\triangle PCD\)

∠CDP + 29° = ∠DCB
Step 2

In \(\triangle PCD\), produce side \(DC\) to \(B\), so that \(\angle DCB\) is an exterior angle of the triangle at \(C\). For a triangle:

\[ \text{exterior angle} = \text{sum of the two interior opposite angles} \] \[ \angle CDP + \angle CPD = \angle DCB \]

We know \(\angle CPD = \angle BPD = 29^\circ\). But what is \(\angle DCB\)? Since \(C\), \(B\), \(D\), \(A\) are concyclic, \(\angle DCB\) is an inscribed angle subtending the same arc \(DB\) as… let's use the careful route below.

Hint: \(\angle DCB\) is the angle at \(C\) of the cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\). It is related to the angles at \(A\) and \(D\).
Equation: \(\angle CDP + 29^\circ = \angle DCB\).
Step 3

Now find \(\angle DCB\). In \(\triangle ABC\), the angles at \(A\) and \(C\) are known: \[ \angle ACB = 43^\circ,\qquad \angle BAC = \angle DAC + \angle BAD = ? \]

We need \(\angle DAC\). Since \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\) are concyclic, \(\angle DAC\) (which subtends \(DC\)) equals \(\angle DBC\) in the same segment. Also by the tangent–chord theorem at chord \(AB\), \(\angle BAT = \angle ACB = 43^\circ\).

But the cleanest route: \(\angle CAD = \angle ACD = 55^\circ\) (isosceles from alternate segment, since chord \(AC\) gives \(\angle ACD\)). Actually \(\angle ACD=55^\circ\) and \(\angle ACB = 43^\circ\), so

\[ \angle DCB = \angle ACD + \angle ACB = 55^\circ + 43^\circ = 98^\circ \]
Hint: At \(C\), the two angles \(\angle ACD\) and \(\angle ACB\) lie on the same side of line \(CB\), so \(\angle DCB = \angle ACD + \angle ACB\).
\(\angle DCB = 55^\circ + 43^\circ = 98^\circ\).
Step 4
\[ \angle CDP + 29^\circ = 98^\circ \] \[ \angle CDP = 98^\circ - 29^\circ = 69^\circ \]
Hint: Simple subtraction. \(\angle CDP\) is the interior angle at \(D\) of \(\triangle PCD\).
\(\angle CDP = 69^\circ\).

3Step 3 — Exterior Angle = Interior Opposite Angle (Cyclic Quadrilateral)

∠CBE = ∠CDP = 69°
Step 5

Consider the cyclic quadrilateral \(BCDE\) (since \(B\), \(C\), \(D\), \(E\) all lie on the circle). At vertex \(D\), the exterior angle \(\angle CDP\) forms a straight line with the interior angle \(\angle CDE\):

\[ \angle CDP + \angle CDE = 180^\circ \]

Also in cyclic quadrilateral \(BCDE\), opposite interior angles sum to \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle CDE + \angle CBE = 180^\circ \]

Comparing the two equations:

\[ \angle CBE = \angle CDP = 69^\circ \]
Hint: The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle. Here the exterior angle at \(D\) (namely \(\angle CDP\), since \(P\) lies on \(DE\) produced) equals the interior opposite angle at \(B\).
∴ \(\angle CBE = 69^\circ\). Answer: B

✓Summary of Answers

  • Angle sum at \(C\): \(\angle DCB = 55^\circ + 43^\circ = 98^\circ\)
  • Exterior angle of \(\triangle PCD\): \(\angle CDP = 98^\circ - 29^\circ = 69^\circ\)
  • Exterior angle of cyclic quadrilateral: \(\angle CBE = \angle CDP = 69^\circ\)
  • Final answer: B. \(69^\circ\)
