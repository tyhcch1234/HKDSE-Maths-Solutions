Question

Make $a$ the subject of the formula $$ \frac{4-3a}{b}=5 . $$

Step-by-step Solution

1Clear the fraction — multiply both sides by $b$.

$$ \frac{4-3a}{b}\times b = 5\times b $$ $$ 4-3a = 5b \qquad (\text{1M}) $$

Hint: whatever you multiply on the left, do the same on the right — the scale must stay balanced.
Clearing a Denominator \(\dfrac{A}{B}=C\ \Longrightarrow\ A=BC\quad(B\neq0)\) Multiplying both sides by \(b\) removes the fraction: \(4-3a=5b\).
2Collect the $a$-term on one side.

Add $3a$ to both sides, then subtract $5b$: $$ 4 = 5b + 3a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3a = 4 - 5b \qquad (\text{1M}) $$

Hint: move the constant $4$ to the right and the $b$-term to the left so all $a$'s stand together.
Transposition of Terms \(a+b=c\ \Longrightarrow\ a=c-b\) Move \(-3a\) and \(5b\) across so that all \(a\)-terms stand together: \(3a=4-5b\).
3Divide both sides by $3$ to isolate $a$.

$$ \frac{3a}{3}=\frac{4-5b}{3} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=\frac{4-5b}{3} \qquad (\text{1A}) $$

Hint: divide every single term on the right by $3$ — you cannot leave $4$ untouched.
Balancing Method for a Linear Equation \(ax=b\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a\neq0)\) Dividing every term by \(3\) gives \(a=\dfrac{4-5b}{3}\).
Answer: $\;a=\dfrac{4-5b}{3}$

Question. Make \(a\) the subject of the formula \(\dfrac{5-2a}{b}=3\). [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Multiply both sides by \(b\): \(5-2a=3b\).
Step 2. Isolate the \(a\)-term: \(2a=5-3b\).
Step 3. Divide both sides by \(2\): \(a=\dfrac{5-3b}{2}\).
Step 4. Check with \(b=1\): \(a=\dfrac{5-3}{2}=1\), and \(\dfrac{5-2}{1}=3\) \u2713.
Answer: \(a=\dfrac{5-3b}{2}\)
