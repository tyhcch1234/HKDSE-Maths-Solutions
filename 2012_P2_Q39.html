[2012 MC #39] The figure shows the graph of a trigonometric function for $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$.
The graph attains its maximum value $7$ at $x=0^\circ$ and $x=180^\circ$, and its minimum value $1$ at $x=90^\circ$. Which of the following is the graph?
A.  $y=1+3\cos\dfrac{x^\circ}{2}$
B.  $y=1+3\cos 2x^\circ$
C.  $y=4+3\cos\dfrac{x^\circ}{2}$
D.  $y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ$
STEP 1 · READ THE GRAPH From the figure:
• Maximum value = $7$ (at $x=0^\circ$ and $x=180^\circ$)
• Minimum value = $1$ (at $x=90^\circ$)
So the graph of $y$ lies between $1$ and $7$.
STEP 2 · FIND AMPLITUDE & VERTICAL SHIFT For $y=a+b\cos(\text{something})$: $$b=\frac{7-1}{2}=3,\qquad a=\frac{7+1}{2}=4$$ Hence the function has value $4$ at the "middle" and oscillates $\pm 3$: $$y=4+3\cos(\theta)$$ This immediately eliminates options A and B (which use $1$, not $4$).
Edges of the curve: $4+3=7$ (max) and $4-3=1$ (min). ✔
STEP 3 · CHECK THE PERIOD Using $y=4+3\cos(kx^\circ)$:
The cosine has its minimum when $kx^\circ = 180^\circ$.
But the graph is minimum at $x=90^\circ$: $$k\cdot 90^\circ = 180^\circ \Rightarrow k=2$$ So the angle is $2x^\circ$, i.e. $y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ$.
Equivalently, $\cos x^\circ$ completes a full cycle over $360^\circ$; this curve repeats in $180^\circ$, so it is halved in period → factor $2$.
STEP 4 · VERIFY OPTION D Check $y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ$ at the key points: $$x=0^\circ:\ y=4+3\cos 0^\circ=4+3=7 \ ✔$$ $$x=90^\circ:\ y=4+3\cos 180^\circ=4-3=1 \ ✔$$ $$x=180^\circ:\ y=4+3\cos 360^\circ=4+3=7 \ ✔$$ The curve starts at a maximum, dips to exactly $1$ at $90^\circ$ and returns to $7$ at $180^\circ$ — matching the figure.
Option C would give $y=4+3\cos 45^\circ\approx 6.12$ at $x=90^\circ$, which is not the minimum. ✘
ANSWER:  D  —  $y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ$
