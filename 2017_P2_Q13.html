[2017 MC #13]

In the figure, the 1st pattern consists of $1$ dot. For any positive integer $n$, the $(n+1)$th pattern is formed by adding $(2n+2)$ dots to the $n$th pattern. Find the number of dots in the 7th pattern.

A. 41
B. 55
C. 71
D. 161
STEP 1 · Set up the notation Let $T(n)$ be the number of dots in the $n$th pattern.
Recurrence Relation \(T(n+1)=T(n)+(2n+2),\quad T(1)=1\) Each pattern adds \(2n+2\) dots to the previous one, starting from \(T(1)=1\).
From the question: the rule is $T(n+1)=T(n)+(2n+2)$, and the starting value is $$T(1)=1 .$$ Hint: Always name your sequence first and write down the first term.
STEP 2 · List the increments For $n=1,2,3,\dots$ the number of dots added is $$2n+2 = 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12,\ 14,\ \dots$$ So to reach the 7th pattern we start at $T(1)$ and add $6$ increments (for $n=1$ up to $n=6$). Hint: Going from the 1st to the 7th pattern needs $7-1=6$ additions.
STEP 3 · Recurse from $T(7)$ $$T(7)=T(6)+14 \quad (\text{using } n=6)$$ $$=T(5)+12+14 \quad (\text{using } n=5)$$ $$\ \ \vdots$$ $$=T(1)+4+6+8+10+12+14 .$$ Hint: Keep substituting until you reach the known value $T(1)$.
STEP 4 · Evaluate the sum
Sum of an Arithmetic Series \(S=\dfrac{n\,(a+\ell)}{2}\) The increments \(4,6,\dots,14\) form an A.S. with \(6\) terms, so the sum is \(\dfrac{6(4+14)}{2}=54\).
$$T(7)=1+(4+6+8+10+12+14)$$ The bracket is an arithmetic series with first term $4$,
last term $14$, $6$ terms: $$4+6+8+10+12+14=\frac{6(4+14)}{2}=54 .$$ So $$T(7)=1+54=55 .$$ Hint: Sum of an A.S. $=\dfrac{\text{no. of terms}\times(\text{first}+\text{last})}{2}$.
STEP 5 · Check against the options $T(7)=55$, which matches option B.
Checking with a Closed Form \(T(n)=n^{2}+n-1\) The closed form gives \(T(7)=49+7-1=55\), confirming the recurrence result.
(Note: $T(n)=n^2+n-1$ gives $T(7)=49+7-1=55$ — a quick verification.) Hint: A closed-form check guards against arithmetic slips.
Answer: B  (55 dots) The 7th pattern contains 55 dots.

Question. The number of dots in the \(n\)th pattern of a sequence is \(T(n)\), where \(T(n+1)=T(n)+(3n+1)\) and \(T(1)=2\). Find \(T(6)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The increments for \(n=1,2,3,4,5\) are \(4,7,10,13,16\).
Step 2. Reaching the 6th pattern needs \(6-1=5\) additions, so \(T(6)=T(1)+(4+7+10+13+16)\).
Step 3. The bracket is an A.S. with first term \(4\), last term \(16\) and \(5\) terms: sum \(=\dfrac{5(4+16)}{2}=50\).
Step 4. Therefore \(T(6)=2+50=52\).
Answer: \(T(6)=52\)
