\(y=a+\log_b x\) \(y=\log_c x\) x-intercepts lengths \(OS, OT\)
HKDSE 2021 · Paper 2 · Q33

Let \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) be positive constants. On the same rectangular coordinate system, the graph of \(y=a+\log_b x\) and the graph of \(y=\log_c x\) cut the \(x\)-axis at the points \(S\) and \(T\) respectively. Denote the origin by \(O\). Find \(OT:OS\).

  • A. \(1:b^a\)
  • B. \(1:c^a\)
  • C. \(b^a:1\)
  • D. \(c^a:1\)

Step-by-step Solution

1
Converting a Logarithmic Equation to Index Form \(\log_{b}x=y\iff x=b^{y}\) Setting \(y=0\) gives \(\log_{b}x=-a\), so \(S=(b^{-a},0)\).
Find the x-intercept \(S\) of \(y=a+\log_b x\).
Put \(y=0\): \[ 0 = a+\log_b x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log_b x=-a \] Rewriting in exponential form, \[ x=b^{-a} \] So \(\;S=(b^{-a},\,0)\). A log equation turns into an index equation: \(\log_b x=-a \iff x=b^{-a}\).
2
Find the x-intercept \(T\) of \(y=\log_c x\).
Put \(y=0\): \[ 0 = \log_c x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=c^{0}=1 \] So \(\;T=(1,\,0)\). Any log of \(1\) is \(0\),
because \(c^{0}=1\) for every positive base \(c\).
3
Distance from the Origin on an Axis \(OX=x_{X}-0\) Both \(S\) and \(T\) lie on the positive \(x\)-axis, so \(OS=b^{-a}\) and \(OT=1\).
Express the two lengths as coordinates.
Since \(O=(0,0)\), and both \(S\) and \(T\) lie on the \(x\)-axis, \[ OT = 1-0 = 1, \qquad OS = b^{-a}-0 = b^{-a}. \] Both points are to the right of \(O\) on the positive \(x\)-axis,
so each length is just the \(x\)-coordinate.
4
Index Law for Negative Exponents \(b^{a}\cdot b^{-a}=b^{0}=1\) Multiplying both parts of \(1:b^{-a}\) by \(b^{a}\) clears the negative index.
Form the ratio and simplify with index laws.
\[ OT:OS = 1:b^{-a} \] Multiply both parts by \(b^{a}\) (an index law: \(b^{a}\cdot b^{-a}=b^{0}=1\)): \[ 1:b^{-a} \;=\; b^{a}:1 \] To clear a negative index in a ratio,
multiply both terms by the corresponding positive power.
5
Compare with the options.
The required ratio is \(\;b^{a}:1\), which matches option C. Note \(c\) plays no role — it only fixes where the graph sits,
not where it cuts the axis.
Answer
C. \(\;b^{a}:1\)

Question. The graph of \(y=a+\log_{b}x\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(S\), and the graph of \(y=\log_{b}x-2\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(T\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are positive constants with \(b\neq1\). Find \(OS:OT\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. At \(S\): \(0=a+\log_{b}x\), so \(\log_{b}x=-a\) and \(x=b^{-a}\).
Step 2. At \(T\): \(0=\log_{b}x-2\), so \(\log_{b}x=2\) and \(x=b^{2}\).
Step 3. Hence \(OS=b^{-a}\) and \(OT=b^{2}\), giving \(OS:OT=b^{-a}:b^{2}\).
Step 4. Multiply both parts by \(b^{a}\): \(1:b^{a+2}\).
Answer: \(1:b^{a+2}\)
