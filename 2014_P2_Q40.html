Q40 The Question

In the figure, \(AB\) is a vertical pole standing on the horizontal ground \(BCD\), where \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\). If the angle between the plane \(ACD\) and the horizontal ground is \(\theta\), then \(\tan\theta =\)
A. \(\dfrac{8}{15}\)
B. \(\dfrac{15}{8}\)
C. \(\dfrac{15}{17}\)
D. \(\dfrac{17}{15}\)

Given in the figure: \(AB = 8\text{ m}\), \(BC = 8\text{ m}\), \(BD = 15\text{ m}\), with \(AB\) vertical and \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\).

Step 1 Find the length \(CD\)

Since \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\), triangle \(BCD\) is right-angled at \(B\). We apply Pythagoras' theorem on the horizontal ground.

BC = 8 BD = 15 CD = 17

Step 1

Apply Pythagoras' theorem to right-angled triangle \(BCD\):

\[ CD^2 = BC^2 + BD^2 = 8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289 \] \[ CD = \sqrt{289} = 17 \text{ m} \]
Hint: A \(8\text{–}15\text{–}17\) triangle is a Pythagorean triple, so the hypotenuse is exactly \(17\).
\(CD = 17\text{ m}\)
Step 2

This right angle at \(B\) is crucial: it lets us treat the base triangle as a flat right triangle before moving into 3D.

Hint: The angle between plane \(ACD\) and the ground is not just a simple right-triangle angle — we must project \(A\) onto the line \(CD\).
Ready to find the perpendicular height from \(B\) to \(CD\).

Step 2 Locate the foot \(H\) on \(CD\)

To find the angle between the plane \(ACD\) and the ground, we need a line in the plane \(ACD\) perpendicular to the intersection line \(CD\). Let \(H\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(B\) to \(CD\).

Move point \(H\) along \(CD\): Position
∠BHC = 90° BH = 120/17 m
0 / 3
Step 1

Since \(AB\) is vertical, \(AB \perp\) the horizontal ground, so \(AB \perp CD\). Also \(BH \perp CD\) by construction. Thus \(CD \perp\) the plane \(ABH\).

Hint: A line perpendicular to two intersecting lines in a plane is perpendicular to the whole plane.
\(CD \perp ABH\)
Step 2

Because \(CD \perp ABH\), the angle between plane \(ACD\) and the ground is the angle \(\angle AHB\) in the vertical cross-section \(ABH\).

Hint: The "angle between planes" reduces to an angle in the perpendicular cross-section.
\(\theta = \angle AHB\)
Step 3

Find \(BH\) using the area of \(\triangle BCD\) in two ways:

\[ \tfrac12 \cdot BC \cdot BD = \tfrac12 \cdot CD \cdot BH \] \[ \tfrac12 (8)(15) = \tfrac12 (17)(BH) \] \[ 120 = 17\,BH \quad \Rightarrow \quad BH = \frac{120}{17} \text{ m} \]
Hint: The area formula \(\tfrac12 bh\) works from any chosen base — pick \(CD\) to expose \(BH\).
\(BH = \dfrac{120}{17}\text{ m}\)

Step 3 Compute \(\tan\theta = \tan\angle AHB\)

Now look at right triangle \(ABH\): \(AB = 8\) is vertical, \(BH = \frac{120}{17}\) is horizontal, and \(\theta = \angle AHB\). Adjust the slider to change \(AB\) and watch \(\tan\theta\) change.

Step 1

In the right-angled triangle \(ABH\) (right angle at \(B\)):

\[ \tan\theta = \frac{AB}{BH} = \frac{8}{\frac{120}{17}} = \frac{8 \times 17}{120} \]
Hint: \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{AB}{BH}\).
\(\tan\theta = \dfrac{136}{120} = \dfrac{17}{15}\)
Step 2

Simplify \(\dfrac{136}{120}\) by dividing numerator and denominator by 8:

\[ \frac{136}{120} = \frac{136 \div 8}{120 \div 8} = \frac{17}{15} \]
Hint: The final answer matches option D.
\(\tan\theta = \dfrac{17}{15}\)  →  Answer: D

Summary of the Solution

  • Since \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\), triangle \(BCD\) is right-angled at \(B\): \[CD = \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = 17\text{ m}\]
  • Let \(H\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(B\) to \(CD\). Then \(BH \perp CD\) and \(AB \perp CD\), so \(CD \perp\) plane \(ABH\).
  • Therefore the angle between plane \(ACD\) and the ground is \(\theta = \angle AHB\).
  • Area of \(\triangle BCD\) gives \[\tfrac12(8)(15) = \tfrac12(17)(BH) \;\Rightarrow\; BH = \tfrac{120}{17}\text{ m}\]
  • In right triangle \(ABH\): \[\tan\theta = \frac{AB}{BH} = \frac{8}{120/17} = \frac{136}{120} = \frac{17}{15}\]
  • Final answer: \[\tan\theta = \frac{17}{15}\]
  • Correct option: D  (\(\frac{17}{15}\))
