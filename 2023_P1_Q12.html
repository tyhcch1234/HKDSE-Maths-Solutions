QQuestion

It is given that \(f(x)\) is partly constant and partly varies as \(x^{2}\). Suppose that \(f(10)=62\) and \(f(15)=122\).

  1. Find \(f(5)\). [3 marks]
  2. Suppose that \(U(0,\,u)\) and \(V(5,\,v)\) are points lying on the graph of \(y=f(x)\). The horizontal line passing through \(V\) cuts the \(y\)-axis at the point \(W\). Denote the circle which passes through \(U\), \(V\) and \(W\) by \(C\). Express the circumference of \(C\) in terms of \(\pi\). [4 marks]

2Step-by-Step Solution

Tap a step to open it. Read the hint first and try it yourself!

1 Set up the variation model
Hint — what does “partly constant and partly varies as \(x^2\)” mean?
“Partly constant” contributes a fixed number \(k\) (independent of \(x\)).
“Partly varies as \(x^2\)” contributes \(a\,x^{2}\) where \(a\) is a constant of variation.

So the function has the form

\[ f(x) = k + a\,x^{2} \]

Two unknown constants \(k\) and \(a\) \(\Rightarrow\) we need the two given data points to find them.

Meaning of symbolValue
\(k\)the constant part (unknown)
\(a\)constant of variation for the \(x^2\) term (unknown)
\(x\)the input of the function
2 Form two equations from \(f(10)=62\) and \(f(15)=122\)
Hint: Substitute \(x=10\) and \(x=15\) into \(f(x)=k+a\,x^{2}\).
\[ k + a(10)^{2} = 62 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k + 100a = 62 \tag{1} \] \[ k + a(15)^{2} = 122 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k + 225a = 122 \tag{2} \]

This is a pair of simultaneous linear equations in \(k\) and \(a\).

\(k+100a=62\) ✔ (from \(f(10)=62\))
\(k+225a=122\) ✔ (from \(f(15)=122\))
3 Solve for \(a\) and \(k\)
Hint: Subtract (1) from (2) — the \(k\)'s cancel, leaving only \(a\).
\[ (2)-(1):\quad (225a-100a) = 122-62 \] \[ 125a = 60 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \frac{60}{125} = \frac{12}{25} = 0.48 \]

⚠️ Careful — let's re-check: \(60/125 = 0.48\), which does not satisfy both data points. Let's instead solve properly using both points together with \(f\)'s form; a cleaner route appears in Step 4.

Actually, substituting \(a=0.48\) gives \(k = 62-48 = 14\), then \(f(15)=14+225(0.48)=122\) ✔. So \(k = 14\), \(a = 0.48\), and \(f(x) = 14 + 0.48x^{2}\).
4 (a) Find \(f(5)\)
Hint: Now that \(f(x)=14+0.48x^{2}\), substitute \(x=5\).
\[ f(5) = 14 + 0.48(5)^{2} = 14 + 0.48(25) = 14 + 12 = 26 \]
Answer (a): \(f(5) = 26\)

Check with the ratio idea: from \(f(10)=62\) to \(f(15)=122\) the “\(x^2\) part” increases by \(122-62=60\) as \(x^2\) goes from 100 to 225 (rise 125). So \(a=60/125=0.48\). ✔

5 (b) Locate \(U\), \(V\) and \(W\)
Hint: \(U\) and \(V\) lie on \(y=f(x)\). Use \(f(0)\) for \(U\) (which has \(x=0\)) and \(f(5)\) for \(V\).
\(W\) is where the horizontal line through \(V\) meets the \(y\)-axis — so \(W\) has the same \(y\)-coordinate as \(V\), and \(x=0\).
\[ u = f(0) = 14 + 0.48(0)^{2} = 14 \quad\Rightarrow\quad U(0,\,14) \] \[ v = f(5) = 26 \quad\Rightarrow\quad V(5,\,26) \] \[ W = (0,\,26) \]
\(U(0,14)\), \(V(5,26)\), \(W(0,26)\)

Notice: \(U\) and \(W\) both lie on the \(y\)-axis, so \(UW\) is a vertical chord.

6 Recognise the right angle at \(W\)
Hint — a key geometry fact: because \(W\) lies on the horizontal line through \(V\), \(WV\) is horizontal. And \(WU\) is vertical (both on the \(y\)-axis). A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular.

Therefore \(\angle UWV = 90^{\circ}\).

By the converse of the angle in a semicircle (Thales' theorem), the circle through \(U\), \(V\) and \(W\) must have \(UV\) as a diameter.

\(UV\) is a diameter of circle \(C\).
7 Find the diameter \(UV\) by the distance formula
Hint — distance formula: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\). Don't forget to square before adding, and take the positive root at the end.
\[ UV = \sqrt{(5-0)^2+(26-14)^2} = \sqrt{25+144} = \sqrt{169} = 13 \]
Diameter \(= 13\), so radius \(r = \dfrac{13}{2}\).
8 Compute the circumference of \(C\)
Hint — circumference formula: \(C_{\text{circ}} = 2\pi r\) (or \(\pi d\)). Leave your answer in terms of \(\pi\).
\[ \text{Circumference} = \pi \times UV = \pi \times 13 = 13\pi \]
Answer (b): the circumference of \(C\) is \(13\pi\).

★Key Takeaways

  • Partial variation template: “partly constant and partly varies as \(x^2\)” \(\Rightarrow f(x)=k+ax^{2}\). Two constants \(\Rightarrow\) two data points.
  • Subtract to eliminate: \((k+225a)-(k+100a)=125a\), giving \(a=\dfrac{122-62}{225-100}\).
  • Geometry shortcut: horizontal line + vertical line \(\Rightarrow 90^{\circ}\); angle in a semicircle \(\Rightarrow\) the third side is a diameter.
  • Distance formula: \(UV=\sqrt{(5-0)^2+(26-14)^2}=\sqrt{169}=13\) — a nice integer always helps.
  • Circumference: \(\pi \times \text{diameter} = 13\pi\).
