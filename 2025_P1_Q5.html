# Polynomials # Common Factor # Difference of Squares # Grouping

Q The Question

Factorize

(4 marks) (a)  $10pr - 6qr$
(b)  $25p^{2} - 9q^{2}$
(c)  $25p^{2} - 9q^{2} - 10pr + 6qr$
Strategy tip: Part (c) is not a coincidence — it is built from parts (a) and (b). Part (a) gives you one factor, part (b) gives you another, and part (c) asks you to combine them by grouping.

≡ Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 — Part (a): Spot the common factor

Look at $10pr - 6qr$. Both terms contain the factor $r$, and both numerical coefficients $10$ and $6$ share the factor $2$.

$10pr - 6qr$
Hint — Highest Common Factor (HCF):
HCF of $10$ and $6$ is $2$.
The variable $r$ appears in both terms, so it is also common.
Common factor $= 2r$.

Take out $2r$ from each term:

$10pr - 6qr = 2r(5p - 3q)$

✔ Answer (a): $2r(5p - 3q)$  — [1A]

Step 2 — Part (b): Recognise a difference of two squares

The expression $25p^{2} - 9q^{2}$ has the form $A^{2} - B^{2}$.

Hint — Formula (not yet substituted):
$A^{2} - B^{2} = (A + B)(A - B)$

Here:  $A = 5p$  (since $A^{2} = 25p^{2}$)
   $B = 3q$  (since $B^{2} = 9q^{2}$)

Substitute $A = 5p$ and $B = 3q$ into the formula:

$25p^{2} - 9q^{2} = (5p + 3q)(5p - 3q)$

✔ Answer (b): $(5p + 3q)(5p - 3q)$  — [1A]

Step 3 — Part (c): Reorder and group the terms

Part (c) combines the pieces from (a) and (b). Write it so the two "types" of terms sit together.

$25p^{2} - 9q^{2} - 10pr + 6qr$

Group the squares together, and the $r$-terms together. Notice we take out a minus sign from the second group:

$= (25p^{2} - 9q^{2}) \; - \; (10pr - 6qr)$
Why the parenthesis changes signs: inside the second bracket we need $+10pr - 6qr$ after multiplying by $-1$. That is $-1 \times (10pr - 6qr) = -10pr + 6qr$. ✓

Step 4 — Substitute the results from (a) and (b)

Now replace each bracket with the factorised form you already found.

$(25p^{2} - 9q^{2}) - (10pr - 6qr)$
$= (5p + 3q)(5p - 3q) - 2r(5p - 3q)$
Hint — The hidden common bracket:
Look carefully: $(5p - 3q)$ appears in both terms.
It acts just like the letter $r$ did in part (a) — it can be taken out as a common factor.

This substitution line earns the [1M] method mark.

Step 5 — Factor out the common bracket

Treat $(5p - 3q)$ as a single object and take it out front.

$(5p + 3q)\underbrace{(5p - 3q)}_{\text{common}} \; - \; 2r\underbrace{(5p - 3q)}_{\text{common}}$
Hint — Compare with part (a):
In (a): $5p \cdot (2r) - 3q \cdot (2r) = (2r)(5p - 3q)$
Here: $(5p + 3q)\cdot(\;) - 2r\cdot(\;) = (5p - 3q)(5p + 3q - 2r)$
The bracket plays exactly the role of $2r$.
$= (5p - 3q)(5p + 3q - 2r)$

✔ Answer (c): $(5p - 3q)(5p + 3q - 2r)$  — [1A]

Step 6 — Check your answer by expanding

Always verify. Multiply the brackets back out:

$(5p - 3q)(5p + 3q - 2r)$
$= 5p(5p + 3q - 2r) - 3q(5p + 3q - 2r)$
$= 25p^{2} + 15pq - 10pr - 15pq - 9q^{2} + 6qr$
$= 25p^{2} - 9q^{2} - 10pr + 6qr$   ✔ matches the original
Exam tip: The $+15pq$ and $-15pq$ cancel — that is why the original question had no $pq$ term. Seeing which terms are missing often reveals the hidden structure.

✓ Marking Scheme at a Glance

1A (a)  $10pr - 6qr = 2r(5p - 3q)$
1A (b)  $25p^{2} - 9q^{2} = (5p + 3q)(5p - 3q)$
1M (c)  $= (5p + 3q)(5p - 3q) - 2r(5p - 3q)$  (method: linking a & b)
1A (c)  $= (5p - 3q)(5p + 3q - 2r)$
