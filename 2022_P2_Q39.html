Question

[2022MC#39]

For $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$, how many roots does the equation $$\sin^2 x = 6\cos^2 x$$ have?

  • A. 2
  • B. 3
  • C. 4
  • D. 5
Step 1 · Use the Pythagorean identity

Recall the fundamental identity:

$$\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$$

Substitute this into $\sin^2 x = 6\cos^2 x$ to turn the equation into a single-trig-function equation.

The diagram highlights the unit circle — every solution is a point on it where the height equals the required value.
Pythagorean Identity \(\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x=1\) Replacing \(\cos^{2}x\) by \(1-\sin^{2}x\) leaves an equation in \(\sin x\) alone.
Step 2 · Reduce to $\sin x = k$

\[ \sin^2 x = 6\cos^2 x = 6(1-\sin^2 x) \] \[ \sin^2 x = 6 - 6\sin^2 x \] \[ 7\sin^2 x = 6 \] \[ \sin^2 x = \frac{6}{7} \]

So $\displaystyle \sin x = \pm\sqrt{\frac{6}{7}} \approx \pm 0.9258$.

Reducing to a Single Trigonometric Ratio \(\sin^{2}x=6\cos^{2}x\ \Rightarrow\ 7\sin^{2}x=6\) \(\sin^{2}x=\dfrac{6}{7}\), so \(\sin x=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{6}{7}}\).
Step 3 · Count roots for $\sin x = +\sqrt{6/7}$

For $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$, the equation $\sin x = k>0$ has two solutions in the first and second quadrants.

$$x \approx 67.79^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad x \approx 112.21^\circ$$

The diagram shows the upper red line and the two green intersection points on the circle.

Number of Solutions of sin x = k \(-1 Each of \(\sin x=+\sqrt{\dfrac{6}{7}}\) and \(\sin x=-\sqrt{\dfrac{6}{7}}\) gives two roots, so four in total.
Step 4 · Count roots for $\sin x = -\sqrt{6/7}$

For $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$, the equation $\sin x = k<0$ has two solutions in the third and fourth quadrants.

$$x \approx 247.79^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad x \approx 292.21^\circ$$

The diagram shows the lower red line and the other two green intersection points.

Step 5 · Add up the roots

Combining the two cases:

$$67.79^\circ,\;112.21^\circ,\;247.79^\circ,\;292.21^\circ$$

All four lie in the required interval $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$.

Key idea: each horizontal line $y=\pm\sqrt{6/7}$ cuts the unit circle twice within one full revolution → $2+2=4$ roots.
Final answer
C. 4 roots

Question. For \(0^{\circ}\le x<360^{\circ}\), how many roots does the equation \(\sin^{2}x=4\cos^{2}x\) have? A. \(2\) B. \(3\) C. \(4\) D. \(6\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Use \(\cos^{2}x=1-\sin^{2}x\): \(\sin^{2}x=4\left(1-\sin^{2}x\right)\).
Step 2. So \(5\sin^{2}x=4\), i.e. \(\sin^{2}x=\dfrac{4}{5}\) and \(\sin x=\pm\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\).
Step 3. \(\sin x=+\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\) has two roots, one in the first and one in the second quadrant.
Step 4. \(\sin x=-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\) has two roots, one in the third and one in the fourth quadrant.
Step 5. All four lie in \(0^{\circ}\le x<360^{\circ}\), so there are \(4\) roots.
Answer: C. \(4\)
