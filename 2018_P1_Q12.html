Polynomials — Factor Theorem & Nature of Roots

Find unknown coefficients a, b from conditions on \(f(x)\), then judge a claim about irrational roots.

Factor Theorem Remainder Theorem Simultaneous Equations Discriminant Δ Irrational Roots

QQuestion

Let \(f(x)=4x(x+1)^2+ax+b\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. It is given that \(x-3\) is a factor of \(f(x)\). When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+2\), the remainder is \(2b+165\).

3 marks (a) Find \(a\) and \(b\).
4 marks (b) Someone claims that the equation \(f(x)=0\) has at least one irrational root. Do you agree? Explain your answer.
🎯 Core idea: “\(x-3\) is a factor” \(\Rightarrow f(3)=0\) (Factor Theorem). “Remainder when divided by \(x+2\) is \(2b+165\)” \(\Rightarrow f(-2)=2b+165\) (Remainder Theorem). Two conditions \(\Rightarrow\) two equations \(\Rightarrow\) solve for \(a,b\). In (b), factorise \(f(x)\), tackle the quadratic with the discriminant, and remember: an irrational root must be real but not rational.

✦Guided Solution

1 Use the factor condition \(x-3\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) ▼

By the Factor Theorem, if \((x-c)\) is a factor of a polynomial, then the polynomial equals \(0\) at \(x=c\). Here \(c=3\).

\[ f(3)=0 \]
Hint: Factor Theorem: \((x-c)\) is a factor \(\iff f(c)=0\).
2 Substitute \(x=3\) to obtain the first equation ▼

Compute \(f(3)\) using the definition \(f(x)=4x(x+1)^2+ax+b\):

\[4(3)(3+1)^2+a(3)+b=0\] \[4(3)(16)+3a+b=0\] \[192+3a+b=0\] \[\boxed{3a+b=-192}\tag{1}\]
Hint: Carefully compute \((3+1)^2=16\), then multiply by \(4\) and \(3\).
3 Use the remainder condition when divided by \(x+2\) ▼

By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder when \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x-c)\) is \(f(c)\). Here the divisor is \(x+2=x-(-2)\), so \(c=-2\).

\[ f(-2)=2b+165 \]
Hint: \(x+2=x-(-2)\), so substitute \(x=-2\).
4 Substitute \(x=-2\) to obtain the second equation ▼
\[4(-2)(-2+1)^2+a(-2)+b=2b+165\] \[4(-2)(1)-2a+b=2b+165\] \[-8-2a+b=2b+165\] \[-2a-b=173\] \[\boxed{2a+b=-173}\tag{2}\]
Watch out: \(( -1)^2=+1\). A very common slip is to write \(-1\).
5 Solve the simultaneous equations for \(a\) and \(b\) ▼

We have:

\[3a+b=-192 \tag{1}\] \[2a+b=-173 \tag{2}\]

Subtract \((2)\) from \((1)\):

\[(3a+b)-(2a+b)=-192-(-173)\] \[a=-19\]

Substitute \(a=-19\) into \((2)\):

\[2(-19)+b=-173 \Rightarrow -38+b=-173 \Rightarrow b=-135\]
Answer (a): \(a=-19\) and \(b=-135\). So \(f(x)=4x(x+1)^2-19x-135\).
6 (b) Set up \(f(x)=0\) and expand ▼
\[4x(x+1)^2-19x-135=0\] \[4x(x^2+2x+1)-19x-135=0\] \[4x^3+8x^2+4x-19x-135=0\] \[4x^3+8x^2-15x-135=0\]
Hint: Expand \((x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1\) first, then multiply each term by \(4x\).
7 Factorise using the known factor \((x-3)\) ▼

Since \(x-3\) is a factor, divide \(4x^3+8x^2-15x-135\) by \((x-3)\):

\[(x-3)(4x^2+20x+45)=0\]

So either

\[x=3 \quad\text{or}\quad 4x^2+20x+45=0\]
Check: \((x-3)(4x^2+20x+45)=4x^3+20x^2+45x-12x^2-60x-135=4x^3+8x^2-15x-135\). ✓
8 (b) Examine the quadratic \(4x^2+20x+45=0\) with the discriminant ▼

For \(4x^2+20x+45=0\), \(a=4,\ b=20,\ c=45\):

\[\Delta = b^2-4ac = 20^2-4(4)(45) = 400-720 = -320 < 0\]

A negative discriminant means the quadratic has no real roots.

Hint: \(\Delta>0\): two distinct real roots; \(\Delta=0\): one repeated real root; \(\Delta<0\): no real roots.
9 (b) Conclusion — the claim is disagreed ▼

The only real root of \(f(x)=0\) is \(x=3\). Note that \(3\) is an integer, hence rational — not irrational. Since the quadratic part gives no real roots, there cannot be any irrational root.

Conclusion: The claim is disagreed.
