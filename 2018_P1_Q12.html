Step 1 — Given: $x-3$ is a factor of $f(x)$

cubic curve $y=f(x)$ root / factor point remainder $(x=-2)$ quadratic factor
HKDSE 2018 Paper 1 · Q12 · Polynomials

[2018#12]

Let $f(x)=4x(x+1)^2+ax+b$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. It is given that $x-3$ is a factor of $f(x)$.

When $f(x)$ is divided by $x+2$, the remainder is $2b+165$.

(a) Find $a$ and $b$.   [3 marks]

(b) Someone claims that the equation $f(x)=0$ has at least one irrational root. Do you agree? Explain your answer.   [4 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

1Use the factor $x-3$ (Factor Theorem).
Factor Theorem \((x-c)\ \text{is a factor of }f(x)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ f(c)=0\) Since \(x-2\) is a factor, \(f(2)=0\) gives \(36+2a+b=0\).

Since $x-3$ is a factor, $f(3)=0$:

$$4(3)(3+1)^2+a(3)+b=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 192+3a+b=0$$

$$\boxed{\,3a+b=-192\,}\quad\text{—— (1)}$$

Hint: “$x-c$ is a factor” $\iff f(c)=0$. Here $c=3$.

2Use the remainder condition at $x=-2$.
Remainder Theorem \(\text{remainder of }f(x)\div(x-c)=f(c)\) The remainder on division by \(x+1\) is \(f(-1)=b-a\), which equals 15.

Dividing by $x+2$ gives remainder $f(-2)$, which equals $2b+165$:

$$4(-2)(-2+1)^2+a(-2)+b=2b+165$$

$$-8-2a+b=2b+165 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{\,2a+b=-173\,}\quad\text{—— (2)}$$

Hint: Remainder Theorem — remainder of $f(x)\div(x+2)$ is $f(-2)$.

3Solve the simultaneous equations.

(1) $-\;$ (2): $\;(3a+b)-(2a+b)=-192-(-173)$

$$a=-19$$

Substitute into (1): $3(-19)+b=-192 \Rightarrow b=-192+57=-135$.

So $f(x)=4x(x+1)^2-19x-135$.

Hint: subtract to eliminate $b$;
always check by substituting back.

4Expand $f(x)$ and factorise by $(x-3)$.

$$f(x)=4x^3+8x^2-15x-135$$

Since $x-3$ is a factor (known / by division):

$$f(x)=(x-3)(4x^2+20x+45)$$

Hint: expand $4x(x+1)^2=4x^3+8x^2+4x$,
then add $-19x-135$.

5So $f(x)=0$ gives $x=3$ or $4x^2+20x+45=0$.
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For \(2x^{2}+8x+1\), \(\Delta=64-8=56\), which is positive but not a perfect square.

Test the discriminant of the quadratic factor:

$$\Delta=20^2-4(4)(45)=400-720=-320<0$$

Hint: $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ decides the nature of roots of a quadratic.

6Conclusion.

$\Delta<0$ means $4x^2+20x+45=0$ has no real roots.

The only real root of $f(x)=0$ is $x=3$,
and $3$ is a rational number (indeed an integer).

Therefore the claim is disagreed.

Hint: an irrational root must be a real root;
since the quadratic has none and $3$ is rational, no irrational root exists.

Answer:
(a) $a=-19,\; b=-135$
(b) Disagree. $f(x)=(x-3)(4x^2+20x+45)$ and $4x^2+20x+45=0$ has discriminant $20^2-4(4)(45)=-320<0$, so it has no real roots. The only real root is $x=3$, which is rational — hence no irrational root.

Question. It is given that \(f(x)=2x(x+1)^{2}+ax+b\), where \(x-2\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) and the remainder when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+1\) is \(15\).
(a) Find \(a\) and \(b\).
(b) A student claims that all the roots of \(f(x)=0\) are rational numbers. Do you agree? Explain.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. By the factor theorem, \(f(2)=0\): \(2(2)(3)^{2}+2a+b=36+2a+b=0\quad\cdots(1)\).
Step 2. By the remainder theorem, \(f(-1)=15\): \(2(-1)(0)^{2}-a+b=b-a=15\quad\cdots(2)\).
Step 3. From (2), \(b=a+15\). Substituting into (1): \(36+2a+a+15=0\), so \(3a=-51\), \(a=-17\) and \(b=-2\).
Step 4. Then \(f(x)=2x^{3}+4x^{2}-15x-2=(x-2)(2x^{2}+8x+1)\).
Step 5. For \(2x^{2}+8x+1=0\): \(\Delta=8^{2}-4(2)(1)=56>0\) but \(56\) is not a perfect square, so these two roots are irrational.
Step 6. Hence not all roots are rational — the claim is disagreed.
Answer: (a) \(a=-17\), \(b=-2\) (b) Disagree: \(\Delta=56\) is not a perfect square, so two roots are irrational.
