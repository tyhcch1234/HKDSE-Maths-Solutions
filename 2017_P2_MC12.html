It is given that \(y\) is the sum of two parts, one part is a constant and the other part varies as \(x^2\). When \(x = 1\), \(y = 7\) and when \(x = 2\), \(y = 13\). If \(x = 3\), then \(y =\)

A. 19
B. 20
C. 23
D. 47
💡

Key insight: "One part is a constant" → \(k_1\). "The other part varies as \(x^2\)" → \(k_2 x^2\). So \(y = k_1 + k_2 x^2\). Use the given data to find \(k_1\) and \(k_2\).

Let the constant part be \(k_1\) and the part that varies as \(x^2\) be \(k_2 x^2\), where \(k_1\) and \(k_2\) are non-zero constants.

Thus:

\[ y = k_1 + k_2 x^2 \]

Hint: \(k_1\) and \(k_2\) are just numbers we need to find. The equation is linear in \(k_1\) and \(k_2\) (though quadratic in \(x\)).

Substitute \(x = 1, y = 7\) and \(x = 2, y = 13\) into \(y = k_1 + k_2 x^2\):

When \(x=1\), \(y=7\): \[ 7 = k_1 + k_2(1)^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_1 + k_2 = 7 \quad \text{...(1)} \]

When \(x=2\), \(y=13\): \[ 13 = k_1 + k_2(2)^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_1 + 4k_2 = 13 \quad \text{...(2)} \]

Check: Equation (1) has \(k_2 \times 1^2 = k_2\). Equation (2) has \(k_2 \times 2^2 = 4k_2\). Good.

Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):

\[ (k_1 + 4k_2) - (k_1 + k_2) = 13 - 7 \]

\[ 3k_2 = 6 \]

\[ k_2 = 2 \]

Substitute \(k_2 = 2\) into (1): \(k_1 + 2 = 7\) ⇒ \(k_1 = 5\).

So the equation is: \[ y = 5 + 2x^2 \]

Substitute \(x = 3\) into \(y = 5 + 2x^2\):

\[ y = 5 + 2(3)^2 = 5 + 2(9) = 5 + 18 = 23 \]

Answer: C. 23
