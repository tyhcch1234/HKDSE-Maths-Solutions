Angles in a Parallelogram with an Isosceles Triangle

Parallelogram Isosceles Triangle Co-interior Angles Angle Sum of Triangle

Q Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. \(E\) is a point lying on \(CD\) such that \(BE=CE\). If \(\angle ADC = 114^\circ\), then \(\angle ABE =\)
A. \(48^\circ\)
B. \(57^\circ\)
C. \(62^\circ\)
D. \(66^\circ\)
(1 mark — multiple choice)

a Part (a) — Set up the angle facts in the parallelogram

Drag the slider to change \(\angle ADC\) and watch how the labelled angles update. Notice \(AB \parallel DC\) and \(AD \parallel BC\).

Step 11M
Since \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal and adjacent angles are supplementary: \[ \angle ABC = \angle ADC = 114^\circ \] \[ \angle BCD = 180^\circ - 114^\circ = 66^\circ \]
Adjacent angles on the same side of a parallelogram add up to \(180^\circ\) (co-interior angles with \(AB \parallel DC\)).
Step 21A
Because \(AB \parallel DC\), the alternate interior angles give \[ \angle BEC = \angle ABE \] (Wait — we actually need \(\angle BCE\) first; but the key parallel fact is \(AB \parallel DC\).)
Use \(AB \parallel DC\) carefully: \(\angle ABE\) and \(\angle BEC\) are not directly equal — instead relate the angles inside \(\triangle BCE\).
\(\angle BCD = 66^\circ\)

b Part (b) — Use \(BE = CE\) to find \(\angle ABE\)

Step 11M
In \(\triangle BCE\), the side \(BE = CE\), so \(\triangle BCE\) is isosceles with base \(BC\). Hence \[ \angle EBC = \angle BCE \]
Equal sides \(BE = CE\) sit opposite equal base angles \(\angle BCE\) and \(\angle EBC\).
\(\angle EBC = \angle BCE\)
Step 21M
The angle \(\angle BCD\) is split by the segment \(CE\) into \(\angle BCE\) and \(\angle ECD\). But actually, since \(E\) lies on \(CD\), \(\angle BCE\) is part of \(\angle BCD\): \[ \angle BCE = \angle BCD \quad (\text{since } E \text{ lies on } CD) \]
Careful: \(E\) is on \(CD\), so ray \(CE\) is the same direction as ray \(CD\). Therefore \(\angle BCE\) is the angle between \(BC\) and \(CD\), i.e. \(\angle BCD\).
\(\angle BCE = \angle BCD = 66^\circ\)
Step 31M
From the isosceles property, \(\angle EBC = \angle BCE = 66^\circ\). Now use the angle sum of \(\triangle BCE\): \[ \angle BEC = 180^\circ - 66^\circ - 66^\circ = 48^\circ \]
Angle sum of a triangle \(= 180^\circ\).
\(\angle BEC = 48^\circ\)
Step 41A
Since \(AB \parallel DC\) (and \(E\) lies on \(DC\)), the alternate interior angles give \[ \angle ABE = \angle BEC = 48^\circ \]
\(AB \parallel DC\) cut by transversal \(BE\) gives alternate interior angles equal: \(\angle ABE = \angle BEC\).
\(\angle ABE = 48^\circ\) → Answer A

★ Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle ADC = 114^\circ \Rightarrow \angle ABC = 114^\circ\) and \(\angle BCD = 66^\circ\)
  • \(BE = CE \Rightarrow \triangle BCE\) is isosceles \(\Rightarrow \angle EBC = \angle BCE = 66^\circ\)
  • Angle sum of \(\triangle BCE\): \(\angle BEC = 180^\circ - 66^\circ - 66^\circ = 48^\circ\)
  • \(AB \parallel DC\) (alternate interior angles): \(\angle ABE = \angle BEC = 48^\circ\)
  • \(\therefore \angle ABE = 48^\circ\) — Option A
