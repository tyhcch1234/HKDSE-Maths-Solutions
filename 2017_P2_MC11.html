Question

The scale of a map is \(1 : 20\,000\). If the area of a zoo on the map is \(4\ \mathrm{cm}^2\), then the actual area of the zoo is

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click each step

The scale \(1 : 20\,000\) is a length ratio:

\[ \frac{\text{length on map}}{\text{actual length}} = \frac{1}{20\,000} \]

💡 Hint: The \(1 : 20\,000\) tells you that every \(1\) unit on the map represents \(20\,000\) of the same units in real life. It is a length ratio — not an area ratio.

The zoo on the map and the actual zoo are similar figures. For similar figures, if the length ratio is \(k\), then the area ratio is \(k^2\). Here \(k = \frac{1}{20\,000}\):

\[ \frac{\text{area on map}}{\text{actual area}} = k^{2} = \left(\frac{1}{20\,000}\right)^{2} = \frac{1}{4 \times 10^{8}} \]

Length ratio

\(1 : 20\,000\)

Area ratio

\(1 : 4 \times 10^{8}\)

Map area \(= 4\ \mathrm{cm}^2\). Using the area ratio:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{actual area} &= 4 \times \left(20\,000\right)^{2}\ \mathrm{cm}^2\\[4pt] &= 4 \times 4 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{cm}^2\\[4pt] &= 1.6 \times 10^{9}\ \mathrm{cm}^2 \end{aligned} \]

💡 Hint: \((20\,000)^2 = 4 \times 10^8\). The map area must be multiplied — because a larger actual figure means a larger actual area.

The answer choices are in \(\mathrm{m}^2\), not \(\mathrm{cm}^2\), so convert. Since \(1\ \mathrm{m} = 100\ \mathrm{cm}\):

\[ 1\ \mathrm{m}^2 = (100)^2\ \mathrm{cm}^2 = 10^4\ \mathrm{cm}^2 \]

So to change \(\mathrm{cm}^2\) into \(\mathrm{m}^2\), divide by \(100^2 = 10^4\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{actual area} &= \frac{1.6 \times 10^{9}}{100^{2}}\ \mathrm{m}^2\\[4pt] &= (1.6 \times 10^{9}) \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m}^2\\[4pt] &= 1.6 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{m}^2 \end{aligned} \]

✅ Answer: B  (\(1.6 \times 10^{5}\ \mathrm{m}^2\))

  • Forgetting to square the ratio → \(4 \times 20\,000 = 80\,000\ \mathrm{cm}^2 = 8\ \mathrm{m}^2\) (wrong).
  • Forgetting the unit conversion → \(1.6 \times 10^{9}\) is too large for a zoo, but it is in \(\mathrm{cm}^2\), not \(\mathrm{m}^2\).
  • Dividing instead of multiplying → the actual area must be larger than the map area, so multiply.

Check by estimation: a zoo of \(1.6 \times 10^5\ \mathrm{m}^2\) (\(= 0.16\ \mathrm{km}^2\)) is about 400 m × 400 m — a reasonable size. ✔

Key Formula Summary

For similar figures

\[ \frac{\text{area}_1}{\text{area}_2} = \left(\frac{\text{length}_1}{\text{length}_2}\right)^{2} \]

Unit conversion

\[ 1\ \mathrm{m}^2 = 10^{4}\ \mathrm{cm}^2 \]

Apply this to any map/scale problem: take the square of the scale for areas, then convert units carefully.
