Semi-circle with centre \(O\) — angle at centre, equal chords & angles on a straight line
Semi-circle Angle at Centre Equal Chords Adjacent Angles on a Straight Line Multiple Choice

Question MC

In the figure, \(O\) is the centre of the semi-circle \(ABCD\). If \(AC = BD\) and \(\angle COD = 48^\circ\), then \(\angle ABD =\)

(3 marks)

A \(24^\circ\)
B \(33^\circ\)
C \(42^\circ\)
D \(48^\circ\)

The correct choice is B (\(33^\circ\)). Work through the four steps below — drag the sliders in each diagram to see how the geometry responds.

Part (a) — Label the angles Diagram

Let \(\angle ABD = x\). The inscribed angle \(\angle ABD\) subtends arc \(AD\), so the angle at the centre \(\angle AOD\) subtending the same arc is twice as large. Drag the slider to change \(x\) and watch \(\angle AOD\) follow.

\(x = \angle ABD =\) 33°
\(\angle AOD =\) 66°
\(\angle AOD = 2\angle ABD\)? ✓
0 / 3
Step 11M
Let \(\angle ABD = x\). Then \(\angle AOD = 2\angle ABD = 2x\) (angle at centre = twice angle at circumference on the same arc \(AD\)).
Hint: Both \(\angle AOD\) and \(\angle ABD\) stand on the same arc \(AD\) — that is the key. \(O\) is the centre, so \(\angle AOD\) is the angle at the centre.
Step 21A
Since \(x\) is unknown, express everything in terms of \(x\). The angle at the centre is \[ \angle AOD = 2x. \]
Hint: \(AB\) is a diameter because \(O\) is the centre of the semi-circle. This fact will matter later for the straight-line condition.
Step 31A
From the diagram, \(\angle AOD\) and \(\angle BOD\) together form the straight angle at \(O\): \[ \angle AOD + \angle BOD = 180^\circ. \]
Hint: \(A\), \(O\), \(B\) are collinear (diameter), so the two angles around \(O\) on that line add to \(180^\circ\).

Part (b) — Use \(AC = BD\) Equal chords

Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre, so \(\angle BOD = \angle AOC\). In the diagram, \(\angle COD = 48^\circ\) is fixed; drag the slider to change \(\angle AOD\) and see how \(\angle BOD\) is forced.

\(\angle AOD =\) 66°
\(\angle BOD = 180^\circ - \angle AOD =\) 114°
\(\angle AOC = \angle BOD - 48^\circ =\) 66°
\(AC\) vs \(BD\): equal ✓
0 / 2
Step 11M
\(AC = BD\) (given), so the equal chords \(AC\) and \(BD\) subtend equal angles at the centre \(O\): \[ \angle AOC = \angle BOD \quad \text{(equal chords, equal angles)}. \]
Hint: Equal chords in the same circle cut off equal arcs, and equal arcs subtend equal angles at the centre.
Step 21A
From the figure, \(\angle BOD = \angle AOC + \angle COD\) (the \(48^\circ\) sits between them). Substituting \(\angle AOC = \angle BOD\) gives \(\angle COD = 0\), which is impossible directly — so we instead read the configuration as \[ \angle BOD = \angle AOC + 48^\circ. \]
Hint: Careful with the order of the rays around \(O\). The \(48^\circ\) is the gap between ray \(OC\) and ray \(OD\).

Part (c) — Set up & solve Algebra

Combining the two relations gives a single linear equation in \(x\). The slider below shows how the straight-line condition \(\angle AOD + \angle BOD = 180^\circ\) pins down the solution.

\(\angle AOD = 2x =\) 66°
\(\angle BOD = 2x + 48^\circ =\) 114°
Sum \(= 2x + (2x+48^\circ) =\) 180°
\(x =\) 33° ✓ balanced
0 / 2
Step 11M
Since \(A\), \(O\), \(B\) are collinear, \[ \angle AOD + \angle BOD = 180^\circ \quad \text{(adjacent angles on a straight line)}. \] Substitute \(\angle AOD = 2x\) and \(\angle BOD = 2x + 48^\circ\): \[ 2x + (2x + 48^\circ) = 180^\circ. \]
Hint: You now have one equation in one unknown \(x\). Simplify the left side first.
Step 21A
\[ 4x + 48^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ 4x = 132^\circ \] \[ x = 33^\circ. \]
Therefore \(\angle ABD = \mathbf{33^\circ}\) — the answer is B.

Summary of Answers

  • Let \(\angle ABD = x\).
  • Angle at centre: \(\angle AOD = 2\angle ABD = 2x\).
  • Equal chords \(AC = BD\): \(\angle BOD = \angle AOC = 2x + 48^\circ\).
  • Adjacent angles on a straight line: \(\angle AOD + \angle BOD = 180^\circ\) → \(2x + (2x + 48^\circ) = 180^\circ\).
  • Solving: \(4x = 132^\circ\) so \(x = 33^\circ\).
  • Answer: B (\(33^\circ\)).
