2023 Paper 1 — Question 6

Topic: Rate, Ratio and Variations

Ratio Variations Substitution 4 marks

Let \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) be non-zero numbers such that \(7a = 6b\) and \(\dfrac{4a-3c}{2b-c} = 9\). Find

(a) \(\quad a:b:c\),

(b) \(\quad \dfrac{5a+8b}{7b+3c}\).

💡 Core Idea

Two equations, three unknowns — but the answer only wants ratios. That means we don't need the actual values of \(a,b,c\); we only need to know how big they are relative to each other.

  • 1. Use \(7a=6b\) to get a ratio \(a:b\) directly.
  • 2. Use the fraction equation to find how \(c\) compares to \(b\), giving \(b:c\).
  • 3. Combine the two ratios into \(a:b:c\) using a common value of \(b\).
  • 4. In part (b), substitute \(a=6k,\;b=7k,\;c=17k\) — the \(k\) cancels out.

🧩 Step-by-Step Solution

1 Find \(a:b\)

Hint: The ratio \(a:b\) can be read off from \(7a=6b\) using the rule \(a:b = (\text{coefficient of }b) : (\text{coefficient of }a)\) — i.e. cross the numbers over.

Cross-multiply / divide both sides by \(7b\):

\[ 7a = 6b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a}{b} = \frac{6}{7} \]

So we obtain the ratio:

\[ a:b = 6:7 \]

2 Use the fraction to relate \(a\), \(b\), \(c\)

Hint: \(\dfrac{4a-3c}{2b-c}=9\) means \(4a-3c = 9(2b-c)\). Expand and collect terms.

Cross-multiply by \(2b-c\) (which is non-zero since \(a,b,c\neq0\)):

\[ 4a - 3c = 9(2b - c) \]

\[ 4a - 3c = 18b - 9c \]

Bring the \(c\)-terms together:

\[ 4a - 3c + 9c = 18b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4a + 6c = 18b \]

3 Substitute \(a = \tfrac{6}{7}b\) to isolate \(c\)

Hint: We already know \(a\) in terms of \(b\) from Step 1. Put it in and solve for \(c\) in terms of \(b\).

From Step 1: \(a = \dfrac{6}{7}b\). Substitute into \(4a+6c=18b\):

\[ 4\left(\frac{6}{7}b\right) + 6c = 18b \]

\[ \frac{24}{7}b + 6c = 18b \]

\[ 6c = 18b - \frac{24}{7}b = \frac{126b - 24b}{7} = \frac{102b}{7} \]

\[ c = \frac{102b}{7} \div 6 = \frac{17b}{7} \]

Check with the official solution: \(c=\dfrac{17}{7}b\) ✓

4 Combine into \(a:b:c\)

Hint: For \(b:c\), cross-multiply \(c = \frac{17}{7}b\) into \(b:c\). Then make the \(b\)-values in \(a:b\) and \(b:c\) match.

From \(c = \dfrac{17}{7}b\) we get \(7c = 17b\), i.e.

\[ b:c = 7:17 \]

Now combine — the \(b\)-value is already \(7\) in both ratios, so no scaling is needed:

\(a\) \(b\) \(c\)
\(a:b=6:7\)67—
\(b:c=7:17\)—717
\(a:b:c\)6717

\[ \boxed{a:b:c = 6:7:17} \]

5 Part (b): introduce the parameter \(k\)

Hint: A ratio \(a:b:c = 6:7:17\) means there exists a non-zero constant \(k\) with \(a=6k,\;b=7k,\;c=17k\). Every term then carries a factor of \(k\).

Let \(a = 6k\), \(\;b = 7k\), \(\;c = 17k\), where \(k \neq 0\). Then

\[ \frac{5a+8b}{7b+3c} = \frac{5(6k)+8(7k)}{7(7k)+3(17k)} \]

Simplify numerator and denominator separately:

\[ \text{Numerator} = 30k + 56k = 86k \]

\[ \text{Denominator} = 49k + 51k = 100k \]

\[ \frac{5a+8b}{7b+3c} = \frac{86k}{100k} = \frac{86}{100} \]

Notice \(k\) cancels — the answer is independent of the actual values. Cancel by \(2\):

\[ \boxed{\frac{5a+8b}{7b+3c} = \frac{43}{50}} \]

(a)

\(a:b:c = 6:7:17\)
