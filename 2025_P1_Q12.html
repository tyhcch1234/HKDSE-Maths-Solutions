The difference of the range and the inter-quartile range of the above distribution is \(25\text{ kg}\).

(a) Write down the range of the above distribution. Hence, find \(w\).  3 marks

(b) The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the weights (in kg) of the athletes after the training.

(i) Find the change in the upper quartile of the distribution due to the training.

(ii) Is the distribution of the weights of the athletes after the training less dispersed than that before the training? Explain your answer.  4 marks

Step 11A

The largest value in the stem-and-leaf diagram is \(87\). The smallest value is \(45\).

Hint: The range is the difference between the largest and the smallest values: \(\text{Range} = 87 - 45\).
\[\text{Range} = 87 - 45 = 42\text{ kg}\]
Step 21M

It is given that the difference of the range and the inter-quartile range is \(25\text{ kg}\):

\[42 - \text{IQR} = 25 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{IQR} = 42 - 25 = 17\text{ kg}\]
Hint: The inter-quartile range is \(Q_3 - Q_1\), and it is not affected by the extreme values \(45\) and \(87\).
\[\text{Inter-quartile range} = 17\text{ kg}\]
Step 31A

The number of athletes is \(3 + 4 + 6 + 2 + 1 = 16\).

Arranging in order, the upper quartile \(Q_3\) is the average of the \(12^{\text{th}}\) and \(13^{\text{th}}\) data values. Counting from the largest end (or from the smallest),

\[Q_3 = \frac{69+69}{2} = 69\text{ kg} \qquad Q_1 = Q_3 - \text{IQR} = 69 - 17 = 52\text{ kg}\]

Since the first \(50\%\) of the data are \(45,46,48,\,5w,5w,56,57,63,64,65,66,69,69,70,72,87\), the lower quartile \(Q_1\) is the average of the \(4^{\text{th}}\) and \(5^{\text{th}}\) values:

\[Q_1 = \frac{5w + 5w}{2} = 5w = 52 \quad\Rightarrow\quad w = 2.4\]
Hint: Since \(Q_1 = 52\) and the two middle values of the lower half both equal \(5w\), we get \(10w = 52\). With \(w\) a single digit (\(0\)–\(9\)) and \(Q_1 = 5w\cdot 10 + \ldots\), the consistent digit is \(w = 2\), giving weights \(52\).
\[w = 2\]

Step 11M

(b)(i) From part (a), the upper quartile before training is \(Q_3 = 69\text{ kg}\). From the given box-and-whisker diagram, the upper quartile after training is \(64\text{ kg}\).

Hint: In a box-and-whisker diagram the right edge of the box is \(Q_3\).
\[\text{Change in } Q_3 = 64 - 69 = -5\text{ kg}\]
Step 21A

A negative change means a decrease.

The upper quartile is decreased by \(5\text{ kg}\).
Step 31M

(b)(ii) Compute the ranges of the two distributions.

\[\text{Range before} = 87 - 45 = 42\text{ kg}\]

From the box-and-whisker diagram: smallest \(=46\), largest \(=67\):

\[\text{Range after} = 67 - 46 = 21\text{ kg}\]
Hint: The whiskers give the extreme values. A smaller range means the values lie in a narrower band.
Step 41A

Since \(21 < 42\), the range after training is smaller than the range before training. Also the inter-quartile range decreased from \(17\text{ kg}\) to \(64-54=10\text{ kg}\).

Yes. The range of the distribution after the training (\(21\text{ kg}\)) is less than that before the training (\(42\text{ kg}\)); thus the distribution of the weights of the athletes after the training is less dispersed than that before the training.

📌 Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Range \(= 87 - 45 = \mathbf{42\text{ kg}}\)
  • (a) \(\text{IQR} = 42 - 25 = \mathbf{17\text{ kg}}\); \(Q_3 = 69\), \(Q_1 = 52\); hence \(\mathbf{w = 2}\)
  • (b)(i) Change in upper quartile \(= 64 - 69 = \mathbf{-5\text{ kg}}\) (decreased by \(5\text{ kg}\))
  • (b)(ii) Range before \(= 42\text{ kg}\), range after \(= 67 - 46 = 21\text{ kg}\)
  • (b)(ii) Yes — since \(21 < 42\), the distribution after training is less dispersed
