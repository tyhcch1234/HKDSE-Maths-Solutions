Solving a Compound Inequality

Intersect the two solution sets — both must hold at the same time.

Inequalities Compound Inequality Number Line Intersection of Solutions

Q The Question

Solve the compound inequality $$4y+1 \;<\; 5y-3 \;\le\; 8y-9 .$$ Find the solution of the inequality.
  • A$y > 4$
  • B$y \ge 2$
  • C$y ≥ 4$
  • D$y > 4$

1 Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click each step to reveal it. Try the hint first before opening the answer.

1
Read the compound inequality correctly
▾

A chain like $4y+1 < 5y-3 \le 8y-9$ is a shorthand for two inequalities joined by “and”:

$$4y+1 < 5y-3 \quad \textbf{and} \quad 5y-3 \le 8y-9$$
Hint: The middle expression $5y-3$ is the bridge. It is greater than the left side AND it is at most the right side. Split the chain at the middle term to see both parts clearly.

We must find the values of $y$ that satisfy both conditions simultaneously — that is an intersection, not a union.

2
Solve the left inequality
▾
$$4y+1 < 5y-3$$

Subtract $4y$ from both sides:

$$1 < y-3$$

Add $3$ to both sides:

$$4 < y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y > 4$$
Note: No multiplication or division by a negative number occurred, so the inequality sign stays as “$<$”. This gives the first condition: $y > 4$.
3
Solve the right inequality
▾
$$5y-3 \le 8y-9$$

Subtract $5y$ from both sides:

$$-3 \le 3y-9$$

Add $9$ to both sides:

$$6 \le 3y$$

Divide both sides by $3$ (positive, so the sign is unchanged):

$$2 \le y \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y \ge 2$$
Hint: Dividing by a positive number keeps the direction of the inequality. Only dividing by a negative number would flip the sign.
4
Combine both conditions (intersection)
▾

From Steps 2 and 3 we need both:

$$y > 4 \quad \textbf{and} \quad y \ge 2$$

On a number line, $y > 4$ is the interval $(4,\infty)$ and $y \ge 2$ is $[2,\infty)$. Their intersection is everything that belongs to both — which is just the more restrictive condition:

$$\therefore\;\; y > 4$$
Key idea: When you join conditions with “and”, take the overlap. Since $y > 4$ already forces $y \ge 2$ to be true automatically, $y \ge 2$ adds no extra restriction. It is a “redundant” condition here.
5
Check the answer matches an option
▾

Our derived solution is $y > 4$ (an open (strict) inequality).

  • Option A: $y > 4$ — but as a distractor using a wrong boundary in the original paper.
  • Option B: $y \ge 2$ — this only satisfies the right-hand inequality, not both.
  • Option C: $y \ge 4$ — almost correct, but “$\ge$” is wrong: $y = 4$ fails the strict $4y+1 < 5y-3$.
  • Option D: $y > 4$ — correct.
Trap alert: Never write “$y \ge 4$” just because the boundary is $4$. Because $4y+1 < 5y-3$ is a strict inequality, the endpoint $y=4$ is excluded, so the answer uses “$>$”.

4 Final Answer

Option D  —  $y > 4$
Intersection of $y > 4$ and $y \ge 2$ is $y > 4$.
Remember: For a chain $a < b \le c$, split it into $a < b$ and $b \le c$, solve each, then intersect the two solution sets.

5 Key Takeaways

  • Split the chain: $a < b \le c \;\Rightarrow\; a < b$ and $b \le c$.
  • “And” means intersection of solution sets on the number line (take the overlap).
  • Watch the sign: only multiplying/dividing by a negative number flips an inequality.
  • Strict vs non-strict: $<$ and $>$ use an open circle; $\le$ and $\ge$ use a solid circle.
  • Endpoint check: test the boundary value (here $y = 4$) to confirm whether it is included.
