Statistics — how mean and variance behave under a linear transformation \(ax+b\).

Variance Standard Deviation Linear Transformation Effect of \(a\) and \(b\)

QUESTION

If the variance of the five numbers \(x_1,\ x_2,\ x_3,\ x_4\) and \(x_5\) is \(13\), then the variance of the five numbers \[3x_1+4,\quad 3x_2+4,\quad 3x_3+4,\quad 3x_4+4 \quad\text{and}\quad 3x_5+4\] is

  • A. 39
  • B. 43
  • C. 117
  • D. 121

PART (a) The two key rules 1M + 1A

Before computing, we need to know exactly what adding a constant and multiplying by a constant do to a data set. Drag the sliders below to build intuition.

Original var: 13.00
Original sd: 3.61
New var: 117.00
New sd: 10.82
\(a^2\): 9.00
Mean shift: +4
1 Adding a constant \(b\) Concept

Every value moves by the same amount \(b\), so the mean moves too but the distances between values do not change:

\[ \operatorname{Var}(x_i+b)=\operatorname{Var}(x_i) \]
Hint: Try the slider \(a=1\), \(b=4\). The red dots simply slide along the number line — the spread bars stay the same length.
2 Multiplying by a constant \(a\) Key Rule

Every deviation from the mean is scaled by \(a\), so the variance — which is built from squares of deviations — is scaled by \(a^2\):

\[ \operatorname{Var}(a\,x_i)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(x_i) \]
Hint: Raise \(a\) to \(3\) with the slider and look at the spread bars: they triple in length, so the variance grows by \(3^2=9\) times.
3 Combine both 1M

Applying \(x\mapsto ax+b\) combines the two effects. The multiplier acts on the variance, the shift does nothing:

\[ \boxed{\operatorname{Var}(a\,x_i+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(x_i)} \]
Hint: This is exactly the transformation \(3x_i+4\), so \(a=3\) and \(b=4\).

PART (b) Compute the new variance 1A

We are given \(\operatorname{Var}(x_i)=13\) and the new numbers are \(3x_i+4\). Substitute \(a=3\) and \(b=4\).

\(a^2\): 9.00
Correct: \(a^2\cdot 13\) = 117.00
Trap: \(a\cdot 13\) = 39.00
1 Identify the transformation 1M

Compare with \(a\,x_i+b\):

\[ x_i \;\longmapsto\; 3x_i+4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=3,\qquad b=4 \]
Hint: The shift \(+4\) only moves the data; it is not the multiplier.
2 Apply the variance rule 1A
\[ \operatorname{Var}(3x_i+4)=3^2\operatorname{Var}(x_i)=9\times 13=117 \]
Hint: Option A (39) arises if you forget to square the multiplier — the classic mistake!
New variance \(=117\)  →  Option C

SUMMARY Answers at a glance

  • Rule 1 (shift): \(\operatorname{Var}(x_i+b)=\operatorname{Var}(x_i)\) — adding a constant changes nothing.
  • Rule 2 (scale): \(\operatorname{Var}(a\,x_i)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(x_i)\) — the multiplier is squared.
  • Combined: \(\operatorname{Var}(a\,x_i+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(x_i)\).
  • Here \(a=3\), so \(\operatorname{Var}(3x_i+4)=3^2\times 13=9\times 13=\mathbf{117}\).
  • Final answer: C. 117
