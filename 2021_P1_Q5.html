Interactive Model: Sticker Transfer

Step 1: Initial situation — girl $x$ stickers, boy $3x$ stickers.
Girl Boy Transferred stickers
HKDSE 2021 Paper 1 — Q5
[4 marks] The number of stickers owned by a boy is 3 times that owned by a girl. If the boy gives 20 of his stickers to the girl, then the number of stickers owned by the girl is 2 times that owned by the boy. Find the total number of stickers owned by the boy and the girl.

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Let the unknown be \(x\)
Choosing the Unknown in a Word Problem \(\text{let } x=\text{smaller quantity},\ \text{others}=kx\) With the girl holding \(x\) stickers the boy holds \(3x\), so only one letter is needed.
1A

Let \(x\) be the number of stickers owned by the girl.

Then the number of stickers owned by the boy is \(3x\), since “the boy's is 3 times the girl's”.

Hint: label the smaller/unknown quantity as \(x\) and express everything else in terms of \(x\).

Step 2Set up the equation after the transfer
Distributive Law \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) Expanding \(2(3x-20)\) gives \(6x-40\) in the equation \(2(3x-20)=x+20\).
1M + 1A

The boy gives 20 stickers to the girl, so:

\[ \text{Boy after} = 3x - 20,\qquad \text{Girl after} = x + 20 \]

“The girl's new amount is 2 times the boy's new amount” gives:

\[ 2(3x - 20) = x + 20 \]

Hint: the \(20\) leaves the boy and arrives at the girl — one subtraction, one addition.

Step 3Solve the linear equation
Balancing Method for a Linear Equation \(ax+b=cx+d\ \Longrightarrow\ (a-c)x=d-b\) Gathering terms: \(6x-x=20+40\), so \(5x=60\) and \(x=12\).

\[ 6x - 40 = x + 20 \]

\[ 6x - x = 20 + 40 \]

\[ 5x = 60 \]

\[ x = 12 \]

Hint: expand the bracket, then gather \(x\)-terms on one side and numbers on the other.

Step 4Interpret \(x\) and find the total 1A

Girl originally: \(x = 12\); Boy originally: \(3x = 36\).

Total \(= 3x + x = 4x = 4(12) = 48\).

Hint: the question asks for the total, not just \(x\) — remember to add both people's stickers.

Answer: The total number of stickers owned by the boy and the girl is 48.

Question. The number of marbles owned by Ann is \(4\) times that owned by Ben. If Ann gives \(11\) of her marbles to Ben, then the number of marbles owned by Ben is \(3\) times that owned by Ann. Find the total number of marbles owned by Ann and Ben. [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(x\) be the number of marbles Ben owns;
then Ann owns \(4x\).
Step 2. After the transfer Ann has \(4x-11\) and Ben has \(x+11\).
Step 3. Ben now has 3 times Ann: \(x+11=3(4x-11)\), i.e. \(x+11=12x-33\).
Step 4. \(44=11x\), so \(x=4\);
hence Ann had \(16\) and Ben had \(4\). Check: after the transfer \(5\) and \(15\), and \(15=3\times5\) \u2713.
Step 5. Total \(=16+4=20\).
Answer: \(20\) marbles
