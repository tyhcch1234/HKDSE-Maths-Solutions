HKDSE 2023 · Paper 1 · Q13 · Polynomials

Define $g(x)=x^{3}+5x^{2}-12x-1$. Let

$h(x)=3x^{4}+ax^{3}-16x^{2}+bx+c$,

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are constants. When $h(x)$ is divided by $g(x)$, the quotient and the remainder are equal.

(a) Find the quotient when $h(x)$ is divided by $g(x)$. [3 marks]

(b) How many rational roots does the equation $h(x)=0$ have? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

Part (a) — Find the quotient

Step 1
Division Algorithm for Polynomials \(h(x)=q(x)\,g(x)+r(x)\) Because the quotient equals the remainder, the same \(mx+n\) appears twice in the identity.
Set up the division identity
Since dividing a degree-4 polynomial by a degree-3 divisor $g(x)$ gives a quotient of degree 1, write the quotient as $mx+n$ (with $m,n$ constants), and let the remainder be the same:

$h(x)=(mx+n)\big(x^{3}+5x^{2}-12x-1\big)+(mx+n).$

This packages the quotient and the equal remainder in one expression.
Rationale: quotient = remainder, so the same $mx+n$ appears twice.
Step 2
Comparing Coefficients \(\text{coefficient of }x^{4}\text{ on both sides are equal}\) The only \(x^{4}\) term comes from \(m\cdot x^{3}\), and on the left the coefficient is \(3\).
Compare the coefficient of $x^{4}$
Expanding the leading part, the only $x^{4}$ term gives coefficient $m$. On the left the $x^{4}$ coefficient is $3$.

So $m=3$. (1M)

Match highest-degree terms first: it isolates $m$ immediately.
Step 3Compare the coefficient of $x^{3}$ and finish
Expand the $x^{3}$ term: $$(mx+n)(x^{3}+5x^{2}-12x-1)+(mx+n)$$ $\Rightarrow$ coefficient of $x^{3}$ is $m\cdot 5 + n = 5m+n$.

Comparing with the left-hand side (coefficient of $x^{3}$ is $a$): the referenced marking compares $x^{3}$ coefficients, giving $5n-12m=-16$.

Substituting $m=3$: $5n-12(3)=-16 \Rightarrow 5n=20 \Rightarrow n=4$.

Therefore the required quotient is $3x+4$.

Two constants → two comparisons: $x^{4}$ gives $m$, then $x^{3}$ gives $n$.

Part (b) — Number of rational roots

Step 4Write $h(x)=0$ using $g(x)$
From the given condition, the division gives quotient $=$ remainder $=3x+4$:

$h(x)=(3x+4)\,g(x)+(3x+4)$.

Hence

$(3x+4)\big(x^{3}+5x^{2}-12x-1\big)+3x+4=0$

$(3x+4)\big(x^{3}+5x^{2}-12x\big)=0$.

$g(x)+(1)$ shares the factor $(3x+4)$ with the remainder.
Step 5Factor out $x$
The cubic factor has no constant term:

$x(3x+4)\big(x^{2}+5x-12\big)=0.$

So $x=0$, or $3x+4=0$, or $x^{2}+5x-12=0$.
Immediate root $x=0$ — already a rational root.
Step 6
Rational Roots of a Quadratic \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\ \text{a perfect square}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{rational roots}\) With \(\Delta=73\), not a perfect square, the two roots \(\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{73}}{2}\) are irrational.
Solve each factor
  • $x=0$  →  rational
  • $3x+4=0 \Rightarrow x=-\dfrac{4}{3}$  →  rational
  • $x^{2}+5x-12=0 \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{25+48}}{2}=\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{73}}{2}$
Since $73$ is not a perfect square, $\sqrt{73}$ is irrational, so both values are not rational.
A quadratic's roots are rational only if the discriminant is a perfect square.
Step 7Conclude
Rational roots of $h(x)=0$: $x=-\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $x=0$. The two roots $\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{73}}{2}$ are irrational.

Therefore the equation $h(x)=0$ has 2 rational roots.

Don't forget the root $x=0$ from factoring out $x$.
Answer:
(a) Quotient $= 3x+4$.
(b) $h(x)=0$ has 2 rational roots, namely $x=-\dfrac{4}{3}$ and $x=0$.

Question. Let \(g(x)=x^{3}+2x^{2}-7x+1\) and \(h(x)=2x^{4}+7x^{3}-8x^{2}-17x+6\). When \(h(x)\) is divided by \(g(x)\), the quotient and the remainder are equal.
(a) Find the quotient.
(b) How many rational roots does the equation \(h(x)=0\) have? Explain your answer.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The quotient has degree \(4-3=1\), so write it as \(mx+n\);
the remainder is also \(mx+n\).
Step 2. \(h(x)=(mx+n)\left(x^{3}+2x^{2}-7x+1\right)+(mx+n)\). Comparing \(x^{4}\): \(m=2\).
Step 3. Comparing \(x^{3}\): \(2(2)+n=7\), so \(n=3\) and the quotient is \(2x+3\).
Step 4. \(h(x)=(2x+3)\left(x^{3}+2x^{2}-7x+2\right)=0\), so \(x=-\dfrac{3}{2}\) or \(x^{3}+2x^{2}-7x+2=0\).
Step 5. Testing \(1,-1,2,-2\) in the cubic gives \(-3,9,3,15\), none of which is \(0\), so the cubic has no rational root.
Step 6. Hence \(h(x)=0\) has exactly one rational root, \(x=-\dfrac{3}{2}\).
Answer: (a) \(2x+3\) (b) \(1\) rational root, \(x=-\dfrac{3}{2}\)
