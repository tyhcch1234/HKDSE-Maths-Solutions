Area of a Right-Angled Triangle — finding the correct formula

Hypotenuse QR = p Leg PR = q Leg PQ = p·cosθ Right angle at P Angle θ at Q
QUESTION

Area of a Right Triangle

It is given that \(\triangle PQR\) is a right-angled triangle with \(\angle QPR = 90^\circ\). Let the area of \(\triangle PQR\) be \(S\). If \(QR = p\), \(PR = q\) and \(\angle PQR = \theta\), which of the following must be true?

A. \(S=\dfrac{1}{2}pq\)
B. \(S=\dfrac{1}{2}pq\sin\theta\)
C. \(S=\dfrac{1}{2}pq\cos\theta\)
D. \(S=\dfrac{1}{2}pq\tan\theta\)
STEP 1 · IDENTIFY THE SIDES

The right angle is at \(P\), so the side opposite to it is the hypotenuse:

\[ QR = p \quad\text{(hypotenuse)} \]

The two legs of the right triangle are:

\[ PQ \quad\text{and}\quad PR = q \]

Key idea: the hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.

STEP 2 · EXPRESS PQ USING θ

At vertex \(Q\), the angle \(\theta = \angle PQR\) has:

  • adjacent side \(= PQ\)
  • hypotenuse \(= QR = p\)

Using cosine ratio:

\[ \cos\theta = \frac{PQ}{QR} \;\Rightarrow\; PQ = p\cos\theta \]
STEP 3 · COMPUTE THE AREA

For a right triangle, area \(= \tfrac12 \times (\text{base}) \times (\text{height})\). Take the legs \(PQ\) and \(PR\):

\[ S = \tfrac12 \times PQ \times PR \] \[ S = \tfrac12 \,(p\cos\theta)(q) = \tfrac12 \, p q \cos\theta \]

This matches option C.

Check: when \(\theta=0\), \(PQ=p\) and the formula gives \(S=\tfrac12 pq\), the full leg–leg area. Consistent ✓

Final Answer: C  —  \(S=\dfrac{1}{2}pq\cos\theta\)
