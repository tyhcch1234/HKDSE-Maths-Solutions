2014 Paper 1 · Question 7

Polynomials Remainder Theorem Factor Theorem Rational Roots

📋 Question

Let \( f(x) = 4x^3 - 5x^2 - 18x + c \), where \( c \) is a constant.

When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - 2 \), the remainder is \( -33 \).

(a) Is \( x + 1 \) a factor of \( f(x) \)? Explain your answer.

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \( f(x) = 0 \) are rational numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

[5 marks]

Core Concepts

  • • Remainder Theorem: The remainder when \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - a \) is \( f(a) \).
  • • Factor Theorem: \( x - a \) is a factor of \( f(x) \) iff \( f(a) = 0 \).
  • • Rational numbers: Numbers that can be written as \( \dfrac{p}{q} \) where \( p,q \) are integers and \( q \neq 0 \).

🧭 Step-by-Step Solution

💡 Hint: Use the Remainder Theorem. When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - 2 \), the remainder equals \( f(2) \).

Given the remainder is \(-33\), so \( f(2) = -33 \):

\[ 4(2)^3 - 5(2)^2 - 18(2) + c = -33 \]

\[ 32 - 20 - 36 + c = -33 \]

\[ -24 + c = -33 \]

\[ \Rightarrow c = -9 \]

💡 Hint: By the Factor Theorem, \( x + 1 = x - (-1) \) is a factor iff \( f(-1) = 0 \).

Now \( f(x) = 4x^3 - 5x^2 - 18x - 9 \). Compute \( f(-1) \):

\[ f(-1) = 4(-1)^3 - 5(-1)^2 - 18(-1) - 9 \]

\[ = -4 - 5 + 18 - 9 \]

\[ = 0 \]

Since \( f(-1) = 0 \), \( x + 1 \) is a factor of \( f(x) \).

💡 Hint: Since \( x+1 \) is a factor, divide \( f(x) \) by \( x+1 \) (long division or inspection). The quotient is a quadratic, which can be factorised further.

\[ f(x) = (x+1)(4x^2 - 9x - 9) \]

Factorise the quadratic \( 4x^2 - 9x - 9 \):

Find two numbers with product \(4 \times (-9) = -36\) and sum \(-9\): they are \(-12\) and \(3\).

\[ 4x^2 - 12x + 3x - 9 = 4x(x-3) + 3(x-3) = (x-3)(4x+3) \]

\[ \therefore f(x) = (x+1)(x-3)(4x+3) \]

💡 Hint: Set each factor to zero. A rational number is any number of the form \( \frac{p}{q} \) with integers \( p, q \) (\( q \neq 0 \)).

\[ (x+1)(x-3)(4x+3) = 0 \]

\[ x = -1, \quad x = 3, \quad \text{or} \quad x = -\tfrac{3}{4} \]

The three roots are \(-1\), \(3\), and \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\).

Each root can be written as a ratio of integers: \(-1 = \frac{-1}{1}\), \(3 = \frac{3}{1}\), \(-\frac{3}{4} = \frac{-3}{4}\).

Thus, all roots are rational numbers. The claim is agreed.
