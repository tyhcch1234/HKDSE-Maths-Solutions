Question

Let $f(x)=4x^{3}-5x^{2}-18x+c$, where $c$ is a constant.

When $f(x)$ is divided by $x-2$, the remainder is $-33$.

(a) Is $x+1$ a factor of $f(x)$? Explain your answer.

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $f(x)=0$ are rational numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

[5 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

1Use the Remainder Theorem to find $c$
Remainder on dividing $f(x)$ by $x-2$ is $f(2)$, and $f(2)=-33$:
$f(2)=4(2)^3-5(2)^2-18(2)+c$
$=32-20-36+c=-24+c$
So $-24+c=-33\;\Rightarrow\; c=-9$.
Remainder Theorem: the remainder of $f(x)\div(x-a)$ is $f(a)$.
2Test the factor $x+1$
With $c=-9$, $f(x)=4x^{3}-5x^{2}-18x-9$.
Factor Theorem: $x+1$ is a factor $\iff f(-1)=0$.
$f(-1)=4(-1)^3-5(-1)^2-18(-1)-9$
$=-4-5+18-9=0$.
Check whether $x=-1$ makes the polynomial vanish.
3Conclusion for part (a)
Since $f(-1)=0$, by the Factor Theorem,
$x+1$ is a factor of $f(x)$.
A zero remainder means the linear expression divides exactly.
4Factorise fully to find all roots
$f(x)=0:\quad 4x^{3}-5x^{2}-18x-9=0$
$(x+1)(4x^{2}-9x-9)=0$
Now $4x^{2}-9x-9=(x-3)(4x+3)$.
$\Rightarrow (x+1)(x-3)(4x+3)=0$
$\Rightarrow x=-1,\; x=3,\; x=-\dfrac{3}{4}$.
Divide by the known factor, then factorise the quadratic by grouping.
5Are all roots rational?
The three roots are $-1$, $3$ and $-\dfrac{3}{4}$.
Each is expressible as $\dfrac{p}{q}$ with integers $p,q$ ($q\neq0$),
so all are rational numbers.
Test each root: is it a fraction or integer?
Answer:
(a) $c=-9$ and $f(-1)=0$, so $x+1$ is a factor of $f(x)$.
(b) $f(x)=(x+1)(x-3)(4x+3)=0$ gives $x=-1$, $x=3$, $x=-\tfrac{3}{4}$. All three roots are rational, therefore the claim is agreed.
