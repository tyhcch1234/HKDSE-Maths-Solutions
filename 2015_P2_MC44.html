Topic: 14. Probability

Bag \(P\) contains 2 red balls and 4 green balls while bag \(Q\) contains 1 red ball and 3 green balls. If a bag is randomly chosen and then a ball is randomly drawn from the bag, find the probability that a green ball is drawn.

A. \(\dfrac{3}{10}\)
B. \(\dfrac{7}{10}\)
C. \(\dfrac{7}{24}\)
D. \(\dfrac{17}{24}\)

Answer: D (revealed after you complete the steps).

1 · Visualise the two bags

Bag \(P\) 6 balls total

\(P(\text{green}\mid P)=\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}\)

Bag \(Q\) 4 balls total

\(P(\text{green}\mid Q)=\dfrac{3}{4}\)

Notice the two bags have different total numbers of balls (6 and 4). This is why we cannot simply add the numerators.

3 · Step-by-step solution

0 / 5

Step 1 — Understand what is being asked

A bag is chosen at random, then a ball is drawn. There are two possible “routes” to a green ball:

  • Route 1: choose Bag \(P\) and draw a green ball
  • Route 2: choose Bag \(Q\) and draw a green ball
Law of Total Probability: \[ P(G)=P(G\mid P)\,P(P)+P(G\mid Q)\,P(Q) \] where \(P(P)\) and \(P(Q)\) are the probabilities of choosing each bag.

Step 2 — Probability of choosing each bag

There are two bags and the bag is chosen randomly, so each is equally likely:

\[ P(P)=\frac{1}{2}, \qquad P(Q)=\frac{1}{2} \]

“Randomly chosen bag” ⇒ uniform choice between the two bags.

Step 3 — Conditional probability of a green ball

Bag \(P\): 2 red + 4 green = 6 balls

\[ P(G\mid P)=\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3} \]

Bag \(Q\): 1 red + 3 green = 4 balls

\[ P(G\mid Q)=\frac{3}{4} \]

Conditional probability given the bag ⇒ denominator = total balls inside that bag only.

Step 4 — Multiply along each branch

Multiply the probability of the route by the conditional probability of green:

\[ P(G \cap P)=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{2}{3}=\frac{1}{3} \]

\[ P(G \cap Q)=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3}{8} \]

“AND” ⇒ multiply. Always multiply the bag probability by the conditional green probability.

Step 5 — Add the two routes (final answer)

The two routes are mutually exclusive, so add their probabilities:

\[ P(G)=\frac{1}{3}+\frac{3}{8}=\frac{8}{24}+\frac{9}{24}=\frac{17}{24} \]

Answer

\(\boxed{\text{D. } \dfrac{17}{24}}\)

“OR” ⇒ add. The two paths cannot happen at the same time (the bag is either \(P\) or \(Q\)).

4 · Quick summary

Law of Total Probability

\[ P(G)=\sum_i P(G\mid B_i)\,P(B_i) \]

Key operations

Follow the tree: multiply along a branch, add across branches.

Common trap: adding \(\frac{4}{6}+\frac{3}{4}\) directly (wrong, ignores the equal bag choice) or using \((4+3)/(6+4)\) (wrong, bags are not combined into one pool).
