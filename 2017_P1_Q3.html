Question [2017 #3]
Factorize [3 marks]
(a)   $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$,
(b)   $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y$.
Factorising a Quadratic of the Form x^{2}+bx+c \(x^{2}+bx+c=(x+m)(x+n),\quad mn=c,\ \ m+n=b\) The numbers \(m=-3\), \(n=-1\) multiply to \(+3y^{2}\) and add to \(-4y\), giving brackets \((x-3y)\) and \((x-y)\).
1Part (a) — Decide the method
We need two numbers that multiply to $+3$ and add to $-4$.
These are $-3$ and $-1$, so the two brackets are $(x-3y)$ and $(x-y)$.
Hint: split the middle term: $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}=x^{2}-3xy-xy+3y^{2}$.
2Part (a) — Factorize by grouping
$x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$
$= x^{2}-3xy-xy+3y^{2}$
$= x(x-3y)-y(x-3y)$    (group in pairs)
$= (x-3y)(x-y)$
Check: $(x-3y)(x-y)=x^{2}-xy-3xy+3y^{2}=x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$ ✓
3Part (b) — Reuse the result from (a)
Part (b) shares the first three terms, so replace them with the answer from (a): $$x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y = (x-3y)(x-y)+11x-33y.$$
Hint: look for the common factor $(x-3y)$ — the extra terms are $+11(x-3y)$.
Common Factor Law \(AB+AC=A(B+C)\) \(11x-33y=11(x-3y)\), so \((x-3y)\) is common to both terms of part (b).
4Part (b) — Take out the common factor
$$11x-33y = 11(x-3y)$$ So $$(x-3y)(x-y)+11(x-3y) = (x-3y)\big[(x-y)+11\big].$$
Hint: the common factor $(x-3y)$ appears in both terms.
5Simplify the second bracket
$(x-3y)\big[(x-y)+11\big] = (x-3y)(x-y+11)$
Fully factorized — no further common factor remains.
Answer (a)  $(x-3y)(x-y)$
(b)  $(x-3y)(x-y+11)$

Question. Factorize  [3 marks]
(a) \(x^{2}+5xy+6y^{2}\),
(b) \(x^{2}+5xy+6y^{2}-4x-8y\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. For (a) we need two numbers multiplying to \(+6y^{2}\) and adding to \(+5y\): they are \(2y\) and \(3y\).
Step 2. Split the middle term and group: \(x^{2}+2xy+3xy+6y^{2}=x(x+2y)+3y(x+2y)=(x+2y)(x+3y)\).
Step 3. For (b), replace the first three terms by the answer to (a): \((x+2y)(x+3y)-4x-8y\).
Step 4. Since \(-4x-8y=-4(x+2y)\), take out the common factor \((x+2y)\): \((x+2y)\big[(x+3y)-4\big]=(x+2y)(x+3y-4)\).
Answer: (a) \((x+2y)(x+3y)\)  (b) \((x+2y)(x+3y-4)\)
