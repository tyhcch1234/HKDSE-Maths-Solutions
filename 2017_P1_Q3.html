HKDSE Math 2017 Paper 1 Q3
Polynomials Factorisation Taking out Common Factor

Factorize the following

(a)   $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$,
(b)   $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y$.   [3 marks]

Key skill: factorise a quadratic in two variables by inspection, then reuse that result and take out the common factor to finish part (b).

Part (a): Factorise $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$

Click each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict before you open it!

The expression $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$ is a quadratic in two variables. Think of it as $x^{2}-(4y)\,x+3y^{2}$: treat $y$ as just another number (a "coefficient" that happens to contain a letter).

Compare with   $x^{2}+bx+c$,   where here   $b=-4y$   and   $c=3y^{2}$.
Hint
There is no common factor across $x^{2},\,4xy,\,3y^{2}$ other than $1$, so we must split into two brackets of the form $(x+\cdots)(x+\cdots)$.

We need two quantities whose product is $3y^{2}$ and whose sum is $-4y$. Both must involve $y$: try $-y$ and $-3y$.

Product: $(-y)(-3y)=3y^{2}$  ✓
Sum: $(-y)+(-3y)=-4y$  ✓
Hint
Because the sum is negative and the product is positive, both numbers are negative.

Insert $-y$ and $-3y$ into the brackets:

Answer (a)
$x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}=(x-y)(x-3y)$  1A

Check by expansion:

$(x-y)(x-3y)=x^{2}-3xy-xy+3y^{2}=x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$ ✓

Interactive check

Drag the slider to choose a value of $y$ (keeping $x=5$). Both sides of $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}=(x-y)(x-3y)$ must stay equal.

Visual model: why $(x-y)(x-3y)$ works

An area (rectangle) model with lengths $x-y$ and $x-3y$.

$(x-y)(x-3y)=x\!\cdot\!x-3xy-xy+3y^{2}=x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}$

Part (b): Factorise $x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y$

The trick: part (a) is hiding inside part (b)!

The first three terms are exactly the expression from part (a):

$x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y=\underbrace{(x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2})}_{\text{part (a)}}+11x-33y$

Using $(x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2})=(x-y)(x-3y)$:

$=(x-y)(x-3y)+11x-33y$  1M
Hint
The "1M" mark is awarded here — for correctly using the result of (a) inside (b). Many students lose this mark by re-factorising from scratch.

Look closely at the leftover pair of terms. Both $11x$ and $33y$ are multiples of $11$ — but that is not the factor we need. We want the same factor as the first bracket, namely $(x-3y)$.

$11x-33y=11(x-3y)$
Hint
Check: $11(x-3y)=11x-33y$ ✓. The expression $11x-33y$ is chosen so that $(x-3y)$ appears — that is the whole point of the question design.

Now both terms share the factor $(x-3y)$:

$(x-y)(x-3y)+11(x-3y)=(x-3y)\big[(x-y)+11\big]$
Answer (b)
$x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y=(x-3y)(x-y+11)$  1A

Expand to verify:

$(x-3y)(x-y+11)=x^{2}-xy+11x-3xy+3y^{2}-33y=x^{2}-4xy+3y^{2}+11x-33y$ ✓

Why $(x-3y)$ is the common factor

Slide to change $x$; the two expressions below must always agree.

Concept flow

Part (a) builds the factor $(x-3y)$; part (b) reuses it together with the re-written $11x-33y = 11(x-3y)$.
