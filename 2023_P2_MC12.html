Map Scale & Area — Rate, Ratio and Variations

Ratio Scale Similar Figures Units Conversion Area Scaling (k²)

QQuestion

The scale of a map is \(1 : 50\,000\). If the actual area of an airport is \(10\ \text{km}^2\), then the area of this airport on the map is

  • A\(2\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • B\(4\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • C\(20\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • D\(40\ \text{cm}^2\)
Tap an answer to check it — or work through the steps below.

▶Visualise It: Length ratio vs Area ratio

Length scale factor (map : ground) 1 : 50 000
1 : 1 000　·　1 : 10 000　·　1 : 50 000　·　1 : 100 000　·　1 : 250 000
Actual area \(A\)
10 km²
Map area \(A \times k^2\)
40 cm²
\(A_{\text{map}} = 10\ \text{km}^2 \times \left(\tfrac{1}{50\,000}\right)^2\)

✎Step-by-Step Solution

1
Understand what the ratio \(1:50\,000\) means
⌄

A map scale \(1:50\,000\) is a length ratio:

\[ 1\ \text{cm on the map} \;=\; 50\,000\ \text{cm on the ground} \]

So the map is a similar figure (a shrunk copy) of the real airport — every length is multiplied by the same factor.

Hint: A ratio like "\(1:n\)" always compares lengths first. It never directly tells you how areas compare.
2
Turn the length ratio into an area ratio
⌄

Area is 2-dimensional. When every length is scaled by a factor \(k\), every area is scaled by \(k^2\).

\[ \frac{\text{Map area}}{\text{Actual area}} = \left(\frac{1}{50\,000}\right)^{2} = \frac{1}{2.5\times 10^{9}} \]

Check: \(50\,000^2 = 2\,500\,000\,000 = 2.5\times 10^{9}\).

Hint – Similar figures: If the length ratio is \(1:n\), then the area ratio is \(1:n^2\). This is the single most important idea in this question.
3
Convert \(10\ \text{km}^2\) into \(\text{cm}^2\)
⌄

The map area will come out in \(\text{cm}^2\), so the actual area must use the same unit.

\[ 1\ \text{km}=1000\ \text{m},\qquad 1\ \text{m}=100\ \text{cm} \] \[ 1\ \text{km}^2 = (1000\times 100)^2\ \text{cm}^2 = (10^5)^2\ \text{cm}^2 = 10^{10}\ \text{cm}^2 \] \[ 10\ \text{km}^2 = 10 \times 10^{10}\ \text{cm}^2 = 10^{11}\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint: Convert length before squaring. \(1\ \text{km}^2\) is not \(1000\ \text{cm}^2\); it is \((100\,000)^2 = 10^{10}\ \text{cm}^2\).
4
Multiply actual area by the area ratio
⌄
\[ A_{\text{map}} = 10\ \text{km}^2 \times \left(\frac{1}{50\,000}\right)^{2} \] \[ = 10 \times (1000\times 100)^2 \div (2.5\times 10^{9})\ \text{cm}^2 \] \[ = 10 \times 10^{10} \div (2.5\times 10^{9})\ \text{cm}^2 \] \[ = 10^{11} \div (2.5\times 10^{9})\ \text{cm}^2 \] \[ = 40\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint – quick check: \(2.5\times 10^{9} \times 40 = 10^{11}\).
5
Choose the correct option
⌄

The map area is \(40\ \text{cm}^2\), so the answer is D.

Why not A / B / C? Forgetting to square the scale gives \(10^7\ \text{cm}^2\)-type errors, and mixing up units of \(\text{km}^2\) and \(\text{cm}^2\) easily produces options like C.

Key exam trap: A linear ratio must be squared before it can be applied to areas.
