Polynomial division Remainder / Factor Theorem Roots of equations Discriminant

Question

Paper 1 · 12 · Total 7 marks

The polynomial \(P(x)\) is divisible by \(x-5\). When \(P(x)\) is divided by \(x^{2}+x+1\), the quotient and the remainder are \(2x^{2}-37\) and \(cx+c-1\) respectively, where \(c\) is a constant.
(a)3 marks
Find \(c\).
(b)1 mark
Prove that \(x+3\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).
(c)3 marks
Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \(P(x)=0\) are real numbers. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.

Core ideas in one glance

ToolWhat it gives you
Division identity \(P(x) = (x^{2}+x+1)(2x^{2}-37) + (cx+c-1)\)
“Divisible by \(x-5\)” \(P(5)=0\) by the Factor Theorem
“\(x+3\) is a factor” Show \(P(-3)=0\) (converse of Factor Theorem)
“All roots real?” Check the discriminant \(\Delta = b^{2}-4ac\) of the leftover quadratic

Step-by-step guided solution

Click a step to reveal it. Try the hints before reading the algebra.

1 Write the division statement as an equation ▼

When a polynomial is divided, dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder. Here the divisor is \(x^{2}+x+1\), the quotient is \(2x^{2}-37\) and the remainder is \(cx+c-1\).

\[ P(x) = (x^{2}+x+1)(2x^{2}-37) + (cx+c-1) \]
Hint – Why is the remainder \(cx+c-1\)?
The divisor \(x^{2}+x+1\) is of degree 2, so the remainder must have degree at most 1, i.e. of the form \(ax+b\). Here it is given as \(cx+c-1\), whose coefficients happen to share the same letter \(c\).
2 Part (a): use “divisible by \(x-5\)” → \(P(5)=0\) ▼

Being divisible by \(x-5\) means \(x-5\) is a factor, so by the Factor Theorem,

\[ P(5)=0. \]
Hint – Factor Theorem
\(x-a\) is a factor of \(P(x)\) \(\iff P(a)=0\).
With \(a=5\), substituting \(x=5\) must give zero — this is the equation that pins down \(c\).
3 Part (a): substitute \(x=5\) and solve for \(c\) ▼

Put \(x=5\) into the division identity:

\[ (5^{2}+5+1)\bigl(2(5^{2})-37\bigr) + 5c + c - 1 = 0 \]

Evaluate each bracket:

\[ (25+5+1)(50-37) + 6c - 1 = 0 \] \[ (31)(13) + 6c - 1 = 0 \] \[ 403 + 6c - 1 = 0 \] \[ 6c + 402 = 0 \]
\[ c = -67 \]
Hint – mental check
\(5^{2}+5+1=31\) and \(2(5^{2})-37=50-37=13\), so the product is \(403\). Then \(6c = -402\) immediately gives \(c=-67\).
4 Write out \(P(x)\) in expanded form ▼

Substitute \(c=-67\) back into the division identity:

\[ P(x) = (x^{2}+x+1)(2x^{2}-37) - 67x - 68 \]

Expand the product:

\[ x^{2}(2x^{2}-37) + x(2x^{2}-37) + 1(2x^{2}-37) \] \[ = 2x^{4} - 37x^{2} + 2x^{3} - 37x + 2x^{2} - 37 \]
\[ P(x) = 2x^{4} + 2x^{3} - 35x^{2} - 67x - 68 - 67x - 68 \]

Combining like terms (careful with the two \(-x\) and constant terms):

\[ P(x) = 2x^{4} + 2x^{3} - 35x^{2} - 104x - 105 \]
Hint – don’t lose terms
\(-37x - 67x = -104x\) and \(-68\) is the constant. The \(x^{2}\) terms are \(-37x^{2} + 2x^{2} = -35x^{2}\).
5 Part (b): prove \(x+3\) is a factor by showing \(P(-3)=0\) ▼

\(x+3 = x-(-3)\), so we test \(P(-3)\):

\[ P(-3) = 2(-3)^{4} + 2(-3)^{3} - 35(-3)^{2} - 104(-3) - 105 \] \[ = 2(81) + 2(-27) - 35(9) + 312 - 105 \] \[ = 162 - 54 - 315 + 312 - 105 \] \[ = 0 \]
Since \(P(-3)=0\), by the Factor Theorem \(x+3\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).
Hint – sign care
\((-3)^{4}=+81\), \((-3)^{3}=-27\), \((-3)^{2}=+9\). The term \(-104(-3)\) becomes \(+312\).
6 Part (c): split \(P(x)\) using the two known factors ▼

From part (b) we know \(x+3\) is a factor, and the question says \(P(x)\) is divisible by \(x-5\), so \(x-5\) is also a factor. Since \(P\) has degree 4, the remaining factor is a quadratic:

\[ P(x) = (x+3)(x-5)(2x^{2}+6x+7) \]
Hint – how do we know the leading term?
The \(x^{4}\) term of \(P\) has coefficient 2, and each of \((x+3)\), \((x-5)\) contributes a leading \(x\), so the quadratic must start with \(2x^{2}\). You can verify by expanding: \((x+3)(x-5)=x^{2}-2x-15\), and \((x^{2}-2x-15)(2x^{2}+6x+7)\) gives back \(2x^{4}+2x^{3}-35x^{2}-104x-105\).
7 Part (c): examine each factor’s roots ▼

Setting \(P(x)=0\):

\[ (x+3)(x-5)(2x^{2}+6x+7)=0 \]

The two linear factors give real roots straight away:

\[ x = -3 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 5 \]

Now test the quadratic factor \(2x^{2}+6x+7\):

\[ \Delta = b^{2}-4ac = 6^{2}-4(2)(7) = 36 - 56 = -20 \]
Since \(\Delta = -20 < 0\), the equation \(2x^{2}+6x+7=0\) has no real roots.
8 Part (c): state the conclusion clearly ▼

The equation \(P(x)=0\) has only two real roots, \(x=-3\) and \(x=5\); the quadratic factor contributes non-real (complex) roots.

Therefore the claim that all roots of \(P(x)=0\) are real is NOT correct.
Exam tip
A full-mark explanation must explicitly mention the discriminant of the remaining quadratic and show \(\Delta<0\), not just state “not correct”.

Final answers

(a) \(c=-67\)  ·  (b) \(P(-3)=0 \Rightarrow x+3\) is a factor  ·  (c) Not correct — \(\Delta=-20<0\)
