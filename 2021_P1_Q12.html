Question

The polynomial $P(x)$ is divisible by $x-5$. When $P(x)$ is divided by $x^2+x+1$, the quotient and the remainder are $2x^2-37$ and $cx+c-1$ respectively, where $c$ is a constant.

(a) Find $c$. [3 marks]

(b) Prove that $x+3$ is a factor of $P(x)$. [1 mark]

(c) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $P(x)=0$ are real numbers. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

Solution

STEP 1 · Part (a)

Set up $P(x)$ using the division algorithm with divisor $x^2+x+1$, quotient $2x^2-37$ and remainder $cx+c-1$:

$P(x)=(x^2+x+1)(2x^2-37)+cx+c-1$

Also, $P(x)$ is divisible by $x-5$, so by the Factor Theorem:

$P(5)=0$

Two given facts: the division identity and the factor condition $P(5)=0$.
Factor Theorem \(P(a)=0\ \iff\ x-a\ \text{is a factor of}\ P(x)\) \(P(x)\) is divisible by \(x-5\), so \(P(5)=0\), which pins down \(c\).
Division Algorithm \(P(x)=D(x)\,Q(x)+R(x)\) With divisor \(x^{2}+x+1\), quotient \(2x^{2}-37\) and remainder \(cx+c-1\), we get \(P(x)=(x^{2}+x+1)(2x^{2}-37)+cx+c-1\).
STEP 2 · Part (a) — solve for c

Substitute $x=5$ into the identity:

$P(5)=(5^2+5+1)\big(2(5)^2-37\big)+5c+c-1=0$

$(31)(50-37)+6c-1=0$

$403+6c-1=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 6c+402=0$

$c=-67$

The factor $x^2+x+1$ gives $31$, and the remainder $cx+c-1$ becomes $6c-1$.
STEP 3 · Part (b) — expand P(x)

With $c=-67$, the remainder is $-67x-68$, so:

$P(x)=(x^2+x+1)(2x^2-37)-67x-68$

$=2x^4+2x^3-35x^2-104x-105$

Expand carefully: $(x^2+x+1)(2x^2-37)=2x^4+2x^3-35x^2-37x-37$, then combine the $-67x-68$.
STEP 4 · Part (b) — show the factor

Evaluate $P(-3)$:

$P(-3)=2(-3)^4+2(-3)^3-35(-3)^2-104(-3)-105$

$=162-54-315+312-105=0$

Since $P(-3)=0$, by the Factor Theorem $x+3$ is a factor of $P(x)$.

A zero remainder at $x=-3$ means the line $x=-3$ is a root — the curve crosses there.
STEP 5 · Part (c) — factorise fully

We know $x-5$ and $x+3$ are factors, and the leading term is $2x^4$:

$P(x)=(x+3)(x-5)(2x^2+6x+7)$

The quadratic remainder $2x^2+6x+7$ cannot be factorised over the reals — test the discriminant:

$\Delta=6^2-4(2)(7)=36-56=-20$

Divide $2x^4+2x^3-35x^2-104x-105$ by $(x-5)(x+3)=x^2-2x-15$ to obtain $2x^2+6x+7$.
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For \(2x^{2}+6x+7\), \(\Delta=36-56=-20<0\), so its two roots are non-real.
STEP 6 · Part (c) — conclusion

Since $\Delta=-20<0$, the equation $2x^2+6x+7=0$ has no real roots (its two roots are complex conjugates).

So $P(x)=0$ has only the real roots $x=-3$ and $x=5$, together with two non-real roots.

The claim that all roots are real is therefore not correct.

A negative discriminant for the surviving quadratic means the curve touches/crosses only at $x=-3$ and $x=5$ on the real axis.
Answer:  (a) $c=-67$  ·  (b) $P(-3)=0$, so $x+3$ is a factor  ·  (c) Not correct — $P(x)=(x+3)(x-5)(2x^2+6x+7)$ and $2x^2+6x+7$ has $\Delta=-20<0$, so two roots are non-real.

Question. Let \(P(x)=(x^{2}+x+3)(x^{2}-6)+cx+c-1\), where \(c\) is a constant. It is given that \(P(3)=0\).  [6 marks]
(a) Find \(c\).
(b) Expand \(P(x)\) and show that \(x+2\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).
(c) Someone claims that all the roots of \(P(x)=0\) are real numbers. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(P(3)=(9+3+3)(9-6)+3c+c-1=45+4c-1=0\), so \(4c=-44\) and \(c=-11\).
Step 2. The remainder is \(-11x-12\), so \(P(x)=(x^{2}+x+3)(x^{2}-6)-11x-12=x^{4}+x^{3}-3x^{2}-17x-30\).
Step 3. \(P(-2)=16-8-12+34-30=0\), so by the factor theorem \(x+2\) is a factor of \(P(x)\).
Step 4. Since \(x-3\) is also a factor, \(P(x)=(x-3)(x+2)(x^{2}+2x+5)\). For \(x^{2}+2x+5\), \(\Delta=4-20=-16<0\), so its roots are non-real.
Step 5. Only \(x=3\) and \(x=-2\) are real roots, so the claim is not correct.
Answer: (a) \(c=-11\)  (b) \(P(x)=x^{4}+x^{3}-3x^{2}-17x-30\), \(P(-2)=0\)  (c) Not correct — \(P(x)=(x-3)(x+2)(x^{2}+2x+5)\) with \(\Delta=-16<0\)
