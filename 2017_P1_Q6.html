[2017 #6]
The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-3,\,4)\) and \((9,\,-9)\) respectively. \(A\) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin through \(90^\circ\) to \(A'\). \(B'\) is the reflection image of \(B\) with respect to the \(x\)-axis.

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(A'\) and \(B'\).
(b) Prove that \(AB\) is perpendicular to \(A'B'\).
[4 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Rotating A anticlockwise 90°

Rotation about the origin anticlockwise by \(90^\circ\) sends \((x,\,y)\rightarrow(-y,\,x)\).

For \(A(-3,\,4)\):

$$A' = (-4,\;-3)$$

Check: \(A\) is in quadrant II; after \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise it lands in quadrant III.
STEP 2 · Reflecting B across the x-axis

Reflection in the \(x\)-axis sends \((x,\,y)\rightarrow(x,\,-y)\).

For \(B(9,\,-9)\):

$$B' = (9,\;9)$$

Only the sign of the \(y\)-coordinate changes.
STEP 3 · Slope of AB

Slope formula: \(\;m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).

$$m_{AB}=\frac{4-(-9)}{-3-9}=\frac{13}{-12}=-\frac{13}{12}$$

The line \(AB\) descends from left to right, so its slope is negative.
STEP 4 · Slope of A′B′

Using \(A'(-4,\,-3)\) and \(B'(9,\,9)\):

$$m_{A'B'}=\frac{-3-9}{-4-9}=\frac{-12}{-13}=\frac{12}{13}$$

This line rises from left to right, so its slope is positive.
STEP 5 · Product of the slopes

$$m_{AB}\times m_{A'B'}=\left(-\frac{13}{12}\right)\times\frac{12}{13}=-1$$

When the product of two slopes equals \(-1\), the two lines are perpendicular.

Since both lines are non-vertical, this test is valid.
(a) \(A'=(-4,\,-3)\), \(B'=(9,\,9)\)  ·  (b) \(m_{AB}\times m_{A'B'}=-1\), so \(AB\perp A'B'\).
