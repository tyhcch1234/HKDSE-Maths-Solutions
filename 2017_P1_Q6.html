Coordinate Geometry — Rotation & Reflection of Points

Coordinates of a Point Rotation about the Origin Reflection in the x-axis Slope of a Line Perpendicular Lines

QQuestion

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-3,\ 4)\) and \((9,\ -9)\) respectively. \(A\) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin through \(90^\circ\) to \(A'\\). \(B'\\) is the reflection image of \(B\) with respect to the \(x\)-axis.

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(A'\\) and \(B'\\).

(b) Prove that \(AB\) is perpendicular to \(A'B'\\).

[4 marks]

aPart (a) — Coordinates of \(A'\\) and \(B'\\)2 marks

90°
\(A = (-3,\ 4)\)
\(A'\\) rotated θ° = (-4, -3)
\(B = (9,\ -9)\)
\(B'\\) reflected = (9, 9)
Step 11M

Rotating a point \((x,\ y)\) anticlockwise about the origin through \(90^\circ\) sends it to \((-y,\ x)\).

Apply this to \(A(-3,\ 4)\):

\[ A' = (-4,\ -3) \]
Hint: Check with the canvas — drag the slider to \(90^\circ\) and watch \(A\) sweep anticlockwise onto the point labelled \(A'\\).
\(A' = (-4,\ -3)\)   (1A)
Step 21A

Reflecting a point \((x,\ y)\) in the \(x\)-axis changes the sign of the \(y\)-coordinate only, giving \((x,\ -y)\).

Apply this to \(B(9,\ -9)\):

\[ B' = (9,\ 9) \]
Hint: Reflection in the \(x\)-axis is a "mirror flip" vertically — the \(x\)-coordinate stays the same, the \(y\)-coordinate flips sign.
\(B' = (9,\ 9)\)   (1A)
0 / 2

bPart (b) — Prove \(AB \perp A'B'\\)2 marks

\(m_{AB} = \dfrac{-13}{12}\)
\(m_{A'B'} = \dfrac{12}{13}\)
\(m_{AB}\times m_{A'B'} = \dfrac{-13}{12}\times\dfrac{12}{13} = \mathbf{-1}\)
Step 11M

Gradient of \(AB\), using \(A(-3,\ 4)\) and \(B(9,\ -9)\):

\[ m_{AB} = \frac{4 - (-9)}{-3 - 9} = \frac{4 + 9}{-12} = \frac{13}{-12} = -\frac{13}{12} \]
Hint: Gradient \(=\dfrac{y_B - y_A}{x_B - x_A}\). Keep the signs of both the numerator and denominator carefully.
Step 21M

Gradient of \(A'B'\\), using \(A'(-4,\ -3)\) and \(B'(9,\ 9)\):

\[ m_{A'B'} = \frac{-3 - 9}{-4 - 9} = \frac{-12}{-13} = \frac{12}{13} \]
Hint: A negative divided by a negative gives a positive — the two minus signs cancel. Do not drop them!
Step 31A

Take the product of the two gradients:

\[ m_{AB} \times m_{A'B'} = \left(-\frac{13}{12}\right)\left(\frac{12}{13}\right) = -1 \]

Since the product of the gradients is \(-1\), the line \(AB\) is perpendicular to the line \(A'B'\\).

Hint: The product-of-gradients test: two (non-vertical) lines are perpendicular if and only if \(m_1 m_2 = -1\). On the canvas, the right-angle mark at the intersection confirms this visually.
\(m_{AB}\times m_{A'B'} = -1 \implies AB \perp A'B'\\)   (1A)

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(A' = (-4,\ -3)\)  and  \(B' = (9,\ 9)\)
  • Part (b): \(m_{AB} = -\dfrac{13}{12}\),   \(m_{A'B'} = \dfrac{12}{13}\)
  • Product: \(m_{AB}\times m_{A'B'} = \left(-\dfrac{13}{12}\right)\!\left(\dfrac{12}{13}\right) = -1\)
  • Conclusion: \(\therefore\ AB \perp A'B'\\)  (since the product of the two gradients is \(-1\))
