HKDSE 2014 · Paper 2 · Q4

Question

Let \(a\) be a constant. If the quadratic equation \[ x^{2}+ax+a=1 \] has equal roots, then \(a=\)
A. \(-1\) B. \(2\) C. \(0\) or \(-4\) D. \(0\) or \(4\)

Step-by-step Solution

1Write the equation in standard form

Move every term to one side so the equation reads \(ax^{2}+bx+c=0\):

\[ x^{2}+ax+a-1=0 \]

So here \(a_{\text{coeff}}=1\), \(b=a\) and \(c=a-1\).

Hint: "equal roots" only makes sense once the equation is in standard form.
2Set the discriminant equal to zero

A quadratic has equal (repeated) roots exactly when

\[ \Delta=b^{2}-4ac=0 \]
\[ a^{2}-4(1)(a-1)=0 \]
Hint: equal roots \(\iff \Delta=0\); two distinct real roots \(\iff \Delta>0\).
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) With \(a_{\text{coeff}}=1\), \(b=a\) and \(c=a-1\), the equation has equal roots exactly when \(\Delta=0\).
3Simplify and factorise
\[ a^{2}-4a+4=0 \]
\[ (a-2)^{2}=0 \]
Hint: this is a perfect square — \(a^{2}-4a+4=(a-2)^{2}\).
Perfect Square Identity \((a-b)^{2}=a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}\) \(a^{2}-4a+4=(a-2)^{2}\), which is why the two roots of the discriminant collapse into one value.
4Solve for \(a\)
\[ a-2=0 \;\Rightarrow\; a=2 \;\text{(repeated)} \]

Only one value of \(a\) makes the discriminant zero.

Hint: a repeated root of the discriminant gives one value of \(a\), not two.
5Check against the options

\(a=2\) matches option B. (Options C and D would come from a different equation, e.g. \(a^{2}-4a=0\).)

Hint: always re-read the options after solving to avoid mis-copying.
Answer: B. \(2\)

Question. Let \(k\) be a constant. If the quadratic equation \(x^{2}+2kx+k+6=0\) has equal roots, then \(k=\)
A. \(-2\) or \(3\)    B. \(2\) or \(-3\)    C. \(-2\) only    D. \(3\) only  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Here the coefficients are \(a=1\), \(b=2k\), \(c=k+6\).
Step 2. Equal roots \u21d4 \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac=0\): \((2k)^{2}-4(1)(k+6)=0\), i.e. \(4k^{2}-4k-24=0\).
Step 3. Divide by \(4\): \(k^{2}-k-6=0\), so \((k-3)(k+2)=0\).
Step 4. Hence \(k=3\) or \(k=-2\) — two values, option A.
Answer: A. \(k=-2\) or \(k=3\)
