The Question

[2012 MC #29] The bar chart below shows the distribution of the numbers of rings owned by the girls in a group. Find the standard deviation of the distribution correct to 2 decimal places.

Total girls = 22
  • A. 1.04
  • B. 1.16
  • C. 1.19
  • D. 2.09
Marks: this is a multiple-choice question (1 mark for the correct option). The full working is broken down below so you can see exactly where each number comes from.

Step 1 Count the girls (total frequency \(N\))
Reading the bar chart heights: \(2, 8, 4, 6, 2\) girls for \(0,1,2,3,4\) rings respectively. \[ N = 2 + 8 + 4 + 6 + 2 = 22 \]
Hint: The total frequency is the number of girls — every time you move the bar-chart slider above, this total never changes. It is the denominator of every average.
Step 2 Compute the total number of rings
Multiply each value by its frequency and add: \[ 0\times 2 + 1\times 8 + 2\times 4 + 3\times 6 + 4\times 2 = 0 + 8 + 8 + 18 + 8 = 42 \]
Hint: A zero-frequency-value product (\(0\times 2 = 0\)) still contributes nothing to the sum — but it still counts towards \(N\). That is why the mean is not \(42/20\).
Step 3 Mean of the distribution
\[ \mu = \frac{\sum x f}{N} = \frac{42}{22} = \frac{21}{11} \approx 1.9091 \]
Answer (a): \(\mu = \dfrac{21}{11} \approx 1.91\) (correct to 2 d.p.)
Hint: Keep the exact fraction \(\frac{21}{11}\) for the variance step — rounding early to \(1.91\) would slightly change the final standard deviation and could push you to the wrong option.

Part (b) — Find the variance \(\sigma^2\) Sum of squared deviations from the mean, divided by \(N\)

s = 1.00
σ² = 164/121 ≈ 1.3554
Step 1 Deviation of each value from the mean
Use the exact mean \(\mu = \frac{21}{11}\). Compute \(x - \frac{21}{11}\) for each ring count: \[ 0 - \tfrac{21}{11} = -\tfrac{21}{11}, \quad 1 - \tfrac{21}{11} = -\tfrac{10}{11}, \quad 2 - \tfrac{21}{11} = \tfrac{1}{11}, \quad 3 - \tfrac{21}{11} = \tfrac{12}{11}, \quad 4 - \tfrac{21}{11} = \tfrac{23}{11} \]
Hint: Deviations can be negative — the girls with 0 or 1 ring sit below the mean. Notice that the number of rings below the mean (2 & 8 girls) noticeably exceeds the number who own 3 or 4 rings.
Step 2 Square each deviation and weight by frequency
\[ \sum f(x-\mu)^2 = \left(0-\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 2 + \left(1-\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 8 + \left(2-\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 4 + \left(3-\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 6 + \left(4-\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 2 \] \[ = \left(\tfrac{21}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 2 + \left(\tfrac{10}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 8 + \left(\tfrac{1}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 4 + \left(\tfrac{12}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 6 + \left(\tfrac{23}{11}\right)^2\!\cdot 2 \]
Hint: Squaring removes the signs, so both "far below" and "far above" the mean count as spread — exactly what variance is measuring.
Step 3 Variance of the distribution
With a common denominator \(121\): \[ \sum f(x-\mu)^2 = \frac{2(441) + 8(100) + 4(1) + 6(144) + 2(529)}{121} = \frac{882 + 800 + 4 + 864 + 1058}{121} = \frac{3608}{121} \] \[ \sigma^2 = \frac{\sum f(x-\mu)^2}{N} = \frac{3608/121}{22} = \frac{3608}{2662} = \frac{164}{121} \]
Answer (b): \(\sigma^2 = \dfrac{164}{121} \approx 1.3554\)
Hint: Always divide by \(N = 22\) (the total frequency), not by the number of distinct values (5). This is the key trap in this question.

Part (c) — Standard deviation to 2 decimal places Take the square root and round; compare with the four options

2 d.p.
σ ≈ 1.164204…
Step 1 Take the positive square root of the variance
\[ \sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2} = \sqrt{\frac{164}{121}} = \frac{\sqrt{164}}{11} \]
Hint: Standard deviation is always taken as the non-negative root. The denominator \(121=11^2\) comes out of the radical neatly.
Step 2 Evaluate numerically
\[ \sqrt{164} \approx 12.806248 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sigma \approx \frac{12.806248}{11} \approx 1.164204407 \]
Hint: Keep plenty of decimal places until the very last step; only round when the question asks for it.
Step 3 Round to 2 d.p. and choose the option
\[ \sigma \approx 1.16 \quad (\text{correct to } 2 \text{ d.p.}) \] Comparing with the options:

Summary of Answers

  • Total number of girls: \(N = 22\)
  • Total rings: \(\sum xf = 42\)
  • Mean: \(\mu = \dfrac{42}{22} = \dfrac{21}{11} \approx 1.91\)
  • Variance: \(\sigma^2 = \dfrac{164}{121} \approx 1.3554\)
  • Standard deviation: \(\sigma = \sqrt{\dfrac{164}{121}} \approx 1.1642\)
  • Correct option: B (1.16) — σ ≈ 1.16
