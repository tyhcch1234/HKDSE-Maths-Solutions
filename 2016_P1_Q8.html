The Question

It is given that \( f(x) \) is the sum of two parts, one part varies as \( x \) and the other part varies as \( x^2 \). Suppose that \( f(3) = 48 \) and \( f(9) = 198 \).

  1. (a) Find \( f(x) \).
  2. (b) Solve the equation \( f(x) = 90 \). [5 marks]

Step-by-Step Solution

“One part varies as \( x \)” means \( hx \) (direct variation).

“The other part varies as \( x^2 \)” means \( kx^2 \) (direct square variation).

Since \( f(x) \) is the sum:

\( f(x) = hx + kx^2 \)

where \( h \) and \( k \) are non-zero constants to be found.

💡 Hint: Direct variation \( y \propto x \) means \( y = hx \). Direct square variation \( y \propto x^2 \) means \( y = kx^2 \).

When \( x = 3 \):

\( f(3) = h(3) + k(3)^2 = 48 \)

\( 3h + 9k = 48 \)  … (1)

When \( x = 9 \):

\( f(9) = h(9) + k(9)^2 = 198 \)

\( 9h + 81k = 198 \)  … (2)

💡 Hint: We now have a system of two linear equations in \( h \) and \( k \). We can solve them by elimination or substitution.

Method: elimination.

Multiply (1) by 3:

\( 9h + 27k = 144 \)  … (3)

(2) − (3):

\( (81k - 27k) = 198 - 144 \)

\( 54k = 54 \)

\( k = 1 \)

Substitute \( k = 1 \) into (1):

\( 3h + 9(1) = 48 \)

\( 3h = 39 \)

\( h = 13 \)

Therefore:

\( f(x) = 13x + x^2 \)

💡 Hint: Always verify: \( f(3) = 13(3) + 3^2 = 39 + 9 = 48 \) ✓ and \( f(9) = 13(9) + 9^2 = 117 + 81 = 198 \) ✓

\( f(x) = 90 \)

\( 13x + x^2 = 90 \)

Bring all terms to one side:

\( x^2 + 13x - 90 = 0 \)

💡 Hint: This is a quadratic equation in standard form \( ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \). We can solve it by factorisation or the quadratic formula.

We need two numbers that multiply to \(-90\) and add to \(13\):

\( 18 \) and \( -5 \)   (since \( 18 \times (-5) = -90 \) and \( 18 + (-5) = 13 \))

So:

\( (x + 18)(x - 5) = 0 \)

Hence:

\( x = -18 \)   or   \( x = 5 \)

💡 Hint: You can also use the quadratic formula: \( x = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{13^2 - 4(1)(-90)}}{2} = \frac{-13 \pm \sqrt{169 + 360}}{2} = \frac{-13 \pm 23}{2} \).
