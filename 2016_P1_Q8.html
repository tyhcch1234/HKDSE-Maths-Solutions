HKDSE 2016 Paper 1 · Q8 · 2. Rate, Ratio and Variations

Question. It is given that $f(x)$ is the sum of two parts, one part varies as $x$ and the other part varies as $x^2$. Suppose that $f(3)=48$ and $f(9)=198$.

(a) Find $f(x)$.

(b) Solve the equation $f(x)=90$.  [5 marks]

1 Set up the model from "varies as"
Direct Variation\(y\propto x^{n}\ \Rightarrow\ y=kx^{n}\)One part varies as \(x\) and the other as \(x^{2}\), so the function must take the form \(hx+kx^{2}\).

Since one part varies as $x$ and the other part varies as $x^2$, we may write $f(x)=hx+kx^2$ for some non‑zero constants $h$ and $k$.

Substituting the two given values of $x$:

$f(3)=3h+9k=48$   … (1)

$f(9)=9h+81k=198$   … (2)

Hint: the words "varies as" translate directly into the coefficients $hx$ and $kx^2$.
2 Eliminate one unknown
Elimination Method for Simultaneous Equations\(\begin{cases}h+3k=16\\ h+9k=22\end{cases}\ \Rightarrow\ 6k=6\)Dividing by \(3\) and by \(9\) makes \(h\) the same in both equations, so subtracting eliminates it.

Divide (1) by 3 and (2) by 9 to tidy up:

(1) $\Rightarrow h+3k=16$

(2) $\Rightarrow h+9k=22$

Subtract: $(h+9k)-(h+3k)=22-16$, so $6k=6$, giving $k=1$.

Hint: scaling each equation makes the substitutions much lighter.
3 Back‑substitute to find the other constant

Put $k=1$ into $h+3k=16$:

$h+3(1)=16 \Rightarrow h=13$.

Hence $f(x)=13x+x^2$.

Hint: always substitute back into the simpler of the two equations.
4 Solve $f(x)=90$ (factorise)
Cross Method of Factorisation\((x+p)(x+q)=x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq\)Two numbers multiplying to \(-90\) and adding to \(13\) are \(18\) and \(-5\), giving \((x-5)(x+18)=0\).

$13x+x^2=90$

$x^2+13x-90=0$

Factorising: $(x-5)(x+18)=0$.

Hint: look for two numbers multiplying to $-90$ and adding to $13$.
5 Read off the roots & check

$(x-5)(x+18)=0 \Rightarrow x=5$ or $x=-18$.

Check: $f(5)=13(5)+5^2=65+25=90$ ✓   and   $f(-18)=13(-18)+(-18)^2=-234+324=90$ ✓

Hint: both roots satisfy the equation — a quadratic generally gives two solutions.
Answer: (a) $f(x)=13x+x^2$    (b) $x=5$ or $x=-18$

Question. It is given that \(g(x)\) is the sum of two parts, one part varies as \(x\) and the other part varies as \(x^{2}\). Suppose that \(g(2)=20\) and \(g(4)=64\).
(a) Find \(g(x)\).
(b) Solve the equation \(g(x)=7\). [5 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write \(g(x)=hx+kx^{2}\). Then \(g(2)=2h+4k=20\) … (1) and \(g(4)=4h+16k=64\) … (2).
Step 2. Divide (1) by \(2\) and (2) by \(4\): \(h+2k=10\) and \(h+4k=16\).
Step 3. Subtract: \(2k=6\), so \(k=3\); then \(h+2(3)=10\) gives \(h=4\). Hence \(g(x)=4x+3x^{2}\).
Step 4. Set \(4x+3x^{2}=7\): \(3x^{2}+4x-7=0\), which factorises as \((3x+7)(x-1)=0\).
Step 5. So \(x=1\) or \(x=-\dfrac{7}{3}\). Check: \(g(1)=4+3=7\) ✓ and \(g\left(-\dfrac{7}{3}\right)=-\dfrac{28}{3}+\dfrac{49}{3}=7\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(g(x)=4x+3x^{2}\) (b) \(x=1\) or \(x=-\dfrac{7}{3}\)
