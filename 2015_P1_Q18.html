2015 · P1 · Q18

Let $f(x)=2x^{2}-4kx+3k^{2}+5$, where $k$ is a real constant.

(a) Does the graph of $y=f(x)$ cut the $x$-axis? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

(b) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of $y=f(x)$. [3 marks]

(c) In the same rectangular coordinate system, let $S$ and $T$ be moving points on the graph of $y=f(x)$ and the graph of $y=2-f(x)$ respectively. Denote the origin by $O$. Someone claims that when $S$ and $T$ are nearest to each other, the circumcentre of $\triangle OST$ lies on the $x$-axis. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

(a) Does $y=f(x)$ cut the $x$-axis?
STEP 1

Compute the discriminant of $f(x)=0$

Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For \(2x^{2}-4kx+3k^{2}+5=0\) we have \(a=2\), \(b=-4k\), \(c=3k^{2}+5\), so \(\Delta=-8k^{2}-40\).
$$2x^{2}-4kx+3k^{2}+5=0$$ $$\Delta=(-4k)^{2}-4(2)(3k^{2}+5)=16k^{2}-24k^{2}-40=-8k^{2}-40$$
Hint: for a quadratic $ax^2+bx+c$, use $\Delta=b^2-4ac$ with $a=2$, $b=-4k$, $c=3k^{2}+5$.
STEP 2

Sign of $\Delta$ and its meaning

Since $k^{2}\ge 0$ for all real $k$, $$\Delta=-8k^{2}-40\le -40<0$$
Hint: $k^2$ can never be negative, so $-8k^2$ can never be positive.
STEP 3

Conclusion for (a)

$\Delta<0$ means the equation $f(x)=0$ has no real roots,
so the graph of $y=f(x)$ does not cut the $x$-axis.
Hint: no real roots ⇔ the parabola lies entirely above (or below) the $x$-axis.
(b) Completing the square
STEP 4

Express $f(x)$ in vertex (completing-the-square) form

Completing the Square \(ax^{2}+bx+c=a\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^{2}+c-\dfrac{b^{2}}{4a}\) Here \(f(x)=2(x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+5\), so the vertex is \(\left(k,\ k^{2}+5\right)\).
$$f(x)=2x^{2}-4kx+3k^{2}+5$$ $$=2\left(x^{2}-2kx\right)+3k^{2}+5$$ $$=2\left(x^{2}-2kx+k^{2}-k^{2}\right)+3k^{2}+5$$ $$=2(x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+5$$ Hence the vertex of $y=f(x)$ is $\left(k,\ k^{2}+5\right)$.
Hint: add and subtract the square of half the $x$-coefficient inside the bracket, then the vertex is at $x=h$ for $y=a(x-h)^2+c$.
(c) Nearest points $S$, $T$ and the circumcentre of $\triangle OST$
STEP 5

Vertex of $y=2-f(x)$

From (b), the vertex of $y=f(x)$ is $\left(k,\ k^{2}+5\right)$. Reflecting and shifting gives the vertex of $y=2-f(x)$: $$y=2-f(x)=2-\left[2(x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+5\right]$$ $$=-2(x-k)^{2}-k^{2}-3$$ So its vertex is $\left(k,\ -k^{2}-3\right)$.
Hint: $2-f(x)$ has the same axis of symmetry $x=k$ (an upside-down parabola).
STEP 6

Nearest pair $S$, $T$

The two graphs have the same axis of symmetry $x=k$ but open in opposite directions,
so the nearest pair is vertically aligned at $x=k$: $$S=\left(k,\ k^{2}+5\right),\qquad T=\left(k,\ -k^{2}-3\right)$$
Hint: moving $S$ or $T$ off $x=k$ only increases their separation.
STEP 7

Perpendicular bisector of $ST$

Perpendicular Bisector \(PA=PB\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P\ \text{lies on the perpendicular bisector of }AB\) The circumcentre is equidistant from \(S\left(k,k^{2}+5\right)\) and \(T\left(k,-k^{2}-3\right)\), so its \(y\)-coordinate is the mid-point value \(1\).
$ST$ is a vertical line, so its perpendicular bisector is a horizontal line.
The circumcentre of $\triangle OST$ lies on this perpendicular bisector,
so its $y$-coordinate is the midpoint's $y$-coordinate: $$y=\frac{(k^{2}+5)+(-k^{2}-3)}{2}=\frac{2}{2}=1$$
Hint: every point equidistant from $S$ and $T$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $ST$.
STEP 8

Decision on the claim

The $x$-axis is $y=0$. Since the circumcentre has $y$-coordinate $1\neq 0$,
it does not lie on the $x$-axis.
Therefore the claim is incorrect.
Hint: compare the found $y$-coordinate with $0$.
Answers:
(a) $\Delta=-8k^{2}-40<0$, so the graph does not cut the $x$-axis.
(b) Vertex $=\left(k,\ k^{2}+5\right)$.
(c) The circumcentre has $y$-coordinate $1\neq 0$; the claim is incorrect.

Question. Let \(f(x)=3x^{2}+6kx+4k^{2}+2\), where \(k\) is a real constant.
(a) Show that the graph of \(y=f(x)\) does not cut the \(x\)-axis.
(b) Find the vertex of \(y=f(x)\).
(c) The points \(S\) and \(T\) are the nearest pair of points on the graphs of \(y=f(x)\) and \(y=2-f(x)\). Find the \(y\)-coordinate of the circumcentre of \(\triangle OST\), and hence decide whether the circumcentre lies on the \(x\)-axis.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(\Delta=(6k)^{2}-4(3)(4k^{2}+2)=36k^{2}-48k^{2}-24=-12k^{2}-24\).
Step 2. Since \(k^{2}\ge0\), \(\Delta=-12k^{2}-24\le-24<0\), so \(f(x)=0\) has no real roots and the graph does not cut the \(x\)-axis.
Step 3. (b) \(f(x)=3(x^{2}+2kx)+4k^{2}+2=3\left[(x+k)^{2}-k^{2}\right]+4k^{2}+2=3(x+k)^{2}+k^{2}+2\), so the vertex is \(\left(-k,\ k^{2}+2\right)\).
Step 4. (c) \(y=2-f(x)=2-3(x+k)^{2}-k^{2}-2=-3(x+k)^{2}-k^{2}\), whose vertex is \(\left(-k,\ -k^{2}\right)\). Both graphs share the axis \(x=-k\) and open in opposite directions,
so \(S\left(-k,k^{2}+2\right)\) and \(T\left(-k,-k^{2}\right)\) are the nearest pair.
Step 5. The perpendicular bisector of the vertical segment \(ST\) is horizontal,
with \(y=\dfrac{(k^{2}+2)+(-k^{2})}{2}=1\). The circumcentre has \(y\)-coordinate \(1\neq0\), so it does not lie on the \(x\)-axis.
Answer: (a) \(\Delta=-12k^{2}-24<0\) (b) \(\left(-k,\ k^{2}+2\right)\) (c) \(y=1\), so the claim is incorrect.
