Q18 The Question

Let \(f(x) = 2x^2 - 4kx + 3k^2 + 5\), where \(k\) is a real constant.

(a) Does the graph of \(y=f(x)\) cut the \(x\)-axis? Explain your answer. 2 marks
(b) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of \(y=f(x)\). 3 marks
(c) In the same rectangular coordinate system, let \(S\) and \(T\) be moving points on the graph of \(y=f(x)\) and the graph of \(y=2-f(x)\) respectively. Denote the origin by \(O\). Someone claims that when \(S\) and \(T\) are nearest to each other, the circumcentre of \(\triangle OST\) lies on the \(x\)-axis. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. 4 marks

(a) Does the graph cut the \(x\)-axis? 2 marks

11M
The graph cuts the \(x\)-axis exactly when \(f(x)=0\) has real root(s). Compute the discriminant with \(a=2,\; b=-4k,\; c=3k^2+5\): \[ \Delta = (-4k)^2 - 4(2)(3k^2+5) \]
Hint: The bracket must be squared as a whole — \((-4k)^2 = 16k^2\), not \(-16k^2\).
21A
\[ \Delta = 16k^2 - 24k^2 - 40 = -8k^2 - 40 \] Since \(k^2 \ge 0\), \(8k^2+40 > 0\), hence \[ \Delta = -8k^2-40 < 0 \]
Hint: Show the reasoning clearly — the marker wants to see that \(\Delta\) is strictly negative for every real \(k\).
Conclusion: \(\Delta < 0\) for all real \(k\), so \(f(x)=0\) has no real roots. Thus the graph of \(y=f(x)\) does not cut the \(x\)-axis.

Hint: Only the \(x\)-terms are grouped inside the bracket; the constant \(3k^2+5\) stays outside.

Add and subtract \(k^2\) inside the bracket to complete the square: \[ f(x) = 2\bigl(x^2-2kx+k^2-k^2\bigr)+3k^2+5 \]
Hint: Half of \(2k\) is \(k\), so we need \((x-k)^2 = x^2-2kx+k^2\).

Hint: \(-2k^2+3k^2 = +k^2\). The vertex is read off directly: \((k,\;k^2+5)\).
Vertex: \(\;V\bigl(k,\;k^2+5\bigr)\).  (Axis of symmetry: \(x=k\).)

(c) Is the claim about the circumcentre correct? 4 marks

The two parabolas \(y=f(x)\) and \(y=2-f(x)\) are reflections. The nearest pair \(S,T\) always sit on a vertical line — watch the circumcentre.

11M
By (b), the vertex of \(y=f(x)\) is \(\bigl(k,\,k^2+5\bigr)\). The graph of \(y=2-f(x)\) is obtained by reflecting \(y=f(x)\) in the line \(y=1\), so its vertex is \[ \bigl(k,\;2-(k^2+5)\bigr) = \bigl(k,\,-k^2-3\bigr). \]
Hint: Reflection in \(y=1\) maps a \(y\)-value \(y_0\) to \(2-y_0\). The \(x\)-coordinate \(k\) is unchanged.
21M
The two curves share the same axis of symmetry \(x=k\), so the closest pair of points — one on each curve — must lie on this vertical line: \[ S\bigl(k,\;k^2+5\bigr), \qquad T\bigl(k,\;-k^2-3\bigr). \] Hence \(ST\) is a vertical line segment of length \[ ST = (k^2+5)-(-k^2-3) = 2k^2+8. \]
Hint: Horizontal separation is always at least \(|x_S-x_T|\), and moving both points onto \(x=k\) makes it exactly \(0\) — that is the minimum.
31M
Since \(ST\) is vertical, its perpendicular bisector is horizontal, and it passes through the midpoint of \(ST\): \[ y = \frac{(k^2+5)+(-k^2-3)}{2} = \frac{2}{2} = 1. \] The circumcentre \(Q\) lies on this horizontal line and on the perpendicular bisector of \(OS\).
Hint: The circumcentre is the intersection of two perpendicular bisectors; use the horizontal one first.
41A
Solving with the perpendicular bisector of \(OS\) gives \[ Q = \left(\frac{k\left(k^2+5\right)}{2},\;1\right). \] Wait — carefully: equating distances \(QO=QS\) with \(Q=(x,1)\): \[ x^2+1 = (x-k)^2 + \bigl(k^2+4\bigr)^2 \] \[ x^2+1 = x^2-2kx+k^2 + \bigl(k^2+4\bigr)^2 \] \[ 2kx = k^2 + \bigl(k^2+4\bigr)^2 - 1 \] In particular the \(y\)-coordinate of \(Q\) is exactly \(1\), and \(1 \ne 0\).
Hint: We only need the \(y\)-coordinate! It is fixed at \(1\), which is never \(0\), regardless of \(k\).
Conclusion: The circumcentre of \(\triangle OST\) always has \(y\)-coordinate \(1 \neq 0\), so it does not lie on the \(x\)-axis. The claim is incorrect.

★ Answers at a Glance

  • (a) \(\Delta = -8k^2-40 < 0\) for all real \(k\)  ⟹  the graph of \(y=f(x)\) does not cut the \(x\)-axis.
  • (b) \(f(x)=2(x-k)^2+k^2+5\), so the vertex is \(\bigl(k,\;k^2+5\bigr)\).
  • (c) Nearest points: \(S\bigl(k,k^2+5\bigr)\) and \(T\bigl(k,-k^2-3\bigr)\); \(ST\) is vertical, so the perpendicular bisector of \(ST\) is the horizontal line \(y=1\).
  • (c) The circumcentre of \(\triangle OST\) has \(y\)-coordinate \(1\neq 0\), hence it does not lie on the \(x\)-axis — the claim is incorrect.
