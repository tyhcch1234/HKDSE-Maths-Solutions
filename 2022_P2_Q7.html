Solve the compound inequality

The solution of $$3y-5 < 5y+1 \le 11$$ is

A.   $-2 \le y < 3$ B.   $-3 \le y < 2$ C.   $-2 < y \le 3$ D.   $-3 < y \le 2$
1 Split the double inequality
$$3y-5 < 5y+1 \le 11$$ means both conditions must hold at the same time: $$3y-5 < 5y+1 \quad \text{and} \quad 5y+1 \le 11$$
A “$A < B \le C$” statement is short for “$A < B$ and $B \le C$”.
Double Inequality \(A \(3y-5<5y+1\le11\) splits into \(3y-5<5y+1\) and \(5y+1\le11\).
2 Solve the left inequality
$$3y-5 < 5y+1$$ Subtract $3y$ from both sides: $$-5 < 2y+1$$ Subtract $1$ from both sides: $$-6 < 2y$$ Divide by $2$ (positive, so the inequality sign is unchanged): $$-3 < y$$
This gives the lower bound: $y$ must be strictly greater than $-3$.
Dividing an Inequality by a Positive Number \(a0)\) Dividing \(-6<2y\) by \(2\) leaves the sign unchanged, giving \(-3
3 Solve the right inequality
$$5y+1 \le 11$$ Subtract $1$ from both sides: $$5y \le 10$$ Divide by $5$: $$y \le 2$$
This gives the upper bound: $y$ may be at most $2$.
4 Combine the two conditions
$$y > -3 \quad \text{and} \quad y \le 2$$ Overlap of the two sets: $$-3 < y \le 2$$
Both conditions must be satisfied simultaneously — take the intersection of the two solution sets. Note the open end at $-3$ and the solid end at $2$.
Answer:   $\boxed{-3 < y \le 2}$   →   Option D
