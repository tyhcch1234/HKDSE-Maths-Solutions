Question 16. In the figure, ABC is a straight line. If $AB = 24\text{ cm}$, $AD = 40\text{ cm}$, $BD = 32\text{ cm}$ and $CD = 68\text{ cm}$, then $BC =$

A. 43 cm
B. 54 cm
C. 55 cm
D. 60 cm
STEP 1 · Read the figure
$A$, $B$, $C$ lie on a straight line, so $BC = AC - AB$.
Two triangles share the vertex $D$: $\triangle ABD$ (sides $24,32,40$) and $\triangle BCD$ (sides $BD=32$, $CD=68$).
Hint: Notice $24,32,40$ are all multiples of $8$: $3,4,5$. That is a Pythagorean triple!
STEP 2 · Test for a right angle in △ABD
Converse of Pythagoras Theorem \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \angle C=90^{\circ}\) \(24^{2}+32^{2}=1600=40^{2}\), so the angle at \(B\) between \(AB\) and \(BD\) is a right angle.
$$AB^2+BD^2 = 24^2+32^2 = 576+1024 = 1600$$ $$AD^2 = 40^2 = 1600$$ Since $AB^2+BD^2=AD^2$, $$\therefore \angle ABD = 90^\circ \quad (\text{converse of Pythagoras' theorem})$$
Key idea: the right angle is at $B$, between $AB$ and $BD$ — not at $A$ or $D$.
STEP 3 · Apply Pythagoras in △BCD
Pythagoras Theorem \(c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle BCD\) the hypotenuse is \(CD\), so \(BC^{2}=CD^{2}-BD^{2}\).
Angles on a Straight Line \(\angle ABD+\angle DBC=180^{\circ}\) \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) lie on a straight line, so \(\angle DBC=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\).
Since $ABC$ is a straight line, $\angle DBC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.
So $\triangle BCD$ is right-angled at $B$: $$BC^2 + BD^2 = CD^2$$ $$BC^2 = CD^2 - BD^2 = 68^2-32^2$$
STEP 4 · Evaluate BC
$$BC = \sqrt{68^2-32^2} = \sqrt{4624-1024} = \sqrt{3600} = 60\text{ cm}$$
AnswerBC = 60 cm  →  Option D

Question. In the figure, \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) lie on a straight line. \(AB=18\text{ cm}\), \(BD=24\text{ cm}\), \(AD=30\text{ cm}\) and \(CD=40\text{ cm}\). Find \(BC\). A. \(24\text{ cm}\) B. \(28\text{ cm}\) C. \(32\text{ cm}\) D. \(36\text{ cm}\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. In \(\triangle ABD\): \(AB^{2}+BD^{2}=18^{2}+24^{2}=324+576=900\) and \(AD^{2}=30^{2}=900\).
Step 2. Since \(AB^{2}+BD^{2}=AD^{2}\), by the converse of the Pythagoras theorem \(\angle ABD=90^{\circ}\).
Step 3. As \(ABC\) is a straight line, \(\angle DBC=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\), so \(\triangle BCD\) is right-angled at \(B\).
Step 4. \(BC^{2}=CD^{2}-BD^{2}=40^{2}-24^{2}=1600-576=1024\), hence \(BC=32\text{ cm}\).
Answer: C. \(BC=32\text{ cm}\)
Exam tip: Whenever you get three sides, always square them and check for a Pythagorean triple — spotting $24:32:40 = 3:4:5$ immediately gives the $90^\circ$ angle at $B$.
