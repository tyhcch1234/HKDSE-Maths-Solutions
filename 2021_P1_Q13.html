HKDSE 2021 · Paper 1 · Q13

Topic 12 · Coordinate Geometry

The equation of the circle $C$ is $$x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-16y-69=0 .$$ Let $G$ be the centre of $C$. Denote the origin by $O$.

(a) Find $OG$. [2 marks]

(b) Does $O$ lie inside $C$? Explain your answer. [1 mark]

(c) Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that $OP=GP$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$. Suppose that $\Gamma$ cuts $C$ at the points $M$ and $N$. Find the area of the quadrilateral $OMGN$. [4 marks]

STEP 1 · FIND THE CENTRE G

Complete the square for the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-16y-69=0$:

$$(x-6)^{2}+(y-8)^{2}=69+36+64=169 .$$

Hence the centre is

$$G=(6,\;8),\qquad r=\sqrt{169}=13 .$$
STEP 2 · (a) DISTANCE OG
$OG=\sqrt{(6-0)^{2}+(8-0)^{2}}=\sqrt{36+64}$
$=\sqrt{100}=10 .$
(a) $\;OG=10$
STEP 3 · (b) IS O INSIDE C ?

The radius of $C$ is $13$. Since $$OG=10<13=\text{radius},$$ the origin $O$ lies inside $C$.

(b) Yes — $O$ lies inside $C$ because $OG=10<13$.
Hint: a point is inside a circle exactly when its distance from the centre is less than the radius.
STEP 4 · (c) THE LOCUS Γ

$OP=GP$ means $P$ is equidistant from $O$ and $G$, so $\Gamma$ is the perpendicular bisector of $OG$.

By symmetry, $\Gamma$ meets $C$ at $M$ and $N$ with $GM=GN=13$ (radii), and it passes through the mid-point $Q$ of $OG$:

$$GQ=\tfrac{1}{2}OG=\tfrac{1}{2}(10)=5 .$$
STEP 5 · (c) HALF-CHORD MQ

Since $\Gamma\perp OG$ at $Q$, we have $\angle GQM=90^{\circ}$. By Pythagoras,

$$MQ=\sqrt{GM^{2}-GQ^{2}}=\sqrt{13^{2}-5^{2}}=\sqrt{144}=12 .$$

So $MQ=NQ=12$.

STEP 6 · (c) RHOMBUS AND AREA

Because $O,M$ lie on $\Gamma$, $OM=GM$; because $O,N$ lie on $\Gamma$, $ON=GN$. Also $GM=GN$ (radii). Hence $$OM=MG=GN=NO,$$ so $OMGN$ is a rhombus with perpendicular diagonals $OG$ and $MN$.

Area $=4\times\dfrac{1}{2}(GQ)(MQ)=4\times\dfrac{1}{2}(5)(12)$

$$=4\times 30 = 120 .$$
(c) Area of $OMGN = 120$ square units.
