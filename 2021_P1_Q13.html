QUESTION Full Question

The equation of the circle \(C\) is \[ x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-16y-69=0. \] Let \(G\) be the centre of \(C\). Denote the origin by \(O\).

(a) Find \(OG\). 2 marks

(b) Does \(O\) lie inside \(C\)? Explain your answer. 1 mark

(c) Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(OP=GP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at the points \(M\) and \(N\). Find the area of the quadrilateral \(OMGN\). 4 marks

PART (a) Finding \(OG\)

Centre \(G\) = (6, 8) \(OG\) = 10 Radius \(r\) = 13
Step 11M

Complete the square (or use the formula) to find the centre \(G\) of the circle.

\[ x^{2}-12x + y^{2}-16y = 69 \] \[ (x-6)^{2}-36 + (y-8)^{2}-64 = 69 \] \[ (x-6)^{2} + (y-8)^{2} = 169 \]
Hint: The centre is \((6,8)\) because \((x-6)^2+(y-8)^2=r^2\) has centre \((6,8)\).
Step 21A

Distance from the origin \(O(0,0)\) to \(G(6,8)\):

\[ OG = \sqrt{(6-0)^{2}+(8-0)^{2}} = \sqrt{36+64} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \]
Hint: Apply the distance formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).
Answer: \(OG = 10\)
0 / 2

PART (b) Is \(O\) inside \(C\)?

\(OG\) = 10 Radius = 13 Since \(OG < r\), O is inside C
Step 11A

The radius of \(C\):

\[ r = \sqrt{6^2+8^2+69} = \sqrt{36+64+69} = \sqrt{169} = 13 \]

From part (a), \(OG = 10\).

Since \(OG = 10 < 13 = r\), the origin \(O\) lies inside the circle \(C\).

Hint: A point is inside a circle if its distance from the centre is less than the radius.
Answer: Yes, \(O\) lies inside \(C\) because \(OG < r\).

PART (c) Area of Quadrilateral \(OMGN\)

\(GQ\) = 5 \(MQ\) = 12 \(GM\) = 13 Area = 120
Step 11M

The locus of \(P\) with \(OP=GP\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(OG\).

Since \(O = (0,0)\) and \(G = (6,8)\), the midpoint \(Q\) of \(OG\) is:

\[ Q = \left(\frac{0+6}{2}, \frac{0+8}{2}\right) = (3,4) \] \[ GQ = \frac{1}{2} OG = \frac{1}{2}(10) = 5 \]
Hint: The perpendicular bisector of \(OG\) is exactly the set of points equidistant from \(O\) and \(G\).
Step 21M

Since \(Q\) is the midpoint of chord \(MN\) and \(\angle GQM = 90^\circ\), triangle \(GQM\) is right-angled at \(Q\).

\[ GM = r = 13 \qquad GQ = 5 \] \[ MQ = \sqrt{GM^{2}-GQ^{2}} = \sqrt{13^{2}-5^{2}} = \sqrt{169-25} = \sqrt{144} = 12 \]
Hint: The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
Step 31M

Both \(O\) and \(G\) lie on the perpendicular bisector \(\Gamma\) of \(OG\), so \(OM=GM\) and \(ON=GN\). Also \(GM=GN\).

Hence \(OM = MG = GN = ON\), so the quadrilateral \(OMGN\) is a rhombus.

The area of a rhombus is \(\frac{1}{2} \times (\text{diagonal}_1) \times (\text{diagonal}_2)\). Diagonals: \(MN = 2 MQ = 24\), \(OG = 10\).

\[ \text{Area} = 4 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}(GQ)(MQ)\right) = 4 \times \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 12 = 120 \]
Hint: The rhombus splits into 4 congruent right-angled triangles with legs \(GQ=5\) and \(MQ=12\).
Answer: Area of \(OMGN = 120\) square units.

★ Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Centre \(G=(6,8)\) and \(OG = 10\)
  • (b) Radius \(r = 13\). Since \(OG = 10 < 13\), \(O\) lies inside \(C\).
  • (c) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(OG\), meeting \(C\) at \(M,N\). Quadrilateral \(OMGN\) is a rhombus with diagonals \(OG=10\) and \(MN=24\). Area \(=120\).
