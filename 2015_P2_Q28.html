The bar chart below shows the distribution of the numbers of keys owned by the students in a class. Find the probability that a randomly selected student from the class owns 3 keys.

💡 The bar for 3 keys has height 8 → 8 students own exactly 3 keys.

Add up all the frequencies (number of students) from every bar:

Total = \(12 + 16 + 8 + 4 + 4\)
Total = \(\mathbf{44}\) students

🔍 Hint: This total (44) will be the denominator in our probability fraction.

The question asks for the probability that a randomly selected student owns exactly 3 keys.

From the chart, the number of students who own 3 keys is the height of the third bar:

Favourable outcomes = \(\mathbf{8}\) students

For equally likely outcomes, the probability is:

\[ P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} \]

Substitute the values we found:

\[ P(\text{owns 3 keys}) = \frac{8}{44} \]

Divide numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor, which is 4:

\[ \frac{8}{44} = \frac{8 \div 4}{44 \div 4} = \frac{2}{11} \]

✅ This matches Option B.

Answer: B \(\frac{2}{11}\)

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Always read the height of the relevant bar for the numerator.
  • Add all bar heights to get the total number of students (denominator).
  • Simplify the resulting fraction to match the options.
  • Double-check: probability should be between 0 and 1.
