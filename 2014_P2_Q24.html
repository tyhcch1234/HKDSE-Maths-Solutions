Locus of a point equidistant from two parallel straight lines
Straight Lines Slope Parallel Lines Perpendicular Distance Locus

Question Full Question

[2014MC#24] The equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \[2x+3y=5 \quad\text{and}\quad 4x+6y=7\] respectively. If \(P\) is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_1\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_2\), then the locus of \(P\) is a

  • A. circle.
  • B. square.
  • C. parabola.
  • D. straight line.

· Answer: D

Part (a) Compare the slopes — decide the relation between \(L_1\) and \(L_2\)

Write each line in the form \(y=mx+c\) and read off the slopes \(m_1,\;m_2\).

5.0
slope of \(L_1\): −2/3
slope of \(L_2\): −2/3
\(m_1 = m_2\) ⇒ parallel
Step 1

Make \(y\) the subject of \(L_1\):

\[2x+3y=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 3y = -2x + 5 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\tfrac{2}{3}x + \tfrac{5}{3}\]
The coefficient of \(x\) when the equation is in the form \(y=mx+c\) is the slope.
slope of \(L_1 = -\dfrac{2}{3}\)
Step 2

Make \(y\) the subject of \(L_2\):

\[4x+6y=7 \;\Rightarrow\; 6y=-4x+7 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\tfrac{4}{6}x+\tfrac{7}{6}=-\tfrac{2}{3}x+\tfrac{7}{6}\]
Always simplify the fraction \(-\tfrac{4}{6}\) to compare with \(L_1\).
slope of \(L_2 = -\dfrac{2}{3}\)
Step 3

Since \(m_1 = m_2 = -\tfrac{2}{3}\) but the \(y\)-intercepts differ (\(\tfrac{5}{3}\neq\tfrac{7}{6}\)), the two lines are parallel and distinct.

Equal slopes + different intercepts ⇒ parallel lines never meet.
\(L_1 /\!/ L_2\) and \(L_1 \neq L_2\)

Part (b) Locus of \(P\) equidistant from \(L_1\) and \(L_2\)

difference: 0.000

The locus is the mid-line \(8x+12y=17\). Since \(L_1,L_2\) are parallel, every point on this mid-line is equally far from both lines.

Step 1

Use the perpendicular distance formula. For a line \(ax+by+c=0\), distance from \(P(x,y)\) is \(\dfrac{|ax+by+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\).

\[d(P,L_1)=\frac{|2x+3y-5|}{\sqrt{2^2+3^2}} = \frac{|2x+3y-5|}{\sqrt{13}}\] \[d(P,L_2)=\frac{|4x+6y-7|}{\sqrt{4^2+6^2}} = \frac{|4x+6y-7|}{\sqrt{52}} = \frac{|4x+6y-7|}{2\sqrt{13}}\]
Note \(\sqrt{52}=2\sqrt{13}\), so the denominators are related by a factor \(2\).
\(d(P,L_1)=\dfrac{|2x+3y-5|}{\sqrt{13}},\quad d(P,L_2)=\dfrac{|4x+6y-7|}{2\sqrt{13}}\)
Step 2

Set the two distances equal:

\[\frac{|2x+3y-5|}{\sqrt{13}} = \frac{|4x+6y-7|}{2\sqrt{13}}\] Multiplying both sides by \(2\sqrt{13}\): \[2|2x+3y-5| = |4x+6y-7|\]
Since \(4x+6y-7 = 2(2x+3y-5)+3\), the expression inside the modulus is never the same. Solve both the same-sign and opposite-sign cases.
Step 3

Case 1 (same sign): \(2(2x+3y-5) = 4x+6y-7\) gives \(4x+6y-10=4x+6y-7\), i.e. \(-10=-7\) — impossible, no solution.
Case 2 (opposite signs): \(2(2x+3y-5) = -(4x+6y-7)\):

\[4x+6y-10 = -4x-6y+7 \;\Rightarrow\; 8x+12y = 17\]
Dividing by 4: \(2x+3y=\tfrac{17}{4}\), a line parallel to both \(L_1\) and \(L_2\).
Locus: \(8x+12y=17\) — a straight line (answer D)
Step 4

Geometric interpretation (faster route): For two parallel lines, the set of points equidistant from both is the line halfway between them — a single straight line. Every other conic (circle, parabola, square) is ruled out because the two distances stay proportional.

"Locus of a point equidistant from two parallel lines is a straight line." Remember this as a standard result.
Answer: D — straight line

Part (c) Why not a circle, square or parabola?

Toggle each option to see the shape it would require. Only one of them can contain all points \(P\) with \(d(P,L_1)=d(P,L_2)\).

Only the straight line fits all equidistant points.
Step 11R

Reason it out by elimination:

  • Circle — the locus would need a fixed centre, but \(L_1,L_2\) have no single centre point; a perpendicular-distance equality to two parallel lines produces a line, not a circle.
  • Square — a square is a 2-D region, not a locus curve.
  • Parabola — a parabola is the locus equidistant from a point (focus) and a line (directrix), not between two lines.
  • Straight line — ✔ the mid-line \(8x+12y=17\) works.
Remember the three classic loci: fixed distance from a point → circle; to one line → parallel lines; to two parallel lines → the mid-line.
The locus is a straight line — D

Summary Final Answers

  • Slopes: \(m_1=m_2=-\dfrac{2}{3}\)  ⇒  \(L_1 /\!/ L_2\) (distinct)
  • Equidistant condition: \(\dfrac{|2x+3y-5|}{\sqrt{13}} = \dfrac{|4x+6y-7|}{2\sqrt{13}}\)
  • Locus: \(8x+12y=17\), i.e. \(2x+3y=\dfrac{17}{4}\)
  • Answer: D — straight line
  • Key fact: Locus of a point equidistant from two parallel lines is a straight line (the mid-line).
