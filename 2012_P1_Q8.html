Q8 The Question 5 marks

In Figure 1, \(AB\), \(BC\), \(CD\) and \(AD\) are chords of the circle. \(AC\) and \(BD\) intersect at \(E\). It is given that \(BE=8\text{ cm}\), \(CE=20\text{ cm}\) and \(DE=15\text{ cm}\).

(a) Write down a pair of similar triangles in Figure 1. Also find \(AE\). (3 marks)

(b) Suppose that \(AB=10\text{ cm}\). Are \(AC\) and \(BD\) perpendicular to each other? Explain your answer. (2 marks)

Original Figure

a Part (a) — Similar Triangles & Finding \(AE\) 3 marks

AE = 6.0
Step 11A

In the circle, the two triangles \(\triangle AEB\) and \(\triangle DEC\) share the vertex \(E\) where the chords \(AC\) and \(BD\) intersect. Examine their angles:

\[ \angle AEB = \angle DEC \quad (\text{vertically opposite angles}) \] \[ \angle ABE = \angle DCE \quad (\angle \text{ in the same segment, subtended by arc } AD) \]

Hence two pairs of equal angles are found, so \(\triangle AEB \sim \triangle DEC\).

Hint: Chords of the same circle subtend equal angles in the same segment — this is the standard way to spot similar triangles in an "X"-shaped chord crossing.
Step 21M

Since the triangles are similar, corresponding sides are proportional. Match the sides opposite equal angles:

\[ \frac{AE}{DE} = \frac{BE}{CE} \]

(Equivalently, \(AE \cdot CE = BE \cdot DE\), the intersecting chords theorem.)

Hint: Make sure corresponding vertices are matched: \(A \leftrightarrow D\), \(E \leftrightarrow E\), \(B \leftrightarrow C\).
Step 31A

Substitute the given lengths \(BE=8\), \(CE=20\), \(DE=15\):

\[ \frac{AE}{15} = \frac{8}{20} = \frac{2}{5} \] \[ AE = 15 \times \frac{2}{5} = 6 \text{ cm} \]
Answer: \(AE = 6\text{ cm}\)

Also accepted: \(\triangle AED \sim \triangle BEC\) with \(\dfrac{AE}{BE}=\dfrac{DE}{CE}\) gives the same result.

b Part (b) — Are \(AC\) and \(BD\) Perpendicular? 2 marks

AB = 10.0
Step 11M

From part (a), \(AE=6\text{ cm}\), \(BE=8\text{ cm}\), and we are now told \(AB=10\text{ cm}\). Check the converse of the Pythagorean theorem in \(\triangle AEB\):

\[ AE^2 + BE^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 \] \[ AB^2 = 10^2 = 100 \]

So \(AE^2 + BE^2 = AB^2\).

Hint: A \(6\text{-}8\text{-}10\) triangle is a scaled \(3\text{-}4\text{-}5\) right triangle — a classic signal that a right angle is hidden somewhere.
Step 21A

By the converse of the Pythagorean theorem, \(\angle AEB = 90^\circ\) (right angle). Since \(A\), \(E\), \(C\) are collinear and \(B\), \(E\), \(D\) are collinear, the angle between the lines \(AC\) and \(BD\) is \(90^\circ\).

Answer: Yes — \(AC\) and \(BD\) are perpendicular to each other.

Note: the fact that \(AB=10\) forces \(\angle AEB=90^\circ\); this is independent of the circle geometry beyond part (a).

✓ Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(\triangle AEB \sim \triangle DEC\) (also \(\triangle AED \sim \triangle BEC\)). Therefore \(AE = \mathbf{6\text{ cm}}\).
  • Part (b): \(AE^2+BE^2 = 6^2+8^2 = 100 = 10^2 = AB^2\), so \(\angle AEB = 90^\circ\) by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem. Hence \(AC \perp BD\).

Key idea: intersecting chords create similar triangles; the ratio of corresponding sides gives the missing chord segment. The Pythagorean converse then tests perpendicularity.
