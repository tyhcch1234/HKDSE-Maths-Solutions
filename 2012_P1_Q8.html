QUESTION[5 marks]
In Figure 1, $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $AD$ are chords of the circle. $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at $E$. It is given that $BE = 8\text{ cm}$, $CE = 20\text{ cm}$ and $DE = 15\text{ cm}$.
  1. (a) Write down a pair of similar triangles in Figure 1. Also find $AE$.
  2. (b) Suppose that $AB = 10\text{ cm}$. Are $AC$ and $BD$ perpendicular to each other? Explain your answer.
STEP 1 · RECOGNISE THE INTERSECTING-CHORD ANGLES

$AC$ and $BD$ are two chords of the circle meeting inside at $E$. Using the angle in the same segment (angles subtended by the same arc):

$$\angle AED = \angle BEC \quad(\text{vertically opposite angles})$$

$$\angle ADE = \angle BCE \quad(\angle \text{s in the same segment, arc } AB)$$

$$\angle DAE = \angle CBE \quad(\angle \text{s in the same segment, arc } CD)$$

Three equal angles ⇒ the two triangles are equiangular, hence similar.

STEP 2 · STATE THE SIMILAR TRIANGLES (Part a)

$$\triangle AED \sim \triangle BEC$$

Equivalently (with corresponding vertices matched on the other side of $E$): $$\triangle AEB \sim \triangle DEC$$

Either pair is accepted; write the vertices in the correct correspondence order.

ANSWER (a) – part 1

$\triangle AED \sim \triangle BEC$ (or $\triangle AEB \sim \triangle DEC$).

STEP 3 · FIND $AE$ USING RATIOS (Part a)

From the similarity, corresponding sides are in proportion:

$$\frac{AE}{BE} = \frac{DE}{CE}$$

Substitute $BE = 8$, $DE = 15$, $CE = 20$:

$$\frac{AE}{8} = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}$$

$$AE = 8 \times \frac{3}{4} = 6\text{ cm}$$

ANSWER (a) – part 2

$AE = 6\text{ cm}$

Check with the intersecting-chords theorem: $AE \cdot CE = BE \cdot DE \Rightarrow 6 \times 20 = 8 \times 15 = 120$ ✓

STEP 4 · TEST PERPENDICULARITY IN $\triangle AEB$ (Part b)

Given $AB = 10\text{ cm}$, and we know $AE = 6$, $BE = 8$.

Compute the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides:

$$AE^2 + BE^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10^2 = AB^2$$

By the converse of the Pythagorean theorem, $\triangle AEB$ is right-angled at $E$.

$$\angle AEB = 90^\circ$$

STEP 5 · CONCLUDE

Since $\angle AEB = 90^\circ$, the two chords $AC$ and $BD$ meet at right angles at $E$:

$$AC \perp BD$$

ANSWER (b)

Yes, $AC$ and $BD$ are perpendicular to each other.

Reason: $AE^2 + BE^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 100 = AB^2$, so $\angle AEB = 90^\circ$ (converse of Pythagoras' theorem).
