Coordinate Geometry — Orthocentre of a triangle and slopes of altitudes

Straight lines Orthocentre Altitudes & slopes Perpendicular lines y-intercept

Question MC · 5 options

It is given that \(a\) is a positive constant. The straight line \(2x+5y=a\) cuts the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis at the points \(P\) and \(Q\) respectively. Let \(R\) be a point lying on the \(y\)-axis such that the \(x\)-coordinate of the orthocentre of \(\Delta PQR\) is \(10\). Find the \(y\)-coordinate of \(R\).

A. \(-25\)    B. \(-4\)    C. \(4\)    D. \(25\)

Part (a) — Locate \(P\) and \(Q\); find the slope of \(PQ\)Setup

\(P=\left(\tfrac{a}{2},\,0\right)\) = (5, 0) \(Q=\left(0,\,\tfrac{a}{5}\right)\) = (0, 2) slope \(PQ\) = -0.4
Step 11M

Find the \(x\)-intercept \(P\) by putting \(y=0\):

\[ 2x+5(0)=a \;\Longrightarrow\; x=\frac{a}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P=\left(\frac{a}{2},\,0\right) \]
Use the slider above: as \(a\) changes, \(P\) slides along the \(x\)-axis but always stays on the positive side because \(a>0\).
Step 21M

Find the \(y\)-intercept \(Q\) by putting \(x=0\):

\[ 2(0)+5y=a \;\Longrightarrow\; y=\frac{a}{5} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q=\left(0,\,\frac{a}{5}\right) \]
Note both coordinates are positive since \(a>0\), so \(P\) and \(Q\) lie on the positive axes.
\(P=\left(\dfrac{a}{2},0\right)\), \(Q=\left(0,\dfrac{a}{5}\right)\)
Step 31A

Slope of \(PQ\) using the two intercepts:

\[ m_{PQ}=\frac{\tfrac{a}{5}-0}{0-\tfrac{a}{2}}=\frac{\;\tfrac{a}{5}\;}{-\tfrac{a}{2}} = -\frac{a}{5}\cdot\frac{2}{a}=-\frac{2}{5} \]
The \(a\) cancels — the slope of the line \(2x+5y=a\) is always \(-\tfrac{2}{5}\), independent of \(a\). Match this with the equation \(y=-\tfrac{2}{5}x+\tfrac{a}{5}\).
\(m_{PQ}=-\dfrac{2}{5}\)

Part (b) — Use the orthocentre condition to find the \(y\)-coordinate of \(R\)Key step

\(x\)-coordinate of orthocentre = 10.00 slope \(RH\) × slope \(PQ\) = -1.00 Target: \(y_R=-25\)
Step 11M

Let \(H\) be the orthocentre of \(\Delta PQR\). Since \(Q\) and \(R\) both lie on the \(y\)-axis, \(QR\) is a vertical line.

\[ QR:\; x=0 \]

The altitude from \(P\) must be perpendicular to \(QR\), so it is a horizontal line through \(P\):

\[ \text{altitude from }P:\; y=0 \]
Toggle “Show altitudes” in the diagram: the altitude from \(P\) always stays horizontal because \(QR\) is vertical.
\(H\) lies on \(y=0\), i.e. the \(y\)-coordinate of \(H\) is \(0\).
Step 21M

Let \(r\) be the \(y\)-coordinate of \(R\), so \(R=(0,r)\). Given the \(x\)-coordinate of \(H\) is \(10\), we have

\[ H=(10,\,0) \]

Now \(H\) also lies on the altitude from \(R\), which is perpendicular to \(PQ\):

\[ m_{RH}\times m_{PQ}=-1 \]
This is the orthocentre condition: each vertex–orthocentre segment is an altitude, hence perpendicular to the opposite side.
\(H=(10,\,0)\)
Step 31M

Compute the slope of \(RH\) from \(R=(0,r)\) to \(H=(10,0)\):

\[ m_{RH}=\frac{0-r}{10-0}=-\frac{r}{10} \]

We know \(m_{PQ}=-\dfrac{2}{5}\). Apply the perpendicular condition:

\[ \left(-\frac{r}{10}\right)\left(-\frac{2}{5}\right)=-1 \]
Watch the signs: the product of the two negative slopes must equal \(-1\). This is where sign errors usually happen.
Step 41A

Solve for \(r\):

\[ \frac{2r}{50}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{r}{25}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; r=-25 \]
Use the slider in the diagram to set \(y_R=-25\) and check that the altitude from \(R\) really passes through \(H=(10,0)\) and is perpendicular to \(PQ\).
\(y\)-coordinate of \(R\) = \(-25\)  →  Option A

Summary of Answers

  • \(P=\left(\dfrac{a}{2},\,0\right)\) and \(Q=\left(0,\,\dfrac{a}{5}\right)\).
  • Slope of \(PQ\): \(\;m_{PQ}=-\dfrac{2}{5}\).
  • Since \(QR\) is vertical, the altitude from \(P\) is horizontal, so the orthocentre is \(H=(10,\,0)\).
  • Altitude condition: \(\left(-\dfrac{r}{10}\right)\left(-\dfrac{2}{5}\right)=-1\;\Rightarrow\; r=-25\).
  • Final answer: \(y\)-coordinate of \(R=-25\) — Option A.
