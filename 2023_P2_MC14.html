HKDSE Math 2023 Paper 2 Q14

Recurrence Relation: Finding a Term without Listing Every Term

Formulas & Sequences Recurrence Relation Substitution MC Answer

Question

Let \(a_n\) be the \(n\)th term of a sequence. It is given that

\[ a_6 = 23,\qquad a_8 = 60,\qquad a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}\ \text{ for any positive integer } n. \]

Find \(a_3\).

A. 4
B. 5
C. 9
D. 14
Key idea: The relation \(a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}\) can be rearranged. Instead of moving forwards (which needs a starting point), we move backwards from the two known terms \(a_6\) and \(a_8\) to reach \(a_3\).

See the Relation at Work

Rearranging the given relation gives the backwards formula:

\[ a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}\ \Longrightarrow\ a_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1} \]

So the term two places before equals the difference of the next two terms. Drag the slider to pick a starting index \(n\) and watch the substitution happen:

Note: \(a_7\) is not given — it is the bridge value we must find first.

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Tap a step to open it. Try to predict each number before you look!

1Rearranging the Relation▶

We are given the relation for any positive integer \(n\):

\[ a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1} \]

To go backwards (from bigger indices to smaller ones), make \(a_n\) the subject:

\[ a_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1} \]
Hint: This is the same relation, just rearranged. It lets us find an earlier term using two later terms — perfect, because \(a_6\) and \(a_8\) are given.
2Find the Missing Bridge Value \(a_7\)▶

We know \(a_6=23\) and \(a_8=60\), but we do not know \(a_7\). Put \(n=6\) into the relation:

\[ a_{6+2}=a_6+a_{6+1}\ \Rightarrow\ a_8=a_6+a_7 \]

Substitute the known values:

\[ 60=23+a_7\ \Rightarrow\ a_7=60-23=37 \]
Hint: The value \(37\) is not in the answer options — it is an intermediate step that unlocks the rest of the working.
3Step Back to \(a_5\)▶

Now use the backwards formula with \(n=5\):

\[ a_5=a_{7}-a_{6} \]
\[ a_5=37-23=14 \]
Hint: Check: does \(a_5+a_6=a_7\)? Yes — \(14+23=37\). \(\checkmark\)
4Step Back to \(a_4\)▶

Use the backwards formula with \(n=4\):

\[ a_4=a_{6}-a_{5} \]
\[ a_4=23-14=9 \]
Hint: Notice the pattern — each new term is the difference of the two terms on its right.
5Reach the Target \(a_3\)▶

Use the backwards formula with \(n=3\):

\[ a_3=a_{5}-a_{4} \]
\[ a_3=14-9=5 \]
Hint: Check: \(a_3+a_4=5+9=14=a_5\). \(\checkmark\)
6Conclusion & Answer Choice▶

We have found \(a_3=5\). The sequence so far is:

\[ a_3=5,\ a_4=9,\ a_5=14,\ a_6=23,\ a_7=37,\ a_8=60 \]

Indeed, working forwards: \(5+9=14\), \(9+14=23\), \(14+23=37\), \(23+37=60\). All consistent.

Correct Option
B. 5
