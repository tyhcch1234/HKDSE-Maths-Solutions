Solve the compound inequality

\[ 3y - 5 < 5y + 1 \le 11 \]
A. \(-2 \le y < 3\)
B. \(-2 < y \le 3\)
C. \(-3 < y \le 2\)
D. \(-3 \le y < 2\)

🧩 Step-by-step solution

The chain \(3y-5 < 5y+1 \le 11\) means two inequalities joined by “and”:

\[ 3y-5 < 5y+1 \quad \text{and} \quad 5y+1 \le 11 \]

Hint: “\(a < b \le c\)” is shorthand for “\(a < b\) and \(b \le c\)”.

\(3y - 5 < 5y + 1\)

Subtract \(5y\) from both sides: \(3y - 5 - 5y < 1 \Rightarrow -2y - 5 < 1\)

Add \(5\) to both sides: \(-2y < 6\)

Divide by \(-2\) (flip sign): \(y > -3\)

Hint: Dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

\(5y + 1 \le 11\)

Subtract \(1\): \(5y \le 10\)

Divide by \(5\) (positive, so sign stays): \(y \le 2\)

Hint: No sign flip because we divided by \(+5\).

From steps 2 & 3:

\(y > -3\) and \(y \le 2\)

\[ -3 < y \le 2 \]

Hint: “and” means intersection → the solution must satisfy both inequalities.

The solution is \(\boxed{-3 < y \le 2}\).

This matches option C.
