QQuestion

Independent Events Complement Rule "At least one" Multiplication Rule

There are three questions in a test. The probabilities that a student answers the first question correctly, the second question correctly and the third question correctly are $0.6$, $0.7$ and $0.8$ respectively. Find the probability that the student answers at least 1 question correctly in this test.

1Step-by-Step Solution

1Understand what is given ▼

Let us name the events clearly:

$$P(\text{Q1 correct}) = 0.6,\quad P(\text{Q2 correct}) = 0.7,\quad P(\text{Q3 correct}) = 0.8$$

The three questions are answered independently of one another, so the result on one question does not affect the others.

Hint — Complement: The probability of an event not happening is $1 - P(\text{event})$.
2Write down the complement probabilities ▼

Working out a "not correct" (wrong) probability for each question:

$$P(\text{Q1 wrong}) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$$ $$P(\text{Q2 wrong}) = 1 - 0.7 = 0.3$$ $$P(\text{Q3 wrong}) = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2$$
Hint — Formula used: $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$, where $A'$ is the complement of event $A$.
3Use the "at least one" trick ▼

"At least 1 correct" covers many cases (exactly 1, exactly 2, all 3 correct). Counting them all is long.

Instead, consider the opposite event: "all wrong", i.e. 0 questions correct.

$$P(\text{at least 1 correct}) = 1 - P(\text{all wrong})$$
Hint — Why valid? "At least 1" and "none" are complementary events. Together they fill the whole sample space.
4Multiply the wrong probabilities ▼

Since the questions are independent, multiply the three "wrong" probabilities:

$$P(\text{all wrong}) = (1-0.6)(1-0.7)(1-0.8)$$ $$= 0.4 \times 0.3 \times 0.2$$ $$= 0.024$$
Hint — Multiplication Rule: For independent events $A$, $B$, $C$: $P(A \cap B \cap C) = P(A)\cdot P(B)\cdot P(C)$.
5Subtract from 1 to get the answer ▼

Now substitute back into the complement formula:

$$P(\text{at least 1 correct}) = 1 - 0.024 = 0.976$$

Therefore the required probability is $0.976$.

Common trap: Choice A ($0.024$) is the probability of all wrong, not "at least one correct". Always double-check which event the question actually asks for!
