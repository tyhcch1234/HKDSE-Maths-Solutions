Q9

Let \( f(x) = x^{13} - 2x + k \), where \( k \) is a constant. If \( f(x) \) is divisible by \( x + 1 \), find the remainder when \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - 1 \).

1 Visualise the Remainder Theorem

The Remainder Theorem says: when a polynomial \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder equals \(f(a)\).

2 Step-by-Step Solution

Since \(x + 1\) is a factor of \(f(x)\), by the Factor Theorem:

\(f(-1) = 0\)

💡 Hint: The Factor Theorem states that \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) if and only if \(f(a) = 0\). Here \((x + 1) = (x - (-1))\), so \(a = -1\).

Substitute \(x = -1\) into \(f(x) = x^{13} - 2x + k\):

\((-1)^{13} - 2(-1) + k = 0\)

💡 Hint: \((-1)^{13} = -1\) because the exponent is odd. So: \(-1 + 2 + k = 0\).

Simplify the equation:

\(-1 + 2 + k = 0\)

\(1 + k = 0\)

\(k = -1\)

When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x - 1\), the remainder is \(f(1)\).

With \(k = -1\), we have \(f(x) = x^{13} - 2x - 1\).

\(f(1) = (1)^{13} - 2(1) - 1\)

💡 Hint: The Remainder Theorem: when \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(f(a)\). Here \(a = 1\).

\(f(1) = 1 - 2 - 1\)

\(f(1) = -2\)

Therefore, the remainder is \(-2\).

Answer: D. \(-2\)

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Factor Theorem: \((x - a)\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) \(\iff\) \(f(a) = 0\).
  • Remainder Theorem: When \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x - a)\), the remainder is \(f(a)\).
  • Don't waste time expanding \(x^{13}\)! Always use substitution.
  • Remember: \((-1)^{\text{odd}} = -1\) and \((1)^{\text{any}} = 1\).
