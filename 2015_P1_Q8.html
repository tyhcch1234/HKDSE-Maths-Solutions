Q8 The Question 5 marks

In Figure 1, \(ABCD\) is a circle. \(E\) is a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(BC = CE\). It is given that \(AB = AD\), \(\angle ADB = 58^\circ\) and \(\angle CBD = 25^\circ\).

Find \(\angle BDC\) and \(\angle ABE\).

Part (a) Finding \(\angle BDC\) 1M + 1A

Use the equal sides \(AB = AD\) and the cyclic quadrilateral property to find \(\angle BDC\).

\(\angle ABD = \) 58°
\(\angle ADB = \) 58°
\(\angle CBD = \) 25°
\(\angle BDC = \) 39°
Step 11M

Since \(AB = AD\), triangle \(ABD\) is isosceles, so its base angles are equal:

\[ \angle ABD = \angle ADB = 58^\circ \]
Hint: Equal sides \(\Rightarrow\) equal angles. These are the angles opposite the equal sides.
Step 21M

For the cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\), opposite angles are supplementary:

\[ \angle ABC + \angle ADC = 180^\circ \] \[ (58^\circ + 25^\circ) + (58^\circ + \angle BDC) = 180^\circ \]
Hint: Split \(\angle ABC = \angle ABD + \angle DBC\) and \(\angle ADC = \angle ADB + \angle BDC\).
Step 31A

Simplify and solve for \(\angle BDC\):

\[ 141^\circ + \angle BDC = 180^\circ \] \[ \angle BDC = 39^\circ \]
Answer: \(\angle BDC = 39^\circ\)

Part (b) Finding \(\angle ABE\) 1M + 1M + 1A

Use \(BC = CE\) (isosceles triangle \(BCE\)) and the angles-in-the-same-segment theorem.

\(\angle BCE = \) 58°
\(\angle BEC = \) 61°
\(\angle BAC = \angle BDC = \) 39°
\(\angle ABE = \) 22°
Step 11M

Angles in the same segment subtended by chord \(BC\) are equal, so:

\[ \angle BCE = \angle BDC = 58^\circ \]
Hint: Wait — actually \(\angle BCE\) is an angle at \(C\) inside triangle \(BCE\), and by the cyclic property \(\angle BCA = \angle BDA = 58^\circ\). Since \(E\) lies on \(AC\), \(\angle BCE = \angle BCA = 58^\circ\).
Step 21M

Since \(BC = CE\), triangle \(BCE\) is isosceles with vertex at \(C\). So:

\[ \angle BEC = \frac{180^\circ - \angle BCE}{2} = \frac{180^\circ - 58^\circ}{2} = 61^\circ \]
Hint: The two base angles \(\angle CBE\) and \(\angle BEC\) are equal.
Step 31M

Angles in the same segment subtended by chord \(BC\) from the circle give \(\angle BAC = \angle BDC = 39^\circ\).

Now in the figure, \(\angle ABE\) is the angle at \(B\) between \(BA\) and \(BE\). Since \(E\) lies on \(AC\), we use the exterior angle of triangle \(ABE\):

\[ \angle BEC = \angle ABE + \angle BAE \] \[ 61^\circ = \angle ABE + 39^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle BAE = \angle BAC = 39^\circ\) because \(E\) lies on line \(AC\).
Step 41A

Therefore:

\[ \angle ABE = 61^\circ - 39^\circ = 22^\circ \]
Answer: \(\angle ABE = 22^\circ\)
0 / 4

✓ Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle ABD = \angle ADB = 58^\circ\) — base angles of isosceles triangle \(ABD\).
  • \(\angle ABC + \angle ADC = 180^\circ\) — opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\).
  • \(\angle BDC = 39^\circ\) final
  • \(\angle BCE = 58^\circ\) — angle in the same segment (or cyclic property).
  • Triangle \(BCE\) is isosceles with \(BC = CE\), giving \(\angle BEC = 61^\circ\).
  • \(\angle ABE = 61^\circ - 39^\circ = 22^\circ\) final
