Formulas and Sequences

The 1st term and the 38th term of an arithmetic sequence are $666$ and $555$ respectively. Find
  • (a) the common difference of the sequence, [2 marks]
  • (b) the greatest value of $n$ such that the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence is positive. [3 marks]
Step 1Set up the general term
General Term of an Arithmetic Sequence \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\) Here \\(a=666\\) and \\(T(38)=555\\), so \\(555=666+37d\\).

Let $d$ be the common difference and $T(n)$ the $n$-th term. For an arithmetic sequence, $$T(n)=a+(n-1)d .$$ Here the 1st term is $a=666$, and we are told the 38th term is $555$.

Hint: write down a = 666 and T(38) = 555 before touching any algebra.
Step 2Part (a): find the common difference $d$

Substitute $n=38$ into the general term:

$$555 = 666 + (38-1)d$$ $$555 = 666 + 37d$$ $$37d = 555-666 = -111$$ $$d = \frac{-111}{37} = -3$$

So the sequence decreases by $3$ each step: $666,\,663,\,660,\dots$

Hint: the negative common difference means the terms shrink — the figure shows the first few terms with $666$ as the tallest bar.
Step 3Part (b): write $S_n$, the sum of the first $n$ terms
Sum of the First n Terms of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl[2a+(n-1)d\bigr]\) With \\(a=666\\) and \\(d=-3\\), \\(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}(1335-3n)\\).

Using $S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\big[2a+(n-1)d\big]$ with $a=666$ and $d=-3$:

$$S_n=\frac{n}{2}\Big[2(666)+(n-1)(-3)\Big]$$ $$S_n=\frac{n}{2}\big[1332-3n+3\big]=\frac{n}{2}\big(1335-3n\big)$$
Hint: keep the $\tfrac{n}{2}$ factor outside; the bracketed part carries all the $n$-dependence.
Step 4Part (b): impose the condition $S_n>0$

We need the sum of the first $n$ terms to be positive:

$$\frac{n}{2}\big(1335-3n\big) > 0$$

Since $n$ is a positive integer, $n>0$, so we may divide both sides by $\dfrac{n}{2}$:

$$1335-3n > 0$$ $$3(445-n) > 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n < 445$$
Hint: dividing by a positive quantity keeps the direction of the inequality unchanged.
Step 5Solve the quadratic inequality precisely

Expanding first then factoring gives the same result and avoids any cancellation worry:

$$\frac{n}{2}(1335-3n)>0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 1335n-3n^{2}>0$$ $$3n(445-n)>0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; n(n-445)<0$$

The critical values are $n=0$ and $n=445$, and a parabola opening upward is below the axis between its roots, so

$$0 < n < 445$$
Hint: $S_n$ is a downward parabola in $n$; it is positive only before it cuts the axis at $n=445$.
Step 6Pick the greatest integer $n$
Integer Solutions of a Linear Inequality \(n The condition \\(n<445\\) leaves \\(n=444\\) as the largest possible number of terms.

Since $n$ must be a positive integer, the largest $n$ satisfying $n<445$ is

$$n = 444 .$$

Check: $S_{444}=\dfrac{444}{2}\big(1335-3(444)\big)=222(3)=666>0$  ✓
but $S_{445}=\dfrac{445}{2}\big(1335-1335\big)=0$, which is not positive.

Hint: $S_{445}=0$ is the boundary value — it fails the strict inequality "$>0$".
Answer (a) the common difference $d=-3$
(b) the greatest value of $n$ is $\;444$

Question. The 1st term of an arithmetic sequence is \\(500\\) and the 30th term is \\(384\\). (a) Find the common difference \\(d\\). (b) Express \\(S_n\\), the sum of the first \\(n\\) terms, in terms of \\(n\\). (c) Find the greatest value of \\(n\\) such that \\(S_n>0\\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \\(T(30)=500+29d=384\\), so \\(29d=-116\\) and \\(d=-4\\).
Step 2. (b) \\(S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl[2(500)+(n-1)(-4)\bigr]=\dfrac{n}{2}(1004-4n)=2n(251-n)\\).
Step 3. (c) \\(S_n>0\\) means \\(2n(251-n)>0\\); since \\(n>0\\), we need \\(251-n>0\\), i.e. \\(n<251\\).
Step 4. The greatest such integer is \\(n=250\\) (check: \\(S_{250}=500>0\\) but \\(S_{251}=0\\)).
Answer: (a) \\(d=-4\\) \quad (b) \\(S_n=2n(251-n)\\) \quad (c) \\(250\)
