HKDSE Mathematics — Paper 1

Q1 · Laws of Indices

Simplify and express with positive indices. [3 marks]

Question

$$\frac{(mn^{-2})^5}{m^{-4}}$$

Simplify and express your answer with positive indices.

Indices Power of Product Quotient Law Negative Indices

1 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1. Apply the power of a product law

−

Distribute the exponent 5 to both \(m\) and \(n\):

$$(mn^{-2})^5 = m^5 \cdot (n^{-2})^5$$
💡 Hint: \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\). Each factor inside the bracket gets the outside power.

Step 2. Apply the power of a power law

+

Multiply the inner and outer exponents:

$$(n^{-2})^5 = n^{-2 \times 5} = n^{-10}$$

So the numerator becomes:

$$m^5 n^{-10}$$
💡 Hint: \((a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}\). Multiply the powers.

Step 3. Rewrite the fraction

+

Substitute the simplified numerator over the denominator \(m^{-4}\):

$$\frac{m^5 n^{-10}}{m^{-4}}$$

Step 4. Simplify the \(m\) terms

+

Use the quotient law: subtract the exponent in the denominator from the exponent in the numerator.

$$\frac{m^5}{m^{-4}} = m^{5 - (-4)} = m^{5 + 4} = m^9$$
💡 Hint: \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\). Be careful with double negatives!

Step 5. Convert negative index to positive

+

The \(n\) term still has a negative exponent. Move it to the denominator to make it positive:

$$n^{-10} = \frac{1}{n^{10}}$$
💡 Hint: \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\). Positive indices only in the final answer.

Step 6. Final Answer 🎉

+

Combine all simplified parts:

$$\frac{m^9}{n^{10}}$$

All indices are now positive. ✅
