Trapezium ABCD Diagonals AC & DE Mid-point E Intersection F Shaded ΔCEF
Question. In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a trapezium with \(AD \parallel BC\) and \(AD:BC = 2:3\). Let \(E\) be the mid-point of \(BC\). \(AC\) and \(DE\) intersect at \(F\). If the area of \(\triangle CEF\) is \(36\ \text{cm}^2\), then the area of the trapezium \(ABCD\) is
A. 216 cm² B. 264 cm² C. 280 cm² D. 320 cm²
STEP 1 · Set up the lengths Since \(AD:BC = 2:3\), write \(AD = 4a\) so that \(BC = 6a\).
As \(E\) is the mid-point of \(BC\): \(BE = EC = 3a\).
STEP 2 · Prove similar triangles \(\triangle ADF\) and \(\triangle CEF\): \[\angle AFD = \angle CFE \ (\text{vert. opp. }\angle s)\] \[\angle ADF = \angle CEF \ (\text{alt. }\angle s,\ AD \parallel EC)\] \[\angle DAF = \angle ECF \ (\text{alt. }\angle s,\ AD \parallel EC)\] \(\therefore \triangle ADF \sim \triangle CEF\) (AAA).
STEP 3 · Ratio of corresponding sides \[\frac{DF}{EF}=\frac{AD}{CE}=\frac{4a}{3a}=\frac{4}{3}\quad(\text{corr. sides, }\sim\Delta s)\]
STEP 4 · Area of ΔCDE Since \(\triangle CDE\) and \(\triangle CEF\) share the same height from \(C\) to line \(DE\): \[\frac{[CDE]}{[CEF]}=\frac{DE}{EF}=\frac{EF+DF}{EF}=1+\frac{DF}{EF}=1+\frac{4}{3}=\frac{7}{3}\] \[\therefore [CDE]=36\times\frac{7}{3}=84\ \text{cm}^2\]
STEP 5 · Area of trapezium ABCD Let \(h\) be the distance between \(AD\) and \(BC\). Then \[\frac{[ABCD]}{[CDE]}=\frac{(AD+BC)\cdot h\div 2}{EC\cdot h\div 2}=\frac{4a+6a}{3a}=\frac{10}{3}\] \[\therefore [ABCD]=84\times\frac{10}{3}=280\ \text{cm}^2\]
Answer: C · 280 cm²
💡 Key ideas: similar triangles give the side ratio \(4:3\); equal heights turn a side ratio into an area ratio.
