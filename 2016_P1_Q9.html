(a) Find \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\).
(b) If a plant is randomly selected from the garden, find the probability that the height of the selected plant is less than 1.25 m but not less than 0.65 m.

Part (a) — Finding \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\) 3A

Each entry in a cumulative frequency table counts everything up to the upper class boundary of a class. Move the slider (or drag) to highlight a boundary on the bar chart and read how many plants lie to its left.

Boundary: 0.65 m Cumulative frequency: x Frequencies added: 2 + 4
1 / 4
11AFind \(x\)

The cumulative frequency at 0.65 m is the total number of plants with height less than 0.65 m — that is the first two classes (0.1–0.3 and 0.4–0.6).

\[ x = 2 + 4 = 6 \]
Hint: “Height less than 0.65 m” stops exactly at the upper boundary of the 0.4–0.6 class, so only those two classes are counted.
Answer: \(x = 6\)
21AFind \(y\)

The cumulative frequency at 1.25 m is everything below the 1.25 m boundary; the value 37 at 1.55 m already includes the 1.3–1.5 class (frequency 15).

\[ y = 37 - 15 = 22 \]
Hint: Work backwards from a known cumulative frequency. Subtracting the frequency of the class that sits immediately before the next boundary removes it.
Answer: \(y = 22\)
31AFind \(z\)

\(z\) is the cumulative frequency at the last boundary 1.85 m — i.e. the total number of plants. Add the last class on top of 37.

\[ z = 37 + 3 = 40 \]
Hint: The final cumulative frequency always equals the total frequency, so \(z\) is the number of plants in the garden.
Answer: \(z = 40\) (total number of plants)
4✔Check consistency

Verify with the frequency table. Since \(a+b+c+15+3=40\) and the cumulative counts give \(a=2,\;a+4=x=6\), we get \(b = 13 - 6 = 7\) and \(c = 22 - 13 = 9\). Then

\[ 2 + 4 + 7 + 9 + 15 + 3 = 40 \;\checkmark \]
Hint: Any two of the tables can be used to fill in the other — a quick check like this catches arithmetic slips.
All consistent: \(x=6,\; y=22,\; z=40\)

Part (b) — Required Probability 1M + 1A

Use the toggle to shade the plants with height less than 1.25 m, then switch to shading the excluded lower part. The wanted group is what remains.

\(<1.25\) m: 22 \(<0.65\) m: 6 Wanted: 16 plants
1 / 3
11MTranslate the event into counts

“Height less than 1.25 m but not less than 0.65 m” means \(0.65 \le h < 1.25\). From part (a):

\[ \#(h<1.25) = y = 22, \qquad \#(h<0.65) = x = 6 \]
Hint: “not less than 0.65” means we must remove the plants that are shorter than 0.65 m from the group below 1.25 m.
Wanted count \(= 22 - 6 = 16\) plants (Method mark)
21MSet up the probability

Total number of plants is \(z = 40\). The required probability is

\[ \text{P} = \frac{22 - 6}{40} = \frac{16}{40} \]
Hint: Probability \(=\) favourable outcomes \(\div\) total outcomes. The denominator is the total number of plants, not the cumulative figure at 1.25 m.
\(\text{P} = \dfrac{22-6}{40}\)
31ASimplify
\[ \frac{16}{40} = \frac{2}{5} \]
Hint: Always simplify to lowest terms — \(\frac{2}{5}\) is the expected answer form.
Answer: \(\dfrac{2}{5}\) (or \(0.4\))

Summary of Answers

  • (a)   \(x = 6\)  (from \(2+4\)),   \(y = 22\)  (from \(37-15\)),   \(z = 40\)  (from \(37+3\))
  • (b)   Required probability \(= \dfrac{22-6}{40} = \dfrac{2}{5}\)
  • Key idea: In a cumulative frequency table, the entry at a boundary equals the sum of all class frequencies below that boundary.
  • Key idea: The final cumulative frequency equals the total frequency (and the denominator in any probability).
