Polynomials Identities Comparing Coefficients

Question

If \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are non-zero constants such that

$$x(x+3a) + a \equiv x^{2} + 2(bx + c),$$

then \(a : b : c =\)

A. \(2:3:1\)
B. \(2:3:4\)
C. \(3:2:6\)
D. \(6:4:3\)

Step-by-step Solution

Step 0 / 4
1

Expand the left-hand side

Distribute \(x\) into the bracket \((x+3a)\):

$$x(x+3a) + a = x^{2} + 3ax + a$$

💡 Hint

\(x \cdot x = x^{2}\) and \(x \cdot 3a = 3ax\). The \(+a\) term stays unchanged since it's outside the bracket.

2

Expand the right-hand side

Distribute the factor \(2\) into \((bx + c)\):

$$x^{2} + 2(bx + c) = x^{2} + 2bx + 2c$$

💡 Hint

Multiply each term inside the bracket by \(2\): \(2 \cdot bx = 2bx\) and \(2 \cdot c = 2c\).

3

Match coefficients (Identity)

Since the two sides are identically equal for all values of \(x\), the coefficients of corresponding terms must match:

Term LHS coefficient RHS coefficient Equation
\(x^{2}\) \(1\) \(1\) ✓ matches
\(x\) \(3a\) \(2b\) \(3a = 2b\)
constant \(a\) \(2c\) \(a = 2c\)

💡 Hint

For an identity, the coefficients of like terms on both sides must be equal. Compare the \(x\) terms and the constant terms.

4

Solve for the ratio \(a:b:c\)

From the two equations:

\(3a = 2b\)  ⟹  \(b = \dfrac{3a}{2}\)

\(a = 2c\)  ⟹  \(c = \dfrac{a}{2}\)

Choose \(a = 2\) for easy numbers (since \(a\) is non-zero, the ratio is scale-independent):

$$a = 2,\quad b = \frac{3(2)}{2} = 3,\quad c = \frac{2}{2} = 1$$

✓ Answer

\(a : b : c = 2 : 3 : 1\)

Therefore the correct option is A.

Key Takeaway

When two polynomials are identically equal, the coefficients of corresponding powers of \(x\) must be equal. Here, comparing the \(x\)-terms gives \(3a = 2b\), and comparing the constant terms gives \(a = 2c\). Pick a convenient value for one variable (like \(a=2\)) and find the rest — the ratio is scale-independent.
