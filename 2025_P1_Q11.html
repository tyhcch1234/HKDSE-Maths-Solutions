QUESTION

[2025 #11] It is given that $p(x)$ partly varies as $x$ and partly varies as $x^2$. Suppose that $p(7)=56$ and $p(9)=216$.

(a) Find $p(x)$. (3 marks)

(b) Let $c$ be a real constant. Find the range of values of $c$ such that the equation $p(x)=c$ has two distinct real roots. (3 marks)

STEP 1
Partial Variation \(p(x)=ax+bx^2\) Here \(p\) is partly constant multiple of \(x\) and partly of \(x^2\), so two data pairs are needed.
Set up the partial variation.

Since $p(x)$ partly varies as $x$ and partly varies as $x^2$, we write

$p(x)=ax+bx^2$

where $a$ and $b$ are non-zero constants.

"Partly varies as" means a sum of two direct variations — one linear term, one quadratic term.
STEP 2
Solving Simultaneous Linear Equations \(\begin{cases} a+2b=8\\ a+5b=5\end{cases}\) Subtracting one equation from the other eliminates \(a\) and gives \(b\).
Substitute the two given data points.

$p(7)=56$:   $7a+49b=56$  …… (1)

$p(9)=216$:   $9a+81b=216$  …… (2)

Each data point gives one linear equation in the unknowns $a,\,b$.
STEP 3Solve the simultaneous equations.
Divide (1) by 7:   $a+7b=8$  …… (1′)
Divide (2) by 9:   $a+9b=24$  …… (2′)

$(2^\prime)-(1^\prime)$:   $2b=16 \Rightarrow b=8$.

Substitute into (1′):   $a+7(8)=8 \Rightarrow a=-48$.

Therefore $\;p(x)=-48x+8x^2$.

Dividing each equation by the coefficient of $a$ makes the elimination quick.
STEP 4
Discriminant Condition for Two Distinct Real Roots \(\Delta=b^2-4ac>0\) A quadratic equation has two distinct real roots exactly when its discriminant is positive.
Part (b) — Form the quadratic equation $p(x)=c$.

$p(x)=c \;\Rightarrow\; -48x+8x^2=c \;\Rightarrow\; 8x^2-48x-c=0$.

Rearrange into the standard form $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$ to apply the discriminant.
STEP 5Apply the discriminant condition.

For two distinct real roots, we need $\Delta>0$:

$(-48)^2-4(8)(-c)>0$

$2304+32c>0$

$32c>-2304$

$c>-72$

$\Delta>0$: two distinct real roots. (In Step 3, $C=-c$, so careful with the negative sign.)
(a)  $p(x)=-48x+8x^2$     (b)  $c>-72$
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. It is given that \(p(x)=ax+bx^2\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are non-zero constants. If \(p(2)=16\) and \(p(5)=25\),
(a) find \(a\) and \(b\), and
(b) find the range of values of \(c\) such that the equation \(p(x)=c\) has two distinct real roots.

Show the worked solution
1. \(p(2)=2a+4b=16\), so \(a+2b=8\).
2. \(p(5)=5a+25b=25\), so \(a+5b=5\).
3. Subtracting gives \(3b=-3\), so \(b=-1\) and \(a=10\). Thus \(p(x)=10x-x^2\).
4. \(10x-x^2=c\), i.e. \(x^2-10x+c=0\), with \(\Delta=100-4c\).
5. Two distinct real roots need \(100-4c>0\), so \(c<25\).
Answer: (a) \(a=10,\ b=-1\) (b) \(c<25\)
