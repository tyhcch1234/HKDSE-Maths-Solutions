Coordinate Geometry

Equation of a circle, distance between points, and the angle subtended by a chord — tested through three true/false statements.

Circle Equation Centre & Radius Distance Formula Angle in a Semicircle Multiple Choice

Question

The equation of the circle \(C\) is \[ 2x^2 + 2y^2 - 4x + 8y - 5 = 0 . \] The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((-1,\,2)\) and \((4,\,0)\) respectively.

Which of the following is/are true?

  1. The radius of \(C\) is \(5\).
  2. The mid-point of \(PQ\) lies outside \(C\).
  3. If \(G\) is the centre of \(C\), then \(\angle PGQ\) is an acute angle.

A. I only    B. II only    C. I and III only    D. II and III only

Statement I Check the radius

Statement I claims the radius of \(C\) is \(5\). Reduce the equation to standard form and read off the centre and radius.

5.0
Centre \(G = (1,-2)\) True radius \(=\sqrt{19}/2 \approx 2.179\) Loose radius \(= 5.0\)
0 / 3
1Make the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) equal to 1

Divide the whole equation by \(2\):

\[ x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y - \tfrac{5}{2} = 0 \]
Hint: A circle equation must look like \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\) before you can complete the square.
2Complete the square for \(x\) and \(y\)
\[ (x^2-2x) + (y^2+4y) = \tfrac{5}{2} \] \[ (x-1)^2 - 1 + (y+2)^2 - 4 = \tfrac{5}{2} \] \[ (x-1)^2 + (y+2)^2 = \tfrac{5}{2} + 5 = \tfrac{15}{2} \]
Hint: Take half of the coefficient of \(x\) (and of \(y\)), square it, and add to both sides.
3Read off centre and radius
\[ G = (1,\,-2), \qquad r = \sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}} = \tfrac{\sqrt{30}}{2} \approx 2.74 \neq 5 \]
Hint: The right-hand side equals \(r^2\), not \(r\). A common trap is \(r=\frac{15}{2}\).
Statement I is FALSE — the radius is \(\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{2}\approx 2.74\), not 5.

Statement II Mid-point of \(PQ\) vs circle \(C\)

Find \(M\), the mid-point of \(PQ\), and compare \(GM\) with the radius of \(C\).

4.0
\(M=(1.5,\,1)\) \(GM \approx 3.041\) \(r = \sqrt{30}/2 \approx 2.739\) Inside / Outside
0 / 3
1Mid-point formula
\[ M = \left(\frac{-1+4}{2},\ \frac{2+0}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{3}{2},\ 1\right) \]
Hint: Add the coordinates of the two endpoints, then halve each sum.
2Distance \(GM\)
\[ GM = \sqrt{\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-1\right)^2 + \left(1-(-2)\right)^2} = \sqrt{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}+9} = \frac{\sqrt{37}}{2} \] \[ GM = \frac{\sqrt{37}}{2} \approx 3.041 \]
Hint: Subtract the centre's coordinates from the point's coordinates — the order does not matter because both terms are squared.
3Compare with the radius
\[ r = \frac{\sqrt{30}}{2} \approx 2.739, \qquad GM = \frac{\sqrt{37}}{2} \approx 3.041 \] Since \(GM > r\), \(M\) lies outside \(C\).
Hint: Comparing \(\sqrt{37} > \sqrt{30}\) is enough — no need for decimals.
Statement II is TRUE — \(GM=\frac{\sqrt{37}}{2} > r=\frac{\sqrt{30}}{2}\), so \(M\) is outside \(C\).

Statement III Is \(\angle PGQ\) acute?

Draw the circle \(D\) with diameter \(PQ\). Its centre is \(M\) and its radius is \(\frac{1}{2}PQ\). Comparing \(GM\) with that radius decides the type of \(\angle PGQ\).

3.04
\(GM \approx 3.041\) \(r_D = \frac{1}{2}PQ \approx 2.693\) \(\angle PGQ \approx 96.1^\circ\) obtuse
0 / 3
1Consider the circle \(D\) with diameter \(PQ\)

Let \(D\) be the circle with diameter \(PQ\). Then \(M\) (the mid-point of \(PQ\)) is the centre of \(D\), and its radius is

\[ r_D = \frac{1}{2}PQ = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(4-(-1))^2+(0-2)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{25+4} = \frac{\sqrt{29}}{2} \approx 2.693 \]
Hint: Angle in a semicircle: a point on circle \(D\) sees \(PQ\) at exactly \(90^\circ\).
2Where does \(G\) lie relative to circle \(D\)?
\[ GM = \frac{\sqrt{37}}{2} \approx 3.041 \;>\; r_D = \frac{\sqrt{29}}{2} \approx 2.693 \] So \(G\) lies outside circle \(D\).
Hint: Compare \(\sqrt{37} > \sqrt{29}\) directly.
3Apply the angle-in-semicircle rule

When a point \(X\) and a fixed chord \(AB\) are given:

  • \(X\) on the circle with diameter \(AB\) \(\Rightarrow \angle AXB = 90^\circ\);
  • \(X\) inside that circle \(\Rightarrow \angle AXB\) is obtuse;
  • \(X\) outside that circle \(\Rightarrow \angle AXB\) is acute.

Here \(G\) is outside \(D\), so \(\angle PGQ\) is acute.

Hint: Move \(G\) with the slider — as \(G\) crosses circle \(D\), watch the marked angle switch between obtuse (inside) and acute (outside).
Statement III is TRUE — \(G\) is outside circle \(D\), hence \(\angle PGQ\) is acute.

Summary & Answer

  • Circle \(C\): \(\;2x^2+2y^2-4x+8y-5=0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=\dfrac{15}{2}\)
  • Centre of \(C\): \(\;G=(1,\,-2)\),   radius \(\;r=\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{2}\approx 2.74\)  → Statement I is false.
  • Mid-point of \(PQ\): \(\;M=\left(\dfrac{3}{2},\,1\right)\),   \(GM=\dfrac{\sqrt{37}}{2}\approx 3.04 > r\)  → \(M\) is outside \(C\): Statement II is true.
  • Circle \(D\) with diameter \(PQ\): radius \(r_D=\dfrac{\sqrt{29}}{2}\approx 2.69\). Since \(GM>r_D\), \(G\) is outside \(D\), so \(\angle PGQ\) is acute: Statement III is true.
Only II and III are true → the answer is D.
