Graph of $y=(h-x)(k-x)$, with $hk<0$

Parabola $y=(h-x)(k-x)$ $x$-intercepts ($h$ and $k$) $y$-intercept $hk<0$

HKDSE 2022 · Paper 2 · Q10

Let $h$ and $k$ be real constants such that $hk<0$. Which of the following statements about the graph of $y=(h-x)(k-x)$ are true?

I.  The graph opens upwards.
II. The graph has two $x$-intercepts.
III.The $y$-intercept of the graph is positive.

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

Expanding a Product of Brackets \((h-x)(k-x)=x^{2}-(h+k)x+hk\) The coefficients are \(a=1\), \(b=-(h+k)\), \(c=hk\), so the \(y\)-intercept is \(hk\).
1Expand into the standard form
$$y=(h-x)(k-x)=x^{2}-(h+k)x+hk$$ The coefficients are $a=1$, $b=-(h+k)$, $c=hk$.
Tip: multiply out the brackets so you can read off $a$, $b$, $c$.
2Statement I — direction of opening
The coefficient of $x^{2}$ is $a=1>0$,
so the parabola is concave upwards.
Statement I is TRUE ✓
A positive leading coefficient always means the graph opens upwards.
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) \(\Delta=(h+k)^{2}-4hk=(h-k)^{2}>0\) because \(hk<0\) makes \(h\neq k\): two \(x\)-intercepts.
3Statement II — number of $x$-intercepts
Studying $x^{2}-(h+k)x+hk=0$: $$\Delta=[-(h+k)]^{2}-4(1)(hk)=(h+k)^{2}-4hk$$ Key step: $(h+k)^{2}=(h-k)^{2}+4hk$,
so $$\Delta=(h-k)^{2}+4hk-4hk=(h-k)^{2}$$ Since $hk<0$ we get $h\neq k$, hence $\Delta=(h-k)^{2}>0$.
So there are two distinct real roots $\Rightarrow$ two $x$-intercepts.
Statement II is TRUE ✓
$\Delta>0 \Leftrightarrow$ the parabola cuts the $x$-axis twice.
4Statement III — sign of the $y$-intercept
At $x=0$: $y=hk$.
Given $hk<0$, the $y$-intercept is negative, not positive.
Statement III is FALSE ✗
Product of two numbers with opposite signs is always negative.
5Conclusion
Only statements I and II are true $\Rightarrow$ choose A.
Answer option matched: A.
Answer: A  (I and II only) ✔
