Trigonometry (59) · Sub-Topic: Trigonometric Identities

T13 Q39

Prove that \((\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^2-1=\sin2\theta\).

Solution

Expand the left-hand side: \[(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^2-1=\sin^2\theta+2\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta-1.\]
Use the identity $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$: \[\sin^2\theta+2\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta-1=1+2\sin\theta\cos\theta-1.\]
Simplify: \[1+2\sin\theta\cos\theta-1=2\sin\theta\cos\theta.\]
Apply the double-angle identity $\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta$: \[2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\sin2\theta.\]
Therefore, $(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^2-1=\sin2\theta$.
