HKDSE Math 2013 Paper 1 Q8

Estimation, Approximation & Errors — maximum absolute error in measurement

QQuestion Overview

Estimation & Approximation Maximum Absolute Error Rounding & Significant Figures Percentage / Bounds Reasoning

A pack of sea salt is termed regular if its weight is measured as 100 g correct to the nearest g.

(a) Find the least possible weight of a regular pack of sea salt.

(b) Is it possible that the total weight of 32 regular packs of sea salt is measured as 3.1 kg correct to the nearest 0.1 kg? Explain your answer.

[5 marks]

1Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click a step to expand it. Try to predict the next line before you reveal it!

1 Understand "measured as 100 g correct to the nearest g" ›

When we say a value is correct to the nearest g, the rounding unit is \(\,1\text{ g}\). The person measuring rounds the true weight to the closest whole gram.

So a pack is regular if its true weight \(w\) rounds to \(100\text{ g}\). That means \(w\) must lie on one side or the other of the two boundary values \(99.5\text{ g}\) and \(100.5\text{ g}\).

Hint The rounding unit here is \(1\text{ g}\) (nearest g). A value exactly half-way, like \(99.5\text{ g}\), is the boundary where the rounding flips from \(99\) to \(100\).
2 Find the maximum absolute error ›

The maximum absolute error is half of the rounding unit:

\[ \text{Max. absolute error} = \frac{1}{2}\times 1\text{ g} = 0.5\text{ g} \quad \text{(1M)} \]

This \(0.5\text{ g}\) is exactly how far the true weight may differ from the recorded value \(100\text{ g}\).

Hint — general formula For a measurement \(x\) correct to the nearest \(u\), the maximum absolute error \(= \dfrac{u}{2}\). Here \(u = 1\text{ g}\), so the error \(= 0.5\text{ g}\).
Regular pack weight \(w\) \[ 100 - 0.5 \le w < 100 + 0.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 99.5 \le w < 100.5 \quad (\text{in g}) \] Note: the lower bound \(99.5\) is included (it rounds up to \(100\)), while \(100.5\) is excluded.
3 Part (a): Least possible weight of one regular pack ›

The least possible weight is the lower bound of \(w\):

\[ \text{Least possible weight} = 100 - 0.5 = 99.5\text{ g} \quad \text{(1M)} \]

Answer (a): \(99.5\text{ g}\) 1A

Why subtract, not add? "Least possible" means the smallest true weight that still rounds to the recorded \(100\text{ g}\). That is the lower endpoint \(100-0.5\).
4 Part (b): Least possible total weight of 32 packs ›

To make the total as small as possible, take every pack at its own least possible weight, \(99.5\text{ g}\):

\[ (99.5)(32) = 3\,184\text{ g} \quad \text{(1M)} \]

Convert to kilograms: \[ 3\,184\text{ g} = 3.184\text{ kg} \quad \text{(1A)} \]

Hint \(1\text{ kg} = 1000\text{ g}\), so divide grams by \(1000\) to get kilograms. Each pack contributes at least \(99.5\text{ g}\), so \(32\) packs contribute at least \(32 \times 99.5\text{ g}\).
5 Part (b): Round \(3.184\text{ kg}\) to the nearest \(0.1\text{ kg}\) ›

The rounding unit for the total is \(0.1\text{ kg}\). Look at the second decimal place of \(3.184\):

\[ 3.184\text{ kg} \approx 3.2\text{ kg} \quad (\text{correct to the nearest } 0.1\text{ kg}) \]

Because \(3.184\) is closer to \(3.2\) than to \(3.1\) (the boundary is \(3.15\)).

Key comparison \[ 3.15 \;\text{(boundary)}\; <\; 3.184 \;<\; 3.25 \] So the least possible total rounds to \(3.2\text{ kg}\), never \(3.1\text{ kg}\).
6 Part (b): Conclusion — possible or impossible? ›

The least possible total of 32 regular packs is \(3.184\text{ kg}\), which already rounds to \(3.2\text{ kg}\) (correct to the nearest \(0.1\text{ kg}\)).

Since any real total is at least \(3.184\text{ kg}\), it can never be as small as \(3.1\text{ kg}\).

\[ \therefore \text{It is } \textbf{impossible} \text{ that the total is measured as } 3.1\text{ kg.} \quad \text{(1A)} \]
Reasoning tip To disprove a possibility, find a lower bound on the total and show that this lower bound is already too large to round down to \(3.1\text{ kg}\). The lower bound \(3.184\text{ kg}\) is greater than \(3.15\text{ kg}\).

4Key Takeaways & Exam Marks

  • Max absolute error \(=\frac{1}{2}\times\) (rounding unit). For nearest g, unit \(=1\text{ g}\), so error \(=0.5\text{ g}\).
  • Least possible value \(=\) recorded value \(- 0.5\text{ g} = 99.5\text{ g}\) 1M 1A
  • Least possible total: use \(99.5\text{ g}\) for every pack: \(32 \times 99.5 = 3\,184\text{ g} = 3.184\text{ kg}\) 1M 1A
  • \(3.184\text{ kg}\) rounds to \(3.2\text{ kg}\), not \(3.1\text{ kg}\), because \(3.184 > 3.15\). Thus the claim is impossible 1A
  • Strategy: to disprove a possibility, establish a bound and show the bound already contradicts the target.
