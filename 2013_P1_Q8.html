Regular pack (99.5 g – 100.5 g) 32 packs total 3.1 kg target range

Question 5 marks

A pack of sea salt is termed regular if its weight is measured as $100\text{ g}$ correct to the nearest g.

(a) Find the least possible weight of a regular pack of sea salt.

(b) Is it possible that the total weight of $32$ regular packs of sea salt is measured as $3.1\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$? Explain your answer.

Maximum Absolute Error \(\text{max. absolute error}=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\text{smallest division}\) The scale is graded in steps of \(1\ ext{g}\), so a \(100\ ext{g}\) pack is accurate only to \(0.5\ ext{g}\).
1Interpret “correct to the nearest g”

When a measured value is rounded to the nearest g, the measuring scale is graded in steps of $1\text{ g}$. The maximum absolute error is half of the smallest division:

$$\text{max. absolute error}=\tfrac{1}{2}\times 1\text{ g}=0.5\text{ g}$$

So a regular pack (measured as $100\text{ g}$) actually weighs between

$$100-0.5=99.5\text{ g}\quad\text{and}\quad 100+0.5=100.5\text{ g}.$$

Hint: “correct to the nearest g” always means half of $1\text{ g}$, i.e. $0.5\text{ g}$ on each side of $100\text{ g}$.
Lower Limit of a Measurement \(x_{\min}=x-\text{max. absolute error}\) The least possible weight of one pack is \(100-0.5=99.5\ ext{g}\).
2Least possible weight of one pack

The lower end of that range is the least possible weight:

$$\text{least possible weight}=100-0.5=99.5\text{ g}$$

Hint: for the least weight, subtract the maximum absolute error from the measured value.
Unit Conversion \(1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}\) The total \(99.5 imes32=3184\ ext{g}\) must be converted before it is rounded to the nearest \(0.1\ ext{kg}\).
3Least possible total weight of 32 packs

The smallest total occurs when every one of the $32$ packs is as light as possible, i.e. each is $99.5\text{ g}$:

$$ \text{least total}=99.5\times 32 = 3184\text{ g}$$

Converting to kilograms:

$$3184\text{ g}=3.184\text{ kg}$$

Hint: $1\text{ kg}=1000\text{ g}$, so divide the number of grams by $1000$.
4Round the least total to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$

The least possible total is $3.184\text{ kg}$. Rounding to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$:

$$3.184\text{ kg} \;\to\; 3.2\text{ kg}$$

Compare with the target reading of $3.1\text{ kg}$ (correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$), which would require the true total to lie in

$$[3.05\text{ kg},\;3.15\text{ kg}).$$

But even the lightest possible total, $3.184\text{ kg}$, is already greater than $3.15\text{ kg}$.

Key idea: the least possible total ($3.184\text{ kg}$) must round to $3.2\text{ kg}$, so every possible total of $32$ regular packs is at least $3.184\text{ kg}$ — never close to $3.1\text{ kg}$.
5Conclusion

$$\text{least possible total}=3.184\text{ kg}=3.2\text{ kg}\ (\text{corr. to the nearest }0.1\text{ kg})$$

Since the smallest conceivable total already rounds to $3.2\text{ kg}$, no arrangement of $32$ regular packs can have a total that is measured as $3.1\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$.

Thus it is impossible.

Hint: “Is it possible…?” → compare the extreme case (the least possible total) with the claimed value.

(a) The least possible weight of a regular pack of sea salt is $99.5\text{ g}$.

(b) No, it is impossible. The least possible total weight of $32$ regular packs is $99.5\times32=3184\text{ g}=3.184\text{ kg}$, which is $3.2\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$. Hence the total weight cannot be measured as $3.1\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$.

Answer: (a) $99.5\text{ g}$  ·  (b) Impossible (least total $=3.184\text{ kg}$, which rounds to $3.2\text{ kg}$, not $3.1\text{ kg}$).

PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. A bag of rice is marked \(250\ \text{g}\), correct to the nearest \(5\ \text{g}\). (a) Find the least possible weight of one bag. (b) 24 such bags are packed together. Is it possible that the total weight is \(5.8\ \text{kg}\), correct to the nearest \(0.1\ \text{kg}\)?

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The smallest division is \(5\ \text{g}\), so the maximum absolute error is \(\tfrac{1}{2}\times5=2.5\ \text{g}\).
Step 2. Least possible weight of one bag \(=250-2.5=247.5\ \text{g}\).
Step 3. Least possible total \(=247.5\times24=5940\ \text{g}=5.94\ \text{kg}\).
Step 4. \(5.8\ \text{kg}\) correct to the nearest \(0.1\ \text{kg}\) requires a total below \(5.85\ \text{kg}\), but even the lightest total is \(5.94\ \text{kg}\). So it is impossible.
Answer: (a) \(247.5\ \text{g}\) (b) No, it is impossible.
