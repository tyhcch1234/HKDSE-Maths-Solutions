Subtraction of Algebraic Fractions

Topic 4 · Polynomials  |  Multiple-choice (4 options)

Question

$$\frac{6}{n-6}-\frac{7}{n-7}=$$
Algebraic fractions Common denominator Expansion & simplification Sign trap: $(n-7)=-(7-n)$

Step-by-step Solution

0 / 5 steps revealed

The two fractions have different denominators:

$$(n-6)\quad\text{and}\quad(n-7)$$

They share no common factor, so the lowest common denominator (L.C.M. of denominators) is simply their product:

$$\text{L.C.D.}=(n-6)(n-7)$$
Hint · Fraction rule: $\dfrac{a}{b}-\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{a\cdot d-c\cdot b}{b\cdot d}$. Never subtract numerators directly — first rewrite each fraction with the common denominator.

Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by the missing factor:

$$\frac{6}{n-6}=\frac{6\,(n-7)}{(n-6)(n-7)}$$
$$\frac{7}{n-7}=\frac{7\,(n-6)}{(n-6)(n-7)}$$
Hint · Why multiply numerator too? $\dfrac{k}{k}=1$, so the fraction's value is unchanged — we only change its appearance. This is the "building up" step.
$$\frac{6}{n-6}-\frac{7}{n-7} =\frac{6(n-7)-7(n-6)}{(n-6)(n-7)}$$

Now expand each product carefully:

$$6(n-7)=6n-42$$
$$7(n-6)=7n-42$$
$$\Rightarrow\;\frac{(6n-42)-(7n-42)}{(n-6)(n-7)}$$
Hint · Error hotspot: the minus sign in front of $7(n-6)$ applies to the whole bracket. So $-(7n-42)=-7n+42$, not $-7n-42$.
$$(6n-42)-(7n-42)$$
$$=6n-42-7n+42$$
$$=-n$$

So the whole expression becomes:

$$\frac{-n}{(n-6)(n-7)}$$
Hint · Collect like terms: $6n-7n=-n$ and $-42+42=0$. The constant terms cancel completely — that is why no constant appears in the answer.

None of the options has $-n$ in the numerator, so place the minus sign on a factor of the denominator:

$$\frac{-n}{(n-6)(n-7)}=\frac{n}{(n-6)\cdot\big[-(n-7)\big]}=\frac{n}{(n-6)(7-n)}$$

Therefore the answer is B.

Hint · Sign rule: $-\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{-a}{b}=\dfrac{a}{-b}$. Since $-(n-7)=7-n$, we can "hide" the negative sign inside the second bracket. Watch out: option D $(n-6)(7-n)$ is only correct because it matches this bracket — option C's denominator $(n-6)(n-7)$ has the wrong sign pairing.

Take-away

$$\frac{6}{n-6}-\frac{7}{n-7}=\frac{n}{(n-6)(7-n)}$$
Answer: B
  • L.C.D. of $(n-6)$ and $(n-7)$ is $(n-6)(n-7)$.
  • Build up each fraction, then subtract the whole numerator in brackets.
  • Expand: $6n-42-7n+42=-n$ — constants cancel.
  • Move the minus into one denominator factor: $-n/(n-7)=n/(7-n)$.
