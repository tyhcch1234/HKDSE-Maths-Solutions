Coordinate Geometry (117) · Sub-Topic: Locus

T12 Q49

A point $P(x, y)$ moves such that its distance from the point $(3, 3)$ equals its distance from the point $(1, 1)$. Describe the locus of $P$.

Solution

Let $P(x,y)$ satisfy $\sqrt{(x-3)^2+(y-3)^2}=\sqrt{(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2}$.
Squaring both sides gives $(x-3)^2+(y-3)^2=(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2$.
Expanding: $x^2-6x+9+y^2-6y+9=x^2-2x+1+y^2-2y+1$.
Simplifying: $-6x-6y+18=-2x-2y+2$.
Rearranging gives $-4x-4y+16=0$, so $x+y=4$.
The locus is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining $(3,3)$ and $(1,1)$.
The locus of $P$ is the straight line $x+y=4$.
