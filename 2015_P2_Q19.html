HKDSE 2015 · Paper 2 · Q19

Trigonometry — Simplifying a Compound Expression

Unit-Circle Reference Diagram

Unit circle Angle θ (reference) 180° − θ 90° + θ 270° + θ 360° + θ

Question

[2015MC#19]
Evaluate $$ \frac{\cos 180^\circ}{1+\sin(90^\circ+\theta)} \;+\; \frac{\cos 360^\circ}{1+\sin(270^\circ+\theta)} \;=\; ? $$
A.  $0$
B.  $\cos\theta$
C.  $\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}$
D.  $\dfrac{2\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta}$

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 · Evaluate the constant cosines
Use the exact values on the unit circle: $$ \cos 180^\circ = -1, \qquad \cos 360^\circ = 1. $$
💡 These are the $x$-coordinates of the points at $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ on the unit circle.
Step 2 · Compound-angle identities
Compound Angle Formula \(\sin(90^{\circ}+\theta)=\cos\theta,\qquad \sin(270^{\circ}+\theta)=-\cos\theta\) Both numerators are sines of a quadrant angle plus \(\theta\), so each becomes a cosine.
$$ \sin(90^\circ+\theta) = \cos\theta, \qquad \sin(270^\circ+\theta) = -\cos\theta. $$
💡 "Change to co-function, keep sign of original quadrant." For $90^\circ+\theta$: sine→cosine, sign $+$. For $270^\circ+\theta$: sine→cosine, sign $-$. This is Step 2 in the model answer: $\dfrac{-1}{1+\cos\theta} + \dfrac{1}{1-\cos\theta}$.
Step 3 · Combine over a common denominator
Adding Algebraic Fractions \(\dfrac{a}{c}+\dfrac{b}{d}=\dfrac{ad+bc}{cd}\) The two fractions are combined over \((1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)\).
$$ \frac{-1}{1+\cos\theta} + \frac{1}{1-\cos\theta} = \frac{-(1-\cos\theta) + (1+\cos\theta)}{(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)} $$
💡 Multiply each fraction top and bottom to match the denominator $(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)$.
Step 4 · Simplify numerator and denominator
Pythagorean Identity \(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta=1\) The denominator \(1-\cos^{2}\theta\) becomes \(\sin^{2}\theta\).
Numerator: $-(1-\cos\theta)+(1+\cos\theta) = -1+\cos\theta+1+\cos\theta = 2\cos\theta$.
Denominator: $(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta) = 1-\cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta$. $$ \therefore \frac{2\cos\theta}{1-\cos^2\theta} = \frac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}. $$
💡 Use the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.
Step 5 · Choose the answer
Comparing with the options, the expression equals $$ \frac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}, $$ which is Option C.
FINAL ANSWER Option C  —  $\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}$

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{\cos(90^{\circ}+\theta)}{1+\sin\theta}+\dfrac{\cos(270^{\circ}+\theta)}{1-\sin\theta}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\cos(90^{\circ}+\theta)=-\sin\theta\) and \(\cos(270^{\circ}+\theta)=\sin\theta\).
Step 2. Common denominator \((1+\sin\theta)(1-\sin\theta)=1-\sin^{2}\theta=\cos^{2}\theta\).
Step 3. Numerator: \(-\sin\theta(1-\sin\theta)+\sin\theta(1+\sin\theta)=2\sin^{2}\theta\).
Step 4. Hence the expression is \(\dfrac{2\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta}=2\tan^{2}\theta\).
Answer: \(2\tan^{2}\theta\)
