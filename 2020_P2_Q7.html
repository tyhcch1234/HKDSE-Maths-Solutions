HKDSE 2020 Paper 2 — Q7 (Polynomials)

If $h$ and $k$ are constants such that \[ (x+h)(x+6)\equiv (x+4)^2+k, \] then $k=$

A. $-28$ B. $-16$ C. $-4$ D. $2$
1Read the given identity

The symbol “$\equiv$” means the two sides are equal for every value of $x$: \[ (x+h)(x+6)\;\equiv\;(x+4)^2+k . \]

A true identity holds for all $x$, so we may pick any convenient value of $x$.
2Method 1 — Substitute a convenient value

Because the identity holds for all $x$, choose $x=-6$, which makes the left-hand side $0$:

\[ \big[(-6)+h\big]\big[(-6)+6\big]=\big[(-6)+4\big]^2+k \] \[ 0\cdot(\,\cdot\,)=(-2)^2+k \] \[ 0=4+k \]

$x=-6$ zeroes out the factor $(x+6)$, killing the unknown $h$ immediately.
3Solve for $k$

\[ 0=4+k \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=-4 . \]

Answer matches option C.
4Method 2 — Compare coefficients

Expand both sides of the identity:

LHS: $(x+h)(x+6)=x^2+(h+6)x+6h$
RHS: $(x+4)^2+k=x^2+8x+(k+16)$

Equating the coefficients of $x$ and the constant terms:

\[ \begin{cases} h+6=8 &\quad\cdots(1)\\[2pt] 6h=k+16 &\quad\cdots(2) \end{cases} \]

Both sides are quadratic in $x$ with equal coefficients — this is the “compare coefficients” technique.
5Eliminate $h$

From $(1)$: $h=2$.
Substitute into $(2)$: $6(2)=k+16 \Rightarrow 12=k+16 \Rightarrow k=-4$.

(Equivalently, $(1)\times 6-(2)$: $\;36=32-k \Rightarrow k=-4$.)

Both methods give the same value, confirming $k=-4$.
✓Check

With $h=2$, LHS $=(x+2)(x+6)=x^2+8x+12$.
With $k=-4$, RHS $=(x+4)^2-4=x^2+8x+16-4=x^2+8x+12$. ✔

Answer: $k=-4$  (Option C)
