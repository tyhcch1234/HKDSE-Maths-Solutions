QUESTION

In Figure 2, the triangular paper card \(PQR\) is held such that \(PQ\) lies on the horizontal ground. It is given that \(PQ = 30\) cm, \(PR = 25\) cm and \(\angle QPR = 95^\circ\).

(a) Find [4 marks]

  1. the length of \(QR\),
  2. \(\angle PQR\).

(b) Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(QR\). A craftsman finds that the angle between \(PR\) and the horizontal ground is \(70^\circ\). The craftsman claims that the angle between \(PM\) and the horizontal ground exceeds \(40^\circ\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

Step 1 · Part (a)(i) — Cosine rule for \(QR\)
Cosine Formula \(c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos C\) In \(\triangle PQR\): \(QR^{2}=30^{2}+25^{2}-2(30)(25)\cos95^{\circ}\), giving \(QR\approx40.7\) cm.

Use the cosine rule in \(\triangle PQR\) with the two given sides and the included angle:

\[ QR^2 = PQ^2 + PR^2 - 2(PQ)(PR)\cos\angle QPR \]

\[ QR^2 = 30^2 + 25^2 - 2(30)(25)\cos 95^\circ \]

\[ QR^2 \approx 900 + 625 - 1500(-0.0871557) \approx 1655.734 \]

\[ QR \approx 40.69070673 \text{ cm} \approx \boxed{40.7 \text{ cm}} \]

Tip: keep the full calculator value \(40.6907\ldots\) for the later parts so rounding errors do not build up.
Step 2 · Part (a)(ii) — Sine rule for \(\angle PQR\)
Sine Formula \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}\) \(\dfrac{\sin\angle PQR}{PR}=\dfrac{\sin\angle QPR}{QR}\), and the obtuse solution is rejected because \(\angle PQR<85^{\circ}\).

Apply the sine rule:

\[ \frac{\sin\angle PQR}{PR} = \frac{\sin\angle QPR}{QR} \]

\[ \sin\angle PQR \approx \frac{25\sin 95^\circ}{40.69070673} \approx 0.612026375 \]

So \(\angle PQR \approx 37.73809375^\circ\) or \(\angle PQR \approx 142.2619063^\circ\).

But the angle sum of \(\triangle PQR\) is \(180^\circ\) and \(\angle QPR = 95^\circ\),
so \(\angle PQR < 85^\circ\). The \(142.26^\circ\) solution is rejected.

\[ \angle PQR \approx 37.73809375^\circ \approx \boxed{37.7^\circ} \]

Step 3 · Part (b) — Length of the median \(PM\)

\(M\) is the mid-point of \(QR\),
so \(QM = \dfrac{QR}{2} \approx 20.34535337\) cm.

Cosine rule in \(\triangle PQM\) with the included angle \(\angle PQM = \angle PQR \approx 37.73809375^\circ\):

\[ PM^2 = PQ^2 + QM^2 - 2(PQ)(QM)\cos\angle PQM \]

\[ PM^2 \approx 30^2 + 20.34535337^2 - 2(30)(20.34535337)\cos 37.73809375^\circ \]

\[ PM \approx 18.66993831 \text{ cm} \]

Step 4 · Part (b) — Vertical rise of \(M\) above the ground

Let \(D\) and \(N\) be the projections of \(R\) and \(M\) on the horizontal ground respectively.
Since \(M\) is the mid-point of \(QR\) and \(D,N\) lie in the same vertical plane, \(MN\) is the mid-segment in \(\triangle QRD\):

\[ MN = \tfrac{1}{2} RD \]

The angle between \(PR\) and the ground is \(70^\circ\),
so \(RD = PR\sin 70^\circ\). Hence

\[ MN = \tfrac{1}{2} PR\sin 70^\circ = \tfrac{1}{2}(25)\sin 70^\circ \approx 11.74615776 \text{ cm} \]

Step 5 · Part (b) — Angle between \(PM\) and the ground
Sine Ratio in a Right Triangle \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle PMN\) the angle between \(PM\) and the ground satisfies \(\sin\angle MPN=\dfrac{MN}{PM}\).

The angle between \(PM\) and the ground is \(\angle MPN\),
where \(PN\) is the projection of \(PM\) on the ground.
In the right-angled triangle \(PMN\) (right angle at \(N\)):

\[ \sin\angle MPN = \frac{MN}{PM} \approx \frac{11.74615776}{18.66993831} \approx 0.629141 \]

\[ \angle MPN \approx 38.98730493^\circ \]

Step 6 · Conclusion — Is the claim correct?

Since

\[ 38.98730493^\circ < 40^\circ \]

the angle between \(PM\) and the horizontal ground does not exceed \(40^\circ\).

Conclusion: The craftsman’s claim is not correct.
\(QR \approx 40.7\) cm, \(\angle PQR \approx 37.7^\circ\), and \(\angle MPN \approx 39.0^\circ < 40^\circ\).

Question. In the figure, the triangular card \(PQR\) is held so that \(PQ\) lies on horizontal ground. It is given that \(PQ=28\text{ cm}\), \(PR=24\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle QPR=100^{\circ}\).
(a) Find \(QR\) and \(\angle PQR\).
(b) Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(QR\). If the angle between \(PR\) and the ground is \(70^{\circ}\), is the angle between \(PM\) and the ground greater than \(40^{\circ}\)? Explain.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Cosine formula: \(QR^{2}=28^{2}+24^{2}-2(28)(24)\cos100^{\circ}=784+576+233.4\approx1593.4\), so \(QR\approx39.9\text{ cm}\).
Step 2. Sine formula: \(\sin\angle PQR=\dfrac{24\sin100^{\circ}}{39.917}\approx0.5921\), so \(\angle PQR\approx36.3^{\circ}\) (the obtuse value is impossible since \(\angle QPR=100^{\circ}\)).
Step 3. \(M\) is the mid-point of \(QR\), so \(QM\approx19.96\) cm.
Cosine formula in \(\triangle PQM\): \(PM^{2}=28^{2}+19.96^{2}-2(28)(19.96)\cos36.3^{\circ}\), giving \(PM\approx16.8\text{ cm}\).
Step 4. The vertical rise of \(M\) is half that of \(R\), so \(MN=\tfrac{1}{2}(24)\sin70^{\circ}\approx11.28\text{ cm}\).
Step 5. In the right-angled \(\triangle PMN\): \(\sin\angle MPN=\dfrac{11.28}{16.78}\approx0.672\), so \(\angle MPN\approx42.2^{\circ}\).
Step 6. Since \(42.2^{\circ}>40^{\circ}\), the angle between \(PM\) and the ground does exceed \(40^{\circ}\) and the claim is correct.
Answer: (a) \(QR\approx39.9\) cm, \(\angle PQR\approx36.3^{\circ}\); (b) yes, the angle is about \(42.2^{\circ}>40^{\circ}\)
