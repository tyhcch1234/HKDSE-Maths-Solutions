Coordinate Geometry — intercepts & slope of a straight line

Equation of a Straight Line x-intercept & y-intercept Slope Inequalities from a Diagram Multiple Choice

Question MC · 4 marks

In the figure, the equation of the straight line \(L\) is

\[ px+qy=7 \]

Which of the following is/are true?

  1. \(p>7\)
  2. \(q>7\)
  3. \(q>p\)
  • A. I only
  • B. II only
  • C. I and III only
  • D. II and III only

In the given figure, \(L\) cuts the positive \(y\)-axis at a point above \(1\), and cuts the positive \(x\)-axis at a point between \(0\) and \(1\).

Original Figure

Part 1 — Rearrange \(L\) and read off slope & intercepts Setup

Step 11M

Make \(y\) the subject of \(px+qy=7\):

\[ qy = -px+7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = -\frac{p}{q}\,x+\frac{7}{q} \]
Hint: Comparing with \(y=mx+c\), the slope is \(m=-\dfrac{p}{q}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \(c=\dfrac{7}{q}\).
Step 21M

Put \(y=0\) to find the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\):

\[ px = 7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \frac{7}{p} \]
Hint: Since \(L\) descends from left to right, the slope \(-\dfrac{p}{q}\) must be negative, so p and q have the same sign. The figure shows both intercepts positive, so \(p>0\) and \(q>0\).

Part 2 — Examine statement I: \(p>7\) Option A

Step 11M

From the figure, the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(1\):

\[ 0 < \frac{7}{p} < 1 \]
Hint: Because \(p>0\), multiplying the inequality by \(p\) does not reverse the sign.
Step 21A

Multiply through by \(p>0\):

\[ 0 < 7 < p \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p>7 \]
Statement I is TRUE. ✓
Hint: Slide \(p\) above to \(7\) or below. Only when \(p>7\) does the \(x\)-intercept \(7/p\) fall inside \((0,1)\), matching the given figure.

Part 3 — Examine statements II & III: \(q>7\) and \(q>p\) Options B, C, D

Step 11M

From the figure, the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\) is greater than \(1\):

\[ \frac{7}{q} > 1 \]
Hint: The point where \(L\) meets the \(y\)-axis is clearly above the mark \(1\) on the \(y\)-axis.
Step 21A

Since \(q>0\) (from the positive intercepts), multiply by \(q\):

\[ 7 > q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q < 7 \]
Statement II \((q>7)\) is FALSE. ✗
Hint: Slide \(q\) up to \(7\) and above — the \(y\)-intercept \(7/q\) drops to or below \(1\), contradicting the figure.
Step 31A

Combine the two results: \(q<7\) and \(7 \[ q < 7 < p \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q < p \]

Statement III \((q>p)\) is FALSE. ✗
Hint: It is impossible for \(q\) both to be less than \(7\) and greater than \(p\) (which is greater than \(7\)). Visually, a steeper line in the figure gives the same conclusion.

Summary of Answers

  • \(L:\; y=-\dfrac{p}{q}x+\dfrac{7}{q}\)  ⇒  slope \(=-\dfrac{p}{q}\), \(y\)-intercept \(=\dfrac{7}{q}\)
  • \(x\)-intercept of \(L\) is \(\dfrac{7}{p}\)
  • From \(0<\dfrac{7}{p}<1\):  \(p>7\) — Statement I is TRUE
  • From \(\dfrac{7}{q}>1\):  \(q<7\) — Statement II (\(q>7\)) is FALSE
  • Since \(q<7p\)) is FALSE
  • Only statement I is true
Answer: A — I only
