Coordinate Geometry — Intersection of a Circle and a Straight Line
Circle Equation Straight Line Quadratic Discriminant Range of k

Q Question

Find the range of values of \(k\) such that the circle \[ x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 2y - 7 = 0 \] and the straight line \[ 3x - 4y + k = 0 \] intersect.

A. \(\;-8 < k < 22\)
B. \(\;-8 \le k \le 22\)
C. \(\;k < -22\) or \(k > 8\)
D. \(\;k \le -22\) or \(k \ge 8\)

A Understanding the Setup (Circle & Line)

First rewrite the circle in centre–radius form, and see what varying \(k\) does to the line. The line \(3x - 4y + k = 0\) is a family of parallel lines — changing \(k\) slides the line but never changes its slope \(\tfrac{3}{4}\).

Step 1

Rewrite the circle by completing the square:

\[ x^2 + 2x + y^2 - 2y = 7 \] \[ (x+1)^2 - 1 + (y-1)^2 - 1 = 7 \] \[ (x+1)^2 + (y-1)^2 = 9 \]
The centre is \((-1,\,1)\) and the radius is \(\sqrt{9} = 3\).
Circle: centre \((-1,\,1)\), radius \(3\)
Step 2

The line \(3x - 4y + k = 0\) has constant slope \(\tfrac{3}{4}\). The distance from the centre \((-1,1)\) to this line is

\[ d = \frac{|3(-1) - 4(1) + k|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2}} = \frac{|k - 7|}{5}. \]
A line intersects a circle of radius \(r\) exactly when \(d \le r\). Here \(r = 3\), so we need \(\dfrac{|k-7|}{5} \le 3 \iff |k-7| \le 15\).
Condition: \(d \le 3\)
Step 3

Solve the inequality:

\[ |k - 7| \le 15 \] \[ -15 \le k - 7 \le 15 \] \[ -8 \le k \le 22 \]
This is exactly option B.
\(-8 \le k \le 22\)

B Algebraic Method — Substitution & Discriminant

The standard exam technique: substitute the line into the circle to get a quadratic in \(x\), then require a real solution (\(\Delta \ge 0\)).

Step 1

From the line, make \(y\) the subject:

\[ 3x - 4y + k = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = \frac{3x + k}{4} \]
This expression for \(y\) is then substituted into the circle equation.
Step 2

Substitute into \(x^2 + y^2 + 2x - 2y - 7 = 0\):

\[ x^2 + \left(\frac{3x+k}{4}\right)^2 + 2x - 2\left(\frac{3x+k}{4}\right) - 7 = 0 \]

Multiply throughout by 16 to clear the denominator:

\[ 16x^2 + (3x+k)^2 + 32x - 8(3x+k) - 112 = 0 \] \[ 16x^2 + (9x^2 + 6kx + k^2) + 32x - 24x - 8k - 112 = 0 \] \[ 25x^2 + (6k + 8)x + (k^2 - 8k - 112) = 0 \quad \cdots (*) \]
Collect like terms carefully: \(6kx + 32x - 24x = (6k+8)x\).
\(25x^2 + (6k+8)x + (k^2 - 8k - 112) = 0\)
Step 3

For intersection, \((*)\) must have a real root, i.e. its discriminant \(\Delta \ge 0\):

\[ \Delta = (6k+8)^2 - 4(25)(k^2 - 8k - 112) \ge 0 \] \[ (36k^2 + 96k + 64) - 100k^2 + 800k + 11200 \ge 0 \] \[ -64k^2 + 896k + 11264 \ge 0 \]

Divide by \(-64\) (reverse the inequality):

\[ k^2 - 14k - 176 \le 0 \]
\(896 \div (-64) = -14\) and \(11264 \div (-64) = -176\).
Step 4

Factorise the quadratic:

\[ k^2 - 14k - 176 = (k + 8)(k - 22) \le 0 \] \[ \therefore\; -8 \le k \le 22 \]
The roots are \(k = -8\) and \(k = 22\). Since the quadratic opens upwards, the solution of \(\le 0\) lies between the roots.
\(-8 \le k \le 22\)  (Answer B)

📋 Summary of Answers

  • Circle centre & radius: \((-1,\,1)\), \(r = 3\)
  • Line: \(3x - 4y + k = 0\) has slope \(\tfrac{3}{4}\) (a family of parallel lines)
  • Distance from centre to line: \(d = \dfrac{|k-7|}{5}\)
  • Intersection condition: \(d \le r \Rightarrow \dfrac{|k-7|}{5} \le 3\)
  • Range of \(k\): \(-8 \le k \le 22\)
  • Correct option: B
