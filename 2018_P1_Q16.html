Formulas & Sequences · Geometric Sequence

Geometric Sequence n-th term Logarithms Inequalities

The 3rd term and the 4th term of a geometric sequence are 720 and 864 respectively.

(a)  Find the 1st term of the sequence.

[2 marks]

(b)  Find the greatest value of $n$ such that the sum of the $(n+1)$-th term and the $(2n+1)$-th term is less than $5\times10^{14}$.

[3 marks]

Key Formula Reminder

n-th term of a G.S.

\( T_n = a\,r^{\,n-1} \)

$a$ = 1st term, $r$ = common ratio

Ratio between terms

\( r = \dfrac{T_{k+1}}{T_k} \)

Applies for every consecutive pair

a

Part (a) — Find the 1st term

Let the first term be $a$ and the common ratio be $r$.

Using \( T_n = a r^{\,n-1} \):

3rd term ($n=3$)

\( a r^{2} = 720 \)

4th term ($n=4$)

\( a r^{3} = 864 \)

Hint: The formula \(T_n = ar^{\,n-1}\) uses \(n-1\) because the 1st term has no multiplication by $r$. So the 3rd term is $ar^{2}$, not $ar^{3}$.

Divide the 4th-term equation by the 3rd-term equation:

\[ \frac{a r^{3}}{a r^{2}} = \frac{864}{720} \]

\[ r = \frac{864}{720} = 1.2 \]

Hint: Dividing cancels the unknown $a$, leaving only $r$. This is the standard trick when two consecutive terms are given.

Put $r = 1.2$ into $a r^{2} = 720$:

\[ a\,(1.2)^{2} = 720 \]

\[ a\,(1.44) = 720 \]

\[ a = \frac{720}{1.44} = 500 \]

✔ Answer (a): The 1st term of the sequence is 500.

b

Part (b) — Greatest value of $n$

We already know $a = 500$, $r = 1.2$.

$(n+1)$-th term

\( T_{n+1} = 500\,(1.2)^{\,n} \)

$(2n+1)$-th term

\( T_{2n+1} = 500\,(1.2)^{\,2n} \)

Hint: Apply \(T_k = ar^{\,k-1}\) carefully. For $k = n+1$ the exponent is $n$; for $k = 2n+1$ the exponent is $2n$.

The sum is less than $5\times10^{14}$:

\[ 500(1.2)^{n} + 500(1.2)^{2n} < 5\times10^{14} \]

Divide every term by $500$:

\[ (1.2)^{n} + (1.2)^{2n} < 10^{12} \]

Let $u = (1.2)^{n} > 0$. Then $(1.2)^{2n} = u^{2}$, giving:

\[ u^{2} + u - 10^{12} < 0 \]

Roots: \( u = \dfrac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4\times10^{12}}}{2} \)

Since $u > 0$, the valid range is:

\[ (1.2)^{n} < \frac{-1 + \sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2} \]

Hint: The negative root is rejected because $(1.2)^n$ is always positive. The discriminant $1+4\times10^{12}$ is positive, so two real roots exist.

Take logarithms (base 10) on both sides. Since $1.2 > 1$, the log is positive and the direction of the inequality is preserved:

\[ n \log 1.2 < \log\!\left( \frac{-1+\sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2} \right) \]

\[ n < \frac{\log\!\left( \frac{-1+\sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2} \right)}{\log 1.2} \]

Evaluating numerically gives:

\( n < 75.77551608 \)

$n$ is a positive integer, and $n < 75.77551608$.

The greatest such integer is $75$.

✔ Answer (b): The greatest value of $n$ is 75.
