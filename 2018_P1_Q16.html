HKDSE 2018 Paper 1 — Question 16

Topic 5 · Formulas and Sequences (Geometric Sequence)

The 3rd term and the 4th term of a geometric sequence are \(720\) and \(864\) respectively.

(a) Find the 1st term of the sequence. [2 marks]
(b) Find the greatest value of \(n\) such that the sum of the \((n+1)\)th term and the \((2n+1)\)th term is less than \(5\times10^{14}\). [3 marks]

Solution

1

Set up the general term and write down the given data 1M

General Term of a Geometric Sequence \(T_{k}=ar^{k-1}\) The third and fourth terms are \(ar^{2}=450\) and \(ar^{3}=675\).

Let \(a\) be the 1st term and \(r\) the common ratio.
Then

\[ T_k = ar^{\,k-1} \] \[ T_3 = ar^{2} = 720, \qquad T_4 = ar^{3} = 864 \]

Hint: write each given term with the formula \(ar^{k-1}\) before doing anything else.

2

Solve for \(a\) and \(r\) 1A

Divide the two equations to eliminate \(a\):

\[ \frac{ar^{3}}{ar^{2}} = \frac{864}{720} \;\Rightarrow\; r = \frac{864}{720} = 1.2 \]

Substitute \(r = 1.2\) back into \(ar^{2}=720\):

\[ a(1.2)^2 = 720 \;\Rightarrow\; a = \frac{720}{1.44} = 500 \]

Hint: dividing consecutive terms removes \(a\) instantly — a standard geometric-sequence trick.

3

(b) Form the inequality 1M

The \((n+1)\)th term and the \((2n+1)\)th term are

\[ T_{n+1} = 500(1.2)^{\,n}, \qquad T_{2n+1} = 500(1.2)^{\,2n} \]

Their sum must be less than \(5\times10^{14}\):

\[ 500(1.2)^n + 500(1.2)^{2n} < 5\times10^{14} \]

Divide throughout by \(500\):

\[ (1.2)^{2n} + (1.2)^{n} < 10^{12} \] \[ (1.2)^{2n} + (1.2)^{n} - 10^{12} < 0 \]

Hint: it is a quadratic inequality in the hidden variable \(x=(1.2)^n\).

4

Solve the quadratic inequality in \(x=(1.2)^n\) 1M

Quadratic Formula \(x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\) The inequality becomes \(x^{2}+x-5\times10^{6}<0\) with \(x=(1.5)^{n}\).

Let \(x = (1.2)^n > 0\). Then

\[ x^{2} + x - 10^{12} < 0 \]

Roots of \(x^{2}+x-10^{12}=0\):

\[ x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^{2} - 4(1)(-10^{12})}}{2(1)} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2} \]

Since \(x>0\),
only the positive root is admissible:

\[ 0 < (1.2)^{n} < \frac{-1 + \sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2} \]

Hint: \(x=(1.2)^n\) is always positive,
so discard the negative root.

5

Take logarithms and find the greatest integer \(n\) 1A

Laws of Logarithms \(\log a^{n}=n\log a\) Taking logarithms turns \((1.5)^{n}<2235.57\) into \(n<\dfrac{\log 2235.57}{\log 1.5}\).

Taking \(\log\) on both sides (base 10):

\[ \log(1.2)^n < \log\!\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2}\right) \] \[ n\log 1.2 < \log\!\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2}\right) \] \[ n < \frac{\log\!\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{4\times10^{12}+1}}{2}\right)}{\log 1.2} = 75.77551608\ldots \]

Since \(n\) must be a positive integer,

\[ n \le 75 \]

Hint: \(\log 1.2>0\),
so the inequality direction is unchanged;
then round down to the nearest integer.

(a) The 1st term is \(\;a = \mathbf{500}\;\) (and \(r = 1.2\)).

(b) \(n < 75.7755\ldots\) and \(n\) is an integer, so the greatest value of \(n\) is \(\;\mathbf{75}\).

Question. In a geometric sequence, the 3rd term is \(450\) and the 4th term is \(675\).
(a) Find the 1st term and the common ratio.
(b) Find the greatest positive integer \(n\) such that \(T_{n+1}+T_{2n+1}<10^{9}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(T_{3}=ar^{2}=450\) and \(T_{4}=ar^{3}=675\).
Step 2. (a) Dividing: \(r=\dfrac{675}{450}=1.5\);
then \(a(1.5)^{2}=450\) gives \(a=\dfrac{450}{2.25}=200\).
Step 3. (b) \(200(1.5)^{n}+200(1.5)^{2n}<10^{9}\), so with \(x=(1.5)^{n}>0\): \(x^{2}+x-5\times10^{6}<0\).
Step 4. (b) The positive root is \(x=\dfrac{-1+\sqrt{2\times10^{7}+1}}{2}\approx2235.57\), so \((1.5)^{n}<2235.57\).
Step 5. (b) Hence \(n<\dfrac{\log 2235.57}{\log 1.5}\approx19.02\), so the greatest integer is \(n=19\).
Answer: (a) \(a=200\), \(r=1.5\) (b) \(n=19\)
