Type 1

Rounding to Decimal Places and Significant Figures · 7 questions

Decimal places, significant figures, trailing zeros

Core idea: Rounding to \(n\) decimal places keeps \(n\) digits after the point. Rounding to \(n\) significant figures keeps \(n\) digits counted from the first non-zero digit — leading zeros are never significant.

Look only at the single digit immediately after the last digit kept: 5 or more rounds up, otherwise it stays. Trailing zeros kept for accuracy must be written.

\(7.8362\to 7.84\ (2\text{ d.p.})\)\(0.004\,728\to 0.004\,73\ (3\text{ s.f.})\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Round 7.8362 to (a) 2 decimal places, (b) 1 decimal place.

Answer(a) 7.84　(b) 7.8
  1. (a) Keep two digits after the point: 7.83. The next digit is 6, which is at least 5, so round up: 7.84.
  2. (b) Keep one digit after the point: 7.8. The next digit is 3, which is less than 5, so leave it: 7.8.
  3. Check: 7.8362 is closer to 7.84 (distance 0.0038) than to 7.83 (distance 0.0062).
Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Round 0.004 728 to 3 significant figures.

Answer0.004 73
  1. The leading zeros 0.00 are place-holders and are not significant; the first significant figure is 4.
  2. Count three significant figures: 4, 7, 2 — but the next digit is 8.
  3. Since 8 ≥ 5, round the 2 up to 3: 0.004 73.
  4. Check: 0.004 728 = \(4.728\times 10^{-3}\), and \(4.728\) to 3 s.f. is \(4.73\).
Common mistake

Counting the leading zeros as significant figures and answering 0.005. Leading zeros only show the size of the number.

Q3★★☆☆3 marks

Round 9.9963 to (a) 3 significant figures, (b) 2 significant figures.

Answer(a) 10.0　(b) 10
  1. (a) Three significant figures of 9.9963 are 9, 9, 9; the next digit is 6, so round up. \(9.99+0.01=10.00\), written to 3 s.f. as 10.0.
  2. (b) Two significant figures are 9, 9; the next digit is 9, so round up again: 10.
  3. Check: 9.9963 is within 0.0037 of 10, so 10.0 is a sensible 3 s.f. answer.
Common mistake

Writing 10.00 for 3 significant figures — the zero held for accuracy must stop at the third significant figure, giving 10.0.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

A stadium is reported to hold 34 567 spectators. Round this number to (a) 3 significant figures, (b) 4 significant figures.

Answer(a) 34 600　(b) 34 570
  1. (a) The first three significant figures of 34 567 are 3, 4, 5; the next digit is 6, which is at least 5, so round the 5 up to 6. All places to the right become zeros: 34 600.
  2. (b) Four significant figures are 3, 4, 5, 6; the next digit is 7, so round the 6 up to 7: 34 570.
  3. Check: 34 600 differs from 34 567 by 67, and 34 570 differs by 3, so each answer is as close as that accuracy allows.
Common mistake

Dropping the digits 5 and 6 entirely and answering 34 500 — after rounding, the empty places must be filled with zeros.

Q5★★☆☆3 marks

Round 0.030 456 to (a) 3 significant figures, (b) 4 significant figures.

Answer(a) 0.0305　(b) 0.030 46
  1. Leading zeros 0.0 are not significant, so the significant figures begin at 3: the digits are 3, 0, 4, 5, 6.
  2. (a) Three significant figures give 3, 0, 4; the next digit is 5, so round up: 0.0305.
  3. (b) Four significant figures give 3, 0, 4, 5; the next digit is 6, so round up: 0.030 46.
  4. Check: the answer still has the same magnitude (about 0.03); only the accuracy changed.
Q6★★★☆4 marks

Round 61.995 to (a) 2 decimal places, (b) 3 significant figures, (c) 2 significant figures.

Answer(a) 62.00　(b) 62.0　(c) 62
  1. (a) Two decimal places keep 61.99; the next digit is 5, so round up. \(61.99+0.01=62.00\), and the two zeros must be written.
  2. (b) Three significant figures keep 6, 1, 9; the next digit is 9, so round up: 62.0.
  3. (c) Two significant figures keep 6, 1; the next digit is 9, so round up: 62.
  4. Check: all three answers equal 62 to the accuracy asked; only the number of digits kept differs.
Common mistake

Writing 62 for part (a) — 62 is only 2 significant figures; 2 decimal places requires 62.00.

Q7★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 1 Q9 ↗

Take \(\pi=3.14159\ldots\).
(a) Write \(\pi\) correct to 4 significant figures.
(b) Using your answer in (a), estimate the circumference of a circle of radius 2.5 cm.
(c) The true circumference is \(15.707\,96\ldots\) cm. Find the absolute error of your estimate in (b).

Answer(a) 3.142　(b) 15.71 cm　(c) about 0.002 cm
  1. (a) The first four significant figures of 3.14159… are 3, 1, 4, 1; the next digit is 5, so round up: 3.142.
  2. (b) \(C=2\pi r\approx 2\times 3.142\times 2.5\).
  3. \(2\times 2.5=5\), so \(C\approx 5\times 3.142=15.71\) cm.
  4. (c) Absolute error \(=15.71-15.707\,96\ldots=0.002\,04\ldots\), i.e. about 0.002 cm.
  5. Check: the error is far smaller than the rounding unit 0.0005 multiplied by 5, namely 0.0025, so the estimate behaves as expected.

Type 2

Scientific Notation and Order of Magnitude · 7 questions

Standard form, multiplying and dividing powers of 10

Core idea: Scientific notation writes a number as \(a\times 10^{n}\) with \(1\le a<10\) and \(n\) an integer. The index \(n\) tells you how far the decimal point moved: right for large numbers, left for small ones.

When multiplying or dividing, handle the numbers and the powers of 10 separately, then adjust the answer back into standard form.

\(a\times 10^{n},\ 1\le a<10\)\(10^{m}\times 10^{n}=10^{m+n}\)\(\frac{10^{m}}{10^{n}}=10^{m-n}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

Write 45 600 in scientific notation.

Answer\(4.56\times 10^{4}\)
  1. Place the decimal point after the first non-zero digit: 4.56.
  2. The point moved 4 places to the left from 45 600, so the index is 4.
  3. Hence \(45\,600=4.56\times 10^{4}\).
  4. Check: \(4.56\times 10\,000=45\,600\).
Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

Write 0.000 307 in scientific notation.

Answer\(3.07\times 10^{-4}\)
  1. The first non-zero digit is 3, so write 3.07.
  2. To get from 3.07 back to 0.000 307 the point moves 4 places to the left, so the index is \(-4\).
  3. Hence \(0.000\,307=3.07\times 10^{-4}\).
  4. Check: \(3.07\times 10^{-4}=3.07\div 10\,000=0.000\,307\).
Common mistake

Writing \(30.7\times 10^{-5}\) — correct value but not standard form, because 30.7 is not less than 10.

Q10★★☆☆3 marks

Evaluate \((3.2\times 10^{5})\times(4.5\times 10^{-3})\), giving your answer in scientific notation.

Answer\(1.44\times 10^{3}\)
  1. Multiply the numbers: \(3.2\times 4.5=14.4\).
  2. Add the indices: \(10^{5}\times 10^{-3}=10^{5-3}=10^{2}\).
  3. So the product is \(14.4\times 10^{2}\).
  4. Convert to standard form: \(14.4\times 10^{2}=1.44\times 10^{3}\).
  5. Check: \(1.44\times 1000=1440\), and \(320\,000\times 0.0045=1440\).
Q11★★☆☆3 marks

Evaluate \(\frac{8.4\times 10^{-6}}{2.1\times 10^{-9}}\), giving your answer in scientific notation.

Answer\(4\times 10^{3}\)
  1. Divide the numbers: \(8.4\div 2.1=4\).
  2. Subtract the indices: \(10^{-6}\div 10^{-9}=10^{-6-(-9)}=10^{3}\).
  3. Hence the quotient is \(4\times 10^{3}\), i.e. 4000.
  4. Check: \(4\times 10^{3}\times 2.1\times 10^{-9}=8.4\times 10^{-6}\).
Common mistake

Adding the indices when dividing — division subtracts indices, so \(-6-(-9)=+3\), not \(-15\).

Q12★★☆☆3 marks

The population of a city is about \(5\times 10^{8}\) and that of a town is about \(2\times 10^{5}\). How many times as large as the town's population is the city's population?

Answer2500 times
  1. Required ratio \(=\frac{5\times 10^{8}}{2\times 10^{5}}\).
  2. Numbers: \(5\div 2=2.5\). Indices: \(10^{8-5}=10^{3}\).
  3. So the ratio is \(2.5\times 10^{3}=2500\).
  4. Check: \(2500\times 2\times 10^{5}=5000\times 10^{5}=5\times 10^{8}\), correct.
Q13★★★☆5 marks

(a) Write 0.000 000 482 in scientific notation.
(b) The diameter of a red blood cell is about \(7\times 10^{-6}\) m. How many such cells, placed in a row, would span a distance of \(3.5\times 10^{-2}\) m?

Answer(a) \(4.82\times 10^{-7}\)　(b) 5000 cells
  1. (a) The first non-zero digit is 4, and the point must move 7 places to reach 0.000 000 482, so the answer is \(4.82\times 10^{-7}\).
  2. (b) Number of cells \(=\frac{3.5\times 10^{-2}}{7\times 10^{-6}}\).
  3. Numbers: \(3.5\div 7=0.5\). Indices: \(10^{-2-(-6)}=10^{4}\).
  4. So the quotient is \(0.5\times 10^{4}=5\times 10^{3}=5000\).
  5. Check: \(5000\times 7\times 10^{-6}=35\,000\times 10^{-6}=3.5\times 10^{-2}\) m.
Q14★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 2 Q6 ↗

One light year is about \(9.46\times 10^{12}\) km. A distant star is \(4.2\times 10^{14}\) km from the Earth.
(a) How many light years away is the star? Give your answer in scientific notation, correct to 3 significant figures.
(b) Light travels at about \(3.0\times 10^{5}\) km/s. How many seconds does light from the star take to reach the Earth? Give your answer in scientific notation.

Answer(a) \(4.44\times 10^{1}\) light years　(b) \(1.4\times 10^{9}\) s
  1. (a) Number of light years \(=\frac{4.2\times 10^{14}}{9.46\times 10^{12}}\).
  2. Numbers: \(4.2\div 9.46=0.44397\ldots\); indices: \(10^{14-12}=10^{2}\).
  3. So the distance is \(0.44397\times 10^{2}=44.397\ldots\) light years, i.e. \(4.44\times 10^{1}\) (3 s.f.).
  4. (b) Time \(=\frac{4.2\times 10^{14}}{3.0\times 10^{5}}\) seconds.
  5. \(4.2\div 3.0=1.4\) and \(10^{14-5}=10^{9}\), so the time is \(1.4\times 10^{9}\) s.
  6. Check: \(1.4\times 10^{9}\) s is about \(1.4\times 10^{9}\div(3.15\times 10^{7})\approx 44\) years, agreeing with (a).
Common mistake

Answering (a) as \(4.44\times 10^{2}\) — the quotient \(0.44397\times 10^{2}\) must be re-written with one digit before the point.

Type 3

Estimation by Rounding to 1 Significant Figure · 7 questions

Rounding every number to 1 s.f. before calculating

Core idea: To estimate a calculation, round every number to 1 significant figure first, then compute. The answer is not meant to be exact — it is meant to be quick and to show the size of the result.

Estimation is also a check: if your exact answer is far from the estimate, you have probably made an arithmetic slip.

\(38\times 21\approx 40\times 20\)\(\frac{4.87\times 19.3}{0.52}\approx\frac{5\times 20}{0.5}\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

Estimate \(38\times 21\) by rounding each number to 1 significant figure.

Answerabout 800
  1. Round to 1 significant figure: \(38\to 40\) and \(21\to 20\).
  2. Multiply: \(40\times 20=800\).
  3. Check against the exact value \(38\times 21=798\), which is very close, so the estimate is reliable.
Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

Estimate \(587\div 19\) by rounding each number to 1 significant figure.

Answerabout 30
  1. Round: \(587\to 600\) and \(19\to 20\).
  2. Divide: \(600\div 20=30\).
  3. Check: the exact value is about 30.9, so the estimate is close.
Q17★★☆☆3 marks

Estimate the value of \(\frac{4.87\times 19.3}{0.52}\) by rounding each number to 1 significant figure.

Answerabout 200
  1. Round each number: \(4.87\to 5\), \(19.3\to 20\), \(0.52\to 0.5\).
  2. Numerator: \(5\times 20=100\).
  3. Divide: \(100\div 0.5=200\).
  4. Check: dividing by 0.5 doubles the numerator, so the estimate 200 is reasonable; the exact value is about 181.
Common mistake

Rounding 0.52 to 1 instead of 0.5 — for numbers below 1 the single significant figure is the first non-zero digit, here 5 in the tenths place.

Q18★★☆☆3 marks

Estimate the value of \(\frac{0.613\times 47.2}{8.9}\), giving your answer to 1 decimal place.

Answerabout 3.3
  1. Round: \(0.613\to 0.6\), \(47.2\to 50\), \(8.9\to 9\).
  2. Numerator: \(0.6\times 50=30\).
  3. Divide: \(30\div 9=3.333\ldots\), so the estimate is 3.3 (1 d.p.).
  4. Check: the exact value is about 3.25, close to the estimate.
Q19★★☆☆4 marks

Estimate the value of \(\frac{(2.98)^{2}\times 41}{0.198}\).

Answerabout 1800
  1. Round each number to 1 significant figure: \(2.98\to 3\), \(41\to 40\), \(0.198\to 0.2\).
  2. Square: \(3^{2}=9\).
  3. Numerator: \(9\times 40=360\).
  4. Divide: \(360\div 0.2=1800\).
  5. Check: dividing by 0.2 multiplies by 5, and \(360\times 5=1800\). The exact value is \(\frac{8.8804\times 41}{0.198}\approx 1839\), so the estimate is within about 2\%.
Q20★★★☆4 marks

A water tank is a cuboid measuring 1.98 m by 0.51 m by 1.02 m.
(a) Estimate its volume by rounding each dimension to 1 significant figure.
(b) The exact volume is about 1.03 m³. Comment on your estimate.

Answer(a) about 1 m³　(b) the estimate is within about 3% — acceptable for a quick check
  1. (a) \(1.98\to 2\), \(0.51\to 0.5\), \(1.02\to 1\).
  2. Volume \(\approx 2\times 0.5\times 1=1\) m³.
  3. (b) Percentage difference \(=\frac{1.03-1}{1.03}\times 100\%\approx 2.9\%\).
  4. The estimate is therefore about 3% below the exact value, which is well within the accuracy expected of a 1-significant-figure estimate.
Common mistake

Rounding 0.51 to 1 rather than 0.5 — the first significant figure of 0.51 is 5, in the tenths place.

Q21★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 2 Q14 ↗

Estimate the value of \(\frac{(6.2\times 10^{5})(3.9\times 10^{-2})}{1.9\times 10^{3}}\) by rounding each coefficient to 1 significant figure.

Answerabout 12
  1. Round the coefficients only: \(6.2\to 6\), \(3.9\to 4\), \(1.9\to 2\). The powers of 10 are exact and are kept unchanged.
  2. Numerator: \(6\times 4\times 10^{5}\times 10^{-2}=24\times 10^{3}\).
  3. Divide: \(\frac{24\times 10^{3}}{2\times 10^{3}}=\frac{24}{2}\times 10^{0}=12\).
  4. Check: \(12\times 1.9\times 10^{3}\approx 2.28\times 10^{4}\), and the exact numerator is \(2.418\times 10^{4}\), so the estimate is sound.
Common mistake

Rounding the powers of 10 too (\(10^{5}\to 10^{5}\), \(10^{-2}\to 10^{0}\)): only the coefficient is rounded to 1 s.f.; changing the index alters the size of the answer completely.

Type 4

Absolute Error and Maximum Absolute Error · 7 questions

Error of a measurement, half the rounding unit

Core idea: Absolute error \(=\) measured value \(-\) true value, so it carries the same unit as the measurement. For a measurement rounded to the nearest unit \(u\), the maximum absolute error is \(\frac{u}{2}\), because the true value can be at most half a unit away.

Read the rounding unit from the accuracy quoted: to the nearest cm means \(u=1\) cm, correct to 2 decimal places means \(u=0.01\).

\(\text{abs. error}=|\text{measured}-\text{true}|\)\(\text{max abs. error}=\frac{u}{2}\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

A pencil is measured as 24 cm long, correct to the nearest cm. Find the maximum absolute error of the measurement.

Answer0.5 cm
  1. The rounding unit is 1 cm (nearest cm).
  2. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{1}{2}=0.5\) cm.
  3. So the true length lies between 23.5 cm and 24.5 cm.
Q23★☆☆☆2 marks

A bag of apples has mass 3.6 kg, correct to the nearest 0.1 kg. Find the maximum absolute error.

Answer0.05 kg
  1. The rounding unit is 0.1 kg.
  2. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{0.1}{2}=0.05\) kg.
  3. Check: the true mass is between 3.55 kg and 3.65 kg, and each end is 0.05 kg from 3.6 kg.
Q24★★☆☆3 marks

A stopwatch records a runner's time as 45 s, correct to the nearest 5 s.
(a) Find the maximum absolute error.
(b) Write down the range of possible values of the true time.

Answer(a) 2.5 s　(b) \(42.5\le t<47.5\)
  1. (a) The rounding unit is 5 s, so the maximum absolute error is \(\frac{5}{2}=2.5\) s.
  2. (b) The true time can be as much as 2.5 s below or above 45 s: \(45-2.5=42.5\) and \(45+2.5=47.5\).
  3. Hence \(42.5\le t<47.5\) seconds (the upper end is excluded because 47.5 s would round up to 50 s).
  4. Check: 44 s lies inside the interval and rounds to 45 s, as required.
Common mistake

Using 5 s as the maximum absolute error — that is the rounding unit, and a reading can be at most half a unit from the true value.

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

A student recorded the mass of a chemical as 260 g, but the true mass is 258 g.
(a) Find the absolute error of the record.
(b) If the balance reading is correct to the nearest 10 g, find the maximum absolute error.

Answer(a) 2 g　(b) 5 g
  1. (a) Absolute error \(=260-258=2\) g.
  2. (b) The rounding unit is 10 g, so the maximum absolute error is \(\frac{10}{2}=5\) g.
  3. Check: the recorded 260 g is consistent with any true mass from 255 g to 265 g, and 258 g lies inside that range.
Q26★★☆☆3 marks

A rod is 63 cm long, correct to the nearest cm.
(a) Find the maximum absolute error.
(b) Write down the interval of possible values of its true length \(L\).

Answer(a) 0.5 cm　(b) \(62.5\le L<63.5\)
  1. (a) Rounding unit \(=1\) cm, so maximum absolute error \(=\frac{1}{2}=0.5\) cm.
  2. (b) \(63-0.5=62.5\) and \(63+0.5=63.5\).
  3. Hence \(62.5\le L<63.5\) cm.
  4. Check: every value in that interval rounds to 63 cm, and 63.5 cm would round to 64 cm, so it is excluded.
Q27★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q6 ↗

A packet of sugar is labelled 1.5 kg, correct to 2 significant figures.
(a) State the rounding unit and hence the maximum absolute error.
(b) Find the interval of possible masses.
(c) Find the maximum absolute error in the total mass of 3 such packets.

Answer(a) 0.1 kg, so 0.05 kg　(b) \(1.45\le m<1.55\)　(c) 0.15 kg
  1. (a) 1.5 kg has 2 significant figures, so it is rounded to the nearest 0.1 kg. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{0.1}{2}=0.05\) kg.
  2. (b) \(1.5-0.05=1.45\) and \(1.5+0.05=1.55\), so \(1.45\le m<1.55\) kg.
  3. (c) Each packet can be up to 0.05 kg out, so 3 packets can be up to \(3\times 0.05=0.15\) kg out.
  4. Check: the greatest possible total is \(3\times 1.55=4.65\) kg and the smallest is \(3\times 1.45=4.35\) kg; each end is 0.15 kg from \(3\times 1.5=4.5\) kg.
Common mistake

Reading 1.5 kg as correct to the nearest 0.1 kg but then using 0.1 kg as the error, or forgetting that errors add when quantities are combined.

Q28★★★☆6 marks

A rectangular field is measured as 40 m by 25 m, each length correct to the nearest metre.
(a) Find the maximum absolute error of each measurement.
(b) Find the greatest and least possible values of the perimeter.
(c) Find the maximum absolute error of the perimeter.

Answer(a) 0.5 m　(b) 132 m and 128 m　(c) 2 m
  1. (a) Each length is rounded to the nearest 1 m, so the maximum absolute error is 0.5 m.
  2. (b) Measured perimeter \(=2(40+25)=130\) m.
  3. Greatest: \(2(40.5+25.5)=2\times 66=132\) m. Least: \(2(39.5+24.5)=2\times 64=128\) m.
  4. (c) Maximum absolute error \(=132-130=130-128=2\) m.
  5. Check: \(2(0.5+0.5)=2\) m, the same value — the four half-metre errors combine as expected.

Type 5

Relative Error and Percentage Error · 7 questions

Comparing errors, percentage error of a measurement

Core idea: Relative error \(=\frac{\text{absolute error}}{\text{true value}}\), so it has no unit and allows errors in different quantities to be compared fairly. Percentage error is the relative error written over 100.

When the true value is unknown, the maximum absolute error divided by the measured value gives the maximum relative error.

\(\text{rel. error}=\frac{\text{abs. error}}{\text{true value}}\)\(\text{percentage error}=\text{rel. error}\times 100\%\)
Q29★☆☆☆2 marks

A shopkeeper estimates that a crate holds 48 bottles, but it actually holds 50.
(a) Find the absolute error.
(b) Find the percentage error.

Answer(a) 2 bottles　(b) 4%
  1. (a) Absolute error \(=|48-50|=2\) bottles.
  2. (b) Relative error \(=\frac{2}{50}=0.04\).
  3. Percentage error \(=0.04\times 100\%=4\%\).
  4. Check: \(4\%\) of 50 is 2, the absolute error.
Common mistake

Dividing by the estimate (48) instead of the true value (50). The definition uses the true value as the denominator.

Q30★☆☆☆2 marks

A shelf is measured as 80 cm wide, correct to the nearest cm. Find the maximum percentage error of the measurement.

Answer0.625%
  1. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{1}{2}=0.5\) cm.
  2. Maximum relative error \(=\frac{0.5}{80}=0.00625\).
  3. Percentage error \(=0.00625\times 100\%=0.625\%\).
  4. Check: \(0.625\%\) of 80 cm is 0.5 cm.
Q31★★☆☆3 marks

A sack of potatoes has mass 4.8 kg, correct to the nearest 0.1 kg. Find the maximum percentage error, correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer1.04%
  1. Rounding unit \(=0.1\) kg, so maximum absolute error \(=\frac{0.1}{2}=0.05\) kg.
  2. Maximum relative error \(=\frac{0.05}{4.8}\).
  3. \(\frac{0.05}{4.8}=0.0104166\ldots\)
  4. Percentage error \(=0.0104166\ldots\times 100\%=1.04166\ldots\%\), i.e. 1.04% (3 s.f.).
Q32★★☆☆3 marks

A student estimates the capacity of a bottle as 250 mL. The true capacity is 240 mL. Find the percentage error, correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer4.17%
  1. Absolute error \(=|250-240|=10\) mL.
  2. The true value is 240 mL, so relative error \(=\frac{10}{240}\).
  3. \(\frac{10}{240}=0.041666\ldots\)
  4. Percentage error \(=4.1666\ldots\%\), i.e. 4.17% (3 s.f.).
Common mistake

Using 250 as the denominator to get 4.00%. The denominator must be the true value 240 mL.

Q33★★☆☆4 marks

The diameter of a circular pond is measured as 12 cm on a scale drawing, correct to the nearest cm.
(a) Find the maximum absolute error of the diameter.
(b) Find the maximum percentage error of the diameter.
(c) The circumference is calculated as \(\pi\times\) diameter. Find the maximum percentage error of the circumference.

Answer(a) 0.5 cm　(b) 4.17%　(c) 4.17%
  1. (a) Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{1}{2}=0.5\) cm.
  2. (b) \(\frac{0.5}{12}=0.041666\ldots\), so the maximum percentage error is 4.17% (3 s.f.).
  3. (c) Circumference \(C=\pi d\), so \(C\) is a constant multiple of \(d\). Every value of \(C\) is multiplied by the same constant, so the relative error is unchanged.
  4. Demonstration: greatest \(C=12.5\pi\), least \(C=11.5\pi\), true \(C=12\pi\). Then \(\frac{12.5\pi-12\pi}{12\pi}=\frac{0.5}{12}=4.17\%\).

A stopwatch shows a time of 3.4 s, correct to 1 decimal place. Find the maximum percentage error of the time, correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer1.47%

Q35★★★☆6 marks

A rectangle measures 8 cm by 5 cm, each length correct to the nearest cm.
(a) Find the greatest possible area.
(b) Find the maximum percentage error in the area, correct to 3 significant figures.
(c) Find the least possible area and its percentage error.

Answer(a) 46.75 cm²　(b) 16.9%　(c) 33.75 cm², 15.6%
  1. Each length can be up to 0.5 cm out: the greatest dimensions are 8.5 cm and 5.5 cm, the least are 7.5 cm and 4.5 cm.
  2. Measured area \(=8\times 5=40\) cm².
  3. (a) Greatest area \(=8.5\times 5.5=46.75\) cm².
  4. (b) Error \(=46.75-40=6.75\) cm², so the percentage error is \(\frac{6.75}{40}\times 100\%=16.875\%\), i.e. 16.9% (3 s.f.).
  5. (c) Least area \(=7.5\times 4.5=33.75\) cm²; error \(=40-33.75=6.25\) cm², giving \(\frac{6.25}{40}\times 100\%=15.625\%\), i.e. 15.6%.
  6. The maximum percentage error is therefore the larger of the two, 16.9%.
Common mistake

Adding the two percentage errors of the sides (\(6.25\%+10\%\)) and stopping there — that is only an approximation; compute the bound areas directly.

Type 6

Measurement Intervals, Upper and Lower Bounds · 7 questions

Intervals, bounds of sums, products and quotients

Core idea: A measurement \(x\) rounded to the nearest unit \(u\) satisfies \(x-\frac{u}{2}\le \text{true value}<x+\frac{u}{2}\). The lower bound is included and the upper bound is not.

For combinations: the upper bound of a sum uses the upper bounds, of a difference uses upper minus lower, of a product of positive quantities uses upper × upper, and of a quotient uses upper \div lower.

\(\text{LB}=x-\frac{u}{2},\ \text{UB}=x+\frac{u}{2}\)\(\text{UB}(a-b)=\text{UB}(a)-\text{LB}(b)\)\(\text{UB}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=\frac{\text{UB}(a)}{\text{LB}(b)}\)
Q36★☆☆☆2 marks

A piece of string is 5.4 cm long, correct to 1 decimal place. Write down the interval of possible values of its true length \(x\).

Answer\(5.35\le x<5.45\)
  1. Correct to 1 decimal place means the rounding unit is 0.1 cm.
  2. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{0.1}{2}=0.05\) cm.
  3. Lower bound \(=5.4-0.05=5.35\); upper bound \(=5.4+0.05=5.45\).
  4. Hence \(5.35\le x<5.45\) cm.
Q37★☆☆☆2 marks

The mass of a parcel is 320 g, correct to 2 significant figures. Find the interval of possible masses \(m\).

Answer\(315\le m<325\)
  1. 320 g to 2 significant figures is rounded to the nearest 10 g, so the rounding unit is 10 g.
  2. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{10}{2}=5\) g.
  3. Lower bound \(=320-5=315\) g; upper bound \(=320+5=325\) g.
  4. Hence \(315\le m<325\) g.
Common mistake

Using a rounding unit of 1 g because the number ends in 0 — with 2 significant figures the accuracy is to the nearest 10 g.

Q38★★☆☆3 marks

A race is timed at 12.5 s, correct to 3 significant figures. Find the lower and upper bounds of the true time \(t\).

Answer\(12.45\le t<12.55\)
  1. 12.5 has 3 significant figures, so it is rounded to the nearest 0.1 s.
  2. Maximum absolute error \(=\frac{0.1}{2}=0.05\) s.
  3. Lower bound \(=12.5-0.05=12.45\); upper bound \(=12.5+0.05=12.55\).
  4. Hence \(12.45\le t<12.55\) s.
  5. Check: 12.52 s lies in the interval and rounds to 12.5 s.
Q39★★☆☆3 marks

A number \(x\) is given as 7, correct to 1 significant figure.
(a) Write down the interval of possible values of \(x\).
(b) Find the upper bound of \(2x+1\).

Answer(a) \(6.5\le x<7.5\)　(b) 16
  1. (a) 7 to 1 significant figure is rounded to the nearest 1, so the maximum absolute error is 0.5. Hence \(6.5\le x<7.5\).
  2. (b) \(2x+1\) increases with \(x\), so its largest value comes from the largest \(x\).
  3. Taking \(x=7.5\): \(2(7.5)+1=16\).
  4. So the upper bound of \(2x+1\) is 16 (not attained, since \(x<7.5\)).
  5. Check with the lower bound: \(x=6.5\) gives 14, and the whole expression lies in \([14,16)\).
Q40★★☆☆4 marks

(a) Find the upper bound of the total mass of the five parcels.
(b) Find the lower bound of the total mass.

Answer(a) 23.25 kg　(b) 22.75 kg
  1. Each mass \(m\) satisfies \(4.55\le m<4.65\).
  2. (a) Upper bound of the total \(=5\times 4.65=23.25\) kg.
  3. (b) Lower bound of the total \(=5\times 4.55=22.75\) kg.

Q41★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2018 Paper 2 Q14 ↗

Two numbers are given as \(x=3.6\) and \(y=8.2\), each correct to 2 significant figures.
(a) Write down the upper bound of \(x\) and the lower bound of \(y\).
(b) Hence find the upper bound of \(\frac{x}{y}\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer(a) \(\text{UB}(x)=3.65\), \(\text{LB}(y)=8.15\)　(b) 0.448
  1. Each number is rounded to the nearest 0.1, so each can be 0.05 out.
  2. (a) \(\text{UB}(x)=3.6+0.05=3.65\) and \(\text{LB}(y)=8.2-0.05=8.15\).
  3. (b) A fraction is largest when the top is largest and the bottom is smallest: \(\frac{x}{y}\le\frac{3.65}{8.15}\).
  4. \(\frac{3.65}{8.15}=0.44785\ldots\)
  5. So the upper bound is 0.448 (3 s.f.).

Q42★★★☆6 marks

A rectangular card measures 15 cm by 9 cm, each length correct to the nearest cm.
(a) Find the lower and upper bounds of its area.
(b) Find the maximum absolute error of the area.
(c) Explain whether an area of 150 cm² is possible.

Answer(a) 123.25 cm² and 147.25 cm²　(b) 12.25 cm²　(c) no, 150 cm² is impossible
  1. Each length can be 0.5 cm out: \(14.5\le l<15.5\) and \(8.5\le w<9.5\).
  2. (a) Lower bound of area \(=14.5\times 8.5=123.25\) cm²; upper bound \(=15.5\times 9.5=147.25\) cm².

  5. Check: \(147.25>135>123.25\), so the measured area lies inside its own bounds, as it must.

Type 7

Error in Calculated Quantities and Suitable Accuracy · 8 questions

Error carried into area, volume and speed; choosing accuracy

Core idea: When measurements are combined, the errors combine too. Compute the quantity at the extreme values of the measurements: the largest answer uses the largest numerator values and the smallest denominator values, and vice versa.

An answer is only as accurate as the least accurate measurement, so do not quote more significant figures than the data justify.

\(A_{\max}=(x+\tfrac{u}{2})^{2}\)\(\text{UB}(ab)=\text{UB}(a)\times\text{UB}(b)\)\(\text{UB}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=\frac{\text{UB}(a)}{\text{LB}(b)}\)
Q43★☆☆☆4 marks

A bookshop sells 198 books at $48 each.
(a) Estimate the total revenue by rounding each number to 1 significant figure.
(b) Find the exact revenue and the absolute error of your estimate.

Answer(a) $10 000　(b) $9504, error $496
  1. (a) \(198\to 200\) and \(48\to 50\), so the estimate is \(200\times 50=10\,000\) dollars.
  2. (b) Exact revenue \(=198\times 48\).
  3. \(198\times 48=198\times(50-2)=9900-396=9504\).
  4. Absolute error \(=10\,000-9504=496\), about 5% of the true value.
  5. The estimate is therefore in the right region but should never be quoted as the actual revenue.
Common mistake

Rounding 48 down to 40 — rounding to 1 significant figure means the nearest such value, and 48 is nearer 50 than 40.

A metal cube has edge 4 cm, correct to the nearest cm. Find the lower and upper bounds of its volume.

Answer42.875 cm³ and 91.125 cm³

  2. Volume \(=e^{3}\).
  3. Lower bound \(=3.5^{3}=3.5\times 3.5\times 3.5=12.25\times 3.5=42.875\) cm³.

Q47★★☆☆4 marks

Answer5.26%

Q48★★☆☆4 marks

Twenty identical marbles together have mass 76 g, correct to the nearest gram.

(b) Find the maximum percentage error of this estimate, correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer(a) 3.8 g　(b) 0.658%
  1. (a) Mass of one marble \(=\frac{76}{20}=3.8\) g.
  2. The total is correct to the nearest gram, so its maximum absolute error is 0.5 g.
  3. (b) The error is shared among 20 marbles: \(\frac{0.5}{20}=0.025\) g each.
  4. Maximum percentage error \(=\frac{0.025}{3.8}\times 100\%=0.65789\ldots\%\), i.e. 0.658% (3 s.f.).

(b) Find the upper bound of its volume.

Answer(a) 0.388 125 m³　(b) 0.584 375 m³　(c) 21.7%

Q50★★★☆7 marksPast paper · 2014 Paper 2 Q11 ↗

A car travels a distance of 240 km, correct to the nearest 10 km, in a time of 3.0 h, correct to the nearest 0.1 h.
(a) Write down the upper and lower bounds of the distance.
(b) Find the upper bound of the average speed, correct to 3 significant figures.
(c) Find the lower bound of the average speed, correct to 3 significant figures.
(d) Is it possible that the true average speed is 84 km/h? Explain.

Answer(a) 235 km and 245 km　(b) 83.1 km/h　(c) 77.0 km/h　(d) no
  1. (a) The distance is rounded to the nearest 10 km, so half a unit is 5 km: \(235\le d<245\).
  2. The time is rounded to the nearest 0.1 h, so half a unit is 0.05 h: \(2.95\le t<3.05\).
  3. (b) Speed is largest when the distance is largest and the time smallest: \(\frac{245}{2.95}=83.0508\ldots\) km/h, i.e. 83.1 km/h (3 s.f.).
  4. (c) Speed is smallest when the distance is smallest and the time largest: \(\frac{235}{3.05}=77.0492\ldots\) km/h, i.e. 77.0 km/h (3 s.f.).
  5. (d) The greatest possible average speed is 83.05… km/h, which is less than 84 km/h, so 84 km/h is impossible.
  6. Check: the central value \(\frac{240}{3}=80\) km/h lies between 77.0 and 83.1, as expected.
Common mistake

Using \(\frac{245}{3.05}\) for the upper bound — the largest quotient needs the smallest denominator.
