HKDSE Math 2012 Paper 2 Q27 Probability

Divisible by 5?

\(9\star\blacklozenge\) is a 3-digit number, where \(\star\) and \(\blacklozenge\) are integers from 0 to 9 inclusive. Find the probability that the 3-digit number is divisible by 5.

(A) \(1/5\)   (B) \(7/33\)   (C) \(20/99\)   (D) \(19/100\)

Step-by-step solution

Step 1: Understand the sample space +

We have a 3-digit number of the form \(9\star\blacklozenge\).

The first digit is fixed as 9.

The second digit \(\star\) can be any integer from \(0\) to \(9\) — that's 10 choices.

The third digit \(\blacklozenge\) can also be any integer from \(0\) to \(9\) — another 10 choices.

Total possible numbers: \(10 \times 10 = 100\).

Step 2: Divisibility rule for 5 +

A number is divisible by 5 only when its last digit is 0 or 5.

In \(9\star\blacklozenge\), the last digit is \(\blacklozenge\).

So the required condition is: \(\blacklozenge = 0\) or \(\blacklozenge = 5\).

Notice: The middle digit \(\star\) does NOT affect divisibility by 5. It can be anything.

Step 3: Count favourable outcomes +

We need \(\blacklozenge = 0\) or \(\blacklozenge = 5\).

For each of these 2 choices of \(\blacklozenge\), the middle digit \(\star\) can still be any of the 10 digits \(0\)–\(9\).

Favourable count: \(2 \times 10 = 20\) numbers.

Step 4: Compute the probability +

Using the formula:

\[ P = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} = \frac{20}{100} \]

\[ P = \frac{1}{5} \]

Answer: (A) \(\frac{1}{5}\)

Key takeaway: For divisibility by 5, only the last digit matters. The middle digit is free — that's why we multiply by 10. Total outcomes = \(10 \times 10 = 100\). Favourable outcomes = \(2 \times 10 = 20\). \(\frac{20}{100} = \frac{1}{5}\).
