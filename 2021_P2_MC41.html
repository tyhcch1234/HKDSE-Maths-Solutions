Coordinate Geometry — the orthocentre of a triangle in the plane

Orthocentre Altitude Perpendicular Lines Slope Product = −1 Simultaneous Equations

QUESTION 2021 MC #41 MC

Let \(O\) be the origin. The coordinates of the point \(P\) are \((26,\,-18)\). If the coordinates of the orthocentre of \(\triangle OPQ\) are \((21,\,-3)\), then the \(y\)-coordinate of \(Q\) is

A. \(-30\)
B. \(-10\)
C. \(10\)
D. \(30\)

STEP 1 Set up the orthocentre condition

The orthocentre \(H=(21,-3)\) is the common point of the three altitudes of \(\triangle OPQ\). Each altitude is perpendicular to its opposite side, so the product of the slopes equals \(-1\).

\(O=(0,0)\) \(P=(26,-18)\) \(Q=(u,\,-30.0)\) \(H\) (orthocentre) = ? Drag the slider…
11M
Let the coordinates of \(Q\) be \((u,v)\). The altitude through \(O\) is perpendicular to \(PQ\), and the altitude through \(P\) is perpendicular to \(OQ\). Therefore \[ \text{slope}(OH)\times \text{slope}(PQ) = -1 . \] Since \(O=(0,0)\) and \(H=(21,-3)\), \[ \text{slope}(OH) = \frac{-3-0}{21-0} = -\frac{1}{7}. \]
Hint: An orthocentre lies on all three altitudes. Pick the two altitudes whose equations are easiest — those through \(O\) and through \(P\).
21A
The slope of \(PQ\) is \[ \frac{v-(-18)}{u-26} = \frac{v+18}{u-26}. \] Using the perpendicular condition \[ \left(-\frac17\right)\cdot\frac{v+18}{u-26} = -1, \] \[ \frac{v+18}{u-26}=7 , \] \[ v+18 = 7u-182 , \] \[ \boxed{\,7u-v=200\,}\quad\cdots(1) \]
Hint: Be careful with signs — \(v-(-18)=v+18\), not \(v-18\).
31A
Now use the altitude through \(P\). The slope of \(PH\) is \[ \frac{-3-(-18)}{21-26}=\frac{15}{-5}=-3 . \] It is perpendicular to \(OQ\), whose slope is \(\dfrac{v}{u}\). Hence \[ (-3)\cdot\frac{v}{u} = -1 , \] \[ \frac{v}{u} = \frac13, \] \[ \boxed{\,u-3v=0\,}\quad\cdots(2) \]
Hint: The altitude through \(P\) is perpendicular to the side \(OQ\) (the side opposite to \(P\)).
41A
Solve the simultaneous equations. From \((2)\), \(u=3v\); substitute into \((1)\): \[ 7(3v)-v = 200 \;\Rightarrow\; 21v-v=200 \;\Rightarrow\; 20v=200, \] \[ \boxed{\,v=10\,}. \] So \(Q=(30,10)\).
Answer: \(v=10\) — option C.
Hint (faster MCQ way): \((1)-(2)\times 7\) gives \(20v=200\) in one line, so \(v=10\) immediately.

The third altitude (through \(Q\), perpendicular to \(OP\)): \(\text{slope}(OP)=\dfrac{-18}{26}=-\dfrac{9}{13}\), so the altitude slope is \(\dfrac{13}{9}\). Its equation is \[ y-10=\frac{13}{9}(x-30). \] Test \(H=(21,-3)\): RHS \(=\dfrac{13}{9}(21-30)=\dfrac{13}{9}(-9)=-13\), and LHS \(=-3-10=-13\). ✓
All three altitudes pass through \(H=(21,-3)\) — the orthocentre is confirmed.
Hint: This consistency check is exactly why the orthocentre is well-defined: the three altitudes always concur.
