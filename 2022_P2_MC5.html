Question 5

If $$\frac{2}{u}+\frac{3}{v}=4$$, then $$u=$$

A. $$\frac{2v}{4v-3}$$
B. $$\frac{2v}{3-4v}$$
C. $$\frac{3v}{4v-2}$$
D. $$\frac{3v}{2-4v}$$

🧩 Step-by-Step Solution

1

Isolate the term containing \(u\)

$$ \frac{2}{u} + \frac{3}{v} = 4 $$

Subtract \(\frac{3}{v}\) from both sides:

$$ \frac{2}{u} = 4 - \frac{3}{v} $$
💡 Hint: To make \(u\) the subject, first move all terms not containing \(u\) to the other side.
2

Combine the right-hand side into a single fraction

Write \(4\) as \(\frac{4v}{v}\) and combine:

$$ 4 - \frac{3}{v} = \frac{4v}{v} - \frac{3}{v} = \frac{4v - 3}{v} $$

So we have:

$$ \frac{2}{u} = \frac{4v-3}{v} $$
💡 Hint: To combine \(4 - \frac{3}{v}\), use a common denominator \(v\). Remember \(4 = \frac{4v}{v}\).
3

Take reciprocals of both sides

Since \(\frac{2}{u} = \frac{4v-3}{v}\), take reciprocal on both sides:

$$ \frac{u}{2} = \frac{v}{4v-3} $$
💡 Hint: If \(\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}\), then \(\frac{b}{a} = \frac{d}{c}\) (provided none are zero).
4

Solve for \(u\)

Multiply both sides by 2:

$$ u = \frac{2v}{4v-3} $$
💡 Hint: Multiply both sides by 2 to isolate \(u\).

Final Answer

$$ \boxed{u = \frac{2v}{4v-3}} $$

Therefore, the correct option is A.
