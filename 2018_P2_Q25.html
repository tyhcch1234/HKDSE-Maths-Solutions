Perpendicular distances
\(d_1\) from \(P\) to \(L_1\) \(d_2\) from \(P\) to \(L_2\)
[2018MC#25]
The equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \[3x-y+7=0 \quad \text{and} \quad 12x-4y-11=0\] respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_1\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L_2\). Find the equation of the locus of \(P\).
A. \(8x-24y-17=0\) B. \(8x-24y+17=0\)
C. \(24x-8y-17=0\) D. \(24x-8y+17=0\)
Step 1 · Compare the slopes Write each line in slope-intercept form. \[3x-y+7=0 \;\Rightarrow\; y=3x+7 \quad\text{so } m_1=3\] \[12x-4y-11=0 \;\Rightarrow\; y=3x-\tfrac{11}{4} \quad\text{so } m_2=3\] Since \(m_1=m_2=3\), the lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are parallel
Condition for Parallel Lines \(m_{1}=m_{2}\) Both \\(3x-y+7=0\\) and \\(12x-4y-11=0\\) have slope \\(3\\), so \\(L_{1}\\) and \\(L_{2}\\) are parallel.
. They are the blue and green lines on the left.
Step 2 · Interpret the locus condition A point equidistant from two parallel lines must lie on the line midway between them
Locus Equidistant from Two Parallel Lines \(d_{1}=d_{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{a line parallel to both, midway between them}\) A point whose perpendicular distances to the two parallel lines are equal must sit on the line halfway between them.
: \[d_1 = d_2\] So the locus of \(P\) is a straight line parallel to \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) (drawn in amber), whose \(y\)-intercept is the average of the two \(y\)-intercepts.
Step 3 · Find the \(y\)-intercepts For \(L_1\): set \(x=0\): \(\;y=7\). \[c_1 = 7\] For \(L_2\): set \(x=0\): \(\;-4y-11=0 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{11}{4}\). \[c_2 = -\tfrac{11}{4}\]
Step 4 · Average to get the locus
Mid-point of Two Intercepts \(c=\dfrac{c_{1}+c_{2}}{2}\) The midway line has the average \\(y\\)-intercept \\(\dfrac{7+\left(-\frac{11}{4}\right)}{2}=\dfrac{17}{8}\\).
The locus passes midway, so its \(y\)-intercept is \[c = \frac{c_1+c_2}{2} = \frac{7+\left(-\tfrac{11}{4}\right)}{2} = \frac{\tfrac{17}{4}}{2} = \frac{17}{8}\] Slope stays \(3\): \[y = 3x + \frac{17}{8}\]
Step 5 · Convert to general form Multiply both sides by \(8\): \[8y = 24x + 17\] \[24x - 8y + 17 = 0\] Check with the distance formula: equal distances give \(\dfrac{(3x-y+7)^{2}}{10}=\dfrac{(12x-4y-11)^{2}}{160}\), i.e. \(16(3x-y+7)^{2}=(12x-4y-11)^{2}\), which reduces to \(24x-8y+17=0\), confirming the answer.
Hint: Always reduce \(L_2\) by dividing by \(4\) first — then the two parallel lines are easy to compare: \(y=3x+7\) and \(y=3x-\tfrac{11}{4}\).
Answer
D. \(\;24x-8y+17=0\)

Show the worked solution
