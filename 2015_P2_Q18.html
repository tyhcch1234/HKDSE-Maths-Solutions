Trigonometry — expressing a length ratio using two right-angled triangles

Right-angled Triangles cos & tan Ratios Shared Side AC Chain of Ratios

QUESTIONPaper 2 · Q18 (Multiple Choice)

In the figure, \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\) (i.e. \(CA \perp AB\)) and \(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\) (i.e. \(CD \perp AD\)), while \(A\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(C\) to \(AB\) and \(D\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(C\) to the line through \(A\) and \(D\). Given \(\angle ABC = \beta\) and \(\angle CAD = \alpha\), find \[ \frac{AD}{AB} = \;? \]

A. \(\cos\alpha\tan\beta\)    B. \(\sin\alpha\tan\beta\)    C. \(\dfrac{\cos\alpha}{\tan\beta}\)    D. \(\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\tan\beta}\)

Original Figure

PART (a)Finding the lengths in \(\triangle ACD\)

Start with the right-angled triangle \(\triangle ACD\), where \(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\) and \(\angle CAD = \alpha\).

Angle \(\alpha\) 35°
Step 1

In the right-angled triangle \(\triangle ACD\), the hypotenuse is \(AC\) (opposite the right angle at \(D\)). Using the cosine ratio:

\[ \cos\alpha = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{AD}{AC} \]
Hint: The angle \(\alpha\) sits at vertex \(A\) of \(\triangle ACD\). The side next to it is \(AD\) and the hypotenuse is \(AC\).
Step 2

Rearranging for \(AD\):

\[ AD = AC\cos\alpha \quad\cdots(1) \]
Result: \(AD = AC\cos\alpha\)
Hint: This is the key bridge — \(AD\) is expressed using the shared side \(AC\) and \(\alpha\).

PART (b)(i)Finding the lengths in \(\triangle ABC\)

Now use the right-angled triangle \(\triangle ABC\), where \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\) and \(\angle ABC = \beta\).

Angle \(\beta\) 35°
Step 1

In the right-angled triangle \(\triangle ABC\), the hypotenuse is \(AB\) (opposite the right angle at \(C\)). Using the tangent ratio at \(\beta\):

\[ \tan\beta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{AC}{AB} \]
Hint: \(AC\) is opposite the angle \(\beta\), and \(AB\) is the side adjacent to \(\beta\).
Step 2

Rearranging for \(AC\):

\[ AC = AB\tan\beta \quad\cdots(2) \]
Result: \(AC = AB\tan\beta\)
Hint: Both triangle relationships now share the same side \(AC\), so we can connect them.
0 / 2

PART (b)(ii)Combining equations (1) and (2)

We now eliminate the shared side \(AC\) to obtain the required ratio \(\dfrac{AD}{AB}\).

Angle \(\alpha\) 35°
Angle \(\beta\) 35°
Step 1

Substitute equation (2) into equation (1):

\[ AD = AC\cos\alpha = (AB\tan\beta)\cos\alpha \]
Hint: Equation (1) gives \(AD\) in terms of \(AC\); equation (2) gives \(AC\) in terms of \(AB\). Chain them together.
Step 2

Divide both sides by \(AB\):

\[ \frac{AD}{AB} = \cos\alpha\tan\beta \]
\(\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \cos\alpha\tan\beta\)   →   Answer A
Hint: The shared side \(AC\) cancels out neatly, leaving a pure trigonometric expression.

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(\;AD = AC\cos\alpha\)
  • Part (b)(i): \(\;AC = AB\tan\beta\)
  • Part (b)(ii): \(\;\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \cos\alpha\tan\beta\)
  • Correct option: A \(\cos\alpha\tan\beta\)
