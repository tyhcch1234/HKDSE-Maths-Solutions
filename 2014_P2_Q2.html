Step 1: start with the four-term expression $u^2-v^2-5u+5v$.

Group 1 — difference of squares Group 2 — common factor 5 Shared factor $(u-v)$
HKDSE 2014 · Paper 2 · Q2 Polynomials

Factorise $u^{2}-v^{2}-5u+5v$.

$u^{2}-v^{2}-5u+5v=$

  • A$(u-v)(u+v-5)$
  • B$(u-v)(u+v+5)$
  • C$(u+v)(u-v-5)$
  • D$(u+v)(u-v+5)$

Step-by-step solution

Difference of Two Squares \(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)\) The first pair \(u^{2}-v^{2}\) is a difference of two squares and factorizes to \((u-v)(u+v)\).
1Group the four terms into two pairs

$u^{2}-v^{2}-5u+5v=\underbrace{(u^{2}-v^{2})}_{(u-v)(u+v)}-\underbrace{5u+5v}_{5(u-v)}$

Hint: the first pair is a difference of two squares;
the second pair has a common factor $5$.

2Factorise each pair

$u^{2}-v^{2}=(u-v)(u+v)$  
 
and 
  $-5u+5v=-5(u-v)$

So the whole expression becomes

$(u-v)(u+v)-5(u-v)$

Hint: write $-5u+5v$ as $-5(u-v)$,
not $5(u-v)$, to match the first pair.

Common Factor Law \(AB+AC=A(B+C)\) Both terms \((u-v)(u+v)\) and \(-5(u-v)\) contain the bracket \(u-v\), the common factor \(A\).
3Take out the common factor $(u-v)$

$(u-v)(u+v)-5(u-v)=(u-v)\big[(u+v)-5\big]$

Hint: $(u-v)$ is the common factor of both terms.

4Simplify and check the choices

$=(u-v)(u+v-5)$  
→  
option A

Quick check: the constant term must be $-5uv+5uv=0$ and $u=-v$ … putting $u=v$ gives $0$,
which both $(u-v)$ factors satisfy.
Expand A to confirm $(u-v)(u+v-5)=u^2-v^2-5u+5v$. ✔

Answer $(u-v)(u+v-5)$  —  Choice A

Question. \(p^{2}-q^{2}-4p+4q=\)
A. \((p-q)(p+q-4)\)    B. \((p-q)(p+q+4)\)    C. \((p+q)(p-q-4)\)    D. \((p+q)(p-q+4)\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Group in pairs: \(p^{2}-q^{2}-4p+4q=\left(p^{2}-q^{2}\right)-(4p-4q)\).
Step 2. Factorise each pair: \(p^{2}-q^{2}=(p-q)(p+q)\) and \(4p-4q=4(p-q)\), so the expression is \((p-q)(p+q)-4(p-q)\).
Step 3. Take out the common factor \((p-q)\): \((p-q)\big[(p+q)-4\big]=(p-q)(p+q-4)\).
Step 4. Check by expanding: \(p^{2}-q^{2}-4p+4q\) \u2713 — option A.
Answer: A. \((p-q)(p+q-4)\)
