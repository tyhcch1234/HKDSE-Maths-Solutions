Statistics — Range, Inter-quartile Range, Mean & Median

Box-and-Whisker Diagram Range & IQR Mean Median Removing Data

QQuestion

There are 33 paintings in an art gallery. The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the prices (in thousand dollars) of the paintings in the art gallery. It is given that the mean of this distribution is 53 thousand dollars.

(a) Find the range and the inter-quartile range of the above distribution.3 marks

(b) Four paintings of respective prices (in thousand dollars) \(32\), \(34\), \(58\) and \(59\) are now donated to a museum. Find the mean and the median of the prices of the remaining paintings in the art gallery.3 marks

Step 11M

Read the two extreme ends and the two edges of the box from the box-and-whisker diagram:

\[ \text{minimum}=18,\quad Q_1=42,\quad Q_2=55,\quad Q_3=63,\quad \text{maximum}=91 \]
Hint: The whiskers end at the minimum and maximum; the box edges are \(Q_1\) and \(Q_3\); the line inside the box is the median \(Q_2\).
Step 21A

Compute the range:

\[ \text{Range}=\text{maximum}-\text{minimum}=91-18=73 \]
Range \(=73\) thousand dollars
Hint: The range depends only on the two extremes — the median slider does not change it.
Step 31A

Compute the inter-quartile range:

\[ \text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1=63-42=21 \]
Inter-quartile range \(=21\) thousand dollars
Hint: IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data and is not affected by extreme values (18 and 91 are ignored).

Remaining count29

Step 11M

First find the total sum of the 33 original prices. Since the mean is \(53\):

\[ \text{Total sum}=33\times 53 = 1749 \ \text{(thousand dollars)} \]
Hint: Total sum \(=(\text{number of items})\times(\text{mean})\).
Step 21M

The four donated paintings are removed, so the new total sum is reduced by the prices 32, 34, 58 and 59:

\[ \text{New sum}=1749-32-34-58-59=1566 \] \[ \text{New count}=33-4=29 \]
Hint: Subtract the exact prices of the removed items, and remember to reduce the number of items from 33 to 29.
Step 31A

Hence the new mean is:

\[ \text{New mean}=\frac{1566}{29}=54 \]
Mean of the remaining paintings \(=54\) thousand dollars
Hint: Compare with the original mean: the donated paintings had an average \(\frac{32+34+58+59}{4}=45.75\), which is below 53, so the new mean rises slightly.
Step 41A

For the median, list the 33 prices in ascending order. The original median is the \(17^{\text{th}}\) value, which is \(55\). Now remove \(32,34,58,59\):

  • \(32,34\) are both below \(55\) → two values are removed from the left of the median.
  • \(58,59\) are both above \(55\) → two values are removed from the right of the median.

For 29 items, the median is the \(\frac{29+1}{2}=15^{\text{th}}\) value of the remaining order. Since two values were removed on each side, the \(15^{\text{th}}\) remaining value is still the original \(17^{\text{th}}\) value:

\[ \text{New median}=55 \]
Median of the remaining paintings \(=55\) thousand dollars
Hint: The median only changes when the balance of values on each side of it changes. Removing equal numbers (2 and 2) from either side keeps the middle value the same.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Range \(=73\) thousand dollars;   Inter-quartile range \(=21\) thousand dollars
  • (b) Mean of remaining paintings \(=54\) thousand dollars;   Median of remaining paintings \(=55\) thousand dollars
  • Key idea: Trimmed data — remove values from both the total sum and the count; removing an equal number of values on each side of the median leaves the median unchanged.
