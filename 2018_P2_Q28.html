HKDSE Math 2018 Paper 2

Q28 · Probability

Probability Combinatorics Sample Space

Two numbers are randomly drawn at the same time from seven cards numbered 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 and 4 respectively. Find the probability that the sum of the numbers drawn is 5.

The seven cards:

1 1 1 2 2 3 4

Cards are identical except for their printed numbers. Two are drawn simultaneously.

→ Step-by-Step Solution

1

Identify the experiment

Two cards are drawn at the same time. This is equivalent to drawing two cards without replacement in any order (a combination, not a permutation).

We need the probability that the sum of the two numbers is 5.

💡 Hint: Since the cards are drawn simultaneously, pairs like (1,4) and (4,1) are the same outcome. We count combinations, not permutations.
2

Count the total number of possible outcomes

Although some cards share the same number, they are distinct physical cards. Think of them as small labels:

  • The three "1" cards: \(1_1, 1_2, 1_3\)
  • The two "2" cards: \(2_1, 2_2\)
  • The "3" card and the "4" card are unique.

We choose 2 cards from 7 distinct cards: \[ C^{7}_{2} = \frac{7 \times 6}{2 \times 1} = 21 \]

⚠️ Common mistake: Treating cards with the same number as identical reduces total outcomes incorrectly. Always treat them as distinct physical objects.
3

Find the favourable outcomes

We need pairs of card numbers that add up to 5. Let’s check all possible number combinations:

1 + 1 = 2
1 + 2 = 3
1 + 3 = 4
1 + 4 = 5 ✅
2 + 2 = 4
2 + 3 = 5 ✅
2 + 4 = 6, 3 + 4 = 7 (too large)

Only (1,4) and (2,3) give sum = 5.

4

Count how many physical card pairs give sum 5

We count combinations of distinct cards:

  • (1, 4): Choose one of the 3 "1" cards and the single "4". Number of ways = \(3 \times 1 = 3\).
    Outcomes: {1₁,4}, {1₂,4}, {1₃,4}
  • (2, 3): Choose one of the 2 "2" cards and the single "3". Number of ways = \(2 \times 1 = 2\).
    Outcomes: {2₁,3}, {2₂,3}

Total favourable outcomes = \(3 + 2 = 5\).

5

Calculate the probability

\[ P(\text{sum} = 5) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} = \frac{5}{21} \]

✅ Answer: \(\displaystyle \frac{5}{21}\) (Option A)
