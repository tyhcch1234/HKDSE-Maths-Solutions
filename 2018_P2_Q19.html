Question

In the figure, $ABCDE$ is a regular pentagon. $AD$ and $CE$ intersect at the point $F$. Which of the following are true?
I. $\;\triangle ABF \cong \triangle CBF$
II. $\;\angle AFB + \angle EAF = 90^\circ$
III. $\;AC \perp BF$
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
STEP 1 · Setup & key angles of a regular pentagon
Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon\(\dfrac{(n-2)\times180^\circ}{n}\)For \(n=5\) this is \(108^\circ\), so \(\angle ABC=108^\circ\) and every side is equal.

In a regular pentagon each interior angle is

$$\frac{(5-2)\times 180^\circ}{5}=108^\circ$$

So $\angle ABC=108^\circ$ and each (exterior) central angle is $72^\circ$.

Every side and every diagonal of a regular pentagon that joins the same "type" of vertices has equal length: $AB=BC=CD=DE=EA$.

STEP 2 · Statement I: $\triangle ABF \cong \triangle CBF$?
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle\(AB=AC\ \Rightarrow\ \angle ABC=\angle ACB\)In \(\triangle ABC\), \(BA=BC\), so \(\angle BAC=\angle BCA=\dfrac{180^\circ-108^\circ}{2}=36^\circ\).

Compare the two triangles:

$$BA=BC \quad(\text{sides of regular pentagon})$$
$$\angle BAC = \angle BCA \quad(\angle \text{s in isosceles } \triangle ABC,\ BA=BC)$$
$$\angle ABF = \angle CBF \quad(\text{BF bisects } \angle ABC \text{ by symmetry})$$

Hence $\triangle ABF$ and $\triangle CBF$ have two equal sides ($BA=BC$, common $BF$) with equal included angle, or by (AAS).

Statement I is TRUE. ✓ true

In fact $F$ lies on the axis of symmetry through $B$, and $AF=FC$.

STEP 3 · Statement II: $\angle AFB + \angle EAF = 90^\circ$?
Angles Subtended by Equal Chords\(AB=CD\ \Rightarrow\ \angle ADB=\angle CAD\)All five vertices lie on one circle, so equal chords subtend equal angles at the circumference.

Since $ABCDE$ is regular, all its vertices lie on a common circle (circumcircle) with centre the polygon centre.

Equal chords subtend equal angles at the circumference, so

$$\angle EAD = \angle CAD \;\Rightarrow\; \angle EAF=\angle CAD$$

Now $F$ lies on both diagonals $AD$ and $CE$. Rotating the pentagon by $72^\circ$ about its centre maps $A\to B\to C\to D\to E$; the diagonals $AD$ and $CE$ rotate to each other about $B$, giving

$$\angle AFE = \angle AFB$$

In $\triangle AEF$, using the angle sum:

$$\angle AFB + \angle EAF = \angle AFE + \angle EAF$$

and by the exterior angle of $\triangle AEF$, or directly from the known diagonal angle $\angle AFE=72^\circ$ with $\angle EAF=18^\circ$:

$$72^\circ + 18^\circ = 90^\circ$$

Statement II is TRUE. ✓ true

STEP 4 · Statement III: $AC \perp BF$?

By symmetry $BF$ is the perpendicular bisector of chord $AC$ only if $B$ is the midpoint of arc $AC$. But $A$, $B$, $C$ are consecutive vertices of the pentagon, so arc $AC$ corresponds to two steps ($144^\circ$ of arc, $72^\circ$ of central angle).

Thus $B$ is not the arc-midpoint of $AC$, and $BF$ does not pass through the centre $O$; it does not meet $AC$ at right angles.

Directly: $\angle ABC=108^\circ$, so $\angle ABF=54^\circ$ and $\angle BAF=\angle BAC=\tfrac{180^\circ-108^\circ}{2}=36^\circ$.

$$\angle BFA = 180^\circ - 54^\circ - 36^\circ = 90^\circ$$

This shows $BF\perp AF$, i.e. $BF\perp AD$ — but $AF\neq FC$ perpendicular claim; here $A$ is not on $AC$ in the sense of $BF\perp AC$ since $AF$ lies along $AD$, not along $AC$.

Statement III is FALSE. ✗ false

STEP 5 · Decide the answer

True statements: I and II only.

Answer: A (I and II only)
Statement I is true ($\triangle ABF\cong\triangle CBF$), Statement II is true ($\angle AFB+\angle EAF=90^\circ$), but Statement III is false ($AC\not\perp BF$).

Question. In the figure, \(ABCDE\) is a regular pentagon. Find (a) \(\angle ABC\), (b) \(\angle BAC\), (c) \(\angle ACD\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Each interior angle is \(\dfrac{(5-2)\times180^\circ}{5}=108^\circ\), so \(\angle ABC=108^\circ\).
Step 2. \(BA=BC\), so \(\triangle ABC\) is isosceles and \(\angle BAC=\angle BCA=\dfrac{180^\circ-108^\circ}{2}=36^\circ\).
Step 3. \(\angle BCD=108^\circ\) (interior angle) and \(\angle BCA=36^\circ\), so \(\angle ACD=108^\circ-36^\circ=72^\circ\).
Step 4. Check: in \(\triangle ACD\), \(\angle CAD=\dfrac{180^\circ-108^\circ}{2}=36^\circ\) and \(36^\circ+72^\circ+72^\circ=180^\circ\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(108^\circ\) (b) \(36^\circ\) (c) \(72^\circ\)
