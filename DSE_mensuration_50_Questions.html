Type 1

Perimeter and Area of Rectilinear and Composite Figures · 7 questions

Splitting and subtracting rectangles, triangles and trapezia

Core idea: A composite figure is cut into rectangles, triangles and trapezia whose areas you already know, or a surrounding rectangle has the cut-out part subtracted. Perimeter is the total length of the boundary — it is not the same as area, and cutting a corner out of a rectangle leaves the perimeter unchanged.

\(A=l\times w\)\(A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h\)\(A=\frac{1}{2}bh\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

A rectangular card measures 12 cm by 7 cm. A square of side 3 cm is cut away from one corner. Find (a) the area, (b) the perimeter of the remaining card.

Answer(a) \(75\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(b) \(38\text{ cm}\)
  1. Area of the original rectangle \(=12\times 7=84\text{ cm}^{2}\); area of the square removed \(=3\times 3=9\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  2. Area remaining \(=84-9=75\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. For the perimeter, removing a corner deletes two 3 cm edges but creates two new 3 cm edges, so the boundary is the same length as the original rectangle.
  4. Perimeter \(=2(12+7)=2\times 19=38\text{ cm}\).
Common mistake

Most candidates subtract \(4\times 3=12\text{ cm}\) from the perimeter. Check the boundary edge by edge: two edges go, two come back.

Q2★☆☆☆3 marks

In the figure, \(ABCDEF\) is an L-shaped figure with \(AB\) horizontal at the bottom and \(AF\) vertical on the left. Going round the boundary: \(AB=10\text{ m}\), \(BC=5\text{ m}\) (vertical, upwards), \(CD=6\text{ m}\) (horizontal, to the left), \(DE=3\text{ m}\) (vertical, upwards), \(EF=4\text{ m}\) (horizontal, to the left) and \(FA=8\text{ m}\) (vertical, downwards back to \(A\)). Every angle is a right angle. Find the area and the perimeter of the figure.

AnswerArea \(=62\text{ m}^{2}\), perimeter \(=36\text{ m}\)
  1. Complete the figure to the 10 m by 8 m rectangle that surrounds it. The missing corner is the rectangle 6 m wide (along \(CD\)) and 3 m high (along \(DE\)).
  2. Check: \(6+4=10\text{ m}\) (the full width) and \(5+3=8\text{ m}\) (the full height), so the missing piece is \(6\times 3=18\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Area \(=10\times 8-18=80-18=62\text{ m}^{2}\).
  4. Perimeter \(=AB+BC+CD+DE+EF+FA=10+5+6+3+4+8=36\text{ m}\).
Q3★★☆☆3 marks

A flower bed is a trapezium whose parallel sides are 14 m and 8 m long and whose perpendicular distance between them is 6 m. Grass seed is sown at a rate of 1 kg for every 15 m². Find (a) the area of the bed, (b) the mass of seed needed, correct to 2 decimal places.

Answer(a) \(66\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) \(4.40\text{ kg}\)
  1. Area of a trapezium \(=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h\) with \(a=14\), \(b=8\), \(h=6\).
  2. Area \(=\frac{1}{2}(14+8)\times 6=\frac{1}{2}\times 22\times 6=66\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Mass of seed \(=66\div 15=4.4\text{ kg}\).
Common mistake

The 6 m is the perpendicular height, not a slanted side, so it must not be used as a side of a triangle. Also \(\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h\) already includes the halving — do not halve twice.

Q4★★☆☆4 marks

A running track end is formed by a rectangle 9 m by 5 m with a semicircle drawn on one of the 5 m sides, outside the rectangle. Take \(\pi=3.14\). Find (a) the area, (b) the perimeter of the figure.

Answer(a) \(54.8\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) \(30.85\text{ m}\)
  1. The diameter of the semicircle is 5 m, so its radius is 2.5 m.
  2. Area \(=9\times 5+\frac{1}{2}\pi(2.5)^{2}=45+0.5\times 3.14\times 6.25=45+9.8125=54.8\text{ m}^{2}\) (to 1 d.p.).
  3. The perimeter is made of the two 9 m sides, the one 5 m side that the semicircle is not drawn on, and the arc.
  4. Arc length \(=\frac{1}{2}\times 2\pi(2.5)=\pi(2.5)=7.85\text{ m}\), so perimeter \(=9+9+5+7.85=30.85\text{ m}\).
Common mistake

Do not add the 5 m side twice — the side carrying the semicircle is replaced by the arc, not kept.

Q5★★☆☆5 marks

A rectangular lawn is 20 m long and 12 m wide. A path of uniform width 1.5 m is laid all the way round the outside of the lawn. (a) Find the area of the path. (b) The paving costs $85 per square metre. Find the total cost.

Answer(a) \(105\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) $8925
  1. The path adds 1.5 m on each side, so the outer rectangle is \((20+3)\) m by \((12+3)\) m.
  2. Outer area \(=23\times 15=345\text{ m}^{2}\); lawn area \(=20\times 12=240\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Area of path \(=345-240=105\text{ m}^{2}\).
  4. Cost \(=105\times 85=8925\) dollars.
Common mistake

Adding only 1.5 m to the length and width (giving \(21.5\times 13.5\)) is the classic error: the width is added at both ends of each side.

Q6★★★☆5 marks

The front elevation of a house is a rectangle 12 m wide and 8 m high with an isosceles triangular roof on top. The roof rises 5 m above the middle of the 12 m side. Find (a) the area of the whole elevation, (b) the perimeter of the elevation.

Answer(a) \(126\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) \(43.6\text{ m}\) (to 3 s.f.)
  1. The roof is an isosceles triangle of base 12 m and height 5 m, so its area is \(\frac{1}{2}\times 12\times 5=30\text{ m}^{2}\).
  2. The rectangle has area \(12\times 8=96\text{ m}^{2}\), so the total area is \(96+30=126\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Each sloping roof edge is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with legs 6 m (half the base) and 5 m: \(\sqrt{6^{2}+5^{2}}=\sqrt{36+25}=\sqrt{61}=7.810\text{ m}\).
  4. Perimeter \(=12+8+8+2\times 7.810=28+15.62=43.6\text{ m}\).
Common mistake

The 5 m is the vertical rise, not the sloping edge. Using 5 m as a roof side gives \(12+8+8+10=38\text{ m}\), which is too small.

Q7★★★☆7 marks

The plan of a room is an L-shape. Starting from the bottom-left corner \(A\) and going anticlockwise: \(AB=12\text{ m}\) (horizontal, bottom), \(BC=9\text{ m}\) (vertical, right side), \(CD=7\text{ m}\) (horizontal, leftwards), \(DE=3.5\text{ m}\) (vertical, upwards), \(EF=5\text{ m}\) (horizontal, leftwards) and \(FA=5.5\text{ m}\) (vertical, downwards to \(A\)). All angles are right angles. (a) Find the area of the floor. (b) Find the perimeter of the floor. (c) Floor tiles cost $320 per square metre. Find the cost of tiling the floor. (d) Skirting is fitted along the whole perimeter except for two doorways of width 0.9 m each. If skirting costs $48 per metre, find the cost of the skirting.

Answer(a) \(90.5\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) \(42\text{ m}\)　(c) $28 960　(d) $1929.6
  1. The figure fits inside a \(12\times 9\) rectangle: check \(CD+EF=7+5=12\) and \(DE+FA=3.5+5.5=9\), so the notch is the \(5\times 3.5\) rectangle at the top right.
  2. Area of floor \(=12\times 9-5\times 3.5=108-17.5=90.5\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Perimeter \(=12+9+7+3.5+5+5.5=42\text{ m}\).
  4. Cost of tiling \(=90.5\times 320=28\,960\) dollars.
  5. Length of skirting \(=42-2\times 0.9=42-1.8=40.2\text{ m}\).
  6. Cost of skirting \(=40.2\times 48=1929.6\) dollars.
Common mistake

Do not subtract the doorway from the area — a doorway is a gap in the wall (perimeter), not a hole in the floor.

Circles: Circumference, Area, Arc Length and Sector Area · 7 questions

Arc, sector and annulus calculations with correct units

Core idea: A sector is the fraction \(\frac{\theta}{360}\) of the whole circle, so both its arc and its area use the same fraction. An annulus (ring) is the difference of two concentric circles, or of two sectors with the same centre. Keep \(\pi\) in the working and only round at the last line.

\(C=2\pi r\)\(\text{arc}=\frac{\theta}{360}\times 2\pi r\)\(A=\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^{2}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

A circular table top has radius 7 cm (a model of a real table). Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its circumference, (b) its area.

Answer(a) \(43.96\text{ cm}\)　(b) \(153.86\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Circumference \(=2\pi r=2\times 3.14\times 7=43.96\text{ cm}\).
  2. Area \(=\pi r^{2}=3.14\times 7^{2}=3.14\times 49=153.86\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. Check: the area is larger than \(r^{2}=49\) and the circumference is a little more than \(6r=42\), as expected.
Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

The circumference of a circular pond is 62.8 m. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its radius, (b) its area.

Answer(a) \(10\text{ m}\)　(b) \(314\text{ m}^{2}\)
  1. From \(C=2\pi r\): \(62.8=2\times 3.14\times r=6.28r\).
  2. So \(r=62.8\div 6.28=10\text{ m}\).
  3. Area \(=\pi r^{2}=3.14\times 100=314\text{ m}^{2}\).
Common mistake

Do not divide the circumference by \(\pi r\) — with \(C=2\pi r\) the radius is \(C\div(2\pi)\), i.e. \(C\div 6.28\), not \(C\div 3.14\).

Q10★★☆☆4 marks

A sector of a circle of radius 9 cm subtends an angle of 140° at the centre. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the arc length, (b) the area of the sector. Give each answer in terms of \(\pi\) and also correct to 2 decimal places.

Answer(a) \(7\pi\approx 21.98\text{ cm}\)　(b) \(31.5\pi\approx 98.91\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. The sector is \(\frac{140}{360}=\frac{7}{18}\) of the whole circle.
  2. Arc \(=\frac{7}{18}\times 2\pi(9)=\frac{7}{18}\times 18\pi=7\pi\approx 21.98\text{ cm}\).
  3. Area \(=\frac{7}{18}\times \pi(9)^{2}=\frac{7}{18}\times 81\pi=\frac{7}{2}\times 9\pi=31.5\pi\approx 98.91\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  4. Check: \(31.5\pi\) is about \(\frac{7}{18}\) of the full circle \(81\pi\approx 254.3\), and \(98.91\div 254.34\approx 0.389=\frac{7}{18}\). ✓
Common mistake

Area uses \(\pi r^{2}\) with \(r\) squared; using \(2\pi r\) gives the arc length instead of the area.

Q11★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 2 Q15 ↗

The angle of a sector is decreased by 50% while its radius is increased by \(k\%\). If the arc length of the sector is unchanged, find \(k\).

Answer\(k=100\)
  1. Arc length \(=2\pi r\times\frac{\theta}{360}=\frac{\pi r\theta}{180}\), so for a fixed arc length the product \(r\theta\) must stay constant.
  2. New angle \(=(1-50\%)\theta=0.5\theta\); new radius \(=\left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)r\).
  3. Equal arc lengths: \(0.5\theta\times\left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)r=r\theta\).
  4. Cancel \(r\theta\): \(0.5\left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)=1\), so \(1+\frac{k}{100}=2\) and \(k=100\)—.
  5. Check: the radius doubles and the angle halves, so the arc is unchanged. ✓
Common mistake

Remember the new radius is \(\left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)r\), not \(kr\). A common wrong answer is \(k=50\), from assuming the two changes are equal and opposite.

Q12★★☆☆5 marks

A circle of diameter 14 cm is inscribed in a square, touching all four sides. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the area of the four corner regions outside the circle, (b) the percentage of the square that is covered by the circle, correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer(a) \(42.14\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(b) 78.5\%
  1. The circle touches all four sides, so the square has side 14 cm and area \(14^{2}=196\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  2. The radius of the circle is 7 cm, so its area is \(3.14\times 49=153.86\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. Area of the corners \(=196-153.86=42.14\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  4. Percentage \(=\frac{153.86}{196}\times 100\%=78.5\%\) (to 3 s.f.).
  5. Check: the circle takes up \(\frac{\pi}{4}\approx 78.5\%\) of the square, which matches.
Q13★★★☆5 marks

A windscreen wiper is 60 cm long. Its pivot is fixed and the blade sweeps through 120°. The rubber part of the blade starts 15 cm from the pivot and ends at the far tip. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the area of the screen swept by the rubber, (b) the length of the outer arc traced by the tip.

Answer(a) \(1125\pi\approx 3532.5\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(b) \(40\pi\approx 125.6\text{ cm}\)
  1. The swept region is an annulus sector: outer radius 60 cm, inner radius 15 cm, same angle 120°.
  2. Area \(=\frac{120}{360}\pi(60^{2}-15^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\pi(3600-225)=\frac{1}{3}\pi(3375)=1125\pi\approx 3532.5\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. The tip traces the outer arc only: \(\frac{120}{360}\times 2\pi(60)=\frac{1}{3}\times 120\pi=40\pi\approx 125.6\text{ cm}\).
Common mistake

Subtract the squares, not the radii: \(60^{2}-15^{2}=3375\), whereas \((60-15)^{2}=2025\) gives a swept area far too small.

Q14★★★☆8 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q19 ↗

In the figure, \(OAB\) and \(OCD\) are sectors with the same centre \(O\) and the same angle \(\theta\), where \(OA=15\text{ cm}\) and \(OC=25\text{ cm}\). The points \(A\), \(C\) lie on one radius and \(B\), \(D\) lie on the other. The area of the shaded region \(ABDC\) is \(60\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\). (a) Find \(\theta\). (b) Find the area of sector \(OAB\). (c) Find the perimeter of sector \(OCD\), leaving your answer in terms of \(\pi\).

Answer(a) \(\theta=54^{\circ}\)　(b) \(33.75\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(c) \(50+7.5\pi\text{ cm}\)
  1. Both sectors have the same angle, so the shaded region is sector \(OCD\) minus sector \(OAB\).
  2. \(\frac{\theta}{360}\pi(25)^{2}-\frac{\theta}{360}\pi(15)^{2}=60\pi\)
  3. \(\frac{\theta}{360}\pi(625-225)=60\pi\)
  4. \(\frac{\theta}{360}\times 400=60\)，so \(\frac{\theta}{360}=\frac{60}{400}=0.15\) and \(\theta=0.15\times 360^{\circ}=54^{\circ}\).
  5. (b) Area of \(OAB\) \(=0.15\times\pi(15)^{2}=0.15\times 225\pi=33.75\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  6. (c) Perimeter of \(OCD=OC+OD+\text{arc }CD\) \(=25+25+0.15\times 2\pi(25)=50+7.5\pi\text{ cm}\).
Common mistake

In (c) the perimeter of a sector includes the two radii. Quoting only \(7.5\pi\text{ cm}\) (the arc) is the standard trap.

Prisms and Cylinders: Volume and Total Surface Area · 7 questions

Prism volume = cross-section x length; cylinder nets

Core idea: For any prism, volume is the area of the uniform cross-section times the length, and the curved (lateral) surface is the perimeter of the cross-section times the length. A cylinder is just a prism whose cross-section is a circle, so it has an extra two circular faces in its total surface area.

\(V=Ah\)\(V=\pi r^{2}h\)\(\text{curved }S=2\pi rh\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. Find (a) its volume, (b) its total surface area.

Answer(a) \(120\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(158\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Volume \(=8\times 5\times 3=120\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  2. Total surface area \(=2(8\times 5+8\times 3+5\times 3)=2(40+24+15)\).
  3. \(=2\times 79=158\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Q16★☆☆☆3 marks

A solid cylinder has base radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its volume, (b) its curved surface area, (c) its total surface area.

Answer(a) \(1538.6\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(439.6\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(c) \(747.32\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Volume \(=\pi r^{2}h=3.14\times 49\times 10=1538.6\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  2. Curved surface \(=2\pi rh=2\times 3.14\times 7\times 10=439.6\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. Two circular ends: \(2\times 3.14\times 49=307.72\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  4. Total surface area \(=439.6+307.72=747.32\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Common mistake

The curved surface alone is not the total surface area — the two circular ends must be added.

Q17★★☆☆4 marks

A prism has as its uniform cross-section a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm, where the angle between the 6 cm and 8 cm sides is a right angle. The length of the prism is 15 cm. Find (a) its volume, (b) its total surface area.

Answer(a) \(360\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(408\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Cross-section area \(=\frac{1}{2}\times 6\times 8=24\text{ cm}^{2}\) (right-angled triangle).
  2. Volume \(=24\times 15=360\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  3. The perimeter of the cross-section is \(6+8+10=24\text{ cm}\), so the lateral surface \(=24\times 15=360\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  4. Total surface area \(=360+2\times 24=408\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Common mistake

Check the right angle is between the 6 cm and 8 cm sides: \(6^{2}+8^{2}=100=10^{2}\), so 10 cm is the hypotenuse, not a leg.

Q18★★☆☆4 marks

A cylindrical water drum has internal radius 20 cm and internal height 35 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its capacity in cm\(^{3}\), (b) its capacity in litres.

Answer(a) \(43\,960\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(43.96\text{ L}\)
  1. Capacity \(=\pi r^{2}h=3.14\times 400\times 35\).
  2. \(=3.14\times 14\,000=43\,960\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  3. Since \(1\text{ L}=1000\text{ cm}^{3}\), capacity \(=43\,960\div 1000=43.96\text{ L}\).
Q19★★☆☆5 marks

A closed cylindrical tank has radius 1.2 m and height 3 m. It is to be painted all over, including the two circular ends. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the total area to be painted, correct to 3 significant figures, (b) the cost at $42 per square metre, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer(a) \(31.7\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) $1329
  1. Curved surface \(=2\pi rh=2\times 3.14\times 1.2\times 3=22.608\text{ m}^{2}\).
  2. Two ends \(=2\pi r^{2}=2\times 3.14\times 1.44=9.0432\text{ m}^{2}\).
  3. Total \(=22.608+9.0432=31.6512\approx 31.7\text{ m}^{2}\) (3 s.f.).
  4. Cost \(=31.6512\times 42=1329.35\approx 1329\) dollars.
  5. Note: use the unrounded area for the cost, otherwise rounding error creeps in.
Common mistake

Multiplying the rounded 31.7 by 42 gives $1331 — always cost from the more precise value.

Q20★★★☆5 marks

A right prism has as its cross-section a regular hexagon of side 6 cm. The length of the prism is 20 cm. Taking \(\sqrt{3}=1.732\), find (a) the area of the hexagonal cross-section, (b) the volume of the prism, (c) the total surface area of the prism, each correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer(a) \(93.5\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(b) \(1870\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(c) \(907\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. A regular hexagon splits into 6 equilateral triangles of side 6 cm, each of area \(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\times 6^{2}=9\sqrt{3}\).
  2. Cross-section area \(=6\times 9\sqrt{3}=54\sqrt{3}=54\times 1.732=93.528\approx 93.5\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  3. Volume \(=93.528\times 20=1870.56\approx 1870\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  4. Lateral surface \(=\) (perimeter of hexagon) \(\times\) length \(=6\times 6\times 20=720\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  5. Total surface area \(=720+2\times 93.528=720+187.06=907.06\approx 907\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Common mistake

The hexagon has six 6 cm sides, so its perimeter is 36 cm, not 6 cm — using one side in the lateral area is a very common slip.

Q21★★★☆7 marks

A solid metal cylinder of radius 6 cm and height 20 cm is melted and recast into a cuboid whose base measures 12 cm by 10 cm. No metal is lost. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the volume of the cylinder in terms of \(\pi\), (b) the height \(h\) of the cuboid, correct to 3 significant figures, (c) the total surface area of the cuboid, correct to 4 significant figures.

Answer(a) \(720\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(18.8\text{ cm}\)　(c) \(1069\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Volume of cylinder \(=\pi(6)^{2}(20)=720\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  2. Metal is conserved: \(12\times 10\times h=720\pi\), so \(120h=720\pi\) and \(h=6\pi\).
  3. \(h=6\times 3.14=18.84\approx 18.8\text{ cm}\) (3 s.f.).
  4. With \(h=18.84\): total surface area \(=2(12\times 10+12\times 18.84+10\times 18.84)\).
  5. \(=2(120+226.08+188.4)=2(534.48)=1068.96\approx 1069\text{ cm}^{2}\) (4 s.f.).
  6. Check: the cylinder volume \(720\times 3.14=2260.8\text{ cm}^{3}\) and \(12\times 10\times 18.84=2260.8\text{ cm}^{3}\). ✓
Common mistake

Recasting keeps the volume, not the surface area — the cylinder and the cuboid have quite different surface areas.

Pyramids, Cones and Spheres: Volume and Surface Area · 7 questions

Slant height, one-third of the prism, curved surfaces

Core idea: A pyramid or cone is exactly one third of the prism or cylinder with the same base and height. For surface area you need the slant height \(l\), found from the height and the base by Pythagoras. A sphere has no net, so its surface area \(4\pi r^{2}\) must simply be remembered.

\(V=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\)\(\text{curved }S=\pi rl\)\(V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3},\ S=4\pi r^{2}\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

A solid cone has base radius 6 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its volume, (b) its curved surface area.

Answer(a) \(301.44\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(188.4\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. Slant height \(l=\sqrt{6^{2}+8^{2}}=\sqrt{100}=10\text{ cm}\).
  2. Volume \(=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h=\frac{1}{3}\times 3.14\times 36\times 8=301.44\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  3. Curved surface \(=\pi rl=3.14\times 6\times 10=188.4\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Common mistake

The curved surface uses the slant height 10 cm, not the vertical height 8 cm.

Q23★☆☆☆3 marks

A solid sphere has radius 9 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) its volume, (b) its surface area, each correct to 2 decimal places.

Answer(a) \(3052.08\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(1017.36\text{ cm}^{2}\)

Similar Figures and Solids: Ratios of Areas and Volumes · 7 questions

Length : area : volume ratios 1 : k : k squared : k cubed

Core idea: If two similar figures have corresponding lengths in the ratio \(k:1\), their areas are in the ratio \(k^{2}:1\) and their volumes in the ratio \(k^{3}:1\). Lengths, areas and volumes never scale by the same number, and the linear ratio is always the square root of an area ratio and the cube root of a volume ratio.

\(\frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}=k^{2}\)\(\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=k^{3}\)\(k=\sqrt{\frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}}=\sqrt[3]{\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}}\)

Conical frustums, submerged solids and capacity

Core idea: A frustum is a cone (or pyramid) with the top cut off parallel to the base, so its volume is the whole solid minus the small similar top. When a solid is fully submerged in a tank, the water rises by the volume of the solid divided by the area of the water surface — that is the key equation for every water-level question.

\(V=\frac{1}{3}\pi h(R^{2}+Rr+r^{2})\)\(S=\pi(R+r)l\)\(\text{rise}=\frac{V_{\text{solid}}}{A_{\text{surface}}}\)

A hemispherical dome has radius 6 m. Only its curved outer surface is painted. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the area painted, (b) the cost at $120 per square metre, correct to the nearest dollar, (c) the mass of paint used if 1 m\(^{2}\) needs 0.35 kg of paint.

Answer(a) \(226.08\text{ m}^{2}\)　(b) $27 130　(c) \(79.1\text{ kg}\)
  1. Curved surface of a hemisphere \(=2\pi r^{2}=2\times 3.14\times 36=226.08\text{ m}^{2}\).
  2. Cost \(=226.08\times 120=27\,129.6\approx 27\,130\) dollars.
  3. Mass of paint \(=226.08\times 0.35=79.128\approx 79.1\text{ kg}\).
Common mistake

\(4\pi r^{2}\) is the whole sphere; a dome is half of it, so \(2\pi r^{2}\). Using the full sphere doubles the cost.

Q46★★☆☆6 marks

Two solid cones are similar. The base radii of the smaller and larger cones are 4 cm and 10 cm respectively. Both are made of the same material. (a) Find the ratio of their volumes. (b) The mass of the smaller cone is 96 g. Find the mass of the larger cone. (c) It costs $3.20 to paint the curved surface of the smaller cone. At the same rate per cm\(^{2}\), find the cost of painting the curved surface of the larger cone.

Answer(a) \(8:125\)　(b) \(1500\text{ g}\)　(c) $20
  1. Linear ratio \(=4:10=2:5\).
  2. (a) Volume ratio \(=2^{3}:5^{3}=8:125\).
  3. (b) Mass is proportional to volume: \(\frac{m}{96}=\frac{125}{8}\), so \(m=96\times\frac{125}{8}=12\times 125=1500\text{ g}\).
  4. (c) Curved areas go as the square of the linear ratio: \(2^{2}:5^{2}=4:25\).
  5. Cost \(=3.20\times\frac{25}{4}=3.20\times 6.25=20\) dollars.
Common mistake

Using the volume ratio for the cost in (c) gives $50 — cost follows area, so the ratio is \(4:25\), not \(8:125\).

Q47★★☆☆5 marks

An open bucket is in the shape of a frustum of a cone, open at the top, with base radii 24 cm and 18 cm and vertical height 8 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the slant height of the bucket, (b) the total area of metal needed (curved surface plus the circular base), (c) the cost of coating that metal at $0.02 per cm\(^{2}\).

Answer(a) \(10\text{ cm}\)　(b) \(744\pi\approx 2336.16\text{ cm}^{2}\)　(c) $46.72
  1. (a) \(l=\sqrt{(24-18)^{2}+8^{2}}=\sqrt{36+64}=10\) cm.
  2. (b) Curved surface \(=\pi(R+r)l=\pi(42)(10)=420\pi\).
  3. Base \(=\pi(18)^{2}=324\pi\). Total \(=420\pi+324\pi=744\pi\approx 2336.16\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  4. (c) Cost \(=2336.16\times 0.02=46.7232\approx 46.72\) dollars.
Common mistake

The bucket has no lid, so only one circle (the base) is added. Adding a top circle gives \(1164\pi\) and about $73.

Q48★★★☆7 marks

A solid brass ornament is made from a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm fixed on top (flat face down). Brass has density 8.5 g/cm\(^{3}\) and costs $0.06 per gram. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the volume of the ornament, (b) its mass in kilograms, correct to 3 significant figures, (c) the cost of the brass, correct to the nearest cent.

Answer(a) \(108\pi\approx 339.12\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(2.88\text{ kg}\)　(c) $172.95
  1. Cylinder \(=\pi(3)^{2}(10)=90\pi\).
  2. Hemisphere \(=\frac{2}{3}\pi(3)^{3}=\frac{2}{3}\pi(27)=18\pi\).
  3. (a) Volume \(=90\pi+18\pi=108\pi\approx 339.12\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  4. (b) Mass \(=339.12\times 8.5=2882.52\text{ g}=2.88252\text{ kg}pprox 2.88\text{ kg}\) (3 s.f.).
  5. (c) Cost \(=2882.52\times 0.06=172.9512\approx 172.95\) dollars.
Common mistake

The hemisphere is \(\frac{2}{3}\pi r^{3}\), not \(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\). Using the full sphere overstates the volume by \(18\pi\) and the cost by about $28.

Q49★★★☆7 marks

A solid metal cylinder has radius 6 cm and height 16 cm. It is melted and recast, with no loss of metal, into small solid spheres each of radius 2 cm. (a) Find the volume of the cylinder in terms of \(\pi\). (b) Find the volume of one small sphere in terms of \(\pi\). (c) Find the number of spheres produced. (d) Find the total surface area of all the spheres, and hence the ratio of this total surface area to the surface area of the original cylinder, in the form \(a:b\).

Answer(a) \(576\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(\frac{32\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(c) 54　(d) \(864\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\); ratio \(36:11\)
  1. (a) \(V=\pi(6)^{2}(16)=576\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  2. (b) \(V=\frac{4}{3}\pi(2)^{3}=\frac{32\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  3. (c) Number \(=576\pi\div\frac{32\pi}{3}=576\times\frac{3}{32}=18\times 3=54\).
  4. (d) Surface area of one sphere \(=4\pi(2)^{2}=16\pi\), so 54 spheres give \(54\times 16\pi=864\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  5. Cylinder surface area \(=2\pi(6)^{2}+2\pi(6)(16)=72\pi+192\pi=264\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\).
  6. Ratio \(=864\pi:264\pi=864:264=36:11\).
Common mistake

Melting and recasting keeps the volume but not the surface area: the 54 small spheres have over three times the surface area of the cylinder. Answering \(1:1\) is the trap.

Q50★★★★8 marks

A container is a frustum of a cone, closed at both ends, with base radii 20 cm and 12 cm and height 30 cm. It is completely filled with water and the whole of its contents is then poured into an empty cylindrical tank of base radius 15 cm and height 20 cm. Taking \(\pi=3.14\), find (a) the volume of water in the container, (b) the capacity of the cylindrical tank, (c) whether the tank overflows, and if so the volume of water that overflows, (d) the depth of water left in the tank.

Answer(a) \(7840\pi\approx 24\,617.6\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(b) \(4500\pi\approx 14\,130\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(c) yes, overflow \(3340\pi\approx 10\,487.6\text{ cm}^{3}\)　(d) \(20\text{ cm}\) (full)
  1. (a) \(V=\frac{1}{3}\pi(30)(20^{2}+20\times 12+12^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\pi(30)(400+240+144)\).
  2. \(=10\pi(784)=7840\pi\approx 24\,617.6\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  3. (b) Tank capacity \(=\pi(15)^{2}(20)=4500\pi\approx 14\,130\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  4. (c) \(7840\pi\gt 4500\pi\), so the tank overflows.
  5. Overflow \(=7840\pi-4500\pi=3340\pi\approx 10\,487.6\text{ cm}^{3}\).
  6. (d) After overflowing, the tank is completely full, so the depth left is the full 20 cm.
  7. Check: \(3340\times 3.14=10\,487.6\text{ cm}^{3}\), i.e. about 10.5 L is lost. ✓
Common mistake

In (d) the depth is not found by dividing the frustum volume by the tank's base area: the tank can only hold its capacity, the rest is on the floor.
