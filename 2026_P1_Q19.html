Question

Let $h(x)=-x^2+30kx+4x-225k^2-40k+3$, where $k$ is a positive constant. On the same rectangular coordinate system, denote the vertex of the graph of $y=h(x)$ and the vertex of the graph of $y=14-h(4-x)$ by $A$ and $B$ respectively.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of $A$. (2 marks)

(b) Write down, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of $B$. (1 mark)

(c) The coordinates of the point $C$ are $(2+48k,\ 7-36k)$. Let $I$ and $J$ be the in-centre and the circumcentre of $\triangle ABC$ respectively.

(i) Let $M$ be the mid-point of $AB$. Describe the geometric relationship between $CM$ and $AB$. Explain your answer.   (ii) Express, in terms of $k$, the distance between $I$ and $J$.   (iii) The coordinates of $D$ are $(6-22k,\ 6-15k)$. Is it possible that $BDIJ$ is a parallelogram? Explain. (9 marks)

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 · Part (a) — Completing the square

Collect the $x$-terms:

$$h(x)=-x^2+(30k+4)x-225k^2-40k+3$$

Complete the square on $-x^2+(30k+4)x$:

$$\begin{aligned}h(x)&=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+(15k+2)^2-225k^2-40k+3\\&=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+\bigl(225k^2+60k+4\bigr)-225k^2-40k+3\\&=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+20k+7\end{aligned}$$

Answer (a)
Vertex $A=(15k+2,\ 20k+7)$
Tip: the vertex of $y=-[x-p]^2+q$ is at $(p,q)$.
Completing the Square \(y=a\left(x-h\right)^{2}+c\ \to\ \text{vertex }(h,c)\) Collecting the \(x\)-terms gives \(h(x)=-\left[x-(15k+2)\right]^{2}+20k+7\), so \(A\) is \((15k+2,20k+7)\).
Step 2 · Part (b) — Vertex of $y=14-h(4-x)$

Substitute $4-x$ into $h$:

$$h(4-x)=-x^2+(4-30k)x-225k^2+80k+3$$

$$y=14-h(4-x)=x^2+(30k-4)x+225k^2-80k+11$$

The vertex has $x$-coordinate $-\dfrac{30k-4}{2}=2-15k$. Substituting back gives $y_B=7-20k$, so:

Answer (b)
$B=(2-15k,\ 7-20k)$
Vertex of a Quadratic \(x_{\text{vertex}}=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\) For \(y=x^{2}+(30k-4)x+\dots\) the vertex has \(x=2-15k\), giving \(B(2-15k,7-20k)\).
Step 3 · Part (c)(i) — $CM \perp AB$

Mid-point of $AB$:

$$M=\left(\frac{(15k+2)+(2-15k)}{2},\ \frac{(20k+7)+(7-20k)}{2}\right)=(2,\ 7)$$

Vectors along $CM$ and $AB$:

$$\overrightarrow{CM}=(-48k,\ 36k),\qquad \overrightarrow{AB}=(-30k,\ -40k)$$

Dot product:

$$\overrightarrow{CM}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=(-48k)(-30k)+(36k)(-40k)=1440k^2-1440k^2=0$$

Answer (c)(i)
Since $CM\perp AB$ and $M$ is the mid-point of $AB$, $CM$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.
Dot Product of Perpendicular Vectors \(\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v}=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \vec{u}\perp\vec{v}\) \(\overrightarrow{CM}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=1440k^{2}-1440k^{2}=0\), so \(CM\perp AB\).
Step 4 · Part (c)(ii) — Triangle side lengths

Since $C$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, $CA=CB$ and $\triangle ABC$ is isosceles.

$$AM=\tfrac12 AB=\tfrac12\sqrt{(30k)^2+(40k)^2}=25k,\qquad CM=\sqrt{(48k)^2+(36k)^2}=60k$$

$$CA=CB=\sqrt{(25k)^2+(60k)^2}=65k$$

The 25-60-65 triangle is a $5\times(5,12,13)$ triangle.
Step 5 · Part (c)(ii) — In-centre and circumcentre

For an isosceles triangle, both $I$ and $J$ lie on the symmetry axis $CM$. With $M$ at the origin and the $y$-axis along $CM$:

$$\text{Area}=\tfrac12(50k)(60k)=1500k^2,\quad s=\frac{50k+65k+65k}{2}=90k$$

$$r=\frac{1500k^2}{90k}=\frac{50k}{3}\ \Rightarrow\ I=\left(0,\ \frac{50k}{3}\right)$$

Circumcentre: $25k^2+j^2=(60k-j)^2 \Rightarrow j=\dfrac{595k}{24}\ \Rightarrow\ J=\left(0,\ \dfrac{595k}{24}\right)$

$$IJ=\left|\frac{595k}{24}-\frac{50k}{3}\right|=\frac{595k-400k}{24}=\frac{195k}{24}$$

Answer (c)(ii)
$IJ=\dfrac{65k}{8}$
Step 6 · Part (c)(iii) — Parallelogram $BDIJ$?

Return to the original coordinates. The direction $\left(\tfrac45,-\tfrac35\right)$ is a unit vector along $MC$:

$$I=(2,\ 7)+\frac{50k}{3}\left(\tfrac45,-\tfrac35\right)=\left(2+\frac{40k}{3},\ 7-10k\right)$$

$$J=(2,\ 7)+\frac{595k}{24}\left(\tfrac45,-\tfrac35\right)=\left(2+\frac{119k}{6},\ 7-\frac{119k}{8}\right)$$

$BDIJ$ is a parallelogram iff its diagonals $BI$ and $DJ$ bisect each other, i.e. $B+I=D+J$.

$x$-coordinates: $(2-15k)+\left(2+\frac{40k}{3}\right)=(6-22k)+\left(2+\frac{119k}{6}\right)\Rightarrow k=8$

$y$-coordinates: $(7-20k)+(7-10k)=(6-15k)+\left(7-\frac{119k}{8}\right)\Rightarrow k=8$

Answer (c)(iii)
Yes — the conditions are consistent and hold when $k=8$.
Summary of Answers
(a) $A=(15k+2,\ 20k+7)$
(b) $B=(2-15k,\ 7-20k)$
(c)(i) $CM$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$
(c)(ii) $IJ=\dfrac{65k}{8}$
(c)(iii) Yes, possible when $k=8$

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(h(x)=-\left[x^{2}-(8k+4)x\right]-16k^{2}-8k+6\).
Step 2. \(=-\left[x-(4k+2)\right]^{2}+(4k+2)^{2}-16k^{2}-8k+6=-\left[x-(4k+2)\right]^{2}+8k+10\), so \(A(4k+2,8k+10)\).
