HKDSE 2026 · Paper 1 · Q19

Quadratic Functions & Triangle Centres

Question

Let \(h(x)=-x^2+30kx+4x-225k^2-40k+3\), where \(k\) is a positive constant. On the same rectangular coordinate system, denote the vertex of the graph of \(y=h(x)\) and the vertex of the graph of \(y=14-h(4-x)\) by \(A\) and \(B\) respectively.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of \(A\). (2 marks)

(b) Write down, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of \(B\). (1 mark)

(c) The coordinates of the point \(C\) are \((2+48k,\ 7-36k)\). Let \(I\) and \(J\) be the in-centre and the circumcentre of \(\triangle ABC\) respectively.

    (i) Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(AB\). Describe the geometric relationship between \(CM\) and \(AB\). Explain your answer.

    (ii) Express, in terms of \(k\), the distance between \(I\) and \(J\).

    (iii) The coordinates of the point \(D\) are \((6-22k,\ 6-15k)\). Is it possible that \(BDIJ\) is a parallelogram? Explain your answer. (9 marks)

Solution

Part (a)

First collect the \(x\)-terms:

\[ h(x)=-x^2+(30k+4)x-225k^2-40k+3 \]

Complete the square on \(-x^2+(30k+4)x\):

\[ \begin{aligned} h(x)&=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+(15k+2)^2-225k^2-40k+3\\[2pt] &=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+\bigl(225k^2+60k+4\bigr)-225k^2-40k+3\\[2pt] &=-\bigl[x-(15k+2)\bigr]^2+20k+7 \end{aligned} \]

Hence the vertex is \(\boxed{A=(15k+2,\ 20k+7)}\).

Part (b)

Compute \(14-h(4-x)\):

\[ h(4-x)=-(4-x)^2+(30k+4)(4-x)-225k^2-40k+3=-x^2+(4-30k)x-225k^2+80k+3 \]

\[ y=14-h(4-x)=x^2+(30k-4)x+225k^2-80k+11 \]

Its vertex has \(x\)-coordinate \(-\tfrac{30k-4}{2}=2-15k\). Substituting back,

\[ y_B=(2-15k)^2+(30k-4)(2-15k)+225k^2-80k+11=7-20k \]

Hence \(\boxed{B=(2-15k,\ 7-20k)}\).

Part (c)(i)

The mid-point of \(AB\) is

\[ M=\left(\frac{(15k+2)+(2-15k)}{2},\ \frac{(20k+7)+(7-20k)}{2}\right)=(2,\ 7). \]

Now

\[ \overrightarrow{CM}=(2,\ 7)-(2+48k,\ 7-36k)=(-48k,\ 36k) \]

\[ \overrightarrow{AB}=(2-15k,\ 7-20k)-(15k+2,\ 20k+7)=(-30k,\ -40k) \]

Their dot product is

\[ \overrightarrow{CM}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}=(-48k)(-30k)+(36k)(-40k)=1440k^2-1440k^2=0 \]

So \(CM\perp AB\), and since \(M\) is the mid-point of \(AB\), \(CM\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

Part (c)(ii)

Because \(C\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\), we have \(CA=CB\), so \(\triangle ABC\) is isosceles.

Measure the sides: \(AM=\frac12 AB=\frac12\sqrt{(30k)^2+(40k)^2}=25k\), and \(CM=\sqrt{(48k)^2+(36k)^2}=60k\). Thus \(CA=CB=\sqrt{(25k)^2+(60k)^2}=65k\).

For an isosceles triangle, the in-centre \(I\) and the circumcentre \(J\) both lie on the symmetry axis \(CM\). Place \(M\) at the origin with the \(y\)-axis along \(CM\) toward \(C\):

Area \(=\tfrac12(50k)(60k)=1500k^2\), semi-perimeter \(s=\tfrac{50k+65k+65k}{2}=90k\), so the inradius is \(r=\tfrac{1500k^2}{90k}=\tfrac{50k}{3}\), giving \(I=(0,\tfrac{50k}{3})\).

The circumcentre satisfies \(25k^2+j^2=(60k-j)^2\), giving \(j=\tfrac{595k}{24}\), so \(J=(0,\tfrac{595k}{24})\).

\[ IJ=\left|\frac{595k}{24}-\frac{50k}{3}\right|=\frac{595k-400k}{24}=\frac{195k}{24}=\frac{65k}{8} \]

Hence \(\boxed{IJ=\dfrac{65k}{8}}\).

Part (c)(iii)

In the original coordinates, the in-centre and circumcentre are

\[ I=(2,\ 7)+\frac{50k}{3}\left(\frac45,\ -\frac35\right)=\left(2+\frac{40k}{3},\ 7-10k\right) \]

\[ J=(2,\ 7)+\frac{595k}{24}\left(\frac45,\ -\frac35\right)=\left(2+\frac{119k}{6},\ 7-\frac{119k}{8}\right) \]

A quadrilateral \(BDIJ\) is a parallelogram if and only if its diagonals bisect each other, i.e. \(B+J=D+I\).

Equating the \(x\)-coordinates:

\[ (2-15k)+\left(2+\frac{119k}{6}\right)=(6-22k)+\left(2+\frac{40k}{3}\right)\ \Rightarrow\ k=\frac{8}{27} \]

Equating the \(y\)-coordinates:

\[ (7-20k)+\left(7-\frac{119k}{8}\right)=(6-15k)+(7-10k)\ \Rightarrow\ k=\frac{8}{79} \]

The two conditions give different values of \(k\), so they cannot hold simultaneously. Therefore \(\boxed{BDIJ \text{ can never be a parallelogram}}\).

Answers: (a) \(A=(15k+2,\ 20k+7)\)   (b) \(B=(2-15k,\ 7-20k)\)   (c)(i) \(CM\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\)   (c)(ii) \(IJ=\frac{65k}{8}\)   (c)(iii) Not possible.
