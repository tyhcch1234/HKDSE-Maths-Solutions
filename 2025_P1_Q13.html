Locus and equation of a straight line — the perpendicular bisector of a segment, and the area of a quadrilateral.

Locus Perpendicular Bisector Equation of a Line Area of Quadrilateral

📄 Question

The straight line \(L\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis and the positive \(y\)-axis at the points \(M\) and \(N\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(MP = NP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that the equation of \(\Gamma\) is \(3x - 2y - 30 = 0\).

  1. Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(MN\). 1 mark
  2. Find the equation of \(L\). 3 marks
  3. Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(L\) and the \(x\)-axis at the points \(Q\) and \(R\) respectively. Find the area of the quadrilateral \(ONQR\), where \(O\) is the origin. 3 marks

(a) Geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(MN\)

Foot of perpendicular \(F\): Slope of \(MN\): Slope of \(\Gamma\): Product:
Line \(MN\) (locus line \(L\)) \(\Gamma\) (perpendicular bisector) Moving point \(P\)
Step 11M

Since \(P\) is a moving point with \(MP = NP\), \(P\) is equidistant from the two fixed points \(M\) and \(N\).

Hint: The locus of points equidistant from two fixed points is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them.
Step 21A

Therefore \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\). Equivalently, \(\Gamma \perp MN\) and \(\Gamma\) passes through the midpoint of \(MN\).

Answer: \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\).
0 / 2

(b) Find the equation of \(L\)

\(M\): \(N\): Midpoint of \(MN\): Midpoint on \(\Gamma\)?
Step 11M

Let \((a,0)\) and \((0,b)\) be the coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\) respectively, where \(a,b>0\).

The slope of \(MN\) is \(\dfrac{b-0}{0-a} = -\dfrac{b}{a}\).

From (a), \(\Gamma \perp MN\) and the slope of \(\Gamma\) is \(\dfrac{3}{2}\). So

\[ \left(-\frac{b}{a}\right)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right) = -1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{3b}{2a}=1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2a = 3b . \]
Hint: Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\).
Step 21M

The midpoint of \(MN\) is \(\left(\dfrac{a}{2},\ \dfrac{b}{2}\right)\). Since \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\), this midpoint lies on \(\Gamma\):

\[ 3\left(\frac{a}{2}\right) - 2\left(\frac{b}{2}\right) - 30 = 0 . \]
Hint: The perpendicular bisector passes through the midpoint of the segment.
Step 31M + 1A

From \(2a=3b\), write \(b=\dfrac{2a}{3}\). Substitute into the midpoint equation:

\[ 3\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)-2\left(\frac{a}{3}\right)-30 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{3a}{2}-\frac{2a}{3}=30 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{5a}{6}=30 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=36 . \]

So \(b = \dfrac{2(36)}{3}=24\). Thus \(M=(36,0)\), \(N=(0,24)\).

The slope of \(L\) is \(-\dfrac{b}{a}=-\dfrac{24}{36}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\), and

\[ y - 24 = -\frac{2}{3}(x-0) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3y - 72 = -2x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2x + 3y - 72 = 0 . \]
Answer: The equation of \(L\) is \(2x + 3y - 72 = 0\).
0 / 3

(c) Area of the quadrilateral \(ONQR\)

\(Q\): \(R\): Area \(\triangle ONQ\): Area \(\triangle OQR\): Total area:
Step 11M

Point \(Q\) is the intersection of \(\Gamma\) and \(L\):

\[ \begin{cases}3x-2y-30=0\\ 2x+3y-72=0\end{cases} \]

Solve: multiply the first by \(3\) and the second by \(2\):

\[ 9x-6y-90=0,\qquad 4x+6y-144=0 \]

Adding gives \(13x-234=0\), so \(x=18\). Then \(2(18)+3y-72=0 \Rightarrow y=12\).

Hence \(Q=(18,12)\).

Hint: \(Q\) lies on both \(\Gamma\) and \(L\) simultaneously.
Step 21M

Point \(R\) is the \(x\)-intercept of \(\Gamma\). Put \(y=0\) in \(3x-2y-30=0\):

\[ 3x - 0 - 30 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 10 . \]

So \(R=(10,0)\). (Also \(N=(0,24)\) from part (b).)

Hint: The \(x\)-intercept is found by setting \(y=0\).
Step 31M + 1A

The quadrilateral \(ONQR\) has vertices in order \(O(0,0) \to N(0,24) \to Q(18,12) \to R(10,0)\).

Split it by the diagonal \(OQ\) into \(\triangle ONQ\) and \(\triangle OQR\).

\(\triangle ONQ\) — base \(ON\) along the \(y\)-axis has length \(24\), height from \(Q\) to the \(y\)-axis is \(18\):

\[ \text{Area} = \frac{24 \times 18}{2} = 216 . \]

\(\triangle OQR\) — base \(OR\) along the \(x\)-axis has length \(10\), height from \(Q\) to the \(x\)-axis is \(12\):

\[ \text{Area} = \frac{10 \times 12}{2} = 60 . \]

Therefore

\[ \text{Area of } ONQR = 216 + 60 = 276 . \]
Answer: The area of quadrilateral \(ONQR\) is \(276\) square units.
0 / 3

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\).
  • (b) The equation of \(L\) is \(2x + 3y - 72 = 0\). (So \(M=(36,0)\), \(N=(0,24)\).)
  • (c) \(Q=(18,12)\), \(R=(10,0)\), and the area of \(ONQR\) is \(276\).
