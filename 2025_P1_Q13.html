Question [2025 #13]

The straight line \(L\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis and the positive \(y\)-axis at the points \(M\) and \(N\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(MP=NP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that the equation of \(\Gamma\) is \[3x-2y-30=0.\]

(a) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(MN\). (1 mark)

(b) Find the equation of \(L\). (3 marks)

(c) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(L\) and the \(x\)-axis at the points \(Q\) and \(R\) respectively. Find the area of the quadrilateral \(ONQR\), where \(O\) is the origin. (3 marks)

STEP 1 · UNDERSTAND THE LOCUS \(\Gamma\)

For a moving point \(P\) with \(MP=NP\), \(P\) is always equidistant from \(M\) and \(N\). Hence the locus \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(MN\).

So \(\Gamma\) makes a right angle with \(MN\) and passes through the mid-point of \(MN\). This is the key idea for both (a) and
(b).

STEP 2 · PART (a) — Geometric relationship
Perpendicular Bisector as a Locus \(PA=PB\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P\ \text{lies on the perpendicular bisector of}\ AB\) Every point \(P\) with \(MP=NP\) lies on the line that cuts \(MN\) at right angles through its mid-point.

Since \(\Gamma\) is the locus of points equidistant from \(M\) and \(N\),

\[\Gamma \text{ is the perpendicular bisector of } MN.\]

(Equivalently: \(\Gamma \perp MN\),
and \(\Gamma\) passes through the mid-point of \(MN\).)

Answer (a): \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\). (1M)
STEP 3 · PART (b) — Equation of \(L\)
Perpendicular Lines \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) \(\Gamma\) has slope \(\dfrac{3}{2}\), so the slope of \(MN\) must be \(-\dfrac{2}{3}\), giving \(2a=3b\).

Let \(M=(a,0)\) and \(N=(0,b)\) with \(a,b>0\). Then the slope of \(MN\) is

\[m_{MN}=\frac{0-b}{a-0}=-\frac{b}{a}.\]

Since \(MN\perp\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma:3x-2y-30=0\) has slope \(\dfrac{3}{2}\),

\[\left(-\frac{b}{a}\right)\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3b=2a \;\Longrightarrow\; 2a=3b.\]

The mid-point of \(MN\) lies on \(\Gamma\):

\[\text{Mid-point}=\left(\frac{a}{2},\frac{b}{2}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; 3\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)-2\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)-30=0.\] \[3a-2b=60.\]

Solving \(\begin{cases}2a=3b\\3a-2b=60\end{cases}\): from \(a=\tfrac{3b}{2}\),

\[3\left(\tfrac{3b}{2}\right)-2b=60 \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac{9b}{2}-2b=60 \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac{5b}{2}=60 \;\Rightarrow\; b=24,\; a=36.\]

So \(M=(36,0)\), \(N=(0,24)\). The equation of \(L\) (intercept form):

\[\frac{x}{36}+\frac{y}{24}=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2x+3y-72=0.\]
Answer (b): \(2x+3y-72=0\). (1M + 1M + 1A)
STEP 4 · PART (c) — Find \(Q\) and \(R\)

Point \(Q\) is where \(L\) meets \(\Gamma\):

\[\begin{cases}2x+3y=72\\3x-2y=30\end{cases}\]

Solve: multiply first by 2 and second by 3,
then add.

\[4x+6y=144,\qquad 9x-6y=90 \;\Longrightarrow\; 13x=234 \;\Longrightarrow\; x=18.\]

Then \(2(18)+3y=72 \Rightarrow 3y=36 \Rightarrow y=12\). So

\[Q=(18,12).\]

Point \(R\) is the \(x\)-intercept of \(\Gamma\): set \(y=0\),

\[3x-30=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; R=(10,0).\]

Check: \(N=(0,24)\) from part (b), \(O=(0,0)\),
so \(ON=24\) (a vertical side).

STEP 5 · PART (c) — Area of \(ONQR\)
Area of a Triangle \(\text{area of }ABC=\dfrac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{height}\) Splitting \(ONQR\) along \(OQ\) gives triangles of area \(\dfrac{24\times18}{2}\) and \(\dfrac{10\times12}{2}\).

\(O=(0,0)\), \(N=(0,24)\), \(Q=(18,12)\), \(R=(10,0)\). The quadrilateral \(ONQR\) has a vertical side \(ON\) and the horizontal side \(OR\) on the \(x\)-axis.

Split it along the diagonal \(OQ\):

\[\text{area of }ONQR=\text{area of }ONQ+\text{area of }ORQ.\] \[\text{area of }ONQ=\frac{24\times 18}{2}=216,\qquad \text{area of }ORQ=\frac{10\times 12}{2}=60.\] \[\text{area of }ONQR=216+60=276.\]
Answer (c): The area of quadrilateral \(ONQR\) is \(276\) square units. (1M + 1M + 1A)
Tip: For a quadrilateral with one vertex at the origin, splitting along a diagonal from \(O\) into two triangles is the fastest reliable method.

Question. \(M(8,0)\) and \(N(0,6)\) are two points.
(a) Explain why the locus of a point \(P\) with \(PM=PN\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\).
(b) Find the equation of this locus.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. A point with \(PM=PN\) is equidistant from \(M\) and \(N\),
so it lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\).
Step 2. Mid-point of \(MN=\left(\dfrac{8+0}{2},\dfrac{0+6}{2}\right)=(4,3)\).
Step 3. Slope of \(MN=\dfrac{0-6}{8-0}=-\dfrac{3}{4}\), so the perpendicular bisector has slope \(\dfrac{4}{3}\).
Step 4. \(y-3=\dfrac{4}{3}(x-4)\), which simplifies to \(4x-3y-7=0\).
Answer: (a) perpendicular bisector of \(MN\) (b) \(4x-3y-7=0\)
