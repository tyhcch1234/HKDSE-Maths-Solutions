Straight lines, perpendicularity and the area trapped between two lines and the x-axis

Equation of a Straight Line Perpendicular Lines Intercepts Area of Triangle

QUESTION Full Question

The equation of the straight line \(L_1\) is

\[ 4x + 3y - 36 = 0 \]

The straight line \(L_2\) is perpendicular to \(L_1\) and intersects \(L_1\) at a point lying on the \(y\)-axis. Find the area of the region bounded by \(L_1\), \(L_2\) and the \(x\)-axis.

A. 96
B. 108
C. 150
D. 192

PART A Intercepts of \(L_1\)

Find the \(x\)-intercept and the \(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\).

1 Find the \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\)

On the \(x\)-axis, \(y=0\). Substitute into \(L_1\):

\[ 4x + 3(0) - 36 = 0 \] \[ 4x = 36 \implies x = 9 \]
Hint: The \(x\)-intercept is the point where the line crosses the \(x\)-axis, i.e. \((9,\,0)\).
2 Find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\)

On the \(y\)-axis, \(x=0\). Substitute into \(L_1\):

\[ 4(0) + 3y - 36 = 0 \] \[ 3y = 36 \implies y = 12 \]
Hint: The \(y\)-intercept is \((0,\,12)\). This point also lies on \(L_2\).
Answers \(x\)-intercept = \(9\)   ·   \(y\)-intercept = \(12\)

PART B Equation of \(L_2\) and its \(x\)-intercept

Use the perpendicular condition to find \(L_2\), then find where it cuts the \(x\)-axis.

1 Find the slope of \(L_1\)

Rewrite \(L_1\) in slope-intercept form:

\[ 3y = -4x + 36 \] \[ y = -\tfrac{4}{3}x + 12 \]

So the slope of \(L_1\) is \(m_1 = -\dfrac{4}{3}\).

Hint: Rearranging to \(y=mx+c\) makes both the slope and the \(y\)-intercept visible.
2 Slope of \(L_2\) using perpendicularity

For perpendicular lines, \(m_1 \times m_2 = -1\):

\[ \left(-\tfrac{4}{3}\right) m_2 = -1 \] \[ m_2 = \frac{-1}{-4/3} = \frac{3}{4} \]
Hint: The product of the slopes of perpendicular lines is \(-1\). Here \(m_2=\dfrac34\) — note the positive slope.
3 Equation of \(L_2\)

\(L_2\) passes through the \(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\), namely \((0,\,12)\), with slope \(\dfrac34\):

\[ y - 12 = \tfrac{3}{4}(x - 0) \] \[ y = \tfrac{3}{4}x + 12 \quad\text{or}\quad 3x - 4y + 48 = 0 \]
Hint: Both lines meet on the \(y\)-axis at \((0,12)\), which is the vertex of the bounded region.
4 \(x\)-intercept of \(L_2\)

Set \(y=0\) in \(y=\tfrac34 x + 12\):

\[ 0 = \tfrac34 x + 12 \] \[ \tfrac34 x = -12 \implies x = -16 \]
Hint: The negative \(x\)-intercept means \(L_2\) cuts the \(x\)-axis to the left of the origin — this widens the base of the triangle.
Answers \(L_2:\; y = \tfrac34 x + 12\)   ·   \(x\)-intercept of \(L_2 = -16\)

PART C Area of the Bounded Region

Find the area of the triangle formed by \(L_1\), \(L_2\) and the \(x\)-axis.

1 Identify the base on the \(x\)-axis

The triangle is bounded by \(L_1\), \(L_2\) and the \(x\)-axis. Its vertices are:

\[ (9,\,0),\quad (-16,\,0),\quad (0,\,12) \]

So the base along the \(x\)-axis has length

\[ 9 - (-16) = 25 \]
Hint: The two \(x\)-intercepts \(9\) and \(-16\) mark the ends of the base. Distance = \(|9 - (-16)| = 25\).
2 Height of the triangle

The apex \((0,\,12)\) lies on the \(y\)-axis, so its perpendicular distance to the base (the \(x\)-axis) is the \(y\)-coordinate:

\[ \text{height} = 12 - 0 = 12 \]
Hint: The vertex is the common intersection point on the \(y\)-axis, so the height is simply \(12\).
3 Compute the area

Using \(\text{Area} = \dfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\):

\[ \text{Area} = \tfrac12 (25)(12) = \tfrac12 \times 300 = 150 \]
Answer The required area is \(\mathbf{150}\), so the answer is C.
Hint: Always double-check: base \(=21-(-... )\)? No — here base \(=25\) and height \(=12\), giving \(150\).

  • \(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\): \(12\)  →  point \((0,\,12)\)
  • Slope of \(L_1\): \(-\tfrac43\)  ·  Slope of \(L_2\): \(\tfrac34\)
  • Equation of \(L_2\): \(y = \tfrac34 x + 12\)
  • \(x\)-intercept of \(L_2\): \(-16\)  →  point \((-16,\,0)\)
  • Base of triangle \(= 9-(-16) =\) \(25\)
  • Height of triangle \(=\) \(12\)
  • Required area \(= \dfrac12(25)(12) =\) 150 → Option C
