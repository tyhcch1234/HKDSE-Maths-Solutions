Coordinate Geometry — Locus, Perpendicular Bisector & Circle with a Diameter

Equidistant Locus Perpendicular Bisector Slope & Equation of a Line Circle with AB as Diameter Thales' Theorem

QQuestion 6 marks

It is given that \(A\) and \(B\) are two distinct points in a rectangular coordinate plane. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).

  1. Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(AB\). [1 mark]
  2. Suppose that the coordinates of \(A\) are \((2,-4)\) and the equation of \(\Gamma\) is \(3x+y-12=0\). Find
    1. the equation of the straight line which passes through \(A\) and \(B\), [3 marks]
    2. the equation of the circle with \(AB\) as a diameter. [3 marks]

aPart (a) — Relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(AB\) 1 mark

\(PA\) = — \(PB\) = — Difference = — Slope \(\Gamma\) = — Slope \(AB\) = —
Step 11M

Since every point \(P\) on \(\Gamma\) satisfies \(PA=PB\), the locus \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(AB\).

Equivalently: \(\Gamma\) passes through the midpoint of \(AB\) and is perpendicular to \(AB\).

Hint: "Equidistant from two fixed points" is the classic locus definition of the perpendicular bisector — the set of all points satisfying \(PA=PB\) is a straight line at right angles to \(AB\), through its midpoint.
\(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\). (i.e. \(\Gamma \perp AB\) and \(\Gamma\) passes through the midpoint of \(AB\))

bPart (b)(i) — Equation of line \(AB\) 3 marks

Slope of \(\Gamma\) = — Slope of \(AB\) = — Product of slopes = — Equation of \(AB\) = —
Step 11M

From \(\Gamma:\;3x+y-12=0\), make \(y\) the subject:

\[ y = -3x + 12 \]

So the slope of \(\Gamma\) is \(-3\).

Hint: Rearranging \(3x+y-12=0\) into \(y=mx+c\) form directly reveals the slope \(m\) and the \(y\)-intercept \(c\).
Step 21M

Since \(\Gamma \perp AB\) (from part (a)), the product of their slopes is \(-1\):

\[ m_\Gamma \times m_{AB} = -1 \] \[ -3 \times m_{AB} = -1 \] \[ m_{AB} = \frac{1}{3} \]
Hint: Perpendicular lines satisfy \(m_1 m_2 = -1\). Always take the negative reciprocal of the given slope.
Slope of \(AB\) is \(\dfrac{1}{3}\).
Step 31M

Now use the point-slope form with \(A(2,-4)\) and slope \(\frac{1}{3}\):

\[ y - (-4) = \frac{1}{3}(x-2) \] \[ y+4 = \frac{1}{3}(x-2) \]
Hint: The point-slope form \(y-y_1 = m(x-x_1)\) works for any known point on the line. Substitute \(x_1 = 2,\; y_1 = -4\).
Step 41A

Clear the fraction and rearrange into general form:

\[ 3(y+4) = x-2 \] \[ 3y + 12 = x - 2 \] \[ x - 3y - 14 = 0 \]
The equation of \(AB\) is \(\; x - 3y - 14 = 0 \).

iiPart (b)(ii) — Circle with \(AB\) as a diameter 3 marks

Centre = — Radius = — \(AB\) (diameter) = — \(\angle AQB\) = — Equation = —
Step 11M

The centre of the circle with \(AB\) as diameter is the midpoint of \(AB\), which is also the point of intersection of \(AB\) and \(\Gamma\) (the perpendicular bisector).

Solve the simultaneous equations:

\[ \begin{cases} 3x+y-12=0 & \cdots (1)\\[2pt] x-3y-14=0 & \cdots (2)\end{cases} \]

From (1): \(y = 12-3x\). Substitute into (2):

\[ x - 3(12-3x) - 14 = 0 \] \[ x - 36 + 9x - 14 = 0 \] \[ 10x = 50 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 5 \]

Then \(y = 12 - 3(5) = -3\).

Hint: The centre lies on both the diameter line \(AB\) and the perpendicular bisector \(\Gamma\). Solving these two linear equations gives the centre.
Centre of the circle \(= (5,\,-3)\).
Step 21M

Now find the radius — the distance from the centre \((5,-3)\) to a point on the circle, say \(A(2,-4)\):

\[ r = \sqrt{(2-5)^2 + (-4-(-3))^2} \] \[ r = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-1)^2} \] \[ r = \sqrt{9+1} = \sqrt{10} \]
Hint: Any point on the circle (e.g. \(A\) or \(B\)) gives the same radius, since both are exactly \(r\) from the centre. Verify with \(B\) too.
Radius \(=\sqrt{10}\), so \(r^2 = 10\).
Step 31A

Write the circle equation in centre-radius form \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2 = r^2\) with centre \((h,k) = (5,-3)\) and \(r^2 = 10\):

\[ (x-5)^2 + \bigl(y-(-3)\bigr)^2 = 10 \] \[ (x-5)^2 + (y+3)^2 = 10 \]
Hint: Remember the standard form \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\), replacing \(h,k\) by the centre coordinates and the right side by \(r^2\) (not \(r\)).
The equation of the required circle is \(\;(x-5)^2+(y+3)^2 = 10\).
0 / 3

✓Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) (so \(\Gamma \perp AB\) and \(\Gamma\) passes through the midpoint of \(AB\)).
  • (b)(i) Slope of \(AB = \frac{1}{3}\); the equation of \(AB\) is \(\;x - 3y - 14 = 0\).
  • (b)(ii) Centre \(=(5,\,-3)\), radius \(=\sqrt{10}\), so the circle is \(\;(x-5)^2+(y+3)^2 = 10\).
