Statistics — Median constraint & greatest possible standard deviation
Frequency Distribution Median Standard Deviation Inequalities

Question5 marks

The table below shows the distribution of the numbers of pens owned by some students.

Number of pens56789
Number of students9132\(s\)11

The median of the distribution is \(7\).

(a) Find the least possible value of \(s\).
(b) Write down the greatest possible value of \(s\).
(c) Find the greatest possible standard deviation of the distribution.

Part (a) Find the least possible value of \(s\)2 marks

The data must be arranged in order. Use the condition "median = 7" to constrain the position of the middle student.

10
Total students \(N\) = 33 Cumulative freq. up to 7 pens = 24 Median = 7 ✓
1 Total number of students 1M
\[ \text{Total students} = 9 + 13 + 2 + s + 11 = 35 + s \]
Hint: The median is the \(\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)\)-th datum when \(N\) is odd. Its position depends on the total number of students.
2 Locate the median criterion 1M

For the median to be \(7\), there must be more students with \(\le 7\) pens than half the total, and students with \(\le 6\) pens must be less than half.

\[ \underbrace{9+13+2}_{24} \;\ge\; \frac{35+s}{2} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \underbrace{9+13}_{22} \;<\; \frac{35+s}{2} \]
Hint: The "\(7\)" students must include the middle position(s) but the "\(6\)" students must not reach the middle.
3 Solve the inequalities 1A
\[ 24 \ge \frac{35+s}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; 48 \ge 35+s \;\Rightarrow\; s \le 13 \]
\[ 22 < \frac{35+s}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; 44 < 35+s \;\Rightarrow\; s > 9 \]
Answer: \(9 < s \le 13\). Since \(s\) is a positive integer (number of students), the least possible value of \(s\) is \(\boxed{10}\), and the greatest possible value of \(s\) is \(\boxed{13}\).

Part (b) Write down the greatest possible value of \(s\)1 mark

This follows directly from Part (a): the upper bound of the inequality \(9 < s \le 13\).

Feasible \(s\): \(10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13\) Greatest \(s\) = 13
1 Read off the upper bound 1A

From Part (a), \(s\) must satisfy \(9 < s \le 13\). Since \(s\) must be a non-negative integer:

\[ s \in \{10,\,11,\,12,\,13\} \]
Answer: The greatest possible value of \(s\) is \(\boxed{13}\).
Hint: If \(s = 14\), the total is \(49\), so \(\frac{N+1}{2} = 25\), but only \(9+13+2 = 24\) students have \(\le 7\) pens — the median would become \(8\). That is not allowed.

Part (c) Greatest possible standard deviation2 marks

Test each feasible value of \(s\) and compare the standard deviations. A histogram & the SD readout update live below.

10
Mean \(\bar{x}\) = 7.240 SD \(\sigma\) = 1.5127 Best so far: s = 10
1 Set up the standard-deviation formula 1M

For a frequency distribution, the standard deviation is

\[ \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f x^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f x}{N}\right)^2} \]

Here \(N = 35 + s\). The possible values of \(s\) are \(10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13\). We evaluate \(\sigma\) for each and take the greatest.

Hint: Increasing a frequency at the high end (8 pens) tends to increase spread. Compare all four cases carefully.
2 Evaluate each case 1M
\[ \text{Case 1: } s=10,\ N=45,\quad \sigma \approx 1.512744 \]
\[ \text{Case 2: } s=11,\ N=46,\quad \sigma \approx 1.502990 \]
\[ \text{Case 3: } s=12,\ N=47,\quad \sigma \approx 1.493308 \]
\[ \text{Case 4: } s=13,\ N=48,\quad \sigma \approx 1.483... \]
Hint: As \(s\) increases, the extra data cluster near the mean, which reduces spread — hence the SD decreases.
3 Choose the greatest value 1A

The maximum occurs at \(s = 10\), giving

\[ \sigma_{\max} \approx 1.512744 \approx 1.51 \ (\text{cor. to 3 sig. fig.}) \]
Answer: The greatest possible standard deviation is \(\boxed{1.51}\) (correct to 3 significant figures).
