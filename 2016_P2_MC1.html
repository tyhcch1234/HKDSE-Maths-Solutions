Laws of Indices — Simplify \(8^{222}\cdot 5^{666}\)

Multiple-choice · Topic 8: Laws of Indices, Exponential & Logarithmic Functions

Laws of Indices Power of a Power Prime Factorisation Common Exponent

Question

\[ 8^{222}\cdot 5^{666} = \;? \]
A \(10^{666}\)
B \(10^{888}\)
C \(40^{666}\)
D \(40^{888}\)
Tap an option to check your answer.

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Work through each step. Try it yourself before opening the next card!

0 of 4 steps revealed
1 Look for a hidden connection between the exponents ▼

The two powers are \(8^{222}\) and \(5^{666}\). The bases \(8\) and \(5\) look unrelated — but look at the exponents: \(222\) and \(666\).

\[ 666 = 3 \times 222 \]

So the second exponent is exactly three times the first one. That is the key that lets us combine the two terms.

Law to recall — Power of a Power: \[ \left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{m\times n} \]
  • Here \(a\) = base, \(m\) = inner index, \(n\) = outer index.
  • Goal: make both powers have the same exponent so we can use \(a^n b^n = (ab)^n\).
2 Rewrite \(8\) as a power of \(2\) ▼

Write the base \(8\) in terms of its prime factor:

\[ 8 = 2^{3} \] \[ \Rightarrow \quad 8^{222} = \left(2^{3}\right)^{222} = 2^{3\times 222} = 2^{666} \]

Now both terms carry the exponent \(666\):

\[ 8^{222}\cdot 5^{666} = 2^{666}\cdot 5^{666} \]
Why \(8 = 2^3\)?
  • \(8 = 2\times 2\times 2\), so the prime factorisation is \(2^3\).
  • Then apply the power-of-a-power rule with \(m=3,\ n=222\).
3 Combine the bases using the same exponent ▼

When two powers share the same exponent, multiply the bases:

\[ a^{n}\cdot b^{n} = (ab)^{n} \]

Apply it with \(a=2,\ b=5,\ n=666\):

\[ 2^{666}\cdot 5^{666} = (2\times 5)^{666} = 10^{666} \]
Sanity check with small numbers: \[ 2^{3}\cdot 5^{3} = 8\times 125 = 1000 = 10^{3} \] The rule works exactly the same way with \(n = 666\).
4 Read off the answer ▼

The expression simplifies completely to a single power of \(10\):

\[ 8^{222}\cdot 5^{666} = 2^{666}\cdot 5^{666} = (2\cdot 5)^{666} = 10^{666} \]

Comparing with the options:

  • A. \(10^{666}\) ✔
  • B. \(10^{888}\) ✘ — wrong exponent (adding \(222+666\) is a trap)
  • C. \(40^{666}\) ✘ — \(8\times 5\neq 40\) (you cannot multiply the bases when indices differ)
  • D. \(40^{888}\) ✘ — two mistakes combined

⚠️ Common trap: \(a^{m}\cdot b^{n}\) is not \((ab)^{m+n}\). Only when the indices are equal can you merge the bases.

CORRECT ANSWER
A. \(10^{666}\)

Index Laws Cheat Sheet

Product: \(a^{m}\cdot a^{n} = a^{m+n}\)
Quotient: \(a^{m}\div a^{n} = a^{m-n}\)
Power of a power: \(\left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{mn}\)
Same index: \(a^{n}\cdot b^{n} = (ab)^{n}\)
Zero index: \(a^{0} = 1\)  \((a\neq 0)\)
Negative index: \(a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^{n}}\)
