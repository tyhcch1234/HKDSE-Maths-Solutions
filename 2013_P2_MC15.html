In the figure, the regular octagon is divided into eight identical isosceles triangles and four of them are shaded. The number of axes of reflectional symmetry of the octagon is

A. 2    B. 4    C. 6    D. 8

1 1A

Understand the octagon’s own symmetry. A regular octagon (all sides equal, all angles equal) has \(8\) axes of reflectional symmetry: \(4\) through opposite vertices and \(4\) through midpoints of opposite sides.

💡 A regular \(n\)-gon always has \(n\) axes of reflectional symmetry. For \(n=8\), that’s \(8\) axes – but the shading may break some of them!
2 1M

Test each axis against the shading. The pattern consists of four shaded triangles. For an axis to be a symmetry axis, reflecting every shaded triangle across it must land exactly on another shaded triangle (the shading must be preserved).

💡 Rotate the slider to try all possible axes (0° to 180° covers every line through the centre). The pattern only matches when the axis passes through the centre of a shaded triangle and the opposite white triangle.
3 1A

Count the valid axes. Only \(4\) orientations preserve the shading: the ones along the lines that bisect the shaded triangles. The other \(4\) axes (through vertices of the octagon) swap shaded and unshaded regions, so they are not axes of symmetry of the shaded pattern.

✅ Number of axes of reflectional symmetry = 4 → Answer: B

★ Summary

  • Regular octagon has \(8\) axes of symmetry, but the shading reduces it to \(4\).
  • Only axes that map shaded regions onto shaded regions count.
  • Final answer: B (4)
