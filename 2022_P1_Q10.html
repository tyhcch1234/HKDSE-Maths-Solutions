HKDSE Math 2022 Paper 1 Q10

Rate, Ratio and Variations · Quadratic Functions · Discriminant

Question [Total: 6 marks]

It is given that \(f(x)\) partly varies as \(x^{2}\) and partly varies as \(x\). Suppose that \(f(4) = 96\) and \(f(-5) = 15\).

  1. (a) Find \(f(x)\). [3 marks]
  2. (b) Write down the \(x\)-intercept(s) of the graph of \(y = 8f(x)\). [1 mark]
  3. (c) Let \(k\) be a real constant. Find the range of values of \(k\) such that the equation \(f(x) = k\) has two distinct real roots. [2 marks]

Key Concepts

Partial Variation Quadratic Function Simultaneous Equations x-intercepts Discriminant \(\Delta\)

Part (a) — Find \(f(x)\)

3 marks · Modelling a partial variation

Learning goal

Translate the phrase "partly varies as \(x^2\) and partly varies as \(x\)" into an algebraic model \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\), then use the two given data points to determine \(a\) and \(b\).

🔍 Interactive: Can you hit both data points?

Adjust \(a\) and \(b\). The tool checks whether \(f(4)=96\) and \(f(-5)=15\). Find the pair that satisfies both conditions.

\(f(4)=\) 0 vs 96
\(f(-5)=\) 0 vs 15
✅ Excellent! \(a=3,\ b=12\) works — so \(f(x)=3x^{2}+12x\).

Part (b) — x-intercepts of \(y = 8f(x)\)

1 mark · Reading off a scaled graph

Learning goal

Understand that multiplying a function by a positive constant \(8\) stretches the graph vertically but does not change its \(x\)-intercepts.

📈 Interactive: \(y = f(x)\) vs \(y = 8f(x)\)

Drag the slider to morph \(y=c\,f(x)\) from \(c=1\) to \(c=8\). Watch the roots stay fixed!

c = 1
\(y=f(x)\) (c=1) \(y=c\,f(x)\) (current) \(x\)-intercepts

\(x\)-intercepts are \(x = 0\) and \(x = -4\).

Part (c) — Range of \(k\) for two distinct real roots

2 marks · Discriminant condition

Learning goal

Recognise \(f(x)=k\) as a constant line cutting the parabola, and use the discriminant \(\Delta = b^{2}-4ac > 0\) to guarantee two distinct intersections.

📐 Interactive: Horizontal line \(y=k\)

Drag \(k\). Count how many times the line meets the parabola \(y=3x^{2}+12x\).

k = 0

The vertex is at \((-2,\,-12)\) — the threshold where the line becomes tangent.

(a)

Let \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are non-zero constants.

\[ \begin{cases} 16a+4b=96 \\ 25a-5b=15 \end{cases} \]

Solving gives \(a=3\) and \(b=12\).

Thus \(f(x)=3x^{2}+12x\).

(b)

Set \(8f(x)=0 \Rightarrow 8x(3x+12)=0 \Rightarrow x=0\) or \(x=-4\).

The \(x\)-intercepts are \(0\) and \(-4\).

(c)

\(3x^{2}+12x-k=0\) has two distinct real roots when \(\Delta>0\):

\[ 12^{2}-4(3)(-k)>0 \Rightarrow 144+12k>0 \Rightarrow k>-12. \]
