HKDSE 2022 · Paper 1 · Q10

Rate, Ratio and Variations

It is given that $f(x)$ partly varies as $x^2$ and partly varies as $x$. Suppose that $f(4)=96$ and $f(-5)=15$.

(a) Find $f(x)$. [3 marks]

(b) Write down the $x$-intercept(s) of the graph of $y=8f(x)$. [1 mark]

(c) Let $k$ be a real constant. Find the range of values of $k$ such that the equation $f(x)=k$ has two distinct real roots. [2 marks]

Solution

Step 1Set up the variation model.
Partial Variation \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\) One part varies as \(x^{2}\) and the other as \(x\), so the two terms are added with constants \(a\) and \(b\).

"Partly varies as $x^2$" gives a term $ax^2$;
"partly varies as $x$" gives a term $bx$. Hence \[ f(x) = ax^2 + bx \] where $a$ and $b$ are non-zero constants.

Write the general form of a "partly varies" relation as a sum of the two separate variation terms.
Step 2Substitute the two data points.

From $f(4)=96$: \[ a(4)^2 + b(4) = 96 \;\Rightarrow\; 16a + 4b = 96 \tag{1} \] From $f(-5)=15$: \[ a(-5)^2 + b(-5) = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; 25a - 5b = 15 \tag{2} \]

Substitute each $x$ into $f(x)=ax^2+bx$ and simplify to get a pair of linear equations in $a$ and $b$.
Step 3Solve the simultaneous equations.

Divide (1) by 4: $4a + b = 24 \;\Rightarrow\; b = 24 - 4a$.
Divide (2) by 5: $5a - b = 3$.

Substitute: $5a - (24 - 4a) = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; 9a = 27 \;\Rightarrow\; a = 3$, so $b = 24 - 12 = 12$.

Therefore \[ f(x) = 3x^2 + 12x \]

Use substitution or elimination; always verify with one of the original equations.
Step 4(b) $x$-intercepts of $y = 8f(x)$.
x-intercepts of a Graph \(y=f(x)\ \text{meets the }x\text{-axis where }f(x)=0\) Multiplying \(f\) by \(8\) does not move the \(x\)-intercepts, so \(3x^{2}+12x=0\) gives \(x=0\) and \(x=-4\).

The $x$-intercepts of $y=8f(x)$ occur where $8f(x)=0$,
i.e. where $f(x)=0$ — exactly the same $x$-values as for $y=f(x)$.

\[ 3x^2 + 12x = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x(x+4)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 0 \ \text{or}\ x = -4. \]

Multiplying a function by a non-zero constant does not change the positions of its $x$-intercepts.
Step 5(c) Apply the discriminant condition.
Discriminant \(\Delta=B^{2}-4AC>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{two distinct real roots}\) For \(3x^{2}+8x-k=0\) the condition becomes \(64+12k>0\).

$f(x)=k$ means $3x^2 + 12x - k = 0$. Two distinct real roots requires \[ \Delta = 12^2 - 4(3)(-k) > 0. \]

For a quadratic $Ax^2+Bx+C=0$, distinct real roots ⟺ $\Delta = B^2-4AC > 0$.
Step 6Solve the inequality for $k$.

\[ 144 + 12k > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 12k > -144 \;\Rightarrow\; k > -12. \]

This matches the geometry: the horizontal line $y=k$ cuts the parabola at two points exactly when $k$ is above the vertex value $-12$.
Answer: (a) $f(x)=3x^2+12x$  ·  (b) $x=0$ and $x=-4$  ·  (c) $k>-12$

Question. It is given that \(f(x)\) partly varies as \(x^{2}\) and partly varies as \(x\). Suppose that \(f(2)=28\) and \(f(-3)=3\).
(a) Find \(f(x)\).
(b) Write down the \(x\)-intercepts of the graph of \(y=5f(x)\).
(c) Find the range of values of \(k\) such that \(f(x)=k\) has two distinct real roots.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\). From \(f(2)=28\): \(4a+2b=28\). From \(f(-3)=3\): \(9a-3b=3\).
Step 2. Divide the first equation by \(2\): \(2a+b=14\);
divide the second by \(3\): \(3a-b=1\). Adding gives \(5a=15\), so \(a=3\) and \(b=14-6=8\).
Step 3. Hence \(f(x)=3x^{2}+8x\). Check: \(f(2)=12+16=28\) ✓ and \(f(-3)=27-24=3\) ✓.
Step 4. (b) \(5f(x)=0\) exactly when \(f(x)=0\): \(x(3x+8)=0\), so the \(x\)-intercepts are \(x=0\) and \(x=-\dfrac{8}{3}\).
Step 5. (c) \(3x^{2}+8x-k=0\) has two distinct real roots when \(\Delta=64+12k>0\), i.e. \(k>-\dfrac{16}{3}\).
Answer: (a) \(f(x)=3x^{2}+8x\); (b) \(x=0\) and \(x=-\dfrac{8}{3}\); (c) \(k>-\dfrac{16}{3}\)
