Question

Factorize

  1. $x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}$
  2. $x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}+7x-21y$  [3 marks]

Step-by-step solution

1Recognise the perfect square

Part (a) has three terms. Check whether the first and last are perfect squares:

$$x^{2}=(x)^{2},\qquad 9y^{2}=(3y)^{2},\qquad -6xy=-2\cdot(x)(3y).$$

This matches the pattern $(a-b)^{2}=a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}$ with $a=x$, $b=3y$.

Hint: the middle term must be exactly twice the product of the two square roots — here $2\cdot x\cdot 3y = 6xy$, and the sign is negative.
2Factorize part (a)

$$x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}=(x-3y)^{2}$$

So the square root of the trinomial is the single factor $(x-3y)$. This is the key building block for part (b).

Hint: a perfect-square trinomial has only one repeated factor — useful for the next part.
3Group the four terms (part b)

$$x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}+7x-21y$$

The first three terms form the perfect square from (a); look for a common factor in the remaining terms:

$$7x-21y=7(x-3y)$$

Hint: $7x-21y$ shares the factor $7$, and after factoring it, the bracket $(x-3y)$ appears — exactly matching the square.
4Extract the common bracket

Replace the first three terms by their square and factor:

$$=(x-3y)^{2}+7(x-3y)$$

Treat $(x-3y)$ as a common factor:

$$=(x-3y)\big[(x-3y)+7\big]$$

Hint: think of it as $A^{2}+7A = A(A+7)$ where $A=x-3y$.
5Simplify and verify

$$=(x-3y)(x-3y+7)$$

Check by expanding: $(x-3y)(x-3y)+7(x-3y)=x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}+7x-21y$ ✓

Hint: whenever you factorize 4 terms, expand mentally to confirm the middle cross-terms come back correctly.
Answer (a) $x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}=(x-3y)^{2}$
(b) $x^{2}-6xy+9y^{2}+7x-21y=(x-3y)(x-3y+7)$
