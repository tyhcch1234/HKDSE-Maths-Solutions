Polynomials — Factorization

Topic 4 · Manipulation & Factorization of Polynomials

Perfect Square Difference of Squares Common Factor Grouping

Question

(a) Factorize \(x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2\).

(b) Factorize \(x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2 + 7x - 21y\).

💡

How to approach this question

Part (a) is a standard perfect square trinomial. Part (b) builds on (a) by adding extra terms — look for a common factor after the first step. Work through the steps below to see the full reasoning.

1

Part (a) — Recognize the pattern

We want to factorize \(x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2\).

Check if it fits the perfect square identity: \(a^2 - 2ab + b^2 = (a - b)^2\).

Apply the identity:

\(x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2 = (x - 3y)^2\)

✓ Part (a) answer

2

Part (b) — Split into two groups

Expression: \(x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2 + 7x - 21y\).

The first three terms form the perfect square from part (a). The last two terms have a common factor \(7\).

Hint: \(7x - 21y = 7(x - 3y)\)

Group and factor:

\(= (x - 3y)^2 + 7(x - 3y)\)

Now both terms share the common factor \((x - 3y)\).

3

Extract the common factor

Take out \((x - 3y)\) as a common factor:

\((x - 3y)^2 + 7(x - 3y) = (x - 3y)\big[(x - 3y) + 7\big]\)

Hint: Treat \((x - 3y)\) like a single unit \(u\)

Simplify the bracket:

\(= (x - 3y)(x - 3y + 7)\)

✓ Fully factorized

FINAL ANSWER

(a) \((x - 3y)^2\)

(b) \((x - 3y)(x - 3y + 7)\)

Quick check

Expand the answer to verify it matches the original expression:

\((x - 3y)(x - 3y + 7)\)

\(= (x - 3y)^2 + 7(x - 3y)\)

\(= x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2 + 7x - 21y\) ✓
