Similar triangles, a right-angled trapezium & the shortest distance from a point to a line

Similar Triangles (AAA) Trapezium Pythagoras' Theorem Area Decomposition Shortest Distance

Q13 The Question

Figure 2 shows a trapezium \(ABCD\) with \(AB /\!/ DC\) and \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\). \(E\) is a point lying on \(BC\) such that \(\angle AED = 90^\circ\).

(a) Prove that \(\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD\). 2 marks

(b) It is given that \(AB = 15\text{ cm}\), \(AE = 25\text{ cm}\) and \(CE = 36\text{ cm}\).

(i) Find the length of \(CD\). 2 marks

(ii) Find the area of \(\triangle ADE\). 2 marks

(iii) Is there a point \(F\) lying on \(AD\) such that the distance between \(E\) and \(F\) is less than \(23\text{ cm}\)? Explain your answer. 2 marks

(a) Prove \(\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD\)

The two right-angled triangles share matching angles — drag the slider to see them side by side.
0 / 4
1Establish two right angles1M

Since \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\) and \(AB /\!/ DC\), the co-interior angles give

\[ \angle DCE = 180^\circ - \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]

So both \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle ECD\) contain a right angle:

\[ \angle ABE = \angle DCE = 90^\circ \]
Hint: \(AB /\!/ DC\) means \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle DCB\) are interior angles on the same side — they sum to \(180^\circ\).
2Use the right angle at \(E\)1M

\(B, E, C\) are collinear and \(\angle AED = 90^\circ\). Splitting the straight angle at \(E\):

\[ \angle AEB + \angle AED + \angle CED = 180^\circ \] \[ \angle AEB + 90^\circ + \angle CED = 180^\circ \implies \angle CED = 90^\circ - \angle AEB \]
Hint: Angles on a straight line sum to \(180^\circ\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
3Show another pair of equal angles1M

In \(\triangle ABE\), the angle sum is \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle BAE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \angle AEB = 90^\circ - \angle AEB \]

Comparing with Step 2 we get the equal pair

\[ \angle BAE = \angle CED \]
Hint: Both angles equal the same quantity \(90^\circ - \angle AEB\), so they are equal.
4Conclude with AAA1A

The third pair follows automatically (angle sum of a triangle):

\[ \angle AEB = \angle CDE \]

Therefore the two triangles are equiangular:

\[ \boxed{\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD \quad (\text{AAA})} \]
Answer: \(\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD\) by the AAA similarity condition (both triangles have angles \(90^\circ,\ \angle AEB,\ 90^\circ-\angle AEB\)).

(b)(i) Find the length of \(CD\)

The full trapezium with all known data plotted to scale. Slide to watch \(CD\) grow with \(CE\).
\(BE\) = 20 cm
\(CE\) = 36 cm
\(CD\) = 48.0 cm
0 / 3
1Find \(BE\) using Pythagoras in \(\triangle ABE\)1M

\(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled at \(B\), with \(AB = 15\) and \(AE = 25\):

\[ BE = \sqrt{AE^2 - AB^2} = \sqrt{25^2 - 15^2} = \sqrt{625 - 225} = \sqrt{400} = 20\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(25\text{–}20\text{–}15\) is a \(5\times(3,4,5)\) Pythagorean triple — a very common HKDSE set!
2Use the similarity ratio1M

From part (a), corresponding sides of \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle ECD\) are proportional. Matching the hypotenuse to hypotenuse and the side opposite the marked angle:

\[ \frac{CD}{BE} = \frac{CE}{AB} \]
Hint: Be careful with the order — \(\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD\) means \(A \leftrightarrow E\), \(B \leftrightarrow C\), \(E \leftrightarrow D\). So \(A\!B \leftrightarrow E\!C\) and \(B\!E \leftrightarrow C\!D\).
3Substitute and solve1A
\[ \frac{CD}{20} = \frac{36}{15} \] \[ CD = \frac{36 \times 20}{15} = 48\text{ cm} \]
Answer: \(CD = 48\text{ cm}\).

(b)(ii) Find the area of \(\triangle ADE\)

\(\triangle ABE\) = 150 cm²

\(\triangle ADE\) = 750 cm²
0 / 3
1Set up the area decomposition1M

The trapezium \(ABCD\) is split into three triangles by the segments \(AE\) and \(DE\):

\[ [ABCD] = [ABE] + [ADE] + [ECD] \]

so

\[ [ADE] = [ABCD] - [ABE] - [ECD] \]
Hint: Using the "whole minus parts" trick avoids needing a height for \(\triangle ADE\) directly.
2Compute each area1M

Trapezium (parallel sides \(AB = 15\), \(DC = 48\), height \(BC = BE + EC = 20 + 36 = 56\)):

\[ [ABCD] = \tfrac{1}{2}(15 + 48)(56) = \tfrac{1}{2}(63)(56) = 1764 \]

Right triangles:

\[ [ABE] = \tfrac{1}{2}(15)(20) = 150, \qquad [ECD] = \tfrac{1}{2}(48)(36) = 864 \]
Hint: \(BC = BE + EC = 20 + 36 = 56\text{ cm}\) is the height of the trapezium (since \(AB /\!/ DC\) and \(\angle B = 90^\circ\)).
3Subtract1A
\[ [ADE] = 1764 - 150 - 864 = 750\text{ cm}^2 \]
Answer: The area of \(\triangle ADE\) is \(750\text{ cm}^2\).

(b)(iii) Is there a point \(F\) on \(AD\) with \(EF < 23\text{ cm}\)?

Drag \(F\) along \(AD\) and watch the live distance \(EF\). The perpendicular foot gives the minimum.
\(AD\) = 65 cm
\(EF\) (current) = — cm
Minimum \(EF\) = 23.077 cm
min > 23 cm
0 / 4
1Find \(AD\)1M

Drop a perpendicular from \(A\) to \(DC\). The horizontal leg is \(DC - AB = 48 - 15 = 33\) and the vertical leg is \(BC = 56\):

\[ AD = \sqrt{BC^2 + (CD - AB)^2} = \sqrt{56^2 + 33^2} = \sqrt{3136 + 1089} = \sqrt{4225} = 65\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(56\text{–}33\text{–}65\) is another Pythagorean triple. Sketch a small right triangle with legs \(56\) and \(33\) to see it.
2Shortest distance = perpendicular distance1M

For any point \(F\) on the segment \(AD\), the distance \(EF\) is smallest when \(EF \perp AD\). Using the area of \(\triangle ADE\) found in (b)(ii):

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}(AD)(EF_{\min}) = [ADE] = 750 \] \[ EF_{\min} = \frac{2(750)}{65} = \frac{1500}{65} = \frac{300}{13} \]
Hint: The "base × height" formula works with any chosen base — here use \(AD\) as base and the perpendicular distance as height.
3Evaluate and compare with 231M
\[ EF_{\min} = \frac{300}{13} \approx 23.07692308\text{ cm} \]

Compare:

\[ \frac{300}{13} > 23 \quad \text{since } 300 > 299 = 13 \times 23 \]
Hint: Avoid rounding too early. Exact comparison: \(300/13 > 23 \iff 300 > 299\).
4Conclude1A

Since the minimum possible value of \(EF\) is \(\dfrac{300}{13} \approx 23.077\text{ cm}\), which is greater than \(23\text{ cm}\),

Answer: No. There is no point \(F\) on \(AD\) with \(EF < 23\text{ cm}\), because the shortest distance from \(E\) to the line \(AD\) is \(\dfrac{300}{13} \approx 23.077\text{ cm} > 23\text{ cm}\).

📌 Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\triangle ABE \sim \triangle ECD\) by AAA: both have a right angle \((\angle ABE = \angle DCE = 90^\circ)\) and \(\angle BAE = \angle CED\) (both \(= 90^\circ - \angle AEB\)).
  • (b)(i) \(BE = 20\text{ cm}\), and \(\dfrac{CD}{BE} = \dfrac{CE}{AB}\) gives \(CD = \dfrac{36 \times 20}{15} = \mathbf{48\text{ cm}}\).
  • (b)(ii) \(BC = 56\text{ cm}\), so \([ABCD] = \tfrac{1}{2}(15+48)(56) = 1764\). Then \([ADE] = 1764 - 150 - 864 = \mathbf{750\text{ cm}^2}\).
  • (b)(iii) \(AD = \sqrt{56^2 + 33^2} = 65\text{ cm}\), so \(EF_{\min} = \dfrac{2(750)}{65} = \dfrac{300}{13} \approx 23.077\text{ cm} > 23\text{ cm}\). No such point \(F\) exists.
