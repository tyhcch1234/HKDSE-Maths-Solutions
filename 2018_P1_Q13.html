Trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB // DC$, $\angle ABC=90^\circ$ and $\angle AED=90^\circ$

Question

[2018 #13]

In Figure 2, $ABCD$ is a trapezium with $AB // DC$ and $\angle ABC=90^\circ$. $E$ is a point lying on $BC$ such that $\angle AED=90^\circ$.

(a) Prove that $\triangle ABE\sim\triangle ECD$. [2 marks]

(b) It is given that $AB=15\text{ cm}$, $AE=25\text{ cm}$ and $CE=36\text{ cm}$.

(i) Find the length of $CD$.
(ii) Find the area of $\triangle ADE$.
(iii) Is there a point $F$ lying on $AD$ such that the distance between $E$ and $F$ is less than $23\text{ cm}$? Explain your answer. [6 marks]

Solution

Step 1 · (a) Angle chase
AAA Similarity Test \(\angle A=\angle A^{\prime},\ \angle B=\angle B^{\prime}\ \Longrightarrow\ \triangle ABC\sim\triangle A^{\prime}B^{\prime}C^{\prime}\) The angle chase gives \(\angle ABE=\angle DCE\) and \(\angle BAE=\angle CED\), so the triangles are similar.

Given $\angle ABC=90^\circ$, and $AB // DC$ (interior angles, $AB // DC$):

$$\angle DCE=180^\circ-\angle ABC=90^\circ$$

So $\angle ABE=\angle DCE$. In $\triangle ABE$,
by the angle sum of a triangle:

$$\angle BAE=180^\circ-90^\circ-\angle AEB=90^\circ-\angle AEB$$

Since $\angle AED=90^\circ$ and $B,E,C$ are collinear (angles on a straight line):

$$\angle CED=180^\circ-90^\circ-\angle AEB=90^\circ-\angle AEB$$

Hence $\angle BAE=\angle CED$,
and therefore the third angles are equal too:

$$\angle AEB=\angle CDE \quad\Rightarrow\quad \triangle ABE\sim\triangle ECD \ \text{(AAA)}$$
Step 2 · (b)(i) Find BE

In the right-angled triangle $ABE$ (right angle at $B$),
Pythagoras' theorem gives

$$BE=\sqrt{AE^2-AB^2}=\sqrt{25^2-15^2}=\sqrt{400}=20\text{ cm}$$
Step 3 · (b)(i) Use similarity for CD
Corresponding Sides of Similar Triangles \(\dfrac{CD}{BE}=\dfrac{CE}{AB}\) Corresponding sides of \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle ECD\) are in the same ratio.

From the similarity in (a), corresponding sides are proportional:

$$\frac{CD}{BE}=\frac{CE}{AB}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{CD}{20}=\frac{36}{15}$$ $$CD=\frac{36\times 20}{15}=48\text{ cm}$$
Step 4 · (b)(ii) Area of △ADE
Area of a Trapezium \(\text{Area}=\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)h\) The area of \(\triangle ADE\) is the trapezium minus the two right-angled triangles.

$BC=BE+CE=20+36=56\text{ cm}$. Subtract the two right triangles from the trapezium:

$$\text{Area}=\tfrac12(AB+CD)(BC)-\tfrac12(AB)(BE)-\tfrac12(CD)(CE)$$ $$=\tfrac12(15+48)(56)-\tfrac12(15)(20)-\tfrac12(48)(36)$$ $$=1764-150-864=750\text{ cm}^2$$
Step 5 · (b)(iii) Length AD

Draw the horizontal through $A$;
its vertical drop is $BC$ and horizontal run is $CD-AB$:

$$AD=\sqrt{BC^2+(CD-AB)^2}=\sqrt{56^2+33^2}=\sqrt{3136+1089}=65\text{ cm}$$
Step 6 · (b)(iii) Shortest distance from E to AD

Let $h$ be the perpendicular distance from $E$ to $AD$. Using $\text{Area}=\tfrac12\cdot AD\cdot h$:

$$h=\frac{2\times 750}{65}=\frac{1500}{65}=\frac{300}{13}\approx 23.077\text{ cm}$$

Since $23.077>23$,
every point $F$ on $AD$ satisfies $EF\ge 23.077\text{ cm}>23\text{ cm}$.

Key idea: the closest point of a segment to a fixed point is given by the perpendicular foot (if it lies within the segment). Here $EF_{\min}=300/13$ cm, which already exceeds $23$ cm.
Answers:
(a) $\triangle ABE\sim\triangle ECD$ (AAA).
(b)(i) $CD=48\text{ cm}$  ·  (ii) Area of $\triangle ADE=750\text{ cm}^2$  ·  (iii) No. The shortest distance from $E$ to $AD$ is $\dfrac{300}{13}\approx23.08\text{ cm}>23\text{ cm}$, so no such point $F$ exists.

Question. In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a trapezium with \(AB // DC\) and \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\). \(E\) lies on \(BC\) such that \(\angle AED=90^{\circ}\). Given \(AB=15\ \text{cm}\), \(AE=25\ \text{cm}\) and \(CE=24\ \text{cm}\):
(a) prove that \(\triangle ABE\sim\triangle ECD\);
(b) find \(CD\);
(c) find the area of \(\triangle ADE\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(\angle ABE=90^{\circ}\) and, since \(AB // DC\), \(\angle DCE=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\). Also \(\angle BAE=90^{\circ}-\angle AEB=\angle CED\) (using \(\angle AED=90^{\circ}\) and \(B,E,C\) collinear),

Step 3. (b) From the similarity \(\dfrac{CD}{BE}=\dfrac{CE}{AB}\), so \(CD=\dfrac{24\times20}{15}=32\ \text{cm}\).
Step 4. (c) \(BC=BE+CE=20+24=44\ \text{cm}\), so the trapezium has area \(\tfrac{1}{2}(15+32)(44)=1034\ \text{cm}^{2}\).
Step 5. (c) Subtract the two right triangles: \(1034-\tfrac{1}{2}(15)(20)-\tfrac{1}{2}(32)(24)=1034-150-384=500\ \text{cm}^{2}\).
