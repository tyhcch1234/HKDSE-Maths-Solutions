Changing the Subject of a Formula

Solve for \(a\) in terms of \(b\).

Algebra Formulas Solution of Equations

If \(\dfrac{a+4b}{2a} = 2 + \dfrac{b}{a}\) , then \(a =\)

A. \(\dfrac{2b}{3}\)
B. \(\dfrac{3b}{2}\)
C. \(\dfrac{5b}{6}\)
D. \(\dfrac{6b}{5}\)

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Write down the given equation

\[ \frac{a+4b}{2a} = 2 + \frac{b}{a} \]
Hint

Our goal is to isolate \(a\) on one side of the equation.

2

Multiply both sides by \(2a\)

To eliminate the denominators, multiply every term by \(2a\) (assuming \(a \neq 0\)):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{a+4b}{2a} \cdot 2a &= \left(2 + \frac{b}{a}\right) \cdot 2a \\[4pt] a + 4b &= 4a + 2b \end{aligned} \]
Hint

Distributive law: \((2 + \frac{b}{a}) \cdot 2a = 4a + 2b\).

3

Collect like terms

Move all terms containing \(a\) to one side and the rest to the other:

\[ \begin{aligned} a + 4b &= 4a + 2b \\ -3a &= -2b \end{aligned} \]
Hint

Subtract \(4a\) from both sides, then subtract \(4b\) from both sides.

4

Divide by coefficient of \(a\)

Divide both sides by \(-3\):

\[ \begin{aligned} -3a &= -2b \\ a &= \frac{-2b}{-3} = \frac{2b}{3} \end{aligned} \]

Final answer: \(a = \dfrac{2b}{3}\) (Option A)
