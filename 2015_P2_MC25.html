Coordinate Geometry — Straight lines, intercepts & signs of unknowns

Straight Lines x-intercept / y-intercept Inequalities Multiple Choice

QQuestion

Full question text with marks.

In the figure, the equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \[ ax = 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad bx + cy = 1 \] respectively. Which of the following are true?

  1. \(a < 0\)
  2. \(a < b\)
  3. \(c > 0\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

From the figure: \(L_1\) is vertical, to the left of the \(y\)-axis. \(L_2\) has a negative \(x\)-intercept (smaller than that of \(L_1\)) and a positive \(y\)-intercept, with a positive slope.

Original Figure

aAnalysis of \(L_1:\; ax = 1\)

Show that \(a < 0\) (Statement I is true).

a = -1.50 x-intercept of L₁ = 1/a = -0.667 Sign: negative
Step 1

For the line \(L_1:\; ax = 1\), solve for \(x\):

\[ x = \frac{1}{a} \]
The \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\) is exactly \(1/a\), because \(ax=1\) is a vertical line.
Step 2

From the figure, \(L_1\) lies to the left of the \(y\)-axis, so its \(x\)-intercept is negative:

\[ \frac{1}{a} < 0 \]
A fraction is negative only when its numerator and denominator have opposite signs. Since \(1>0\), the denominator \(a\) must be negative.
\(a < 0\), therefore Statement I is true.
0 / 2

bComparing \(a\) and \(b\)

Show that \(a > b\) so that Statement II (\(a < b\)) is false.

a = -1.50 b = -0.60 1/a = -0.667 1/b = -1.667 order: 1/b < 1/a ? yes
Step 1

For \(L_2:\; bx+cy=1\), set \(y=0\) to find its \(x\)-intercept:

\[ bx = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{1}{b} \]
The \(x\)-intercept of a line is found by putting \(y=0\).
Step 2

From the figure, the \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\) is smaller than that of \(L_2\) (i.e. \(L_1\) is further left):

\[ \frac{1}{a} < \frac{1}{b} \]
Careful! With negative denominators, taking reciprocals reverses the inequality direction. Multiply both sides by \(ab\) — which is positive since both \(a,b<0\).
Step 3

Since \(a<0\) and \(b<0\), the product \(ab > 0\). Multiplying the inequality by the positive number \(ab\) preserves the direction:

\[ \frac{1}{a}\cdot ab < \frac{1}{b}\cdot ab \] \[ b < a \]
Equivalently \(a > b\), so Statement II “\(a < b\)” is false.
Statement II is false. (The correct relation is \(a > b\).)
0 / 3

cSign of \(c\)

Show that \(c > 0\) so that Statement III is true.

b = -0.60 c = 1.20 y-intercept = 1/c = 0.833 above origin? yes
Step 1

For \(L_2:\; bx+cy=1\), set \(x=0\) to find its \(y\)-intercept:

\[ cy = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; y = \frac{1}{c} \]
The \(y\)-intercept of a line is found by putting \(x=0\).
Step 2

From the figure, \(L_2\) cuts the \(y\)-axis above the origin, so its \(y\)-intercept is positive:

\[ \frac{1}{c} > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; c > 0 \]
A fraction \(\tfrac{1}{c}\) is positive exactly when \(c\) is positive (since \(1>0\)).
\(c > 0\), therefore Statement III is true.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • From \(L_1:\;ax=1\), its \(x\)-intercept is \(\dfrac{1}{a}<0\;\Rightarrow\;\) \(a<0\) I true
  • From \(L_2:\;bx+cy=1\) with \(y=0\), its \(x\)-intercept is \(\dfrac{1}{b}\). Since \(\dfrac{1}{a}<\dfrac{1}{b}\) and \(ab>0\), \(a>b\) — so \(a<b\) is false. II false
  • From \(L_2:\;bx+cy=1\) with \(x=0\), its \(y\)-intercept is \(\dfrac{1}{c}>0\;\Rightarrow\;\) \(c>0\) III true
  • True statements: I and III only ⟹ correct option B.
