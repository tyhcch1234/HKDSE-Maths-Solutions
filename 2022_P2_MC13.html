Topic: Rate, Ratio & Variations

Variations Direct & Inverse Non-zero constant Multiple Choice

Question

If $u$ varies directly as the square root of $v$ and inversely as $w$, which of the following are true?

I. $u^{2}$ varies directly as $v$ and inversely as the square of $w$.

II. $v$ varies directly as $w$ and inversely as the square root of $u$.

III. $w$ varies directly as the square root of $v$ and inversely as $u$.

A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

💡 Key Idea: Turn Each Sentence into an Equation

Direct variation means the ratio is constant: “$a$ varies directly as $b$”  →  $a = kb$.

Inverse variation means the product is constant: “$a$ varies inversely as $b$”  →  $a = \dfrac{k}{b}$.

So the first sentence of the question becomes:

$u = k\,\dfrac{\sqrt{v}}{w}$ ,  where $k$ is a non-zero constant.

Our job: rewrite this one equation into the forms claimed by I, II and III, and check whether the “constant” really stays constant.

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Translate the given information into an equation

“$u$ varies directly as $\sqrt{v}$” gives $u \propto \sqrt{v}$. “Inversely as $w$” gives $u \propto \dfrac{1}{w}$.

Combining both with a single constant $k$:

$$u = \frac{k\sqrt{v}}{w}, \qquad k \neq 0$$

🔎 Hint: Why must $k$ be non-zero?

If $k=0$, then $u=0$ always, and "$u$ varies as ..." becomes meaningless. A variation constant is always assumed non-zero unless stated otherwise.

2

Check statement I

Claim: “$u^{2}$ varies directly as $v$ and inversely as the square of $w$.”

Square both sides of $u = \dfrac{k\sqrt{v}}{w}$:

$$u^{2} = \frac{k^{2}\,v}{w^{2}}$$

This is exactly of the form $\dfrac{v}{w^{2}}$ multiplied by a constant $k^{2}$.

✅ Statement I is TRUE.

3

Check statement II

Claim: “$v$ varies directly as $w$ and inversely as the square root of $u$.” That would mean $\dfrac{v\sqrt{u}}{w}$ is constant.

Rearrange the original equation to make $v$ the subject:

$$v = \frac{u^{2}w^{2}}{k^{2}}$$

Now test the claimed constant:

$$\frac{v\sqrt{u}}{w} = \frac{u^{2}w^{2}}{k^{2}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{u}}{w} = \frac{u^{5/2}\,w}{k^{2}}$$

This expression still contains $u$ and $w$, so it is not constant.

❌ Statement II is FALSE.

🔎 Hint: A quick “power” way to check

From $u \propto \dfrac{v^{1/2}}{w}$ we get $v^{1/2} \propto uw$, so $v \propto u^{2}w^{2}$. Comparing with the claim $v \propto \dfrac{w}{\sqrt{u}}$: the power of $u$ is $2$ but the claim needs $-1/2$. Mismatch ⇒ false.

4

Check statement III

Claim: “$w$ varies directly as the square root of $v$ and inversely as $u$.” That would mean $\dfrac{w u}{\sqrt{v}}$ is constant.

From $u = \dfrac{k\sqrt{v}}{w}$, multiply both sides by $w$ and divide by $\sqrt{v}$:

$$\frac{w\,u}{\sqrt{v}} = k$$

Since $k$ is a non-zero constant, the claim holds.

✅ Statement III is TRUE.

5

Conclusion

I. TRUE
II. FALSE
III. TRUE

Only I and III are true.

Answer: B

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Always write the given variation as a single equation first — never try to verify statements by guessing.
  • “Varies directly as $X$ and inversely as $Y$” means the expression $\dfrac{X}{Y}$ multiplied by a constant.
  • To verify a claim, rearrange the original equation and see whether the claimed combination equals a constant.
  • Remember $k \neq 0$; if the constant turns out to depend on a variable, the claim is false.
