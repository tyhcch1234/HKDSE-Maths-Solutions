Question. The figure below consists of eight identical squares. The number of folds of rotational symmetry of the figure is
A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8
STEP 1 · UNDERSTAND THE FIGURE

The figure is built from 8 congruent squares joined edge-to-edge. There is a central \(2\times2\) block of squares, with 4 more squares attached to the four "outer" sides, so the whole shape is a rotated \(3\times3\)-like cross arrangement.

The whole figure is invariant under a rotation of \(90^\circ\) about its centre \(O\), so we look for the largest \(n\) with \(360^\circ/n\) a symmetry.

Hints: a rotational symmetry must map the figure exactly onto itself — every square onto a square, every corner onto a corner.
STEP 2 · LOCATE THE CENTRE

The only possible centre of rotational symmetry is the centre \(O\) of the figure (the shared vertex of the central \(2\times2\) block). No other point leaves the figure unchanged, because the figure is not a disc.

Around \(O\) the 8 squares lie in the directions \(0^\circ,45^\circ,90^\circ,\dots,315^\circ\) — but with a pattern that repeats every \(90^\circ\).

STEP 3 · TEST A 90° ROTATION

Rotate the figure \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise about \(O\). Check that each square lands on another square of the figure:

\[ \text{central block}\;\xrightarrow{\;90^\circ\;}\;\text{central block}, \qquad \text{outer arm}\;\xrightarrow{\;90^\circ\;}\;\text{next outer arm}. \]

Every square maps onto a square of the same type, so \(90^\circ\) is a symmetry of the figure.

Rotational Symmetry \(\text{the figure maps onto itself under a rotation of }\theta\text{ about }O\) A \\(90^\circ\\) turn carries the central block onto itself and each outer arm onto the next one, so \\(90^\circ\\) is a symmetry.
STEP 4 · FIND ALL SYMMETRIC ANGLES

If \(90^\circ\) works, then so do its multiples:

\[ 90^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 270^\circ,\ 360^\circ. \]

Now test a smaller angle. A rotation of \(45^\circ\) about \(O\) would carry an outer arm onto the empty diagonal gap — there is no square there. So \(45^\circ\) is not a symmetry.

Use the red reference square \(R\): under \(45^\circ\) it would move onto blank space, so it can't be a symmetry.
Multiples of a Symmetry Angle \(\theta\ \text{is a symmetry}\ \Longrightarrow\ 2\theta,\ 3\theta,\ \ldots\ \text{are symmetries too}\) Since \\(90^\circ\\) works, \\(180^\circ\\) and \\(270^\circ\\) also work, while \\(45^\circ\\) does not (an arm would land on empty space).
STEP 5 · COUNT THE FOLDS

The symmetric rotations are exactly the multiples of \(90^\circ\):

\[ \{90^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 270^\circ,\ 360^\circ\}, \]

i.e. there are

\[ \frac{360^\circ}{90^\circ}=4 \]

rotational symmetries. In the exam's wording, "the number of folds of rotational symmetry" is the order of the rotation group, \(n=4\).

Therefore the answer is B · 4.

Question. A logo is made of three congruent rectangles arranged about a common centre at intervals of \\(120^\circ\\). Find the order of its rotational symmetry and state the smallest angle of rotation that maps the logo onto itself.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The pattern of rectangles repeats every \\(120^\circ\\) about the centre.
Step 2. A rotation of \\(120^\circ\\) carries each rectangle onto the next one, so \\(120^\circ\\) is a symmetry.
Step 3. A rotation of \\(60^\circ\\) would move a rectangle onto empty space, so \\(60^\circ\\) and any smaller non-zero angle are not symmetries. Hence \\(\\theta=120^\\circ\\).
Step 4. Order \\(=\\dfrac{360^\circ}{120^\circ}=3\\).
Answer: order \\(3\\), smallest angle \\(120^\circ\)
Order of Rotational Symmetry \(n=\dfrac{360^\circ}{\theta}\) With the smallest symmetry angle \\(90^\circ\\), the order is \\(\\dfrac{360^\\circ}{90^\\circ}=4\\).
