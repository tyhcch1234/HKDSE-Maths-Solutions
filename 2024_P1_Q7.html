Question

In a polar coordinate system, \(O\) is the pole. The polar coordinates of the points \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are \(\left(11,59^{\circ}\right)\), \(\left(60,149^{\circ}\right)\) and \(\left(144,239^{\circ}\right)\) respectively.

  1. Find \(\angle POQ\).
  2. Are \(P\), \(O\) and \(R\) collinear? Explain your answer.
  3. Find the perimeter of \(\triangle PQR\). (4 marks)
STEP 1 · Part (a) — angle at O

\[ \angle POQ = 149^{\circ} - 59^{\circ} = 90^{\circ}. \]

Both angles are measured from the initial line, so subtract the smaller from the larger.
STEP 2 · Part (b) — collinearity
Collinear Points on Opposite Rays \(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}=180^{\circ}\) The arguments are \(239^{\circ}\) and \(59^{\circ}\), and \(239^{\circ}-59^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\), so \(O\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\).

The points \(P\) and \(R\) lie at angles \(59^{\circ}\) and \(239^{\circ}\) with the initial line,
and

\[ 239^{\circ} - 59^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}. \]

So \(OP\) and \(OR\) lie on the same straight line through \(O\) in opposite directions.
Hence \(P\), \(O\) and \(R\) are collinear (with \(O\) between \(P\) and \(R\)).

Equal magnitude of argument differing by \(180^{\circ}\) ⟹ opposite rays from the pole.
STEP 3 · Part (c) — side \(PQ\)
Pythagoras' Theorem \(PQ^{2}=OP^{2}+OQ^{2}\) With \(\angle POQ=90^{\circ}\), \(PQ=\sqrt{11^{2}+60^{2}}=\sqrt{3721}=61\).

Since \(\angle POQ = 90^{\circ}\), \(\triangle POQ\) is right-angled at \(O\):

\[ PQ = \sqrt{11^{2}+60^{2}} = \sqrt{121+3600} = \sqrt{3721} = 61. \]

STEP 4 · Part (c) — side \(QR\)

\[ \angle QOR = 239^{\circ} - 149^{\circ} = 90^{\circ}, \]

so \(\triangle QOR\) is right-angled at \(O\):

\[ QR = \sqrt{60^{2}+144^{2}} = \sqrt{3600+20736} = \sqrt{24336} = 156. \]

STEP 5 · Part (c) — side \(PR\) and the perimeter
Perimeter of a Triangle \(\text{perimeter}=PQ+QR+PR\) Since \(O\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\), \(PR=11+144=155\), so the perimeter is \(61+156+155\).

From (b), \(O\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\), so

\[ PR = OP + OR = 11 + 144 = 155. \]

The perimeter of \(\triangle PQR\) is

\[ PQ + QR + PR = 61 + 156 + 155 = 372. \]

Final Answer
(a) \(90^{\circ}\)  |  (b) Yes — \(239^{\circ}-59^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\), so \(P,O,R\) lie on one straight line  |  (c) \(372\)

Question. In the polar coordinate plane, \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) have polar coordinates \((8,25^{\circ})\), \((15,115^{\circ})\) and \((20,205^{\circ})\) respectively.
(a) Find \(\angle POQ\).
(b) Show that \(P\), \(O\) and \(R\) are collinear.
(c) Find the perimeter of \(\triangle PQR\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(\angle POQ=115^{\circ}-25^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\).
Step 2. (b) The arguments of \(P\) and \(R\) differ by \(205^{\circ}-25^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\), so \(OP\) and \(OR\) are opposite rays and \(P\), \(O\), \(R\) are collinear with \(O\) between them.
Step 3. (c) \(\triangle POQ\) is right-angled at \(O\), so \(PQ=\sqrt{8^{2}+15^{2}}=\sqrt{289}=17\).
Step 4. \(\angle QOR=205^{\circ}-115^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\), so \(QR=\sqrt{15^{2}+20^{2}}=\sqrt{625}=25\).
Step 5. From (b), \(PR=8+20=28\), so the perimeter is \(17+25+28=70\).
Answer: (a) \(90^{\circ}\); (b) their arguments differ by \(180^{\circ}\); (c) \(70\)
