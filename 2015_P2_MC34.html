HKDSE Math 2015 Paper 2 Q34 Quadratic Equations

Find $\alpha^2 + \beta^2$ from the Roots

Let $k$ be a constant. If the roots of the quadratic equation $x^2 + kx - 2 = 0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = ?$

The Question

Let $k$ be a constant. If the roots of the quadratic equation $x^2 + kx - 2 = 0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = ?$

A. $k^2 + 4$
B. $k^2$
C. $(k-2)^2$
D. $k^2 - 4$
Progress Step 0 of 4
1

Sum and Product of Roots

For a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), the sum and product of roots are:

\(\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{b}{a}\)

\(\alpha\beta = \dfrac{c}{a}\)

Here, \(a = 1\), \(b = k\), \(c = -2\).

Sum of roots:

\(\alpha + \beta = -k\)

Product of roots:

\(\alpha\beta = -2\)

Hint: Remember the standard form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\). Identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) carefully – signs matter!

2

Algebraic Identity

We need \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2\). Use the identity:

\(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta\)

This is derived from \((\alpha + \beta)^2 = \alpha^2 + 2\alpha\beta + \beta^2\).

Hint: This identity is a standard trick to express \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2\) in terms of the sum and product of roots.

3

Substitute the Values

Substitute \(\alpha + \beta = -k\) and \(\alpha\beta = -2\) into the identity:

\(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (-k)^2 - 2(-2)\)

Watch the signs! \((-k)^2 = k^2\) and \(-2(-2) = +4\).

Hint: A common mistake is writing \((-k)^2\) as \(-k^2\). Squaring a negative gives a positive.

4

Simplify & Answer

Simplify the expression:

\(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = k^2 + 4\)

Therefore, the correct answer is B. \(k^2 + 4\).

Final check: If \(k = 0\), the equation is \(x^2 - 2 = 0\), roots are \(\pm\sqrt{2}\), then \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 2 + 2 = 4\), and \(k^2 + 4 = 0 + 4 = 4\).
