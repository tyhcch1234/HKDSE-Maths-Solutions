Question

The figure shows a regular tetrahedron ABCD. Find the angle between the plane ABC and the plane BCD correct to the nearest degree.

A.  48°
B.  53°
C.  60°
D.  71°
Step 1 · Recognise the angle between two planes
Angle between Two Intersecting Planes \(\theta=\angle AMD\quad\text{with }AM\perp BC,\ DM\perp BC\) The planes \(ABC\) and \(BCD\) meet along \(BC\), so the perpendiculars from \(M\) in each face give the required angle.

The angle between two planes is the angle between the two lines that lie in the planes and are perpendicular to their line of intersection.

The two planes are \(ABC\) and \(BCD\),
so the line of intersection is \(BC\).

In a regular tetrahedron every face is an equilateral triangle,
so the perpendicular from the apex to a side passes through the mid-point of that side.

Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(BC\). Then \(AM \perp BC\) and \(DM \perp BC\).

💡 The required angle is \(\angle AMD\).
Step 2 · Set the side length and find AM, DM
Median of an Equilateral Triangle \(AM=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l\) Each face is equilateral with edge \(l\), so the altitude to the mid-point \(M\) of \(BC\) is \(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}l\).

Let each edge of the regular tetrahedron be \(l\).

Triangle \(ABC\) is equilateral with side \(l\),
and \(M\) is the mid-point of \(BC\), so \(AM\) is a median:

\[AM=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l.\]

Similarly,
in face \(BCD\): \[DM=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l.\]

💡 Note \(AB=AC=BD=CD=AD=l\) and \(BM=MC=\dfrac{l}{2}\).
Step 3 · Apply the cosine formula in △AMD
Cosine Formula \(\cos C=\dfrac{a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}}{2ab}\) In \(\triangle AMD\) the sides \(AM\), \(DM\) and \(AD=l\) are all known, so the angle at \(M\) follows at once.

In triangle \(AMD\) the three sides are \(AM=DM=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\) and \(AD=l\). By the cosine formula:

\[\cos\angle AMD=\frac{AM^{2}+DM^{2}-AD^{2}}{2\cdot AM\cdot DM}\]

\[=\frac{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\right)^{2}-l^{2}}{2\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l}\]

Step 4 · Simplify

Numerator: \(\dfrac{3}{4}l^{2}+\dfrac{3}{4}l^{2}-l^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}l^{2}\).

Denominator: \(2\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l=\dfrac{3}{2}l^{2}\).

\[\cos\angle AMD=\frac{\tfrac12 l^{2}}{\tfrac32 l^{2}}=\frac{1}{3}.\]

Step 5 · Find the angle

\[\angle AMD=\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac13\right)\]

\[\angle AMD\approx 70.52877937^\circ\]

\[\angle AMD\approx 71^\circ \text{ (nearest degree)}\]

Answer: D. 71°
💡 A useful check: for a regular tetrahedron the dihedral angle is always \(\cos^{-1}(1/3)\approx 71^\circ\), independent of edge length.

Question. \(ABCD\) is a regular tetrahedron with edge \(10\text{ cm}\). \(M\) is the mid-point of \(BC\).
(a) Find \(AM\).
(b) Find the angle between the planes \(ABC\) and \(BCD\), correct to \(1\) decimal place.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Face \(ABC\) is equilateral with side \(10\text{ cm}\), so the median \(AM=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(10)=5\sqrt3\approx8.66\text{ cm}\). Similarly \(DM=5\sqrt3\text{ cm}\).
Step 2. The required angle is \(\angle AMD\), with \(AD=10\text{ cm}\). By the cosine formula, \(\cos\angle AMD=\dfrac{AM^{2}+DM^{2}-AD^{2}}{2\cdot AM\cdot DM}\).
Step 3. \(AM^{2}=DM^{2}=75\) and \(AD^{2}=100\), so \(\cos\angle AMD=\dfrac{75+75-100}{2(75)}=\dfrac{50}{150}=\dfrac13\).
Step 4. \(\angle AMD=\cos^{-1}\dfrac13\approx70.5^{\circ}\).
Step 5. So the angle between the two planes is \(70.5^{\circ}\) (correct to \(1\) decimal place).
Answer: (a) \(AM=5\sqrt3\approx8.66\text{ cm}\) (b) \(70.5^{\circ}\)
