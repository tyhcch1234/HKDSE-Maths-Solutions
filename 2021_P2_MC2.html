Formulas and Sequences — Making a the Subject

Rearranging a formula in two variables, then choosing the matching option.

Question

If  $m(m-a)=a(1-m)$ , then  $a=$

  • A. $m$
  • B. $2m$
  • C. $m^2$
  • D. $\dfrac{m^2+m}{2}$

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

1
Expand both sides
▼

Remove the brackets on each side. On the left, distribute $m$; on the right, distribute $a$.

$m(m-a) = a(1-m)$
$m^2 - ma = a - am$
Hint — Distributive Law: $x(y+z)=xy+xz$. Here $m\cdot m = m^2$ and $m\cdot(-a) = -ma$; also $a\cdot 1 = a$ and $a\cdot(-m) = -am$.
2
Notice that $-ma$ and $-am$ are the same term
▼

Multiplication is commutative, so $ma = am$. That means $-ma$ and $-am$ are identical terms — they will cancel.

$m^2 - ma = a - am \;\Longrightarrow\; m^2 - ma = a - ma$

Add $ma$ to both sides to eliminate it:

$m^2 = a$
Hint: $-ma + ma = 0$. Any quantity plus its own negative is zero. This is the key cancellation in this question.
3
General method: collect all $a$ terms on one side
▼

If the cancellation were not obvious, we could always isolate $a$ systematically.

$m^2 - ma = a - am$

Move every term containing $a$ to the right-hand side:

$m^2 = a - am + ma$

Since $-am + ma = 0$ (they are identical terms with opposite signs):

$m^2 = a$
General strategy: Group all terms with the unknown on one side, all other terms on the other side, then factorise the unknown out if needed. Here no factorisation is required because the $a$-terms cancel completely.
4
Verify the result numerically
▼

Substitute a concrete value to double-check. Take $m = 3$, so $a = m^2 = 9$.

LHS $= 3(3-9) = 3(-6) = -18$
RHS $= 9(1-3) = 9(-2) = -18$ ✓
Why verify? In multiple-choice questions, one quick numerical check can eliminate wrong options instantly. Try $m=2 \Rightarrow a=4$: LHS $=2(2-4)=-4$, RHS $=4(1-2)=-4$ ✓.
5
Answer and how the traps are built
▼

The correct option is C. $m^2$

$a = m^2$

Why the other options attract students:

• A ($m$): if you forget that $m\cdot m = m^2$ and stop at "$m = a$".

• B ($2m$): if you wrongly "combine" $m^2$ and $m$ as like terms.

• D ($\frac{m^2+m}{2}$): if you mistakenly keep the $-ma$ and $+am$ terms instead of cancelling them, then divide by $2$.

Exam tip: Always check whether a term is exactly cancelled (like $-ma$ with $+ma$) before doing any division or factorising — it saves a lot of time in Paper 2.
