[2012MC#3]  ·  4. Polynomials
Question. If $p$ and $q$ are constants such that \\[ x^{2}+p \\equiv (x+2)(x+q)+10, \\] then $p=$
  • A.$-4$
  • B.$-2$
  • C.$6$
  • D.$10$
Step 1Read the given identity
\\[ x^{2}+p \\equiv (x+2)(x+q)+10 \\] The LHS has no $x$ term, so the $x$-coefficient on the RHS must be $0$.
Equating coefficients works; substituting a clever value of $x$ works even faster.
💡 An identity (≡) is true for every value of $x$ — so we may choose any convenient $x$.
Step 2Method 1 — substitute the root $x=-2$
Choosing $x=-2$ makes the factor $(x+2)$ vanish: \\[ (-2)^{2}+p = [(-2)+2]\\,[(-2)+q]+10 \\] \\[ 4+p = (0)(q-2)+10 = 10 \\] \\[ p = 10-4 = 6 \\]
💡 The whole product term contains the factor $(x+2)$, so $x=-2$ kills it — only the constant $10$ survives.
Step 3Method 2 — expand and compare coefficients
\\[ x^{2}+p \\equiv (x+2)(x+q)+10 \\] \\[ \\equiv [\\,x^{2}+(q+2)x+2q\\,]+10 \\] \\[ \\equiv x^{2}+(q+2)x+(2q+10) \\]
💡 Expand the bracket first, then collect the constant terms together.
Step 4Compare the $x$ term → find $q$
LHS $x$-coefficient $=0$, RHS $x$-coefficient $=q+2$: \\[ 0 = q+2 \\quad\\Longrightarrow\\quad q = -2 \\]
💡 This is why $x=-2$ was the smart substitution — it is exactly the value making $q+2=0$.
Step 5Compare the constant term → find $p$
LHS constant $=p$, RHS constant $=2q+10$: \\[ p = 2q+10 = 2(-2)+10 \\] \\[ p = -4+10 = 6 \\]
💡 Check: $x^{2}+6 \\equiv (x+2)(x-2)+10 = x^{2}-4+10 = x^{2}+6$ ✓
Answer $p = 6$  →  Option C
