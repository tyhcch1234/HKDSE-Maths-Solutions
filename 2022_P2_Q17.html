HKDSE Math 2022 Paper 2 Q17 · Mensuration — Areas of Triangles with a Common Height

Figure: P–Q–R with M on PQ and N on QR, PM : MQ = 5 : 6, QN : NR = 3 : 4
[2022MC#17]

In the figure, M and N are points lying on PQ and QR respectively such that \(PM : MQ = 5 : 6\) and \(QN : NR = 3 : 4\). If the area of the quadrilateral \(MNRP\) is \(59\text{ cm}^2\), then the area of \(\Delta MNQ\) is

A. \(17\text{ cm}^2\)
B. \(18\text{ cm}^2\)
C. \(19\text{ cm}^2\)
D. \(20\text{ cm}^2\)
Step 1 · Set up the unknown

Let \(x\text{ cm}^2\) be the area of \(\Delta MNQ\), the target quantity.

Our plan: express the area of the large triangle \(\Delta PQR\) in terms of \(x\), then use the given area of the quadrilateral to form an equation.

Key idea: two triangles sharing the same height have areas in the ratio of their bases.
Step 2 · Compare \(\Delta NPQ\) and \(\Delta MNQ\) (common height from \(N\) to line \(PQ\))

Both triangles have their base on line \(PQ\), with the same perpendicular height from \(N\). Hence their areas are in the ratio of the bases:

$$\frac{[\Delta NPQ]}{[\Delta MNQ]}=\frac{PQ}{MQ}=\frac{5+6}{6}=\frac{11}{6}$$ $$\therefore\ [\Delta NPQ]=\frac{11}{6}x\ \text{cm}^2$$
Areas of Triangles with Equal Heights \(\dfrac{\text{area of }NPQ}{\text{area of }MNQ}=\dfrac{PQ}{MQ}\) Both triangles have base on \(PQ\) and the same height from \(N\): \(\dfrac{PQ}{MQ}=\dfrac{5+6}{6}=\dfrac{11}{6}\).
Step 3 · Compare \(\Delta PQR\) and \(\Delta NPQ\) (common height from \(P\) to line \(QR\))

Both triangles have their base on line \(QR\) with the same perpendicular height from \(P\):

$$\frac{[\Delta PQR]}{[\Delta NPQ]}=\frac{QR}{QN}=\frac{QN+NR}{QN}=\frac{3+4}{3}=\frac{7}{3}$$

So

$$[\Delta PQR]=\frac{11}{6}x\times\frac{7}{3}=\frac{77}{18}x\ \text{cm}^2$$
Areas of Triangles Sharing a Height \(\dfrac{\text{area of }PQR}{\text{area of }NPQ}=\dfrac{QR}{QN}\) Both have base on \(QR\) and the same height from \(P\): \(\dfrac{QN+NR}{QN}=\dfrac{3+4}{3}=\dfrac{7}{3}\).
Step 4 · Relate the quadrilateral to \(\Delta PQR\) and \(\Delta MNQ\)

The quadrilateral \(MNRP\) is the part of \(\Delta PQR\) left after removing the small triangle \(\Delta MNQ\):

$$[\ MNRP\ ]=[\Delta PQR]-[\Delta MNQ]=\frac{77}{18}x-x=\frac{59}{18}x\ \text{cm}^2$$
Step 5 · Solve for \(x\)

Given that the area of \(MNRP\) is \(59\text{ cm}^2\):

$$\frac{59}{18}x=59 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=18$$

Therefore the area of \(\Delta MNQ\) is \(18\text{ cm}^2\), i.e. answer B.

Check: with \(x=18\), \(\Delta NPQ = 33\) and \(\Delta PQR = 77\), so the quadrilateral is \(77-18 = 59\ \text{cm}^2\). ✓
Area of \(\Delta MNQ = \) \(18\ \text{cm}^2\)  (B)

Question. In \(\triangle PQR\), \(M\) lies on \(PQ\) and \(N\) lies on \(QR\) with \(PM:MQ=2:3\) and \(QN:NR=1:2\). If the area of quadrilateral \(MNRP\) is \(80\text{ cm}^{2}\), find the area of \(\triangle MNQ\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(\text{area of }MNQ=x\). \(\dfrac{\text{area of }NPQ}{x}=\dfrac{PQ}{MQ}=\dfrac{5}{3}\), so \(\text{area of }NPQ=\dfrac{5}{3}x\).
Step 2. \(\dfrac{\text{area of }PQR}{\text{area of }NPQ}=\dfrac{QR}{QN}=\dfrac{3}{1}\), so \(\text{area of }PQR=5x\).
Step 3. \(\text{area of }MNRP=5x-x=4x=80\).
Step 4. \(x=20\), i.e. \(\text{area of }MNQ=20\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Answer: \(20\ \text{cm}^{2}\)

Method: split \(\Delta PQR\) by introducing \(MN\) and use the common-height area ratio twice.
