Statistics — the weighted mean of two groups
Statistics Weighted Mean Total = Mean × Number Multiple Choice

The QuestionMC

The mean weight of \(60\) actors and \(40\) actresses is \(57\text{ kg}\). If the mean weight of the actors is \(63\text{ kg}\), then the mean weight of the actresses is
  1. A. \(48\text{ kg}\)
  2. B. \(50\text{ kg}\)
  3. C. \(53\text{ kg}\)
  4. D. \(60\text{ kg}\)

Part 1 — See the whole picture

Every mean is really a total divided by a count. So the sum of the weights equals \((\text{mean}) \times (\text{number of people})\). Drag the slider to explore what the combined mean would be for a trial actress mean, and see where the true value \(48\) sits.

Notice: the combined mean only equals \(57\) when the actresses' total makes the grand total exactly \(5700\) kg.

Part 2 — Full solution

Reveal the reasoning one step at a time.

Step 11M
Let \(x\) kg be the mean weight of the actresses (the unknown we want).
Hint: In any "mean" problem, first name the unknown. Because the question asks for the actresses' mean, that is our \(x\).
Step 21M
Convert each mean into a total: \(\text{Total} = \text{mean} \times \text{number of people}\).
\[ \text{Total of actors} = 63 \times 60 = 3780 \text{ kg} \]
\[ \text{Total of actresses} = x \times 40 = 40x \text{ kg} \]
Hint: Never average the two means directly (\( \tfrac{63+x}{2} \)). The two groups are different sizes (\(60\) vs \(40\)), so you must work with totals.
Step 31M
The combined group has \(60+40=100\) people with mean \(57\) kg, so the grand total is
\[ 57 \times (60+40) = 5700 \text{ kg}. \]
Setting the two ways of computing the grand total equal:
\[ 63 \cdot 60 + 40x = 57 \cdot (60+40) \]
Step 41A
Solve for \(x\):
\[ 3780 + 40x = 5700 \]
\[ 40x = 5700 - 3780 = 1920 \]
\[ x = \frac{1920}{40} = 48 \]
Hint: A quick check by reasoning: the overall mean \(57\) is below the actors' mean \(63\), so it must be above the actresses' mean. Options C (\(53\)) and D (\(60\)) are too big / above \(57\) and too close — \(48\) is the only sensible value.
\(\boxed{\text{The mean weight of the actresses is } 48\text{ kg.}}\)  (Option A)

Part 3 — The "see-saw" idea

The combined mean \(57\) must lie between the two group means, and it sits closer to the bigger group (\(60\) people) — like a see-saw balance point. Drag the actuator mean to see the balance point shift.

Actress mean: 48 kg
Group sizes: \(n_1=60,\;n_2=40\) Weighted mean = 57.0 kg Weights 60 : 40 = 3 : 2
Balance formula: \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{n_1\bar{x}_1 + n_2\bar{x}_2}{n_1+n_2} = \dfrac{60(63) + 40(48)}{100} = 57\). The \(3:2\) ratio of group sizes is why \(57\) sits at \(\tfrac{2}{5}\) of the way from \(63\) down to \(48\).

Summary of Answers

  • Let \(x\) kg = mean weight of the actresses.
  • Actors' total: \(63 \times 60 = 3780\) kg.
  • Actresses' total: \(40x\) kg.
  • Grand total: \(57 \times 100 = 5700\) kg.
  • Equation: \(63 \cdot 60 + 40x = 57 \cdot (60+40)\).
  • Solve: \(40x = 1920 \Rightarrow \mathbf{x = 48}\).
  • Answer: A (48 kg)
