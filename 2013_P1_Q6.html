HKDSE 2013 · Paper 1 · Q6Polar & Symmetry

Pole O / polar axis Point A (26, 10°) Point B (26, 130°) Segment AB Axis L (70°) Midpoint M (13, 70°)
QUESTION

In a polar coordinate system, \(O\) is the pole. The polar coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((26,\,10^\circ)\) and \((26,\,130^\circ)\) respectively. Let \(L\) be the axis of reflectional symmetry of \(\triangle OAB\).

(a) Describe the geometric relationship between \(L\) and \(\angle AOB\). (2 marks)

(b) Find the polar coordinates of the point of intersection of \(L\) and \(AB\). (2 marks)

Step 1 · Shape of △OAB

Since \(OA = OB = 26\), \(\triangle OAB\) is isosceles with vertex at \(O\). The angle at the pole is

\[ \angle AOB = 130^\circ - 10^\circ = 120^\circ. \]

Step 2 · Part (a): relationship between L and ∠AOB
Axis of Symmetry of an Isosceles Triangle \(OA=OB\ \Longrightarrow\ \angle AOL=\angle BOL\ \text{and}\ OM\perp AB\) \(OA=OB=26\) makes \( riangle OAB\) isosceles, so the axis \(L\) bisects \(ngle AOB\).

In an isosceles triangle, the axis of symmetry through the vertex bisects the vertex angle.
It also meets the base perpendicularly at its midpoint.

Hence \(L\) bisects \(\angle AOB\),
so \(\angle AOL = \angle BOL = 60^\circ\). Also \(L\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

Step 3 · Direction of L

The axis \(L\) lies halfway between the directions of \(OA\) and \(OB\):

\[ \frac{10^\circ + 130^\circ}{2} = 70^\circ. \]

Step 4 · Part (b): the intersection point
Cosine Ratio \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In the right triangle \(OMA\), \(OM\) is adjacent to the \(60^{\circ}\) angle and \(OA=26\) is the hypotenuse.

Let \(M\) be the intersection of \(L\) and \(AB\). Then \(OM \perp AB\). In the right triangle \(OMA\):

\[ \angle AOM = 60^\circ, \qquad OM = OA\cos 60^\circ = 26 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = 13. \]

So \(M\) lies \(13\) units from \(O\) in the direction \(70^\circ\).

Step 5 · Check
Chord Length Formula \(AB=2r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}\) This checks \(AB=26\sqrt{3}\) against the \(120^{\circ}\) angle at the pole.

\[ AM = OA\sin 60^\circ = 26\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2} = 13\sqrt3 \]

so \(AB = 26\sqrt3\), matching the chord formula

\[ 2\cdot 26\sin 60^\circ = 26\sqrt3. \checkmark \]

Hint: equal radii from the pole mean the triangle is isosceles — use symmetry and right-triangle trigonometry with the \(60^\circ\) angle.
Final Answer:
(a) \(L\) bisects \(\angle AOB\) (and is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\)).
(b) The intersection of \(L\) and \(AB\) has polar coordinates \(\left(13,\,70^\circ\right)\).

Question. \(P\) and \(Q\) are points with polar coordinates \((20,25^{\circ})\) and \((20,115^{\circ})\). \(L\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(PQ\) and meets \(PQ\) at \(M\). Find the polar coordinates of \(M\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(OP=OQ=20\), so \(\triangle OPQ\) is isosceles with \(\angle POQ=115^{\circ}-25^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\).
Step 2. The axis of symmetry \(L\) bisects \(\angle POQ\), so each half is \(45^{\circ}\),
and \(L\) points at \(\dfrac{25^{\circ}+115^{\circ}}{2}=70^{\circ}\).
Step 3. In the right triangle \(OMP\): \(OM=OP\cos45^{\circ}=20\times\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=10\sqrt{2}\approx14.1\).
Step 4. Hence \(M\approx(14.1,\ 70^{\circ})\).
Answer: \(M\approx(14.1,\ 70^{\circ})\)
