Polynomial Identity

Question

If \(m\) and \(n\) are constants such that \((x-5)(x+m)-4(x+n)=x(x+6)+1\), then \(n=\)

  • A. \(-19\)
  • B. \(-3\)
  • C. \(9\)
  • D. \(15\)

Solution

Expand both sides
Expanding a Product of Binomials \((x+p)(x+q)=x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq\) \((x-4)(x+m)=x^{2}+(m-4)x-4m\), and the \(-2(x+n)\) adds \(-2x-2n\).

Left-hand side:

\[ (x-5)(x+m)-4(x+n)=x^2+(m-5)x-5m-4x-4n=x^2+(m-9)x-5m-4n \]

Right-hand side:

\[ x(x+6)+1=x^2+6x+1 \]
Equate the coefficients of \(x\)
Equating Coefficients in an Identity \(\text{coefficient of }x\text{ on the left}=\text{coefficient of }x\text{ on the right}\) \(m-6=5\), so \(m=11\).
\[ m-9=6\ \Rightarrow\ m=15 \]
Equate the constant terms
Solving a Linear Equation \(-44-2n=2\ \to\ n=-23\) With \(m=11\) the constant term \(-4m-2n\) must equal \(2\).
\[ -5m-4n=1\ \Rightarrow\ -5(15)-4n=1\ \Rightarrow\ -4n=76\ \Rightarrow\ n=-19 \]
Final Answer: A
