HKDSE 2017 · Paper 2 · MC25

Perpendicular Distance in Polar Coordinates

Question

The polar coordinates of the points \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are \((3,\,160^\circ)\), \((4,\,280^\circ)\) and \((6,\,340^\circ)\) respectively. The perpendicular distance from \(Q\) to \(PR\) is

  1. \(2\)
  2. \(3\)
  3. \(2\sqrt{3}\)
  4. \(3\sqrt{3}\)

Solution

Step 1 · Notice that line \(PR\) passes through the pole \[ \angle POR = 340^\circ - 160^\circ = 180^\circ, \]

so \(P\) and \(R\) lie on opposite rays from the pole \(O\). Hence the line \(PR\) passes through \(O\).

Step 2 · Angle between \(OQ\) and line \(PR\)

The direction of \(OQ\) is \(280^\circ\), and the direction of line \(PR\) is \(160^\circ\) (equivalently \(340^\circ\)). The angle between them is

\[ 280^\circ - 160^\circ = 120^\circ. \]
Step 3 · Perpendicular distance

Dropping a perpendicular from \(Q\) to the line \(PR\) (which passes through \(O\)) forms a right triangle with hypotenuse \(OQ = 4\):

\[ d = OQ \cdot \sin 120^\circ = 4 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3}. \]
Step 4 · Verify the foot lies on segment \(PR\)

The projection of \(OQ\) onto line \(PR\) is \(4\cos120^\circ = -2\), i.e. the foot is \(2\) units from \(O\) on the \(R\)-side of \(O\). Since \(OR = 6 > 2\), the foot lies on the segment \(PR\).

Final Answer: C. \(2\sqrt{3}\)
