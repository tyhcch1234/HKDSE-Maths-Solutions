Right-angled triangle, cyclic quadrilateral & similar triangles

Right-angled Triangle Cyclic Quadrilateral Exterior Angle of Cyclic Quad. AAA Similarity Pythagoras' Theorem

Question The Problem

In the figure, \(ABC\) is a right-angled triangle with \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\). Let \(D\) and \(E\) be points lying on \(AC\) and \(BC\) respectively such that \(ABED\) is a cyclic quadrilateral. If \(AB = 660\text{ cm}\), \(AD = 572\text{ cm}\) and \(BE = 275\text{ cm}\), then \(CD =\)

A. 429 cm
B. 715 cm
C. 728 cm
D. 845 cm
\(AB = 660\), \(AD = 572\), \(BE = 275\)
Slider \(CD\) = 728 cm
Then \(CE\) = — cm, \(BC\) = — cm

Drag the slider: only when \(CD = 728\) does \(ABED\) become cyclic (the four points lie on one circle).

Step 1 Find \(AE\) in \(\triangle ABE\) 1M

Since \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\), \(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled at \(B\). Apply Pythagoras' Theorem.

\(AB^2 + BE^2 = 660^2 + BE^2 =\) —
\(AE = \sqrt{AB^2+BE^2} =\) — cm
1Recognise the right angle1M

\(ABED\) is a cyclic quadrilateral with \(E\) on \(BC\), and \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\). Since \(B\), \(E\), \(C\) are collinear, \(\angle ABE = 90^\circ\).

Hint: A right angle at \(B\) means we can use Pythagoras' Theorem in \(\triangle ABE\).
2Apply Pythagoras1A
\[ AE = \sqrt{AB^2 + BE^2} = \sqrt{660^2 + 275^2} \] \[ = \sqrt{435600 + 75625} = \sqrt{511225} = 715 \text{ cm} \]
\(AE = 715\) cm

Step 2 Exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral 1M

In a cyclic quadrilateral, an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle. Use this on \(ABED\).

\(\angle ADC =\) 90° (exterior angle of cyclic quad.)
\(\angle EDC =\) 90°
1Exterior angle = interior opposite angle1M

Consider the vertex \(B\) of the cyclic quadrilateral \(ABED\). The exterior angle at \(B\) is \(\angle EBC\) (since \(E\) lies on \(BC\) extended from \(B\)). By the exterior-angle property of a cyclic quadrilateral,

\[ \angle EDC = \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]
Hint: The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle — one of the most useful HKDSE properties.
2Consequence: \(\triangle ADE\) is right-angled1A

Since \(A\), \(D\), \(C\) are collinear, \(\angle ADE\) is supplementary to \(\angle EDC\):

\[ \angle ADE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ \]
\(\angle EDC = \angle ADE = 90^\circ\)

Step 3 Find \(DE\) in \(\triangle ADE\) 1M

With \(\angle ADE = 90^\circ\), \(\triangle ADE\) is right-angled at \(D\). Apply Pythagoras' Theorem again.

\(AE = 715\), \(AD = 572\)
\(DE = \sqrt{AE^2 - AD^2} =\) — cm
1Apply Pythagoras in \(\triangle ADE\)1M
\[ DE = \sqrt{AE^2 - AD^2} = \sqrt{715^2 - 572^2} \] \[ = \sqrt{511225 - 327184} = \sqrt{184041} = 429 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(715\), \(572\), \(429\) form a scaled Pythagorean triple. Indeed \(\gcd(429,572)=\gcd(572,715)=143\), so \((3,4,5)\times 143 = (429, 572, 715)\).
\(DE = 429\) cm
2Interesting: \(DE = 429\) = choice A1A

Note that \(429\) cm is one of the options (A). It is the length of \(DE\), not \(CD\) — a classic distractor. The correct answer must be found via similarity.

Hint: Always double-check what the question asks for. Options A and B here are lengths of other segments (\(DE\) and \(AE\)).

Step 4 Prove \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle EDC\) 1M

With the right angles established, the two triangles are similar by AAA. Set up the ratio of corresponding sides.

\(\angle ABC = \angle EDC = 90^\circ\)
\(\angle ACB = \angle ECD\) (common angle)
\(\angle BAC = \angle DEC\) (\(180^\circ - 90^\circ - \angle C\))
1AAA similarity1M

In \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle EDC\):

  • \(\angle ABC = \angle EDC = 90^\circ\) (proved)
  • \(\angle ACB = \angle ECD\) (common angle)
  • \(\angle BAC = \angle DEC\) (angle sum of triangle)
\[ \therefore \triangle ABC \sim \triangle EDC \quad (\text{AAA}) \]
Hint: Match corresponding vertices carefully: \(A \leftrightarrow E\), \(B \leftrightarrow D\), \(C \leftrightarrow C\).
2Corresponding sides1A

From the similarity, the ratio of corresponding sides is constant:

\[ \frac{AB}{ED} = \frac{BC}{DC} = \frac{AC}{EC} \]

Now \(AB = 660\), \(ED = DE = 429\), \(BC = BE + CE = 275 + CE\), \(DC = CD\), \(AC = AD + CD = 572 + CD\).

\(\dfrac{660}{429} = \dfrac{275 + CE}{CD} = \dfrac{572 + CD}{CE}\)

Step 5 Solve the simultaneous equations 1M 1A

Two unknowns \(CD\) and \(CE\), two equations. Solve by elimination.

\(CD\) from eq. (1): — cm
\(CD\) from eq. (2): — cm
Mismatch: —
1Form equation (1) from \(BC/DC = AB/ED\)1M
\[ \frac{275 + CE}{CD} = \frac{660}{429} \]

Cross-multiplying:

\[ 429(275 + CE) = 660\,CD \] \[ 117975 + 429\,CE = 660\,CD \] \[ 660\,CD - 429\,CE = 117975 \]

Divide through by \(33\):

\[ 20\,CD - 13\,CE = 3575 \quad \cdots (1) \]
2Form equation (2) from \(AC/EC = AB/ED\)1M
\[ \frac{572 + CD}{CE} = \frac{660}{429} \]

Cross-multiplying:

\[ 429(572 + CD) = 660\,CE \] \[ 245388 + 429\,CD = 660\,CE \] \[ 429\,CD - 660\,CE = -245388 \]

Divide through by \(33\):

\[ 13\,CD - 20\,CE = -7436 \quad \cdots (2) \]
Hint: A useful check: \(660/429 = 20/13\) — both equations can be scaled to involve the neat numbers \(13\) and \(20\).
3Eliminate \(CE\)1M
\[ (1) \times 20: \quad 400\,CD - 260\,CE = 71500 \quad \cdots(A) \] \[ (2) \times 13: \quad 169\,CD - 260\,CE = -96668 \quad \cdots(B) \]

Subtracting \((B)\) from \((A)\):

\[ (400 - 169)\,CD = 71500 + 96668 \] \[ 231\,CD = 168168 \] \[ CD = \frac{168168}{231} = 728 \]
Hint: \(168168 = 231 \times 728\). You can verify: \(231 \times 700 = 161700\), \(231 \times 28 = 6468\), total \(168168\). ✓
\(\boxed{CD = 728\text{ cm}}\) → Answer C
4Verify1A

Substitute back to find \(CE\):

\[ 20(728) - 13\,CE = 3575 \;\Rightarrow\; 14560 - 13\,CE = 3575 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = \frac{10985}{13} = 845 \] \[ BC = 275 + 845 = 1120, \quad AC = 572 + 728 = 1300, \quad EC = 845 \]

Check ratios:

\[ \frac{BC}{DC} = \frac{1120}{728} = \frac{20}{13}, \qquad \frac{AB}{ED} = \frac{660}{429} = \frac{20}{13} \checkmark \] \[ \frac{AC}{EC} = \frac{1300}{845} = \frac{20}{13} \checkmark \]
Nice observation: \(\triangle ABC\) is a \(13\)–\(20\)–\(23\)-scaled triangle since \(660 = 33\times20\), \(1120 = 56\times20\)... Actually \(AC=1300\), \(AB=660\), \(BC=1120\): all divisible by \(20\) to give \((33,56,65)\times 20\), a Pythagorean triple since \(33^2+56^2 = 1089+3136=4225=65^2\). ✓
Answer: C. 728 cm
