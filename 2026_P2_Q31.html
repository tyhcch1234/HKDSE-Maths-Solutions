Binary to Denary

Question

\(1010100000001110_2 =\)

  • A. \(21\times2^{11}+14\)
  • B. \(21\times2^{11}+28\)
  • C. \(21\times2^{12}+14\)
  • D. \(21\times2^{12}+28\)

Solution

1-bits The \(1\)-bits sit at positions \(15,13,11,3,2,1\) (rightmost = 0).
Expand
Binary Place Value \(N=\sum b_{k}\,2^{k}\qquad (b_{k}\in\{0,1\})\) A \(1\)-bit in position \(k\) contributes \(2^{k}\), so the six \(1\)-bits at positions \(15,13,11,3,2,1\) give \(2^{15}+2^{13}+2^{11}+2^{3}+2^{2}+2^{1}\).
\(2^{15}+2^{13}+2^{11}+2^3+2^2+2^1\).
Factor
Factorising a Sum of Powers \(2^{a}+2^{b}+2^{c}=2^{c}\left(2^{a-c}+2^{b-c}+1\right)\quad (c Taking out the smallest power \(2^{11}\) leaves \(2^{4}+2^{2}+1=21\), so the high part is \(21\times 2^{11}\).
\(2^{15}+2^{13}+2^{11}=2^{11}(2^4+2^2+1)=21\times2^{11}\).
Remainder
Place Value of the Remainder \(r<2^{11}\quad\text{when the leading term is }2^{11}k\) The remaining bits \(2^{3}+2^{2}+2^{1}=14\) are smaller than \(2^{11}\), so they form the constant term \(r=14\).
\(2^3+2^2+2^1=8+4+2=14\).
Result \(21\times2^{11}+14\).
Answer: A — 21×211+14

Question. A positive integer \(N\) is written in binary with \(1\)-bits at positions \(16,\ 13,\ 10,\ 5,\ 2\) and \(1\) (the rightmost bit is position \(0\)). Express \(N\) in the form \(k\times 2^{m}+r\), where \(k\) is an integer, \(0\le r<2^{m}\), and \(m\) is as large as possible. (3 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Each \(1\)-bit contributes its place value: \(N=2^{16}+2^{13}+2^{10}+2^{5}+2^{2}+2^{1}\).
Step 2. Group the three highest powers: \(2^{16}+2^{13}+2^{10}=2^{10}\left(2^{6}+2^{3}+1\right)=2^{10}(64+8+1)=73\times 2^{10}\).
Step 3. The remaining bits give \(2^{5}+2^{2}+2^{1}=32+4+2=38\).
Step 4. Since \(38<2^{10}\), the required form is \(N=73\times 2^{10}+38\), i.e. \(m=10\), \(k=73\), \(r=38\).
Step 5. Check: \(73\times 1024+38=74752+38=74790\).
Answer: \(N=73\times 2^{10}+38\)
