If $a(a+b)=2(b-a)$, then $b=$
  • A.  $\dfrac{a^2+a}{2+a}$
  • B.  $\dfrac{a^2-2a}{2+a}$
  • C.  $\dfrac{a^2+2a}{2-a}$
  • D.  $\dfrac{a^2-a}{2-a}$
Step 1Expand the left-hand side.
$a(a+b)=2(b-a) \;\Longrightarrow\; a^2+ab=2(b-a)$
Distribute $a$ over the bracket: $a\cdot a+a\cdot b=a^2+ab$.
Step 2Expand the right-hand side.
$a^2+ab=2b-2a$
Multiply out: $2(b-a)=2b-2a$. Careful with the signs.
Distributive Law\(a(b+c)=ab+ac\)\(a(a+b)=a^{2}+ab\) and \(2(b-a)=2b-2a\), so every bracket is expanded first.
Step 3Collect the $b$-terms on one side.
Add $2a$ to both sides: $a^2+ab+2a=2b$
Subtract $ab$ from both sides: $a^2+2a=2b-ab$
Move everything so that only $b$-terms remain on the right.
Step 4Factorise $b$ out of the right-hand side.
$2b-ab=(2-a)b$
Hence $\;(2-a)b=a^2+2a$
$a$ is a common factor of the two $b$-terms: reverse the distributive law.
Common Factor Law\(2b-ab=(2-a)b\)Both terms on the right contain \(b\), so it is taken out to leave \((2-a)b=a^{2}+2a\).
Step 5Divide both sides by $(2-a)$.
$b=\dfrac{a^2+2a}{2-a}$
$a\neq 2$ so the divisor is non-zero — the answer is option C.
Division Property of Equality\(a=b\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{a}{c}=\dfrac{b}{c}\quad(c\neq0)\)Dividing by \(2-a\) (valid since \(a\neq2\)) isolates \(b=\dfrac{a^{2}+2a}{2-a}\).
Answer: C  —  $b=\dfrac{a^2+2a}{2-a}$

Question. If \(a(a-b)=3(b+a)\), then \(b=\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand the left-hand side: \(a^{2}-ab=3b+3a\).
Step 2. Collect the \(b\)-terms on one side: \(a^{2}-3a=3b+ab\).
Step 3. Factorise \(b\) out: \(a^{2}-3a=(3+a)b\).
Step 4. Divide by \(3+a\): \(b=\dfrac{a^{2}-3a}{3+a}\). Check with \(a=1\): \(b=\dfrac{-2}{4}=-0.5\), and \(1(1.5)=3(0.5)\) ✓.
Answer: \(b=\dfrac{a^{2}-3a}{3+a}\)
