Question. Let $a_n$ be the $n$th term of a geometric sequence. It is given that $$a_1 = 8p^2,\qquad a_2 = 1,\qquad a_3 = 27p,$$ where $p$ is a real number. Find $a_4$.

  • A. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
  • B. $\dfrac{2}{9}$
  • C. $\dfrac{9}{2}$
  • D. $\dfrac{81}{4}$
1Use the ratio property of a geometric sequence
Common Ratio of a Geometric Sequence \(\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}=\dfrac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}=r\) So \(\dfrac{1}{8p^{2}}=27p\), the condition that determines \(p\).

In a geometric sequence with common ratio $r$, consecutive terms satisfy $$\frac{a_2}{a_1}=\frac{a_3}{a_2}=r.$$ So the geometric condition links the three given terms:

$$\frac{1}{8p^2}=\frac{27p}{1}$$

Hint: always match the common ratio between the two pairs of consecutive terms.

2Solve for $p$
Solving a Simple Cubic \(p^{3}=\dfrac{1}{216}\ \Rightarrow\ p=\dfrac{1}{6}\) Since \(p\) is real, take the real cube root of both sides directly.

Cross-multiplying gives

$$1=216p^3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p^3=\frac{1}{216}=\frac{1}{6^3}$$ $$p=\frac{1}{6}$$

Hint: since $p$ is real,
take the real cube root directly.

3Find the common ratio $r$

Now substitute $p=\dfrac16$ into $a_1=8p^2$ and $a_2=1$:

$$a_1=8\left(\frac16\right)^2=8\cdot\frac{1}{36}=\frac{8}{36}=\frac{2}{9}$$ $$r=\frac{a_2}{a_1}=\frac{1}{\tfrac29}=\frac{9}{2}$$

Hint: $r$ can be checked using $a_3=27p=\dfrac{27}{6}=\dfrac92$ and $a_3/a_2=\dfrac92. \checkmark$

4Compute $a_4$
General Term of a Geometric Sequence \(a_{n}=a_{1}r^{n-1}\) \(a_{4}=a_{3}r=\dfrac{9}{2}\times\dfrac{9}{2}=\dfrac{81}{4}\).

For a geometric sequence, $a_4 = a_3 \times r$:

$$a_4=\frac{9}{2}\times\frac{9}{2}=\frac{81}{4}$$

Hint: equivalently $a_4=a_2r^{2}=1\cdot\left(\dfrac92\right)^2=\dfrac{81}{4}$.

5Conclusion

The fourth term is $\dfrac{81}{4}$, so the correct option is D.

Answer:  D.  $\;a_4=\dfrac{81}{4}$

Question. Let \(a_{n}\) be the \(n\)th term of a geometric sequence, where \(a_{1}=2p^{2}\), \(a_{2}=1\) and \(a_{3}=32p\), and \(p\) is a real number. Find \(a_{4}\). A. \(16\) B. \(32\) C. \(64\) D. \(128\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The common ratio satisfies \(\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}=\dfrac{a_{3}}{a_{2}}\): \(\dfrac{1}{2p^{2}}=\dfrac{32p}{1}\).
Step 2. Cross-multiplying: \(1=64p^{3}\), so \(p^{3}=\dfrac{1}{64}\) and \(p=\dfrac{1}{4}\).
Step 3. Then \(a_{1}=2\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{1}{8}\) and \(r=\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}=8\).
Step 4. \(a_{3}=32p=8\), so \(a_{4}=a_{3}r=8\times8=64\).
Answer: C. \(64\)
