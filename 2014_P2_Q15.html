HKDSE 2014 · Paper 2 · Q15  —  Mensuration (Pentagon area)

Square BCDE (sides 16 cm) Triangle ABE (AB = AE) Vertices Right / 45° angles Diagonals BE
Question

In the figure, $AB = AE$ and $\angle BAE = \angle BCD = \angle CDE = 90^\circ$. If $BC = CD = DE = 16\text{ cm}$, then the area of the pentagon $ABCDE$ is

  1. A. $71\text{ cm}^2$
  2. B. $128\text{ cm}^2$
  3. C. $192\text{ cm}^2$
  4. D. $224\text{ cm}^2$
Step 1 · Recognise the square Since $BC = CD = DE$ and $\angle BCD = \angle CDE = 90^\circ$, the quadrilateral $BCDE$ has three equal sides and its two included angles at $C$ and $D$ are right angles, so $BCDE$ is a square with side $16\text{ cm}$. $$\therefore\ BE = 16\text{ cm}\quad(\text{diagonal? no — } BE \text{ is a side})$$ Here $B$ and $E$ are the top corners, so $BE$ is the top side: $$BE = 16\text{ cm}.$$
Step 2 · Triangle ABE is right-angled & isosceles $AB = AE$ (given) and $\angle BAE = 90^\circ$ (given), so $\triangle ABE$ is a right-angled isosceles triangle with the right angle at $A$ and base $BE = 16\text{ cm}$. $$\angle ABE = \angle AEB = 45^\circ.$$
Step 3 · Find the legs AB and AE In $\triangle ABE$, using the base $BE = 16$ and the $45^\circ$ angle: $$AB = BE\cos 45^\circ = 16 \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}$$ $$AE = AB = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}.$$
Step 4 · Area of pentagon ABCDE The pentagon is the square $BCDE$ minus the triangle $ABE$: $$\text{Area} = (\text{Area of } BCDE) - (\text{Area of } \triangle ABE)$$ $$= (16)^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\,(8\sqrt{2})(8\sqrt{2})$$ $$= 256 - \tfrac{1}{2}(128) = 256 - 64 = 192\ \text{cm}^2.$$
Final answer:  $192\text{ cm}^2$  (Option C)
💡 Tip: Whenever $\angle BAE = 90^\circ$ with $AB = AE$, the triangle is a $45^\circ\text{-}45^\circ\text{-}90^\circ$ triangle, so its legs are the hypotenuse divided by $\sqrt{2}$.
