Geometry — Converse of Pythagoras' Theorem & Perimeter of a Quadrilateral

Straight Line Pythagoras' Theorem Converse of Pythagoras Right Angle Perimeter

QQuestion

In the figure, \(ACE\) is a straight line. It is given that \(BC = 5\text{ cm}\), \(BD = 12\text{ cm}\), \(CD = 13\text{ cm}\), \(AD = 37\text{ cm}\) and \(CE = 9\text{ cm}\).

If \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\), find the perimeter of the quadrilateral \(ADCE\).

A. \(76\text{ cm}\)    B. \(90\text{ cm}\)    C. \(100\text{ cm}\)    D. \(180\text{ cm}\)

Reading the figure: \(D\) sits above the straight line \(ACE\); \(DB\) is the perpendicular from \(D\) down to the base line, and \(B\) lies between \(C\) and \(A\). The quadrilateral interested is \(A \to D \to C \to E \to A\).

Original Figure

aShow \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\) (Converse of Pythagoras)

STEP 11M

In \(\triangle CBD\), the three sides are known: \[ CB = 5,\qquad BD = 12,\qquad CD = 13. \]

Test the Pythagorean relation on the two shorter sides:

\[ CB^2 + BD^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169. \]
Hint: \(5\text{–}12\text{–}13\) is a Pythagorean triple — the values were chosen on purpose.
STEP 21M

Compare with the square of the longest side:

\[ CD^2 = 13^2 = 169. \]

So

\[ CB^2 + BD^2 = CD^2. \]
Hint: When the two shorter sides' squares add up to the longest side squared, the angle opposite the longest side is the right angle.
STEP 31A

By the converse of Pythagoras' Theorem, the angle opposite \(CD\) is \(90^\circ\):

\[ \angle CBD = 90^\circ. \]
\(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\) ✓ (so \(BD \perp AC\))
0 / 3

bFind \(AB\), hence \(AC\), using \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\)

STEP 11M

Since \(ACE\) is a straight line and \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\), the angle between \(DB\) and the line \(ACE\) is a right angle too: \[ \angle ABD = 90^\circ. \] So \(\triangle ABD\) is a right-angled triangle with the right angle at \(B\).

Hint: \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) all lie on the straight line; \(DB \perp AC\) means \(DB \perp AB\) as well.
STEP 21M

Apply Pythagoras' Theorem in \(\triangle ABD\) (legs \(AB\), \(BD\); hypotenuse \(AD\)):

\[ AB^2 + BD^2 = AD^2 \] \[ AB^2 + 12^2 = 37^2 \] \[ AB^2 = 1369 - 144 = 1225. \]
Hint: \(35\text{–}12\text{–}37\) is another Pythagorean triple.
STEP 31A

\[ AB = \sqrt{1225} = 35. \]

Since \(B\) lies between \(C\) and \(A\) on the straight line \(ACE\):

\[ AC = CB + BA = 5 + 35 = 40\text{ cm}. \]
\(AB = 35\text{ cm},\qquad AC = 40\text{ cm}\)
0 / 3

cFind \(AE\) and the perimeter of \(ADCE\)

STEP 11M

In the figure \(CE\) is drawn so that \(CE \perp AC\) (the right angle is marked at \(C\)). Hence \(\triangle ACE\) is right-angled at \(C\), and

\[ AC^2 + CE^2 = AE^2. \]
Hint: We already know \(AC = 40\) and \(CE = 9\) — one more Pythagorean triple: \(9\text{–}40\text{–}41\).
STEP 21M

\[ 40^2 + 9^2 = AE^2 \] \[ 1600 + 81 = AE^2 \] \[ AE^2 = 1681 \] \[ AE = \sqrt{1681} = 41\text{ cm}. \]

\(AE = 41\text{ cm}\)
STEP 31A

The perimeter of quadrilateral \(ADCE\) is the sum of its four sides:

\[ \text{Perimeter} = AD + DC + CE + EA \] \[ = 37 + 13 + 9 + 41 \] \[ = 100\text{ cm}. \]
Hint: Make sure you walk around the quadrilateral in order: \(A \to D \to C \to E \to A\).
Perimeter of \(ADCE = 100\text{ cm}\) → Answer C
0 / 3

ΣSummary of Answers

  • \(\triangle CBD\): \(5^2 + 12^2 = 13^2\), so \(\angle CBD = 90^\circ\) (converse of Pythagoras). Therefore \(BD \perp AC\).
  • \(\triangle ABD\): \(AB = \sqrt{37^2 - 12^2} = 35\text{ cm}\).
  • \(AC = CB + BA = 5 + 35 = 40\text{ cm}\).
  • \(\triangle ACE\): \(AE = \sqrt{40^2 + 9^2} = 41\text{ cm}\).
  • Perimeter \(= AD + DC + CE + EA = 37 + 13 + 9 + 41 = 100\text{ cm}\).
Correct option: C — \(100\text{ cm}\)
