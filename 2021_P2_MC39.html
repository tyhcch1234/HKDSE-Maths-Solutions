Circle Geometry — Angle in Alternate Segment, Semi-circle & Parallel Lines

Q The Question

In the figure, \(AC\) is a diameter of the circle \(ABCD\). \(EF\) is the tangent to the circle at \(D\) such that \(AB /\!/ EC\). If \(\angle CDF = 49^\circ\) and \(\angle CED = 31^\circ\), then \(\angle ACB =\)

A. \(49^\circ\)    B. \(57^\circ\)    C. \(59^\circ\)    D. \(67^\circ\)

1 mark · Multiple Choice

Observation: \(\angle CAD\) always equals \(\angle CDF\) (angles in the alternate segment), and \(\angle ABC = \angle ADC = 90^\circ\) because \(AC\) is a diameter.

2 Part (b) — Full Step-by-Step Solution

∠ACE = 23° ∠BCE = 90° ∠ACB = 67°
0 / 7
Step 11M Find \(\angle DCE\) using the exterior angle of \(\triangle CDE\).

In \(\triangle CDE\), the angle \(\angle CDF\) is an exterior angle at \(D\):

\[ \angle DCE = \angle CDF - \angle CED = 49^\circ - 31^\circ = 18^\circ \quad (\text{ext. } \angle \text{ of } \triangle) \]
Hint: The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles, so \(\angle CDF=\angle DCE+\angle CED\).
\(\angle DCE = 18^\circ\)
Step 21M Apply the angle in the alternate segment.

Since \(EF\) is tangent at \(D\) and chord \(CD\) subtends \(\angle CAD\) in the alternate segment:

\[ \angle CAD = \angle CDF = 49^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in alt. segment}) \]
Hint: The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
\(\angle CAD = 49^\circ\)
Step 31A Use the angle in a semi-circle.

Since \(AC\) is a diameter of the circle, the angle subtended at \(B\) or \(D\) by \(AC\) is a right angle:

\[ \angle ABC = \angle ADC = 90^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in semi-circle}) \]
Hint: Any angle in a semi-circle is \(90^\circ\). This is the key fact that unlocks both triangles.
\(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\)
Step 41A Find \(\angle ACD\) in \(\triangle ACD\).

In \(\triangle ACD\), the angle sum is \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle ACD = 180^\circ - \angle ADC - \angle CAD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 49^\circ = 41^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle = 180^\circ\). We already know two angles of \(\triangle ACD\).
\(\angle ACD = 41^\circ\)
Step 51A Find \(\angle ACE\).

At vertex \(C\), angle \(\angle ACD\) is split by line \(CE\):

\[ \angle ACE = \angle ACD - \angle DCE = 41^\circ - 18^\circ = 23^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle ACE\) and \(\angle DCE\) together make up \(\angle ACD\) — just subtract.
\(\angle ACE = 23^\circ\)
Step 61A Use \(AB /\!/ EC\) to find \(\angle BCE\).

Since \(AB /\!/ EC\), \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle BCE\) are interior angles on the same side:

\[ \angle BCE = 180^\circ - \angle ABC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ \quad (\text{int. } \angle\text{s, } AB /\!/ EC) \]
Hint: Interior angles on the same side of a transversal sum to \(180^\circ\).
\(\angle BCE = 90^\circ\)
Step 71A Find the required \(\angle ACB\).

At vertex \(C\), line \(CB\) splits \(\angle BCE\):

\[ \angle ACB = \angle BCE - \angle ACE = 90^\circ - 23^\circ = 67^\circ \]
Hint: Subtract \(\angle ACE\) from \(\angle BCE\) to isolate \(\angle ACB\).
\(\angle ACB = 67^\circ \;\Rightarrow\;\) Answer: D

Summary of Results

  • \(\angle DCE = \angle CDF - \angle CED = 49^\circ - 31^\circ = \mathbf{18^\circ}\) (ext. ∠ of △)
  • \(\angle CAD = \angle CDF = \mathbf{49^\circ}\) (∠ in alt. segment)
  • \(\angle ADC = \angle ABC = \mathbf{90^\circ}\) (∠ in semi-circle)
  • \(\angle ACD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 49^\circ = \mathbf{41^\circ}\) (∠ sum of △)
  • \(\angle ACE = 41^\circ - 18^\circ = \mathbf{23^\circ}\)
  • \(\angle BCE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = \mathbf{90^\circ}\) (int. ∠s, \(AB /\!/ EC\))
  • \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ - 23^\circ = \mathbf{67^\circ}\) → Option D
