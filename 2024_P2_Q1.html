2024 MC #1
$(x+3y)^2-(x-3y)^2 = \;?$
  • A. $2x^2$
  • B. $6xy$
  • C. $12xy$
  • D. $2x^2+18y^2$
1

Recognise the difference of two squares

Difference of Two Squares \(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a+b)(a-b)\) Taking \(a=x+3y\) and \(b=x-3y\) turns the expression into a product of two brackets.

The expression is of the form $a^2-b^2$ with

$a=x+3y$  
 
and 
  $b=x-3y$.

Hint: whenever you see one square minus another square, think of the identity $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$.
2

Apply the identity $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$

\[(x+3y)^2-(x-3y)^2=\big[(x+3y)+(x-3y)\big]\big[(x+3y)-(x-3y)\big]\]

Write each bracket carefully — notice the minus sign in front of the second bracket in the right factor.
3

Simplify each bracket

Collecting Like Terms \((x+3y)\pm(x-3y)\ \text{gives}\ 2x\ \text{and}\ 6y\) The \(x\)-terms add while the \(3y\) and \(-3y\) have opposite signs, so the brackets simplify to \(2x\) and \(6y\).

First bracket: $(x+3y)+(x-3y)=x+x+3y-3y=2x$.

Second bracket: $(x+3y)-(x-3y)=x-x+3y+3y=6y$.

Careful: subtracting $(x-3y)$ gives $-x+3y$, so the $3y$ terms add to $6y$.
4

Multiply the two simplified factors

\[(2x)(6y)=12xy\]

The $x^2$ terms and $y^2$ terms cancel — only the cross term $12xy$ survives.
5

Check by direct expansion (verification)

$(x+3y)^2=x^2+6xy+9y^2$

$(x-3y)^2=x^2-6xy+9y^2$

Subtracting: $(x^2+6xy+9y^2)-(x^2-6xy+9y^2)=12xy$. ✓

Common trap: option D ($2x^2+18y^2$) comes from adding the two squares instead of subtracting — the $x^2$ and $y^2$ terms must cancel here.
ANSWER C  —  $12xy$

Question. Simplify \((2x+5y)^{2}-(2x-5y)^{2}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. This is a difference of two squares with \(a=2x+5y\) and \(b=2x-5y\).
Step 2. \((2x+5y)^{2}-(2x-5y)^{2}=\big[(2x+5y)+(2x-5y)\big]\big[(2x+5y)-(2x-5y)\big]\).
Step 3. First bracket: \(2x+5y+2x-5y=4x\). Second bracket: \(2x+5y-2x+5y=10y\).
Step 4. Multiply: \((4x)(10y)=40xy\).
Step 5. Check by expanding: \((4x^{2}+20xy+25y^{2})-(4x^{2}-20xy+25y^{2})=40xy\).
Answer: \(40xy\)
Key skill: difference of two squares $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$.
