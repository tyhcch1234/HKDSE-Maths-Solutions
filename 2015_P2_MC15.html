Q Question Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(N\) is a point lying on \(AC\) and \(E\) is a point lying on \(DN\). If \(DN = 6\text{ cm}\) and \(EN = 5\text{ cm}\), then the area of \(\Delta ABC\) is

A. \(24\text{ cm}^2\)
B. \(30\text{ cm}^2\)
C. \(96\text{ cm}^2\)
D. \(192\text{ cm}^2\)

Given: \(CD = 10\text{ cm}\), \(AE = 13\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 16\text{ cm}\), and \(AB \perp BC\).

1 Part (a) — Find \(CN\) 1M

Apply Pythagoras' theorem to the right triangle \(\Delta CDN\) (right angle at \(N\)).

CN = 8.00 cm DN = 6.00 cm CD = 10.00 cm 6² + 8² = 10² ✓
Step 11M

In \(\Delta CDN\), the right angle is at \(N\), so \(CN\) and \(DN\) are the legs and \(CD\) is the hypotenuse:

\[ CN^2 + DN^2 = CD^2 \] \[ CN = \sqrt{CD^2 - DN^2} = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = 8\text{ cm} \]
Hint: Always identify the hypotenuse first — it is opposite the right angle. Here \(CD = 10\), so it must be the longest side.
\(CN = 8\text{ cm}\)
0 / 1

2 Part (b) — Find \(AN\) and \(AC\) 1M

Apply Pythagoras' theorem to \(\Delta AEN\), then add the segments \(AN + CN\) to get \(AC\).

AN = 12.00 cm CN = 8.00 cm AC = AN + CN = 20.00 cm
Step 11M

In \(\Delta AEN\), the right angle is at \(N\) (since \(N\) lies on \(AC\) and \(DNE \perp AC\)). Hence:

\[ AN^2 + EN^2 = AE^2 \] \[ AN = \sqrt{AE^2 - EN^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm} \]

Since \(N\) lies on \(AC\), the whole side is the sum of the two parts:

\[ AC = AN + CN = 12 + 8 = 20\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(N\) is between \(A\) and \(C\), so \(AC\) is just \(AN + CN\) — no overlap, no subtraction needed.
\(AN = 12\text{ cm},\quad AC = 20\text{ cm}\)
0 / 1

3 Part (c) — Find \(AB\), then the area of \(\Delta ABC\) 1M, 1A

Use Pythagoras in \(\Delta ABC\) (right angle at \(B\)) and then the standard area formula.

BC = 16.00 cm AC = 20.00 cm AB = 12.00 cm Area = ½·AB·BC = 96 cm²
Step 11M

In \(\Delta ABC\), the right angle is at \(B\), so \(AC\) is the hypotenuse:

\[ AB^2 + BC^2 = AC^2 \] \[ AB = \sqrt{AC^2 - BC^2} = \sqrt{20^2 - 16^2} = \sqrt{400 - 256} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(20^2 - 16^2 = (20-16)(20+16) = 4 \times 36 = 144\).
\(AB = 12\text{ cm}\)
Step 21A

Since \(AB \perp BC\), they form the base and height of \(\Delta ABC\):

\[ \text{Area of } \Delta ABC = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot BC = \frac{1}{2} (12)(16) = 96\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: The area of a triangle is \(\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\). The base \(BC = 16\), and the height is the perpendicular distance \(AB = 12\).
Area \(= 96\text{ cm}^2\) — the answer is C.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(CN = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = 8\text{ cm}\)
  • Part (b): \(AN = \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{ cm}\), so \(AC = AN + CN = 12 + 8 = 20\text{ cm}\)
  • Part (c): \(AB = \sqrt{AC^2 - BC^2} = \sqrt{20^2 - 16^2} = 12\text{ cm}\)
  • Final area: \(\frac12 \times AB \times BC = \frac12 \times 12 \times 16 = 96\text{ cm}^2\)
  • Correct option: C — \(96\text{ cm}^2\)
