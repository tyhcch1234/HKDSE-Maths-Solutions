The L.C.M. of $9a^2b$, $12a^4b^3$ and $15a^6$

HKDSE Mathematics 2016 Paper 2, Question 31  ·  Topic 4: Polynomials

The L.C.M. of $9a^2b$, $12a^4b^3$ and $15a^6$ is

  • A. $\;3a^2$
  • B. $\;3a^2b$
  • C. $\;180a^6b^3$
  • D. $\;180a^{12}b^4$

1Prime-factorise each expression

$9a^2b = 3^2 \cdot a^2 \cdot b^1$

$12a^4b^3 = 2^2 \cdot 3^1 \cdot a^4 \cdot b^3$

$15a^6 = 3^1 \cdot 5^1 \cdot a^6$  
(no factor $b$, i.e. $b^0$)

Write each monomial as a product of prime factors and powers.
Treat $a$ and $b$ as separate "factor columns".

2For each factor take the largest power

L.C.M. by Prime Factorisation \(\text{L.C.M.}\left(p^{a},\,p^{b}\right)=p^{\max(a,\,b)}\) The powers of \(a\) are \(2,4,6\), so the L.C.M. takes \(a^{6}\); for \(b\) the powers \(1,3,0\) give \(b^{3}\).

Factor $2$: powers are $2^0,2^2,2^0 \Rightarrow 2^2$

Factor $3$: powers are $3^2,3^1,3^1 \Rightarrow 3^2$

Factor $5$: powers are $5^0,5^0,5^1 \Rightarrow 5^1$

Factor $a$: powers are $a^2,a^4,a^6 \Rightarrow a^6$

Factor $b$: powers are $b^1,b^3,b^0 \Rightarrow b^3$

L.C.M. = take the maximum power of every distinct factor (unlike H.C.F.,
where you take the minimum).

3Multiply to get the L.C.M.

Product Law of Indices \(p^{m}\cdot p^{n}=p^{m+n}\) Multiplying the chosen highest powers \(2^{2}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 5^{1}\cdot a^{6}\cdot b^{3}\) gives the L.C.M. in one product.

$$\text{L.C.M.} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5^1 \cdot a^6 \cdot b^3$$

$$= 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 \cdot a^6b^3$$

Combine the numerical part separately from the algebraic part.

4Evaluate the number

$$4 \times 9 \times 5 = 180$$

$$\therefore \text{L.C.M.} = 180a^6b^3$$

So the answer is option C.

5Check the distractors

A. $3a^2$ — this is the H.C.F., taking the smallest powers,
not the L.C.M.

D. $180a^{12}b^4$ — the powers were added instead of taking the maximum.

A quick sanity check: each expression must divide the L.C.M. exactly.

Answer: Option C  —  the L.C.M. is $180a^6b^3$.

Question. The L.C.M. of \(6x^{3}y^{2}\), \(8x^{4}y\) and \(9y^{5}\) is
A. \(72x^{4}y^{5}\)    B. \(24x^{4}y^{5}\)    C. \(72x^{3}y^{2}\)    D. \(216x^{4}y^{5}\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Prime-factorise: \(6x^{3}y^{2}=2\cdot 3\cdot x^{3}y^{2}\), \(8x^{4}y=2^{3}x^{4}y\), \(9y^{5}=3^{2}y^{5}\).
Step 2. Take the largest power of each factor: \(2^{3}\), \(3^{2}\), \(x^{4}\) (powers \(3,4,0\)) and \(y^{5}\) (powers \(2,1,5\)).
Step 3. So the L.C.M. \(=2^{3}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot x^{4}y^{5}=8\cdot 9\cdot x^{4}y^{5}=72x^{4}y^{5}\), which divides-check every expression.
Option A.
Answer: A. \(72x^{4}y^{5}\)
