Statistics — Mode, Median & Quartiles of a Frequency Distribution

Grouped Data Mode Median Quartiles Cumulative Frequency

Question

The table below shows the distribution of the numbers of merits obtained by some students in a year.

Number of merits obtained678910
Number of students 323628182

Which of the following is true?

  1. The mode of the distribution is 36.
  2. The median of the distribution is 8.
  3. The lower quartile of the distribution is 6.
  4. The upper quartile of the distribution is 10.
AMode \(=36\)  ✗
BMedian \(=8\)  ✗
CLower quartile \(=6\)
DUpper quartile \(=10\)  ✗

Option A — The Mode

7
Frequency at 7 = 36 Maximum frequency = 36 (at 7 merits) Mode = 7 (the value with the highest frequency)
Step 1

Recall: the mode is the data value that occurs most frequently (largest frequency), not the frequency itself.

Hint: Many students wrongly answer "36". 36 is the number of students who got 7 merits — it is a frequency, not the mode.
Step 2

Read the frequencies from the table:

\[ \begin{array}{c|ccccc} \text{Merits} & 6 & 7 & 8 & 9 & 10 \\ \hline \text{Students} & 32 & 36 & 28 & 18 & 2 \end{array} \]

The largest frequency is \(36\), which corresponds to \(\boxed{7}\) merits. Hence the mode is \(7\), so option A is false.

Hint: Always match the biggest frequency back to its value — don't stop at the frequency.
0 / 2

Option B — The Median

58
Total data \(n=32+36+28+18+2=116\) Position 58 → value 7 Median = (7 + 7) ÷ 2 = 7
Step 1

Find the total number of data (students):

\[ n = 32 + 36 + 28 + 18 + 2 = 116 \]
Hint: \(n\) is even, so the median is the mean of the \(\tfrac{n}{2}\)-th and \((\tfrac{n}{2}+1)\)-th ordered data — i.e. the 58th and 59th values.
Step 2

Build the cumulative frequency to locate the 58th and 59th positions:

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} \text{Merits} & \text{Frequency} & \text{Cumulative freq.} \\ \hline 6 & 32 & 32 \\ 7 & 36 & 68 \\ 8 & 28 & 96 \\ 9 & 18 & 114 \\ 10 & 2 & 116 \end{array} \]

Positions \(33\)–\(68\) all take the value \(7\). Since \(58\) and \(59\) lie in this range, both the 58th and 59th data are \(7\).

Hint: Cumulative frequency tells you the range of positions occupied by each value — a fast way to find any quantile.
Step 3

Median \(=\) mean of the 58th and 59th ordered data:

\[ \text{Median} = (7 + 7) \div 2 = 7 \]
Median \(= 7 \ne 8\), so option B is false.

Option C — The Lower Quartile

29
Position 29 → value 6 Lower quartile uses positions \(\tfrac{n}{4}=29\) and \(30\) Q₁ = (6 + 6) ÷ 2 = 6 Q₃ = (8 + 8) ÷ 2 = 8
Step 1

There are \(n = 116\) data. Locate the quartile positions:

\[ \frac{n}{4} = \frac{116}{4} = 29, \qquad \frac{3n}{4} = \frac{348}{4} = 87 \]

So the lower quartile is the mean of the 29th and 30th ordered data, and the upper quartile is the mean of the 87th and 88th ordered data.

Hint: For the lower quartile with even \(n\), use the \(\tfrac{n}{4}\)-th and \((\tfrac{n}{4}+1)\)-th positions. Here that is 29 and 30.
Step 2

Use the cumulative frequency table:

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} \text{Merits} & \text{Freq.} & \text{Cum. freq.} \\ \hline 6 & 32 & 32 \\ 7 & 36 & 68 \\ 8 & 28 & 96 \\ 9 & 18 & 114 \\ 10 & 2 & 116 \end{array} \]

Since the first 32 data all equal \(6\), both positions 29 and 30 fall in the value 6.

Hint: Positions \(1\)–\(32\) are all \(6\). So any position from 1 to 32 gives the value \(6\).
Step 3

Lower quartile:

\[ Q_1 = (6 + 6) \div 2 = 6 \]
Lower quartile \(= 6\) ✓  — option C is true.
Hint: Also check option D while you are here: positions \(87\) and \(88\) both fall in the "8" block (positions \(69\)–\(96\)), so \(Q_3 = (8+8)\div2 = 8 \ne 10\). Option D is false.

Option D — The Upper Quartile

Min \(=6\),   \(Q_1=6\) Median \(=7\) \(Q_3=8\),   Max \(=10\) Five-number summary: 6, 6, 7, 8, 10
Step 1

We found in Part C that \(\tfrac{3n}{4}=87\), so the upper quartile is the mean of the 87th and 88th ordered data.

Hint: Do not confuse the maximum value (10) with the upper quartile. The upper quartile is the value below which about 75% of data lie.
Step 2

From the cumulative frequency table, positions \(69\)–\(96\) all take the value \(8\). Both \(87\) and \(88\) lie in this range, so:

\[ Q_3 = (8 + 8) \div 2 = 8 \]
Upper quartile \(= 8 \ne 10\), so option D is false.
Hint: The full five-number summary is \(\;6,\;6,\;7,\;8,\;10\). Only the lower-quartile claim (option C) matches.

Summary — Answers at a Glance

  • Total number of data: \(n = 116\)
  • Mode \(= 7\)   (largest frequency is 36, at 7 merits)  → A is false
  • Median \(= (7+7)\div2 = 7\)   (58th & 59th data)  → B is false
  • Lower quartile \(= (6+6)\div2 = 6\)   (29th & 30th data)  → C is true
  • Upper quartile \(= (8+8)\div2 = 8\)   (87th & 88th data)  → D is false
  • Five-number summary: \(\;6,\;6,\;7,\;8,\;10\)
  • Correct answer:   C
