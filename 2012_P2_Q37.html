Question  Let \(a_n\) be the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence. If \(a_{18}=26\) and \(a_{23}=61\), which of the following are true?

  1. I. \(\;a_{14}<0\)
  2. II. \(\;a_{1}-a_{2}<0\)
  3. III. \(\;a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\dots+a_{27}>0\)

A. I and II only    B. I and III only
C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

1

Set up the general term

General Term of an Arithmetic Sequence \(a_{n}=a+(n-1)d\) The \(17\)th and \(22\)nd terms give \(a+17d=26\) and \(a+22d=61\), two equations in \(a\) and \(d\).
Let \(a\) be the first term and \(d\) the common difference. Then \[a_n = a+(n-1)d.\] Substituting the two given terms: \[\begin{cases} a+17d=26\\ a+22d=61\end{cases}\]
Hint: two data points → two equations in \(a\) and \(d\).
2

Solve for \(a\) and \(d\)

Elimination Method for Simultaneous Equations \((a+22d)-(a+17d)=61-26\) Subtracting removes \(a\) at once and leaves \(5d=35\), so \(d=7\).
Subtract: \((a+22d)-(a+17d)=61-26\Rightarrow 5d=35\Rightarrow d=7\).
Back-substitute: \(a+17(7)=26\Rightarrow a=26-119=-93\). \[\boxed{a=-93,\qquad d=7}\] So \(a_n=-93+7(n-1)=7n-100\).
A positive \(d=7\) means the sequence increases.
3

Check statement I: \(a_{14}<0\) ?

\[a_{14}=-93+13(7)=-93+91=-2<0.\] ✓ Statement I is TRUE.
The sequence crosses zero between \(n=14\) and \(n=15\) (since \(7n-100=0\Rightarrow n=14.29\)).
4

Check statement II: \(a_1-a_2<0\) ?

\[a_1=-93,\qquad a_2=-93+7=-86.\] \[a_1-a_2=(-93)-(-86)=-7<0.\] ✓ Statement II is TRUE.
Since \(d=7>0\), each term is bigger than the previous one, so \(a_1-a_2=-d<0\) always.
5

Check statement III: \(S_{27}=a_1+\dots+a_{27}>0\) ?

Sum of the First n Terms of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S_{n}=\dfrac{n}{2}\big[\,2a+(n-1)d\,\big]\) Put \(n=27\), \(a=-93\) and \(d=7\) to test whether \(S_{27}\) is positive.
Using \(S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\big[\,2a+(n-1)d\,\big]\) with \(n=27\): \[S_{27}=\frac{27}{2}\big[\,2(-93)+(27-1)(7)\,\big]\] \[=\frac{27}{2}\big[-186+182\big]=\frac{27}{2}(-4)=-54<0.\] ✗ Statement III is FALSE.
Early negative terms dominate the sum — the average of \(a_1\) and \(a_{27}\) is negative.
6

Summarise and choose the option

I ✓\(a_{14}=-2<0\) — TRUE II ✓\(a_1-a_2=-7<0\) — TRUE III ✗\(S_{27}=-54\not>0\) — FALSE

Only I and II are true.

Match with the options: "I and II only" is option A.
Answer: ​ A  (I and II only)

Question. In an arithmetic sequence, the \(7\)th term is \(11\) and the \(12\)th term is \(31\).
(a) Find the first term and the common difference.
(b) Is the \(4\)th term negative?
(c) Find the sum of the first \(10\) terms.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let the first term be \(a\) and the common difference \(d\). Then \(a+6d=11\) and \(a+11d=31\).
Step 2. Subtracting: \(5d=20\), so \(d=4\);
then \(a=11-6(4)=-13\). Hence \(a_{n}=-13+4(n-1)=4n-17\).
Step 3. (b) \(a_{4}=4(4)-17=-1<0\), so yes, the \(4\)th term is negative. (The sequence is zero when \(4n-17=0\), i.e. \(n=4.25\).)
Step 4. (c) \(S_{10}=\dfrac{10}{2}\big[2(-13)+(10-1)(4)\big]=5(-26+36)=50\).
Step 5. Check the terms: \(-13,-9,-5,-1,3,7,11,15,19,23\) add up to \(50\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(a=-13\), \(d=4\) (b) yes, \(a_{4}=-1<0\) (c) \(S_{10}=50\)
