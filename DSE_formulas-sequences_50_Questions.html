Type 1

Substitution into Formulas and Change of Subject · 7 questions

Rearranging formulas, including x on both sides

Core idea: Substituting means replacing each letter by its value, with brackets round negatives. Changing the subject is the same balancing process used to solve an equation: clear fractions first, expand brackets, then collect every term containing the new subject on one side and factorise it out before dividing. If the subject appears in a denominator, multiply through by that denominator first.

\(\frac{a}{b}=c\Rightarrow a=bc\)\(Ax+B=Cx\Rightarrow x(C-A)=B\)\(\text{reverse operations in reverse order}\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Given \(v=u+at\), find \(v\) when \(u=8\), \(a=-2.5\) and \(t=6\).

Answer\(v=-7\)
  1. Substitute each value: \(v=8+(-2.5)(6)\).
  2. \(=8-15\).
  3. \(=-7\).
Common mistake

The deceleration \(-2.5\) must keep its sign: \(8+(-2.5)(6)=8-15\), not \(8+15\).

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Make \(x\) the subject of the formula \(y=3x-7\).

Answer\(x=\frac{y+7}{3}\)
  1. Add \(7\) to both sides: \(y+7=3x\).
  2. Divide both sides by \(3\): \(x=\frac{y+7}{3}\).
  3. Check with \(x=2\): then \(y=6-7=-1\), and \(\frac{-1+7}{3}=2\) ✓.
Q3★★☆☆3 marks

Make \(r\) the subject of \(A=\pi r^{2}\), where \(r>0\).

Answer\(r=\sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}\)
  1. Divide both sides by \(\pi\): \(\frac{A}{\pi}=r^{2}\).
  2. Take the positive square root, since \(r>0\): \(r=\sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}\).
  3. Check with \(r=3\): \(A=9\pi\), and \(\sqrt{\frac{9\pi}{\pi}}=\sqrt{9}=3\) ✓.
Common mistake

Do not write \(r=\frac{A}{\pi}\) — the square must be undone by a square root, not by dividing.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

Make \(a\) the subject of \(S=\frac{n}{2}(a+l)\).

Answer\(a=\frac{2S}{n}-l\)
  1. Multiply both sides by \(2\): \(2S=n(a+l)\).
  2. Divide by \(n\): \(\frac{2S}{n}=a+l\).
  3. Subtract \(l\): \(a=\frac{2S}{n}-l\).
  4. Check with \(n=4,\ a=3,\ l=7\): \(S=\frac{4}{2}(10)=20\), and \(\frac{40}{4}-7=10-7=3\) ✓.
Common mistake

Multiply by \(2\) before dividing by \(n\); dividing only part of the right side by \(n\) gives \(\frac{2S}{n}=a+\frac{l}{n}\), which is wrong.

Q5★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2026 Paper 1 Q1 ↗

Make \(x\) the subject of the formula \(\frac{x}{3}-1=\frac{2x-y}{4}\).

Answer\(x=\frac{3y-12}{2}\)
  1. Multiply every term by the L.C.M. \(12\): \(4x-12=3(2x-y)\).
  2. Expand the right side: \(4x-12=6x-3y\).
  3. Collect the \(x\) terms on one side: \(-12+3y=6x-4x\), so \(-12+3y=2x\).
  4. Divide by \(2\): \(x=\frac{3y-12}{2}\).
  5. Check with \(y=6\): \(x=3\), and the original gives \(\frac{3}{3}-1=0\) and \(\frac{6-6}{4}=0\) ✓.
Common mistake

Multiplying by \(12\) must clear both denominators: \(\frac{x}{3}\times 12=4x\) and \(\frac{2x-y}{4}\times 12=3(2x-y)\). Forgetting the bracket loses the \(-3y\).

Q6★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 1 Q2 ↗

Make \(y\) the subject of the formula \(\frac{2y+3}{y-1}=x\).

Answer\(y=\frac{x+3}{x-2}\)
  1. Multiply both sides by \((y-1)\): \(2y+3=x(y-1)\).
  2. Expand: \(2y+3=xy-x\).
  3. Collect the \(y\) terms on one side: \(2y-xy=-x-3\).
  4. Factorise: \(y(2-x)=-x-3\).
  5. Divide by \((2-x)\) and multiply top and bottom by \(-1\): \(y=\frac{-x-3}{2-x}=\frac{x+3}{x-2}\).
  6. Check with \(x=5\): \(y=\frac{8}{3}\), and \(\frac{2(8/3)+3}{8/3-1}=\frac{25/3}{5/3}=5\) ✓.
Common mistake

After factorising you get \(y(2-x)\), so dividing gives \(\frac{-x-3}{2-x}\). Flipping only one sign when tidying up gives \(\frac{x+3}{2-x}\), which is the negative of the correct answer.

Q7★★★☆5 marks

The total surface area of a closed cylinder is \(A=2\pi r(r+h)\).
(a) Make \(h\) the subject of the formula.
(b) Find \(h\) when \(A=176\pi\) and \(r=4\).

Answer(a) \(h=\frac{A}{2\pi r}-r\)　(b) \(h=18\)
  1. (a) Divide both sides by \(2\pi r\): \(\frac{A}{2\pi r}=r+h\).
  2. Subtract \(r\): \(h=\frac{A}{2\pi r}-r\).
  3. (b) Substitute \(A=176\pi\), \(r=4\): \(h=\frac{176\pi}{2\pi(4)}-4\).
  4. \(=\frac{176\pi}{8\pi}-4=22-4\).
  5. \(=18\).
  6. Check: \(A=2\pi(4)(4+18)=8\pi(22)=176\pi\) ✓.
Common mistake

Divide by \(2\pi r\) as a single block: \(\frac{A}{2\pi r}=r+h\). Dividing term by term, e.g. subtracting \(r\) before dividing, changes the answer completely.

Type 2

Constructing a Formula from Words or a Pattern · 7 questions

Turning a description or table into an algebraic rule

Core idea: A formula is a rule that works for every input, so it needs a variable part and a fixed part. From a word problem, separate the one-off charge (the constant) from the rate (the coefficient), then test the formula on one given case. From a number pattern, subtract consecutive terms to find the step, then adjust the constant so that the first term comes out right.

\(T_{n}=\text{step}\times n+\text{constant}\)\(\text{total}=\text{fixed}+\text{rate}\times\text{quantity}\)\(\text{check } T_{1}\text{ and }T_{2}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

A delivery firm charges a fixed fee of $35 plus $4.50 for each kilometre travelled. Write a formula for the charge \$C when the distance is \(d\) km, and find \(C\) when \(d=12\).

Answer\(C=35+4.5d\), and \(C=89\)
  1. The fixed fee \(35\) is paid whatever the distance, so it is the constant.
  2. The distance part is \(4.5\times d\), giving \(C=35+4.5d\).
  3. Put \(d=12\): \(C=35+4.5(12)=35+54=89\).
  4. So the charge for \(12\) km is \$89.
Common mistake

Do not write \(C=(35+4.5)d\): the \(35\) is paid once, not once per kilometre.

Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

A pattern of squares is made from matchsticks. The 1st figure uses \(5\) matchsticks and each new figure adds \(4\) more.
(a) Write down a formula for the number of matchsticks \(M\) in the \(n\)th figure.
(b) Find the number of matchsticks in the 12th figure.

Answer(a) \(M=4n+1\)　(b) 49
  1. Each figure adds \(4\) matchsticks, so the variable part is \(4n\).
  2. Try \(M=4n+c\) with \(n=1\): \(4+c=5\), so \(c=1\).
  3. Therefore \(M=4n+1\).
  4. (b) \(M=4(12)+1=48+1=49\).
  5. Check with \(n=2\): \(4(2)+1=9\), which is \(5+4\) ✓.
Common mistake

The first figure already has \(5\) sticks, so the rule is \(4n+1\), not \(4n\). Substituting \(n=1\) is the quickest way to find the constant.

Q10★★☆☆3 marks

The table shows the number of tiles \(T\) used in a border of length \(n\):

\(n\)1234
\(T\)7121722

(a) Find a formula for \(T\) in terms of \(n\).
(b) How many tiles are needed for a border of length \(20\)?

Answer(a) \(T=5n+2\)　(b) 102
  1. The values increase by \(12-7=5\) each time, so the coefficient of \(n\) is \(5\).
  2. Write \(T=5n+c\) and use \(n=1,\ T=7\): \(5+c=7\), so \(c=2\).
  3. Hence \(T=5n+2\); check \(n=3\): \(15+2=17\) ✓.
  4. (b) \(T=5(20)+2=100+2=102\) tiles.
Common mistake

\(T=5n\) alone gives \(5\) when \(n=1\), but the table says \(7\). The constant \(+2\) is found by substituting one known pair.

Q11★★☆☆3 marks

A gym charges a joining fee of \$300 and then \$180 per month. Let \$T be the total amount paid after \(m\) months.
(a) Write a formula for \(T\) in terms of \(m\).
(b) Find \(m\) when \(T=2100\).

Answer(a) \(T=300+180m\)　(b) \(m=10\)
  1. (a) The joining fee \(300\) is paid once and \(180\) is paid every month: \(T=300+180m\).
  2. (b) Put \(T=2100\): \(300+180m=2100\).
  3. Subtract \(300\): \(180m=1800\).
  4. Divide by \(180\): \(m=10\).
  5. Check: \(300+180(10)=300+1800=2100\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(180m\) counts only the monthly payments — the \(300\) is separate. Answers such as \(m=\frac{2100}{180}=11.67\) forget it.

Q12★★☆☆4 marks

Dots are arranged in hexagonal rings. The 1st ring has \(6\) dots and each later ring has \(5\) more dots than the one before.
(a) Write down a formula for the number of dots \(D\) in the \(n\)th ring.
(b) Which ring contains exactly \(101\) dots?

Answer(a) \(D=5n+1\)　(b) the 20th ring
  1. (a) The step is \(5\), so \(D=5n+c\); using \(n=1,\ D=6\) gives \(5+c=6\), so \(c=1\).
  2. Hence \(D=5n+1\); check \(n=2\): \(11=6+5\) ✓.
  3. (b) Solve \(5n+1=101\): \(5n=100\), so \(n=20\).
  4. Check: \(5(20)+1=101\) ✓.
Common mistake

Part (b) asks for the ring number, so the answer is \(n=20\), not \(101\). Rearranging \(5n+1=101\) changes the subject, it does not give the number of dots.

Q13★★★☆4 marks

A tank holds \(600\) litres of water and is emptied at a steady rate of \(15\) litres per minute. After \(t\) minutes the volume is \(V\) litres.
(a) Write a formula for \(V\) in terms of \(t\).
(b) Find \(V\) after \(25\) minutes.
(c) How long does the tank take to empty?

Answer(a) \(V=600-15t\)　(b) \(225\) litres　(c) \(40\) minutes
  1. (a) The volume decreases by \(15t\), so \(V=600-15t\).
  2. (b) \(V=600-15(25)=600-375=225\).
  3. (c) Empty means \(V=0\): \(600-15t=0\), so \(15t=600\) and \(t=40\).
  4. Check (c): after \(40\) minutes the water removed is \(15\times 40=600\) litres, exactly the capacity ✓.
Common mistake

A decreasing quantity needs a minus sign: \(V=600-15t\), not \(600+15t\). Also the formula only makes sense for \(0\le t\le 40\).

Q14★★★☆5 marks

A sales assistant earns a basic salary of \$12\,000 per month plus a commission of \(5\%\) of her sales.
(a) Write a formula for her monthly income \$S when her sales are \$\(s\).
(b) Find \(S\) when \(s=80\,000\).
(c) Find the sales needed for a monthly income of \$19\,500.

Answer(a) \(S=12\,000+0.05s\)　(b) \$16\,000　(c) \$150\,000
  1. (a) \(5\%\) of \(s\) is \(0.05s\), so \(S=12\,000+0.05s\).
  2. (b) \(S=12\,000+0.05(80\,000)=12\,000+4000=16\,000\).
  3. (c) Put \(S=19\,500\): \(12\,000+0.05s=19\,500\).
  4. Subtract \(12\,000\): \(0.05s=7500\).
  5. Divide by \(0.05\): \(s=150\,000\).
  6. Check: \(5\%\) of \(150\,000\) is \(7500\), and \(12\,000+7500=19\,500\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(5\%\) is \(0.05\), not \(5\). Using \(5s\) makes the income absurd; also in (c) divide by \(0.05\) (multiply by \(20\)), not by \(5\).

Type 3

Arithmetic Sequences: Common Difference and nth Term · 7 questions

Using \(T_{n}=a+(n-1)d\)

Core idea: In an arithmetic sequence each term is obtained by adding the same number \(d\), the common difference. Always check with \(T_{2}-T_{1}=T_{3}-T_{2}\). The nth term is \(T_{n}=a+(n-1)d\) — note \(n-1\), not \(n\). Two given terms give two simultaneous equations in \(a\) and \(d\); subtract them to get \(d\) first.

\(d=T_{n+1}-T_{n}\)\(T_{n}=a+(n-1)d\)\(T_{m}-T_{k}=(m-k)d\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

For the arithmetic sequence \(5,\ 9,\ 13,\ \ldots\), find the common difference \(d\) and the 10th term.

Answer\(d=4\), \(T_{10}=41\)
  1. \(d=9-5=4\) (check: \(13-9=4\) ✓).
  2. \(T_{n}=a+(n-1)d\) with \(a=5\), \(d=4\).
  3. \(T_{10}=5+(10-1)(4)=5+9(4)=5+36=41\).
Common mistake

There are \(9\) steps from the 1st term to the 10th, not \(10\). Using \(10d\) gives \(45\).

Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the 20th term of the arithmetic sequence \(7,\ 4,\ 1,\ \ldots\).

Answer\(-50\)
  1. \(d=4-7=-3\).
  2. \(T_{20}=7+(20-1)(-3)\).
  3. \(=7+19(-3)=7-57\).
  4. \(=-50\).
Common mistake

\(19\times(-3)=-57\), so the term is \(7-57=-50\). Dropping the minus sign gives \(+64\).

Q17★★☆☆3 marks

In an arithmetic sequence, the 5th term is \(17\) and the 9th term is \(33\). Find the first term \(a\) and the common difference \(d\), and hence write down the general term \(T_{n}\).

Answer\(a=1\), \(d=4\), \(T_{n}=4n-3\)
  1. \(T_{5}=a+4d=17\) … (1) and \(T_{9}=a+8d=33\) … (2).
  2. (2) − (1): \(4d=16\), so \(d=4\).
  3. Substitute into (1): \(a+16=17\), so \(a=1\).
  4. \(T_{n}=1+(n-1)(4)=4n-3\).
  5. Check: \(T_{5}=20-3=17\) ✓ and \(T_{9}=36-3=33\) ✓.
Common mistake

Subtracting the equations eliminates \(a\) because both terms use the same \(a\). Writing \(T_{9}=a+9d\) instead of \(a+8d\) gives \(d=3.4\), which does not fit the data.

Q18★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

Determine whether each of the following is an arithmetic sequence. Justify your answer.
(a) \(8-4a,\ 9-5a,\ 10-6a\)
(b) \(3,\ 7,\ 12,\ 18,\ \ldots\)

Answer(a) Yes, common difference \(1-a\)　(b) No, the differences are \(4,5,6\)
  1. A sequence is arithmetic exactly when consecutive differences are equal.
  2. (a) \(T_{2}-T_{1}=(9-5a)-(8-4a)=1-a\).
  3. \(T_{3}-T_{2}=(10-6a)-(9-5a)=1-a\).
  4. Both differences equal \(1-a\), so (a) is an arithmetic sequence (whatever the value of \(a\)).
  5. (b) \(7-3=4\), \(12-7=5\), \(18-12=6\). The differences are not constant, so (b) is not arithmetic.
Common mistake

In (a) the terms involve the letter \(a\), but \(a\) is a fixed constant here — the differences only have to match each other, not be numbers.

Q19★★☆☆4 marks

Insert three numbers between \(4\) and \(24\) so that all five numbers form an arithmetic sequence.

Answer\(9,\ 14,\ 19\)
  1. Five terms means four equal steps from \(4\) to \(24\).
  2. \(T_{5}=a+4d\), so \(4+4d=24\).
  3. \(4d=20\), giving \(d=5\).
  4. The terms are \(4,\ 9,\ 14,\ 19,\ 24\).
  5. Check: each step is \(5\) and the last term is \(24\) ✓.
Common mistake

Three inserted numbers create four gaps, so divide by \(4\), not by \(3\). Dividing by \(3\) gives \(d=\frac{20}{3}\) and non-integer terms.

Q20★★★☆5 marks

In an arithmetic sequence, the 3rd term is \(10\) and the 11th term is \(42\).
(a) Find the first term and the common difference.
(b) Find the first term of the sequence that is greater than \(200\).

Answer(a) \(a=2,\ d=4\)　(b) the 51st term, which is \(202\)
  1. (a) \(T_{3}=a+2d=10\) … (1) and \(T_{11}=a+10d=42\) … (2).
  2. (2) − (1): \(8d=32\), so \(d=4\). Then \(a+8=10\), so \(a=2\).
  3. Hence \(T_{n}=2+4(n-1)=4n-2\).
  4. (b) Solve \(4n-2>200\): \(4n>202\), so \(n>50.5\).
  5. The least integer is \(n=51\), and \(T_{51}=4(51)-2=202\).
  6. Check: \(T_{50}=198<200\) and \(T_{51}=202>200\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(n>50.5\) means the first integer is \(51\), not \(50\). Always test both sides of the boundary.

Q21★★★☆5 marks

A theatre has \(18\) seats in the first row. Each row behind has \(2\) more seats than the row in front, and there are \(25\) rows.
(a) Write down a formula for the number of seats in the \(n\)th row.
(b) Which row has exactly \(60\) seats?
(c) Is there a row with exactly \(75\) seats? Explain.

Answer(a) \(T_{n}=2n+16\)　(b) the 22nd row　(c) No, since \(2n+16\) is always even
  1. (a) \(a=18\), \(d=2\), so \(T_{n}=18+(n-1)(2)=2n+16\).
  2. (b) Solve \(2n+16=60\): \(2n=44\), so \(n=22\).
  3. Check: row 22 has \(2(22)+16=60\) seats ✓.
  4. (c) Solve \(2n+16=75\): \(2n=59\), so \(n=29.5\), which is not a whole number.
  5. Also \(2n+16=2(n+8)\) is always even, so it can never equal the odd number \(75\). Hence no such row exists.
Common mistake

In (c) a non-integer \(n\) means no such row exists — do not round \(29.5\) up to \(30\) and claim row 30.

Type 4

Arithmetic Sums: Number of Terms and Sum of an A.P. · 7 questions

Counting the terms first, then applying \(S_{n}\)

Core idea: Two formulas give the sum of the first \(n\) terms: \(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}(a+l)\) when you know the last term, and \(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}\left[2a+(n-1)d\right]\) otherwise. Most marks are lost by mis-counting \(n\), so always find \(n\) from \(l=a+(n-1)d\) before summing, and remember \(T_{n}=S_{n}-S_{n-1}\).

\(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}(a+l)\)\(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\)\(n=\frac{l-a}{d}+1\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the sum of the first \(20\) terms of the arithmetic sequence \(3,\ 7,\ 11,\ \ldots\).

Answer\(820\)
  1. \(a=3\), \(d=4\), \(n=20\).
  2. First find the last term: \(T_{20}=3+19(4)=79\).
  3. \(S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}(3+79)=10(82)\).
  4. \(=820\).
Common mistake

\(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}(a+l)\) uses the first and last terms. Using \(T_{1}\) twice, or \(n=21\), gives the wrong total.

Q23★☆☆☆3 marks

Find the sum of the arithmetic series \(2+5+8+\cdots+62\).

Answer\(672\)
  1. \(a=2\), \(d=3\), last term \(l=62\).
  2. Find \(n\): \(62=2+(n-1)(3)\), so \(60=3(n-1)\) and \(n-1=20\), giving \(n=21\).
  3. \(S_{21}=\frac{21}{2}(2+62)=\frac{21}{2}(64)=21\times 32\).
  4. \(=672\).
  5. Check: \(S_{21}\) should have \(21\) terms, and the average term is \(\frac{2+62}{2}=32\), so \(21\times 32=672\) ✓.
Common mistake

There are \(21\) terms, not \(20\): the number of terms is \(\frac{62-2}{3}+1=21\).

Q24★★☆☆4 marks

Find the sum of all the multiples of \(7\) between \(100\) and \(300\).

Answer\(5586\)
  1. The first multiple of \(7\) above \(100\) is \(105=7\times 15\).
  2. The last multiple below \(300\) is \(294=7\times 42\).
  3. Number of terms: \(\frac{294-105}{7}+1=27+1=28\).
  4. \(S_{28}=\frac{28}{2}(105+294)=14(399)\).
  5. \(=5586\).
  6. Check: \(399\times 14=399\times 10+399\times 4=3990+1596=5586\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(100\) and \(300\) are not multiples of \(7\), so the series starts at \(105\) and ends at \(294\). Using \(100\) or \(300\) as an endpoint corrupts the count.

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

The sum of the first \(n\) terms of a sequence is \(S_{n}=n^{2}+3n\).
(a) Find the 10th term.
(b) Find the first term.

Answer(a) \(T_{10}=22\)　(b) \(T_{1}=4\)
  1. (a) \(T_{10}=S_{10}-S_{9}\).
  2. \(S_{10}=100+30=130\) and \(S_{9}=81+27=108\).
  3. \(T_{10}=130-108=22\).
  4. (b) \(S_{1}\) contains only the first term, so \(T_{1}=S_{1}=1+3=4\).
  5. Check: the sequence is \(4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ldots\) (common difference \(2\)), and \(S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}[8+2(n-1)]=n(n+3)=n^{2}+3n\) ✓ — so \(T_{10}=4+9(2)=22\) ✓.
Common mistake

The 10th term is not \(S_{10}\): \(S_{10}\) is the total of the first ten terms. Use \(T_{n}=S_{n}-S_{n-1}\).

Q26★★☆☆4 marks

Find the sum of all the three-digit numbers that are divisible by \(5\).

Answer\(98\,550\)
  1. The numbers are \(100,\ 105,\ 110,\ \ldots,\ 995\), an arithmetic sequence with \(d=5\).
  2. Number of terms: \(n=\frac{995-100}{5}+1=179+1=180\).
  3. \(S_{180}=\frac{180}{2}(100+995)=90(1095)\).
  4. \(=98\,550\).
  5. Check: the average term is \(\frac{1095}{2}=547.5\) and \(180\times 547.5=98\,550\) ✓.
Common mistake

The first three-digit multiple of \(5\) is \(100\) and the last is \(995\), not \(999\). Including \(999\) breaks the common difference of \(5\).

Q27★★★☆5 marks

A concert hall has \(20\) seats in the first row. Each row behind has \(3\) more seats than the row in front, and there are \(30\) rows.
(a) Find the number of seats in the last row.
(b) Find the total number of seats in the hall.
(c) A second hall has \(30\) rows of \(30\) seats each. Find the difference in the number of seats between the two halls.

Answer(a) \(107\) seats　(b) \(1905\) seats　(c) \(1005\) seats

Type 5

Geometric Sequences: Common Ratio and nth Term · 7 questions

Using \(T_{n}=ar^{n-1}\)

Core idea: In a geometric sequence each term is the previous term multiplied by the same number \(r\), the common ratio: check \(\frac{T_{2}}{T_{1}}=\frac{T_{3}}{T_{2}}\). The nth term is \(T_{n}=ar^{n-1}\). Two given terms divide to eliminate \(a\) and give \(r^{m-k}\), so take the appropriate root. A negative ratio makes the signs alternate, and \(r\) may be a fraction below \(1\), so the terms shrink.

\(r=\frac{T_{n+1}}{T_{n}}\)\(T_{n}=ar^{n-1}\)\(\frac{T_{m}}{T_{k}}=r^{m-k}\)
Q29★☆☆☆2 marks

For the geometric sequence \(3,\ 6,\ 12,\ \ldots\), find the common ratio and the 8th term.

Answer\(r=2\), \(T_{8}=384\)

  2. \(T_{n}=ar^{n-1}\) with \(a=3\), \(r=2\).
  3. \(T_{8}=3(2)^{7}=3(128)=384\).
Common mistake

The 8th term uses \(r^{7}\), not \(r^{8}\). There are only \(7\) multiplications from the 1st term to the 8th.

Geometric Sums and Applications · 7 questions

Growth, depreciation, instalments and sums to infinity

Core idea: The sum of the first \(n\) terms of a geometric sequence is \(S_{n}=\frac{a(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}\) for \(r\neq 1\), which also works as \(\frac{a(1-r^{n})}{1-r}\). If \(|r|<1\) the series converges and the sum to infinity is \(S_{\infty}=\frac{a}{1-r}\). Repeated percentage change is geometric: growth by \(p\%\) per period means \(r=1+\frac{p}{100}\), depreciation means \(r=1-\frac{p}{100}\).

\(S_{n}=\frac{a(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}\)\(S_{\infty}=\frac{a}{1-r},\ |r|<1\)\(\text{growth: } r=1+\frac{p}{100}\)
Q36★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the sum of the first \(6\) terms of the geometric sequence \(2,\ 6,\ 18,\ \ldots\).

Answer\(728\)
  1. \(a=2\), \(r=3\), \(n=6\).
  2. \(S_{6}=\frac{2(3^{6}-1)}{3-1}=\frac{2(729-1)}{2}\).
  3. \(=728\).
  4. Check by adding directly: \(2+6+18+54+162+486=728\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(3^{6}=729\), not \(18\). Also the denominator is \(r-1=2\), not \(r\).

Dividing by \(1-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\) means multiplying by \(2\). Forgetting this factor gives \(\frac{255}{256}\) instead of \(\frac{255}{128}\).

Exam Level: Mixed and Recursive Sequences · 8 questions

Recurrence relations, real-life patterns, multi-part sums

Core idea: A recurrence relation defines each term from earlier ones, so build the terms one at a time (or work backwards when later terms are given). If the increments themselves form an arithmetic sequence, the total is an arithmetic sum. In long questions identify which tool each part needs: \(T_{n}\) for a single term, \(S_{n}\) for a total, and an inequality when the question asks for a least value.

\(T_{n+1}=f(T_{n})\)\(T_{n}=S_{n}-S_{n-1}\)\(\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2k+2)=n(n+3)\)

Each term needs the previous term, so \(T_{3}\) cannot be found straight from \(T_{1}\). Skipping \(T_{2}\) gives \(7\) instead of \(15\).

  1. Mary's salaries form an arithmetic sequence with \(a=300\,000\), \(d=10\,000\); Peter's form a geometric sequence with \(a=250\,000\), \(r=1.05\). The year 2020 is the 1st term.
  2. (a) 2029 is the 10th term: \(T_{10}=300\,000+9(10\,000)=390\,000\).
  3. (b) Peter: \(T_{10}=250\,000(1.05)^{9}\). Since \((1.05)^{9}=1.5513282\ldots\), \(T_{10}=387\,832.05\ldots\approx 387\,832\).
  4. (c) \(S_{10}=\frac{10}{2}(300\,000+390\,000)=5(690\,000)=3\,450\,000\).
  5. (d) In 2029 Peter earns \(387\,832<390\,000\), so test the next year. Mary in 2030: \(390\,000+10\,000=400\,000\).
  6. Peter in 2030: \(250\,000(1.05)^{10}=250\,000(1.6288946\ldots)=407\,223.6\), about \$407\,224.
  7. Since \(407\,224>400\,000\), Peter's salary first exceeds Mary's in 2030.
  8. Check the previous year really is below: \(387\,832<390\,000\) ✓, so the crossing lies between 2029 and 2030.
Common mistake

Year 10 is 2029, because the year is \(2020+(n-1)\). For 2030 the geometric term uses \((1.05)^{10}\); using \((1.05)^{9}\) keeps Peter below Mary and shifts the answer.
