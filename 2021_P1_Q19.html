HKDSE Math 2021 Paper 1 Q19

Quadratic Functions & Circle

Let $f(x)=x^2-12kx-14x+36k^2+89k+53$, where $k$ is a positive constant. On the same rectangular coordinate system, denote the vertex of the graph of $y=f(x)$ and the vertex of the graph of $y=f(14-x)$ by $Q$ and $R$ respectively.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, express, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of $Q$. [2 marks]

(b) Write down, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of $R$. [1 mark]

(c) The coordinates of the point $S$ are $(7,\;4-3k)$. Denote the inscribed circle of $\triangle QRS$ by $C$. [9 marks]

(i) Express, in terms of $k$, the equation of the straight line which passes through $Q$ and $S$.

(ii) Express, in terms of $k$, the equation of $C$.

(iii) Suppose that $QS$ is the tangent to $C$ at the point $T$. Let $U$ be the centre of $C$. It is given that the coordinates of the point $V$ are $(-29,\,-14)$. Is it possible that $STUV$ is a rectangle? Explain your answer.

Step-by-step solution

(a) Coordinates of $Q$ (vertex of $y=f(x)$)

Rewrite $f(x)$ and complete the square:

$f(x)=x^2-(12k+14)x+36k^2+89k+53$

$=\left[x-(6k+7)\right]^2-(6k+7)^2+36k^2+89k+53$

$=\left[x-(6k+7)\right]^2-\left(36k^2+84k+49\right)+36k^2+89k+53$

$=\left[x-(6k+7)\right]^2+5k+4$

$Q=\left(6k+7,\;5k+4\right)$

(b) Coordinates of $R$ (vertex of $y=f(14-x)$)

Using the completed-square form of $f$:

$f(14-x)=\left[(14-x)-(6k+7)\right]^2+5k+4=\left(7-6k-x\right)^2+5k+4$

$=\left[x-(7-6k)\right]^2+5k+4$

$R=\left(7-6k,\;5k+4\right)$

Note: $Q$ and $R$ have the same $y$-coordinate, so $QR$ is horizontal and symmetric about $x=7$.

(c)(i) Equation of the line through $Q$ and $S$

$Q=\left(6k+7,\;5k+4\right)$ and $S=\left(7,\;4-3k\right)$. The slope is

$m=\dfrac{(4-3k)-(5k+4)}{7-(6k+7)}=\dfrac{-8k}{-6k}=\dfrac{4}{3}$

Using point $S$:

$y-(4-3k)=\dfrac{4}{3}(x-7)\;\Rightarrow\;3y-12+9k=4x-28$

$4x-3y-9k-16=0$

(c)(ii) Equation of the inscribed circle $C$

First find the side lengths of $\triangle QRS$. Since $Q$ and $R$ lie on $y=5k+4$ and $S$ lies on $x=7$:

$QR=(6k+7)-(7-6k)=12k$

$QS=RS=\sqrt{(6k)^2+(8k)^2}=10k$

The triangle is isosceles with height $8k$ (from $S$ to $QR$). Its area and semi-perimeter are

$\text{Area}=\dfrac{1}{2}(12k)(8k)=48k^2,\qquad s=\dfrac{12k+10k+10k}{2}=16k$

so the inradius is $r=\dfrac{\text{Area}}{s}=\dfrac{48k^2}{16k}=3k$.

The incenter $U$ lies on the axis of symmetry $x=7$, at distance $r$ below $QR$:

$U=\left(7,\;(5k+4)-3k\right)=\left(7,\;2k+4\right)$

$C:\quad (x-7)^2+(y-2k-4)^2=9k^2$

(c)(iii) Is $STUV$ a rectangle?

Since $QS$ is tangent to $C$ at $T$ and $U$ is the centre, the radius $UT$ is perpendicular to $QS$, i.e. $UT\perp ST$. For $STUV$ to be a rectangle, the opposite sides must be parallel:

  • $UV\parallel ST\parallel QS$ (slope $\frac{4}{3}$):

$\text{slope of }UV=\dfrac{-14-(2k+4)}{-29-7}=\dfrac{9+k}{18}=\dfrac{4}{3}\;\Rightarrow\;k=15$

  • $SV\parallel UT\perp QS$ (slope $-\frac{3}{4}$):

$\text{slope of }SV=\dfrac{-14-(4-3k)}{-29-7}=\dfrac{6-k}{12}=-\dfrac{3}{4}\;\Rightarrow\;k=15$

Both conditions give the same value $k=15$, which is a positive constant. Hence the quadrilateral $STUV$ has $ST\parallel UV$, $TU\parallel SV$ and a right angle at $T$, so it is a rectangle.

Yes — $STUV$ is a rectangle when $k=15$.
