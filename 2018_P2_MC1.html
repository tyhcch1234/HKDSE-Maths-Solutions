HKDSE Math 2018 · Paper 2 · Q1

Simplify an Exponential Expression Using the Laws of Indices

Multiple Choice · Topic 8: Laws of Indices, Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Laws of Indices Change of Base Power of a Power Quotient Law

QQuestion

$\dfrac{8^{\,2n+1}}{4^{\,3n+1}} \;=\; ?$
A.  $1$
B.  $2$
C.  $2^{\,n}$
D.  $2^{-n}$

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

1 Identify the strategy: make the bases the same ▼

The expression mixes bases $8$ and $4$. The key idea is to rewrite both bases as powers of the same small number.

Notice that:

$$8 = 2^{3} \qquad\text{and}\qquad 4 = 2^{2}$$

Since both $8$ and $4$ are powers of $2$, we can convert everything to base $2$ and then apply the index laws.

Reason: The laws of indices (multiplying / dividing powers) only apply directly when the bases are identical. Choosing the smallest prime base $2$ is always a safe move when both numbers are powers of $2$.
2 Convert the numerator $8^{\,2n+1}$ to base $2$ ▼

Replace $8$ by $2^{3}$:

$$8^{\,2n+1} = \left(2^{3}\right)^{\,2n+1}$$

Apply the power-of-a-power law: $\left(a^{m}\right)^{k}=a^{m\times k}$.

$$\left(2^{3}\right)^{\,2n+1} = 2^{\,3(2n+1)} = 2^{\,6n+3}$$
$(a^{m})^{k} = a^{mk}$ — multiply the two exponents. Here $m=3$ and $k=2n+1$, so the new exponent is $3\times(2n+1)=6n+3$.
3 Convert the denominator $4^{\,3n+1}$ to base $2$ ▼

Replace $4$ by $2^{2}$:

$$4^{\,3n+1} = \left(2^{2}\right)^{\,3n+1}$$

Again apply the power-of-a-power law:

$$\left(2^{2}\right)^{\,3n+1} = 2^{\,2(3n+1)} = 2^{\,6n+2}$$
Multiply carefully: $2\times(3n+1) = 6n + 2$. Notice this exponent is one less than the numerator's $6n+3$ — that difference is the whole point of the question!
4 Combine using the quotient law ▼

Now both top and bottom are powers of $2$. Write the fraction:

$$\frac{8^{\,2n+1}}{4^{\,3n+1}} = \frac{2^{\,6n+3}}{2^{\,6n+2}}$$

Apply the quotient law: $\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{k}} = a^{\,m-k}$.

$$\frac{2^{\,6n+3}}{2^{\,6n+2}} = 2^{\,(6n+3)-(6n+2)} = 2^{\,1}$$
$(6n+3)-(6n+2) = 6n+3-6n-2 = 1$. The $6n$ terms cancel out — that is why the answer does not depend on $n$.
5 Evaluate the final power and conclude ▼
$$2^{1} = 2$$

Therefore the expression is a constant equal to $2$, regardless of the value of $n$.

$$\boxed{\;\frac{8^{\,2n+1}}{4^{\,3n+1}} = 2\;}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\textbf{Answer: B}$$

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • am · ak = am+k — product law
  • am ÷ ak = am−k — quotient law
  • (am)k = amk — power-of-a-power law
  • When bases differ but share a common root, rewrite both as powers of the same base (here base $2$).
  • After simplifying, the variable $n$ cancels out — the expression is a constant.
