Mensuration — Area of a quadrilateral split by a perpendicular

QQuestion

In the figure, the area of quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is

  • A. \(144\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • B. \(160\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • C. \(178\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • D. \(288\ \text{cm}^2\)

Given in the figure: \(BC = 26\ \text{cm}\), \(CD = 6\ \text{cm}\), \(AD = 8\ \text{cm}\), \(\angle CAD = 90^\circ\) and \(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\).

Answer: (A)

Key observation: \(\angle CAD = 90^\circ\), so \(\triangle ACD\) is right-angled at \(A\). If \(AB \perp AC\) at \(A\), then \(ABCD\) splits neatly into two right-angled triangles \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle ACD\) that share the side \(AC\).

1Step 1 — Find \(AC\) (the shared side)

Which right-angled triangle is the easiest place to start?

Step 1

In \(\triangle ACD\), the right angle is at \(A\) and \(CD\) is the hypotenuse. By Pythagoras' theorem,

\[ AC = \sqrt{CD^2 + AD^2} = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{36+64} = \sqrt{100} = 10\ \text{cm}. \]
Hint: \(6\!-\!8\!-\!10\) is a scaled \(3\!-\!4\!-\!5\) triple — you can spot it instantly.
\(AC = 10\ \text{cm}\)
0 / 1

2Step 2 — Find \(AB\) using \(\triangle ABC\)

Now use the other right-angled triangle with the known hypotenuse \(BC = 26\).

Step 2

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle BAC = 90^\circ\) (given in the figure), and the hypotenuse is \(BC\). So

\[ AB = \sqrt{BC^2 - AC^2} = \sqrt{26^2 - 10^2} = \sqrt{676 - 100} = \sqrt{576} = 24\ \text{cm}. \]
Hint: \(10\!-\!24\!-\!26\) is another scaled \(5\!-\!12\!-\!13\) triple.
\(AB = 24\ \text{cm}\)
0 / 1

3Step 3 — Areas of the two triangles

Both triangles have known perpendicular sides, so \(\text{Area} = \tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\).

Step 3

Area of \(\triangle ACD\) (right angle at \(A\)):

\[ [ACD] = \frac{AC \times AD}{2} = \frac{10 \times 8}{2} = 24\ \text{cm}^2. \]
Hint: The two legs \(AC\) and \(AD\) are perpendicular, so they can serve directly as base and height.
\([ACD] = 24\ \text{cm}^2\)
Step 4

Area of \(\triangle ABC\) (right angle at \(A\)):

\[ [ABC] = \frac{AB \times AC}{2} = \frac{24 \times 10}{2} = 120\ \text{cm}^2. \]
Hint: Both legs are known: \(AB = 24\) and \(AC = 10\).
\([ABC] = 120\ \text{cm}^2\)
0 / 2

4Step 4 — Total area of \(ABCD\)

The diagonal \(AC\) splits \(ABCD\) into \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle ACD\), so add their areas.

Step 5

Add the two triangular areas:

\[ [ABCD] = [ABC] + [ACD] = 120 + 24 = 144\ \text{cm}^2. \]
Hint: Because \(AC\) is a common side and both triangles have a right angle at \(A\), the quadrilateral area is just the sum — no overlapping or gaps.
\([ABCD] = 144\ \text{cm}^2\)  →  Answer: A
0 / 1

Summary of the Solution

  • Given: \(BC = 26\ \text{cm}\), \(CD = 6\ \text{cm}\), \(AD = 8\ \text{cm}\), \(\angle CAD = 90^\circ\), \(\angle BAC = 90^\circ\).
  • In \(\triangle ACD\): \(AC = \sqrt{6^2+8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\ \text{cm}\)  (Pythagoras).
  • In \(\triangle ABC\): \(AB = \sqrt{26^2 - 10^2} = \sqrt{576} = 24\ \text{cm}\)  (Pythagoras).
  • \([ACD] = \tfrac12(10)(8) = 24\ \text{cm}^2\).
  • \([ABC] = \tfrac12(24)(10) = 120\ \text{cm}^2\).
  • \([ABCD] = 120 + 24 = 144\ \text{cm}^2\).
Final answer: \([ABCD] = 144\ \text{cm}^2\)  →  Option A
