If the price of a souvenir is increased by 70% and then decreased by 60%, find the percentage change in the price of the souvenir.

Step-by-step solution

1

Assign a variable to the original price

Let the original price be \(P\). We will express the final price in terms of \(P\).

Why assign a variable? The actual price is not given, so we use a symbol \(P\) to represent it. The percentage change will be independent of \(P\).
2

Apply the first increase (70%)

After a 70% increase, the new price is: \[ P \times (1 + 70\%) = P \times 1.7 \]

Formula: New value = Original × (1 + percentage increase). Here, \(70\% = 0.7\), so multiplier is \(1.7\).
3

Apply the second change (60% decrease)

Then a 60% decrease acts on the new price: \[ (P \times 1.7) \times (1 - 60\%) = 1.7P \times 0.4 = 0.68P \]

Formula: After a decrease of \(d\%\), multiply by \((1 - d\%)\). Here \(60\% = 0.6\), so multiplier is \(0.4\).
4

Compute the overall percentage change

Percentage change \( = \frac{\text{Final} - \text{Original}}{\text{Original}} \times 100\% \) \[ = \frac{0.68P - P}{P} \times 100\% = \frac{-0.32P}{P} \times 100\% = -32\% \]

Important: The percentage change is negative, meaning a net decrease. Do not simply add the percentages: \(70\% - 60\% = 10\%\) is incorrect because the 60% decrease is applied to the already increased price.
Answer: B. −32%
