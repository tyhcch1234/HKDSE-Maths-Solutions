Bearings of two points from a common origin — solving with parallel lines & the isosceles triangle

Bearings True North Isosceles Triangle Alternate Angles Angle Sum of Δ

QUESTION The problem

[2013 MC #20]

In the figure, the bearing of \(P\) from \(O\) is \(\text{S}86^\circ\text{E}\) and the bearing of \(Q\) from \(O\) is \(\text{N}32^\circ\text{E}\). If \(P\) and \(Q\) are equidistant from \(O\), then the bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\) is

  1. \(\text{N}24^\circ\text{W}\).
  2. \(\text{N}27^\circ\text{W}\).
  3. \(\text{S}24^\circ\text{E}\).
  4. \(\text{S}27^\circ\text{E}\).

(Answer: D)

Bearing of \(P\) from \(O\) = S86°E Bearing of \(Q\) from \(O\) = N32°E \(OP = OQ\) (given)

Drag the slider to spin the whole picture — bearings never change under rotation, only the drawing does. This builds intuition that we may choose any convenient direction for “North”.

STEP 1 Find \(\angle POQ\) at the origin \(O\)

\(86^\circ + \beta = \) 86° \(\beta = \) 32° \(\angle POQ = 180^\circ - 86^\circ - \beta = \) 62°
Step 11M
The bearing \(\text{N}32^\circ\text{E}\) means \(Q\) is measured \(32^\circ\) clockwise from North. The bearing \(\text{S}86^\circ\text{E}\) means \(P\) is measured \(86^\circ\) clockwise from South, i.e. \(180^\circ - 86^\circ = 94^\circ\) clockwise from North.
Hint: Convert every bearing into an angle measured clockwise from North. Then the angle between two rays is just the difference of those clockwise readings.
Readings: \(Q \to 32^\circ\), \(\;P \to 94^\circ\) (from North, clockwise).
Step 21A
\[ \angle POQ = 94^\circ - 32^\circ = 62^\circ \] Equivalently, using the straight line \(N\!-\!O\!-\!S\): \[ \angle POQ = 180^\circ - 32^\circ - 86^\circ = 62^\circ . \]
Hint: The North–South line is straight, so the three angles at \(O\) on that side (\(32^\circ\), \(\angle POQ\), \(86^\circ\)) add up to \(180^\circ\).
\(\boxed{\angle POQ = 62^\circ}\)

STEP 2 Use \(OP = OQ\): the isosceles triangle \(\triangle POQ\)

\(OP = OQ = \) 5.0 units \(\angle PQO = \angle QPO = \dfrac{180^\circ-62^\circ}{2} = 59^\circ\) base angles of isosceles \(\triangle\)
Step 11M
Since \(OP = OQ\), \(\triangle POQ\) is isosceles with \(O\) as the vertex. Hence the base angles are equal: \[ \angle PQO = \angle QPO . \]
Hint: “Equidistant from \(O\)” is the key phrase — it gives you the equal sides, and equal sides give equal base angles (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
\(\angle PQO = \angle QPO\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\))
Step 21A
Angle sum of a triangle: \[ \angle PQO + \angle QPO = 180^\circ - 62^\circ = 118^\circ \] \[ \angle PQO = \angle QPO = 118^\circ \div 2 = 59^\circ . \]
Hint: Move the radius slider — the triangle stays isosceles and \(\angle PQO\) stays \(59^\circ\) forever. That is the power of the “equidistant” condition.
\(\boxed{\angle PQO = 59^\circ}\)
0 / 2

STEP 3 Convert to the bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\)

\(\angle HQP = 59^\circ - 32^\circ = \) 27° Bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\) = S27°E
Step 11M
At \(Q\), draw the North line and let \(H\) be the point due south of \(Q\) on the horizontal East–West line through \(O\). Because the two North lines are parallel, \[ \angle HQO = 32^\circ \quad (\text{alt. } \angle\text{s, } // \text{ lines}). \]
Hint: A bearing must always be measured from the North line at that point. So we must plant a new compass at \(Q\), not reuse the one at \(O\).
\(\angle HQO = 32^\circ\)
Step 21A
We know \(\angle HQP\) is the whole angle from South round to \(P\). Splitting it at the ray \(QO\): \[ \angle HQP = \angle HQO + \angle OQP \] but careful — \(QO\) lies inside the angle between \(QH\) and \(QP\). Using the triangle result: \[ \angle HQP = 59^\circ - 32^\circ = 27^\circ . \]
Hint: Rotate the toggle for \(H\) on and off. Watch that the ray \(QO\) sits between the South direction and \(QP\), so the \(32^\circ\) must be subtracted, not added.
\(\boxed{\angle HQP = 27^\circ}\)
Step 31A
The point \(H\) is due south of \(Q\), and \(P\) lies on the east side of the South line through \(Q\). Therefore the bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\) is \[ \text{S}27^\circ\text{E}. \] So the answer is D.
Hint: Bearing format is: first letter = N or S, then the angle, then E or W. South first because \(H\) is due south; E last because \(P\) is to the east of that line.
Bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\) = \(\boxed{\text{S}27^\circ\text{E}}\)  →  Answer D

SUMMARY Answers at a glance

  • Bearing of \(Q\) from \(O\): \(32^\circ\) clockwise from North; bearing of \(P\) from \(O\): \(94^\circ\) clockwise from North.
  • \(\angle POQ = 94^\circ - 32^\circ = \mathbf{62^\circ}\).
  • \(OP = OQ \Rightarrow \triangle POQ\) is isosceles \(\Rightarrow \angle PQO = \angle QPO\).
  • \(\angle PQO = (180^\circ - 62^\circ) \div 2 = \mathbf{59^\circ}\).
  • With \(H\) due south of \(Q\): \(\angle HQO = 32^\circ\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, // lines).
  • \(\angle HQP = 59^\circ - 32^\circ = \mathbf{27^\circ}\), and \(P\) is to the east of \(QH\).
  • Bearing of \(P\) from \(Q\) = S27°E → Option D.
