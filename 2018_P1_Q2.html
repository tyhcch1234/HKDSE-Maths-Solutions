Laws of Indices

Simplify and express with positive indices

Simplify

$$ \frac{xy^7}{(x^{-2}y^3)^4} $$

and express your answer with positive indices.

✓ Step-by-Step Solution

Click each step to reveal the working. Try to solve it yourself first!

Apply the power of a product rule: $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$

$$ (x^{-2}y^3)^4 = (x^{-2})^4 \cdot (y^3)^4 = x^{-8}y^{12} $$
Hint: $(a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}$. So $(-2)\times 4 = -8$ and $3 \times 4 = 12$.

Substitute the expanded denominator back into the expression:

$$ \frac{xy^7}{x^{-8}y^{12}} $$
Hint: Use the quotient rule for indices: $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$.

Divide the powers of $x$: numerator exponent minus denominator exponent.

$$ \frac{x^1}{x^{-8}} = x^{1 - (-8)} = x^{1+8} = x^9 $$
Hint: Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: $1 - (-8) = 1 + 8 = 9$.

Divide the powers of $y$: numerator exponent minus denominator exponent.

$$ \frac{y^7}{y^{12}} = y^{7 - 12} = y^{-5} $$
Hint: The result has a negative index, so we will need to rewrite it with a positive index.

Use the negative index rule: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$

$$ y^{-5} = \frac{1}{y^5} $$
Final answer:
$$ \boxed{\frac{x^9}{y^5}} $$

🔑 Key Rules to Remember

Power of a Product

$$(ab)^n = a^n b^n$$

Power of a Power

$$(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$

Quotient Rule

$$\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$$

Negative Index

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$$
