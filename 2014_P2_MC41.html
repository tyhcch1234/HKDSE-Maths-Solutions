QUESTION2014 Paper 2 · Q41

Multiple choice — find \(\angle QPS\).

In the figure, \(PQS\) is a circle. \(PQ\) is produced to \(R\) such that \(RS\) is the tangent to the circle at \(S\). \(I\) is the in-centre of \(\triangle QRS\). If \(\angle IRQ = 12^\circ\) and \(\angle PSQ = 70^\circ\), then \(\angle QPS =\)
  1. \(24^\circ\)
  2. \(37^\circ\)
  3. \(43^\circ\)
  4. \(62^\circ\)

STEP 1Part (a) — The Alternate Segment Theorem

Compare the angle between tangent \(RS\) and chord \(SQ\) with the angle in the alternate segment.

70°
Step 11A

Let \(\angle QPS = x\).

In the circle, \(RS\) is the tangent at \(S\) and \(SQ\) is a chord. By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between the tangent \(RS\) and the chord \(SQ\) equals the inscribed angle in the alternate segment, i.e. the angle subtended by \(SQ\) at \(P\):

\[ \angle QSR = \angle QPS = x \]
Hint: The "alternate segment" is the segment on the other side of the chord \(SQ\) from \(R\). So \(\angle QSR\) (next to the tangent) matches \(\angle QPS\) (in the circle).
0 / 1

STEP 2Part (b) — Using the In-centre \(I\)

The in-centre lies on the angle bisector, so \(IR\) bisects \(\angle QRS\).

12°
Step 11M

Since \(I\) is the in-centre of \(\triangle QRS\), the line \(RI\) bisects the interior angle at \(R\):

\[ \angle IRQ = \tfrac{1}{2}\angle QRS \]
Hint: The in-centre is the intersection of the three angle bisectors of a triangle — always equidistant from the three sides.
Step 21A

Given \(\angle IRQ = 12^\circ\):

\[ \angle QRS = 2\,\angle IRQ = 2(12^\circ) = 24^\circ \]
\(\angle QRS = 24^\circ\)
0 / 2

STEP 3Part (c) — Angle Sum in \(\triangle PRS\)

Put everything together and solve for \(x = \angle QPS\).

70°
12°
Step 11M

In \(\triangle PRS\): \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are collinear (since \(PQ\) is produced to \(R\)), so the angle at \(P\) is still \(x\).

The three angles of \(\triangle PRS\) are

\[ \angle RPS = x,\qquad \angle PSR = \angle PSQ + \angle QSR = 70^\circ + x,\qquad \angle PRS = 24^\circ \]
Hint: At \(S\), the ray \(SQ\) lies inside angle \(PSR\), so \(\angle PSR = \angle PSQ + \angle QSR\).
Step 21M

Apply the angle sum of a triangle:

\[ x + (70^\circ + x) + 24^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ 2x + 94^\circ = 180^\circ \]
Hint: Collect like terms first: \(x + x = 2x\), and \(70^\circ + 24^\circ = 94^\circ\).
Step 31A

Solve for \(x\):

\[ 2x = 86^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 43^\circ \]
\(\angle QPS = 43^\circ\) — the answer is C.
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • Alternate segment theorem: \(\angle QSR = \angle QPS = x\)
  • In-centre \(I\) bisects \(\angle QRS\): \(\angle QRS = 2\,\angle IRQ = 2(12^\circ) = 24^\circ\)
  • Angle sum in \(\triangle PRS\): \(x + (70^\circ + x) + 24^\circ = 180^\circ\)
  • Final result: \(\angle QPS = x = \big<\,43^\circ\,\big>\)
  • Correct option: C
