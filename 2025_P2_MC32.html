Question 32 — L.C.M. of Polynomials

Polynomials Factorization L.C.M. Difference of Squares Sum/Difference of Cubes

Q Question

The L.C.M. of $\;p^2 - 4q^2\;$, $\;p^3 - 8q^3\;$ and $\;(p + 2q)|p^2 - 4q^2|\;$ is

Core Concept

To find the L.C.M. of polynomials, first factorize each expression completely, then take the highest power of every distinct factor that appears. This is the same principle as finding the L.C.M. of integers (e.g. $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, $18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$, so $\text{L.C.M.} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 = 36$).

S Step-by-Step Solution

Hint: Recognize the difference of squares

$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$

$$p^2 - 4q^2 = p^2 - (2q)^2 = (p - 2q)(p + 2q)$$

(p − 2q) (p + 2q)

Each factor appears to the power 1.

Hint: Difference of cubes

$a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$

$$p^3 - 8q^3 = p^3 - (2q)^3 = (p - 2q)\bigl(p^2 + 2pq + 4q^2\bigr)$$

(p − 2q) (p² + 2pq + 4q²)

Note: the quadratic factor $p^2 + 2pq + 4q^2$ has a negative discriminant, so it cannot be factorized further over the reals.

Note: $(p+2q)$ and $|p^2 - 4q^2|$

Since the L.C.M. takes absolute values of factors anyway (up to sign), we can work with the expression $p^2 - 4q^2$.

$$(p + 2q)(p^2 - 4q^2) = (p + 2q)\cdot(p - 2q)(p + 2q)$$

$$= (p - 2q)(p + 2q)^2$$

(p − 2q) (p + 2q)2

$$\text{L.C.M.} = (p-2q)^1(p+2q)^2(p^2+2pq+4q^2)^1$$

$$= (p+2q)^2\underbrace{(p-2q)(p^2+2pq+4q^2)}_{= \; p^3 - 8q^3}$$

$$\therefore \text{L.C.M.} = (p+2q)^2(p^3 - 8q^3)$$

Correct Answer

$(p + 2q)^2(p^3 - 8q^3)$

Common Mistakes

  • ✗ Choosing D — using $(p+2q)$ to the power $1$ only, instead of realizing the third expression contributes $(p+2q)^2$.
  • ✗ Choosing B — forgetting that L.C.M. must be divisible by all given expressions, not just the first.
  • ✗ Trying to expand the quadratic $p^2+2pq+4q^2$ further; it is irreducible over the reals.
  • ✗ Confusing L.C.M. with H.C.F. (which would be $(p-2q)$).
