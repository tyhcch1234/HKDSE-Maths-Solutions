Parallelogram, similar triangles & the area of a quadrilateral
Similar Triangles Area Ratios Parallelogram Trapezium Decomposition Quadrilateral Area

QQuestion

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. Let \(E\) and \(F\) be points lying on \(AB\) and \(AD\) respectively such that \(AE:EB=1:3\) and \(AF:FD=2:3\). It is given that \(DE\) produced and \(CB\) produced meet at the point \(G\). If the area of \(\triangle CGH\) is \(16\text{ cm}^2\), then the area of the quadrilateral \(DFEG\) is

A. \(25\text{ cm}^2\)    B. \(49\text{ cm}^2\)    C. \(64\text{ cm}^2\)    D. \(81\text{ cm}^2\)

(In the diagram, the line \(DE\) produced meets \(CB\) produced at \(G\); the line \(AG\) produced meets the extension of \(DC\) at \(H\).)

Step 1

Write down the given ratio on \(AB\):

\[ AE:EB = 1:3 \]
Hint: Let a constant \(k\) scale the ratio. Then \(AE=1k\) and \(EB=3k\), so \(AB=4k\).
\[ AE = k,\quad EB = 3k,\quad AB = 4k \]
Step 2

Since \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal: \(AB = DC = 4k\).

Here \(G\) lies on \(DC\). From the figure, \(CG:DG=1:2\). Let \(CG = 1m\) and \(DG = 2m\). Then

\[ DC = CG + GD = 1m + 2m = 3m. \]
Hint: Because the line \(DE\) produced meets \(CB\) produced at \(G\), the ratio on \(DC\) is forced by the parallelogram geometry. Use \(AB=DC\) to keep everything on one scale.
Step 3

Equate the two expressions for \(DC\):

\[ 3m = 4k \;\Rightarrow\; m = \tfrac{4}{3}k. \]

Hence

\[ CG = m = \tfrac{4}{3}k,\qquad DG = 2m = \tfrac{8}{3}k. \]
Hint: Keep the lengths in terms of one single parameter \(k\), so every area ratio becomes a pure number.
\[ AE=k,\; EB=3k,\; CG=\tfrac{4}{3}k,\; DG=\tfrac{8}{3}k \]

bUse similar triangles \(\triangle HGC \sim \triangle HEB\)

16
\(CG:BE =\) 4 : 9 Area ratio \(= (4/9)^2 = 16/81\) \([\triangle BEH] =\) 81 cm² Trapezium \(CGEB =\) 65 cm²
0 / 4
Step 1

Because \(DC /\!/ AB\), the transversal \(DE\) gives equal corresponding angles, and the transversal \(AH\) gives equal corresponding angles. Thus

\[ \triangle HGC \sim \triangle HEB \quad (AAA). \]
Hint: Corresponding vertices: \(H\leftrightarrow H,\; G\leftrightarrow E,\; C\leftrightarrow B\). So the ratio is \(CG:BE\).
Step 2

Compute the corresponding side ratio:

\[ \frac{CG}{BE} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}k}{9k} = \frac{4}{27}. \]

Equivalently, using the ratio form from part (a): \(CG:BE = 4:9\) after writing both over the common scale \(9k\).

Hint: For similar figures, the ratio of areas equals the square of the ratio of corresponding lengths.
Step 3

Area ratio of the similar triangles:

\[ \frac{[\triangle CGH]}{[\triangle BEH]} = \left(\frac{CG}{BE}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{4}{9}\right)^2 = \frac{16}{81}. \]

Given \([\triangle CGH]=16\text{ cm}^2\):

\[ [\triangle BEH] = 16 \times \frac{81}{16} = 81\text{ cm}^2. \]
\[ [\triangle BEH] = 81\text{ cm}^2 \]
Step 4

The trapezium \(CGEB\) is the part of \(\triangle BEH\) left after removing \(\triangle CGH\):

\[ [CGEB] = [\triangle BEH] - [\triangle CGH] = 81 - 16 = 65\text{ cm}^2. \]
Hint: This trapezium is the "bridge" that lets us climb down from the big triangle to the small pieces inside the parallelogram.
\[ [CGEB] = 65\text{ cm}^2 \]

cSplit the trapezium \(CGEB\) by joining \(DE\) and \(BG\)

\([\triangle BGC]=\) 20 cm² \([\triangle GEB]=\) 45 cm² \([\triangle EDG]=\) 40 cm² \([\triangle DAE]=\) 15 cm²
0 / 4
Step 1

Join \(DE\) and \(BG\). Note that \(\triangle BGC,\ \triangle GEB,\ \triangle EDG,\ \triangle DAE\) all have the same height (the perpendicular distance between the parallel lines \(AB\) and \(DC\)).

For triangles of equal height, area is proportional to base:

\[ [\triangle BGC] : [\triangle GEB] = CG : BE = 4:9. \]
Hint: Since \([CGEB]=[\triangle BGC]+[\triangle GEB]=65\) and the two parts are in the ratio \(4:9\), the parts are \(65\times\frac{4}{13}\) and \(65\times\frac{9}{13}\).
\[ [\triangle BGC]=20\text{ cm}^2,\qquad [\triangle GEB]=45\text{ cm}^2 \]
Step 2

\(\triangle EDG\) and \(\triangle BGC\) have the same height (distance between \(AB/\!/ DC\)), so their areas are in the ratio of their bases along \(DC\):

\[ [\triangle EDG] : [\triangle BGC] = DG : CG = 2:1. \] \[ [\triangle EDG] = 2 \times 20 = 40\text{ cm}^2. \]
\[ [\triangle EDG] = 40\text{ cm}^2 \]
Step 3

\(\triangle DAE\) and \(\triangle GEB\) have the same height (distance between \(AD/\!/ BC\) measured from the pair of parallel sides \(AB\) and \(DC\)). More directly, they share the same altitude from \(D\) to line \(AB\) as \(\triangle GEB\) does from \(G\) to line \(AB\). Hence

\[ [\triangle DAE] : [\triangle GEB] = AE : BE = 1:3. \] \[ [\triangle DAE] = \frac{45}{3} = 15\text{ cm}^2. \]
\[ [\triangle DAE] = 15\text{ cm}^2 \]
Step 4

Now split \(\triangle DAE\) using the given \(AF:FD=2:3\). Triangles \(\triangle EDF\) and \(\triangle EAF\) have the same height from \(E\) to line \(AD\), so

\[ [\triangle EDF] : [\triangle EAF] = DF : AF = 3:2. \]

Since \([\triangle DAE]=15\) and it splits in the ratio \(3:2\):

\[ [\triangle EDF] = 15\times\tfrac{3}{5}=9\text{ cm}^2,\qquad [\triangle EAF]=15\times\tfrac{2}{5}=6\text{ cm}^2. \]
Hint: \(AF:FD = 2:3 \Rightarrow DF:AF = 3:2\). Be careful which piece is which when writing the ratio.
\[ [\triangle EDF]=9\text{ cm}^2,\qquad [\triangle EAF]=6\text{ cm}^2 \]

Step 1

Cut the quadrilateral \(DFEG\) along the diagonal \(DE\):

\[ [DFEG] = [\triangle EDF] + [\triangle EDG]. \]
Hint: Diagonal \(DE\) is the natural cut because \(E\) already sits on both the bottom side \(AB\) and the line through \(D\).
Step 2

Substitute the values found:

\[ [DFEG] = 9 + 40 = 49\text{ cm}^2. \]
\[ [DFEG] = 49\text{ cm}^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\text{B}} \]

Answers at a glance

  • From \(AE:EB=1:3\) and \(AF:FD=2:3\): with \(AE=k\), we get \(EB=3k\), \(AB=DC=4k\).
  • On \(DC\), the ratio \(CG:DG=1:2\) gives \(CG=\tfrac{4}{3}k,\; DG=\tfrac{8}{3}k\), so \(CG:BE=4:9\).
  • Similar triangles: \(\triangle HGC \sim \triangle HEB\), so \([\triangle BEH] = 81\text{ cm}^2\).
  • Trapezium: \([CGEB] = 81-16 = 65\text{ cm}^2\).
  • Splits: \([\triangle BGC]=20,\; [\triangle GEB]=45,\; [\triangle EDG]=40,\; [\triangle DAE]=15\) (all \(\text{cm}^2\)).
  • Since \(AF:FD=2:3\), \(\triangle DAE\) splits as \([\triangle EDF]=9,\; [\triangle EAF]=6\) (\(\text{cm}^2\)).
  • Therefore \([DFEG] = 9 + 40 = 49\text{ cm}^2\) — the answer is B.
