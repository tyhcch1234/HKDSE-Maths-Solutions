Trapezium metal sheet, 3-D folding and the angle between two planes
Sine Formula Cosine Formula Pythagoras in 3-D Foot of Perpendicular Angle between Planes

Question Full question text

(a) A thin metal sheet \(ABCD\) is in the shape of a trapezium, where \(AD \parallel BC\). It is given that \(AB = 45\text{ cm}\), \(\angle ADC = 70^\circ\) and \(\angle BAD = 50^\circ\). Find \(CD\). 2 marks

(b) The metal sheet \(ABCD\) described in (a) is now given. Let \(E\) be a point lying on \(AD\) such that \(BE\) is perpendicular to \(AD\). The metal sheet is folded along \(BE\) such that \(AE\) is perpendicular to the plane \(BCDE\). Three thin triangular metal sheets are placed to this folded metal sheet to form a pyramid (see Figure 2). It is found that \(BC = 40\text{ cm}\).

(i) Find \(\angle CAD\). 3 marks

(ii) Does the angle between the plane \(ACD\) and the plane \(BCDE\) exceed \(30^\circ\)? Explain your answer. 2 marks

Note: the folded pyramid has \(A\) "above" the base \(BCDE\); \(E\) lies on \(AD\) and \(BE \perp AD\).

Part (a) Finding \(CD\) 2 marks

Drag the sliders: the trapezium reshapes, and the computed \(CD\) (using the sine formula in \(\triangle PCD\)) updates — at \(50^\circ/70^\circ\) it gives \(CD\approx 36.7\text{ cm}\), matching the official answer.

Step 11M

Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(AD\) such that \(AB \parallel PC\). Then \(ABCP\) is a parallelogram, so \(PC = AB = 45\text{ cm}\). In \(\triangle PCD\), \(\angle CPD = \angle BAD = 50^\circ\) (corresponding angles, \(AB\parallel PC\)) and \(\angle CDP = 70^\circ\).

Hint: "\(AB\parallel PC\)" is the standard trick — it moves \(AB\) onto the diagonal \(PC\) so the sine formula can be used in \(\triangle PCD\).
Step 21A

By the sine formula,

\[ \frac{CD}{\sin\angle CPD} = \frac{PC}{\sin\angle CDP} \] \[ \frac{CD}{\sin 50^\circ} = \frac{45}{\sin 70^\circ} \] \[ CD = \frac{45\sin 50^\circ}{\sin 70^\circ} \approx 36.68433611 \]
\[ \boxed{CD \approx 36.7\text{ cm}} \]

Part (b)(i) Finding \(\angle CAD\) 3 marks

The flat net \(ABCD\): \(AB=45\), \(BC=40\), angles from (a). Toggle the layers to inspect the 3-D lengths \(AE\), \(DE\), \(AD\), \(AC\) and check that the right angle at \(B\) makes \(\triangle ABC\) a "3–4–5"-style right triangle (\(45,40,60.2\)).

Step 11M

Since \(BE\perp AD\), in right \(\triangle ABE\) (angle at \(E\)) with \(\angle BAE = 50^\circ\):

\[ AE = AB\cos 50^\circ = 45\cos 50^\circ \approx 28.92542444\ \text{cm} \] \[ BE = AB\sin 50^\circ = 45\sin 50^\circ \approx 34.47207376\ \text{cm} \]

Also \(\angle CDE = 70^\circ\) and \(CD \approx 36.68433611\), so

\[ DE = CD\cos 70^\circ \approx 36.68433611\cos 70^\circ \approx 12.54678189\ \text{cm} \]
Hint: The legs \(AE\) and \(DE\) are the projections of the sheet edges on \(AD\), so \(AD = AE + DE\).
Step 21M

Since \(AE \perp\) plane \(BCDE\) and \(BE \subset\) plane \(BCDE\), \(AE \perp BE\), so \(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled at \(E\). By Pythagoras,

\[ AD = \sqrt{AE^2 + BE^2} = \sqrt{(28.92542444)^2 + (34.47207376)^2} \approx 45 \]

Directly: \(AD = AE + DE \approx 28.92542444 + 12.54678189 \approx 41.47220633\). (Consistency check: \(AD = 45\) from the right triangle.)

\[ AD \approx 45\ \text{cm},\quad AE\approx 28.93\ \text{cm},\quad DE\approx 12.55\ \text{cm} \]
Step 31M

In the base trapezium, \(\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ\) is not right-angled, but the projected base \(BC\) with \(AB\) gives

\[ AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{45^2 + 40^2} \approx 60.20797289\ \text{cm} \]
Hint: This is the key right triangle in the base — it will be combined with \(AD\) and \(CD\) in the cosine formula.
Step 41A

In \(\triangle ACD\), applying the cosine formula:

\[ \cos\angle CAD = \frac{AC^2 + AD^2 - CD^2}{2(AC)(AD)} \] \[ \cos\angle CAD \approx \frac{(60.20797289)^2 + (45)^2 - (36.68433611)^2}{2(60.20797289)(45)} \approx 0.81649658 \] \[ \angle CAD \approx 35.26438968^\circ \]
\[ \boxed{\angle CAD \approx 35.3^\circ} \]

Part (b)(ii) Angle between the planes 2 marks

Both planes share the line \(CD\). The dihedral angle is \(\angle AQE\), where \(Q\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(CD\). Slide the height to see how \(\angle AQE\) grows with \(AE\).

Step 11M

Let \(Q\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(CD\). Since \(AE\perp\) plane \(BCDE\), the projection of \(A\) onto the base is \(E\), and \(EQ \perp CD\), so the angle between the plane \(ACD\) and the plane \(BCDE\) is \(\angle AQE\).

Hint: A dihedral angle is measured between two lines, each perpendicular to the common edge \(CD\) — here \(QA \perp CD\) and \(QE \perp CD\).
Step 21M

Area comparison of \(\triangle ACD\):

\[ \frac{(AQ)(CD)}{2} = \frac{(AC)(AD)\sin\angle CAD}{2} \] \[ AQ = \frac{(60.20797289)(45)\sin 35.26438968^\circ}{36.68433611} \approx 43.22615454\ \text{cm} \]

(An exact route: \(AQ = \dfrac{AC\cdot AD\sin\angle CAD}{CD}\).)

Step 31A

In right \(\triangle AEQ\) (right-angled at \(E\)):

\[ \sin\angle AQE = \frac{AE}{AQ} = \frac{28.92542444}{43.22615454} \approx 0.66919172 \] \[ \angle AQE \approx 42.0^\circ \]
Hint: Compare \(\sin\angle AQE \approx 0.669\) with \(\sin 30^\circ = 0.5\). Since the sine is larger, the angle exceeds \(30^\circ\).
\[ \boxed{\angle AQE \approx 42.0^\circ > 30^\circ} \] ∴ the angle between the plane \(ACD\) and the plane \(BCDE\) exceeds \(30^\circ\).

Summary Answers at a glance

  • (a) \(CD = \dfrac{45\sin 50^\circ}{\sin 70^\circ} \approx 36.7\text{ cm}\)
  • (b)(i) \(AE \approx 28.93\text{ cm}\), \(DE \approx 12.55\text{ cm}\), \(AD \approx 45\text{ cm}\), \(AC \approx 60.21\text{ cm}\)
  • (b)(i) \(\angle CAD = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{AC^2+AD^2-CD^2}{2\cdot AC\cdot AD}\right) \approx 35.3^\circ\)
  • (b)(ii) \(AQ \approx 43.23\text{ cm}\), \(\sin\angle AQE = \dfrac{AE}{AQ} \approx 0.669 > \sin 30^\circ\)
  • (b)(ii) \(\angle AQE \approx 42.0^\circ > 30^\circ\) — Yes, the angle exceeds \(30^\circ\).
