Type 1

Polynomial Notation and Values · 7 questions

Degree, coefficients, constants and evaluating f(a)

Core idea: A polynomial in \(x\) is a sum of terms of the form \(ax^{k}\) where \(k\) is a whole number. Write it in descending powers first: then the degree is the highest power, the leading coefficient is the number attached to it, and the constant term has no \(x\). To evaluate \(f(a)\) substitute \(x=a\) into every term — with brackets around negative values.

\(f(a)=a_{n}a^{n}+\cdots+a_{1}a+a_{0}\)\(\deg(P\cdot Q)=\deg P+\deg Q\)\((-a)^{2}=a^{2},\ (-a)^{3}=-a^{3}\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Let \(f(x)=3x^{2}-4x+7\). Find \(f(2)\) and \(f(0)\).

Answer\(f(2)=11\) and \(f(0)=7\)
  1. Substitute \(x=2\) into every term: \(f(2)=3(2)^{2}-4(2)+7\).
  2. Work out the powers first: \(=3(4)-8+7=12-8+7\).
  3. \(=11\).
  4. For \(f(0)\), every term containing \(x\) becomes \(0\), so only the constant survives: \(f(0)=3(0)^{2}-4(0)+7=7\).
Common mistake

\(3(2)^{2}=3\times 4=12\), not \((3\times 2)^{2}=36\). Square the \(x\) before multiplying by the coefficient.

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

For the polynomial \(g(x)=5-2x^{3}+x^{5}-4x\), write down (a) its degree, (b) its leading coefficient, (c) its constant term.

Answer(a) 5　(b) 1　(c) 5
  1. Rewrite in descending powers: \(g(x)=x^{5}-2x^{3}-4x+5\).
  2. (a) The highest power of \(x\) present is \(5\), so the degree is \(5\).
  3. (b) The term \(x^{5}\) means \(1\cdot x^{5}\), so the leading coefficient is \(1\).
  4. (c) The term without \(x\) is \(5\), so the constant term is \(5\).
Common mistake

The degree counts the highest power, not the number of terms — this polynomial has four terms but degree five. Also \(x^{5}\) has coefficient \(1\), not \(0\).

Q3★★☆☆3 marks

Let \(f(x)=x^{3}-2x^{2}+kx-5\), where \(k\) is a constant. If \(f(2)=7\), find \(k\).

Answer\(k=6\)
  1. Substitute \(x=2\): \(f(2)=(2)^{3}-2(2)^{2}+k(2)-5\).
  2. \(=8-2(4)+2k-5=8-8+2k-5=2k-5\).
  3. Given \(f(2)=7\): \(2k-5=7\), so \(2k=12\) and \(k=6\).
  4. Check: with \(k=6\), \(f(2)=8-8+12-5=7\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(-2(2)^{2}=-8\), not \(+8\): the minus sign belongs to the whole term, and the squaring must be done first.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

Expand \((2x^{2}-3)(x^{2}+4x-1)\). Hence state the coefficient of \(x^{2}\) and the constant term of the product.

Answer\(2x^{4}+8x^{3}-5x^{2}-12x+3\); coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is \(-5\), constant term is \(3\)
  1. Multiply each term of the first bracket by the whole second bracket.
  2. \(2x^{2}(x^{2}+4x-1)=2x^{4}+8x^{3}-2x^{2}\).
  3. \(-3(x^{2}+4x-1)=-3x^{2}-12x+3\).
  4. Add like terms: \(2x^{4}+8x^{3}+(-2-3)x^{2}-12x+3=2x^{4}+8x^{3}-5x^{2}-12x+3\).
  5. So the coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is \(-5\) and the constant term is \(3\).
Common mistake

The \(x^{2}\) term is produced twice, by \(2x^{2}\times(-1)\) and by \((-3)\times x^{2}\). Reading off only \(-2\) gives the wrong coefficient.

Q5★★☆☆3 marks

Let \(P(x)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}+4\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. If \(P(1)=10\) and \(P(-1)=6\), find \(a\) and \(b\).

Answer\(a=2\) and \(b=4\)
  1. \(P(1)=a(1)^{3}+b(1)^{2}+4=a+b+4\). Given \(P(1)=10\): \(a+b=6\) … (1).
  2. \(P(-1)=a(-1)^{3}+b(-1)^{2}+4=-a+b+4\). Given \(P(-1)=6\): \(-a+b=2\) … (2).
  3. (1) + (2): \(2b=8\), so \(b=4\). Then (1) gives \(a=2\).
  4. Check: \(P(1)=2+4+4=10\) ✓ and \(P(-1)=-2+4+4=6\) ✓.
Common mistake

At \(x=-1\) the cubic term flips sign (\(-a\)) but the squared term does not (\(+b\)). Signing both as negative produces no solution.

Q6★★★☆4 marks

Let \(f(x)=2x^{3}-3x^{2}+4x-1\). Find \(f(x+1)\), giving your answer in descending powers of \(x\).

Answer\(2x^{3}+3x^{2}+4x+2\)
  1. First write the two expansions needed: \((x+1)^{3}=x^{3}+3x^{2}+3x+1\) and \((x+1)^{2}=x^{2}+2x+1\).
  2. \(2(x+1)^{3}=2x^{3}+6x^{2}+6x+2\) and \(-3(x+1)^{2}=-3x^{2}-6x-3\).
  3. So \(f(x+1)=2x^{3}+6x^{2}+6x+2-3x^{2}-6x-3+4x+4-1\).
  4. Collect like terms: \(2x^{3}+(6-3)x^{2}+(6-6+4)x+(2-3+4-1)=2x^{3}+3x^{2}+4x+2\).
  5. Check with \(x=0\): the answer gives \(2\), and directly \(f(1)=2-3+4-1=2\) ✓.
Common mistake

The \(-3\) outside \((x+1)^{2}\) multiplies all three terms, so the constant \(-3\) must not be dropped; forgetting it gives \(+5\) at the end instead of \(+2\).

Q7★★★☆5 marks

Let \(f(x)=x^{3}+px^{2}+qx+6\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are constants. It is given that \(f(1)=16\) and \(f(-1)=2\).
(a) Find \(p\) and \(q\).
(b) Hence find \(f(2)\).

Answer(a) \(p=3,\ q=6\)　(b) \(f(2)=38\)
  1. (a) \(f(1)=1+p+q+6=16\), so \(p+q=9\) … (1).
  2. \(f(-1)=-1+p-q+6=2\), so \(p-q=-3\) … (2).
  3. (1) + (2): \(2p=6\), giving \(p=3\); then (1) gives \(q=6\). Hence \(f(x)=x^{3}+3x^{2}+6x+6\).
  4. (b) \(f(2)=2^{3}+3(2)^{2}+6(2)+6=8+12+12+6=38\).
  5. Check (a) with \(f(-1)\): \(-1+3-6+6=2\) ✓.
Common mistake

A common slip is to write \(f(-1)=-1-p+q+6\): the \(px^{2}\) term is \(+p\) at \(x=-1\) because \((-1)^{2}=1\).

Type 2

Addition, Subtraction and Multiplication of Polynomials · 7 questions

Collecting like terms and expanding products

Core idea: Adding or subtracting polynomials only combines like terms — same power of \(x\). For subtraction, put a bracket round the polynomial being taken away and change every sign inside it. To multiply, multiply every term of one bracket by every term of the other, then collect; the number of terms in the product before collecting equals the product of the numbers of terms.

\(-(a+b-c)=-a-b+c\)\((a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd\)\(\deg(AB)=\deg A+\deg B\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

Simplify \((3x^{2}-5x+2)+(x^{2}+7x-9)\).

Answer\(4x^{2}+2x-7\)
  1. Group like terms: \((3x^{2}+x^{2})+(-5x+7x)+(2-9)\).
  2. \(=4x^{2}+2x-7\).
  3. Check by putting \(x=1\): the original gives \((3-5+2)+(1+7-9)=0+(-1)=-1\), and \(4+2-7=-1\) ✓.
Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

Simplify \((5x^{3}-2x^{2}+4)-(2x^{3}+x^{2}-6x+1)\).

Answer\(3x^{3}-3x^{2}+6x+3\)
  1. Write the subtraction with a bracket and change the signs of the second polynomial: \(5x^{3}-2x^{2}+4-2x^{3}-x^{2}+6x-1\).
  2. Group like terms: \((5-2)x^{3}+(-2-1)x^{2}+6x+(4-1)\).
  3. \(=3x^{3}-3x^{2}+6x+3\).
  4. Check at \(x=1\): original \(=(5-2+4)-(2+1-6+1)=7-(-2)=9\), and \(3-3+6+3=9\) ✓.
Common mistake

Subtracting a bracket changes the sign of every term inside it, so \(-(-6x)=+6x\). Sign errors here are the most common lost mark in this type.

Q10★★☆☆3 marks

Simplify \(2x(3x-4)-3x^{2}(x-2)\).

Answer\(-3x^{3}+12x^{2}-8x\)
  1. Expand each product separately: \(2x(3x-4)=6x^{2}-8x\).
  2. \(3x^{2}(x-2)=3x^{3}-6x^{2}\), so \(-3x^{2}(x-2)=-3x^{3}+6x^{2}\).
  3. Add: \(6x^{2}-8x-3x^{3}+6x^{2}=-3x^{3}+12x^{2}-8x\).
  4. Check at \(x=2\): original \(=4(6-4)-12(0)=8\), answer \(=-24+48-16=8\) ✓.
Common mistake

The minus sign in front of \(3x^{2}(x-2)\) must multiply both terms: \(-3x^{2}\times(-2)=+6x^{2}\), not \(-6x^{2}\).

Q11★★☆☆3 marks

Expand and simplify \((x-3)(2x+5)\).

Answer\(2x^{2}-x-15\)
  1. Multiply each term of the first bracket by each term of the second: \(x(2x)+x(5)+(-3)(2x)+(-3)(5)\).
  2. \(=2x^{2}+5x-6x-15\).
  3. Collect the \(x\) terms: \(5x-6x=-x\), so the answer is \(2x^{2}-x-15\).
  4. Check at \(x=1\): \((-2)(7)=-14\) and \(2-1-15=-14\) ✓.
Common mistake

Do not forget the two middle products; writing \((x-3)(2x+5)=2x^{2}-15\) omits the \(x\) term completely.

Q12★★☆☆4 marks

Expand \((2x-1)(x^{2}+3x-4)\).

Answer\(2x^{3}+5x^{2}-11x+4\)
  1. \(2x(x^{2}+3x-4)=2x^{3}+6x^{2}-8x\).
  2. \(-1(x^{2}+3x-4)=-x^{2}-3x+4\).
  3. Add like terms: \(2x^{3}+(6-1)x^{2}+(-8-3)x+4=2x^{3}+5x^{2}-11x+4\).
  4. Check at \(x=-1\): \((-3)(1-3-4)=(-3)(-6)=18\), and \(-2+5+11+4=18\) ✓.
Common mistake

A cubic times a linear must give four collected terms but six products. Missing the \(-1\times(-4)=+4\) loses the constant.

Q13★★★☆4 marks

Expand and simplify \((x+2)^{2}(2x-1)\).

Answer\(2x^{3}+7x^{2}+4x-4\)
  1. Square first: \((x+2)^{2}=x^{2}+4x+4\).
  2. Multiply by \((2x-1)\): \(2x(x^{2}+4x+4)=2x^{3}+8x^{2}+8x\).
  3. \(-1(x^{2}+4x+4)=-x^{2}-4x-4\).
  4. Add: \(2x^{3}+(8-1)x^{2}+(8-4)x-4=2x^{3}+7x^{2}+4x-4\).
  5. Check at \(x=1\): \((3)^{2}(1)=9\) and \(2+7+4-4=9\) ✓.
Common mistake

\((x+2)^{2}\) is \(x^{2}+4x+4\), not \(x^{2}+4\). Squaring a bracket never leaves two terms.

Q14★★★☆5 marks

A rectangle has length \((3x+2)\) cm and width \((2x-5)\) cm, where \(x>2.5\).
(a) Express the area of the rectangle in terms of \(x\).
(b) Find the area when \(x=4\).
(c) Express the perimeter in terms of \(x\).

Answer(a) \((6x^{2}-11x-10)\) cm\(^{2}\)　(b) 42 cm\(^{2}\)　(c) \((10x-6)\) cm
  1. (a) Area \(=(3x+2)(2x-5)=6x^{2}-15x+4x-10=6x^{2}-11x-10\) cm\(^{2}\).
  2. (b) Put \(x=4\): \(6(16)-11(4)-10=96-44-10=42\) cm\(^{2}\).
  3. Direct check for (b): length \(=14\) cm, width \(=3\) cm, so area \(=14\times 3=42\) cm\(^{2}\) ✓.
  4. (c) Perimeter \(=2[(3x+2)+(2x-5)]=2(5x-3)=10x-6\) cm.
Common mistake

The area of the two middle products must be added with their signs: \(-15x+4x=-11x\). A perimeter of \(5x-3\) forgets the factor \(2\).

Type 3

Identities and Comparing Coefficients · 7 questions

Equating coefficients of \(x^{n}\) on both sides

Core idea: An identity \(\equiv\) is true for every value of \(x\), so the coefficients of each power of \(x\) must match on the two sides. Practical routine: expand the product side fully, line the terms up in descending powers, then compare the \(x^{3}\), \(x^{2}\), \(x\) coefficients and the constants one at a time. Start with the leading coefficient and the constant — they give one unknown each immediately. Alternatively substitute a clever value (such as \(x=8\) when \((x-8)\) appears) to kill a whole product.

\(Ax^{2}+Bx+C\equiv ax^{2}+bx+c\Rightarrow A=a,\ B=b,\ C=c\)\((x+p)(x+q)=x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq\)\((x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)=ax^{3}+(b-2a)x^{2}+(c-2b)x-2c\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

If \((x+3)(x-5)\equiv x^{2}+ax+b\), find \(a\) and \(b\).

Answer\(a=-2\) and \(b=-15\)
  1. Expand the left side: \((x+3)(x-5)=x^{2}-5x+3x-15=x^{2}-2x-15\).
  2. Compare coefficients with \(x^{2}+ax+b\): the \(x\) coefficient is \(a=-2\) and the constant is \(b=-15\).
  3. Check with \(x=0\): left \(=3\times(-5)=-15\), right \(=b=-15\) ✓.
Common mistake

\(b\) is the constant term of the expanded product, \(3\times(-5)=-15\), not \(3\) or \(-5\).

Q16★☆☆☆3 marks

If \(A(x+1)+B(x-2)\equiv 5x-4\) for all values of \(x\), find the constants \(A\) and \(B\).

Answer\(A=2\) and \(B=3\)
  1. Expand the left side: \(Ax+A+Bx-2B=(A+B)x+(A-2B)\).
  2. Compare coefficients: \(A+B=5\) … (1) and \(A-2B=-4\) … (2).
  3. (1) − (2): \(3B=9\), so \(B=3\); then \(A=2\).
  4. Check: \(2(x+1)+3(x-2)=2x+2+3x-6=5x-4\) ✓.
Common mistake

The constant part is \(A-2B\), not \(A-B\): the \(-2\) multiplies the whole of \(B\).

Q17★★☆☆3 marks

If \((2x-1)(x+4)\equiv 2x^{2}+px+q\), find \(p\) and \(q\).

Answer\(p=7\) and \(q=-4\)
  1. Expand: \((2x-1)(x+4)=2x^{2}+8x-x-4\).
  2. \(=2x^{2}+7x-4\).
  3. Compare with \(2x^{2}+px+q\): \(p=7\) and \(q=-4\).
Common mistake

Comparing only the visible numbers gives \(p=-1\) or \(p=8\); you must collect the two \(x\) products first.

Q18★★☆☆4 marks

If \(x^{2}+ax+7\equiv (x+2)(x+b)+3\) for all \(x\), find \(a\) and \(b\).

Answer\(a=4\) and \(b=2\)
  1. Expand the right side: \((x+2)(x+b)+3=x^{2}+bx+2x+2b+3=x^{2}+(b+2)x+(2b+3)\).
  2. Compare the constant terms: \(7=2b+3\), so \(2b=4\) and \(b=2\).
  3. Compare the \(x\) coefficients: \(a=b+2=4\).
  4. Check: \((x+2)(x+2)+3=x^{2}+4x+4+3=x^{2}+4x+7\) ✓.
Common mistake

The \(+3\) outside the bracket must be added to the constant after expanding — it does not join the \(x\) coefficient.

Q19★★☆☆5 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 1 Q12 ↗

It is given that \(x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx+6\equiv (x+1)(x-2)(x+c)\), where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are constants.
(a) Find \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\).
(b) Hence solve the equation \(x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx+6=0\).

Answer(a) \(a=-4,\ b=1,\ c=-3\)　(b) \(x=-1,\ 2\) or \(3\)
  1. (a) Expand the right side in two steps. First \((x+1)(x-2)=x^{2}-x-2\).
  2. Then \((x^{2}-x-2)(x+c)=x^{3}+cx^{2}-x^{2}-cx-2x-2c=x^{3}+(c-1)x^{2}+(-c-2)x-2c\).
  3. Compare constants: \(-2c=6\), so \(c=-3\).
  4. Compare \(x^{2}\): \(a=c-1=-4\). Compare \(x\): \(b=-c-2=3-2=1\).
  5. So the polynomial is \(x^{3}-4x^{2}+x+6=(x+1)(x-2)(x-3)\).
  6. (b) A product is zero when a factor is zero: \(x=-1\), \(x=2\) or \(x=3\).
  7. Check by expansion: \((x+1)(x-2)(x-3)=(x^{2}-x-2)(x-3)=x^{3}-4x^{2}+x+6\) ✓.
Common mistake

The constant term of the product is \((1)(-2)(c)=-2c\), so \(c=-3\), not \(+3\). Getting the sign of \(c\) wrong flips both \(a\) and \(b\).

Q20★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q4 ↗

If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are constants such that \((x-5)(x+\alpha)-4\equiv (x-7)^{2}+\beta\) for all values of \(x\), find \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

Answer\(\alpha=-9\) and \(\beta=-8\)
  1. Method 1 — substitute a clever value. Put \(x=5\) so that the factor \((x-5)\) becomes zero.
  2. Left side: \((5-5)(5+\alpha)-4=0-4=-4\). Right side: \((5-7)^{2}+\beta=4+\beta\).
  3. So \(-4=4+\beta\), giving \(\beta=-8\).
  4. Method 2 — compare coefficients. Left: \(x^{2}+(\alpha-5)x+(-5\alpha-4)\). Right: \(x^{2}-14x+(49+\beta)\).
  5. Compare the \(x\) coefficients: \(\alpha-5=-14\), so \(\alpha=-9\).
  6. Check the constant: \(-5(-9)-4=41\) and \(49+(-8)=41\) ✓.
Common mistake

Substituting \(x=7\) instead of \(x=5\) kills nothing useful on the left. Choose the value that makes a bracket zero on the side you can evaluate.

Q21★★★☆5 marks

It is given that \(3x^{2}+x+1\equiv A(x-1)^{2}+B(x-1)+C\) for all values of \(x\), where \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are constants.
(a) Find \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\).
(b) Hence write down the value of the expression when \(x=1\).

Answer(a) \(A=3,\ B=7,\ C=5\)　(b) 5
  1. Expand the right side: \(A(x^{2}-2x+1)+Bx-B+C=Ax^{2}+(-2A+B)x+(A-B+C)\).
  2. Compare \(x^{2}\): \(A=3\).
  3. Compare \(x\): \(-2A+B=1\), so \(-6+B=1\) and \(B=7\).
  4. Compare constants: \(A-B+C=1\), so \(3-7+C=1\) and \(C=5\).
  5. Check by expanding: \(3(x-1)^{2}+7(x-1)+5=3x^{2}-6x+3+7x-7+5=3x^{2}+x+1\) ✓.
  6. (b) Put \(x=1\): all the \((x-1)\) terms vanish, so the value is \(C=5\); directly \(3+1+1=5\) ✓.
Common mistake

Comparing constants gives \(A-B+C\), not \(C\). The \(+A\) from \(A(x-1)^{2}\) and \(-B\) from \(B(x-1)\) must both be included.

Type 4

Factorisation: Common Factor, Grouping, Cross Method · 7 questions

Take out the HCF first, then group or cross-multiply

Core idea: Always take out the highest common factor (HCF) before anything else — it makes every later step smaller. For four terms, group them in pairs so that the same bracket appears twice, then take the bracket out. For a quadratic \(ax^{2}+bx+c\), look for two numbers whose product is \(ac\) and whose sum is \(b\); split the middle term and factorise by grouping (the cross method).

\(ab+ac=a(b+c)\)\(ax^{2}+bx+c\): product \(ac\), sum \(b\)\(ac+ad+bc+bd=(a+b)(c+d)\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

Factorise \(6x^{2}-9x\).

Answer\(3x(2x-3)\)
  1. The HCF of \(6x^{2}\) and \(9x\) is \(3x\).
  2. \(6x^{2}-9x=3x\cdot 2x-3x\cdot 3\).
  3. \(=3x(2x-3)\).
  4. Check by expanding: \(3x(2x-3)=6x^{2}-9x\) ✓.
Common mistake

Taking out only \(3\) leaves \(3(2x^{2}-3x)\), which is not fully factorised — the \(x\) is also common.

Q23★☆☆☆2 marks

Factorise \(x^{2}+7x+12\).

Answer\((x+3)(x+4)\)
  1. We need two numbers with product \(12\) and sum \(7\): they are \(3\) and \(4\).
  2. \(x^{2}+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4)\).
  3. Check by expanding: \(x^{2}+4x+3x+12=x^{2}+7x+12\) ✓.
Q24★★☆☆3 marks

Factorise \(2x^{2}-5x-3\).

Answer\((2x+1)(x-3)\)
  1. Here \(ac=2\times(-3)=-6\) and \(b=-5\). The two numbers are \(1\) and \(-6\) (product \(-6\), sum \(-5\)).
  2. Split the middle term: \(2x^{2}+x-6x-3\).
  3. Group: \(x(2x+1)-3(2x+1)\).
  4. \(=(2x+1)(x-3)\). Check: \(2x^{2}-6x+x-3=2x^{2}-5x-3\) ✓.
Common mistake

The two numbers must have product \(ac=-6\), not \(c=-3\). Using \(1\) and \(-3\) gives \(-2\) as the sum and the split fails.

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

Factorise \(2x^{3}+6x^{2}-x-3\).

Answer\((x+3)(2x^{2}-1)\)
  1. Group in pairs: \((2x^{3}+6x^{2})+(-x-3)\).
  2. \(=2x^{2}(x+3)-1(x+3)\).
  3. The bracket \((x+3)\) is common: \(=(x+3)(2x^{2}-1)\).
  4. Check: \((x+3)(2x^{2}-1)=2x^{3}-x+6x^{2}-3=2x^{3}+6x^{2}-x-3\) ✓.
Common mistake

From \(-x-3\) the common factor is \(-1\), giving \(-1(x+3)\). Writing \(+1(x-3)\) does not match the first bracket.

Q26★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2026 Paper 1 Q4 ↗

Factorise
(a) \(2x^{2}y+5xy-12y\),

Algebraic Identities: Squares, Difference of Squares, Cubes · 7 questions

Recognising patterns in both directions

Core idea: These three identities work in both directions — expand a product, or spot the pattern and factorise. Check a difference of squares by looking for two perfect squares with a minus between them; check a perfect square by confirming the middle term is exactly twice the product of the two square roots. For cubes, the quadratic factor \(a^{2}\mp ab+b^{2}\) never factorises further over the rationals.

\(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)\)\((a\pm b)^{2}=a^{2}\pm 2ab+b^{2}\)\(a^{3}\pm b^{3}=(a\pm b)(a^{2}\mp ab+b^{2})\)

Type 6

Remainder Theorem and Factor Theorem · 7 questions

Substituting the zero of the divisor

Core idea: When \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x-a)\), the remainder is \(f(a)\) — no long division needed. If the divisor is \((bx-a)\), solve \(bx-a=0\) first and substitute that value. The factor theorem is the special case remainder \(=0\): \((x-a)\) is a factor exactly when \(f(a)=0\). A remainder question therefore becomes a substitution question, and a factor question becomes a short equation.

\(f(x)=(x-a)Q(x)+f(a)\)\(\text{divisor }bx-a:\ x=\frac{a}{b}\)\((x-a)\text{ is a factor}\iff f(a)=0\)

Dividing by \(x-2\) means substituting \(x=+2\). Using \(-2\) gives the remainder for the divisor \(x+2\).

Exam Level: Long Division, HCF/LCM, Multi-part Proofs · 8 questions

Putting the whole topic together under exam pressure

Core idea: Paper 1 polynomial questions chain the skills: use the factor theorem to find an unknown constant, divide to get the quadratic quotient, then factorise it fully. Long division must be laid out with descending powers and a placeholder \(0x\) for any missing power. For HCF and LCM, factorise every polynomial into primes first — the HCF takes the lowest power of each common factor and the LCM takes the highest power of every factor.

\(f(x)=(x-a)Q(x)+R\)\(\text{HCF}: \text{lowest powers} \quad \text{LCM}: \text{highest powers}\)\(\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}=P\times Q\)

  4. Subtract: \((3x+6)-(3x+6)=0\). Quotient \(x+3\), remainder \(0\).

  2. \(=\frac{2}{9}+\frac{1}{9}-\frac{57}{9}+\frac{54}{9}=\frac{0}{9}=0\), so \(3x-1\) is a factor.
  3. (b) Divide \(6x^{3}+x^{2}-19x+6\) by \(3x-1\): \(6x^{3}\div 3x=2x^{2}\), subtract \(6x^{3}-2x^{2}\) to get \(3x^{2}-19x\).
  4. \(3x^{2}\div 3x=x\), subtract \(3x^{2}-x\) to get \(-18x+6\); then \(-18x\div 3x=-6\), subtract \(-18x+6\) to get \(0\).
  5. So \(f(x)=(3x-1)(2x^{2}+x-6)=(3x-1)(2x-3)(x+2)\).
  6. (c) \(x=\frac{1}{3}\), \(x=\frac{3}{2}\) or \(x=-2\).
  7. Check by substituting \(x=-2\): \(6(-8)+4+38+6=-48+48=0\) ✓.
Common mistake

The factor is \(3x-1\), so the root is \(x=\frac{1}{3}\), not \(x=-\frac{1}{3}\). Solve \(3x-1=0\) first, then substitute.

Q49★★★☆5 marks

(a) Show that \(n^{3}-n\equiv (n-1)n(n+1)\).
(b) Explain why \(n^{3}-n\) is divisible by \(6\) for every positive integer \(n\).
(c) Verify the result for \(n=10\) by finding the quotient.

Answer(a) \((n-1)n(n+1)=n^{3}-n\)　(b) one of the three consecutive integers is even and one is a multiple of \(3\)　(c) \(990=6\times 165\)
  1. (a) Expand the product: \((n-1)n(n+1)=n(n^{2}-1)=n^{3}-n\).
  2. So the two expressions are identical for every value of \(n\) — an identity.
  3. (b) By (a), \(n^{3}-n=(n-1)n(n+1)\) is the product of three consecutive integers.
  4. Among three consecutive integers exactly one is even, so the product is divisible by \(2\).
  5. Among three consecutive integers exactly one is a multiple of \(3\), so the product is divisible by \(3\).
  6. Since \(2\) and \(3\) are coprime, the product is divisible by \(2\times 3=6\).
  7. (c) For \(n=10\): \(10^{3}-10=1000-10=990\), and \(990\div 6=165\), so \(990=6\times 165\) ✓.
  8. Check the factor form too: \(9\times 10\times 11=990\) ✓.
Common mistake

Do not verify only with one number: divisibility by \(6\) needs both a factor \(2\) and a factor \(3\), and the proof must explain why both are always present.

Q50★★★★8 marks

Let \(f(x)=2x^{3}-3x^{2}-11x+6\).
(a) Show that \(x-3\) is a factor of \(f(x)\).
(b) Factorise \(f(x)\) completely.
(c) Solve the equation \(f(x)=0\).
(d) Find the remainder when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+1\).
(e) Write down the remainder when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(2x-1\), giving a reason.

Answer(a) \(f(3)=0\)　(b) \((x-3)(x+2)(2x-1)\)　(c) \(x=3,\ -2,\ \frac{1}{2}\)　(d) \(12\)　(e) \(0\), since \(2x-1\) is a factor
  1. (a) \(f(3)=2(27)-3(9)-11(3)+6=54-27-33+6=0\).
  2. Since \(f(3)=0\), by the factor theorem \(x-3\) is a factor of \(f(x)\).
  3. (b) Divide: \(f(x)=(x-3)(2x^{2}+3x-2)\). Verify by expanding: \((x-3)(2x^{2}+3x-2)=2x^{3}+3x^{2}-2x-6x^{2}-9x+6=2x^{3}-3x^{2}-11x+6\) ✓.
  4. Factorise \(2x^{2}+3x-2=(2x-1)(x+2)\), so \(f(x)=(x-3)(2x-1)(x+2)\).
  5. (c) \(x=3\), \(x=\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x=-2\).
  6. (d) Remainder \(=f(-1)=2(-1)-3(1)+11+6=-2-3+11+6=12\).
  7. (e) By the factor theorem with \(2x-1=0\), \(x=\frac{1}{2}\): \(f\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=2\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)-3\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{11}{2}+6=\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{11}{2}+6\)
  8. \(=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{11}{2}+6=-6+6=0\). The remainder is \(0\), which also follows from \((2x-1)\) being a factor found in (b).
Common mistake

In (e) the divisor \(2x-1\) needs \(x=\frac{1}{2}\), not \(x=2\). Also a zero remainder is consistent with \((2x-1)\) appearing in the factorisation — a free check.
