Equilateral Triangle Congruent Triangles (SAS) Angle Sum of Triangle Corresponding Angles

QQuestion

In the figure, \(ABC\) is an equilateral triangle. Let \(D\) and \(E\) be points lying on \(AC\) and \(BC\) respectively such that \(AD = CE\). If \(\angle CBD = 38^\circ\), then \(\angle AEB = \)
A. 73° B. 75° C. 78° D. 82°

Step 11M

Since \(ABC\) is equilateral, all sides are equal and all interior angles are \(60^\circ\):

\[ AB = BC = CA, \qquad \angle ABC = \angle BCA = \angle CAB = 60^\circ. \]
Equilateral \(\triangle\) = equiangular \(\triangle\): every interior angle is \(60^\circ\).
Step 21A

From the slider diagram, \(D\) lies on \(AC\) and \(E\) lies on \(BC\). The condition \(AD = CE\) means \(BE = BC - CE = AC - AD = CD\).

\[ BE = CD \]
Subtract equal lengths from equal sides of the equilateral triangle.
\(BE = CD\)
0 / 2

BPart (b) — Proving \(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD\)

This is the key step of the solution: show the two triangles are congruent by SAS.

AB = — BC = — BE = — CD = —
Step 11M

In \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle BCD\):

\[ AB = BC \qquad (\text{given, equilateral } \triangle) \] \[ \angle ABE = \angle BCD \qquad (\text{prop. of equilateral } \triangle) \] \[ BE = CD \qquad (\text{proved in part (a)}) \]
Look for two sides and the included angle in the two triangles.
Step 21A

Therefore, by the SAS congruence criterion:

\[ \triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD \]
SAS: two sides and the included angle of one triangle equal the corresponding parts of the other.
\(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD\) (SAS)
0 / 2

CPart (c) — Finding \(\angle AEB\)

Use corresponding angles of the congruent triangles, then the angle sum of \(\triangle ABE\).

∠ABE = — ∠BAE = — ∠AEB = —
Step 11M

Since \(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD\), corresponding angles are equal:

\[ \angle BAE = \angle CBD = 38^\circ \qquad (\text{corr. } \angle s,\ \cong \triangle s) \]
Match the vertices in the same order: \(A \leftrightarrow B\), \(B \leftrightarrow C\), \(E \leftrightarrow D\).
Step 21M

Also, in the equilateral triangle:

\[ \angle ABE = 60^\circ \qquad (\text{prop. of equilateral } \triangle) \]
\(\angle ABE\) is the angle at \(B\) between \(BA\) and \(BE\), i.e. an interior angle of the equilateral triangle.
Step 31M

In \(\triangle ABE\), using the angle sum of a triangle:

\[ \angle AEB = 180^\circ - \angle ABE - \angle BAE \qquad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle) \] \[ = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 38^\circ = 82^\circ \]
The three angles of any triangle add up to \(180^\circ\).
\(\angle AEB = 82^\circ\) (Option D)
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(BE = CD\) (since \(AD = CE\) and \(AB = BC = CA\))
  • Part (b): \(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD\) (SAS)
  • Part (c): \(\angle BAE = 38^\circ\), \(\angle ABE = 60^\circ\), so \(\angle AEB = 82^\circ\)
  • Final answer: Option D — \(82^\circ\)
