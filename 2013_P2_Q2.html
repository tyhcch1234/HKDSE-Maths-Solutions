[2013 MC #2]

If \(\dfrac{y-1}{c}=\dfrac{y+1}{d}\), then \(y=\)

A. \(\dfrac{c-d}{c+d}\)
B. \(\dfrac{d-c}{c+d}\)
C. \(\dfrac{c+d}{c-d}\)
D. \(\dfrac{c+d}{d-c}\)  ✔
1 Start with the given equation
\[ \frac{y-1}{c}=\frac{y+1}{d} \]
Hint: A fraction equation is easiest to clear by cross-multiplying.
2 Cross-multiply
\[ d(y-1)=c(y+1) \]
Hint: Multiply both sides by \(cd\), the common denominator.
Cross-Multiplication\(\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}\ \Rightarrow\ ad=bc\)Multiplying both sides by \(cd\) clears the denominators \(c\) and \(d\) in one move.
3 Expand both brackets
\[ dy-d=cy+c \]
Hint: Distribute carefully; the minus sign applies to both terms? No — only to the \(1\).
Distributive Law\(a(b+c)=ab+ac\)The brackets \(d(y-1)\) and \(c(y+1)\) must be expanded before the \(y\)-terms can be collected.
4 Collect the \(y\)-terms on one side
\[ dy-cy=c+d \]
Hint: Add \(d\) to both sides, then subtract \(cy\) from both sides.
5 Factor out \(y\) on the left
\[ (d-c)\,y=c+d \]
Hint: \(dy-cy=y(d-c)\) — the common factor is \(y\).
Common Factor Law\(AB+AC=A(B+C)\)The two \(y\)-terms \(dy-cy\) share the factor \(y\), so they become \(y(d-c)\).
6 Divide both sides by \((d-c)\)
\[ y=\frac{c+d}{d-c} \]
Hint: This matches option D.
Answer: \(y=\dfrac{c+d}{d-c}\)  →  Option D

Question. Make \(x\) the subject of the formula \(\dfrac{x+3}{a}=\dfrac{x-2}{b}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Cross-multiply: \(b(x+3)=a(x-2)\).
Step 2. Expand both sides: \(bx+3b=ax-2a\).
Step 3. Collect the \(x\)-terms on the left and the constants on the right: \(bx-ax=-2a-3b\), i.e. \(x(b-a)=-(2a+3b)\).
Step 4. Divide by \(b-a\) and absorb the minus sign: \(x=\dfrac{2a+3b}{a-b}\). Check with \(a=5\), \(b=7\): \(x=-15.5\), and both sides equal \(-2.5\).
Answer: \(x=\dfrac{2a+3b}{a-b}\)
