Question

Consider the following system of inequalities:

\[ \begin{cases} x-2y \le 1\\ x+4y \le 13\\ 2x-y \ge -1 \end{cases} \]

Let \(R\) be the region which represents the solution of the above system of inequalities. \(P(x,y)=5x-2y+c\,\), where \((x,y)\) is a point lying in \(R\). Find the constant \(c\) such that the least value of \(P\) is \(22\).

  1. A. \(1\)
  2. B. \(23\)
  3. C. \(25\)
  4. D. \(29\)

Step-by-step Solution

1Rewrite each inequality as a line.
Elimination Method \(a_1x+b_1y=c_1,\quad a_2x+b_2y=c_2\) Solve each pair of boundary lines to obtain the three vertices of the feasible region.

The three boundary lines are \[ L_1:\ x-2y=1,\qquad L_2:\ x+4y=13,\qquad L_3:\ 2x-y=-1 . \] The region \(R\) is the closed triangle formed by these three lines.

Hint: In linear programming, the optimum of a linear function over a polygon always occurs at a vertex.
2Find all intersection points (vertices of \(R\)).

\(L_1 \cap L_2\): subtracting gives \(6y=12\Rightarrow y=2\),
then \(x=5\). → \((5,2)\)

\(L_1 \cap L_3\): from \(L_1\), \(x=1+2y\). Substituting into \(L_3\): \[2(1+2y)-y=-1\Rightarrow 2+3y=-1\Rightarrow y=-1,\quad x=-1.\ \to (-1,-1)\]

\(L_2 \cap L_3\): from \(L_3\), \(y=2x+1\). Substituting into \(L_2\): \[x+4(2x+1)=13\Rightarrow 9x=9\Rightarrow x=1,\quad y=3.\ \to (1,3)\]

Hint: The three vertices of \(R\) are \((5,2)\), \((-1,-1)\) and \((1,3)\).
3Evaluate \(P\) at each vertex (in terms of \(c\)).
Vertex Theorem for Linear Programming \(P=ax+by+c\text{ is optimised at a vertex of the region}\) A linear function over a polygon takes its greatest and least values at corner points, so only the vertices need to be tested.

\[P(x,y)=5x-2y+c\] \[P(5,2)=25-4+c = 21+c\] \[P(-1,-1)=-5+2+c=-3+c\] \[P(1,3)=5-6+c=-1+c\]

Hint: Only the constant term changes; the “c” is the same for all vertices.
4Impose “least value of \(P\) is 22”.
Maximum and Minimum of a Linear Function \(\min P=\min\{P(A),P(B),P(C)\}\) Compare the values at all vertices: the smallest one gives the minimum of \(P\).

The least value of \(P\) over \(R\) is the smallest of the three vertex values.
Note \(-3+c < -1+c < 21+c\), so the minimum is at \((-1,-1)\). \[ \min P = -3+c = 22 \]

Hint: Compare the three expressions \(-3+c,\;-1+c,\;21+c\) — the smallest is \(-3+c\).
5Solve for \(c\).

\[ -3+c=22 \;\Longrightarrow\; c=25 . \] Equivalently,
requiring all vertex values \(\ge 22\): \[ 21+c\ge 22\Rightarrow c\ge 1,\quad -3+c\ge 22\Rightarrow c\ge 25,\quad -1+c\ge 22\Rightarrow c\ge 23. \] Combining (taking the largest lower bound): \(c\ge 25\). The *smallest* such \(c\) that makes the minimum equal to \(22\) is \(\boxed{25}\).

Hint: The binding constraint is the smallest vertex value; the answer is the largest of the three candidates, but the question asks for the value making the minimum exactly 22.
Answer: C. \(c=25\)

Question. The feasible region is the triangle with vertices \(A(6,1)\), \(B(-1,-2)\) and \(C(2,4)\). If \(P=4x-3y+k\) and the minimum value of \(P\) is \(15\), find \(k\).

Show the worked solution
1. Test \(P=4x-3y\) at each vertex: at \(A\), \(P=24-3=21\).
2. At \(B\), \(P=-4+6=2\);
at \(C\), \(P=8-12=-4\).
3. The minimum is \(-4\), so \(-4+k=15\).
4. Hence \(k=19\).
Answer: \(k=19\)
