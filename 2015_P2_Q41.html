[2015MC#41]
Find the constant \(k\) such that the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-6y+k=0\) and the straight line \(x+y+4=0\) intersect at only one point.
A. −16
B. −8
C. 8
D. 16
STEP 1 · Make the line the subject From \(x+y+4=0\): \[ y = -x-4 \qquad \cdots (1) \] We substitute this into the circle so that only one variable remains.
STEP 2 · Substitute into the circle \[ x^{2}+(-x-4)^{2}+2x-6(-x-4)+k=0 \] Expand \((x+4)^2=x^2+8x+16\): \[ x^{2}+x^{2}+8x+16+2x+6x+24+k=0 \] \[ 2x^{2}+16x+(k+40)=0 \qquad \cdots (2) \]
STEP 3 · “Only one point” ⇒ discriminant = 0 Equation (2) must have exactly one real root: \[ \Delta = 0 \] \[ (16)^{2}-4(2)(k+40)=0 \]
STEP 4 · Solve for k \[ 256-8k-320=0 \] \[ -8k-64=0 \] \[ k = -8 \]
Sanity check: the tangent line touches the circle at exactly one point — the diagram shows the point of tangency.
Answer: B  (k = −8)
Note: Eliminating \(x\) instead (write \(x=-y-4\)) gives the same value of \(k\). This is a good exercise to verify the result.
