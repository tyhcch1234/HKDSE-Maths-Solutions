QUESTION

In the figure, $ABCD$ are points on a circle. $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at the point $E$. $BD$ is produced to the point $T$ such that $TC$ is tangent to the circle. It is given that $AB \parallel TC$. If $\angle AED = 85^\circ$ and $\angle CTE = 23^\circ$, then $\angle ADE =$

  • A. $39^\circ$
  • B. $56^\circ$
  • C. $62^\circ$
  • D. $72^\circ$
💡 Angle chase: use $AB\parallel TC$ with alternating angles, the tangent–chord (alternate segment) theorem, and angles in the same segment.
Step 1 · Parallel lines (transversal BD) Since $AB \parallel TC$ and $BDT$ is a transversal, the alternate angles are equal: $$\angle ABD = \angle CTE = 23^\circ.$$
Step 2 · Angles on a straight line & triangle ABE $A$, $E$, $C$ are collinear and $B$, $E$, $D$ are collinear. $\angle AED$ and $\angle AEB$ are angles on the straight line $BD$, so $$\angle AEB = 180^\circ - 85^\circ = 95^\circ.$$ In $\triangle ABE$: $$\angle BAE = 180^\circ - 95^\circ - 23^\circ = 62^\circ,$$ and since $AC$ is the same line, $\angle BAC = 62^\circ$.
Step 3 · Parallel lines again (transversal AC) Using $AB \parallel TC$ with transversal $ACT$ (alternate angles): $$\angle TCA = \angle BAC = 62^\circ.$$
Step 4 · Tangent–chord angle (alternate segment) The tangent $TC$ and chord $CA$ make $\angle TCA$, which equals the angle in the alternate segment, $\angle CBA$: $$\angle ABC = \angle TCA = 62^\circ.$$
Step 5 · Angle sum in triangle ABC In $\triangle ABC$: $$\angle ACB = 180^\circ - \angle BAC - \angle ABC = 180^\circ - 62^\circ - 62^\circ = 56^\circ.$$
Step 6 · Angles in the same segment $C$ and $D$ lie on the same arc $AB$, so the angles subtended by arc $AB$ are equal: $$\angle ADB = \angle ACB = 56^\circ.$$ Since $D$, $E$, $T$ lie on line $BDT$, $\angle ADE = \angle ADB = 56^\circ$.
✅ Answer: B — $\angle ADE = 56^\circ$
