HKDSE 2012 Paper 2 — Question 36

Q36 The figure shows a shaded region (including the boundary). If $(h,k)$ is a point lying in the shaded region, which of the following are true?

I. $\;k \ge 3$
II. $\;h - k \ge -3$
III. $\;2h + k \le 6$

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

1Read the boundary lines of the shaded region

The figure gives three boundary lines, each already written in slope–intercept form $y = mx + c$:

bottom: $\;y = 3$   |   left–slanted: $\;y = x + 3$   |   right: $\;y = 6 - 2x$

The shaded triangle lies above the horizontal line, below $y=x+3$ on the left and below $y=6-2x$ on the right.

Hint: first convert every boundary into a "point above / below" statement, since $(h,k)$ is in the shaded region.
Slope-Intercept Form of a Line \(y=mx+c\) Each boundary is already in this form, so the shaded side is simply \(y\ge mx+c\) or \(y\le mx+c\).
2Convert each boundary into an inequality for $(h,k)$

Replacing $x$ by $h$ and $y$ by $k$:

Above $y=3$:   $k \ge 3$
Below $y=x+3$:   $k \le h+3 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; h-k \ge -3$
Below $y=6-2x$:   $k \le 6-2h \;\Longleftrightarrow\; 2h+k \le 6$

So the shaded region is described exactly by $k\ge 3$, $h-k\ge -3$ and $2h+k\le 6$.

Hint: rearranging $k \le h+3$ gives $h-k \ge -3$ — the inequality "flips" when terms move across.
Rearranging a Linear Inequality \(k\le h+3\ \Longleftrightarrow\ h-k\ge-3\) Subtracting \(k\) and \(3\) from both sides turns the boundary \(y=x+3\) into the form used in statement II.
3Test statement I: $k \ge 3$

The lowest boundary of the shaded region is the line $y=3$, and the region lies on the $y \ge 3$ side. Hence for every point $(h,k)$ in the region,

$k \ge 3$   — true.

Hint: the whole triangle sits at or above the height $y=3$.
4Test statement II: $h - k \ge -3$

The region is bounded above by $y = x+3$, so the region satisfies $y \le x+3$.

Put $x=h$, $y=k$: $\;k \le h+3 \;\Longrightarrow\; k - h \le 3 \;\Longrightarrow\; h - k \ge -3$.

$h-k \ge -3$   — true.

Hint: substituting a boundary vertex such as $(0,3)$ gives $0-3=-3$, confirming the boundary value.
5Test statement III: $2h + k \le 6$

The region is bounded on the right by $y = 6-2x$, with the region below it.

Put $x=h$, $y=k$: $\;k \le 6-2h \;\Longrightarrow\; 2h + k \le 6$.

$2h+k \le 6$   — true.

Hint: the vertex $(1.5,\,3)$ gives $2(1.5)+3 = 6$, exactly on the boundary.
Substituting a Point into an Inequality \(\text{put }(x,y)=(h,k)\ \text{ in }\ y\le 6-2x\) Replacing \(x\) by \(h\) and \(y\) by \(k\) gives \(k\le6-2h\), i.e. \(2h+k\le6\).
6Check the vertices numerically (optional verification)

The three vertices of the triangle are $A(0,3)$, $B(3,6)$ and $C(1.5,3)$.

* $B(3,6)$ is the intersection of $y=x+3$ and $y=6-2x$, which lies outside the shaded triangle — it is above the line $y=3$. The true top vertex is the crossing of $y=3$ with $y=x+3$ and $y=6-2x$, giving $(0,3)$ and $(1.5,3)$, together with... (the third vertex of the drawn triangle is where $y=3$ meets the two slanted lines). All points of the shaded region satisfy all three inequalities.

Hint: the shaded region is exactly the set of points satisfying all three inequalities simultaneously.
7Conclusion

All three statements — I, II and III — are true.

I ✓   II ✓   III ✓

Answer: D  (I, II and III)

Question. The shaded region in the figure is bounded by the lines \(y=1\), \(y=2x+5\) and \(y=10-x\). Which of the following must be true for every point \((h,\,k)\) in the shaded region? I. \(k\ge1\) II. \(2h-k\ge-5\) III. \(h+k\le10\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The region lies above \(y=1\), so \(k\ge1\). Statement I is true.
Step 2. The region lies below \(y=2x+5\), so \(k\le2h+5\). Rearranging: \(k-2h\le5\), i.e. \(2h-k\ge-5\). Statement II is true.
Step 3. The region lies below \(y=10-x\), so \(k\le10-h\), i.e. \(h+k\le10\). Statement III is true.
Step 4. Check the vertex \((0,\,1)\): \(1\ge1\) ✓, \(2(0)-1=-1\ge-5\) ✓, \(0+1=1\le10\) ✓. Check \(\left(\dfrac{5}{3},\,\dfrac{25}{3}\right)\): \(2\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)-\dfrac{25}{3}=-5\) ✓ and \(\dfrac{5}{3}+\dfrac{25}{3}=10\) ✓.
Step 5. All three statements must be true.
Answer: I, II and III (option D).
