2023 Paper 1 · Question 16

Coordinate Geometry — Roots of a Quadratic & Ratio of Intercepts on a Line

Sum & Product of Roots Circle Equation Line cutting a Circle Ratio OQ : QR = 1 : 4

Question

(a) Let \(a\) and \(b\) be real constants. If the roots of the equation \[x^2+ax+b=0\] are \(p\) and \(5p\), prove that \[5a^2=36b.\] 2 marks

(b) Denote the circle \(x^2+y^2-6x-12y+20=0\) by \(C\). Find the constant \(m\) such that the straight line \(y=mx\) cuts \(C\) at the points \(Q\) and \(R\) with \(OQ:QR=1:4\), where \(O\) is the origin. 3 marks

Part (a) Prove 5a² = 36b

Use the sum and product of roots, then eliminate \(p\).

0 / 3
Step 11M Write down the sum and product of the roots.

Since the roots of \(x^2+ax+b=0\) are \(p\) and \(5p\), by the sum and product of roots:

\[ p+5p=-a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 6p=-a \] \[ p\cdot(5p)=b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5p^2=b \]
Hint: For \(x^2+ax+b=0\), sum of roots \(=-\dfrac{a}{1}=-a\) and product of roots \(=\dfrac{b}{1}=b\).
Step 21M Eliminate \(p\).

From \(6p=-a\) we get \(p=-\dfrac{a}{6}\). Substitute into \(5p^2=b\):

\[ 5\left(\frac{-a}{6}\right)^{2}=b \] \[ 5\cdot\frac{a^2}{36}=b \]
Hint: Make \(p\) the subject in one equation, then substitute into the other.
Step 31 Conclude the required identity.

Multiply both sides by \(36\):

\[ 5a^{2}=36b \qquad\blacksquare \]
Proved: \(5a^{2}=36b\).
Check numerically: with \(p=1.5\), \(a=-9\), \(b=11.25\); then \(5a^2=405\) and \(36b=405\) ✓

Part (b) Find m

The line \(y=mx\) meets \(C\) at \(Q\) and \(R\) with \(OQ:QR=1:4\), i.e. \(OQ:OR=1:5\).

0 / 5
Step 11M Set up the ratio condition with coordinates.

Let \(t\) be the \(x\)-coordinate of \(Q\). Since \(Q\) and \(R\) both lie on \(y=mx\), their \(y\)-coordinates are \(mt\) and \(m\cdot(\text{x-coord of }R)\).

Given \(OQ:QR=1:4\), we have \(OQ:OR=1:(1+4)=1:5\). Since \(O,Q,R\) are collinear through the origin, the \(x\)-coordinates are in the same ratio, so the \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\) is \(5t\).

Hint: \(Q\) lies between \(O\) and \(R\). If \(OQ\) is 1 part and \(QR\) is 4 parts, then \(OR\) is 5 parts.
Step 21M Substitute \(y=mx\) into the circle.

Substitute \(y=mx\) into \(x^{2}+y^{2}-6x-12y+20=0\):

\[ x^{2}+(mx)^{2}-6x-12(mx)+20=0 \] \[ (m^{2}+1)x^{2}-(12m+6)x+20=0 \]
Hint: The \(x\)-coordinates of the intersection points \(Q\) and \(R\) are exactly the two roots of this quadratic.
Step 31M Apply the result of part (a).

Divide through by \((m^{2}+1)\) so the equation has the form \(x^2+Ax+B=0\) with roots \(t\) and \(5t\):

\[ x^{2}-\frac{6(2m+1)}{m^{2}+1}x+\frac{20}{m^{2}+1}=0 \]

Here \(A=-\dfrac{6(2m+1)}{m^{2}+1}\) plays the role of \(a\), and \(B=\dfrac{20}{m^{2}+1}\) plays the role of \(b\). By part (a), \(5A^{2}=36B\):

\[ 5\left(\frac{-6(2m+1)}{m^{2}+1}\right)^{2}=36\left(\frac{20}{m^{2}+1}\right) \]
Hint: The roots of this quadratic are \(t\) and \(5t\) — the same ratio structure as part (a).
Step 41M Simplify to find \(m\).

Since \(m^2+1\neq0\),

\[ \frac{5\cdot 36(2m+1)^{2}}{(m^{2}+1)^{2}}=\frac{720}{m^{2}+1} \] \[ \frac{180(2m+1)^{2}}{(m^{2}+1)^{2}}=\frac{720}{m^{2}+1} \] \[ 180(2m+1)^{2}=720(m^{2}+1) \] \[ (2m+1)^{2}=4(m^{2}+1) \] \[ 4m^{2}+4m+1=4m^{2}+4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4m=3 \]
Hint: Expand \((2m+1)^2\) carefully, then cancel \(4m^2\) appearing on both sides.
Step 51A Final answer.

Solving \(4m=3\):

\[ m=\frac{3}{4} \]
Answer: \(m=\dfrac{3}{4}\).
Check: With \(m=\tfrac34\), the intersections are \(Q=(2,1.5)\) and \(R=(10,7.5)\), so \(OQ:OR=2:10=1:5\) ✓

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Roots are \(p\) and \(5p\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(6p=-a\) and \(5p^{2}=b\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\boxed{5a^{2}=36b}\)
  • (b) \(OQ:QR=1:4 \Rightarrow OQ:OR=1:5\); let \(Q=(t,mt)\), \(R=(5t,5mt)\).
  • Substituting \(y=mx\) gives \((m^{2}+1)x^{2}-6(2m+1)x+20=0\) with roots \(t,\;5t\).
  • By part (a): \(5\left(\dfrac{-6(2m+1)}{m^{2}+1}\right)^{2}=36\left(\dfrac{20}{m^{2}+1}\right)\Rightarrow (2m+1)^{2}=4(m^{2}+1)\).
  • \(4m=3 \Rightarrow \boxed{m=\dfrac{3}{4}}\)
