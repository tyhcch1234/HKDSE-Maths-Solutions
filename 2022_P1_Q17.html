Step 0 — overview: roots α, β of x² + cx − 9 = 0, then an AP with terms c², α²+β², 85.

HKDSE 2022 Paper 1 — Q17

Question

Let $c$ be a real constant. The roots of the equation $x^{2}+cx-9=0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

(a) Express $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$ in terms of $c$. [3 marks]

(b) The 1st term, the 2nd term and the 3rd term of an arithmetic sequence are $c^{2}$, $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$ and $85$ respectively. Find the least value of $n$ such that the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence is greater than $2\times10^{6}$. [4 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

1Use sum & product of roots
Sum and Product of Roots \(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{b}{a},\qquad \alpha\beta=\dfrac{c}{a}\) For \(x^{2}+cx-9=0\) this gives \(\alpha+\beta=-c\) and \(\alpha\beta=-9\).

For $x^{2}+cx-9=0$ with roots $\alpha,\beta$:

$\alpha+\beta=-c,\qquad \alpha\beta=-9$

Hint: For $ax^2+bx+c=0$, sum $=-\frac{b}{a}$ and product $=\frac{c}{a}$.
2Expand $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$

$\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta=(-c)^{2}-2(-9)$

$\phantom{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}}=c^{2}+18$

Hint: use the identity $a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab$.
3Find $c$ from the AP condition
Common Difference of an Arithmetic Sequence \(T_{2}-T_{1}=T_{3}-T_{2}\) The middle of three consecutive AP terms is their average, which gives \(2\left(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\right)=c^{2}+85\).

AP terms: $c^{2},\ \alpha^{2}+\beta^{2},\ 85$ — so the middle term is the average of the outer two:

$\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}-c^{2}=85-(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})$

$(c^{2}+18)-c^{2}=85-(c^{2}+18)\ \Rightarrow\ 18=67-c^{2}$

$c^{2}=49$

Hint: common difference $d = T_2-T_1 = T_3-T_2$.
4Identify the AP

First term $a=c^{2}=49$; second term $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=c^{2}+18=67$.

Common difference $d=67-49=18$.

Hint: the AP is $49,\ 67,\ 85,\ldots$
5Form the sum inequality
Sum of the First n Terms \(S_{n}=\dfrac{n}{2}\left[2a+(n-1)d\right]\) With \(a=49\) and \(d=18\) the sum becomes \(9n^{2}+40n\), which must exceed \(2\times10^{6}\).

$S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\big[2(49)+(n-1)(18)\big]>2\times10^{6}$

$\dfrac{n}{2}\big(98+18n-18\big)>2\times10^{6}$

$\dfrac{n}{2}(18n+80)>2\times10^{6}\ \Rightarrow\ 9n^{2}+40n-2\times10^{6}>0$

Hint: $S_n=\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]$.
6Solve for the least integer $n$

$n<\dfrac{-40-\sqrt{40^{2}-4(9)(-2\times10^{6})}}{2(9)}\quad$ or $\quad n>\dfrac{-40+\sqrt{40^{2}-4(9)(-2\times10^{6})}}{2(9)}$

$n<-473.6319808\quad$ or $\quad n>469.1875364$

Since $n$ is a positive integer, $n\ge 470$.

Hint: reject the negative root; take the smallest integer above 469.19.
Answer: (a) $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=c^{2}+18$   •   (b) least value of $n$ is $470$

Question. It is given that \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the roots of the equation \(x^{2}+cx-16=0\), where \(c\) is a constant. (a) Express \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\) in terms of \(c\). (b) If \(c^{2}\), \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\) and \(100\) are in arithmetic sequence, find \(c^{2}\). (c) Hence find the least value of \(n\) such that the sum of the first \(n\) terms of the arithmetic sequence exceeds \(10^{6}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Sum and product of roots: \(\alpha+\beta=-c\) and \(\alpha\beta=-16\).
Step 2. \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta=c^{2}-2(-16)=c^{2}+32\).
Step 3. The three terms \(c^{2},\ c^{2}+32,\ 100\) are in AP, so the middle term is their average: \(2(c^{2}+32)=c^{2}+100\).
Step 4. Thus \(2c^{2}+64=c^{2}+100\), giving \(c^{2}=36\). The AP is \(36,\ 68,\ 100\) with \(a=36\) and \(d=32\).
Step 5. \(S_{n}=\dfrac{n}{2}\left[72+32(n-1)\right]=\dfrac{n}{2}(32n+40)=16n^{2}+20n\), and we need \(16n^{2}+20n>10^{6}\).
Step 6. Check: \(n=249\) gives \(16(249)^{2}+20(249)=996\,996<10^{6}\), while \(n=250\) gives \(1\,005\,000>10^{6}\). Hence the least value is \(n=250\).
Answer: (a) \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=c^{2}+32\); (b) \(c^{2}=36\); (c) \(n=250\)
