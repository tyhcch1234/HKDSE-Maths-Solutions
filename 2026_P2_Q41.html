QUESTION TRIGONOMETRY

For \(90^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\), how many roots does the equation

\(6\sin^4\theta - 5\sin^2\theta + 1 = 0\)

have?

A. \(2\)
B. \(4\)
C. \(6\)
D. \(8\)

Step-by-step solution

Step 1 · Substitute
Quadratic in sin squared theta \(u=\sin^{2}\theta\ \Rightarrow\ 4u^{2}-5u+1=0\) The substitution turns the quartic into a quadratic, which factorises as \((4u-1)(u-1)=0\), so \(u=\tfrac14\) or \(u=1\).

Let \(u=\sin^2\theta\). Then the equation becomes a quadratic in \(u\):

\[ 6u^2 - 5u + 1 = 0 \]

Factorise:

\[ (2u-1)(3u-1)=0 \]
\[ u=\tfrac12 \quad\text{or}\quad u=\tfrac13 \]
Since \(u=\sin^2\theta\ge 0\), both values are acceptable.
Step 2 · Case u = 1/2
Solving sin squared theta equals k \(\sin^{2}\theta=k\iff \sin\theta=\pm\sqrt{k}\) Taking both signs is essential: \(\sin\theta=\tfrac12\) and \(\sin\theta=-\tfrac12\) each contribute roots, which is why \(u=\tfrac14\) gives four roots.

Here \(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12\), so

\[ \sin\theta=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt2} \]

Within \(90^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\):

\[ \theta = 135^\circ,\; 225^\circ,\; 315^\circ \]

That gives \(3\) roots.

\(\sin\theta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\) gives \(\theta=135^\circ\) only; \(\sin\theta=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\) gives \(225^\circ\) and \(315^\circ\).
Step 3 · Case u = 1/3

Here \(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac13\), so

\[ \sin\theta=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt3} \]

Within \(90^\circ\le\theta\le 360^\circ\):

\[ \theta \approx 144.7^\circ,\; 215.3^\circ,\; 324.7^\circ \]

That gives \(3\) more roots.

\(\sin\theta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\) gives \(\theta\approx 144.7^\circ\); \(\sin\theta=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\) gives \(215.3^\circ\) and \(324.7^\circ\).
Step 4 · Total
Counting Roots in a Given Interval \(\text{total}=\text{roots of case }1+\text{roots of case }2\) Each value of \(\sin\theta\) in \((-1,1)\) gives two roots over a full period, so \(2+4=6\) roots lie in \(0^{\circ}\le\theta\le360^{\circ}\).

Adding the roots from both cases:

\[ 3 + 3 = 6 \]

The equation has \(6\) roots in the given interval.

Answer: C — 6 roots

Question. For \(0^{\circ}\le\theta\le360^{\circ}\), how many roots does the equation \(4\sin^{4}\theta-5\sin^{2}\theta+1=0\) have? (3 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(u=\sin^{2}\theta\): \(4u^{2}-5u+1=0\) factorises as \((4u-1)(u-1)=0\), so \(u=\tfrac14\) or \(u=1\).
Step 2. Case \(u=1\): \(\sin\theta=\pm 1\), giving \(\theta=90^{\circ}\) and \(270^{\circ}\) — two roots.
Step 3. Case \(u=\tfrac14\): \(\sin\theta=\pm\tfrac12\). \(\sin\theta=\tfrac12\) gives \(30^{\circ},150^{\circ}\); \(\sin\theta=-\tfrac12\) gives \(210^{\circ},330^{\circ}\) — four roots.
Step 4. Total: \(2+4=6\) roots in \(0^{\circ}\le\theta\le360^{\circ}\).
Answer: \(6\) roots
