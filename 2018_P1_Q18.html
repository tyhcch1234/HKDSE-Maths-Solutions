Functions and Graphs — direct variation, completing the square & the circumcentre of a triangle

Partial Variation Quadratic Function Completing the Square Vertex Circumcentre Angle in Semicircle

Question Full Question Text [8 marks]

It is given that \(f(x)\) partly varies as \(x^2\) and partly varies as \(x\). Suppose that \(f(2)=60\) and \(f(3)=99\).

(a) Find \(f(x)\). [3 marks]

(b) Let \(Q\) be the vertex of the graph of \(y=f(x)\) and \(R\) be the vertex of the graph of \(y=27-f(x)\).

(i) Using the method of completing the square, find the coordinates of \(Q\).
(ii) Write down the coordinates of \(R\).
(iii) The coordinates of the point \(S\) are \((56,0)\). Let \(P\) be the circumcentre of \(\triangle QRS\). Describe the geometric relationship between \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\). Explain your answer. [5 marks]

Part (a) Finding \(f(x)\) — setting up the variation

"Partly varies as \(x^2\) and partly varies as \(x\)" means there exist non-zero constants \(a,b\) such that \[ f(x)=ax^2+bx . \] The two data points \((2,60)\) and \((3,99)\) give two equations in \(a\) and \(b\).

f(2) = 60.0
f(3) = 99.0
Both points matched? YES
STEP 11M

Translate the words into a quadratic model with two unknown constants:

\[ f(x)=ax^{2}+bx, \qquad a\neq 0,\; b\neq 0 . \]
Hint: "Partly varies as \(x^2\) and partly varies as \(x\)" always gives a sum of two terms: one proportional to \(x^2\), one proportional to \(x\). No constant term appears.
STEP 21M

Substitute the two data points. From \(f(2)=60\):

\[ a(2)^2+b(2)=60 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4a+2b=60 \quad \cdots (1) \]

From \(f(3)=99\):

\[ a(3)^2+b(3)=99 \;\Longrightarrow\; 9a+3b=99 \quad \cdots (2) \]
Hint: Divide (1) by 2 to get \(2a+b=30\); divide (2) by 3 to get \(3a+b=33\). Subtracting gives \(a=3\) immediately.
STEP 31A

Subtract the simplified equations:

\[ (3a+b)-(2a+b)=33-30 \;\Longrightarrow\; a=3 \] \[ \text{Then } 2(3)+b=30 \;\Longrightarrow\; b=24 \]

Therefore:

\[ f(x)=3x^{2}+24x \]
Hint: Always substitute the found value of \(a\) back into the simpler equation to get \(b\), then verify with the other data point: \(f(2)=3(4)+24(2)=12+48=60\) ✓.

Part (b)(i) Vertex \(Q\) by completing the square

Start from the result of part (a): \(f(x)=3x^{2}+24x\). Complete the square to read off the vertex.

Probe point: (-2.0, -36.0)
Vertex Q = (-4, -48)
Minimum value of \(f\) = -48
STEP 11M

Factor out the coefficient of \(x^2\) from the first two terms:

\[ f(x)=3x^{2}+24x=3\left(x^{2}+8x\right) \]
Hint: You must factor out the \(3\) before completing the square, otherwise the added constant will be wrong by a factor of 3.
STEP 21M

Add and subtract \(\left(\tfrac{8}{2}\right)^{2}=16\) inside the bracket:

\[ f(x)=3\left(x^{2}+8x+16-16\right)=3\left[(x+4)^{2}-16\right] \] \[ f(x)=3(x+4)^{2}-48 \]
Hint: Half of \(8\) is \(4\), and \(4^2=16\). The bracket \((x+4)^2\) reveals the axis of symmetry \(x=-4\).
STEP 31A

In the form \(f(x)=3(x+4)^{2}-48\), the squared term is never negative, so \(f(x)\ge -48\), with equality when \(x+4=0\).

Hence the vertex (minimum point) is at:

\[ Q=(-4,\,-48) \]
Hint: General rule: \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\) has vertex \((h,k)\) and axis of symmetry \(x=h\). Here \(h=-4\) (note the sign flip!) and \(k=-48\).

Part (b)(ii) Vertex \(R\) of \(y=27-f(x)\)

The transformation \(y=27-f(x)\) is a reflection in the x-axis (because of the minus sign) followed by a vertical translation of \(+27\). Both operations keep the x-coordinate of the vertex and reverse/translate the y-coordinate.

Q (min of \(f\)) = (-4, -48)
vertex of \(y=-f(x)\) = (-4, 48)
R (vertex of \(27-f\)) = (-4, 75)
STEP 11M

Express \(27-f(x)\) using the completed-square form of \(f\):

\[ 27-f(x)=27-\left[3(x+4)^{2}-48\right]=27-3(x+4)^{2}+48 \] \[ 27-f(x)=75-3(x+4)^{2} \]
Hint: Watch the double negative: \(-\big(3(x+4)^2-48\big)=-3(x+4)^2+48\). Then combine \(27+48=75\).
STEP 21A

Now the coefficient of \((x+4)^{2}\) is \(-3<0\), so the parabola opens downward and the vertex is a maximum.

The maximum value \(75\) occurs when \(x+4=0\), i.e. \(x=-4\). Hence:

\[ R=(-4,\,75) \]
Hint: Quick alternative: the vertex of \(f\) is \((-4,-48)\). Reflecting in the x-axis sends \(-48\to 48\); translating up by \(27\) sends \(48\to 75\). So \(R=(-4,75)\) with no algebra needed — but you must still justify it.

Part (b)(iii) Geometrical relationship between \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\)

\(S=(56,0)\). We already have \(Q=(-4,-48)\) and \(R=(-4,75)\). Notice \(Q\) and \(R\) have the same x-coordinate, so \(QR\) is a vertical segment. We must find the circumcentre \(P\) of \(\triangle QRS\).

slope \(QS\) = 4/5 = 0.800
slope \(RS\) = -5/4 = -1.250
product = -1 → \(\angle QSR=90^\circ\)
radius \(P S\) = 61.5
\(P\) = midpoint of \(QR\) = (-4, 13.5)
STEP 11M

Compute the slope of \(QS\) using \(Q(-4,-48)\) and \(S(56,0)\):

\[ m_{QS}=\frac{-48-0}{-4-56}=\frac{-48}{-60}=\frac{4}{5} \]

Compute the slope of \(RS\) using \(R(-4,75)\) and \(S(56,0)\):

\[ m_{RS}=\frac{75-0}{-4-56}=\frac{75}{-60}=-\frac{5}{4} \]
Hint: The slopes \(\tfrac{4}{5}\) and \(-\tfrac{5}{4}\) are negative reciprocals of each other. That is the classic signal of perpendicular lines — check the product equals \(-1\).
STEP 21M

Multiply the two slopes:

\[ m_{QS}\cdot m_{RS}=\frac{4}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{4}\right)=-1 \]

Therefore \(QS\perp RS\), so:

\[ \angle QSR=90^\circ \]
Hint: Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their slopes is \(-1\). A right angle at a vertex of a triangle is exactly the condition needed for the next step.
STEP 31A

By the converse of the angle in a semicircle theorem, if \(\angle QSR=90^\circ\), then \(QR\) must be a diameter of the circumcircle of \(\triangle QRS\).

Since \(P\) is the circumcentre (the centre of that circle), \(P\) lies on \(QR\) and is equidistant from \(Q\) and \(R\). Hence:

\(P\) is the mid-point of the line segment joining \(Q\) and \(R\), i.e. \(PQ=PR\).
Hint: Verification — midpoint of \(QR\) is \(\left(-4,\dfrac{-48+75}{2}\right)=(-4,13.5)\). Then \(PQ=13.5+48=61.5\) and \(PS=\sqrt{(56+4)^2+(0-13.5)^2}=\sqrt{3600+182.25}=\sqrt{3782.25}=61.5\). Equal radii confirm \(P\) is the circumcentre. ✓

Summary of Answers

  • (a)   \(f(x)=3x^{2}+24x\)   (so \(a=3,\; b=24\))
  • (b)(i)   \(f(x)=3(x+4)^{2}-48\), hence \(Q=(-4,\,-48)\)
  • (b)(ii)   \(R=(-4,\,75)\), since \(27-f(x)=75-3(x+4)^{2}\)
  • (b)(iii)   \(m_{QS}\cdot m_{RS}=\tfrac{4}{5}\times\left(-\tfrac{5}{4}\right)=-1\), so \(\angle QSR=90^\circ\)
  • (b)(iii)   Hence \(QR\) is a diameter of the circumcircle; therefore \(P\) is the mid-point of \(QR\), i.e. \(P=(-4,\,13.5)\) and \(PQ=PR=PS=61.5\)
