Question 18

It is given that $f(x)$ partly varies as $x^2$ and partly varies as $x$. Suppose that $f(2)=60$ and $f(3)=99$.

(a) Find $f(x)$. [3 marks]

(b) Let $Q$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=f(x)$ and $R$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=27-f(x)$.

(i) Using the method of completing the square, find the coordinates of $Q$.
(ii) Write down the coordinates of $R$.
(iii) The coordinates of the point $S$ are $(56,0)$. Let $P$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle QRS$. Describe the geometric relationship between $P$, $Q$ and $R$. Explain your answer. [5 marks]

1Set up the variation model (Part a)
Partial Variation \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\qquad(a\neq0,\ b\neq0)\) "Partly varies as \(x^{2}\) and partly as \(x\)" means a sum of the two terms.

"Partly varies as $x^2$ and partly varies as $x$" means a sum of a term in $x^2$ and a term in $x$:

$$f(x)=ax^2+bx,\qquad a\neq 0,\ b\neq 0$$

Hint: "partly as … and partly as …" always gives a sum of the two terms, with unknown constants.

2Use the two given values

$f(2)=60:\quad a(2)^2+b(2)=60 \;\Rightarrow\; 4a+2b=60$

$f(3)=99:\quad a(3)^2+b(3)=99 \;\Rightarrow\; 9a+3b=99$

Two data points $\Rightarrow$ two equations in $a$ and $b$.

3Solve for $a$, $b$  a = 3, b = 24

From $4a+2b=60$: divide by 2  $\Rightarrow$  $2a+b=30\;\dots(1)$
From $9a+3b=99$: divide by 3  $\Rightarrow$  $3a+b=33\;\dots(2)$

$(2)-(1):\quad a=3.$   Substituting into $(1):\quad 2(3)+b=30 \Rightarrow b=24.$

$$\therefore\; f(x)=3x^2+24x$$

4Complete the square for $f(x)$  (Part b(i))
Completing the Square \(a x^{2}+bx=a\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{b^{2}}{4a}\) Taking out 2 and adding \(3^{2}=9\) inside turns \(2x^{2}+12x\) into \(2(x+3)^{2}-18\).

$$f(x)=3x^2+24x=3\left(x^2+8x\right)$$

$$=3\left(x^2+8x+16-16\right)$$

$$=3\left(x+4\right)^2-48$$

Hint: take out the coefficient of $x^2$ first; then add and subtract $\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2=16$.

5Read off the vertex $Q$
Vertex of a Quadratic Function \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{vertex}=(h,k)\) Here \(y=2(x+3)^{2}-18\), so the vertex is \((-3,-18)\).

For $y=3(x+4)^2-48$, the vertex occurs when $(x+4)^2$ is minimised, i.e. $x=-4$. Then $y=-48$.

$$Q(-4,\;-48)$$

Vertex form $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ gives vertex $(h,k)$ — here $h=-4$, $k=-48$.

6Vertex $R$ of $y=27-f(x)$  (Part b(ii))

Reflecting $f$ in the $x$-axis and shifting up by 27 flips the vertex vertically:

$$y=27-f(x)=27-\left[3(x+4)^2-48\right]=75-3(x+4)^2$$

The vertex is at the same $x$ but with $y$-coordinate $27-(-48)=75$:

$$R(-4,\;75)$$

7Slopes of $QS$ and $RS$  (Part b(iii))

With $Q(-4,-48)$, $R(-4,75)$ and $S(56,0)$:

$$m_{QS}=\frac{-48-0}{-4-56}=\frac{-48}{-60}=\frac{4}{5}$$

$$m_{RS}=\frac{75-0}{-4-56}=\frac{75}{-60}=-\frac{5}{4}$$

8$QS \perp RS$ (product of slopes $=-1$)

$$m_{QS}\times m_{RS}=\frac{4}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{4}\right)=-1$$

Hence $QS\perp RS$, so $\angle QSR = 90^\circ$ — triangle $QRS$ is right-angled at $S$.

Hint: perpendicular lines $\iff$ product of slopes $=-1$.

9Conclude the relationship of $P$, $Q$, $R$

In a right-angled triangle the circumcentre is the mid-point of the hypotenuse (Thales' Theorem: the angle in a semicircle is a right angle, so $QR$ is a diameter).

Here the hypotenuse is $QR$. Therefore $P$ is the mid-point of the line segment joining $Q$ and $R$.

$$P=\left(\frac{-4+(-4)}{2},\frac{-48+75}{2}\right)=\left(-4,\;\frac{27}{2}\right)$$

Answers:

(a) $f(x)=3x^2+24x$

(b)(i) $Q(-4,-48)$    (b)(ii) $R(-4,75)$

(b)(iii) Since $m_{QS}\times m_{RS}=-1$, $\angle QSR=90^\circ$; so $QR$ is a diameter of the circumcircle of $\triangle QRS$. Hence the circumcentre $P$ is the mid-point of $QR$, i.e. $P\left(-4,\dfrac{27}{2}\right)$.

Question. It is given that \(f(x)\) partly varies as \(x^{2}\) and partly varies as \(x\). If \(f(2)=32\) and \(f(3)=54\): (a) find \(f(x)\); (b) by completing the square, find the vertex of the graph of \(y=f(x)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(f(x)=ax^{2}+bx\) with \(a\neq0\), \(b\neq0\).
Step 2. (a) \(4a+2b=32\) and \(9a+3b=54\), i.e. \(2a+b=16\) and \(3a+b=18\).
Step 3. (a) Subtracting: \(a=2\), then \(b=16-4=12\). So \(f(x)=2x^{2}+12x\).
Step 4. (b) \(f(x)=2(x^{2}+6x)=2\left[(x+3)^{2}-9\right]=2(x+3)^{2}-18\), so the vertex is \((-3,-18)\).
Answer: (a) \(f(x)=2x^{2}+12x\) (b) \((-3,-18)\)
