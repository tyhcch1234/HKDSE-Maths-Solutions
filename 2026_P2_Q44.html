Standard Score

Question

The table below shows the scores (in marks) and the corresponding standard scores of four students in a test.

Score (marks)\(\alpha\)\(40\)\(58\)\(\beta\)
Standard score\(-1\)\(0\)\(2\)\(3\)

Find \(\beta-\alpha\).

  • A. \(31\)
  • B. \(36\)
  • C. \(54\)
  • D. \(98\)

Solution

Formula
Standard Score \(z=\dfrac{x-\bar{x}}{s}\) The standard score measures how many standard deviations a mark lies from the mean, so a single \((x,z)\) pair determines \(s\) once \(\bar{x}\) is known.
Standard score \(z=\dfrac{x-\bar x}{s}\).
Mean & s.d.
Mark from a Standard Score \(x=\bar{x}+zs\) Rearranging gives \(2=\dfrac{58-40}{s}\), so \(s=9\); every other standard score can then be converted back to a mark.
\(z=0\) at \(x=40\Rightarrow\bar x=40\); \(z=2\) at \(x=58\Rightarrow2=\dfrac{58-40}{s}\Rightarrow s=9\).
Find alpha \(-1=\dfrac{\alpha-40}{9}\Rightarrow\alpha=31\).
Find beta \(3=\dfrac{\beta-40}{9}\Rightarrow\beta=67\).
Result
Difference of Two Standardised Marks \(\beta-\alpha=(\bar{x}+z_{\beta}s)-(\bar{x}+z_{\alpha}s)=s\left(z_{\beta}-z_{\alpha}\right)\) The mean cancels, so the gap is simply \(9\times\left(3-(-1)\right)=36\).
\(\beta-\alpha=67-31=36\).
Answer: B — 36

Question. In a test, the standard score of a mark of \(60\) is \(1.5\) and the standard score of a mark of \(45\) is \(0\). A candidate scoring \(\alpha\) has standard score \(-2\), and a candidate scoring \(\beta\) has standard score \(2.5\). Find \(\beta-\alpha\). (4 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(z=0\) at \(x=45\), so the mean is \(\bar{x}=45\).
Step 2. \(1.5=\dfrac{60-45}{s}=\dfrac{15}{s}\), so \(s=10\).
Step 3. \(-2=\dfrac{\alpha-45}{10}\), so \(\alpha=45-20=25\).
Step 4. \(2.5=\dfrac{\beta-45}{10}\), so \(\beta=45+25=70\).
Step 5. \(\beta-\alpha=70-25=45\).
Answer: \(\beta-\alpha=45\)
