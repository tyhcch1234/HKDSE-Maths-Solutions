2015 Paper 2 · Question 8

Reading the signs of the constants a and b from the graph of \(y=a(x+b)^2\)

Quadratic Functions Vertex Form \(y=a(x+b)^2\) Sign of Coefficients Graph Reading

Question MC · 1 mark

The figure shows the graph of \(y=a(x+b)^2\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. Which of the following is true?

  1. \(a>0\) and \(b>0\)
  2. \(a>0\) and \(b<0\)
  3. \(a<0\) and \(b>0\)
  4. \(a<0\) and \(b<0\)

The given graph: a U-shaped curve touching the \(x\)-axis at a single point on the negative \(x\)-axis.

Part (a) — Determining the sign of \(a\) shape of curve

curve: y =
opening:
1 Identify the shape of the curve 1M

The graph is a U-shaped parabola that opens upward — the two arms go up as we move away from the vertex.

Hint: When a parabola opens upward, its leading coefficient is positive. If it opened downward (an ∩-shape), the leading coefficient would be negative.
Therefore \(a > 0\).
2 Confirm by expanding the function 1A

Expand to see the coefficient of \(x^2\):

\[ y = a(x+b)^2 = a\bigl(x^2 + 2bx + b^2\bigr) = ax^2 + 2ab\,x + ab^2 \]

The coefficient of \(x^2\) is exactly \(a\).

Hint: The sign of the \(x^2\)-coefficient decides the direction of opening — positive \(\Rightarrow\) up, negative \(\Rightarrow\) down.
The graph opens upward, so the \(x^2\)-coefficient \(a > 0\).

Part (b) — Determining the sign of \(b\) position of the vertex

curve: y =
vertex at x = −b = , y = 0 (touches the x-axis)
1 Locate the vertex from the form \(y=a(x+b)^2\) 1M

The vertex form of a parabola is \(y = a(x-h)^2 + k\), where the vertex is \((h,\,k)\).

Comparing with \(y = a(x+b)^2 = a\bigl(x-(-b)\bigr)^2 + 0\):

\[ h = -b, \qquad k = 0 \]
Hint: A square is never negative — so \((x+b)^2 \ge 0\). Since \(a>0\), the minimum value of \(y\) is \(0\), giving \(k=0\). The vertex lies on the \(x\)-axis.
Vertex is \(\bigl(-b,\;0\bigr)\).
2 Read the vertex position off the given graph 1M

In the question's graph, the lowest point (the vertex / touching point) lies on the \(x\)-axis, to the left of the origin — i.e. on the negative \(x\)-axis.

Hence the \(x\)-coordinate of the vertex is negative:

\[ -b < 0 \]
Hint: Left of \(O\) means a negative \(x\)-value. So \(-b<0\) — that is not the same as \(b<0\)! Be careful with the sign in front of \(b\).
\(-b < 0\)
3 Solve the inequality for \(b\) 1A

Multiply the inequality \(-b < 0\) by \(-1\) (remember to reverse the direction of the inequality sign):

\[ -b < 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; b > 0 \]
Hint: Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number flips the sign. So from \(-b<0\) we get \(b>0\) — the vertex to the left of \(O\) always corresponds to \(b>0\).
\(b > 0\)

Summary of the answer

  • The parabola opens upward \(\;\Rightarrow\;\) \(a > 0\)
  • The vertex \((-b,\,0)\) lies on the negative \(x\)-axis \(\;\Rightarrow\;\) \(-b < 0 \;\Rightarrow\;\) \(b > 0\)
  • Both constants are positive.
\[ \boxed{\,a>0 \;\text{ and }\; b>0\,} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \boxed{\text{Option A}} \]
