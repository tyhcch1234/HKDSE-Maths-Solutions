Coordinate Geometry — In-centre of a triangle formed by three straight lines

Straight Lines In-centre Angle Bisector Tangent ⊥ Radius Symmetry

Question Multiple Choice

The equation of the three sides of a triangle are given. Use the in-centre condition to find \(a\).

The equation of the three sides of a triangle are \[ 4x+3y=24,\qquad 4x-3y=24,\qquad x=a, \] where \(a\) is a constant. If the \(x\)-coordinate of the in-centre of the triangle is \(31\), then \(a=\)
A 15
B 31
C 45
D 51

Part (a) — Sketch & Use Symmetry Setup

\(L_1: 4x+3y=24\) and \(L_2: 4x-3y=24\) meet at \(C\). A third line \(L: x=a\) closes the triangle. The in-centre \(I\) lies on every angle bisector.

a = 51
C = (6, 0) in-centre = (31, 0) in-radius = 25
1
Find where \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) meet
1M
\[ 4x+3y=24 \quad\cdots(1)\qquad 4x-3y=24 \quad\cdots(2) \] \[ (1)+(2):\quad 8x=48 \;\Rightarrow\; x=6 \] Substitute into (1): \(\;4(6)+3y=24 \;\Rightarrow\; y=0\).
Hint: Adding the two equations kills the \(y\)-term instantly because the coefficients \(+3\) and \(-3\) are opposites.
The two lines meet at \(C(6,\;0)\), which lies on the \(x\)-axis.
2
Identify the axis of symmetry
1M
\[ L_1:\;4x+3y=24 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad y=\frac{24-4x}{3}=-\frac{4}{3}x+8 \] \[ L_2:\;4x-3y=24 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad y=\frac{4x-24}{3}=\frac{4}{3}x-8 \] The slopes \(-\tfrac43\) and \(+\tfrac43\) are negatives of each other, so \(L_2\) is the reflection image of \(L_1\) about the \(x\)-axis. Hence the triangle is **symmetric about the \(x\)-axis**.
Hint: Reflection in the \(x\)-axis changes \(y \mapsto -y\), which flips the sign of the slope — exactly what we see here.
The \(x\)-axis is the axis of symmetry of the triangle, so it passes through the in-centre \(I\) and bisects \(\angle ACB\).
3
Locate the in-centre and the in-radius
1A
Since \(I\) lies on the \(x\)-axis and its \(x\)-coordinate is \(31\), \[ I=(31,\;0). \] The third side is the vertical line \(L: x=a\). Because \(I\) must lie strictly inside the triangle, \(a>31\). The distance from \(I\) to the line \(x=a\) equals the in-radius: \[ r=\bigl|a-31\bigr|=a-31. \]
Hint: The in-radius is the perpendicular distance from the in-centre to any side. For the vertical side \(x=a\) this distance is measured horizontally.
\(I=(31,0)\) and the in-radius is \(\;r=a-31\).
4
Use tangent ⊥ radius and \(\tan\angle PCI=\tfrac43\)
1M
Let \(P\) be the point of contact of \(L_2\) onto the inscribed circle. Then \(IP\perp L_2\) (tangent \(\perp\) radius), so \(\triangle CPI\) is right-angled at \(P\). The slope of \(L_2\) is \(\tfrac43\), so \[ \tan\angle PCI=\frac{4}{3}. \] Drop the perpendicular from \(P\) to \(CI\) (i.e. use the right triangle with \(\angle PCI\)): \[ \sin\angle PCI=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} =\frac{4}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}=\frac{4}{5}. \]
Hint: A slope of \(\tfrac43\) corresponds to a \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) right triangle: \(\tan\theta=\tfrac43\Rightarrow\sin\theta=\tfrac45\).
\(\tan\angle PCI=\dfrac43\) and \(\sin\angle PCI=\dfrac45\).
5
Solve for \(a\)
1A
In the right-angled triangle \(CPI\), \(\;IP=r=a-31\;\) and \(\;CI=31-6=25\): \[ \sin\angle PCI=\frac{IP}{CI} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a-31}{25}=\frac{4}{5}. \] \[ a-31=25\times\frac45=20 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=51. \]
Hint: Match the side opposite the angle \(P\widehat{C}I\) with the hypotenuse \(CI\), then cross-multiply.
\[ \boxed{a=51} \qquad\text{Answer: D} \]

The in-centre depends on \(a\)
1M
For a triangle symmetric about the \(x\)-axis with apex \(C(6,0)\), the in-centre always lies on the \(x\)-axis. Its position is determined by the distance \(r=a-31\) to the vertical side. Slide \(a\) and observe that the inscribed circle grows or shrinks, pushing \(I\) along the axis.
Hint: Whatever \(a\) is, \(I\) stays on the \(x\)-axis — this is forced by symmetry, not a coincidence.
\(I\) is on the \(x\)-axis for every value of \(a\).
2
Tangency fixes the ratio
1A
The circle touches \(L_2\) at \(P\), so \(IP\perp L_2\). In the right triangle \(CPI\): \[ \sin\angle PCI=\frac{IP}{CI}=\frac{a-31}{25}. \] But \(\angle PCI\) is completely determined by the slope of \(L_2\): \[ \sin\angle PCI=\frac{4}{5}\quad(\text{from the }3\text{–}4\text{–}5\text{ triangle}). \] Equating the two expressions gives the unique solution \(a=51\) — exactly the value that makes the readout \(\sin\angle PCI=0.800\).
Hint: Pause the slider at \(a=51\) and check that the readout shows \(\sin\angle PCI=0.800=\tfrac45\).
\[ \frac{a-31}{25}=\frac45 \;\Longrightarrow\; a=51 \]

Summary of Answers

  • \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) meet at \(C(6,\,0)\) — found by solving \(4x+3y=24\) and \(4x-3y=24\).
  • The \(x\)-axis is the axis of symmetry (slopes \(-\tfrac43\) and \(+\tfrac43\)), so the in-centre lies on it.
  • Given the \(x\)-coordinate is \(31\), the in-centre is \(I(31,\,0)\) and the in-radius is \(\;r=a-31\).
  • Tangent ⊥ radius gives \(\triangle CPI\) right-angled at \(P\), and \(\tan\angle PCI=\tfrac43\Rightarrow\sin\angle PCI=\tfrac45\).
  • Therefore \(\dfrac{a-31}{25}=\dfrac45\), giving \(\;a-31=20\), so \(a=51\).
  • Correct option: D.

Key techniques: solving simultaneous linear equations · line symmetry about an axis · perpendicular distance / in-radius · tangent–radius right angle · 3–4–5 triangle ratios.
