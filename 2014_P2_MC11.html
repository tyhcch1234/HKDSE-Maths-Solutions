Estimation, Approximation & Error

#Maximum Absolute Error #Rectangular Sheet #Inequalities

The width and the length of a thin rectangular metal sheet are measured as 8 cm and 10 cm correct to the nearest cm respectively. Let \(x\text{ cm}^2\) be the actual area of the metal sheet. Find the range of values of \(x\).

Step-by-step solution

1

Understand the measurement error

The width is measured as 8 cm correct to the nearest cm. By the Maximum Absolute Error concept, the actual width \(w\) satisfies:

\(7.5 \le w < 8.5\)

Because 8 cm rounded to nearest cm means the true value lies within \(\pm 0.5\) cm.

2

Same for the length

The length is measured as 10 cm correct to the nearest cm.

\(9.5 \le l < 10.5\)
3

Relate width and length to the area

The actual area is \(x = w \times l\). Since both \(w\) and \(l\) are positive and within their own ranges, we can multiply the corresponding bounds.

\(7.5 \times 9.5 \;\le\; x \;<\; 8.5 \times 10.5\)

(Lower bound uses the smallest \(w\) and smallest \(l\); upper bound uses the largest \(w\) and largest \(l\).)

4

Compute the numerical bounds

Lower bound
\(7.5 \times 9.5 = 71.25\)
Upper bound
\(8.5 \times 10.5 = 89.25\)

So the range of \(x\) is:

\(71.25 \le x < 89.25\)
✔ Option A

Key idea

When a measurement \(m\) is given correct to the nearest unit, the maximum absolute error is \(0.5\) of that unit. For a product of two positive measurements, the smallest possible product is the product of the smallest possible values, and the largest possible product is the product of the largest possible values.
