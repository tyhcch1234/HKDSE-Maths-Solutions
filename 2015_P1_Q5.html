HKDSE Math 2015 · Paper 1 · Q5

Inequalities

(a) Find the range of values of $x$ which satisfy both

$\dfrac{7-3x}{5} \le 2(x+2)$   and   $4x - 13 > 0$.

(b) Write down the least integer which satisfies both inequalities in (a).

🧮 Linear inequalities 🔗 Intersection of ranges 📏 Number line 🎯 Least integer
[4 marks]
1 Solve the first inequality: $\dfrac{7-3x}{5} \le 2(x+2)$

Multiply both sides by 5 (positive, so inequality sign stays):

\[ 7 - 3x \le 10(x+2) \]

Expand the right side:

\[ 7 - 3x \le 10x + 20 \]

Bring terms with $x$ to one side and numbers to the other:

\[ -13 \le 13x \]

Divide by 13 (positive):

\[ x \ge -1 \]
Hint: When multiplying or dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign. Here we only multiplied/divided by positive numbers (5 and 13), so the sign remains.
✅ First inequality gives: $x \ge -1$
2 Solve the second inequality: $4x - 13 > 0$

Add $13$ to both sides:

\[ 4x > 13 \]

Divide by 4 (positive):

\[ x > \frac{13}{4} \]
Note: \(\frac{13}{4} = 3.25\). So $x$ must be strictly greater than 3.25.
✅ Second inequality gives: $x > \frac{13}{4}$
3 Combine both inequalities

We need values of $x$ that satisfy both conditions:

\[ x \ge -1 \quad \text{and} \quad x > \frac{13}{4} \]

On the number line, the intersection is the set of $x$ greater than $\frac{13}{4}$.

Since $\frac{13}{4} = 3.25$ is already greater than $-1$, the stricter condition dominates.

\[ \boxed{x > \frac{13}{4}} \]
Thinking tip: The combined range is the overlap of the two ranges. Here it's simply \(x > \frac{13}{4}\) because \(\frac{13}{4} > -1\).
✅ (a) Required range: $\displaystyle x > \frac{13}{4}$
4 Find the least integer satisfying both (Part b)

We need the smallest integer $x$ such that $x > \frac{13}{4} = 3.25$.

Integers greater than 3.25 are: $4, 5, 6, \dots$

Therefore the least integer is 4.

✅ (b) Least integer = 4
Check: $x = 4$ gives $4 > 3.25$ ✓. $x = 3$ gives $3 > 3.25$ ✗. So the answer is 4.
