If $\dfrac{2}{u}+\dfrac{3}{v}=4$, then $u=$

A. $\dfrac{2v}{4v-3}$
B. $\dfrac{2v}{3-4v}$
C. $\dfrac{3v}{4v-2}$
D. $\dfrac{3v}{2-4v}$
1Start from the given equation

$$\frac{2}{u}+\frac{3}{v}=4$$

Step 1: the only information given — one equation linking $u$ and $v$.

2Move the $\frac{3}{v}$ term to the right

$$\frac{2}{u}=4-\frac{3}{v}$$

Step 2: isolate the term containing $u$ on the left-hand side.

3Combine the right side into a single fraction

$$4-\frac{3}{v}=\frac{4v}{v}-\frac{3}{v}=\frac{4v-3}{v}$$

Step 3: use $4=\frac{4v}{v}$ so both terms share denominator $v$.

Common Denominator \(\dfrac{a}{b}-\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ad-bc}{bd}\) Writing \(4=\dfrac{4v}{v}\) puts both terms over \(v\), giving \(\dfrac{4v-3}{v}\).
4Take reciprocals of both sides

$$\frac{2}{u}=\frac{4v-3}{v}\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{u}{2}=\frac{v}{4v-3}$$

Step 4: reciprocal of $\frac{2}{u}$ is $\frac{u}{2}$; reciprocal of $\frac{4v-3}{v}$ is $\frac{v}{4v-3}$.

Reciprocal of a Fraction \(\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{b}{a}=\dfrac{d}{c}\) Taking reciprocals of \(\dfrac{2}{u}=\dfrac{4v-3}{v}\) gives \(\dfrac{u}{2}=\dfrac{v}{4v-3}\).
5Multiply by $2$ to get $u$

$$u=2\times\frac{v}{4v-3}=\frac{2v}{4v-3}$$

Step 5: multiply both sides by $2$ — this matches option A.

Answer: $\;u=\dfrac{2v}{4v-3}\;$  —  Option A

Question. If \(\dfrac{3}{x}+\dfrac{2}{y}=5\), express \(x\) in terms of \(y\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\dfrac{3}{x}=5-\dfrac{2}{y}\).
Step 2. Combine over \(y\): \(5-\dfrac{2}{y}=\dfrac{5y-2}{y}\).
Step 3. Take reciprocals: \(\dfrac{x}{3}=\dfrac{y}{5y-2}\).
Step 4. Multiply by \(3\): \(x=\dfrac{3y}{5y-2}\).
Answer: \(x=\dfrac{3y}{5y-2}\)
