QUESTION 19 · 12 marks

The coordinates of the centre of the circle $C$ are $(8,2)$. Denote the radius of $C$ by $r$. Let $L$ be the straight line $kx-5y-21=0$, where $k$ is a constant.

It is given that $L$ is a tangent to $C$.

(a) Find the equation of $C$ in terms of $r$. Hence, express $r^{2}$ in terms of $k$.  [4 marks]

(b) $L$ passes through the point $D\,(18,39)$.

(i) Find $r$.

(ii) It is given that $L$ cuts the $y$-axis at the point $E$. Let $F$ be a point such that $C$ is the inscribed circle of $\Delta DEF$. Is $\Delta DEF$ an obtuse-angled triangle? Explain your answer.  [8 marks]

Step 1 · Equation of C and distance from centre to line L Circle with centre $G(8,2)$ and radius $r$: $$C:\ (x-8)^2+(y-2)^2=r^2 .$$ Since $L:\ kx-5y-21=0$ is a tangent to $C$, the perpendicular distance from $G$ to $L$ equals $r$: $$\frac{|k(8)-5(2)-21|}{\sqrt{k^2+(-5)^2}}=r .$$ $$|8k-31|=\sqrt{k^2+25}\;r .$$
Step 2 · Square and simplify to get $r^2$ in terms of $k$ Squaring both sides: $$(8k-31)^2=(k^2+25)r^2 .$$ $$r^2=\frac{(8k-31)^2}{k^2+25}=\frac{64k^2-496k+961}{k^2+25} .$$ (Equivalently, substituting $y=\dfrac{kx-21}{5}$ into the circle and setting the discriminant to $0$ gives the same result.)
Step 3 · (b)(i) Use $D(18,39)$ lying on $L$ to find $k$ $$k(18)-5(39)-21=0 \Rightarrow 18k-195-21=0 \Rightarrow 18k=216 \Rightarrow k=12 .$$ So $$L:\ 12x-5y-21=0 .$$
Step 4 · (b)(i) Compute $r$ $$r^2=\frac{64(12)^2-496(12)+961}{12^2+25} =\frac{9216-5952+961}{169}=\frac{4225}{169}=25 .$$ $$r=5 .$$ The tangent point of $L$ on $C$ is $\left(\dfrac{96}{13},\dfrac{171}{13}\right)$, and $GD\perp L$.
Step 5 · (b)(ii) The three vertices $-E$, $D$ and centre $G$ $L$ meets the $y$-axis ($x=0$) at $$-5y-21=0 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{21}{5}=-4.2,\qquad E=\left(0,-\tfrac{21}{5}\right).$$ Centre $G=(8,2)$. Then $$DG^2=(18-8)^2+(39-2)^2=100+1369=1469,\quad DG=\sqrt{1469}\approx 38.33 .$$ $$EG^2=(8-0)^2+\left(2+\tfrac{21}{5}\right)^2=64+\left(\tfrac{31}{5}\right)^2=\frac{1600+961}{25}=\frac{2561}{25},$$ $$EG=\frac{\sqrt{2561}}{5}\approx 10.12 .$$
Step 6 · (b)(ii) Half-angles of $\Delta DEF$ at $D$ and $E$ Since $C$ is the inscribed circle, $G$ is the incentre, so $GD$ and $GE$ bisect $\angle FDE$ and $\angle DEF$. $$\sin\angle EDG=\frac{r}{DG}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{1469}}\ \Rightarrow\ \angle EDG\approx 7.4959^\circ,$$ $$\sin\angle DEG=\frac{r}{EG}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{2561}/5}=\frac{25}{\sqrt{2561}}\ \Rightarrow\ \angle DEG\approx 29.6045^\circ .$$
Step 7 · (b)(ii) Decide whether the triangle is obtuse-angled By symmetry of tangents, $\angle EDG=\angle FDG$ and $\angle DEG=\angle FEG$. Hence $$\angle DFE=180^\circ-(\angle EDG+\angle FDG)-(\angle DEG+\angle FEG)$$ $$\approx 180^\circ-2(7.4958764^\circ)-2(29.60445074^\circ)\approx 105.7994^\circ>90^\circ .$$ Therefore $\Delta DEF$ is an obtuse-angled triangle.
Answers:
(a) $C:(x-8)^2+(y-2)^2=r^2$ and $r^2=\dfrac{64k^2-496k+961}{k^2+25}$.
(b)(i) $k=12$, $\;r=5$.
(b)(ii) $E=\left(0,-\tfrac{21}{5}\right)$; $\angle DFE\approx 105.8^\circ>90^\circ$, so $\Delta DEF$ is obtuse-angled.
Exam tips: The key idea in (a) is distance from centre to tangent = radius (equivalently, discriminant $=0$). In (b)(ii), the centre of an inscribed circle is the incentre, so the lines joining it to the vertices are angle bisectors — this gives the half-angles directly from $\sin = \dfrac{r}{\text{hypotenuse}}$.
