Coordinate Geometry — Tangency, Radius and the Incentre of a Triangle

Circle Equation Tangent Condition Discriminant = 0 Incentre Angle Sum of Triangle

Question Full Statement

The coordinates of the centre of the circle \(C\) are \((8,2)\). Denote the radius of \(C\) by \(r\). Let \(L\) be the straight line \(kx-5y-21=0\), where \(k\) is a constant.

It is given that \(L\) is a tangent to \(C\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\) in terms of \(r\). Hence, express \(r^{2}\) in terms of \(k\). [4 marks]

(b) \(L\) passes through the point \(D\,(18,39)\).

(i) Find \(r\). [3 marks]

(ii) It is given that \(L\) cuts the \(y\)-axis at the point \(E\). Let \(F\) be a point such that \(C\) is the inscribed circle of \(\Delta DEF\). Is \(\Delta DEF\) an obtuse-angled triangle? Explain your answer. [5 marks]

Hint: A circle with centre \((h,k)\) and radius \(r\) has equation \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\). Here \((h,k)=(8,2)\).
Equation of \(C\): \((x-8)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=r^{2}\)
Step 21M
From \(L:\ kx-5y-21=0\), make \(y\) the subject: \[ y=\frac{kx-21}{5}. \] Substitute into the circle: \[ (x-8)^{2}+\left(\frac{kx-21}{5}-2\right)^{2}=r^{2}. \] Multiplying by \(25\) and expanding gives the quadratic in \(x\): \[ (k^{2}+25)x^{2}+(-62k-400)x+2561-25r^{2}=0. \]
Hint: Tangency means the line meets the circle at exactly one point, so this quadratic has a repeated root.
\((k^{2}+25)x^{2}+(-62k-400)x+2561-25r^{2}=0\)
Step 31M
Set the discriminant to zero: \[ \Delta=(-62k-400)^{2}-4(k^{2}+25)(2561-25r^{2})=0. \] Divide both sides by \(100\): \[ (31k+200)^{2}-(k^{2}+25)(2561-25r^{2})=0. \]
Hint: Since \(L\) is a tangent, the line touches \(C\) at exactly one point \(\Rightarrow\) \(\Delta=0\).
\((31k+200)^{2}-(k^{2}+25)(2561-25r^{2})=0\)
Step 41A
Expanding and simplifying: \[ (k^{2}+25)(2561-25r^{2})=(31k+200)^{2} \] \[ 2561k^{2}+64025-25r^{2}(k^{2}+25)=961k^{2}+12400k+40000 \] \[ 1600k^{2}-12400k+24025=25r^{2}(k^{2}+25) \] \[ \boxed{\,r^{2}=\frac{64k^{2}-496k+961}{k^{2}+25}\,} \]
Hint: Check with \(k=12\): \(r^{2}=\dfrac{64(144)-496(12)+961}{169}=\dfrac{4321}{169}=25.568\ldots\), and indeed \(r^{2}\) must be positive.
\[ r^{2}=\frac{64k^{2}-496k+961}{k^{2}+25} \]

Step 11M
Since \(L\) passes through \(D(18,39)\), substitute into \(kx-5y-21=0\): \[ 18k-5(39)-21=0 \] \[ 18k-195-21=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 18k=216 \;\Rightarrow\; k=12. \]
Hint: "\(L\) passes through \(D\)" means \(D\) satisfies the equation of \(L\).
\(k=12\)
Step 21M
Substitute \(k=12\) into the formula from (a): \[ r^{2}=\frac{64(12)^{2}-496(12)+961}{12^{2}+25}. \]
Hint: \(12^2+25=144+25=169\), and \(64(144)-496(12)+961=9216-5952+961\).
\[ r^{2}=\frac{4225}{169} \]
Step 31A
Since \(4225=65^{2}\) and \(169=13^{2}\): \[ r^{2}=\left(\frac{65}{13}\right)^{2}=5^{2} \] \[ \boxed{\,r=5\,} \]
Hint: \(r>0\), so take the positive square root. So \(C:\ (x-8)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=25\).
\(r=5\)

Part (b)(ii) — Is \(\Delta DEF\) Obtuse-angled? 5 marks

0 / 6
Step 11M
\(L\) cuts the \(y\)-axis where \(x=0\): \[ 12(0)-5y-21=0 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac{21}{5}. \] So \(E=\left(0,\,-\dfrac{21}{5}\right)\).
Hint: The \(y\)-intercept is found by putting \(x=0\) in the equation of \(L\).
\(E=\left(0,\,-\dfrac{21}{5}\right)\)
Step 21M
\(G\), the centre of \(C\), is the incentre of \(\Delta DEF\). So the centre is \(G(8,2)\) and \[ DG^{2}=(18-8)^{2}+(39-2)^{2}=10^{2}+37^{2}=100+1369=1469. \]
Hint: In a triangle, the incentre is the centre of the inscribed circle — it is equidistant (\(=r\)) from all three sides.
\(DG=\sqrt{1469}\)
Step 31M
In the right triangle formed by the radius to a side, \(\sin\angle EDG=\dfrac{r}{DG}\): \[ \angle EDG=\operatorname{arcsin}\!\left(\frac{5}{\sqrt{1469}}\right)\approx 7.49585764^{\circ}. \] Similarly, \(EG^{2}=(8-0)^2+\left(2+\frac{21}{5}\right)^2=64+\left(\frac{31}{5}\right)^{2}=\frac{2561}{25}\), so \(EG=\dfrac{\sqrt{2561}}{5}\), and \[ \angle DEG=\operatorname{arcsin}\!\left(\frac{r}{EG}\right)=\operatorname{arcsin}\!\left(\frac{25}{\sqrt{2561}}\right)\approx 29.60445074^{\circ}. \]
Hint: These two angles are measured at the vertices \(D\) and \(E\), and both halves of the full triangle angles are equal: \(\angle EDG=\angle FDG\) and \(\angle DEG=\angle FEG\).
\(\angle EDG\approx7.50^{\circ}\), \(\angle DEG\approx29.60^{\circ}\)
Step 41M
Sum of the angles of \(\Delta DEF\): \[ \angle DFE = 180^{\circ}-(\angle EDG+\angle FDG)-(\angle DEG+\angle FEG). \]
Hint: The incentre lies on the angle bisectors, so each full vertex angle at \(D\) and \(E\) is twice the half-angle we computed.
\[\angle DFE=180^{\circ}-2\angle EDG-2\angle DEG\]
Step 51M
Substituting the values: \[ \angle DFE \approx 180^{\circ}-2(7.49585764^{\circ})-2(29.60445074^{\circ}) \] \[ \approx 180^{\circ}-14.99171528^{\circ}-59.20890148^{\circ}\approx 105.7993832^{\circ}. \]
Hint: Compare this angle with \(90^{\circ}\) to decide whether the triangle is obtuse-angled.
\(\angle DFE \approx 105.7993832^{\circ}\)
Step 61A
Since \(105.7993832^{\circ}>90^{\circ}\), the angle \(\angle DFE\) is obtuse: \[ \boxed{\ \Delta DEF \text{ is an obtuse-angled triangle.}\ } \]
Hint: A triangle containing an obtuse angle is called an obtuse-angled triangle.
\(\Delta DEF\) is an obtuse-angled triangle.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Equation of \(C\): \((x-8)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=r^{2}\)
  • (a) \(r^{2}\) in terms of \(k\): \(r^{2}=\dfrac{64k^{2}-496k+961}{k^{2}+25}\)
  • (b)(i) From \(D(18,39)\) on \(L\): \(k=12\), hence \(r=5\), i.e. \(C:\ (x-8)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=25\)
  • (b)(ii) \(E=\left(0,\,-\dfrac{21}{5}\right)\), \(G(8,2)\) is the incentre.
  • (b)(ii) \(\angle EDG\approx7.49585764^{\circ}\), \(\angle DEG\approx29.60445074^{\circ}\)
  • (b)(ii) \(\angle DFE=180^{\circ}-2(\angle EDG+\angle DEG)\approx105.7993832^{\circ}>90^{\circ}\)
  • (b)(ii) \(\Delta DEF\) is an obtuse-angled triangle.
