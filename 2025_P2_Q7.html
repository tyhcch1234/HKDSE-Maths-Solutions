Step 1 — the double inequality on a number line.

Condition 1: $y>4$ Condition 2: $y\ge 2$ Final: $y>4$
HKDSE 2025 · Paper 2 · Q7
The solution of $4y+1 < 5y-3 \le 8y-9$ is
A. $y>2$
B. $y\ge 2$
C. $y\ge 4$
D. $y>4$
1Read the double inequality

\[ 4y+1 \;<\; 5y-3 \;\le\; 8y-9 \]

This is a combined statement: it means the middle quantity $5y-3$ lies between $4y+1$ and $8y-9$. Equivalently it is an intersection of two inequalities:

\[ 4y+1 < 5y-3 \quad \textbf{and} \quad 5y-3 \le 8y-9 \]

Split the chain "\(a
Compound Inequality \(a The chain is split into \(3y+2<5y-8\) and \(5y-8\le 7y-16\).
2Solve the first inequality

\[ 4y+1 < 5y-3 \]

Bring all $y$-terms to one side:

\[ 1+3 < 5y-4y \]

\[ 4 < y \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{y>4} \]

Moving a term across the sign changes its sign. Dividing by a positive number (here $+1$) keeps the direction "<".
Solving a Linear Inequality \(ax+b>0\ \to\ x>-\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a>0)\) \(3y+2<5y-8\) gives \(10<2y\), so \(y>5\).
3Solve the second inequality

\[ 5y-3 \le 8y-9 \]

\[ -3+9 \le 8y-5y \]

\[ 6 \le 3y \]

\[ 2 \le y \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{y\ge 2} \]

Only flip the inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative number. Dividing $6\le 3y$ by $3>0$ keeps "\(\le\)".
4Intersect the two conditions

We need both to hold:

\[ y>4 \quad\text{and}\quad y\ge 2 \]

On the number line, the two solution sets overlap on the region that is inside both:

\[ \{y>4\}\cap\{y\ge 2\} \;=\; y>4 \]

"And" means intersection — keep only the stricter condition. Since $4>2$, every $y>4$ automatically satisfies $y\ge2$, but not vice-versa. Note $y=4$ is excluded (strict "\(>\)" in the first inequality), so the answer must NOT contain "\(\ge 4\)".
Intersection of Solution Sets \(\{y>a\}\cap\{y\ge b\}=\{y>\max(a,b)\}\) The two conditions \(y>5\) and \(y\ge4\) overlap exactly on \(y>5\).
5Final solution & check the options

\[ \boxed{\,y>4\,} \]

Option C ($y\ge4$) and Option B ($y\ge2$) wrongly include the value $y=4$. Test $y=4$ in the original:

\[ 4(4)+1=17,\quad 5(4)-3=17,\quad 8(4)-9=23 \]

\[ 17<17 \text{ is } \textbf{FALSE} \]

So $y=4$ is not a solution — hence a strict "\(>\)" is correct.

Always re-substitute the boundary value to decide between "\(>\)" and "\(\ge\)".
Answer: D  —  \(y>4\) Intersection of \(y>4\) and \(y\ge 2\) is \(y>4\).
