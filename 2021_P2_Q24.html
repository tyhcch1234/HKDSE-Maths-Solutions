In the figure, $\dfrac{AB}{CD}=$

A. $\cos\theta\sin\varphi$
B. $\sin\theta\cos\varphi$
C. $\tan\theta\cos\varphi$
D. $\tan\theta\sin\varphi$
STEP 1 · Identify the right angles & the hypotenuse AB

From the figure:

• In $\triangle ABC$, the right angle is at $C$, i.e. $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$.

• In $\triangle ACD$, the right angle is at $C$, i.e. $\angle ACD = 90^\circ$.

• Angle $\varphi$ is placed at $C$ between $CA$ and $CB$ (i.e. $\angle ACB$ in the small marked position — the angle between $AC$ and $BC$), and angle $\theta$ is at $D$ between $DA$ and $DC$.

So $AB$ is the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle $ABC$, with $\angle BAC$ related to the marked angle $\varphi$.

Hint: Always write down the hypotenuse first — the side opposite the $90^\circ$ angle.
STEP 2 · Express $AB$ in $\triangle ABC$ — equation (1)

In the right-angled triangle $ABC$ with the right angle at $C$:

$$\sin\varphi=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}=\frac{AB}{AC}$$

Rearranging:

$$AB = AC\sin\varphi \quad\cdots(1)$$

Sanity check: $\varphi$ is acute, so $\sin\varphi<1$ and $AB<AC$ — exactly what the figure shows.
Cosine Ratio \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In \(\triangle ABC\) with the right angle at \(C\), \(\cos\varphi=\dfrac{AC}{AB}\), so \(AB=\dfrac{AC}{\cos\varphi}\).
STEP 3 · Express $CD$ in $\triangle ACD$ — equation (2)

In the right-angled triangle $ACD$ with the right angle at $C$, the angle $\theta$ is at $D$:

$$\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}=\frac{AC}{CD}$$

Rearranging for $CD$:

$$CD=\frac{AC}{\tan\theta}\quad\cdots(2)$$

Alternative form: $CD = AC\cot\theta$. Both are equivalent.
Tangent Ratio \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\) In \(\triangle ACD\), \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{AC}{CD}\), so \(CD=\dfrac{AC}{\tan\theta}\).
STEP 4 · Divide (1) by (2) to eliminate $AC$

$(1)\div(2)$:

$$\frac{AB}{CD}=\frac{AC\sin\varphi}{\dfrac{AC}{\tan\theta}}$$

The common length $AC$ cancels:

$$\frac{AB}{CD}=\tan\theta\,\sin\varphi$$

Key idea: the unknown length $AC$ is a bridge — it appears in both triangles, so dividing removes it.
Eliminating a Common Length \(\dfrac{AB}{CD}=\dfrac{AC/\cos\varphi}{AC/\tan\theta}=\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\cos\varphi}\) The length \(AC\) appears in both expressions, so dividing cancels it.
STEP 5 · Conclusion

Therefore

$$\boxed{\frac{AB}{CD}=\tan\theta\sin\varphi}$$

which matches option D.

Final Answer: D  —  $\dfrac{AB}{CD}=\tan\theta\sin\varphi$

Question. In the figure, \(\angle ACB=\angle ACD=90^{\circ}\), \(\angle BAC=\varphi\) and \(\angle ADC=\theta\). Express \(\dfrac{AB}{CD}\) in terms of \(\varphi\) and \(\theta\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. In \(\triangle ABC\): \(\cos\varphi=\dfrac{AC}{AB}\), so \(AB=\dfrac{AC}{\cos\varphi}\).
Step 2. In \(\triangle ACD\): \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{AC}{CD}\), so \(CD=\dfrac{AC}{\tan\theta}\).
Step 3. Dividing: \(\dfrac{AB}{CD}=\dfrac{AC/\cos\varphi}{AC/\tan\theta}=\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\cos\varphi}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{AB}{CD}=\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\cos\varphi}\)
