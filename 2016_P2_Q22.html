Question

In the figure, $ABCD$ is a rhombus. $C$ is the centre of the circle $BDE$ and $ADE$ is a straight line. $BE$ and $CD$ intersect at $F$. If $\angle ADC = 118^\circ$, then $\angle DFE =$

A59°
B62°
C87°
D78°
Quick idea: In a rhombus the opposite sides are parallel, so use co-interior angles along $AD$ and $BC$. Then use “angle at centre = twice angle at circumference” for the circle with centre $C$. Finally use the exterior angle of $\triangle DEF$.
STEP 1 · Co-interior angles (BC // AD)
Interior Angles on the Same Side (BC // AD) \(\angle BCD+\angle ADC=180^{\circ}\) Opposite sides of a rhombus are parallel, so \(\angle BCD=180^{\circ}-118^{\circ}=62^{\circ}\).

Since $ABCD$ is a rhombus, $BC // AD$ and $AD$ (extended to $E$) is a straight line.
So $\angle BCD$ and $\angle ADC$ are interior angles on the same side:

$$\angle BCD = 180^\circ - \angle ADC = 180^\circ - 118^\circ = 62^\circ$$

Reason: interior angles, $BC // AD$.

STEP 2 · Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference
Angle at the Centre is Twice the Angle at the Circumference \(\angle BCD=2\angle BED\) Both angles stand on the arc \(BD\), with \(C\) the centre and \(E\) on the circle.

$C$ is the centre of the circle through $B$, $D$, $E$. Both $CB$ and $CD$ are radii,
so $\angle BCD$ is the angle at the centre subtending the arc $BD$,
and $\angle BED$ is the angle at the circumference on the same arc:

$$\angle BED = \frac{\angle BCD}{2} = \frac{62^\circ}{2} = 31^\circ$$

Reason: $\angle$ at centre $= 2 \times \angle$ at circumference.

STEP 3 · Exterior angle of △DEF
Exterior Angle of a Triangle \(\angle ADF=\angle DEF+\angle DFE\) In \(\triangle DEF\) the exterior angle at \(D\) is \(118^{\circ}\) and one interior opposite angle is \(31^{\circ}\).

In $\triangle DEF$, the side $DE$ lies on the straight line $ADE$,
so $\angle ADF = \angle ADC = 118^\circ$ is the exterior angle at $D$ and $\angle DEF = \angle BED = 31^\circ$ is an interior opposite angle.
Hence

$$\angle DFE = \angle ADF - \angle DEF = 118^\circ - 31^\circ = 87^\circ$$

Reason: exterior angle of a triangle $=$ sum of the two interior opposite angles.

Answer: D. 87°

Question. In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rhombus. \(C\) is the centre of the circle \(BDE\) and \(ADE\) is a straight line. \(BE\) and \(CD\) intersect at \(F\). If \(\angle ADC=124^{\circ}\), then \(\angle DFE=\) A. \(84^{\circ}\) B. \(90^{\circ}\) C. \(96^{\circ}\) D. \(104^{\circ}\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(BC // AD\), so the interior angles on the same side are supplementary: \(\angle BCD=180^{\circ}-124^{\circ}=56^{\circ}\).
Step 2. \(C\) is the centre and \(E\) lies on the circle, so the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on arc \(BD\): \(\angle BED=\dfrac{56^{\circ}}{2}=28^{\circ}\).
Step 3. In \(\triangle DEF\), the exterior angle at \(D\) is \(\angle ADF=124^{\circ}\) and \(\angle DEF=\angle BED=28^{\circ}\).
Step 4. \(\angle DFE=124^{\circ}-28^{\circ}=96^{\circ}\).
Answer: C. \(96^{\circ}\)
