Question 14 9 marks

The full question is reproduced below. Read carefully, then explore each part with the interactive diagrams.

The \(y\)-intercepts of two parallel lines \(L\) and \(\ell\) are \(-1\) and \(-3\) respectively and the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\) is \(3\). \(P\) is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(\ell\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).

(a) (i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\).
(a) (ii) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). [5 marks]

(b) The equation of the circle \(C\) is \((x-6)^2 + y^2 = 4\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(Q\).
(b) (i) Does \(\Gamma\) pass through \(Q\)? Explain your answer.
(b) (ii) If \(L\) cuts \(C\) at \(A\) and \(B\) while \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at \(H\) and \(K\), find the ratio of the area of \(\Delta AQH\) to the area of \(\Delta BQK\). [4 marks]

Part (a)(i) — Geometric Relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\) 1A

\(P\) is always equidistant from the two parallel lines \(L\) and \(\ell\). Drag the slider to move \(P\) along its locus and watch the two perpendicular distances.

Distance to \(L\) = 0.00 Distance to \(\ell\) = 0.00 Equal ✓
Step 11A

\(P\) is equidistant from \(L\) and \(\ell\), and \(L \parallel \ell\).

The locus of points equidistant from two parallel lines is the line midway between them, i.e. the mid-parallel line.

Hint: The two perpendicular distances are equal for every point on that middle line — try dragging \(P\) in the diagram.
\(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\). (and also parallel to \(\ell\), lying halfway between them)

Part (a)(ii) — Equation of \(\Gamma\) 4 marks

Use the given intercepts to find the equations of \(L\), then \(\ell\), then the mid-line \(\Gamma\).

Slope of \(L\) = 1/3 \(\Gamma:\; x - 3y - 6 = 0\)
Step 11M

\(L\) has \(y\)-intercept \(-1\), so it passes through \((0,-1)\). It has \(x\)-intercept \(3\), so it passes through \((3,0)\).

Slope of \(L\):

\[ m_L = \frac{0-(-1)}{3-0} = \frac{1}{3} \]
Hint: Slope \(=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\). Moving right 3 and up 1 gives a gentle rise.
Step 21A

The equation of \(L\) through \((0,-1)\) with slope \(\tfrac13\):

\[ y - (-1) = \tfrac13 (x-0) \;\Rightarrow\; y = \tfrac13 x - 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 3y - 3 = 0 \]
Hint: Multiply by 3: \(3y = x - 3\), rearrange to the general form.
Step 31A

\(\ell \parallel L\), so \(\ell\) also has slope \(\tfrac13\). Its \(y\)-intercept is \(-3\):

\[ \ell:\; y = \tfrac13 x - 3 \;\Rightarrow\; x - 3y - 9 = 0 \]
Hint: Parallel lines share the same slope but have different intercepts.
Step 41A

\(\Gamma\) lies halfway between the two parallel lines. Averaging the constants of the two general-form equations:

\[ \frac{(x-3y-3)+(x-3y-9)}{2} = 0 \] \[ x - 3y - 6 = 0 \]

Equivalently, the \(y\)-intercept of \(\Gamma\) is the average \(\dfrac{-1+(-3)}{2} = -2\), giving \(y = \tfrac13 x - 2\).

Hint: The perpendicular distances from a point on \(\Gamma\) to \(L\) and to \(\ell\) are both \(\dfrac{|-3-(-9)|}{2\sqrt{1^2+(-3)^2}}\) = same value.
\(\Gamma:\; x - 3y - 6 = 0\)

Part (b)(i) — Does \(\Gamma\) pass through \(Q\)? 2 marks

The circle \(C:(x-6)^2+y^2=4\) has centre \(Q(6,0)\) and radius \(2\). Test whether \(Q\) lies on \(\Gamma\).

\(Q\) = (6, 0) LHS of \(\Gamma\): 6 − 3(0) − 6 = 0 On \(\Gamma\) ✓
Step 11A

From \(C:(x-6)^2+y^2=4\), the centre is

\[ Q = (6,\;0) \]
Hint: A circle \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2\) has centre \((a,b)\) and radius \(r\). Here \(r=2\).
Step 21A

Substitute \(x=6,\;y=0\) into \(\Gamma: x-3y-6=0\):

\[ 6 - 3(0) - 6 = 0 \]

Since the left-hand side equals 0, \(Q\) satisfies the equation of \(\Gamma\).

Hint: A point lies on a line if its coordinates satisfy the line's equation exactly.
Yes, \(\Gamma\) passes through \(Q\).

Part (b)(ii) — Ratio of Areas of \(\Delta AQH\) and \(\Delta BQK\) 4 marks

\(L\) cuts \(C\) at \(A,B\); \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at \(H,K\). Compare the areas of the two triangles sharing vertex \(Q\).

Height to \(L\) = 0.949 Height to \(\Gamma\) = 0.949 Area ratio = 1 : 1
Step 11M

Both \(QH\) and \(QK\) are radii of circle \(C\), so

\[ QH = QK = 2 \]
Hint: Any segment from the centre to a point on the circle is a radius.
Step 21M

\(Q\) lies on \(\Gamma\) (from part (b)(i)), and \(Q\) also gives the perpendicular distance from \(Q\) to \(\Gamma\) as \(0\). Wait — carefully: the base \(QH\) lies on \(\Gamma\), so the height of \(\Delta AQH\) from \(A\) to \(QH\) is the distance from \(A\) to \(\Gamma\). Similarly the base \(QK\) lies on \(L\), so the height of \(\Delta BQK\) from \(B\) to \(QK\) is the distance from \(B\) to \(L\).

But \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) are parallel and the distances from \(L\) to \(\Gamma\) is half of the distance between \(L\) and \(\ell\).

Hint: Both triangles share the same vertex \(Q\), and their bases lie on the parallel lines \(L\) and \(\Gamma\).
Step 31A

Using area \(=\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\):

\[ [\Delta AQH] = \tfrac12 \cdot QH \cdot d(A,\Gamma) \] \[ [\Delta BQK] = \tfrac12 \cdot QK \cdot d(B,L) \]

Since \(L\parallel\Gamma\), the perpendicular distance between \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) is constant. The points \(A\) and \(B\) are on \(L\), and \(Q,H,K\) lie on the circle; the perpendicular distances from \(Q\) to \(L\) and from \(Q\) to \(\Gamma\) are equal (because \(Q\) lies on \(\Gamma\), so \(d(Q,\Gamma)=0\), while \(d(Q,L)=\ldots\)).

More directly: the perpendicular distance from \(Q\) to \(L\) equals \(\dfrac{|6-3(0)-3|}{\sqrt{1^2+(-3)^2}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}}\). The perpendicular distance from \(Q\) to \(\Gamma\) is \(0\), but \(H,K\) lie on \(\Gamma\).

Hint: The two triangles have equal bases (radii) and equal heights (distance between the parallel lines), so the areas are equal.
Step 41A

Therefore,

\[ \frac{[\Delta AQH]}{[\Delta BQK]} = 1:1 \]
Hint: Equal bases and equal heights ⇒ equal areas. The required ratio is \(1:1\).
The required ratio is \(1:1\).
0 / 4

Summary of Answers

  • (a)(i) \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\) (it is the line midway between \(L\) and \(\ell\)).
  • (a)(ii) \(\Gamma:\; x - 3y - 6 = 0\).
  • (b)(i) Yes. Substituting \((6,0)\) gives \(6 - 3(0) - 6 = 0\), so \(\Gamma\) passes through \(Q\).
  • (b)(ii) The ratio of the area of \(\Delta AQH\) to the area of \(\Delta BQK\) is \(1:1\).
