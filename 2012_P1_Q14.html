Question

The y-intercepts of two parallel lines $L$ and $\ell$ are $-1$ and $-3$ respectively and the x-intercept of $L$ is $3$. $P$ is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $L$ is equal to the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $\ell$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$.

(a) (i) Describe the geometric relationship between $\Gamma$ and $L$.
    (ii) Find the equation of $\Gamma$.  [5 marks]

(b) The equation of the circle $C$ is $(x-6)^2+y^2=4$. Denote the centre of $C$ by $Q$.
    (i) Does $\Gamma$ pass through $Q$? Explain your answer.
    (ii) If $L$ cuts $C$ at $A$ and $B$ while $\Gamma$ cuts $C$ at $H$ and $K$, find the ratio of the area of $\Delta AQH$ to the area of $\Delta BQK$.  [4 marks]

Solution

Step 1 · (a)(i) Geometric relationship

A point $P$ is equidistant from $L$ and $\ell$, so $P$ lies on one of the two angle bisectors of the pair of parallel lines.

Since $L$ and $\ell$ are parallel, the locus $\Gamma$ (the mid-line) is also a straight line parallel to $L$ (and to $\ell$), lying exactly halfway between them.

Locus Equidistant from Two Parallel Lines \(d(P,L)=d(P,\ell)\;\Longrightarrow\;P\ \text{lies on the line midway between }L\text{ and }\ell\) The locus is a straight line parallel to both given lines, exactly halfway between them.
$\Gamma$ is a straight line parallel to $L$ (and $\ell$), midway between the two lines.
Step 2 · (a)(ii) Equation of L

$L$ has y-intercept $-1$ and x-intercept $3$, so it passes through $(0,-1)$ and $(3,0)$.

$$\text{slope of }L=\frac{0-(-1)}{3-0}=\frac{1}{3}$$ $$L:\; y-(-1)=\tfrac{1}{3}(x-0)\;\Rightarrow\; y=\tfrac{1}{3}x-1 \;\Rightarrow\; x-3y-3=0$$

Check: $(0,-1)$: $0+3-3=0$ ✓   $(3,0)$: $3-0-3=0$ ✓

Slope and Point–Slope Form \(m=\dfrac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}},\qquad y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\) Using the intercepts \((0,-1)\) and \((3,0)\) gives \(m=\dfrac13\), then substitute one point.
Step 3 · (a)(ii) Equation of Γ

$\Gamma$ passes through the midpoint of the two y-intercepts: $\dfrac{-1+(-3)}{2}=-2$, so $\Gamma$ has y-intercept $-2$ and is parallel to $L$ (slope $\tfrac13$).

$$\Gamma:\; y+2=\tfrac{1}{3}(x-0)$$ $$x-3y-6=0$$
Alternative: since $\Gamma$ is midway between $L:\;x-3y-3=0$ and $\ell:\;x-3y-9=0$, at a point of $\Gamma$ the two values $x-3y-3$ and $x-3y-9$ are opposites, so $x-3y-3=-(x-3y-9)$, which gives $x-3y-6=0$. ✓
$\Gamma:\; x-3y-6=0$
Step 4 · (b)(i) Does Γ pass through Q(6,0)?

Centre of $C:(x-6)^2+y^2=4$ is $Q(6,0)$, radius $=2$.

Substitute $Q$ into the equation of $\Gamma$:

$$6-3(0)-6=0 \;\checkmark$$
Yes — $\Gamma$ passes through $Q(6,0)$.
Step 5 · (b)(ii) Ratio of areas

$L$ cuts $C$ at $A$ and $B$; $\Gamma$ cuts $C$ at $H$ and $K$. Since $\Gamma // L$ and both are equidistant from the centre $Q$ (chords $AB$ and $HK$), $QH$ and $QK$ are radii of $C$: $QH=QK=2$.

The perpendicular distances from $Q$ to $L$ and to $\Gamma$ are equal. So the heights of $\Delta AQH$ and $\Delta BQK$ are equal, and the two triangles have equal area.

Perpendicular Distance from a Point to a Line \(d=\dfrac{Ax_{0}+By_{0}+C}{\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}}\) The numerator is taken as positive: if \(Ax_{0}+By_{0}+C\) is negative, use its opposite, because a distance is never negative. For parallel chords, equal distances from the centre give chords of equal length \u2014 the basis of the symmetry used here.
Area ratio $\Delta AQH : \Delta BQK = 1 : 1$
Step 6 · Summary

$\Gamma:\; x-3y-6=0$ is the mid-line, parallel to $L:\;x-3y-3=0$.

$Q(6,0)$ lies on $\Gamma$, and the two chords cut by $L$ and $\Gamma$ on the circle are symmetric about $Q$, giving equal areas.

(a)(i) parallel  |  (a)(ii) $x-3y-6=0$  |  (b)(i) yes  |  (b)(ii) $1:1$

Question. \(L\) is the line passing through \((0,-2)\) and \((4,0)\), and \(\ell\) is the line parallel to \(L\) with \(y\)-intercept \(-6\). \(P\) is a variable point equidistant from \(L\) and \(\ell\), and \(\Gamma\) is the locus of \(P\). Let \(C\) be the circle \((x-8)^{2}+y^{2}=4\) with centre \(Q\). (a)(i) Describe \(\Gamma\). (ii) Find the equations of \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) in the form \(Ax+By+C=0\). (b)(i) Show that \(Q\) lies on \(\Gamma\). (ii) Find the length of the chord that \(L\) cuts off on \(C\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\Gamma\) is a straight line parallel to \(L\) (and \(\ell\)), midway between them.
Step 2. Slope of \(L\): \(\dfrac{0-(-2)}{4-0}=\dfrac12\); through \((0,-2)\): \(y=\dfrac12x-2\), i.e. \(x-2y-4=0\).
Step 3. Midway between the \(y\)-intercepts \(-2\) and \(-6\) is \(-4\), so \(\Gamma\): \(y=\dfrac12x-4\), i.e. \(x-2y-8=0\).
Step 4. (b)(i) Put \(Q(8,0)\): \(8-2(0)-8=0\) ✓, so \(Q\) lies on \(\Gamma\).
Step 5. (ii) Distance from \(Q\) to \(L\): the numerator \(8-0-4=4>0\), so \(d=\dfrac{8-0-4}{\sqrt{1^{2}+(-2)^{2}}}=\dfrac{4}{\sqrt5}\).
Step 6. Half-chord \(=\sqrt{r^{2}-d^{2}}=\sqrt{4-\dfrac{16}{5}}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\), so the chord is \(\dfrac{4}{\sqrt5}\approx1.79\) units.
Answer: (a)(i) a line parallel to \(L\), midway between \(L\) and \(\ell\); (ii) \(L:x-2y-4=0\), \(\Gamma:x-2y-8=0\). (b)(ii) \(\dfrac{4}{\sqrt5}\approx1.79\).
