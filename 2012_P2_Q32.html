2012 MC #32  ·  Paper 2
The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between $x$ and $\log_3 y$. If $y = mn^{x}$, then $n =$
  • A. $\dfrac{1}{81}$
  • B. $\dfrac{1}{9}$
  • C. $9$
  • D. $81$

Step-by-step solution

1

Read the two points off the graph

The straight line cuts the $x$-axis at $(-2,\,0)$ and the $\log_3 y$-axis at $(0,\,4)$.
These two intercepts are all we need — no other data is given.
2

Take $\log_3$ of both sides of $y = mn^{x}$

\[ \begin{aligned} y &= mn^{x}\\[2pt] \log_3 y &= \log_3 (mn^{x})\\ &= \log_3 m + \log_3\!\left(n^{x}\right)\\ &= \log_3 m + x\,\log_3 n \end{aligned} \]
Use $\log(AB)=\log A+\log B$ and $\log A^{k}=k\log A$.
Power Law of Logarithms \(\log_{b}M^{k}=k\log_{b}M\) Bringing down the exponent \(x\) makes \(x\log_{3}n\), so \(\log_{3}n\) becomes the slope of the line.
Product Law of Logarithms \(\log_{b}(MN)=\log_{b}M+\log_{b}N\) It splits \(\log_{3}(mn^{x})\) into the constant \(\log_{3}m\) and the term \(x\log_{3}n\).
3

Compare with the straight line $Y = mx + c$

Plotting $Y=\log_3 y$ against $x$ gives a line whose
slope $=\log_3 n$, and intercept on the $\log_3 y$-axis $=\log_3 m$.
Linearising a curve means the coefficient becomes the slope.
4

Compute the slope from the two intercepts

\[ \log_3 n=\frac{4-0}{0-(-2)}=\frac{4}{2}=2 \]
The slope of $(\log_3 y)$ versus $x$ equals $\log_3 n$.
Gradient of a Straight Line \(m=\dfrac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\) The graph passes through \((-2,\,0)\) and \((0,\,4)\), so \(\log_{3}n=\dfrac{4-0}{0-(-2)}=2\).
5

Convert the logarithm back to $n$

\[ \log_3 n = 2 \;\Longrightarrow\; n = 3^{2} = 9 \]
By definition, $\log_3 n = 2$ means $n = 3^{2}$.
Answer: C. $9$
