HKDSE 2015 Paper 1 — Question 16 · Probability

Red (5) Yellow (6) White (3)

[2015 #16]
A box contains 5 red bowls, 6 yellow bowls and 3 white bowls. If 4 bowls are randomly drawn from the box at the same time,
(a) find the probability that exactly 2 red bowls are drawn.  [2 marks]
(b) find the probability that at least 2 red bowls are drawn.  [2 marks]

Total number of outcomes

Setup Total bowls: $5+6+3=14$. Choosing 4 from 14: $\;C^{14}_4=\dfrac{14!}{4!\,10!}=1001$.
Combination \({}^{n}C_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\) The bowls are drawn at the same time, so order does not matter and the total is \({}^{14}C_{4}=1001\).
All $1001$ outcomes are equally likely. Hint: “drawn at the same time” ⇒ order does not matter ⇒ use combinations $C^n_r$.

Part (a) — exactly 2 red

Step 1 Choose the red bowls: 2 red from 5  
→  $C^{5}_{2}=10$. Hint: first fix the red part of the selection.
Step 2 The other 2 bowls must be non-red
Multiplication Principle of Counting \(N={}^{5}C_{2}\times{}^{9}C_{2}=10\times36=360\) Choosing the 2 red bowls and the 2 non-red bowls are independent choices, so the counts are multiplied.
: there are $6+3=9$ non-red bowls, so $C^{9}_{2}=36$. Hint: total bowls − red bowls = non-red bowls.
Step 3 Number of favourable outcomes $=C^{5}_{2}C^{9}_{2}=10\times36=360$.
Required probability $=\dfrac{C^{5}_{2}C^{9}_{2}}{C^{14}_{4}}=\dfrac{360}{1001}$. Hint: probability $=\dfrac{\text{favourable}}{\text{total}}$.

Part (b) — at least 2 red

Step 4 “At least 2 red” means $2,3$ or $4$ red bowls — split into cases. Hint: write out the cases explicitly to avoid missing one.
Step 5 Case 3 red + 1 non-red: $C^{5}_{3}C^{9}_{1}=10\times9=90$.
Mutually Exclusive Events \(P(A\cup B\cup C)=P(A)+P(B)+P(C)\) The three cases (2, 3 and 4 red bowls) cannot occur together, so their probabilities are added.
Case 4 red: $C^{5}_{4}C^{9}_{0}=5\times1=5$. Hint: $C^{9}_{0}=1$ (choose none).
Step 6 Required probability $=\dfrac{360}{1001}+\dfrac{C^{5}_{3}C^{9}_{1}}{C^{14}_{4}}+\dfrac{C^{5}_{4}}{C^{14}_{4}} =\dfrac{360+90+5}{1001}=\dfrac{455}{1001}=\dfrac{5}{11}$. Hint: $\dfrac{455}{1001}$ reduces by $91$ to give $\dfrac{5}{11}$.
Answer:
(a) $\dfrac{C^{5}_{2}C^{9}_{2}}{C^{14}_{4}}=\dfrac{360}{1001}\approx 0.3596$
(b) $\dfrac{360}{1001}+\dfrac{C^{5}_{3}C^{9}_{1}}{C^{14}_{4}}+\dfrac{C^{5}_{4}}{C^{14}_{4}}=\dfrac{5}{11}\approx 0.4545$

Question. A box contains \(6\) red, \(5\) blue and \(3\) green balls. \(4\) balls are drawn at random from the box.
(a) Find the probability that exactly \(2\) of them are red.
(b) Find the probability that at least \(2\) of them are red.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. There are \(6+5+3=14\) balls, so the total number of outcomes is \({}^{14}C_{4}=1001\).
Step 2. (a) Exactly \(2\) red means \(2\) red from \(6\) and \(2\) non-red from the other \(8\): \({}^{6}C_{2}\times{}^{8}C_{2}=15\times28=420\). Hence \(P=\dfrac{420}{1001}=\dfrac{60}{143}\).
Step 3. (b) Split "at least \(2\) red" into the cases \(2,3,4\) red. \(3\) red and \(1\) non-red: \({}^{6}C_{3}\times{}^{8}C_{1}=20\times8=160\). \(4\) red: \({}^{6}C_{4}=15\).
Step 4. Add the cases: \(\dfrac{420+160+15}{1001}=\dfrac{595}{1001}=\dfrac{85}{143}\).
Step 5. Check: \(60+25=85\) over \(143\), so the two answers are consistent.
Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{420}{1001}=\dfrac{60}{143}\) (b) \(\dfrac{595}{1001}=\dfrac{85}{143}\)
