Probability Combinations Counting

Q The Question

A box contains 5 red bowls, 6 yellow bowls and 3 white bowls. If 4 bowls are randomly drawn from the box at the same time,

  1. find the probability that exactly 2 red bowls are drawn. [2 marks]
  2. find the probability that at least 2 red bowls are drawn. [2 marks]

The box · total 14 bowls

5 red 6 yellow 3 white

S Guided Solution

Since we choose 4 bowls from 14 at the same time, the order does not matter. We use combinations.

💡 Hint: The total number of ways is \( C_4^{14} = \dfrac{14!}{4!(14-4)!} \).

Compute:

$$ C_4^{14} = \dfrac{14 \times 13 \times 12 \times 11}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = 1001 $$

So there are 1001 equally likely outcomes in total.

We need 2 red bowls and the remaining 2 bowls must come from the non-red bowls (yellow + white).

💡 Hint (multiplication principle):
• Choose 2 red from 5 red: \( C_2^5 \)
• Choose 2 non-red from (6 + 3) = 9 non-red: \( C_2^9 \)

Number of favourable outcomes:

$$ C_2^5 \times C_2^9 = 10 \times 36 = 360 $$

Probability (a): \( \dfrac{360}{1001} \approx 0.3596 \)

"At least 2 red" means 2 red or 3 red or 4 red. These are mutually exclusive cases, so we add their probabilities.

💡 Hint – three cases:
• 2 red: \( C_2^5 \times C_2^9 \)
• 3 red: \( C_3^5 \times C_1^9 \)
• 4 red: \( C_4^5 \times C_0^9 \)  (note \( C_0^9 = 1 \))

Number of favourable outcomes:

$$ C_2^5 C_2^9 + C_3^5 C_1^9 + C_4^5 C_0^9 = 360 + 90 + 5 = 455 $$

Probability (b): \( \dfrac{455}{1001} = \dfrac{5}{11} \approx 0.4545 \)

The fraction simplifies: both numbers divide by 91 → \( \frac{455}{1001} = \frac{5}{11} \).

Alternatively, "at least 2 red" is the complement of "0 red or 1 red".

$$ 1 - \dfrac{C_0^5 C_4^9 + C_1^5 C_3^9}{C_4^{14}} = 1 - \dfrac{126 + 420}{1001} = 1 - \dfrac{546}{1001} = \dfrac{455}{1001} = \dfrac{5}{11} $$

Both methods give \( \frac{5}{11} \) — always a good sign! ✅

📝 Key Takeaways

  • 1. When drawing at the same time (order doesn't matter), use combinations \( C_r^n = \dfrac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \).
  • 2. Multiplication principle: "Choose red AND choose non-red" → multiply the combinations.
  • 3. Addition principle: "2 red OR 3 red OR 4 red" (mutually exclusive) → add the probabilities.
  • 4. Always check your answer with the complement: \( P(\text{≥2 red}) = 1 - P(\text{0 or 1 red}) \).
