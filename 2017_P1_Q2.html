Topic 8 — Laws of Indices, Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Step 1 — Simplify the bracket: multiply the outer power into each factor inside.

m‑powers n‑powers final result law applied
[2017 #2]  ·  3 marks

Simplify $\dfrac{(m^{4}n^{-1})^{3}}{(m^{-2})^{5}}$ and express your answer with positive indices.

1Remove the brackets (power of a product)

$(m^{4}n^{-1})^{3} = (m^{4})^{3}\,(n^{-1})^{3} = m^{12}n^{-3}$

$(m^{-2})^{5} = m^{(-2)\times 5} = m^{-10}$

Law: $(ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}$ and $(a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}$ — multiply the exponents.
2Form the single fraction and apply the quotient law

$\dfrac{m^{12}n^{-3}}{m^{-10}} = m^{12-(-10)}\,n^{-3} = m^{12+10}\,n^{-3}$

$\phantom{\dfrac{m^{12}n^{-3}}{m^{-10}}} = \dfrac{m^{22}}{n^{3}}$

Law: $\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{\,m-n}$ — subtract the indices of the same base.
3Write with positive indices

$n^{-3}=\dfrac{1}{n^{3}}$, so the negative index moves to the denominator.

$\dfrac{m^{12}n^{-3}}{m^{-10}} = \dfrac{m^{22}}{n^{3}}$

Law: $a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^{n}}$ — this gives the required positive-index form.
4Final answer

$m$ has index $12+10 = 22$ (positive), $n$ has index $-3$, written as $\dfrac{1}{n^{3}}$.

Therefore $\dfrac{(m^{4}n^{-1})^{3}}{(m^{-2})^{5}} = \dfrac{m^{22}}{n^{3}}$.

Check: all indices are positive ✓
Answer: $\dfrac{m^{22}}{n^{3}}$
