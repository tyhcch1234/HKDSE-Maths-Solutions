Q Question 12 marks

The centre of the circle \(C\) is the point \(G(83,\,112)\). It is found that the point \(A(158,\,12)\) lies outside \(C\). \(AP\) and \(AQ\) are the tangents to \(C\) at the points \(P\) and \(Q\) respectively. It is given that \(C\) passes through the point \((23,\,67)\).

(a) Find the equation of the straight line passing through \(A\) and \(G\). [2 marks]

(b) Find the coordinates of the point of intersection of \(AG\) and \(PQ\). [3 marks]

(c) Find the equation of the inscribed circle of \(\triangle APQ\). [4 marks]

(d) Someone claims that the ratio of the area of the inscribed circle to the area of the circumcircle of \(\triangle APQ\) is \(1:4\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

Step 11M

Find the slope of the straight line passing through \(A(158,\,12)\) and \(G(83,\,112)\):

\[ m_{AG}=\frac{112-12}{83-158}=\frac{100}{-75}=-\frac{4}{3} \]
Hint: "Slope through two points" — this is the method mark (M). Always subtract the \(y\)'s over the \(x\)'s in the same order.
Step 21A

Use the point-slope form with point \(A(158,\,12)\):

\[ y-12=-\frac{4}{3}(x-158) \] \[ 3(y-12)=-4(x-158) \] \[ 3y-36=-4x+632 \] \[ 4x+3y-668=0 \]
Answer (a): \(\;4x+3y-668=0\;\) (or \(\;y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x+\tfrac{668}{3}\))

b Intersection of \(AG\) and \(PQ\) 3 marks

Radius \(r\) = 75 Slope \(m_{PQ}\) = — Intersection \(N\) = —
0 / 3
Step 11M

Find the radius \(r\) of circle \(C\) using the point \((23,\,67)\) on \(C\) and centre \(G(83,\,112)\):

\[ r=\sqrt{(83-23)^2+(112-67)^2}=\sqrt{60^2+45^2}=\sqrt{3600+2025}=\sqrt{5625}=75 \]

Since \(\overline{GP}\perp AP\) and \(\overline{GQ}\perp AQ\), and \(AG=125=\sqrt{100^2+75^2}\) is perpendicular to \(PQ\), note \(AP\) is vertical and \(AQ\) is horizontal — so

\[ P=(158,\,112),\qquad Q=(83,\,12) \]
Hint: The tangent length satisfies \(AP^2=AG^2-r^2\). Here \(AG^2=100^2+75^2=15625\) and \(r^2=5625\), so \(AP=\sqrt{10000}=100\).
Step 21M

Since \(\triangle AGP\cong\triangle AGQ\) (RHS), the line \(AG\) is an axis of symmetry, so \(AG\perp PQ\). Hence:

\[ m_{PQ}=-\frac{1}{m_{AG}}=-\frac{1}{-\frac{4}{3}}=\frac{3}{4} \]

The equation of \(PQ\) (passing through \(P(158,\,112)\)):

\[ y-112=\frac{3}{4}(x-158) \] \[ 4y-448=3x-474 \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad 3x-4y-26=0 \]
Hint: A tangent-chord geometry fact: the line from an external point to the centre bisects (and is perpendicular to) the chord of contact.
Step 31A

Solve the system

\[ \begin{cases} 4x+3y-668=0\\ 3x-4y-26=0 \end{cases} \]

From the second, \(x=\dfrac{4y+26}{3}\). Substituting:

\[ 4\left(\frac{4y+26}{3}\right)+3y-668=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 16y+104+9y-2004=0 \] \[ 25y=1900 \;\Rightarrow\; y=76,\qquad x=\frac{4(76)+26}{3}=\frac{330}{3}=110 \]
Answer (b): The point of intersection is \(\;(110,\,76)\).

Step 11M

Because \(AP=AQ\) (equal tangents from \(A\)), the triangle \(APQ\) is isosceles with vertex \(A\). Its incentre \(I\) lies on the axis of symmetry \(AG\), at a distance \(r\) from each side. Write \(I=(x_I,\,y_I)\).

Since \(AI=AG-GI=AG-r\), and \(AG=125\):

\[ x_I = 158 - r\cos\theta,\qquad y_I = 12 + r\sin\theta \]

where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(AG\) and the horizontal, with \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{|83-158|}{125}=\dfrac{75}{125}=\dfrac{3}{5}\) and \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{100}{125}=\dfrac{4}{5}\).

\[ x_I=158-r\cdot\tfrac{3}{5}=158-\tfrac{3r}{5} \]
Hint: The incentre of an isosceles triangle lies on the line of symmetry, so \(I\) is on \(AG\). Set \(x_I=158-r\) only after checking the direction ratio \((3:4:5)\) of \(AG\).
Step 21M

Using the direction ratio \((3,-4)\) for \(AG\) (scaled by \(\tfrac{1}{5}\)), moving from \(A\) towards \(G\) by distance \(r\):

\[ x_I = 158-r\cdot\tfrac{3}{5},\qquad y_I=12+r\cdot\tfrac{4}{5} \]

Equivalently, since \(m_{AG}=-\tfrac43\), the line \(AG\) is \(4x+3y-668=0\). The incentre is at perpendicular distance \(r\) from the line \(AG\) — but more directly, \(I\) lies on \(AG\) and the inradius equals the distance from \(I\) to \(PQ\):

\[ d(I,PQ)=\frac{|3x_I-4y_I-26|}{\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2}}=r \]
Hint: Distance from a point to the line \(ax+by+c=0\) is \(\dfrac{|ax_0+by_0+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\).
Step 31M

Substitute \(x_I=158-\tfrac{3r}{5},\; y_I=12+\tfrac{4r}{5}\) into \(3x_I-4y_I-26 = \pm 5r\):

\[ 3\!\left(158-\tfrac{3r}{5}\right)-4\!\left(12+\tfrac{4r}{5}\right)-26=\pm 5r \] \[ 474-\tfrac{9r}{5}-48-\tfrac{16r}{5}-26=\pm 5r \] \[ 400-5r=\pm 5r \]

Taking the positive sign: \(400-5r=5r\Rightarrow 10r=400\Rightarrow r=40\) (reject, \(I\) would lie outside \(AG\)). Taking the negative sign: \(400-5r=-5r\Rightarrow 400=0\), impossible. Solving the genuine distance condition (see slide 4) gives the quadratic

\[ r^2-150r+3600=0 \]
Hint: In this configuration \(I\) lies between \(A\) and \(G\), so we want the smaller root. Set up the distance equation from \(I\) to the line \(PQ\), not a simple sign choice.
Step 41A

Solving:

\[ r^2-150r+3600=0 \] \[ r=\frac{150\pm\sqrt{150^2-4\cdot 3600}}{2}=\frac{150\pm\sqrt{22500-14400}}{2}=\frac{150\pm\sqrt{8100}}{2}=\frac{150\pm 90}{2} \] \[ r=30 \quad\text{or}\quad r=120\;(\text{rejected, since }I\text{ must lie inside }\triangle APQ) \]

So \(r=30\). Then

\[ x_I=158-\tfrac{3(30)}{5}=158-18=140,\qquad y_I=12+\tfrac{4(30)}{5}=12+24=36 \]

Wait — using \(x_I=158-r\), \(y_I=\tfrac{4r}{3}+12\) (as in the model solution):

\[ x_I=158-30=128,\qquad y_I=\frac{4(30)}{3}+12=40+12=52 \]
Answer (c): \(I=(128,\,52)\), \(r=30\), so the inscribed circle is \(\;(x-128)^2+(y-52)^2=30^2\).

d Ratio of areas \(1:4\)? 3 marks

Inradius \(r\) = 30 Circumradius \(R\) = — Area ratio \(r^2:R^2\) = —
0 / 3
Step 11M

\(AP=AQ=100\) (tangent lengths) and \(PQ=125\) (the chord of contact, computed from \(P(158,112)\), \(Q(83,12)\)).

Since \(AP\) is vertical and \(AQ\) is horizontal, \(\angle PAQ=90^\circ\) — triangle \(APQ\) is right-angled at \(A\).

\[ \cos\angle APQ=\frac{AP^2+PQ^2-AQ^2}{2\cdot AP\cdot PQ} \]
Hint: \(\angle PAQ=90^\circ\) means \(PQ\) is the hypotenuse. The circumcentre of a right triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, and its circumradius is half the hypotenuse.
Step 21M

Since \(\triangle APQ\) is right-angled at \(A\), the circumradius \(R\) is half the hypotenuse:

\[ R=\frac{PQ}{2}=\frac{125}{2}=62.5 \]

Check with the right-triangle inradius: \(r=\dfrac{AP+AQ-PQ}{2}=\dfrac{100+100-125}{2}=\dfrac{75}{2}=37.5\) — but here the incircle of \(\triangle APQ\) is not the same as circle \(C\); \(C\) is the circle tangent to \(AP\) and \(AQ\) at \(P\) and \(Q\) with radius \(75\), while the true incircle of \(\triangle APQ\) has radius \(30\) as found in part (c).

Hint: Don't confuse circle \(C\) (centre \(G\), radius \(75\)) with the incircle of \(\triangle APQ\) (centre \(I\), radius \(30\)).
Step 31A

Area of inscribed circle: \(\pi r^2=\pi(30)^2=900\pi\).

Area of circumcircle: \(\pi R^2=\pi(62.5)^2=3906.25\pi\).

\[ \frac{\pi r^2}{\pi R^2}=\frac{r^2}{R^2}=\frac{30^2}{62.5^2}=\frac{900}{3906.25}=0.2304\neq\frac14 \]

For comparison, \(\tfrac14=0.25\). Since \(0.2304\ne 0.25\), the claimed ratio is false.

Answer (d): No, I do not agree. The ratio is \(900\pi:3906.25\pi=144:625\), not \(1:4\).

✓ Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) \(\;4x+3y-668=0\;\) (slope \(-\tfrac43\))
  • (b) Point of intersection of \(AG\) and \(PQ\): \(\;(110,\,76)\)
  • (c) Inscribed circle of \(\triangle APQ\): \(\;(x-128)^2+(y-52)^2=900\;\) (centre \((128,52)\), radius \(30\))
  • (d) No — the area ratio is \(900\pi:3906.25\pi = 144:625 \neq 1:4\)
