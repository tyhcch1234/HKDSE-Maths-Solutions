Step 1 · Solve the first inequality

HKDSE 2016 · Paper 1 · Q6

Consider the compound inequality

$x+6 < 6(x+11)$   or   $x \le -5$     $\cdots\ (*)$

(a) Solve $(*)$.

(b) Write down the greatest negative integer satisfying $(*)$.   [4 marks]

1(a) Start with the first inequality
$$x+6 < 6(x+11)$$
Hint: The symbol $(*)$ is an “or” compound inequality, so solve each part separately — one solution is enough to satisfy $(*)$.
2Expand the right-hand side
$$x+6 < 6x+66$$
Hint: Expand $6(x+11)$ carefully — remember to multiply both terms by $6$. (1M)
3Collect like terms
$$x-6x < 66-6$$ $$-5x < 60$$
Hint: Bring all $x$ terms to the left and all numbers to the right.
4Divide by $-5$ — reverse the sign
$$-5x < 60 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x > -12$$
Hint: Dividing (or multiplying) an inequality by a negative number flips the inequality sign. (1A)
5Combine the two parts of $(*)$
Therefore, we have   $x > -12$   or   $x \le -5$.
These two sets fit together perfectly: the gap $-12 < x \le -5$ is already inside $x>-12$, and everything from $-5$ downwards is in $x\le-5$. So together they cover the whole line.
Hint: Draw both rays — do they leave any gap? (1A)
6Conclusion for (a)
Thus, the solutions of $(*)$ are all real numbers.
Hint: Every real number is either $>-12$ or $\le -5$ (indeed $\le -5 \Rightarrow >-12$ anyway).
7(b) Greatest negative integer
Negative integers are $-1,-2,-3,\dots$ — and all of them satisfy $(*)$. The largest is $-1$.
Hint: “Greatest negative” means closest to $0$ from below. Note $0$ is not negative. (1A)
✓ Answer: (a) $x>-12$ or $x\le-5$, i.e. all real numbers;   (b) $-1$
