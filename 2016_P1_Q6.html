Inequalities Compound Inequality Number Line

Consider the compound inequality \(x + 6 < 6(x + 11)\) or \(x \le -5\) ......... (*)

(a) Solve (*).
(b) Write down the greatest negative integer satisfying (*). [4 marks]

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Solve the first inequality

We start with the left part of the compound inequality:

\(x + 6 < 6(x + 11)\)

Expand the bracket on the right-hand side.

Hint: Distribute \(6\) to both terms inside the bracket: \(6 \times x + 6 \times 11 = 6x + 66\).
2

Simplify and collect like terms

\(x + 6 < 6x + 66\)

\(x - 6x < 66 - 6\)

\(-5x < 60\)

Hint: Move all terms containing \(x\) to the left and constants to the right. Remember to subtract \(6\) from both sides and \(6x\) from both sides.
3

Divide by the coefficient of \(x\)

We have \(-5x < 60\).

\(\frac{-5x}{-5} > \frac{60}{-5}\)

\(x > -12\)

Hint: When you divide (or multiply) both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign flips. Here we divide by \(-5\), so \(< \) becomes \(>\).
4

Combine the results (Union)

The original compound inequality uses “or”. We have:

\(x > -12\) or \(x \le -5\)

On the number line:

  • \(x > -12\) represents all numbers to the right of \(-12\).
  • \(x \le -5\) represents all numbers from \(-5\) and to the left.

Since these two intervals overlap, together they cover the entire number line.

Therefore, the solutions of (*) are all real numbers.
Hint: Draw the two intervals on a number line. \(x > -12\) goes right from \(-12\) (open circle). \(x \le -5\) goes left from \(-5\) (closed circle). Together they cover everything.
5

(b) Greatest negative integer satisfying (*)

We need the largest negative integer that belongs to the solution set. Since the solution set is all real numbers, we simply look for the greatest negative integer.

Negative integers: \(-1, -2, -3, \ldots\)

Answer: \(-1\)

Hint: The greatest negative integer is the one closest to zero on the negative side. Since all real numbers are solutions, \(-1\) is valid.
