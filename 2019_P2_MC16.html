Mensuration of a parallelogram and a square — chasing areas through similar triangles

Parallelogram Square Similar Triangles Area Ratio = (Side Ratio)² Multiple-Choice

Question

[2019 MC #16]

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram and \(AEFG\) is a square. It is given that \(BE:EF:FC = 2:7:3\). \(BD\) cuts \(AE\) and \(FG\) at the points \(X\) and \(Y\) respectively. If the area of \(\triangle ABX\) is \(24\text{ cm}^2\), then the area of the quadrilateral \(CDYF\) is

A \(54\text{ cm}^2\)
B \(77\text{ cm}^2\)
C \(81\text{ cm}^2\)
D \(87\text{ cm}^2\)

(Answer: D)

Part 1 — Set up the figure & read off lengths

12

Drag the slider to change the scale unit. The ratio \(BE:EF:FC = 2:7:3\) is fixed, so every length rescales together — the areas change but the geometry (and the answer) does not.

Step 1 1M

Let one part of the ratio be \(k\). Then

\[ BE = 2k,\quad EF = 7k,\quad FC = 3k \]

Since \(AEFG\) is a square, the top side of the parallelogram splits into two pieces:

\[ BF = BE + EF = 2k + 7k = 9k,\qquad FC = 3k \] \[ BC = BF + FC = 9k + 3k = 12k \]
Hint: A square gives \(AE = EF = 7k\), so the vertical height of the parallelogram equals \(7k\) — that will be the "common height" used later.
Step 2 1A

Because \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal:

\[ DA = BC = 12k \]

Also \(AD /\!/ BC\) and \(AB /\!/ DC\), which is the key to every similarity below.

Lengths: \(BE=2k,\ EF=7k,\ FC=3k,\ BF=9k,\ BC=DA=12k\).
0 / 2

Part 2 — \(\triangle ADX \sim \triangle EBX\) : find \(DX:XB\)

Step 1 1M

In \(\triangle ADX\) and \(\triangle EBX\):

  • \(\angle ADX = \angle EBX\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(AD /\!/ BC\))
  • \(\angle AXD = \angle EXB\) (vert. opp. \(\angle\)s)
  • \(\angle DAX = \angle BEX\) (∠ sum of \(\triangle\))

So \(\triangle ADX \sim \triangle EBX\) (AAA).

Hint: The two triangles share the vertex \(X\) and are cut by the two parallel lines \(AD\) and \(BE\). This is the classic "bow-tie" (butterfly) similarity.
Step 2 1A

Corresponding sides are proportional:

\[ \frac{DX}{BX} = \frac{DA}{BE} = \frac{12k}{2k} = \frac{6}{1} \]

Hence \(DX : XB = 6 : 1\).

\(DX:XB = 6:1\), so \(DX\) is 6 times \(BX\).

Part 3 — Use the common height to get Area of \(\triangle ADX\)

Both triangles \(\triangle ADX\) and \(\triangle ABX\) have bases lying on the same line \(BD\) and share the same apex \(A\). Therefore their areas are in the ratio of their bases \(DX:XB\).

Step 1 1M

\(\triangle ADX\) and \(\triangle ABX\) have the same height \(h\) (perpendicular distance from \(A\) to line \(BD\)). Thus

\[ \frac{\text{Area of }\triangle ADX}{\text{Area of }\triangle ABX} = \frac{DX}{BX} = \frac{6}{1} \]
Hint: A common height means area ratio = base ratio. No need to know the actual height!
Step 2 1A

Given \(\text{Area of }\triangle ABX = 24\text{ cm}^2\):

\[ \text{Area of }\triangle ADX = 24 \times 6 = 144\text{ cm}^2 \]

Therefore

\[ \text{Area of }\triangle ADB = 24 + 144 = 168\text{ cm}^2 \]
Area of \(\triangle ADX = 144\text{ cm}^2\), and Area of \(\triangle ADB = 168\text{ cm}^2\).

Part 4 — \(\triangle FBY \sim \triangle ADX\) and finish the area

Step 1 1M

In \(\triangle ADX\) and \(\triangle FBY\):

  • \(\angle ADX = \angle FBY\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(AD /\!/ BC\))
  • \(\angle AXD = \angle FYB\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(AE /\!/ GF\))
  • \(\angle DAX = \angle BFY\) (∠ sum of \(\triangle\))

So \(\triangle ADX \sim \triangle FBY\) (AAA).

Hint: \(Y\) lies on \(FG\) and \(X\) lies on \(AE\), and \(AE /\!/ GF\) (sides of the square). This gives the second pair of equal angles.
Step 2 1M

Corresponding side ratio:

\[ \frac{BF}{DA} = \frac{9k}{12k} = \frac{3}{4} \]

Since areas scale as the square of the side ratio:

\[ \frac{\text{Area of }\triangle FBY}{\text{Area of }\triangle ADX} = \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{16} \] \[ \text{Area of }\triangle FBY = 144 \times \frac{9}{16} = 81\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: \(\left(\frac{9}{12}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2\). Don't forget to square the ratio when comparing areas.
Step 3 1M

Note that \(\triangle BCD\) is made of the quadrilateral \(CDYF\) together with \(\triangle FBY\):

\[ \text{Area of }\triangle BCD = \text{Area of } CDYF + \text{Area of }\triangle FBY \] \[ = (x + 81)\text{ cm}^2 \quad \text{where } x = \text{Area of } CDYF \]
Hint: Look at the diagonal \(BD\): it splits the whole parallelogram into \(\triangle ABD\) and \(\triangle BCD\).
Step 4 1A

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, so a diagonal bisects its area:

\[ \text{Area of }\triangle BCD = \text{Area of }\triangle ADB = 168\text{ cm}^2 \]

Solve for \(x\):

\[ x + 81 = 168 \] \[ x = 87 \]
Area of \(CDYF = 87\text{ cm}^2\) — the answer is D.

Answer at a glance

  • Setup
    \(BE=2k,\ EF=7k,\ FC=3k\), so \(BF=9k\) and \(BC=AD=12k\).
  • \(\triangle ADX \sim \triangle EBX\)
    \(\dfrac{DX}{BX}=\dfrac{AD}{BE}=\dfrac{12k}{2k}=6\), hence \(DX:XB = 6:1\).
  • Common height
    \(\dfrac{\text{Area }\triangle ADX}{\text{Area }\triangle ABX}=6 \Rightarrow \text{Area }\triangle ADX = 144\text{ cm}^2\).
  • Whole half
    \(\text{Area }\triangle ADB = 24+144 = 168\text{ cm}^2\).
  • \(\triangle FBY \sim \triangle ADX\)
    Side ratio \(\dfrac{BF}{DA}=\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac34 \Rightarrow\) area ratio \(=\dfrac{9}{16}\). \(\text{Area }\triangle FBY = 144\times\dfrac{9}{16}=81\text{ cm}^2\).
  • Finish
    \(x+81=168 \Rightarrow x = \boxed{87\text{ cm}^2}\)  (Option D).
