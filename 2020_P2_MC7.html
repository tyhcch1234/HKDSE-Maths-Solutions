Question 7 · Polynomials

MC · Multiple Choice
Q7

If \(h\) and \(k\) are constants such that

\[ (x+h)(x+6) \equiv (x+4)^2 + k \,, \]

then \(k =\)

A \(-28\)
B \(-16\)
C \(-4\)
D \(2\)
Key Concepts: Identities Equating Coefficients Substitution Expansion

Step-by-Step Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning.

We are told that the two expressions are identically equal for all values of \(x\):

\[ (x+h)(x+6) \equiv (x+4)^2 + k \]
💡 Hint: The symbol \(\equiv\) (identity) means both sides are equal for every real number \(x\). This is stronger than an equation — it lets us substitute any convenient value, or compare coefficients.

Because the identity holds for all \(x\), we can pick a value that simplifies the LHS to zero. Notice \(x+6\) becomes \(0\) when \(x=-6\).

Put \(x = -6\) into both sides:

\[ \begin{aligned} [(-6)+h]\,[(-6)+6] &= [(-6)+4]^2 + k \\ (h-6)(0) &= (-2)^2 + k \\ 0 &= 4 + k \end{aligned} \]
✅ Result: \(k = -4\)
💡 Why did this work? Choosing \(x=-6\) made the \((x+6)\) factor zero, so \(h\) disappeared — we didn't even need to find \(h\)!

Expand both sides into standard form \(ax^2+bx+c\).

Left-hand side:

\[ (x+h)(x+6) = x^2 + (h+6)x + 6h \]

Right-hand side:

\[ (x+4)^2 + k = x^2 + 8x + (16+k) \]

Since the identity holds, the coefficients of like terms must match:

\[ \begin{cases} h+6 = 8 & \cdots (1)\\[4pt] 6h = 16+k & \cdots (2) \end{cases} \]
💡 Note: The \(x^2\) coefficients already match (both are \(1\)), so no equation needed for \(x^2\).

From (1):

\[ h = 8 - 6 = 2 \]

Substitute \(h=2\) into (2):

\[ 6(2) = 16 + k \;\Rightarrow\; 12 = 16 + k \;\Rightarrow\; k = -4 \]

Alternatively, eliminate \(h\): do \((1)\times 6 - (2)\):

\[ 36 = 32 - k \;\Rightarrow\; k = -4 \]
✅ Both methods give: \(k = -4\)

Therefore, the answer is

C. \(-4\)
