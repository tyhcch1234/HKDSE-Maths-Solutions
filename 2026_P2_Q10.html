Mixture Ratio

Question

The costs of rice of brand \(S\) and rice of brand \(T\) are $\$12$/kg and $\$18$/kg respectively. If \(s\) kg of rice of brand \(S\) and \(t\) kg of rice of brand \(T\) are mixed so that the cost of the mixture is $\$16$/kg, then \(s:t=\)

  • A. \(1:2\)
  • B. \(2:1\)
  • C. \(2:3\)
  • D. \(3:2\)

Solution

Set up the cost equation
Weighted Average (Alligation) \(12s+18t=16(s+t)\) The \(s\) kg at \$12 plus the \(t\) kg at \$18 must cost the same as \(s+t\) kg at \$16.

Total cost of the mixture equals total cost of the ingredients:

\[ 12s+18t=16(s+t) \]
Simplify
Solving a Linear Equation in Two Variables \(12s+18t=16s+16t\) Expanding and collecting terms gives \(2t=4s\), i.e. \(t=2s\).
\[ 12s+18t=16s+16t\ \Rightarrow\ 2t=4s\ \Rightarrow\ t=2s \]
Find the ratio
Ratio in Simplest Form \(s:t=s:2s=1:2\) Dividing both parts by \(s\) leaves \(1:2\), so the cheaper brand is used twice as much.
\[ s:t=s:2s=1:2 \]

Check (alligation): $16$ is twice as far from $12$ as it is from $18$, so the cheaper brand must be twice as much — $1:2$.

Final Answer: A

Question. A shop mixes two brands of coffee costing \$20 and \$28 per kg. The mixture is sold at \$23 per kg. Find the ratio of the mass of the cheaper brand to the mass of the dearer brand in the mixture.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let the mass of the cheaper brand be \(s\) kg and that of the dearer brand be \(t\) kg. The total cost is \(20s+28t\), which must equal \(23(s+t)\).
Step 2. \(20s+28t=23s+23t\Rightarrow 5t=3s\).
Step 3. Hence \(s:t=5:3\).
Step 4. Check: \(5\) kg at \$20 plus \(3\) kg at \$28 costs \(100+84=184\), and \(8\) kg at \$23 also costs \$184 ✓.
Answer: \(5:3\)
