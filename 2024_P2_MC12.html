Rate, Ratio and Variations — Weighted Average Speed

Rate & Speed Weighted Average Unit Conversion Distance = Speed × Time

Q Question

A car travels at an average speed of 60 km/h for 18 minutes, and then travels at an average speed of 40 km/h for 27 minutes. The average speed of the car for the whole journey is

  • A 48 km/h
  • B 50 km/h
  • C 52 km/h
  • D 54 km/h

2 Step-by-Step Solution

Tap each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict the next line before you open it!

1 What does "average speed" actually mean?▼

Average speed is not the simple average of the two speeds. It is the total distance divided by the total time for the entire journey.

Formula (unsubstituted): \[ \text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}} \]
  • Total distance — the sum of distances covered in both parts of the trip.
  • Total time — the sum of all the time spent, in the same time unit.
2 Spot the unit trap: hours vs. minutes▼

The speeds are given in km/h, but the times are given in minutes. We must convert the times into hours before using the formula.

\[ 18 \text{ min} = \frac{18}{60} \text{ h} = 0.3 \text{ h} \qquad 27 \text{ min} = \frac{27}{60} \text{ h} = 0.45 \text{ h} \]
Conversion rule: \(1 \text{ hour} = 60 \text{ minutes}\), so divide the minutes by 60 to get hours.
3 Find the distance of the first part▼

Using \(\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}\) for the first leg (60 km/h for 18 min = 0.3 h):

\[ d_1 = 60 \times 0.3 = 18 \text{ km} \]

Shortcut used in the model answer: \(60 \times 18\) directly keeps the minutes as the time unit — the final division by \(18+27\) also keeps minutes, so the units cancel consistently.

4 Find the distance of the second part▼

For the second leg (40 km/h for 27 min = 0.45 h):

\[ d_2 = 40 \times 0.45 = 18 \text{ km} \]

Interesting — both legs cover 18 km each, even though the speeds and times differ!

5 Add up total distance and total time▼

Now combine both parts:

\[ \text{Total distance} = 18 + 18 = 36 \text{ km} \] \[ \text{Total time} = 0.3 + 0.45 = 0.75 \text{ h} \]
6 Divide to get the average speed▼
\[ \text{Average speed} = \frac{36}{0.75} = 48 \text{ km/h} \]

Equivalent to the model answer, which keeps everything in minutes:

\[ \frac{60 \times 18 + 40 \times 27}{18 + 27} = \frac{1080 + 1080}{45} = \frac{2160}{45} = 48 \text{ km/h} \]
Why this works: the numerator is (speed × time) summed, which is total distance in "km·min" units. Dividing by total minutes converts it back to km/h.

★ Key Takeaway & Common Mistakes

  • Never just average the two speeds: \((60+40)/2 = 50\) is the classic wrong answer (Option B). It only works if the times are equal, which they are not here.
  • Always check that time units match the speed units. Here, minutes had to be handled carefully.
  • The correct weighted average gives more "weight" to the longer duration: the 40 km/h leg lasts 27 min, so the answer is pulled below 50.
\[ \boxed{\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}} = 48 \text{ km/h}} \]
