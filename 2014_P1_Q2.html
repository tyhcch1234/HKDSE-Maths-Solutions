Step 1 — Factorising $a^2-2a-3$: find two numbers that multiply to $-3$ and add to $-2$.

Question

Factorize

(a)   $a^2-2a-3$

(b)   $ab^2+b^2+a^2-2a-3$   [3 marks]

Step 1Factorize part (a) by cross method
Cross Method \(x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)\) We need \(pq=-3\) and \(p+q=-2\), which are satisfied by \(p=1\), \(q=-3\).

We need two numbers whose product is $-3$ and whose sum is $-2$.

$(-3)\times 1=-3$,   $(-3)+1=-2$ ✓

$a^2-2a-3=(a+1)(a-3)$

Hint: the coefficient of $a^2$ is 1, so just split the constant term.

Step 2Split part (b) into two groups

$ab^2+b^2+a^2-2a-3$

Group the terms containing $b^2$ together, and the rest is exactly part (a):

$=\underbrace{ab^2+b^2}_{b^2\text{-group}}+\underbrace{a^2-2a-3}_{\text{part (a)}}$

Hint: the “leftover” $a^2-2a-3$ is reused from part (a).

Step 3Substitute part (a) and take out the common factor
Common Factor Law \(AB+AC=A(B+C)\) The bracket \((a+1)\) appears in both terms, so it can be taken out as the common factor.

$=b^2(a+1)+(a+1)(a-3)$

Both terms share the common factor $(a+1)$:

$=(a+1)\big[\,b^2+(a-3)\,\big]$

Hint: factor out the whole bracket $(a+1)$, not just $a$.

Step 4Write the final factorised form

$=(a+1)(b^2+a-3)$

Hint: check by expanding back to $ab^2+b^2+a^2-2a-3$.

Answer
(a)   $a^2-2a-3=(a+1)(a-3)$
(b)   $ab^2+b^2+a^2-2a-3=(a+1)(b^2+a-3)$

Question. Factorize
(a) \(a^{2}+4a+3\),
(b) \(ab^{2}+b^{2}+a^{2}+4a+3\). [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) Two numbers with product \(3\) and sum \(4\) are \(1\) and \(3\), so \(a^{2}+4a+3=(a+1)(a+3)\).
Step 2. (b) Group the \(b^{2}\)-terms: \(ab^{2}+b^{2}+a^{2}+4a+3=b^{2}(a+1)+(a^{2}+4a+3)\).
Step 3. Substitute part (a): \(b^{2}(a+1)+(a+1)(a+3)\). Both terms now contain \((a+1)\).
Step 4. Take out the common factor: \((a+1)\left[b^{2}+(a+3)\right]=(a+1)(b^{2}+a+3)\). Expanding back gives \(ab^{2}+b^{2}+a^{2}+4a+3\) \u2713.
Answer: (a) \((a+1)(a+3)\) (b) \((a+1)(b^{2}+a+3)\)
