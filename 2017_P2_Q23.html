Step 1 · Both lines slope upward with different gradients
[2017 MC #23]
In the figure, the equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \(x+my=n\) and \(x+py=q\) respectively. Which of the following are true?
I.  \(m<p\)
II. \(n>q\)
III.\(n+m<p+q\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
STEP 1 · SLOPE SIGNS

Both lines rise to the right ⇒ positive slopes

Slope of a Line in General Form \(Ax+By+C=0 \Rightarrow \text{slope}=-\dfrac{A}{B}\) For \(x+my=n\) the slope is \(-\dfrac{1}{m}\), and a positive slope forces \(m<0\).

Rewrite each line in slope‑intercept form.
From \(x+my=n\):

$$y=\frac{n-x}{m}=-\frac{1}{m}x+\frac{n}{m}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{slope of }L_1=-\frac{1}{m}$$

From \(x+py=q\):

$$y=-\frac{1}{p}x+\frac{q}{p}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{slope of }L_2=-\frac{1}{p}$$

Both slopes are positive,
so \(-\frac{1}{m}>0\) and \(-\frac{1}{p}>0\), giving

$$m<0\quad\text{and}\quad p<0$$
Hint: a positive slope needs a negative denominator here, because the \(x\)‑coefficient is negative.
STEP 2 · COMPARE SLOPES (I)

\(L_1\) is less steep than \(L_2\) ⇒ \(m<p\)

Comparing Inequalities with Negative Numbers \(-\dfrac{1}{m}<-\dfrac{1}{p} \iff m Both denominators are negative, so the comparison of slopes is preserved after rearranging.

Visually the slope of \(L_1\) is smaller than that of \(L_2\):

$$-\frac{1}{m}<-\frac{1}{p}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{m}>\frac{1}{p}$$

Since both \(m,p<0\),
multiplying by the negative \(mp\) reverses the inequality:

$$p>m\quad\text{equivalently}\quad m<p$$

Statement I is TRUE.

Hint: \(-pm>0\) (negative × negative), so the direction of the inequality is preserved when cross‑multiplying in that form.
STEP 3 · COMPARE \(x\)-INTERCEPTS (II)

\(L_1\) meets the \(x\)-axis further right than \(L_2\) ⇒ \(n>q\)

x-intercept of a Line \(\text{put } y=0 \Rightarrow x=n\) The intercepts are \(n\) and \(q\), and \(L_{1}\) cuts the axis further right.

Put \(y=0\) in each equation: \(x=n\) for \(L_1\), and \(x=q\) for \(L_2\).

The \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\) is to the right of that of \(L_2\),
so

$$n>q$$

Statement II is TRUE.

Hint: the equation \(x+my=n\) gives \(x\)-intercept \(n\); this is exactly the number on the right of the equation.
STEP 4 · TEST \(y=-1\) (III)

The crossing at \(y=-1\) lies on the right of \(L_2\)'s

Substitute \(y=-1\) into each line:

For \(L_1\): \(x+m(-1)=n\ \Rightarrow\ x=n+m\)

For \(L_2\): \(x+p(-1)=q\ \Rightarrow\ x=p+q\)

In the figure,
the point of \(L_1\) at \(y=-1\) lies to the right of the point of \(L_2\) at \(y=-1\). Therefore:

$$n+m>p+q$$

But Statement III claims \(n+m<p+q\),
which is the opposite. Statement III is FALSE.

Hint: the crossing point with \(y=-1\) is a quick way to compare \(n+m\) with \(p+q\).
Statements I and II are true, III is false  →  Answer: A
