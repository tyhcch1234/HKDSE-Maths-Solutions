HKDSE Math 2014 Paper 2 Q36

Complex Numbers Conjugate Real β

Question

If \(\beta\) is a real number, then \(\overline{\beta - 2i}\) =

Visualise the Conjugate

Drag the slider to change \(\beta\) (real part). See how \(z = \beta - 2i\) and its conjugate \(\bar{z}\) move.

Reflection across the real axis: \(z = \beta - 2i\) → \(\bar{z} = \beta + 2i\)

Guided Solution

The complex conjugate of \(z = a + bi\) (where \(a,b \in \mathbb{R}\)) is:

\[ \overline{z} = a - bi \]

Hint: The conjugate flips the sign of the imaginary part only. Real part stays the same.

Given \(z = \beta - 2i\) and \(\beta\) is a real number.

So we can write it as:

\[ z = \underbrace{\beta}_{\text{real part}} + \underbrace{(-2)}_{\text{imaginary coefficient}}i \]

Real part \(a\)

\(\beta\)

Imag part \(b\)

\(-2\)

Using \(\overline{a + bi} = a - bi\), substitute \(a = \beta\) and \(b = -2\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \overline{\beta - 2i} &= \beta - (-2)i \\ &= \beta + 2i \end{aligned} \]

Our result is \(\beta + 2i\).

Looking at the options:

  • A. \(\beta - 2i\) ❌ (same as original)
  • B. \(\beta + 2i\) ✅ (matches our calculation)
  • C. \(2 - \beta i\) ❌ (swapped parts)
  • D. \(2 + \beta i\) ❌ (swapped & sign error)

Therefore, the answer is B.

💡 Key Insight

The conjugate of a complex number simply flips the sign of the imaginary part. When the original is \(\beta - 2i\), the conjugate is \(\beta + 2i\) — the real part \(\beta\) is unchanged.
