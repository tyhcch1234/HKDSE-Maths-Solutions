Question

It is given that $$\begin{cases}\log_3 x+\log_3 y=9\\[2pt]\log_x 81-\log_y 9=1\end{cases}\qquad\text{where }0

(a) Let $u=\log_3 y$. Prove that $u^2-3u-18=0$.  (2 marks)

(b) Find $x$.  (2 marks)

Solution

Step 1 Translate the first given equation into $u$.

Let $u=\log_3 y$ and let $v=\log_3 x$. Then $\log_3 x+\log_3 y=9 \;\Rightarrow\; v+u=9$, so $v=9-u$.

This gives the point $(\log_3 x,\log_3 y)=(v,u)$ lying on the line $u+v=9$. The diagram shows this line in teal.

Hint: always name both unknowns when a substitution is suggested; here $v$ is just a helper.
Step 2 Change base of the second equation.
Change of Base Formula \(\log_{b}a=\dfrac{\log_{c}a}{\log_{c}b}\) Using base \(3\): \(\log_{x}81=\dfrac{4}{v}\) and \(\log_{y}9=\dfrac{2}{u}\).

Use $\log_b a=\dfrac{\log_3 a}{\log_3 b}$ (change of base, base $3$):

$$\log_x 81=\frac{\log_3 81}{\log_3 x}=\frac{4}{\log_3 x}=\frac{4}{v},\qquad \log_y 9=\frac{\log_3 9}{\log_3 y}=\frac{2}{\log_3 y}=\frac{2}{u}.$$

So $\log_x 81-\log_y 9=1$ becomes $\dfrac{4}{v}-\dfrac{2}{u}=1$.

Hint: convert everything to base 3 so the problem lives in $(u,v)$ coordinates. (1M)
Step 3 Eliminate $v$ and form the quadratic in $u$.

Substitute $v=9-u$:

$$\frac{4}{9-u}-\frac{2}{u}=1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4u-2(9-u)=u(9-u).$$

Once we reach the target form $u^2-3u-18=0$, this completes part (a).

Hint: multiply both sides by $u(9-u)$ — valid since $0<x<y$ gives $u,v>0$, so $u\ne 0,\;9-u\ne 0$.
Step 4 Expand and rearrange (the required proof).

$$\begin{aligned} 4u-2(9-u)&=u(9-u)\\ 4u-18+2u&=9u-u^2\\ 6u-18&=9u-u^2\\ u^2-3u-18&=0. \end{aligned}$$

Hence $u^2-3u-18=0$, as required.  ✓ (1 mark for the correct proof)

Hint: state clearly "Thus $u^2-3u-18=0$" — the final line is the answer to part (a).
Step 5 Factorise and solve the quadratic.
Factorising a Quadratic \(x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)\) \(u^{2}-3u-18=(u-6)(u+3)\), so \(u=6\) or \(u=-3\).

$$u^2-3u-18=(u-6)(u+3)=0\;\Longrightarrow\;u=6 \;\text{ or }\; u=-3.$$

Each value of $u$ gives a candidate pair $(u,v)$ with $v=9-u$:

$$u=6\Rightarrow v=3;\qquad u=-3\Rightarrow v=12.$$

Hint: keep both roots for now — the condition $0<x<y$ decides later.
Step 6 Convert back to $x,y$.

$$\begin{cases}u=6,\;v=3:\\[2pt] \log_3 y=6,\;\log_3 x=3\end{cases} \;\Longrightarrow\; y=3^6=729,\;x=3^3=27.$$

$$\begin{cases}u=-3,\;v=12:\\[2pt] \log_3 y=-3,\;\log_3 x=12\end{cases} \;\Longrightarrow\; y=3^{-3}=\tfrac{1}{27},\;x=3^{12}=531\,441.$$

Hint: $x=3^{v}$ and $y=3^{u}$ since $u,v$ are base-3 logarithms. (1M for at least one pair)
Step 7 Apply $0<x<y$ to choose the answer.
Definition of Logarithm \(\log_{a}b=c\ \Longleftrightarrow\ a^{c}=b\) \(u=6,v=3\) gives \(x=3^{3}=27\) and \(y=3^{6}=729\), and \(0<27<729\) holds.

Case $u=6,\;v=3$: $\;x=27,\;y=729$, and indeed $0<27<729$. ✓

Case $u=-3,\;v=12$: $\;x=531\,441,\;y=\tfrac{1}{27}$, so $x>y$ (and $x>0$ still holds) — this contradicts $x<y$. ✗

Therefore the only valid solution is $x=27$ (with $y=729$).  (1A)

Hint: the condition $0<x<y$ is exactly what kills the second pair — always check it.
(a) Substituting $v=\log_3 x=9-u$ into $\dfrac{4}{v}-\dfrac{2}{u}=1$ gives $u^2-3u-18=0$.
(b) $u=6$ or $u=-3$; rejecting the pair with $x>y$,  $x=27$ (and $y=729$).

Question. Solve the equation \(\log_{3}x+\log_{3}(x-8)=2\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. By the product law, \(\log_{3}x(x-8)=2\), so \(x(x-8)=3^{2}=9\).
Step 2. \(x^{2}-8x-9=0\), which factorises as \((x-9)(x+1)=0\).
Step 3. Hence \(x=9\) or \(x=-1\).
Step 4. Since \(\log_{3}x\) needs \(x>0\) and \(x-8>0\), we need \(x>8\), so \(x=9\) only.
Answer: \(x=9\)
