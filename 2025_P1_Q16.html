HKDSE Math 2025 · Paper 1 · Q16

Laws of Indices, Exponential & Logarithmic Functions

Logarithms Change of Base Quadratic Equations System of Equations

Question

It is given that

\[ \begin{cases} \log_3 x + \log_3 y = 9 \\[4pt] \log_x 81 - \log_y 9 = 1 \end{cases} \quad \text{where } 0 < x < y. \]

(a) Let \(u = \log_3 y\). Prove that \(u^2 - 3u - 18 = 0\). (2 marks)

(b) Find \(x\). (2 marks)

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

1
Use the first equation to express \(\log_3 x\) in terms of \(u\)
+

The first equation is:

\[ \log_3 x + \log_3 y = 9 \]

Since \(u = \log_3 y\), we substitute:

\[ \log_3 x + u = 9 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \log_3 x = 9 - u \]
💡 Hint: The key is to express everything in terms of \(u = \log_3 y\).
2
Change the base of the logarithms in the second equation
+

The second equation is:

\[ \log_x 81 - \log_y 9 = 1 \]

Use the change-of-base formula \(\displaystyle \log_a b = \frac{\log_c b}{\log_c a}\):

\[ \log_x 81 = \frac{\log_3 81}{\log_3 x}, \qquad \log_y 9 = \frac{\log_3 9}{\log_3 y} \]

So the equation becomes:

\[ \frac{\log_3 81}{\log_3 x} - \frac{\log_3 9}{\log_3 y} = 1 \]
💡 Hint: \(\log_3 81 = 4\) and \(\log_3 9 = 2\). Also, \(\log_3 y = u\).
3
Substitute the known values and \(u\) into the equation
+

Since \(\log_3 81 = 4\), \(\log_3 9 = 2\), and \(\log_3 y = u\):

\[ \frac{4}{\log_3 x} - \frac{2}{u} = 1 \]

From Step 1, \(\log_3 x = 9 - u\). Substitute this:

\[ \frac{4}{9 - u} - \frac{2}{u} = 1 \]
4
Eliminate the fractions and simplify
+

Multiply both sides by \(u(9 - u)\) (valid since \(x, y > 1\) implies \(u \neq 0\) and \(9 - u \neq 0\)):

\[ 4u - 2(9 - u) = u(9 - u) \]

Expand both sides:

\[ 4u - 18 + 2u = 9u - u^2 \]
\[ 6u - 18 = 9u - u^2 \]
5
Rearrange to form the required quadratic equation
+

Bring all terms to one side:

\[ u^2 + 6u - 9u - 18 = 0 \]
\[ u^2 - 3u - 18 = 0 \]
✅ This proves part (a): \(u^2 - 3u - 18 = 0\)
6
Solve the quadratic equation for \(u\)
+

Factorise \(u^2 - 3u - 18\):

\[ (u - 6)(u + 3) = 0 \]

So \(u = 6\) or \(u = -3\).

💡 Hint: Both values are possible for \(u = \log_3 y\). We will check which one satisfies \(0 < x < y\).
7
Find the corresponding \(x\) and \(y\) for each case
+

Recall \(u = \log_3 y\) and \(\log_3 x = 9 - u\).

Case 1: \(u = 6\)

\[ \log_3 y = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 3^6 = 729 \] \[ \log_3 x = 9 - 6 = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3^3 = 27 \]

Case 2: \(u = -3\)

\[ \log_3 y = -3 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 3^{-3} = \frac{1}{27} \] \[ \log_3 x = 9 - (-3) = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3^{12} = 531\,441 \]
8
Apply the condition \(0 < x < y\) to select the correct answer
+

Check the two possible pairs:

  • Case 1: \(x = 27\), \(y = 729\). Here \(0 < 27 < 729\) ✅
  • Case 2: \(x = 531\,441\), \(y = \frac{1}{27}\). Here \(x > y\), which violates \(x < y\). ❌
✅ Therefore, the only valid solution is \(x = 27\).
