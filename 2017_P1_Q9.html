HKDSE Math 2017 Paper 1 Q9 Estimation, Approximation & Error

Standard Bottles & Measured Capacity

Question

A bottle is termed standard if its capacity is measured as 200 mL correct to the nearest 10 mL.

  • (a) Find the least possible capacity of a standard bottle.
  • (b) Someone claims that the total capacity of 120 standard bottles can be measured as 23.3 L correct to the nearest 0.1 L. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

[5 marks]

When a measurement is rounded to the nearest 10 mL, the maximum absolute error is half of the rounding unit.

\[ \text{Max absolute error} = \frac{10}{2} = 5 \text{ mL} \]

💡 Hint: The measurement 200 mL means the true capacity can be up to 5 mL more or 5 mL less than 200 mL.

The least possible capacity is the measured value minus the maximum absolute error.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Least possible capacity} &= 200 - 5 \\ &= 195 \text{ mL} \end{aligned} \]

⚠️ A common mistake is to divide 10 by 2 incorrectly or to think the least is 200 - 10. Always remember: the error is half the rounding unit.

The true capacity lies in the interval [195 mL, 205 mL].

True capacity Range limits Measured value 200 mL

To get the least possible total, assume every bottle has its least possible capacity (195 mL).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Least total} &= 195 \times 120 \\ &= 23\,400 \text{ mL} \\ &= 23.4 \text{ L} \quad (\text{since } 1 \text{ L} = 1000 \text{ mL}) \end{aligned} \]

💡 Hint: Convert mL to L by dividing by 1000.

The claim says the total can be measured as 23.3 L correct to the nearest 0.1 L. That means the true total must lie in the interval:

\[ 23.3 \pm 0.05 \text{ L} = [23.25 \text{ L},\; 23.35 \text{ L}] \]

But the least possible total capacity is 23.4 L, which is greater than 23.35 L.

\(23.4 \text{ L} > 23.35 \text{ L}\)

Therefore, the claim is disagreed.

⚠️ Even with the smallest possible bottles, the total is already larger than the upper bound of the claimed interval.
