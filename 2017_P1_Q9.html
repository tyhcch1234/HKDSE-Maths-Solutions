Estimation, Approximation & Error [5 marks]

A bottle is termed standard if its capacity is measured as $200\text{ mL}$ correct to the nearest $10\text{ mL}$.

(a) Find the least possible capacity of a standard bottle.

(b) Someone claims that the total capacity of $120$ standard bottles can be measured as $23.3\text{ L}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ L}$. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

Step 1Interpret “correct to the nearest 10 mL”.
Measuring $200\text{ mL}$ correct to the nearest $10\text{ mL}$ means the true capacity $C$ lies in the interval $$195\text{ mL} \le C < 205\text{ mL}.$$ So the maximum absolute error is $\dfrac{10}{2} = 5\text{ mL}$.
Hint: maximum absolute error = $\frac{1}{2}\times$ scale unit of the rounding.
Step 2(a) Least possible capacity.
The smallest capacity is the lower end of the interval: $$200 - 5 = 195\text{ mL}.$$
Hint: subtract the maximum absolute error from the nominal value.
Step 3(b) Least possible total of 120 bottles.
Each standard bottle holds at least $195\text{ mL}$, therefore $$195 \times 120 = 23\,400\text{ mL}.$$
Hint: the smallest total comes from using the smallest possible capacity for every bottle.
Step 4Convert to litres.
$$23\,400\text{ mL} = 23.4\text{ L}.$$
Hint: $1\text{ L} = 1000\text{ mL}$.
Step 5Compare with the claimed measurement.
A value measured as $23.3\text{ L}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ L}$ lies in $$23.25\text{ L} \le V < 23.35\text{ L}.$$ But the true total is at least $23.4\text{ L} > 23.35\text{ L}$, which is outside this interval.
Hint: the claimed reading is too small to be possible.
Step 6Conclusion.
Since the minimum possible total $23.4\text{ L}$ already exceeds the upper limit $23.35\text{ L}$ of the claimed reading, the claim is disagreed.
Hint: state clearly “disagree” with the supporting inequality.
Answer: (a) $195\text{ mL}$  ·  (b) Disagree — the least possible total is $23.4\text{ L} > 23.35\text{ L}$.
