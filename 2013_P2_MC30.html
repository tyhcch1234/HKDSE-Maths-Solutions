Question The two pie charts

The pie charts below show the distributions of the profits of stationery shop \(X\) and stationery shop \(Y\) from the sales of stationery in a certain month.

Which of the following must be true?

A. In that month, the profit from the sales of pencils of stationery shop \(X\) is the same as that of stationery shop \(Y\).

B. In that month, the total profit from the sales of pens and notebooks of stationery shop \(X\) is less than the total profit from the sales of rulers and pencils of the shop.

C. \(k = 14\)

D. \(\theta = 36^\circ\)

(1 mark)

Option A Are the two pencil profits equal?

Option A claims the profit from pencils is the same for both shops. But a pie chart only shows a proportion, not an actual amount.

600
1200
Pencil profit of \(X\) \(=\tfrac{36}{360}\times\) total \(X\) \(=\) 60k
Pencil profit of \(Y\) \(=10\%\times\) total \(Y\) \(=\) 120k
Are they equal? No
1A pie chart gives shares, not amounts

From chart \(X\): pencil profit \(= \dfrac{36^\circ}{360^\circ}=\dfrac{1}{10}=10\%\) of shop \(X\)'s total profit.

From chart \(Y\): pencil profit \(=10\%\) of shop \(Y\)'s total profit.

Hint: The two percentages happen to be equal, but the two totals need not be equal. Without knowing the total profit of each shop, the two pencil profits cannot be compared.
2Check with numbers1A

Let total profit of \(X = \$600\,000\) and total profit of \(Y=\$1\,200\,000\).

\[P_X(\text{pencil}) = 10\% \times 600 = 60 \quad(\$\,\text{thousand})\] \[P_Y(\text{pencil}) = 10\% \times 1200 = 120 \quad(\$\,\text{thousand})\] \[\Rightarrow 60 \neq 120\]
Hint: We chose the totals so that the pencil profits differ — proving option A is not necessarily true.
Conclusion: Option A is not necessarily true. ✗

Option B Pens + notebooks vs rulers + pencils (shop \(X\))

Because all percentages in option B come from the same shop \(X\), here we really can compare the shares — this is the key idea of the question.

90°
Pens + Notebooks \(= 60^\circ + 34^\circ =\) 94°
Rulers + Pencils \(= 68^\circ + 36^\circ =\) 104°
Comparison: 94° < 104°
1Express both groups as angles of the same chart

Pens and notebooks of shop \(X\): total angle

\[ = 60^\circ + \theta = 60^\circ + 34^\circ = 94^\circ \]

Rulers and pencils of shop \(X\): total angle

\[ = 68^\circ + 36^\circ = 104^\circ \]
Hint: Since both groups belong to the same pie chart (same total profit of \(X\)), comparing angles is exactly the same as comparing the actual profits.
2Compare1A
\[94^\circ < 104^\circ\] \[ \frac{60^\circ+\theta}{360^\circ} < \frac{68^\circ+36^\circ}{360^\circ} \]

i.e. the fraction of \(X\)'s profit from pens & notebooks is less than the fraction from rulers & pencils.

Conclusion: Option B is true. ✓ (This is the answer.)
3Why the other options fail1A

A — needs the two total profits to compare amounts; not given. ✗

C — \(k = 100-46-16-12-10 = 16 \neq 14\). ✗

D — \(\theta = 34^\circ \neq 36^\circ\). ✗

And in option B the numbers both come from chart \(X\), so the comparison is valid: \(94^\circ<104^\circ\), so B is always true.

Final answer: B

Answers At a glance

  • Chart \(X\): \(60^\circ+\theta+68^\circ+36^\circ+162^\circ=360^\circ\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\theta = 34^\circ\)
  • Chart \(Y\): \(46\%+16\%+k\%+12\%+10\%=100\%\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(k = 16\) (so option C, \(k=14\), is false)
  • Option D (\(\theta = 36^\circ\)) is false since \(\theta = 34^\circ\)
  • Option A needs the two total profits, which are not given — cannot be compared
  • Pens & notebooks of \(X\) \(= 60^\circ+34^\circ = 94^\circ\); rulers & pencils of \(X\) \(=68^\circ+36^\circ=104^\circ\)
  • Since \(94^\circ < 104^\circ\) within the same chart, the pens+notebooks profit of \(X\) is less than its rulers+pencils profit
  • Therefore the answer is B
