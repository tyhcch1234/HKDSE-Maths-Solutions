Statistics — Mean, Median & Stem-and-Leaf Diagram

Stem-and-Leaf Diagram Mean Median Data Ordering Proof by Contradiction

Q10 The Question

Tom conducts a survey on the numbers of hours spent on doing homework in a week by secondary students. Questionnaires are sent out and twenty of them are returned. The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the numbers of hours recorded in the twenty questionnaires:

Stem (tens)Leaf (units)
10 0 1 1 2 3 4 5 5 6 6 7 7
20 0 0 5 8
34 6
  1. Find the mean and the median of the numbers of hours recorded in the twenty questionnaires. 2 marks
  2. Tom receives four more questionnaires. He finds that the mean of the numbers of hours recorded in these four questionnaires is 18. It is found that the numbers of hours recorded in two of these four questionnaires are 19 and 20.
    1. Write down the mean of the numbers of hours recorded in the twenty-four questionnaires. 1 mark
    2. Is it possible that the median of the numbers of hours recorded in the twenty-four questionnaires is the same as the median found in (a)? Explain your answer. 4 marks

a Part (a) — Mean & Median of the 20 Values

Sum of the 20 values —
Mean \(= \dfrac{\text{sum}}{20}\) —
10th value / 11th value —
Median —
Step 11MRead the data & set up the mean
Reading the stem-and-leaf diagram (stem = tens, leaf = units), the 13 values with stem 1 are \[10,10,11,11,12,13,14,15,15,16,16,17,17\] the 5 values with stem 2 are \[20,20,20,25,28\] and the 2 values with stem 3 are \[34,36.\] So there are \(13+5+2=20\) values in total. \[\text{Mean}=\frac{\text{sum of all 20 values}}{20}\]
Hint: The stem is the tens digit and the leaf is the units digit, so the entry \(\text{“}2\mid 5\text{”}\) means \(25\) hours, not \(2.5\) hours.
Step 21AEvaluate the mean
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Sum} &= (10+10+11+11+12+13+14+15+15+16+16+17+17)\\ &\qquad +(20+20+20+25+28)+(34+36)\\ &= 177+113+70\\ &= 360 \end{aligned} \] \[\text{Mean}=\frac{360}{20}=18\]
Hint: Grouping by stem makes the addition safe: \(13\) values contribute \(177\), \(5\) values contribute \(113\), \(2\) values contribute \(70\). Check: \(177+113+70=360\).
Mean \(=18\).
Step 31ALocate the median
With \(n=20\) (even), the median is the average of the \(\frac{20}{2}=10\)th and the \(\left(\frac{20}{2}+1\right)=11\)th values of the ordered list: \[ \underbrace{10,10,11,11,12,13,14,15,15}_{\text{1st to 9th}}, \underbrace{16}_{\text{10th}}, \underbrace{16}_{\text{11th}}, 17,17,20,20,20,25,28,34,36 \] \[\text{Median}=\frac{16+16}{2}=16\]
Hint: The two middle values coincide here (\(16\) and \(16\)), so the median is exactly \(16\). Note the median \(\ne\) mean — the data are slightly skewed to the right by \(34\) and \(36\).
Median \(=16\).

i Part (b)(i) — The New Mean of 24 Values

Old sum (20 values) 360
Sum of 4 new values \(a+b+19+20\) —
New mean \(=\dfrac{360+(a+b+19+20)}{24}\) —
Constraint \(a+b\) (must equal 33) —
Step 11MTotal hours of the 4 new questionnaires
The mean of the 4 new questionnaires is \(18\), so their total is \[4\times 18 = 72\ \text{hours}.\] (Indeed \(19+20+a+b=72\), so \(a+b=33\).)
Hint: \(\text{Mean}\times\text{number of items}=\text{total}\). This works in reverse too: total \(\div\) count \(=\) mean.
Step 21ACombine with the original 20 values
The original 20 questionnaires have total \(20\times 18 = 360\) hours (as found in (a)). Hence the 24 questionnaires have total \[360+72=432\ \text{hours}.\] Therefore \[\text{New mean}=\frac{432}{24}=18.\]
Hint: Both the old group and the new group have mean \(18\), so mixing them cannot change the overall mean — a useful check. Drag the sliders above: as long as \(a+b=33\), the new mean stays at \(18\).
Mean of the 24 questionnaires \(=18\).

ii Part (b)(ii) — Can the Median Stay at 16?

Chosen \(a\) 16  →  \(b=33-a\) 17
Numbers \(\le 16\)  —
Numbers \(\ge 16\)  —
12th & 13th values —
Median of the 24 values —
Step 11MTranslate the condition \(a+b=33\)
Let \(a\) and \(b\) be the numbers of hours recorded in the two remaining questionnaires. Then \[a+b+19+20=4\times 18=72\] so that \[a+b=33.\]
Hint: This is the bridge between (b)(i) and the median question — it converts the “mean” information into an equation in \(a\) and \(b\) only.
Step 21MAdd the natural bound \(a\le 16\), \(b\le 16\)
Suppose the median of the 24 values is still \(16\), as in (a). In the 24-value ordered list, the median sits between the 12th and the 13th values, so the 12th value and the 13th value must both equal \(16\) (or their average must be \(16\), which forces the same conclusion here since the values are whole numbers and the surrounding values are known). Hence we need at least twelve values \(\le 16\), and at least twelve values \(\ge 16\). Counting the values \(\le 16\): \[10,10,11,11,12,13,14,15,15,16,16\] gives \(11\) values (the \(16\)s are the 10th and 11th), plus the new values \(a,b,19,20\) with \(a,b\le 16\) — at most \(4\) more. For the 12th and 13th values to be \(16\), we need \(a\le 16\) and \(b\le 16\) simultaneously, i.e. \(a+b\le 32\).
Hint: The two extra values \(19\) and \(20\) are both \(>16\) and sit beyond the median; they cannot help push the median down. Only \(a\) and \(b\) can be \(\le 16\).
Step 31MContradiction with \(a+b=33\)
Step 1 gives \(a+b=33\), while Step 2 requires \(a+b\le 32\): \[a+b=33\ \ \text{but}\ \ a+b\le 32.\] This is a contradiction. Hence the assumption is false.
Hint: This is a classic proof by contradiction. We assumed the median is \(16\), derived a statement that must hold (\(a+b\le 32\)), and showed it clashes with the given fact \(a+b=33\).
Step 41AConclusion
Since the assumption leads to a contradiction, the median of the 24 numbers of hours cannot be the same as the median found in (a), i.e. it cannot be \(16\).
Hint: Try the slider above: whatever \(a\) you pick (so that \(a+b=33\)), the 12th and 13th values can never both be \(16\) — the median always shifts to \(17\) or more (or below \(16\), but then it is not \(16\) either).
No, it is impossible. Assuming the median is \(16\) forces \(a\le 16\) and \(b\le 16\), hence \(a+b\le 32\), which contradicts the known fact \(a+b=33\). Therefore the median of the 24 questionnaires cannot be \(16\).

✓ Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Mean \(=\dfrac{360}{20}=\mathbf{18}\) (1A)  ·  Median \(=\dfrac{16+16}{2}=\mathbf{16}\) (1A)
  • (b)(i) Total of 24 questionnaires \(=360+4\times 18=432\), so the new mean \(=\dfrac{432}{24}=\mathbf{18}\) (1A)
  • (b)(ii) Let the 2 unknown values be \(a,b\). Since \(\dfrac{a+b+19+20}{4}=18\), we get \(a+b=33\) (1M).
  • (b)(ii) If the median were \(16\), then \(a\le 16\) and \(b\le 16\), so \(a+b\le 32\) (1M) — contradicting \(a+b=33\) (1M).
  • (b)(ii) Hence it is impossible for the median of the 24 numbers of hours to be the same as the median in (a) (1A).
