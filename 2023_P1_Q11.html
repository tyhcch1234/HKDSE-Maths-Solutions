The table below shows the distribution of the numbers of calculators owned by a class of students.

Number of calculators owned1234
Number of students85\(n\)1

The mean of the distribution is 2.

  1. Find the median, the inter-quartile range and the variance of the distribution. [5 marks]
  2. Two students now withdraw from the class. It is found that the mean of the distribution remains unchanged. Is there any change in the range of the distribution due to the withdrawal of the two students? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

Part (a) — Median, IQR, Variance 5 marks

Total students 20 · Mean 2.00 · Median — · IQR — · Variance —

Drag the slider to see how the frequency distribution changes. Only \(n=6\) makes the mean equal 2.

Step 11MFind \(n\) from the mean condition

Total number of students \(=8+5+n+1=n+14\). Total number of calculators:

\[ 1(8)+2(5)+3(n)+4(1) = 8+10+3n+4 = 3n+22 .\]

Since the mean is \(2\):

\[ \frac{8+10+3n+4}{8+5+n+1}=2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{3n+22}{n+14}=2 .\] \[ 3n+22 = 2n+28 \;\Longrightarrow\; n = 6 .\]
Hint: Write “mean \(=\) (sum of data) \(\div\) (number of data)” first, then cross-multiply to get a linear equation in \(n\).
Answer: \(n=6\) (so there are \(20\) students).
Step 21AThe median

With \(n=6\), the ordered data are:

\[ \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{8},\;\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{5},\;\underbrace{3,\ldots,3}_{6},\;4 .\]

There are \(20\) values, so the median is the average of the \(10^{\text{th}}\) and \(11^{\text{th}}\) values.

The \(10^{\text{th}}\) value is \(2\) (since the first \(8\) values are \(1\) and the next \(5\) are \(2\)). The \(11^{\text{th}}\) value is also \(2\).

Hint: For an even number of data (\(20\)), use the mean of the two middle values. Visualise the cumulative frequency: the \(10^{\text{th}}\) and \(11^{\text{th}}\) dots both sit in the “2 calculators” column.
Answer: Median \(=2\).
Step 31M1AThe inter-quartile range

Lower quartile \(Q_1\) = value at position \(\frac{20+1}{4}=5.25\), i.e. the \(5^{\text{th}}\) value, which is \(1\).

Upper quartile \(Q_3\) = value at position \(\frac{3(20+1)}{4}=15.75\), i.e. the \(16^{\text{th}}\) value.

The first \(8+5=13\) values are \(\le 2\), and the next \(6\) are \(3\), so the \(16^{\text{th}}\) value is \(3\).

\[ \text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1 = 3-1 = 2 .\]
Hint: In the cumulative-frequency picture, \(Q_1\) is where the curve crosses \(25\%\) and \(Q_3\) where it crosses \(75\%\). Rounding the position (HKDSE convention) sends \(5.25\to 5^{\text{th}}\) value and \(15.75\to 16^{\text{th}}\) value.
Answer: Inter-quartile range \(=2\).
Step 41AThe variance

Use \(\text{Variance}=\dfrac{\sum f x^2}{\sum f}-\bar{x}^{2}\).

\[ \sum f x^2 = 1^2(8)+2^2(5)+3^2(6)+4^2(1) = 8+20+54+16 = 98 .\] \[ \bar{x}=2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bar{x}^2 = 4 .\] \[ \text{Variance} = \frac{98}{20}-4 = 4.9-4 = 0.9 .\]
Hint: Do not forget to square the values before multiplying by their frequencies. A quick check: the variance must be non-negative and less than the spread of the data (\(\le 4\) here).
Answer: Variance \(=0.9\).

Part (b) — Does the range change? 2 marks

Active students 18 · Remaining sum 36 · New mean 2.00 · New range 3 · Minimum 1 · Maximum 4
Step 11MNote the original range and list the possibilities

Originally the data set contains values \(1,2,3,4\), so

\[ \text{Original range}=4-1=3 .\]

The total sum is \(1(8)+2(5)+3(6)+4(1)=40\) with \(20\) students. After two students withdraw, the mean is unchanged (still \(2\)), so their two values must have sum \(4\).

Possible pairs (with values restricted to \(1,2,3,4\)) that sum to \(4\):

Case 1: \(2+2\) (both own \(2\) calculators); Case 2: \(1+3\) (or \(3+1\) — one owns \(1\), the other owns \(3\)).

Hint: “Mean unchanged” means the two students removed must have a mean of \(2\) → their sum is \(4\). Listing the possible pairs is the key move.
Step 21MCheck the new range in each case

Case 1 (both own \(2\)): the minimum is still \(1\) and the maximum is still \(4\), so range \(=4-1=3\) — unchanged.

Case 2 (one owns \(1\), one owns \(3\)): there are still students with \(1,2\) and \(4\) calculators (and \(3\) calculators remain too, since \(n=6>1\)), so the minimum is still \(1\) and the maximum is still \(4\): range \(=4-1=3\) — unchanged.

Hint: Removing a value does not change the range unless you remove all occurrences of the current minimum or maximum. Here the classes of \(1\) and \(4\) still contain members, so the extremes survive.
Step 31AConclusion

In both possible cases the range of the distribution is still \(3\).

Hint: To earn the “A” mark, state the conclusion explicitly and refer back to both cases — do not just say “no change”.
Answer: No, there is no change in the range of the distribution due to the withdrawal of the two students; the range remains \(3\).

Final Answers at a Glance

  • \(n=6\); total number of students \(=20\)
  • Mean \(=2\) (given)
  • Median \(=2\)
  • Inter-quartile range \(=3-1=2\)
  • Variance \(=\dfrac{98}{20}-2^2 = 0.9\)
  • Original range \(=4-1=3\)
  • Withdrawal cases: \((2,2)\) or \((1,3)\) — both keep min \(=1\) and max \(=4\)
  • No change in the range; the range remains \(3\)
