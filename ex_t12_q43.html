Coordinate Geometry (117) · Sub-Topic: Locus

T12 Q43

Find the equations of the two lines that make up the locus of points equidistant from the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis.

Solution

Let a point on the locus be $P(x,y)$.
The distance from $P$ to the $x$-axis is $y$ when $y\ge 0$ and $-y$ when $y<0$; in either case its square is $y^2$.
Similarly the distance from $P$ to the $y$-axis has square $x^2$ ($x$ when $x\ge 0$, $-x$ when $x<0$).
Equidistance gives $y^2=x^2$.
So $x^2-y^2=0$.
Factorizing, $(x-y)(x+y)=0$, hence $y=x$ or $y=-x$.
The equations of the two lines are $y=x$ and $y=-x$.
