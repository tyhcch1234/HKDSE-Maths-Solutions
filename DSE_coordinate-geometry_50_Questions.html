Type 1

Distance, Midpoint and Slope between Two Points · 7 questions

Read a pair of coordinates and use the three basic formulas

Core idea: three formulas do all the work. The distance formula is Pythagoras on the horizontal and vertical gaps; the midpoint formula averages the two \(x\)'s and the two \(y\)'s separately; the slope formula is rise over run. Every later topic — line equations, perpendicularity, circles, locus — is built from these three.

Watch the order of subtraction: \((x_{1},y_{1})\) and \((x_{2},y_{2})\) may be used either way round, but the same order must be kept on the top and on the bottom.

\(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\)\(M=\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2}\right)\)\(m=\dfrac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 2 Q24 ↗

In the rectangular coordinate plane, \(A(1,2)\) and \(B(7,10)\) are two points. Find the length of \(AB\).

Answer\(AB=10\)
  1. \(AB=\sqrt{(7-1)^{2}+(10-2)^{2}}\)
  2. \(=\sqrt{6^{2}+8^{2}}=\sqrt{36+64}\)
  3. \(=\sqrt{100}=10\).
Common mistake

Subtracting in different orders on the two lines, e.g. \((7-1)\) but \((2-10)\). Because both gaps are squared the answer survives here, but the habit breaks the slope formula later.

Q2★☆☆☆3 marks

Let \(P(-3,5)\) and \(Q(7,-1)\). Find (a) the coordinates of the midpoint \(M\) of \(PQ\), (b) the slope of \(PQ\).

Answer(a) \(M(2,2)\)　(b) slope \(=-\dfrac{3}{5}\)
  1. (a) \(x\)-coordinate: \(\dfrac{-3+7}{2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2\); \(y\)-coordinate: \(\dfrac{5+(-1)}{2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2\). So \(M(2,2)\).
  2. (b) \(m=\dfrac{-1-5}{7-(-3)}=\dfrac{-6}{10}=-\dfrac{3}{5}\).
  3. The line falls from left to right, so a negative slope is expected.
Q3★★☆☆4 marks

\(M(5,-1)\) is the midpoint of the line segment joining \(A(2,3)\) and \(B\). Find (a) the coordinates of \(B\), (b) the length of \(AB\).

Answer(a) \(B(8,-5)\)　(b) \(AB=10\)
  1. (a) Let \(B=(x,y)\). Then \(\dfrac{2+x}{2}=5\), so \(x=10-2=8\); and \(\dfrac{3+y}{2}=-1\), so \(y=-2-3=-5\). Hence \(B(8,-5)\).
  2. (b) \(AB=\sqrt{(8-2)^{2}+(-5-3)^{2}}=\sqrt{36+64}\).
  3. \(AB=\sqrt{100}=10\).
  4. Check: \(M\) should be halfway, and \(AM=\sqrt{9+16}=5=\tfrac{1}{2}(10)\). ✔
Common mistake

Halving \(M\)'s coordinates to get \(B\). The midpoint is the average, so \(B=2M-A\), not \(M/2\).

Q4★★☆☆4 marks

The vertices of a triangle are \(A(1,1)\), \(B(5,3)\) and \(C(3,7)\). (a) Show that \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\) and that \(AB=BC\). (b) Hence find the area of \(\triangle ABC\).

Answer(a) product of slopes \(=-1\) and \(AB=BC=2\sqrt{5}\)　(b) area \(=10\) square units
  1. (a) Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{3-1}{5-1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\); slope of \(BC=\dfrac{7-3}{3-5}=\dfrac{4}{-2}=-2\).
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{2}\times(-2)=-1\), so \(AB\perp BC\) and \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\).
  3. \(AB=\sqrt{4^{2}+2^{2}}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}\) and \(BC=\sqrt{(-2)^{2}+4^{2}}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}\). So \(AB=BC\).
  4. (b) The two perpendicular sides are the base and the height: area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(2\sqrt{5})(2\sqrt{5})=\tfrac{1}{2}(20)=10\) square units.
  5. Check with the shoelace formula: \(\tfrac{1}{2}\left|1(3-7)+5(7-1)+3(1-3)\right|=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|-4+30-6\right|=10\). ✔
Q5★★☆☆4 marks

Three points are \(A(2,-1)\), \(B(5,5)\) and \(C(9,13)\). Show that \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are collinear.

AnswerSlope \(AB=\) slope \(BC=2\), and \(B\) is common, so the three points lie on one straight line
  1. Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{5-(-1)}{5-2}=\dfrac{6}{3}=2\).
  2. Slope of \(BC=\dfrac{13-5}{9-5}=\dfrac{8}{4}=2\).
  3. The two segments share the point \(B\) and have the same slope, so \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) lie on a single straight line.
  4. Extra check: the equation of the line is \(y=2x-5\), and \(2(9)-5=13\), which is the \(y\)-coordinate of \(C\). ✔
Common mistake

Proving \(AB+BC=AC\) with decimals. Slopes are exact; use the slope test (or show all three points satisfy one line equation).

Q6★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q24 ↗

Let \(P(2,5)\) and \(Q(8,1)\). The point \(R\) lies on the \(x\)-axis and is equidistant from \(P\) and \(Q\). (a) Find the coordinates of \(R\). (b) Hence find the common distance \(RP=RQ\), leaving your answer in surd form.

Answer(a) \(R(3,0)\)　(b) \(RP=RQ=\sqrt{26}\)
  1. (a) A point on the \(x\)-axis is \(R(x,0)\). Equal distances means \(RP^{2}=RQ^{2}\).
  2. \((x-2)^{2}+(0-5)^{2}=(x-8)^{2}+(0-1)^{2}\)
  3. \(x^{2}-4x+4+25=x^{2}-16x+64+1\)
  4. \(-4x+29=-16x+65\), so \(12x=36\) and \(x=3\). Hence \(R(3,0)\).
  5. (b) \(RP=\sqrt{(3-2)^{2}+(0-5)^{2}}=\sqrt{1+25}=\sqrt{26}\).
  6. Check: \(RQ=\sqrt{(3-8)^{2}+(0-1)^{2}}=\sqrt{25+1}=\sqrt{26}\). ✔ (\(\sqrt{26}\approx 5.10\))
Q7★★★☆7 marks

Let \(A(-2,3)\) and \(B(10,-3)\). (a) Find the coordinates of the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\). (b) \(P\) is a point on the \(y\)-axis such that \(PA=PB\). Find the coordinates of \(P\). (c) Describe the locus of all points equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\) by giving its equation.

Answer(a) \(M(4,0)\)　(b) \(P(0,-8)\)　(c) \(y=2x-8\), i.e. \(2x-y-8=0\)
  1. (a) \(M=\left(\dfrac{-2+10}{2},\dfrac{3+(-3)}{2}\right)=\left(\dfrac{8}{2},\dfrac{0}{2}\right)=M(4,0)\).
  2. (b) Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{-3-3}{10-(-2)}=\dfrac{-6}{12}=-\dfrac{1}{2}\), so the perpendicular bisector has slope \(2\).
  3. Through \(M(4,0)\): \(y-0=2(x-4)\), i.e. \(y=2x-8\).
  4. \(P\) is on the \(y\)-axis, so put \(x=0\): \(y=-8\), giving \(P(0,-8)\).
  5. Check: \(PA=\sqrt{2^{2}+11^{2}}=\sqrt{125}\) and \(PB=\sqrt{10^{2}+5^{2}}=\sqrt{125}\). ✔
  6. (c) The locus of points equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\) is exactly the perpendicular bisector found above: \(y=2x-8\).
Common mistake

Using slope \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\) for the bisector. A perpendicular bisector needs the negative reciprocal, \(+2\).

Type 2

Equations of Straight Lines · 7 questions

Point-slope, two-point and general form; intercepts

Core idea: one point plus one slope fixes a straight line. Write \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\), then tidy into the general form \(Ax+By+C=0\) with integer coefficients if possible. With two points, compute the slope first and then use either point — the answer is the same.

Two shortcuts worth memorising: the intercept form \(\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1\) for a line cutting the axes at \((a,0)\) and \((0,b)\); and "read off" the slope from \(Ax+By+C=0\) as \(m=-\frac{A}{B}\).

\(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\)\(\dfrac{x}{a}+\dfrac{y}{b}=1\)\(Ax+By+C=0,\ m=-\dfrac{A}{B}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the equation of the straight line passing through \((2,3)\) with slope \(4\), giving your answer in the form \(Ax+By+C=0\).

Answer\(4x-y-5=0\)
  1. Point-slope form: \(y-3=4(x-2)\).
  2. \(y-3=4x-8\), so \(y=4x-5\).
  3. Rearranged: \(4x-y-5=0\).
  4. Check: put \(x=2\), then \(4(2)-5=3\), which is the given \(y\). ✔
Common mistake

Writing \(y+3=4(x+2)\). The formula subtracts the point's coordinates, so \((2,3)\) gives \(y-3\) and \(x-2\).

Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

Find the equation of the straight line through \(A(-1,4)\) and \(B(3,-4)\).

Answer\(2x+y-2=0\) (i.e. \(y=-2x+2\))
  1. Slope \(m=\dfrac{-4-4}{3-(-1)}=\dfrac{-8}{4}=-2\).
  2. Using \(A(-1,4)\): \(y-4=-2(x+1)\).
  3. \(y-4=-2x-2\), so \(y=-2x+2\), i.e. \(2x+y-2=0\).
  4. Check with \(B(3,-4)\): \(2(3)+(-4)-2=0\). ✔
Q10★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 2 Q25 ↗

The straight line \(L\) has equation \(3x+4y-12=0\). (a) Find the \(x\)-intercept and the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\). (b) Find the slope of \(L\). (c) Hence find the area of the triangle that \(L\) cuts off from the first quadrant.

Answer(a) \(x\)-intercept \(4\), \(y\)-intercept \(3\)　(b) \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\)　(c) \(6\) square units
  1. (a) Put \(y=0\): \(3x=12\), so \(x=4\); the \(x\)-intercept is \(4\). Put \(x=0\): \(4y=12\), so \(y=3\); the \(y\)-intercept is \(3\).
  2. (b) Make \(y\) the subject: \(4y=-3x+12\), so \(y=-\dfrac{3}{4}x+3\) and the slope is \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\).
  3. (c) The intercepts give a right-angled triangle with legs \(4\) and \(3\): area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(4)(3)=6\) square units.
Common mistake

Calling the \(x\)-intercept \(-4\). Substituting \(y=0\) gives a positive \(x\) here; do the arithmetic instead of reading the constant term.

Q11★★☆☆4 marks

A straight line has \(x\)-intercept \(6\) and \(y\)-intercept \(-4\). Find its equation in the form \(Ax+By+C=0\).

Answer\(2x-3y-12=0\)
  1. Intercept form: \(\dfrac{x}{6}+\dfrac{y}{-4}=1\).
  2. Multiply by \(12\): \(2x-3y=12\).
  3. So \(2x-3y-12=0\).
  4. Check: \(x=6,\ y=0 \Rightarrow 12-0-12=0\) ✔ and \(x=0,\ y=-4 \Rightarrow 0+12-12=0\). ✔
Q12★★☆☆5 marks

The line \(L_{1}\) has equation \(2x+5y=10\). The line \(L_{2}\) passes through the point \(P(4,-1)\) and has the same \(x\)-intercept as \(L_{1}\). Find the equation of \(L_{2}\).

Answer\(x-y-5=0\) (i.e. \(y=x-5\))
  1. \(x\)-intercept of \(L_{1}\): put \(y=0\), then \(2x=10\) and \(x=5\). So the point is \((5,0)\).
  2. Slope of \(L_{2}\) through \((5,0)\) and \(P(4,-1)\): \(m=\dfrac{0-(-1)}{5-4}=1\).
  3. Point-slope form: \(y-0=1(x-5)\), so \(y=x-5\).
  4. Rearranged: \(x-y-5=0\).
  5. Check \(P(4,-1)\): \(4-(-1)-5=0\). ✔
Common mistake

Taking the \(x\)-intercept of \(2x+5y=10\) as \(10\). Divide by the coefficient of \(x\): \(x=5\).

Q13★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 2 Q26 ↗

It is given that the straight line \(L: y=mx+c\) passes through \(A(1,7)\) and \(B(4,1)\). (a) Find \(m\) and \(c\). (b) Determine whether the point \(C(7,-5)\) lies on \(L\). (c) Find the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\).

Answer(a) \(m=-2,\ c=9\)　(b) yes, \(C\) lies on \(L\)　(c) \(\dfrac{9}{2}\)
  1. (a) \(m=\dfrac{1-7}{4-1}=\dfrac{-6}{3}=-2\). Using \(A(1,7)\): \(7=-2(1)+c\), so \(c=9\). Hence \(y=-2x+9\).
  2. (b) Put \(x=7\): \(y=-14+9=-5\), which equals the \(y\)-coordinate of \(C\). So \(C(7,-5)\) lies on \(L\).
  3. (c) Put \(y=0\): \(0=-2x+9\), so \(x=\dfrac{9}{2}=4.5\). The \(x\)-intercept is \(\dfrac{9}{2}\).
  4. Check: \(m=-2\) is negative and both points satisfy \(y=-2x+9\) \(\Rightarrow\) consistent.
Q14★★★☆7 marks

Let \(A(2,5)\) and \(B(6,-1)\), and let \(O\) be the origin. \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\). (a) Find the equation of \(AB\) in the form \(Ax+By+C=0\). (b) Find the equation of the line \(OM\). (c) Find the area of \(\triangle OAB\).

Answer(a) \(3x+2y-16=0\)　(b) \(x-2y=0\)　(c) \(16\) square units
  1. (a) Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{-1-5}{6-2}=\dfrac{-6}{4}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\). Using \(A(2,5)\): \(y-5=-\dfrac{3}{2}(x-2)\).
  2. Multiply by \(2\): \(2y-10=-3x+6\), so \(3x+2y-16=0\).
  3. (b) \(M=\left(\dfrac{2+6}{2},\dfrac{5+(-1)}{2}\right)=(4,2)\). The line \(OM\) passes through \((0,0)\) and \((4,2)\), so its slope is \(\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\).
  4. Hence \(y=\dfrac{1}{2}x\), i.e. \(x-2y=0\).
  5. (c) Area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|x_{A}y_{B}-x_{B}y_{A}\right|=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|2(-1)-6(5)\right|=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|-32\right|=16\) square units.
  6. Check by the shoelace formula on \((0,0),(2,5),(6,-1)\): \(\tfrac{1}{2}\left|0(5)+2(-1)+6(0)-0(2)-5(6)-(-1)(0)\right|=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|-32\right|=16\). ✔
Common mistake

Using \(\frac{1}{2}\,AB\cdot OM\) as the area. \(OM\) is a median, not an altitude — either use the shoelace formula or drop a perpendicular.

Type 3

Parallel and Perpendicular Lines · 7 questions

Equal slopes for parallel, product \(-1\) for perpendicular

Core idea: parallel lines have the same slope; perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\), i.e. \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\). So a line perpendicular to one of slope \(\frac{a}{b}\) has slope \(-\frac{b}{a}\) — flip it and change the sign.

Vertical lines are the exception: a line \(x=k\) has undefined slope and is perpendicular to every horizontal line \(y=c\). If a question involves a vertical side, argue with "vertical \(\perp\) horizontal" instead of slopes.

\(m_{1}=m_{2}\ \Leftrightarrow\ L_{1}\parallel L_{2}\)\(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\ \Leftrightarrow\ L_{1}\perp L_{2}\)\(m_{\perp}=-\dfrac{1}{m}\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the slope of a straight line that is perpendicular to the line \(y=3x+1\).

Answer\(-\dfrac{1}{3}\)
  1. The given line has slope \(3\).
  2. For perpendicular lines \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\), so the required slope is \(-\dfrac{1}{3}\).
Common mistake

Answering \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) or \(-3\). Perpendicular means negative reciprocal: flip and change sign.

Q16★☆☆☆3 marks

Find the equation of the straight line passing through \((2,-3)\) and parallel to the line \(y=2x+5\).

Answer\(y=2x-7\) (i.e. \(2x-y-7=0\))
  1. Parallel lines have the same slope, so the required slope is \(2\).
  2. Point-slope form: \(y-(-3)=2(x-2)\).
  3. \(y+3=2x-4\), so \(y=2x-7\).
  4. Check: \(x=2 \Rightarrow y=-3\). ✔
Q17★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q26 ↗

Find the equation of the straight line passing through \((4,1)\) and perpendicular to the line \(2x+3y-6=0\).

Answer\(3x-2y-10=0\)
  1. Rewrite the given line: \(3y=-2x+6\), so \(y=-\dfrac{2}{3}x+2\) and its slope is \(-\dfrac{2}{3}\).
  2. Required slope \(=-\dfrac{1}{-2/3}=\dfrac{3}{2}\).
  3. Point-slope form: \(y-1=\dfrac{3}{2}(x-4)\).
  4. Multiply by \(2\): \(2y-2=3x-12\), so \(3x-2y-10=0\).
  5. Check: \(3(4)-2(1)-10=0\). ✔

Taking the slope of \(2x+3y-6=0\) as \(2\). Isolate \(y\) first; the slope is \(-\frac{A}{B}=-\frac{2}{3}\).

Q18★★☆☆4 marks

The lines \(L_{1}: 4x-6y+5=0\) and \(L_{2}: 3x+2y-1=0\) are given. Show that \(L_{1}\perp L_{2}\).

  2. For \(L_{2}\): \(2y=-3x+1\), so \(y=-\dfrac{3}{2}x+\dfrac{1}{2}\); slope \(=-\dfrac{3}{2}\).
  3. \(\dfrac{2}{3}\times\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=-1\), therefore \(L_{1}\perp L_{2}\).
Q19★★☆☆5 marks

The vertices of \(\triangle ABC\) are \(A(-2,-1)\), \(B(3,1)\) and \(C(1,6)\). (a) Show that \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\). (b) Hence find the area of \(\triangle ABC\).

Answer(a) slope \(AB=\dfrac{2}{5}\), slope \(BC=-\dfrac{5}{2}\), product \(=-1\)　(b) area \(=14.5\) square units
  1. (a) Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{1-(-1)}{3-(-2)}=\dfrac{2}{5}\).
  2. Slope of \(BC=\dfrac{6-1}{1-3}=\dfrac{5}{-2}=-\dfrac{5}{2}\).
  3. \(\dfrac{2}{5}\times\left(-\dfrac{5}{2}\right)=-1\), so \(AB\perp BC\) and \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\).
  4. (b) \(AB=\sqrt{5^{2}+2^{2}}=\sqrt{29}\) and \(BC=\sqrt{(-2)^{2}+5^{2}}=\sqrt{29}\).
  5. Area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{29})(\sqrt{29})=\dfrac{29}{2}=14.5\) square units.

Multiplying \(AB\) by \(AC\) for the area. Only the two perpendicular sides, \(AB\) and \(BC\), form base and height.

The line \(L\) has equation \(3x+4y-24=0\). (a) Find the slope of \(L\). (b) Find the equation of the line through the origin \(O\) perpendicular to \(L\). (c) Let \(N\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(O\) to \(L\). Find the coordinates of \(N\).

  4. Substitute: \(3x+4\left(\dfrac{4}{3}x\right)=24 \Rightarrow 3x+\dfrac{16}{3}x=24 \Rightarrow \dfrac{25}{3}x=24\), so \(x=\dfrac{72}{25}\) and \(y=\dfrac{4}{3}\cdot\dfrac{72}{25}=\dfrac{96}{25}\).
  5. Hence \(N\left(\dfrac{72}{25},\dfrac{96}{25}\right)\approx(2.88,3.84)\).
  6. Check: \(ON=\sqrt{2.88^{2}+3.84^{2}}=\sqrt{8.2944+14.7456}=\sqrt{23.04}=4.8=\dfrac{24}{5}\), which is the perpendicular distance from \(O\) to \(L\). ✔

Type 4

Intersection of Lines and Area from Vertices · 7 questions

Solve simultaneously, then use the vertex area formula

Core idea: two straight lines meet at the single point that satisfies both equations — solve them simultaneously. That point is often a vertex of a triangle, so intersection and area questions come as a pair: find the three vertices first, then apply the area formula.

For \(A(x_{1},y_{1})\), \(B(x_{2},y_{2})\), \(C(x_{3},y_{3})\) the signed "shoelace" sum below gives twice the area; take the numerical value. When one side is horizontal or vertical, \(\tfrac{1}{2}\times\)base\(\times\)height is quicker.

Equations of Circles: Centre, Radius and Completing the Square · 7 questions

Standard form, general form and completing the square

Core idea: a circle is the set of points at a fixed distance \(r\) from a fixed centre \((h,k)\), which gives the standard form. Expanding it gives the general form \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\), where the coefficients of \(x^{2}\) and \(y^{2}\) are equal (both \(1\) after division) and there is no \(xy\) term.

To go backwards, complete the square twice: centre \(=\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)\) and \(r=\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{4}+\frac{E^{2}}{4}-F}\). A circle exists only if that expression is positive.

\((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\)\(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\)\(\text{centre}\left(-\tfrac{D}{2},-\tfrac{E}{2}\right),\ r=\sqrt{\tfrac{D^{2}+E^{2}}{4}-F}\)

Tangent to a Circle, Distance from a Point to a Line, and Locus · 7 questions

One formula links tangency, perpendicular distance and locus

Core idea: the perpendicular distance from a point to a line measures how far a point is from a line, and it settles every tangency question. A line is a tangent to a circle exactly when the perpendicular distance from the centre to the line equals the radius; if the distance is smaller the line is a chord, if larger it misses the circle.

A locus question is just a description written as an equation: "equidistant from two points" gives the perpendicular bisector, "a fixed distance from a point" gives a circle, and \(PA^{2}+PB^{2}=k\) also gives a circle after completing the square.

\(d=\dfrac{|Ax_{0}+By_{0}+C|}{\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}}\)\(d=r\ \Leftrightarrow\ \text{tangent}\)\(\text{tangent}\perp\text{radius at the point of contact}\)

Trying to divide by zero for the slope of \(C_{1}B\). A vertical line has undefined slope; state "vertical" and use "horizontal \(\perp\) vertical".

Q46★★☆☆6 marks

The lines \(L_{1}: 2x+y-8=0\) and \(L_{2}: x-2y+1=0\) intersect at \(P\). (a) Find the coordinates of \(P\). (b) Show that \(L_{1}\perp L_{2}\). (c) \(L_{1}\) and \(L_{2}\) cut the \(y\)-axis at \(A\) and \(B\) respectively. Find the area of \(\triangle PAB\).

Answer(a) \(P(3,2)\)　(b) slopes \(-2\) and \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) give product \(-1\)　(c) \(11.25\) square units
  1. (a) From \(L_{1}\): \(y=8-2x\). Substitute into \(L_{2}\): \(x-2(8-2x)+1=0\).
  2. \(x-16+4x+1=0 \Rightarrow 5x=15 \Rightarrow x=3\), and \(y=8-6=2\). So \(P(3,2)\).
  3. (b) \(L_{1}: y=-2x+8\), slope \(-2\); \(L_{2}: y=\dfrac{1}{2}x+\dfrac{1}{2}\), slope \(\dfrac{1}{2}\). Product \(=-1\), so \(L_{1}\perp L_{2}\).
  4. (c) On the \(y\)-axis (\(x=0\)): \(A(0,8)\) and \(B\left(0,\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\), so \(AB=8-\dfrac{1}{2}=\dfrac{15}{2}\).
  5. The perpendicular height from \(P\) to the \(y\)-axis is the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\), namely \(3\).
  6. Area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{15}{2}\cdot 3=\dfrac{45}{4}=11.25\) square units.
Q47★★☆☆7 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 1 Q17 ↗

The circle \(C\) has equation \(x^{2}+y^{2}-6x+2y-15=0\). (a) Find the centre and the radius of \(C\). (b) Show that the point \(A(7,2)\) lies on \(C\). (c) Find the equation of the tangent to \(C\) at \(A\), in the form \(Ax+By+C=0\).

Answer(a) centre \((3,-1)\), radius \(5\)　(b) \(7^{2}+2^{2}-42+4-15=0\)　(c) \(4x+3y-34=0\)
  1. (a) \((x-3)^{2}+(y+1)^{2}=9+1+15=25\). Centre \((3,-1)\), radius \(5\).
  2. (b) \(x^{2}+y^{2}-6x+2y-15=49+4-42+4-15=0\). ✔ So \(A\) lies on \(C\).
  3. (c) Slope of the radius \(CA=\dfrac{2-(-1)}{7-3}=\dfrac{3}{4}\), so the tangent slope is \(-\dfrac{4}{3}\).
  4. \(y-2=-\dfrac{4}{3}(x-7)\)
  5. Multiply by \(3\): \(3y-6=-4x+28 \Rightarrow 4x+3y-34=0\).
  6. Check tangency: \(d=\dfrac{|4(3)+3(-1)-34|}{5}=\dfrac{|-25|}{5}=5=r\). ✔
Common mistake

Using the radius slope \(\frac{3}{4}\) for the tangent. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

Q48★★★☆8 marks

\(A(-2,1)\), \(B(6,5)\) and \(C(5,-3)\) are three points. (a) Find the area of \(\triangle ABC\). (b) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\). (c) Show that \(C\) lies on this perpendicular bisector, and hence state what can be concluded about \(CA\) and \(CB\).

Answer(a) \(30\) square units　(b) \(2x+y-7=0\)　(c) \(C\) satisfies the equation, so \(CA=CB=\sqrt{65}\)
  1. (a) Area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|-2(5-(-3))+6(-3-1)+5(1-5)\right|=\tfrac{1}{2}\left|-16-24-20\right|=30\) square units.
  2. (b) Midpoint of \(AB=\left(\dfrac{-2+6}{2},\dfrac{1+5}{2}\right)=(2,3)\).
  3. Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{5-1}{6-(-2)}=\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}\), so the perpendicular slope is \(-2\).
  4. \(y-3=-2(x-2) \Rightarrow y=-2x+7\), i.e. \(2x+y-7=0\).
  5. (c) Put \(C(5,-3)\): \(2(5)+(-3)-7=10-3-7=0\). ✔ So \(C\) lies on the perpendicular bisector.
  6. Every point on a perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the two ends, so \(CA=CB\).
  7. Check: \(CA=\sqrt{7^{2}+4^{2}}=\sqrt{65}\) and \(CB=\sqrt{1^{2}+8^{2}}=\sqrt{65}\). ✔ \(\triangle ABC\) is isosceles.
Common mistake

Using \(AB\) as the base with \(C\)'s \(y\)-coordinate as the height. \(AB\) is not horizontal; use the vertex formula or split the triangle.

Q49★★★☆8 marks

The circle \(C\) has equation \(x^{2}+y^{2}=25\) and the line \(L\) has equation \(3x+4y=15\). (a) Show that \(L\) cuts \(C\) at two points, and find their coordinates. (b) Find the length of the chord that \(L\) cuts off from \(C\). (c) Find the area of the triangle formed by the origin \(O\) and the two points of intersection.

Answer(a) \((5,0)\) and \(\left(-\dfrac{7}{5},\dfrac{24}{5}\right)\)　(b) \(8\)　(c) \(12\) square units
  1. Distance from \(O(0,0)\) to \(L\): \(d=\dfrac{|3(0)+4(0)-15|}{5}=3\). Since \(d=3<5=r\), \(L\) meets \(C\) twice.
  2. (a) \(y=\dfrac{15-3x}{4}\). Substituting into \(x^{2}+y^{2}=25\): \(x^{2}+\dfrac{(15-3x)^{2}}{16}=25\).
  3. \(16x^{2}+225-90x+9x^{2}=400 \Rightarrow 25x^{2}-90x-175=0 \Rightarrow 5x^{2}-18x-35=0\).
  4. Factorise: \((x-5)(5x+7)=0\), so \(x=5\) or \(x=-\dfrac{7}{5}\).
  5. Then \(y=\dfrac{15-15}{4}=0\), or \(y=\dfrac{15+\frac{21}{5}}{4}=\dfrac{96}{20}=\dfrac{24}{5}\). Points: \((5,0)\) and \(\left(-\dfrac{7}{5},\dfrac{24}{5}\right)\).
  6. (b) Half-chord \(=\sqrt{r^{2}-d^{2}}=\sqrt{25-9}=4\), so the chord is \(2\times 4=8\) units.
  7. Check with the coordinates: \(\sqrt{\left(5+\tfrac{7}{5}\right)^{2}+\left(0-\tfrac{24}{5}\right)^{2}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{1024}{25}+\tfrac{576}{25}}=\sqrt{64}=8\). ✔
  8. (c) The triangle has base \(8\) (the chord) and height \(d=3\), so area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(8)(3)=12\) square units.
Q50★★★☆8 marks

The circle \(C\) has equation \(x^{2}+y^{2}=25\) and \(P(13,0)\) is a point outside \(C\). (a) Show that \(P\) lies outside \(C\). (b) Find the length of a tangent from \(P\) to \(C\). (c) Find the equations of the two tangents from \(P\) to \(C\).

Answer(a) \(OP=13>5\)　(b) \(12\)　(c) \(5x-12y-65=0\) and \(5x+12y-65=0\)
  1. (a) \(OP=13\) and the radius is \(5\). Since \(13>5\), \(P\) is outside \(C\).
  2. (b) If \(T\) is the point of contact, \(\angle OTP=90^{\circ}\), so \(PT^{2}=OP^{2}-r^{2}=169-25=144\) and \(PT=12\).
  3. (c) Let a tangent be \(y=m(x-13)\), i.e. \(mx-y-13m=0\).
  4. Tangency: \(\dfrac{|m(0)-0-13m|}{\sqrt{m^{2}+1}}=5 \Rightarrow \dfrac{169m^{2}}{m^{2}+1}=25\).
  5. \(169m^{2}=25m^{2}+25 \Rightarrow 144m^{2}=25 \Rightarrow m=\pm\dfrac{5}{12}\).
  6. With \(m=\dfrac{5}{12}\): \(y=\dfrac{5}{12}(x-13) \Rightarrow 12y=5x-65 \Rightarrow 5x-12y-65=0\).
  7. With \(m=-\dfrac{5}{12}\): \(y=-\dfrac{5}{12}(x-13) \Rightarrow 12y=-5x+65 \Rightarrow 5x+12y-65=0\).
  8. Check: distance from \(O\) to \(5x-12y-65=0\) is \(\dfrac{65}{13}=5=r\). ✔
Common mistake

Assuming the tangents cannot be vertical, or losing one sign. Every tangent through an external point has a slope here, but the squared equation must be solved for both roots.
