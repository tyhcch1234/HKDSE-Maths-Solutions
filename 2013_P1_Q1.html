Laws of Indices
Question 3 marks

Simplify $\dfrac{x^{20}y^{13}}{(x^5y)^6}$ and express your answer with positive indices.

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Expand the Denominator

Apply the Power of a Product law: $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$

$$\dfrac{x^{20}y^{13}}{(x^5y)^6} = \dfrac{x^{20}y^{13}}{(x^5)^6 \cdot y^6}$$
💡 Hint: The exponent 6 applies to both $x^5$ and $y$ inside the bracket.
2

Simplify the Powers

Apply the Power of a Power law: $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$

$$(x^5)^6 = x^{5 \times 6} = x^{30}$$

So the expression becomes:

$$\dfrac{x^{20}y^{13}}{x^{30}y^6}$$
3

Combine & Apply Negative Indices

Use the quotient law: $\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$

$$= x^{20-30} \cdot y^{13-6}$$
$$= x^{-10} \cdot y^{7}$$

Finally, express with positive indices: $a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$

$$\boxed{\dfrac{y^7}{x^{10}}}$$

Final Answer

$$\dfrac{y^7}{x^{10}}$$
