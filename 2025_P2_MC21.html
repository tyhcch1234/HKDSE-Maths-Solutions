Geometry — Area of a Triangle, Pythagoras’ Theorem & Perpendicular Distance
Rectangle Area of Triangle Pythagoras’ Theorem Perpendicular Distance Similar / 3-D Relation

QUESTION The Problem

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rectangle. It is given that \(AE=20\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle AED=90^\circ\). If the area of \(\triangle ADE\) is \(150\text{ cm}^2\), then the perpendicular distance from \(E\) to \(CD\) is

A. \(9\text{ cm}\) B. \(12\text{ cm}\) C. \(15\text{ cm}\) D. \(16\text{ cm}\)

Original Figure

PART A Find \(DE\) and \(AD\) from the Area

DE = 15 cm AD = 25 cm Area = 150 cm²
0 / 4
1Write the area formula for \(\triangle ADE\)

Since \(\angle AED=90^\circ\), the two legs \(AE\) and \(DE\) are perpendicular, so

\[ \text{Area of } \triangle ADE=\tfrac12 \times AE \times DE \]
Hint: The right angle at \(E\) means \(AE\) and \(DE\) are the base and height — no need for trigonometry.
2Substitute the values and solve for \(DE\)
\[ \tfrac12 \times 20 \times DE = 150 \] \[ 10\,DE = 150 \;\Longrightarrow\; DE = 15\text{ cm} \]
\(DE = 15\text{ cm}\)
Hint: Always keep the unit cm — the MCQ options all carry cm.
3Apply Pythagoras’ Theorem in \(\triangle ADE\)
\[ AD^2 = AE^2 + DE^2 \] \[ AD^2 = 20^2 + 15^2 = 400 + 225 = 625 \]
Hint: \(AD\) is the hypotenuse of the right triangle \(AED\), so it is the longest side.
4Take the square root
\[ AD = \sqrt{625} = 25\text{ cm} \]
\(AD = 25\text{ cm}\)
Hint: \(15\text{–}20\text{–}25\) is a scaled \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) Pythagorean triple — recognising it saves time in the exam.

PART B Find the Perpendicular Distance from \(E\) to \(CD\)

EF = 12 cm x = 9 cm distance E→CD = 21 cm
0 / 4
1Find \(EF\), the distance from \(E\) to \(AD\)

Let \(F\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(E\) to \(AD\). Using \(AD\) as base:

\[ \tfrac12 \times AD \times EF = 150 \] \[ \tfrac12 \times 25 \times EF = 150 \]
Hint: Same triangle, same area — just a different choice of base. Area is invariant.
2Solve for \(EF\)
\[ 12.5\,EF = 150 \;\Longrightarrow\; EF = 12\text{ cm} \]
\(EF = 12\text{ cm}\)
Hint: Equivalently, \(EF = \dfrac{AE \times DE}{AD} = \dfrac{20 \times 15}{25} = 12\).
3Relate the required distance \(x\) to \(EF\)

Let \(x\) cm be the perpendicular distance from \(E\) to \(CD\), and let \(G\) be the foot of this perpendicular. Then \(\angle DGE = 90^\circ\) and, by Pythagoras in \(\triangle DGE\),

\[ x^2 + EF^2 = DE^2 \]
Hint: \(EF\) measures the vertical offset from \(E\) to the line \(AD\); \(x\) measures the horizontal offset from \(E\) to the line \(CD\). Together they form the legs of a right triangle with hypotenuse \(DE = 15\).
4Compute \(x\)
\[ x^2 + 12^2 = 15^2 \] \[ x^2 = 225 - 144 = 81 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 9 \]
\(x = 9\text{ cm}\)  →  Answer: A
Hint: \(9\text{–}12\text{–}15\) is again a \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) triple. The perpendicular distance from \(E\) to \(CD\) is \(x = 9\text{ cm}\).

Summary of Results

  • Area relation: \(\tfrac12 \times AE \times DE = 150 \;\Rightarrow\; DE = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}}\)
  • Pythagoras in \(\triangle ADE\): \(AD = \sqrt{20^2+15^2} = \mathbf{25\text{ cm}}\)
  • Altitude to \(AD\): \(\tfrac12 \times 25 \times EF = 150 \;\Rightarrow\; EF = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}\)
  • Distance from \(E\) to \(CD\): \(x = \sqrt{DE^2 - EF^2} = \sqrt{225-144} = \mathbf{9\text{ cm}}\)
  • Correct option: A
