Coordinate Geometry — the in-centre of a right triangle

In-centre Tangent Properties Pythagoras' Theorem Ratio \(p:q\)

Question Multiple Choice

Let \(O\) be the origin. The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((p,\,0)\) and \((0,\,q)\) respectively, where \(p\) and \(q\) are positive numbers. If the in-centre of \(\Delta OPQ\) lies on the straight line \(3x+4y=3p\), then \(p:q=\)

A. \(2:3\) B. \(4:3\) C. \(4:9\) D. \(7:24\)

Part (a) — Setting up the picture Explore

Drag the slider to change the shape of \(\Delta OPQ\). Watch how the in-centre \(I=(r,r)\) and the line \(3x+4y=3p\) move. The in-centre always sits at distance \(r\) from both legs (the axes) — so it lies on \(y=x\).

p = — q = — r = — 3p₁+4q₁ vs 3p

The axes are perpendicular, so the right angle at \(O\) makes \(OUIV\) a square of side \(r\), giving \(I=(r,r)\).

Part (b)(i) — Tangent lengths give \(PQ=p+q-2r\) Method

PU = p − r = — QV = q − r = — PQ = —
0 / 3
Step 1

Let \(I\) be the in-centre and \(r\) the in-radius. Since \(OP\) and \(OQ\) lie along the perpendicular axes, \(IU\perp OP\) and \(IV\perp OQ\) with \(IU=IV=r\). The angle at \(O\) is a right angle.

Hint: A quadrilateral with three right angles and two equal adjacent sides is a square — so \(OUIV\) is a square of side \(r\).
\(I = (r,\;r)\)
Step 2

Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length. From \(P\):

\[ PU = PW = p-r \]

From \(Q\):

\[ QV = QW = q-r \]
Hint: \(P\) touches the incircle at two points — one on \(OP\), one on \(PQ\). Both tangent segments from \(P\) have the same length.
Step 3

The hypotenuse \(PQ\) is split by the point of contact \(W\) into \(PW+QW\):

\[ PQ = PW + QW = (p-r)+(q-r) = p+q-2r \]
Hint: This is the key formula — it lets us write \(PQ\) without square roots.
\(PQ = p+q-2r\)

Part (b)(ii) — In-centre lies on \(3x+4y=3p\) Substitute

I = (r, r) = — 3r + 4r = — 3p = —
0 / 2
Step 1

From Part (b)(i), \(I=(r,r)\). Substitute \(x=r,\ y=r\) into \(3x+4y=3p\):

\[ 3(r)+4(r) = 3p \] \[ 7r = 3p \]
Hint: The in-centre of a right triangle always has equal \(x\) and \(y\) coordinates equal to the in-radius.
\(r = \dfrac{3p}{7}\)
Step 2

Now use Pythagoras on \(\Delta OPQ\) together with \(PQ=p+q-2r\):

\[ OP^2+OQ^2 = PQ^2 \] \[ p^2 + q^2 = \left(p+q-\tfrac{6p}{7}\right)^2 = \left(\tfrac{p}{7}+q\right)^2 \]
Hint: Remember \(2r = \dfrac{6p}{7}\) — substitute this into \(p+q-2r\).

Part (b)(iii) — Solve for \(p:q\) Algebra

Candidates: A 2:3 · B 4:3 · C 4:9 · D 7:24
0 / 4
Step 1

Expand \(\left(\tfrac{p}{7}+q\right)^2\):

\[ p^2+q^2 = \frac{p^2}{49} + \frac{2pq}{7} + q^2 \]
Hint: Use \((a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2\) with \(a=\tfrac{p}{7},\ b=q\).
Step 2

Since \(p,q>0\), divide both sides by \(q^2\) (non-zero) and cancel the \(q^2\) terms:

\[ p^2 = \frac{p^2}{49} + \frac{2pq}{7} \] \[ \frac{48\,p^2}{49} = \frac{2pq}{7} \]
Hint: The \(q^2\) on both sides cancels — this is why the answer is a pure ratio \(p:q\).
Step 3

Divide both sides by \(p\) (since \(p>0\)):

\[ \frac{48p}{49} = \frac{2q}{7} \]
Hint: Cross-multiply: \(48p \cdot 7 = 2q \cdot 49 \Rightarrow 336p = 98q\).
Step 4

Simplify the ratio:

\[ \frac{p}{q} = \frac{2 \times 49}{48 \times 7} = \frac{98}{336} = \frac{7}{24} \]
Hint: Divide numerator and denominator by 14: \(98\div 14 = 7\) and \(336 \div 14 = 24\).
\(p:q = 7:24\)  →  Answer: D

Summary of Answers

  • The in-centre of \(\Delta OPQ\) is \(I=(r,r)\) because the legs lie on the perpendicular axes. (square \(OUIV\))
  • Tangent lengths give \(PU=p-r\) and \(QV=q-r\), so \(PQ = p+q-2r\).
  • Substituting \(I=(r,r)\) into \(3x+4y=3p\): \(\;7r=3p\;\Rightarrow\;r=\dfrac{3p}{7}\).
  • Pythagoras: \(p^2+q^2=\left(\dfrac{p}{7}+q\right)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{48p^2}{49}=\dfrac{2pq}{7}\).
  • \(p:q = 7:24\) — correct option: D
