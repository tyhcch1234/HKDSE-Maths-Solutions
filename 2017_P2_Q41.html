HKDSE 2017 Paper 2 · Q41 · Coordinate Geometry

Triangle OPQ Incircle In-centre I Line 3x+4y=3p Tangency pts
[2017 MC #41]
Let \(O\) be the origin. The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((p,0)\) and \((0,q)\) respectively, where \(p\) and \(q\) are positive numbers. If the in-centre of \(\triangle OPQ\) lies on the straight line \(3x+4y=3p\), then \(p\!:\!q=\)
A. \(2:3\)    B. \(4:3\)    C. \(4:9\)    D. \(7:24\)
Step 1 · Set up the triangle Right triangle with right angle at \(O\). Legs \(OP=p\) (on \(x\)-axis) and \(OQ=q\) (on \(y\)-axis). The hypotenuse is \[ PQ=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}. \]
Step 2 · In-centre of a right triangle Let the in-radius be \(r\). The incircle touches \(OP\) at \(U(p-r,0)\) and \(OQ\) at \(V(0,q-r)\). Since \(OU\perp OQ\) and both tangents are perpendicular to radii, \[ I=(r,r). \] (The in-centre lies on the angle bisector of the right angle, so \(x_I=y_I=r\).)
Tangent lengths: \(PU=p-r\), \(QV=q-r\), so on the hypotenuse \(PQ=(p-r)+(q-r)=p+q-2r\).
Step 3 · Relation from hypotenuse Comparing with \(PQ=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}\): \[ p+q-2r=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}. \] Equivalently, for a right triangle the in-radius is \(r=\dfrac{p+q-\sqrt{p^2+q^2}}{2}\).
Step 4 · Apply the line condition In-centre \(I=(r,r)\) lies on \(3x+4y=3p\): \[ 3r+4r=3p \;\Rightarrow\; r=\frac{3p}{7}. \]
Step 5 · Solve for \(p:q\) Substitute \(r=\dfrac{3p}{7}\) into \(p+q-2r=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}\): \[ p+q-\frac{6p}{7}=\sqrt{p^2+q^2} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{p}{7}+q=\sqrt{p^2+q^2}. \] Square both sides: \[ \frac{p^2}{49}+\frac{2pq}{7}+q^2=p^2+q^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{2pq}{7}=\frac{48p^2}{49}. \] Cancel \(p>0\) and simplify: \[ \frac{2q}{7}=\frac{48p}{49} \;\Rightarrow\; q=\frac{24p}{7} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{p}{q}=\frac{7}{24}. \]
Answer: D  ·  \(p:q=7:24\)
