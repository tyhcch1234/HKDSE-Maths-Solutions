Estimation, Approximation and Error

A packet of cheese is termed regular if its weight is measured as $220\text{ g}$ correct to the nearest $10\text{ g}$. Someone claims that the total weight of $250$ regular packets of cheese can be measured as $53.6\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [3 marks]
1Setup the bounds
A packet measures $220\text{ g}$ correct to the nearest $10\text{ g}$, so $220-5 \le w < 220+5$, i.e. \[ 215\text{ g} \le w < 225\text{ g}. \] For $250$ packets, the total weight $W$ satisfies \[ 250(215) \le W < 250(225) \] \[ 53750\text{ g} \le W < 56250\text{ g}. \]
Multiply the per‑packet bounds by 250 — do not use the rounded value $53.6$ as a bound.
Maximum Absolute Error \(\text{max. absolute error}=\dfrac{\text{scale interval}}{2}\) The mass is given to the nearest \(10\text{ g}\), so half of \(10\text{ g}\) is the largest possible error.
2Compare with the claim
Expressed in kilograms, \[ 53.75\text{ kg} \le W < 56.25\text{ kg}. \] A reading of $53.6\text{ kg}$ correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$ corresponds to the interval \[ 53.55\text{ kg} \le \text{claim} < 53.65\text{ kg}. \] But the smallest possible total is $53.75\text{ kg}$, and \[ 53.75\text{ kg} > 53.65\text{ kg}. \]
The claim's interval lies entirely below the true minimum — the two intervals do not overlap.
Range of a Measured Value \(\text{measured value}-\text{max. abs. error}\ \le\ \text{true value}\ \le\ \text{measured value}+\text{max. abs. error}\) Each \(215\text{ g}\) to \(225\text{ g}\) reading spreads \(5\text{ g}\) either side before the total is taken.
3Conclude
Since every possible total ($W \ge 53.75\text{ kg}$) is larger than the largest value the claim allows ($53.65\text{ kg}$), the reading $53.6\text{ kg}$ is impossible.
Therefore the claim is not correct.
Answer: The lowest possible total weight is $250(0.22-0.005)=53.75\text{ kg} > 53.65\text{ kg}$, so a measured total of $53.6\text{ kg}$ (correct to the nearest $0.1\text{ kg}$) cannot occur. The claim is NOT correct.
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. A carton holds \(120\) identical packets. Each packet is labelled \(12\text{ g}\), correct to the nearest \(0.5\text{ g}\). Find the greatest and the least possible total mass of the packets in the carton.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Max. absolute error \(=\dfrac{0.5}{2}=0.25\text{ g}\), so one packet weighs between \(11.75\text{ g}\) and \(12.25\text{ g}\).
Step 2. Greatest total \(=120\times12.25=1470\text{ g}\).
Step 3. Least total \(=120\times11.75=1410\text{ g}\).
Answer: from \(1410\text{ g}\) to \(1470\text{ g}\)
