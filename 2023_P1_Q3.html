Estimation Approximation Error Rounding Number Sense

HKDSE Math 2023 Paper 1 Q3

Topic: Estimation, Approximation and Error · [3 marks]

A packet of cheese is termed regular if its weight is measured as 220 g correct to the nearest 10 g. Someone claims that the total weight of 250 regular packets of cheese can be measured as 53.6 kg correct to the nearest 0.1 kg. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.

Key idea: rounding intervals — find the smallest and largest possible true value from a rounded measurement.

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

0 / 6 steps revealed
1 Understand what "correct to the nearest 10 g" means ▼

A measurement of \(220\text{ g}\) correct to the nearest \(10\text{ g}\) means the true weight could be up to half of the rounding unit away from 220 g.

Hint (maximum error): Maximum absolute error \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\times(\text{rounding unit})=\dfrac{1}{2}\times 10=5\text{ g}\).

So each regular packet's true weight lies in the interval

\[ 220-5\le w < 220+5 \quad\text{so}\quad 215\text{ g}\le w < 225\text{ g}. \]
2 Find the least possible weight of one packet ▼

From Step 1, the least possible true weight of one regular packet is

\[ w_{\min}=220-5=215\text{ g}. \]
Hint: The maximum error \(5\text{ g}\) is subtracted from the measured value to get the smallest possible weight. (We don't include 225 g as a possible maximum because the rounding was down at 225 g — but using 215 g is enough for this comparison.)
3 Convert grams to kilograms ▼

The claim is in kilograms, so convert:

\[ w_{\min}=215\text{ g}=0.215\text{ kg}. \]
Hint: \(1\text{ kg}=1000\text{ g}\), so divide grams by 1000 to get kg.

Careful: the model answer uses \(0.22-0.005=0.215\text{ kg}\) in step 4. Keep that in mind.

4 Find the least possible total weight of 250 packets ▼

Multiply the least weight of one packet by the number of packets:

\[ W_{\min}=0.215\times 250=53.75\text{ kg}. \]
Hint: \(0.22-0.005=0.215\) (in kg), then multiply by \(250\). This is exactly the first line of the official solution: \((0.22-0.005)\times 250=53.75\text{ kg}\).
5 Compare with the claimed measurement ▼

The claim is that the total weight can be measured as \(53.6\text{ kg}\) correct to the nearest \(0.1\text{ kg}\). That means the true total would be at least

\[ 53.6-0.05=53.55\text{ kg}. \]

But we found the least possible total is \(53.75\text{ kg}\). Since

\[ 53.75\text{ kg} > 53.65\text{ kg}, \]

the smallest possible total is already larger than the largest value that would round to \(53.6\text{ kg}\).

Hint: Any true total weight rounds to \(53.6\text{ kg}\) only if it lies in \([53.55,\,53.65)\text{ kg}\). But our minimum is \(53.75\text{ kg}\) — outside that interval.
6 Conclusion ▼

The total weight of \(250\) regular packets is always at least \(53.75\text{ kg}\), which is greater than \(53.65\text{ kg}\). Therefore the total cannot be measured as \(53.6\text{ kg}\) correct to the nearest \(0.1\text{ kg}\).

The claim is not correct.

✔ Final Answer: The claim is not correct.
Reason: The least possible total weight is \((0.22-0.005)\times 250=53.75\text{ kg} > 53.65\text{ kg}\).
