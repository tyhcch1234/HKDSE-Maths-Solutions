Angle in a Semicircle & Equal Chords

Geometry · Circle Properties · Multiple Choice (Answer C)

QUESTION 2017 MC Q21

Read the figure, then follow the interactive steps below.

In the figure, \(AD\) is a diameter of the circle \(ABCDE\). If \(BC = CD\) and \(\angle ABC = 110^\circ\), then \(\angle BED =\)

A. \(20^\circ\)     B. \(35^\circ\)     C. \(40^\circ\)     D. \(55^\circ\)

110°
\(\angle ABD\) = 90° (∠ in semi-circle)
\(\angle CBD\) = 20°
\(\angle CED\) = 20° (same segment)
\(\angle BEC\) = 20° (equal chords)
\(\angle BED\) = 40°

PART 1 Use the Diameter: \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\)

Because \(AD\) is a diameter, the angle subtended by \(AD\) at any point on the circle is a right angle.

1 Spot the diameter and its subtended angle 1M

\(AD\) is a diameter of the circle, and \(B\) lies on the circle. By the theorem “angle in a semicircle”,

\[ \angle ABD = 90^\circ. \]
Hint: Whenever you see a diameter, immediately mark a \(90^\circ\) angle at any point on the circle connected to both ends of the diameter.
Result: \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\)
0 / 1

PART 2 Find \(\angle CBD = 20^\circ\)

Split \(\angle ABC\) using the right angle we just found.

1 Write \(\angle ABC\) as a sum 1M

From the figure, the ray \(BD\) lies inside \(\angle ABC\), so

\[ \angle ABC = \angle ABD + \angle CBD. \]
Hint: Whenever an angle is split by an internal ray, split it into a sum of the two smaller angles.
2 Substitute and solve 1A

Substituting \(\angle ABC = 110^\circ\) and \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\):

\[ 110^\circ = 90^\circ + \angle CBD \;\Longrightarrow\; \angle CBD = 110^\circ - 90^\circ = 20^\circ. \]
Hint: This is the “difference” step — the two angles \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle ABD\) overlap on \(\angle ABD\), so their difference is \(\angle CBD\).
Result: \(\angle CBD = 20^\circ\)
0 / 2

PART 3 Angles in the Same Segment: \(\angle CED = 20^\circ\)

\(\angle CBD\) and \(\angle CED\) both stand on the same chord \(CD\).

1 Identify the common chord 1M

Both \(\angle CBD\) (vertex \(B\)) and \(\angle CED\) (vertex \(E\)) subtend the same chord \(CD\) from the same side. By the theorem “angles in the same segment are equal”,

\[ \angle CED = \angle CBD = 20^\circ. \]
Hint: Look for two angles whose endpoints are the same pair of points on the circle. If the vertices sit on the same arc, the angles are equal.
Result: \(\angle CED = 20^\circ\)
0 / 1

PART 4 Equal Chords ⇒ Equal Angles: \(\angle BEC = 20^\circ\)

Since \(BC = CD\), the chords are equal — and equal chords subtend equal angles at the circumference.

1 Use equal chords to equal angles 1M

Given \(BC = CD\), the chords \(BC\) and \(CD\) are equal. Angles subtended by equal chords at the circumference are equal, i.e. \(\angle BEC\) (subtended by \(BC\)) equals \(\angle CED\) (subtended by \(CD\)):

\[ \angle BEC = \angle CED = 20^\circ. \]
Hint: Equal chord lengths ⇒ equal arcs ⇒ equal inscribed angles. You can also think of it as the triangle \(\triangle BEC\) being isosceles with \(BC=CD\) … but here the cleanest rule is “equal chords, equal angles”.
Result: \(\angle BEC = 20^\circ\)
0 / 1

PART 5 Sum to Get \(\angle BED = 40^\circ\)

Finally, add the two parts of \(\angle BED\).

1 Split \(\angle BED\) at \(E\) 1M

The ray \(EC\) lies inside \(\angle BED\), so

\[ \angle BED = \angle BEC + \angle CED. \]
Hint: Toggling “Highlight \(\angle BED\)” in the Explore figure shows the two sub-angles side by side.
2 Substitute the values 1A
\[ \angle BED = 20^\circ + 20^\circ = 40^\circ. \]
Final answer: \(\angle BED = 40^\circ\)  →  Option C
0 / 2

Summary of Answers & Reasoning

  • \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\)  — angle in a semicircle (since \(AD\) is a diameter).
  • \(\angle CBD = 110^\circ - 90^\circ = 20^\circ\)  — angle subtraction at \(B\).
  • \(\angle CED = \angle CBD = 20^\circ\)  — angles in the same segment (both subtend chord \(CD\)).
  • \(\angle BEC = \angle CED = 20^\circ\)  — equal chords \(BC = CD\) subtend equal angles.
  • \(\angle BED = \angle BEC + \angle CED = 20^\circ + 20^\circ = \mathbf{40^\circ}\)  — angle sum at \(E\).

Correct choice: C. \(40^\circ\)
