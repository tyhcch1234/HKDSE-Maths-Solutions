Step 1 — Compare the identity $f(x)\equiv(x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)$ with $f(x)=3x^{3}-7x^{2}+kx-8$.

Given polynomial $f(x)$ Factorised form Matched coefficients Discriminant check
[2013 #12]

Let $f(x)=3x^{3}-7x^{2}+kx-8$, where $k$ is a constant. It is given that $f(x)\equiv (x-2)\left(ax^{2}+bx+c\right)$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are constants.

(a) Find $a$, $b$ and $c$.   [4 marks]

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $f(x)=0$ are real numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer.   [3 marks]

1
Step 1 — Equate the two expressions

Since the identity holds for all $x$, expand the right-hand side and match coefficients:

$$(x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx-2ax^{2}-2bx-2c$$ $$=ax^{3}+(b-2a)x^{2}+(c-2b)x-2c$$

Hint: write the expansion in descending powers of $x$ first — every matching term becomes visible.

2
Step 2 — Compare coefficients: find $a$ and $c$

Compare the $x^{3}$-terms: $a = 3$.

Compare the constant terms: $-2c = -8 \;\Rightarrow\; c = 4$.

Hint: the leading and constant coefficients are the fastest to read off — do these two first.

3
Step 3 — Compare $x^{2}$-terms to find $b$

Note the coefficient of $x^{2}$ on the left is $-7$, and on the right (from the expansion) it is $b-2a=b-6$.

$$b-2a=-7 \;\Rightarrow\; b-6=-7 \;\Rightarrow\; b=-1.$$

Hint: keep $k$ untouched — it is the coefficient of $x$, which we do not need for part (a).

4
Step 4 — Result of part (a)

$a=3,\quad b=-1,\quad c=4$.

$$f(x)=(x-2)\left(3x^{2}-x+4\right)$$

Hint: the left-hand coefficient of $x$, namely $k$, must equal $c-2b=4+2=6$ — useful for a quick check.

5
Step 5 — Part (b): how to test the claim

The roots of $f(x)=0$ come from $(x-2)\left(3x^{2}-x+4\right)=0$. One root is obviously $x=2$; the other two (if any) come from the quadratic factor. Test the quadratic factor's discriminant.

$$3x^{2}-x+4=0,\qquad a=3,\; b=-1,\; c=4$$

Hint: a product is zero exactly when one factor is zero, so the claim fails if the quadratic has no real roots.

6
Step 6 — Compute the discriminant of $3x^{2}-x+4$ $$\Delta = b^{2}-4ac = (-1)^{2}-4(3)(4) = 1-48 = -47$$ $$\Delta = -47 < 0$$

Hint: a negative discriminant means the quadratic factor has no real roots.

7
Step 7 — Conclusion

Since $\Delta<0$, the equation $3x^{2}-x+4=0$ has no real roots, so $f(x)=0$ has only one real root, $x=2$.

Thus the claim is disagreed.

Hint: an explanation question needs the reason (negative discriminant $\Rightarrow$ no real roots), not just "No".

Answer (a) $a=3$, $b=-1$, $c=4$.  |  (b) Disagree — $\Delta=(-1)^{2}-4(3)(4)=-47<0$, so $3x^{2}-x+4=0$ has no real roots, hence $f(x)=0$ has non-real roots.
