HKDSE 2013 · Paper 1 Question 12 Polynomials

HKDSE Math 2013 Paper 1 Q12

Identities of polynomials · comparing coefficients · nature of roots (discriminant)

The Question

Let \(f(x)=3x^{3}-7x^{2}+kx-8\), where \(k\) is a constant. It is given that \[f(x)\equiv (x-2)\left(ax^{2}+bx+c\right),\] where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are constants.

(a) Find \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\). 4 marks

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \(f(x)=0\) are real numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer. 3 marks

Polynomial identity Comparing coefficients Factor theorem idea Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) “Do you agree?” – justify

Step-by-Step Solution

The symbol \(\equiv\) means the two expressions are identical for every value of \(x\). Therefore the coefficient of each power of \(x\) on the left must equal the coefficient of the same power of \(x\) on the right.

Key idea: \(3x^{3}-7x^{2}+kx-8\equiv (x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)\)
→ compare \(x^{3}\), \(x^{2}\), \(x^{1}\), and the constant term separately.

Multiply out \((x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)\):

\[ (x-2)(ax^{2}+bx+c)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx-2ax^{2}-2bx-2c \]

Group by powers of \(x\):

\[ =\;ax^{3}+(b-2a)x^{2}+(c-2b)x-2c \]
Hint: compare the expanded form \(\;ax^{3}+(b-2a)x^{2}+(c-2b)x-2c\;\) with the given \(\;3x^{3}-7x^{2}+kx-8\).
coefficient of \(x^{3}\): \(a=3\) 1A
constant term: \(-2c=-8 \Rightarrow c=4\) 1A
Hint: the \(x^{3}\)-term on the left is \(3x^{3}\) and on the right is \(ax^{3}\). The constant term on the left is \(-8\) and on the right is \(-2c\).

On the right, the \(x^{2}\)-coefficient from the expansion is \(b-2a\). With \(a=3\): \(b-6\).

\[ b-6=-7 \;\Rightarrow\; b=-1 \]
\(a=3,\quad b=-1,\quad c=4\) 1M1A
Hint: \(a=3\) was found in Step 3, so \(2a=6\). Then \(b-2a=b-6\).

Using the factorised form with \(a=3,\ b=-1,\ c=4\): \[f(x)=(x-2)(3x^{2}-x+4).\]

So \(f(x)=0\) gives \((x-2)(3x^{2}-x+4)=0\), i.e. \(x=2\) or \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\).

Hint: one root is \(x=2\) (real). The question is whether \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\) also has real roots — use the discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\).

For \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\): \(a=3,\ b=-1,\ c=4\).

\[ \Delta=(-1)^{2}-4(3)(4)=1-48=-47 < 0 \]

Since \(\Delta<0\), the equation \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\) has no real roots (its two roots are non-real / complex conjugates).

Hint: \(\Delta>0\): two distinct real roots; \(\Delta=0\): one repeated real root; \(\Delta<0\): no real roots.

\(f(x)=0\) has a real root \(x=2\) but the other factor gives no real root. Hence not all roots are real — the claim is disagreed.

Disagree — \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\) has no real roots (\(\Delta=-47<0\)). 1M+1A

Visual: Why \(\Delta<0\) means “no real roots”

The graph of \(y=3x^{2}-x+4\) opens upwards and never touches the \(x\)-axis (there is no real solution to \(y=0\)).

\(y=3x^{2}-x+4\)
Vertex: \(x=\dfrac{1}{6},\ y\approx 3.92 > 0\)
\(\Delta=(-1)^2-4(3)(4)=-47<0\)

The roots are \(x=\dfrac{1\pm i\sqrt{47}}{6}\) — non-real. On the real plane the parabola simply stays above the \(x\)-axis.

Answer Summary

PART (a)
\(a=3,\quad b=-1,\quad c=4\)
PART (b)
Disagree. \(f(x)=(x-2)(3x^{2}-x+4)\) and \(\Delta=-47<0\), so \(3x^{2}-x+4=0\) has no real roots.
