Arithmetic & Geometric Sequences · Sum of First n Terms

📐 Arithmetic Sequence 📊 Geometric Sequence 🔢 Sum of \(n\) Terms 📉 Quadratic Inequality
📝 Question

Let \(T(n)\) be the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence such that \(T(1) \neq T(2)\) and \(T(47) = 456\). It is given that \(T(9),\, T(47),\, T(199)\) is a geometric sequence.

(a) Find \(T(1)\). 3 marks

(b) Find the least value of \(n\) such that the sum of the first \(n\) terms of the arithmetic sequence is greater than \(10^6\). 3 marks

🔍 Step-by-step Solution — Part (a)
1
Set up the arithmetic sequence
▼

For an arithmetic sequence with first term \(a\) and common difference \(d\):

\[ T(n) = a + (n-1)d \]
💡 Hint: The general term formula. \(a\) = first term, \(d\) = common difference (how much we add each time).
2
Use \(T(47)=456\) to relate \(a\) and \(d\)
▼

Substitute \(n=47\) into the general term:

\[ T(47) = a + 46d = 456 \quad \text{... (1)} \]
💡 Hint: \(T(47)\) means the 47th term, so the power of \(d\) is \(47-1=46\).
3
Express \(T(9)\) and \(T(199)\) in terms of \(a\) and \(d\)
▼
\[ T(9) = a + 8d \]
\[ T(199) = a + 198d \]
4
Apply the geometric sequence condition
▼

Since \(T(9),\, T(47),\, T(199)\) form a geometric sequence, the ratio of consecutive terms is constant:

\[ \frac{T(47)}{T(9)} = \frac{T(199)}{T(47)} \]
\[ \frac{456}{T(9)} = \frac{T(199)}{456} \]
💡 Hint: Geometric sequence means common ratio between consecutive terms. Also, \((T(47))^2 = T(9) \times T(199)\) — the "middle term squared" property.
5
Substitute & form a quadratic in \(d\)
▼

Using \(T(9)=a+8d\) and \(T(199)=a+198d\):

\[ \frac{456}{a+8d} = \frac{a+198d}{456} \]

But from (1): \(a = 456 - 46d\). So:

\[ \frac{456}{456 - 38d} = \frac{456 + 152d}{456} \]
\[ 456^2 = (456-38d)(456+152d) \]

Expanding and simplifying:

\[ 51984d - 5776d^2 = 0 \]
\[ d(51984 - 5776d) = 0 \]
6
Use \(T(1) \neq T(2)\) to choose \(d\), then find \(T(1)\)
▼

\(T(1) \neq T(2)\) means \(d \neq 0\). So \(d = \frac{51984}{5776} = 9\).

Substitute back into (1):

\[ a + 46(9) = 456 \]
\[ a = 456 - 414 = 42 \]

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{T(1) = 42} \]
✅ Check: \(T(9)=42+8(9)=114\), \(T(47)=456\), \(T(199)=42+198(9)=1824\). Ratio: \(456/114=4\), \(1824/456=4\). ✓

🔍 Step-by-step Solution — Part (b)
1
Recall \(a\) and \(d\) from part (a)
▼

From part (a): \(a = 42\), \(d = 9\). So:

\[ T(n) = 42 + (n-1) \cdot 9 = 9n + 33 \]
2
Write the sum of the first \(n\) terms
▼

The sum of an arithmetic sequence is:

\[ S(n) = \frac{n}{2}\big[2a + (n-1)d\big] \]

Substitute \(a=42\), \(d=9\):

\[ S(n) = \frac{n}{2}\big[2(42) + (n-1)(9)\big] \]
\[ S(n) = \frac{n}{2}(84 + 9n - 9) = \frac{n(9n + 75)}{2} \]
💡 Hint: The arithmetic sum formula. \(\frac{n}{2}(\text{first} + \text{last})\), or \(\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\).
3
Set up the inequality \(S(n) > 10^6\)
▼
\[ \frac{n(9n+75)}{2} > 10^6 \]
\[ 9n^2 + 75n > 2 \times 10^6 \]
\[ 9n^2 + 75n - 2 \times 10^6 > 0 \]
4
Solve the quadratic inequality
▼

Find the roots of \(9n^2 + 75n - 2 \times 10^6 = 0\) using the quadratic formula:

\[ n = \frac{-75 \pm \sqrt{75^2 - 4(9)(-2 \times 10^6)}}{2(9)} \]
\[ n = \frac{-75 \pm \sqrt{5625 + 72 \times 10^6}}{18} \]
\[ n \approx -475.5896 \quad \text{or} \quad n \approx 467.2563 \]
💡 Hint: Since \(9 > 0\), the parabola opens upward. The inequality \(> 0\) holds when \(n\) is outside the roots: \(n < n_1\) or \(n > n_2\).
5
Interpret \(n\) and find the least integer
▼

Since \(n\) is the number of terms, \(n\) must be a positive integer. The condition \(S(n) > 10^6\) holds when:

\[ n > 467.2563 \]

The least positive integer satisfying this is:

\[ \boxed{n = 468} \]
✅ Check: \(S(467) \approx 999{,}891 < 10^6\), but \(S(468) \approx 1{,}004{,}112 > 10^6\). So \(n=468\) is indeed the least.
