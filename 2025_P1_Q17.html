Let $T(n)$ be the $n$th term of an arithmetic sequence such that $T(1)\neq T(2)$ and $T(47)=456$. It is given that $T(9),\,T(47),\,T(199)$ is a geometric sequence.

(a) Find $T(1)$. (3 marks)

(b) Find the least value of $n$ such that the sum of the first $n$ terms of the arithmetic sequence is greater than $10^{6}$. (3 marks)

Part (a) — Find $T(1)$

1Set up the general term
General Term of an Arithmetic Sequence \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\) With first term \(a\) and common difference \(d\), \(T(47)=a+46d=456\).

Let $a$ be the first term and $d$ the common difference of the arithmetic sequence.

$$T(n)=a+(n-1)d$$

Hint: The condition $T(1)\neq T(2)$ tells us $d\neq 0$.
2Write down $T(9)$, $T(47)$, $T(199)$

$$T(9)=a+8d,\qquad T(47)=a+46d,\qquad T(199)=a+198d$$

Hint: These three values form a geometric sequence, so their ratios must be equal.
3Use the geometric condition
Condition for a Geometric Sequence \(b^{2}=ac\) The middle term \(T(47)\) squared equals \(T(9)\) times \(T(199)\), giving \(532d^{2}=114ad\).

Since $T(9),T(47),T(199)$ form a geometric sequence:

$$T(47)^2=T(9)\cdot T(199)$$

$$\frac{a+46d}{a+8d}=\frac{a+198d}{a+46d}$$

Hint: Cross-multiply to turn the ratio condition into a polynomial equation in $d$.
4Solve for $d$

$$(a+46d)^2=(a+8d)(a+198d)$$

$$a^2+92ad+2116d^2=a^2+206ad+1584d^2$$

$$532d^2-114ad=0 \;\Rightarrow\; d(532d-114a)=0$$

Since $d\neq 0$ (as $T(1)\neq T(2)$):

$$a=\frac{532}{114}\,d=\frac{14}{3}\,d \quad\text{or equivalently}\quad 114a=532d$$

Hint: Reject $d=0$, otherwise $T(1)=T(2)$.
5Use $T(47)=456$ to find $T(1)$

$$T(47)=\frac{456}{1}\;\Rightarrow\; a+46d=456$$

Substituting $a=\frac{14}{3}d$:

$$\frac{14}{3}d+46d=456 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{14+138}{3}d=456 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{152}{3}d=456$$

$$d=456\times\frac{3}{152}=9$$

$$a=\frac{14}{3}\times 9=42$$

Hint: $T(1)=a=42$, and the common difference is $d=9$.
Answer (a) $$T(1)=42$$

Part (b) — Least $n$ with $S(n)>10^{6}$

6Write the sum of the first $n$ terms
Sum of the First n Terms of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\left[2a+(n-1)d\right]\) With \(a=42\) and \(d=9\), \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}(75+9n)\) must exceed \(10^{6}\).

With $a=42$ and $d=9$, the sum of the first $n$ terms is

$$S(n)=\frac{n}{2}\bigl[2(42)+(n-1)(9)\bigr]=\frac{n}{2}(75+9n)$$

Hint: Use the arithmetic series formula $S(n)=\frac{n}{2}\bigl[2a+(n-1)d\bigr]$.
7Form the inequality

$$S(n)>10^{6}\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{n}{2}(75+9n)>10^{6}$$

$$9n^2+75n-2\times 10^{6}>0$$

Hint: Multiply both sides by $2$ and rearrange.
8Solve the quadratic inequality

Using the quadratic formula on $9n^2+75n-2\times 10^{6}=0$:

$$n=\frac{-75\pm\sqrt{75^2-4(9)(-2\times 10^{6})}}{2(9)}$$

$$n\approx -475.5896013 \quad\text{or}\quad n\approx 467.256268$$

Hint: Since $n>0$, the inequality $9n^2+75n-2\times10^6>0$ holds when $n>467.256\ldots$
9Choose the least integer $n$

The smallest integer $n$ satisfying $n>467.256\ldots$ is $n=468$.

Check: $S(467)\approx 999{,}729 < 10^{6}$ and $S(468)\approx 1{,}004{,}010>10^{6}$.

Hint: Round the positive root up to the next integer.
Answer (b) The least value of $n$ is $468$.

Question. \(T(n)\) is the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence with \(T(1)\neq T(2)\). It is given that \(T(4)=18\) and that \(T(1),T(4),T(13)\) form a geometric sequence. (a) Find \(T(1)\). (b) Find the least value of \(n\) such that the sum of the first \(n\) terms exceeds \(500\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\); then \(T(4)=a+3d=18\) and \(T(13)=a+12d\).
Step 2. Geometric condition: \((a+3d)^{2}=a(a+12d)\), so \(a^{2}+6ad+9d^{2}=a^{2}+12ad\) and \(9d^{2}=6ad\).
Step 3. Since \(d\neq0\), \(9d=6a\), so \(a=\dfrac{3}{2}d\); then \(\dfrac{3}{2}d+3d=18\) gives \(d=4\) and \(a=6\).
Step 4. \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\left[12+4(n-1)\right]=2n^{2}+4n\); \(S(14)=448\) and \(S(15)=510\).
Step 5. So the least value is \(n=15\).
Answer: (a) \(T(1)=6\) (b) \(n=15\)

Overall answers: (a) $T(1)=42$  ·  (b) $n=468$.
