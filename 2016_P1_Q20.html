2016 P1 Q20 Coordinate Geometry — In-centre & Circumcentre

O (0,0) P (h,19) Q (40,30) I (in-centre) Circle C Tangents L₁, L₂ S,T,U,V trapezium

△OPQ is an obtuse-angled triangle. Denote the in-centre and the circumcentre of △OPQ by I and J respectively. It is given that P, I and J are collinear.

(a) Prove that OP = PQ.  [3 marks]

(b) A rectangular coordinate system is introduced so that the coordinates of O and Q are (0, 0) and (40, 30) respectively while the y-coordinate of P is 19. Let C be the circle which passes through O, P and Q.

(i) Find the equation of C.

(ii) Let L₁ and L₂ be two tangents to C such that the slope of each tangent is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and the y-intercept of L₁ is greater than that of L₂. L₁ cuts the x-axis and the y-axis at S and T respectively while L₂ cuts the x-axis and the y-axis at U and V respectively. Someone claims that the area of the trapezium STUV exceeds 17 000. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.  [9 marks]

Step 1 · Part (a) — Setup

Since J is the circumcentre of △OPQ, O, P, Q all lie on the circle centred at J, so $JO=JP=JQ$ (radii). Since I is the in-centre, it lies on the angle bisector of $\angle OPQ$, so $\angle IPO=\angle IPQ$.

Given that O, I and J are collinear (with I between O and J, inside the triangle), we have $\angle IPO=\angle JPO$.

Step 2 · Part (a) — Angle chase

In $\triangle JOP$: $JO=JP$ (radii) ⟹ $\angle JOP=\angle JPO$ (base ∠s, isosceles △).

In $\triangle JQP$: $JP=JQ$ (radii) ⟹ $\angle JQP=\angle JPQ$ (base ∠s, isosceles △).

With $\angle JPO=\angle IPO=\angle IPQ=\angle JPQ$, we get $\angle JOP=\angle JQP$.

Step 3 · Part (a) — Congruence ⟹ OP = PQ

In $\triangle JOP$ and $\triangle JQP$:

(i) $JO=JQ$  (radii)
(ii) $\angle JOP=\angle JQP$  (proved)
(iii) $JP=JP$  (common side)

∴ $\triangle JOP \cong \triangle JQP$  (AAS)

Hence $OP=PQ$  (corr. sides, ≅△s)  ∎

Step 4 · Part (b)(i) — Find h and equation of C

Let $P=(h,19)$. By (a), $OP=PQ$:

$$h^2+19^2=(40-h)^2+(30-19)^2$$

$$h^2+361=1600-80h+h^2+121 \;\Rightarrow\; 80h=1360 \;\Rightarrow\; h=17$$

So $P=(17,19)$. Let C be $x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$. C passes through O(0,0) ⟹ $F=0$.

Through Q(40,30): $40D+30E=-2500$. Through P(17,19): $17D+19E=-650$.

Solving: $D=-112,\;E=66$.

Equation of C: $\;x^2+y^2-112x+66y=0$   (centre $(56,-33)$, radius $65$)

Step 5 · Part (b)(ii) — Tangents of slope 3/4

Tangents have the form $y=\dfrac{3}{4}x+c$. Substitute into C:

$$x^2+\left(\tfrac34x+c\right)^2-112x+66\left(\tfrac34x+c\right)=0$$

$$\Rightarrow\;25x^2+(24c-1000)x+16c^2+1056c=0$$

Tangency ⟹ double root ⟹ discriminant $=0$:

$$(24c-1000)^2-4(25)(16c^2+1056c)=0$$

$$16c^2+2400c-15625=0 \;\Rightarrow\; (4c-25)(4c+625)=0$$

$$c=\frac{25}{4}\quad\text{or}\quad c=-\frac{625}{4}$$

Larger y-intercept ⟹ L₁: $y=\dfrac34x+\dfrac{25}{4}$;   L₂: $y=\dfrac34x-\dfrac{625}{4}$.

Step 6 · Part (b)(ii) — The four intercepts

Set $y=0$ and $x=0$ for each line:

$S=\left(-\dfrac{25}{3},\,0\right)$  and  $T=\left(0,\,\dfrac{25}{4}\right)$
$U=\left(\dfrac{625}{3},\,0\right)$  and  $V=\left(0,\,-\dfrac{625}{4}\right)$

Note $STUV$ is a trapezium since the two parallel sides ST and UV both lie on lines of slope $\tfrac34$.

Step 7 · Part (b)(ii) — Area of STUV

Using the surveyor's (shoelace) formula with vertices $S\!\left(-\tfrac{25}{3},0\right),\; T\!\left(0,\tfrac{25}{4}\right),\; U\!\left(\tfrac{625}{3},0\right),\; V\!\left(0,-\tfrac{625}{4}\right)$:

$$\text{Area}=\frac12\left[\frac{625}{3}\cdot\frac{625}{4}+\frac{625}{4}\cdot\frac{25}{3} +\frac{25}{3}\cdot\frac{25}{4}+\frac{25}{4}\cdot\frac{625}{3}\right]$$

$$=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{390625}{12}+\frac{15625}{12}+\frac{625}{12}+\frac{15625}{12}\right] =\frac{105625}{6}$$

Area $=\dfrac{105625}{6}\approx 17604.167>17000$

So the claim is correct.

Hint: alternatively, subtract areas of right triangles from the large triangle, or use $\frac12(\text{sum of parallel sides})\times(\text{distance between them})$.
