Question

Multiple Choice — 4 options

If \( p \) and \( q \) are constants such that \[ x^2 + p \equiv (x+2)(x+q) + 10, \] then \( p = \)

Identity Comparing coefficients Substitution Expansion
🧩

Step-by-step Solution

Step 0 / 5
Step 1 • Understanding the identity

We are given that \( x^2 + p \equiv (x+2)(x+q) + 10 \) for all values of \( x \). This is an identity, meaning both sides are equal for every \( x \).

💡 Hint

An identity holds for all \( x \). We can use this to our advantage — either by substituting a convenient value of \( x \), or by expanding and comparing coefficients.

Step 2 • Expand the right-hand side

Expand \( (x+2)(x+q) + 10 \):

\[ \begin{aligned} (x+2)(x+q) + 10 &= x^2 + qx + 2x + 2q + 10 \\ &= x^2 + (q+2)x + (2q+10) \end{aligned} \]

Key idea: The expanded form is \( x^2 + (q+2)x + (2q+10) \). Now compare with the left-hand side \( x^2 + 0 \cdot x + p \).

Step 3 • Compare coefficients (x-term)

Left-hand side: \( x^2 + 0 \cdot x + p \)   vs   Right-hand side: \( x^2 + (q+2)x + (2q+10) \)

Compare the coefficient of \( x \):

\( 0 = q + 2 \)    \(\Rightarrow\)    \( q = -2 \)

Step 4 • Compare constant terms to find \( p \)

Now compare the constant terms:

\[ p = 2q + 10 \]

Substitute \( q = -2 \):

\[ \begin{aligned} p &= 2(-2) + 10 \\ &= -4 + 10 \\ &= 6 \end{aligned} \]
Step 5 • Alternative (faster) method

Since the identity holds for all \( x \), substitute \( x = -2 \) to make the factor \( (x+2) \) zero:

\[ \begin{aligned} (-2)^2 + p &= (-2+2)(-2+q) + 10 \\ 4 + p &= 0 \cdot (-2+q) + 10 \\ 4 + p &= 10 \\ p &= 6 \end{aligned} \]

🎯 Why this works

By choosing \( x = -2 \), the term \( (x+2) \) becomes 0, eliminating \( q \) entirely. This is often the quickest method in an exam.
