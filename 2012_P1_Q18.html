Trigonometry in 3D — a right pyramid with a square base, sine rule and angles between planes

3D Trigonometry Sine Formula Angle between Planes Angle between Line & Plane Right Pyramid

Q18 The Question

Figure 5(a) shows a right pyramid \(VABCD\) with a square base, where \(\angle VAB = 72^\circ\). The length of a side of the base is \(20\text{ cm}\). Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be the points lying on \(VA\) and \(VD\) respectively such that \(PQ\) is parallel to \(BC\) and \(\angle PBA = 60^\circ\). A geometric model is made by cutting off the pyramid \(VPBCQ\) from \(VABCD\) as shown in Figure 5(b).

(a) Find the length of \(AP\). 2 marks

(b) Let \(\alpha\) be the angle between the plane \(PBCQ\) and the base \(ABCD\).

(i) Find \(\alpha\).  
(ii) Let \(\beta\) be the angle between \(PB\) and the base \(ABCD\). Which one of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) is greater? Explain your answer. 6 marks

Step 11M

Work in the face \(VAB\) (a triangle). Use the sine formula to relate \(AP\), \(AB\) and the known angles.

In \(\triangle APB\), the interior angle at \(A\) is \(\angle PAB = \angle VAB = 72^\circ\), and we are given \(\angle PBA = 60^\circ\).

Hint: First find the third angle \(\angle APB = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 72^\circ = 48^\circ\). Then the sine rule gives a ratio for every side.
Step 21M

Apply the sine formula in \(\triangle APB\):

\[ \frac{AP}{\sin \angle PBA} = \frac{AB}{\sin\angle APB} \] \[ \frac{AP}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{20}{\sin 48^\circ} \]
Hint: Put the unknown \(AP\) in the numerator: \(AP = \dfrac{20\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ}\).
Step 31A

Compute numerically:

\[ AP = \frac{20 \times 0.8660}{0.7431} \approx 23.3070 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: Keep 3 significant figures as required; round only at the end.
Answer: \(AP \approx 23.3\text{ cm}\)

Cross-section through \(T\) (foot of perpendicular from \(P\) to \(BC\)) — the plane angle is \(\angle PTS\).

\(PS =\) 22.17 cm (⊥ to \(AD\))
\(AS =\) 7.20 cm
\(PB =\) 25.60 cm
\(PT =\) 24.56 cm
\(\alpha =\) 58.6°

Step 11M

Let \(S\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(P\) to \(AD\). In right triangle \(APS\):

\[ PS = AP \sin\angle PAD \approx 23.3070 \sin 72^\circ \approx 22.1663 \text{ cm} \] \[ AS = AP \cos\angle PAD \approx 23.3070 \cos 72^\circ \approx 7.2027 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(S\) is the projection of \(P\) onto the plane \(ABCD\) in the direction along the face.
Step 21M

Find \(PB\) using the sine formula in \(\triangle APB\):

\[ \frac{PB}{\sin 72^\circ} = \frac{20}{\sin 48^\circ} \] \[ PB \approx \frac{20\sin 72^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ} \approx 25.5955 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: Now use Pythagoras in \(\triangle PBT\) (right angle at \(T\)).
Step 31M

Let \(T\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(P\) to \(BC\). Then

\[ PT^2 = PB^2 - BT^2 = PB^2 - AS^2 \] \[ PT \approx \sqrt{25.5955^2 - 7.2027^2} \approx 24.5612 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(BT = AS\) because \(ABCD\) is a square and \(P\) lies on the diagonal plane.
Step 41M

Note \(\alpha = \angle PTS\). Use the cosine formula in \(\triangle PST\) (with \(ST = 20\)):

\[ \cos\alpha = \frac{PT^2 + ST^2 - PS^2}{2(PT)(ST)} \] \[ \cos\alpha \approx \frac{24.5612^2 + 20^2 - 22.1663^2}{2(24.5612)(20)} \approx 0.5212 \]
Hint: \(ST = BC = 20\text{ cm}\) because \(PBCQ\) cuts the square base parallel to \(BC\).
Step 51A
\[ \alpha \approx 58.5970^\circ \]
Answer: \(\alpha \approx 58.6^\circ\)

b(ii) Comparing \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)

Right triangle \(PXB\) — the angle between \(PB\) and the base is \(\beta = \angle PBX\).

\(PX =\) 22.17 cm
\(PB =\) 25.60 cm
\(\beta =\) 60.0°
\(\sin\alpha =\) 0.854
\(\sin\beta =\) 0.866
0 / 3
Step 11M

Let \(X\) be the projection of \(P\) onto the base \(ABCD\). Then \(\beta = \angle PBX\).

Note that

\[ PB > PT \] since \(T\) lies on \(BC\) between \(B\) and \(C\), while \(X\) lies on the base plane; the horizontal distance \(BX\) is shorter than \(BT\).

Hint: Both \(PX\) and \(PT\) are heights from \(P\) to the base plane, so \(PX = PT = 22.17\text{ cm}\).
Step 21M

Comparing the sine values:

\[ \sin\alpha = \frac{PX}{PT}, \qquad \sin\beta = \frac{PX}{PB} \]

Since \(PB > PT\),

\[ \frac{PX}{PT} > \frac{PX}{PB} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin\alpha > \sin\beta \]
Hint: Both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are acute angles, and the sine function is increasing on \((0^\circ, 90^\circ)\).
Step 31A

Because \(\sin\alpha > \sin\beta\) and both angles are acute,

\[ \alpha > \beta \]
Answer: \(\alpha\) is greater than \(\beta\).

📌 Final Answers at a Glance

  • Part (a)\(AP = \dfrac{20\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 48^\circ} \approx 23.3\text{ cm}\)
  • Part (b)(i)\(PS \approx 22.17\text{ cm},\; PT \approx 24.56\text{ cm},\; \alpha \approx 58.6^\circ\)
  • Part (b)(ii)\(\alpha > \beta\), because \(\sin\alpha = \dfrac{PX}{PT} > \dfrac{PX}{PB} = \sin\beta\) and both angles are acute.
  • Key ideaThe angle between two planes is found via a perpendicular cross-section; the angle between a line and a plane uses the projection triangle.
