Coordinate Geometry — the orthocentre of a triangle formed with the origin

Coordinate Geometry Orthocentre Altitude Perpendicular Slopes Multiple Choice

The Question Multiple Choice

Let \(O\) be the origin. The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((0,\,60)\) and \((96,\,48)\) respectively. The \(x\)-coordinate of the orthocentre of \(\Delta OPQ\) is

  1. \(6\)
  2. \(32\)
  3. \(45\)
  4. \(48\)
    5. >

Part (a) Set up the triangle & identify the vertical side Understanding

Diagram 1 · Triangle \(OPQ\) with altitudes drag the slider to move \(Q\) horizontally
O = (0, 0) P = (0, 60) Q = (96, 48) x-coord of H = 6
1 Plot the three points and note the shape
\(O=(0,0),\quad P=(0,60),\quad Q=(96,48)\).
Hint: Both \(O\) and \(P\) have \(x=0\), so \(OP\) lies on the \(y\)-axis — it is a vertical line.
2 The altitude from \(Q\) to \(OP\)
Since \(OP\) is vertical, the altitude through \(Q\) must be horizontal.
Therefore every point on this altitude has the same \(y\)-coordinate as \(Q\): \[ y = 48. \]
Hint: A horizontal line has equation \(y=\text{constant}\); a vertical line has equation \(x=\text{constant}\).
Key result: the orthocentre \(H\) lies on the line \(y=48\), so \(y_H = 48\).

Part (b) Use the altitude from \(O\) to \(PQ\)

Diagram 2 · Slopes of \(OH\) and \(PQ\) live slope product readout
slope of \(PQ\) = −0.125 slope of \(OH\) = 8 product = −1
1 Slope of \(PQ\)
\[ m_{PQ}=\frac{48-60}{96-0}=\frac{-12}{96}=-\frac{1}{8} \]
Hint: Slope formula \(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\). Keep it as a fraction to make the perpendicular step clean.
2 \(OH\) is an altitude, so \(OH \perp PQ\)
The orthocentre \(H\) lies on the altitude from \(O\), so \(OH\perp PQ\). Therefore \[ m_{OH}\times m_{PQ}=-1 \] \[ m_{OH}\times\left(-\frac{1}{8}\right)=-1 \;\Rightarrow\; m_{OH}=8. \]
Hint: Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\) (provided neither is vertical).
3 Solve for the \(x\)-coordinate of \(H\)
Let \(H=(h,\,48)\). Since \(m_{OH}=8\) and \(O=(0,0)\), \[ \frac{48-0}{h-0}=8 \] \[ 48=8h \;\Rightarrow\; h=6. \]
Hint: Use \(y_H=48\) from Part (a) and substitute into the slope equation.
Answer: the \(x\)-coordinate of the orthocentre is \(6\). → Option A

Part (c) Verify with the third altitude (from \(P\)) Check

Diagram 3 · The third altitude from \(P\) all three altitudes concur at \(H\)
slope of \(OQ\) = 0.5 slope of altitude from \(P\) = −2 \(y\) at \(x=6\): 48
1 Slope of \(OQ\) and the altitude from \(P\)
\[ m_{OQ}=\frac{48-0}{96-0}=\frac{1}{2} \] The altitude from \(P\) is perpendicular to \(OQ\), so its slope is \[ m_\perp=-\frac{1}{1/2}=-2. \]
Hint: This is a consistency check — all three altitudes must meet at the same point.
2 Equation of the altitude from \(P\) and test \(H=(6,48)\)
Through \(P=(0,60)\) with slope \(-2\): \[ y-60=-2(x-0)\;\Rightarrow\; y=60-2x. \] At \(x=6\): \[ y=60-2(6)=60-12=48 \;\checkmark \] This matches \(y_H=48\), so all three altitudes concur at \[ H=(6,\,48). \]
Hint: Concurrency of the three altitudes confirms the orthocentre. The required \(x\)-coordinate is \(6\).
Final answer: \(x\)-coordinate of the orthocentre \(=6\) → A

Summary of Answers

  • Points\(O=(0,0),\ P=(0,60),\ Q=(96,48)\)
  • Side \(OP\)vertical, \(x=0\) → the altitude from \(Q\) is horizontal: \(y=48\)
  • Slope of \(PQ\)\(m_{PQ}=\dfrac{48-60}{96-0}=-\dfrac{1}{8}\)
  • Slope of \(OH\)\(m_{OH}=8\) (since \(OH \perp PQ\))
  • Solve for \(h\)\(\dfrac{48-0}{h-0}=8 \Rightarrow h=6\)
  • Orthocentre\(H=(6,\,48)\)
  • Answer\(x\)-coordinate \(=6\)  →  Option A
