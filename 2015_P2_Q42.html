HKDSE 2015 · Paper 2 · Q42 · Coordinate Geometry

Question

Let \(O\) be the origin. The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((0,\,60)\) and \((96,\,48)\) respectively. The \(x\)-coordinate of the orthocentre of \(\Delta OPQ\) is
A. 6
B. 32
C. 45
D. 48
STEP 1 · Set up & inspect the triangle

Take the vertices \(O(0,0)\), \(P(0,60)\), \(Q(96,48)\). Watch the special feature: \(O\) and \(P\) share \(x=0\), so side \(OP\) is vertical.

\[OP \perp \text{horizontal line} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the altitude through } Q \text{ is horizontal, i.e. } y = 48.\]

An altitude from a vertex is perpendicular to the opposite side. Since \(OP\) is vertical, the altitude from \(Q\) (opposite \(OP\)) must be horizontal.
STEP 2 · Get the y-coordinate of the orthocentre H

Let the orthocentre be \(H(h,\,k)\). It lies on the altitude through \(Q\), so:

\[k = 48 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H(h,\,48).\]

We only need the \(x\)-coordinate \(h\), so we use a second altitude.

STEP 3 · Use the altitude through O

The altitude through \(O\) is perpendicular to \(PQ\). Slope of \(PQ\):

\[m_{PQ}=\frac{48-60}{96-0}=\frac{-12}{96}=-\frac18.\]

Hence the altitude from \(O\) has slope \(=\,-\dfrac{1}{m_{PQ}}=8\), and its equation is \(y = 8x\).

For perpendicular lines: \(m_1 m_2 = -1\). So \(-\tfrac18 \times m_2 = -1 \Rightarrow m_2 = 8\).
STEP 4 · Intersect the two altitudes

Substitute \(y = 8x\) into the horizontal altitude \(y = 48\):

\[8h = 48\;\Longrightarrow\;h = 6.\]

So the orthocentre is \(H(6,\,48)\) and the required \(x\)-coordinate is \(6\).

STEP 5 · Verify with the slopes of OH and PQ

Check using the perpendicularity of the altitude through \(O\):

\[\text{slope }OH \times \text{slope }PQ=\frac{48-0}{6-0}\times\frac{48-60}{96-0}=8\times\left(-\frac18\right)=-1.\;\checkmark\]

This matches the model answer exactly.

Answer: \(h = 6\)  →  Option A
