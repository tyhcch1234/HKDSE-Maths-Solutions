HKDSE 2023 Paper 2 · Q39 · Coordinate Geometry

The straight line $hx+ky=6$ and the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}-8x-4y-18=0$ intersect at the points $M$ and $N$, where $h$ and $k$ are constants. If the coordinates of the mid-point of $MN$ are $(1,\,0)$, find $k$.
A.  4
B.  6
C.  9
D.  12
STEP 1 · The mid-point lies on the line
Point on a Line \(h(1)+k(0)=6\) The mid-point of the chord lies on the line, so its coordinates satisfy the line equation.

The mid-point of the chord $MN$ is a point on the line $hx+ky=6$ itself.

$$h(1)+k(0)=6 \;\Rightarrow\; h=6$$

So the line becomes $6x+ky=6$,
i.e. $x=1-\dfrac{ky}{6}$.

Hint: the mid-point $(1,0)$ sits on the chord $MN$, so it must satisfy the chord's equation.
STEP 2 · Substitute the line into the circle

Put $x=1-\dfrac{ky}{6}$ into the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}-8x-4y-18=0$:

$$\left(1-\frac{ky}{6}\right)^{2}+y^{2}-8\left(1-\frac{ky}{6}\right)-4y-18=0$$
$$(6-ky)^{2}+36y^{2}-48(6-ky)-144y-648=0$$
STEP 3 · Form a quadratic in $y$

Expanding and collecting terms:

$$(k^{2}+36)y^{2}+36(k-4)y-900=0 \quad\cdots(*)$$

The two intersection points $M$, $N$ have $y$-coordinates equal to the two roots of $(*)$.

STEP 4 · Use the sum of roots
Sum of Roots of a Quadratic Equation \(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{b}{a}\) The sum of the \(y\)-coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\) is zero because their mid-point has \(y=0\).

Let the $y$-coordinates of $M$, $N$ be $m$, $n$. By the sum-of-roots relation:

$$m+n=-\frac{36(k-4)}{k^{2}+36}$$

The $y$-coordinate of the mid-point is $\dfrac{m+n}{2}=0$,
hence $m+n=0$:

$$\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{36(k-4)}{k^{2}+36}\right)=0$$
$$-36(k-4)=0 \;\Rightarrow\; k=4$$
ANSWER
A.  $k=4$

Question. The straight line \(hx+ky=12\) and the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}-8x+2y-8=0\) intersect at the points \(M\) and \(N\), where \(h\) and \(k\) are constants. If the coordinates of the mid-point of \(MN\) are \((3,0)\), find \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The mid-point \((3,0)\) lies on the line: \(h(3)+k(0)=12\), so \(h=4\) and the line is \(x=3-\dfrac{ky}{4}\).
Step 2. Substitute into the circle: \(\left(3-\dfrac{ky}{4}\right)^{2}+y^{2}-8\left(3-\dfrac{ky}{4}\right)+2y-8=0\).
Step 3. Multiply by \(16\) and collect terms: \(\left(k^{2}+16\right)y^{2}+8(k+4)y-368=0\).
Step 4. The \(y\)-coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\) have sum \(-\dfrac{8(k+4)}{k^{2}+16}\),
and their mid-point has \(y=0\), so the sum is \(0\): \(8(k+4)=0\), giving \(k=-4\).
Step 5. Check with \(k=-4\): the line is \(y=x-3\) and the quadratic reduces to \(2y^{2}-23=0\),
whose roots \(\pm\sqrt{11.5}\) give the mid-point \((3,0)\) ✓
Answer: \(k=-4\)
