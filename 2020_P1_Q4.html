HKDSE 2020 Paper 1 · Q4
Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be non-zero numbers such that $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{6}{7}$ and $3a=4c$. Find $\dfrac{b+2c}{a+2b}$.
[3 marks]
STEP 1Link each given ratio to $a$.
From $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{6}{7}$, cross-multiplying gives $7a=6b$, so $b=\dfrac{7a}{6}$.
From $3a=4c$, we get $c=\dfrac{3a}{4}$.
Make $b$ and $c$ the subject so everything is in terms of $a$.
STEP 2Turn both given ratios into one comparison ratio.
$a:b=6:7=12:14$  (×2)
$a:c=4:3=12:9$  (×3)
So $a:b:c=12:14:9$.
Match the $a$ value (LCM of $6$ and $4$ is $12$) first, then combine.
STEP 3Introduce a constant $k$ and substitute.
Let $a=12k$, $b=14k$, $c=9k$, where $k$ is a non-zero constant. Then
$\dfrac{b+2c}{a+2b} =\dfrac{14k+2(9k)}{12k+2(14k)} =\dfrac{14k+18k}{12k+28k} =\dfrac{32k}{40k}.$
The dummy variable $k$ must cancel — a good self-check.
STEP 4Cancel $k$ and simplify the fraction.
$\dfrac{32k}{40k}=\dfrac{32}{40}=\dfrac{4}{5}$.
Divide numerator and denominator by their HCF $8$.
Answer:  $\dfrac{b+2c}{a+2b}=\dfrac{4}{5}$  (i.e. $0.8$)
