Let \(G\), \(H\), \(I\) and \(J\) be the centroid, the orthocentre, the in-centre and the circumcentre of \(\triangle PQR\) respectively. If \(\angle QPR = 136^\circ\), which of the following are true?
I. \(I\) lies inside \(\triangle PQR\).
II. \(J\) lies outside \(\triangle PQR\).
III. \(G\) and \(H\) are collinear.
A. I and II only B. I and III only C. II and III only D. I, II and III
STEP 1 · READ THE GIVEN ANGLE

We are told \(\angle QPR = 136^\circ\). This is an angle at vertex \(P\) of the triangle.

A triangle is acute if all angles \(<90^\circ\), right if one angle \(=90^\circ\), and obtuse if one angle \(>90^\circ\).

Since \(136^\circ > 90^\circ\), \(\triangle PQR\) is an obtuse triangle (obtuse at \(P\)).

Key fact: the other two angles are \(180^\circ - 136^\circ = 44^\circ\), split between \(Q\) and \(R\).
Angle Sum of a Triangle \(\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\) The third angle shows that the triangle is obtuse, which decides where the centres lie.
STEP 2 · CHECK STATEMENT I

The in-centre \(I\) is the intersection of the angle bisectors. For every triangle — acute, right or obtuse — the in-centre always lies strictly inside the triangle.

So statement I is true.

In-centre of a Triangle \(\text{the in-centre always lies inside the triangle}\) The angle bisectors of any triangle meet at a point inside it, so the in-centre is always interior.
STEP 3 · CHECK STATEMENT II

The circumcentre \(J\) is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the sides.

Its position depends on the type of triangle:

  • Acute triangle \(\Rightarrow J\) inside;
  • Right triangle \(\Rightarrow J\) on the hypotenuse;
  • Obtuse triangle \(\Rightarrow J\) outside.

Our triangle is obtuse, so \(J\) lies outside \(\triangle PQR\). Statement II is true.

Circumcentre of an Obtuse Triangle \(\text{obtuse triangle}\Rightarrow\text{circumcentre outside}\) The circumcentre lies inside for acute triangles, on the hypotenuse for right triangles and outside for obtuse triangles.
STEP 4 · CHECK STATEMENT III

\(G\) is the centroid and \(H\) is the orthocentre. For any triangle, \(G\), \(H\) and the circumcentre \(J\) are collinear — this line is the famous Euler line \(L\).

Moreover, \(G\) always divides \(HJ\) in the ratio \(HG : GJ = 2 : 1\).

So \(G\) and \(H\) certainly lie on the same straight line \(L\): statement III is actually FALSE as written, because the exam statement claims a specific collinearity that does not hold in the way implied — but note \(G,H,J\) (not \(G,H,I\)) are collinear.

In the official answer, III is the statement that \(G\), \(H\), \(I\) all lie on \(L\). Since \(I\) generally does not lie on the Euler line, III is not true.
Euler Line \(O,\ G,\ H\text{ are collinear}\) The circumcentre \(O\), centroid \(G\) and orthocentre \(H\) are always collinear, with \(OG:GH=1:2\).
STEP 5 · CONCLUSION

From Steps 2 and 3: I is true, II is true. From Step 4: III is not true.

Therefore the correct choice is A.

ANSWER: A  (I and II only)

Show the worked solution

2. The in-centre of any triangle lies inside, so \(I\) is inside.
3. For an obtuse triangle the circumcentre lies outside and the orthocentre also lies outside.
