[2024MC#23]
In the figure, $ABC$ is a straight line.
$$ \frac{BC}{AD} = \,? $$
A. $\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}$
B. $\dfrac{\tan\phi}{\sin\theta}$
C. $\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\tan\phi}$
D. $\dfrac{\tan\phi}{\cos\theta}$
Step 1 · Read the figure

$A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear, so $AC \perp DC$ — that is, $\triangle BCD$ is right-angled at $C$.

Angle at $A$ in $\triangle ACD$ is $\theta$; angle at $B$ in $\triangle BCD$ is $\phi$.

The length $BC$ is a side of $\triangle BCD$; the length $AD$ is the hypotenuse of $\triangle ACD$.

Step 2 · Express $DC$ in terms of $AD$ (triangle $ACD$)

In right-angled $\triangle ACD$ (right angle at $C$), the side opposite $\theta$ is $DC$ and the hypotenuse is $AD$:

$$ \sin\theta = \frac{DC}{AD} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad DC = AD\,\sin\theta \qquad \cdots (1) $$

The diagram previews this: $DC = AD\sin\theta$.

Sine Ratio \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In right triangle \(ACD\), \(DC=AD\sin\theta\).
Step 3 · Express $DC$ in terms of $BC$ (triangle $BCD$)

In right-angled $\triangle BCD$ (right angle at $C$), the side opposite $\phi$ is $DC$ and the adjacent side is $BC$:

$$ \tan\phi = \frac{DC}{BC} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad DC = BC\,\tan\phi \qquad \cdots (2) $$
Tangent Ratio \(\tan\phi=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\) In right triangle \(BCD\), \(DC=BC\tan\phi\); eliminating \(DC\) gives \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\).
Step 4 · Eliminate $DC$ and solve for $BC/AD$

Equate $(1)$ and $(2)$:

$$ AD\,\sin\theta = BC\,\tan\phi $$

Divide both sides by $AD\tan\phi$:

$$ \frac{BC}{AD} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi} $$

Check: both sides have $DC$ eliminated — the two right triangles share the vertical side $DC$, which links the given information.

Final Answer $$ \frac{BC}{AD} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi} $$
∴ Option A is correct.

Question. In the figure, \(ABC\) is a straight line, \(\angle ACD=\angle BCD=90^\circ\), \(\angle CAD=\theta\) and \(\angle CBD=\phi\). Given \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{5}{13}\) and \(\tan\phi=\dfrac{3}{4}\), find \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\).
Step 2. Substitute: \(\dfrac{5/13}{3/4}=\dfrac{5}{13}\times\dfrac{4}{3}=\dfrac{20}{39}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{20}{39}\)
Common trap: mixing up which side is opposite and which is adjacent for $\tan\phi$. In $\triangle BCD$, $DC$ is opposite $\phi$ and $BC$ is adjacent to $\phi$, so $\tan\phi = DC/BC$ — giving $DC = BC\tan\phi$, hence $BC = DC/\tan\phi$.
