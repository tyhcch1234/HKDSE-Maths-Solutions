Standard scores, mean & standard deviation of examination marks

Standard Score Mean (μ) Standard Deviation (σ) Simultaneous Equations Multiple Choice

QQuestion

In an examination, Peter gets 55 marks and his standard score is \(-3\) while Mary gets 95 marks and her standard score is \(2\). Find the mean of the examination scores.

  • A. 8 marks
  • B. 64 marks
  • C. 75 marks
  • D. 79 marks

1Part (a) — Translating standard scores into equations

The standard score \(z\) of a raw mark \(x\) is defined by \[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}. \] Applying this to Peter and Mary gives a pair of equations in \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\).

Peter's z: -3.00 Mary's z: 2.00 Required: zP = −3 and zM = 2

Drag the sliders. The normal curve shifts with \(\mu\) and widens with \(\sigma\); the red/blue markers show where Peter's and Mary's marks sit. The configuration matches the question only when both readouts equal −3 and 2.

0 / 3
Step 1

Let \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) be the mean and standard deviation of the examination scores. Using \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\):

\[ \frac{55-\mu}{\sigma}=-3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu-3\sigma=55 \quad\cdots(1) \]
Step 2

For Mary:

\[ \frac{95-\mu}{\sigma}=2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu+2\sigma=95 \quad\cdots(2) \]
Hint: Each standard score gives one linear equation. Two people → two equations → a \(2\times 2\) system.
Step 3

So we must solve the system

\[ \begin{cases} \mu-3\sigma=55 &\cdots(1)\\ \mu+2\sigma=95 &\cdots(2) \end{cases} \]
Equations established: \(\mu-3\sigma=55\) and \(\mu+2\sigma=95\).

2Part (b) — Solving for the mean \(\mu\)

Eliminate \(\sigma\) by combining the two equations. The animated lines below show how the intersection point moves as the coefficient of \(\sigma\) changes.

Intersection: μ = 79, σ = 8 μ from elimination: 79
0 / 3
Step 1

Multiply (1) by 2 and (2) by 3 so that the \(\sigma\)-terms cancel when added:

\[ (1)\times 2:\quad 2\mu-6\sigma=110 \] \[ (2)\times 3:\quad 3\mu+6\sigma=285 \]
Step 2

Add the two resulting equations:

\[ (2\mu-6\sigma)+(3\mu+6\sigma)=110+285 \] \[ 5\mu=395 \]
Hint: Adding \(2\times(1)+3\times(2)\) kills the \(\sigma\) terms and leaves \(\mu\) alone — this is the fastest route in a multiple-choice setting.
Step 3

Divide both sides by 5:

\[ \mu=\frac{395}{5}=79 \]
Mean of the examination scores \(\mu=79\) marks → Option D.

3Part (c) — Verification & the meaning of the standard score

Check \(\sigma\) as well, then interpret what \(z=-3\) and \(z=2\) really mean on the distribution.

μ = 79 σ = 8 Peter: 55 marks = μ − 3σ Mary: 95 marks = μ + 2σ
0 / 3
Step 1

Substitute \(\mu=79\) into (2):

\[ 79+2\sigma=95 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2\sigma=16 \;\Longrightarrow\; \sigma=8 \]
Step 2

Verify with (1): \(79-3(8)=79-24=55\) ✓ and separately, \(\dfrac{55-79}{8}=\dfrac{-24}{8}=-3\) ✓, \(\dfrac{95-79}{8}=\dfrac{16}{8}=2\) ✓.

Hint: A standard score tells you how many standard deviations the raw mark lies from the mean. Peter is 3 standard deviations below the mean (a very low position); Mary is 2 standard deviations above it (a strong position).
Step 3

The question asks only for the mean. We have obtained

\[ \mu=79 \text{ marks} \]
Answer: 79 marks (Option D). The mean is 79 marks.

Summary of Answers

  • Let \(\mu\) = mean, \(\sigma\) = standard deviation of the examination scores, with \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\).
  • Peter: \(\dfrac{55-\mu}{\sigma}=-3 \Rightarrow \mu-3\sigma=55\)
  • Mary: \(\dfrac{95-\mu}{\sigma}=2 \Rightarrow \mu+2\sigma=95\)
  • Elimination: \(2\times(1)+3\times(2) \Rightarrow 5\mu=395 \Rightarrow \mu=79\)
  • (Extra) Substituting back gives \(\sigma=8\).
  • Final answer: The mean of the examination scores is 79 marks — Option D.
