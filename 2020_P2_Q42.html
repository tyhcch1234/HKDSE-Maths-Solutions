HKDSE 2020 Paper 2 · Q42

A queue is formed by 6 boys and 5 girls. If no boys are next to each other, how many different queues can be formed?

  • A. 86 400
  • B. 172 800
  • C. 213 444
  • D. 39 916 800

Step-by-step Solution

1 Arrange the 5 girls first
Permutation of Distinct Objects \({}^{n}P_{n}=n!\) The 5 girls are all distinct, so they can be arranged in \(5!=120\) ways.
Treat the girls as the “separators”. Since no two boys may be adjacent,
the boys must be placed in the gaps created by the girls.
First arrange the 5 girls in a row: \[ P_5^5 = 5! = 120 \]
Hint: arrange the group that has no adjacency restriction first.
2 Identify the available gaps
Gap Method for Non-Adjacent Objects \(n\ \text{objects in a row create } n+1\ \text{gaps}\) Five girls create 6 gaps, so the boys can be separated by placing one boy per gap.
With 5 girls placed, the row looks like: \[ \_ \, G \, \_ \, G \, \_ \, G \, \_ \, G \, \_ \, G \, \_ \] There are 6 gaps in total — 1 before the first girl,
4 between the girls, and 1 after the last girl.
Hint: the number of gaps is always (number of girls) + 1 = 6.
3 Place the 6 boys into the 6 gaps
To keep the boys non-adjacent,
each gap can hold at most one boy.
We have exactly 6 boys and exactly 6 gaps, so all gaps must be used — one boy per gap.
The number of ways is: \[ P_6^6 = 6! = 720 \]
Hint: 6 boys → 6 “slots”,
so it is a permutation of 6 distinct boys into 6 distinct gaps.
4 Multiply the two factors
Multiplication Principle \(N=n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{k}\) The girls arrangement and the boys arrangement are independent, so multiply \(120\times720\).
By the multiplication principle, the total number of valid queues is: \[ P_5^5 \times P_6^6 = 120 \times 720 = 86\,400 \]
Hint: independent choices multiply.
5 Select the correct option
\[ 86\,400 \] matches option A.
Hint: check the answer list — option A is 86 400.
Answer: A. 86 400

Question. A queue is formed by 5 boys and 4 girls. If no girls are next to each other, how many different queues can be formed? A. \(2\,880\) B. \(43\,200\) C. \(86\,400\) D. \(362\,880\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Arrange the 5 boys first (no restriction on boys): \(5!=120\) ways.
Step 2. The 5 boys create \(5+1=6\) gaps (before the first boy, between them,
and after the last).
Step 3. Place the 4 distinct girls into 4 of these 6 gaps so that no two are adjacent: \({}^{6}P_{4}=6\times5\times4\times3=360\) ways.
Step 4. Total \(=120\times360=43\,200\).
Answer: B. \(43\,200\)
