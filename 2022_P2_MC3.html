HKDSE Math 2022 Paper 2 Q3 Polynomials

Identity & Comparing Coefficients

Topic: Polynomials · Identities · Substitution Method

Question

If \(m\) and \(n\) are constants such that

\[ (x+3)^2 + mx \equiv (x-n)(x+1) + 2n \]

then \(m =\)

Select an option above to check your answer.

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Expand the Left-Hand Side (LHS)

Expand \((x+3)^2\) using the perfect square identity \((a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{LHS} &= (x+3)^2 + mx \\ &= x^2 + 6x + 9 + mx \\ &= x^2 + (6+m)x + 9 \end{aligned} \]
💡 Hint: Remember to collect like terms. The term \(6x + mx\) can be combined to \((6+m)x\).
2

Expand the Right-Hand Side (RHS)

Expand \((x-n)(x+1)\) using the distributive law (FOIL method):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{RHS} &= (x-n)(x+1) + 2n \\ &= x^2 + x - nx - n + 2n \\ &= x^2 + (1-n)x + n \end{aligned} \]
💡 Hint: Be careful with signs. \(-n \cdot 1 = -n\), and \(-n + 2n = n\).
3

Compare Coefficients of Like Terms

Since the identity holds for all \(x\), the coefficients of corresponding terms must be equal. Equate the \(x^2\) term, \(x\) term, and constant term:

\[ \begin{cases} \text{Coefficient of } x^2: & 1 = 1 \\ \text{Coefficient of } x: & 6+m = 1-n \quad \cdots (1) \\ \text{Constant term:} & 9 = n \quad \cdots (2) \end{cases} \]
💡 Hint: The \(x^2\) term gives no new information (both sides are \(x^2\)). We only need equation (1) and (2).
4

Solve for \(n\) and then \(m\)

From equation (2), we directly get \(n = 9\). Substitute \(n = 9\) into equation (1):

\[ \begin{aligned} 6+m &= 1-n \\ 6+m &= 1-9 \\ 6+m &= -8 \\ m &= -8 - 6 \\ m &= -14 \end{aligned} \]
💡 Hint: Check your algebra: \(1-9 = -8\), then subtract \(6\) from both sides.
★

Alternative Method: Substitute \(x = 0\)

Instead of expanding, we can substitute a convenient value. Since it's an identity, it holds for all \(x\). Let's choose \(x = 0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Put } x = 0: \quad (0+3)^2 + m(0) &= (0-n)(0+1) + 2n \\ 9 &= -n + 2n \\ 9 &= n \end{aligned} \]

Now put \(x = 1\) to find \(m\) (since we already know \(n = 9\)):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Put } x = 1: \quad (1+3)^2 + m(1) &= (1-9)(1+1) + 2(9) \\ 16 + m &= (-8)(2) + 18 \\ 16 + m &= -16 + 18 \\ 16 + m &= 2 \\ m &= -14 \end{aligned} \]
⭐ Quick Tip: Substituting simple values like \(x=0\) or \(x=1\) can be faster than full expansion in multiple-choice questions!
✓

Final Answer

\(m = -14\)
