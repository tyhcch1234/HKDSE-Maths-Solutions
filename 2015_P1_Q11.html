HKDSE 2015 P1 Q11 · Polynomials

Let $f(x)=(x-2)^2(x+h)+k$, where $h$ and $k$ are constants. When $f(x)$ is divided by $x-2$, the remainder is $-5$. It is given that $f(x)$ is divisible by $x-3$.

(a) Find $h$ and $k$. [3 marks]

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $f(x)=0$ are integers. Do you agree? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

1Use the remainder theorem

Dividing by $(x-2)$ leaves remainder $-5$, so

$$f(2)=-5.$$

But $f(2)=(2-2)^2(2+h)+k=0+k=k$.

$$\therefore k=-5.$$

Hint: remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $(x-a)$ is $f(a)$.

2Use divisibility by $x-3$

Since $f(x)$ is divisible by $x-3$, the factor theorem gives

$$f(3)=0.$$

With $k=-5$: $\;(3-2)^2(3+h)-5=0$

$$1\cdot(3+h)-5=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 3+h=5 \;\Rightarrow\; h=2.$$

Hint: divisible by $(x-a)$ means $f(a)=0$.

3Write down $f(x)$

$$f(x)=(x-2)^2(x+2)-5.$$

Expand: $f(x)=(x^2-4x+4)(x+2)-5$

$$f(x)=x^3-2x^2-4x+3.$$

Hint: expand carefully, then subtract the constant $5$.

4Factorise using the known root $x=3$

$$x^3-2x^2-4x+3=0$$

Since $f(3)=0$, $(x-3)$ is a factor:

$$(x-3)(x^2+x-1)=0.$$

Hint: divide the cubic by $(x-3)$.

5Solve the quadratic factor

$$x=3 \quad\text{or}\quad x=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1+4}}{2}=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{5}}{2}.$$

So the roots are $3$, $\dfrac{-1+\sqrt5}{2}$ and $\dfrac{-1-\sqrt5}{2}$.

Hint: quadratic formula $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$.

6Conclusion for part (b)

$\sqrt5$ is irrational, so $\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$ are not integers.

Hence not all roots are integers — the claim is disagreed.

Hint: only $x=3$ is an integer root.

Answer (a) $h=2,\quad k=-5$.
(b) No. $f(x)=0$ gives $(x-3)(x^2+x-1)=0$, so $x=3$ or $x=\dfrac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$; the latter two are not integers. The claim is disagreed.
