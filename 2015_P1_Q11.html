HKDSE Math 2015 Paper 1 Q11 Polynomials

Polynomials — Remainder Theorem & Factor Theorem

Find constants using the Remainder Theorem, then determine whether all roots are integers.

Question

Let \( f(x) = (x - 2)^2 (x + h) + k \), where \( h \) and \( k \) are constants.

When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - 2 \), the remainder is \(-5\). It is given that \( f(x) \) is divisible by \( x - 3 \).

(a) Find \( h \) and \( k \).   [3 marks]

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \( f(x) = 0 \) are integers. Do you agree? Explain your answer.   [3 marks]

Step-by-Step Solution

Part (a) — Find h and k

1 State the Remainder Theorem

When a polynomial \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - a \), the remainder is \( f(a) \).

\[ \text{Remainder when } f(x) \div (x - 2) \text{ is } f(2) = -5 \]
Hint: The divisor is \( x - 2 \), so substitute \( x = 2 \) into \( f(x) \).
2 Use \( f(2) = -5 \) to find \( k \)

Substitute \( x = 2 \) into \( f(x) = (x-2)^2(x+h) + k \):

\( f(2) = (2-2)^2(2+h) + k \)

\( f(2) = 0 \cdot (2+h) + k \)

\( f(2) = k \)

Since \( f(2) = -5 \):

\( k = -5 \)
Hint: The factor \( (x-2)^2 \) vanishes at \( x = 2 \), so only \( k \) remains. This is why \( k \) is the remainder.
3 Use the Factor Theorem for \( h \)

Since \( f(x) \) is divisible by \( x - 3 \), by the Factor Theorem:

\[ f(3) = 0 \]

Now \( k = -5 \), so substitute \( x = 3 \):

\( f(3) = (3-2)^2(3+h) + (-5) = 0 \)

\( (1)^2(3+h) - 5 = 0 \)

\( 3 + h - 5 = 0 \)

\( h - 2 = 0 \)

\( h = 2 \)
Hint: "Divisible by \( x - 3 \)" means \( x = 3 \) is a root, so \( f(3) = 0 \).
4 Write down the final polynomial

Substitute \( h = 2 \) and \( k = -5 \) back into \( f(x) \):

\[ f(x) = (x-2)^2(x + 2) - 5 \]

Part (a) answer:

\( h = 2, \quad k = -5 \)

Part (b) — Are all roots integers?

5 Set up the equation \( f(x) = 0 \)

We want to solve \( f(x) = 0 \):

\( (x-2)^2(x+2) - 5 = 0 \)

\( (x-2)(x-2)(x+2) - 5 = 0 \)

Hint: Expand and simplify to get a cubic equation. It may factorize nicely.
6 Expand and factorize the cubic

Expand \( (x-2)^2(x+2) \):

\( (x^2 - 4x + 4)(x + 2) \)

\( = x^3 + 2x^2 - 4x^2 - 8x + 4x + 8 \)

\( = x^3 - 2x^2 - 4x + 8 \)

So the equation becomes:

\( x^3 - 2x^2 - 4x + 8 - 5 = 0 \)
\( x^3 - 2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0 \)

Since \( x = 3 \) is a root (given), we can factor out \( (x - 3) \):

\( (x - 3)(x^2 + x - 1) = 0 \)
Hint: Test \( x = 3 \) first, then perform polynomial division or inspection to get the quadratic factor.
7 Solve the quadratic factor

Solve \( x^2 + x - 1 = 0 \) using the quadratic formula:

\[ x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4(1)(-1)}}{2} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2} \]

So the roots of \( f(x) = 0 \) are:

\( x = 3 \)

\( x = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 0.618 \)

\( x = \frac{-1 - \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx -1.618 \)

8 Conclusion: Disagree

Since \( \sqrt{5} \) is irrational, both

\( \frac{-1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \) and \( \frac{-1-\sqrt{5}}{2} \)

are not integers.

∴ The claim is disagreed.
Hint: An irrational number (like \( \sqrt{5} \)) cannot be written as a fraction of two integers, so these roots are not integers.
