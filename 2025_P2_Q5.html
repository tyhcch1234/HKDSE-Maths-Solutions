2025 MC #5

Let $k$ be a constant. Solve the equation

$$x^{2}+4x=k^{2}-2k-3.$$

  • A. $\;x=-k+3$  or  $x=k+1$
  • B. $\;x=k-3$  or  $x=k+1$
  • C. $\;x=k-3$  or  $x=-k-1$
  • D. $\;x=-k+3$  or  $x=-k-1$
1Make the right-hand side zero

Bring every term to the left so we get a quadratic equation in $x$:

$$x^{2}+4x\;-\;(k^{2}-2k-3)=0$$

The constant term factorises beautifully:

$$k^{2}-2k-3=(k-3)(k+1)$$

Hint: look for two numbers multiplying to $-3$ and adding to $-2$, i.e. $-3$ and $+1$.

2Factorise by the cross method

$$x^{2}+4x-(k-3)(k+1)=0$$

Try the factor pair $[x-(k-3)]$ and $[x+(k+1)]$:

$$\underbrace{x\cdot x=x^{2}}_{\text{first}}\qquad \underbrace{x(k+1)-x(k-3)=x\big[(k+1)-(k-3)\big]=4x}_{\text{middle}}\qquad \underbrace{-(k-3)(k+1)}_{\text{last}}$$

Hence

$$\big[x-(k-3)\big]\big[x+(k+1)\big]=0$$

Hint: the cross method works because $(k+1)-(k-3)=4$, matching the middle coefficient.

Factorising a Quadratic \(x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)\) Since \((k+4)-(k-2)=6\), the factors are \(x-(k-2)\) and \(x+(k+4)\).
3Apply the zero-product property (Alternative: quadratic formula)

Each factor may be zero:

$$x=k-3\quad\text{or}\quad x=-k-1$$

Alternatively, by the quadratic formula with $a=1,\;b=4,\;c=-(k-3)(k+1)$:

$$x=\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{4^{2}-4[-(k-3)(k+1)]}}{2} =\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{4k^{2}-8k+4}}{2} =\frac{-4\pm\sqrt{4(k-1)^{2}}}{2}$$

$$=\frac{-4\pm 2(k-1)}{2}$$

Hint: $4k^{2}-8k+4=4(k^{2}-2k+1)=4(k-1)^{2}$ is a perfect square, so the roots are rational.

Zero Product Property \(ab=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ a=0\ \text{or}\ b=0\) Each bracket may vanish, giving \(x=k-2\) or \(x=-k-4\).
4Simplify and choose the option

Taking the $+$ sign:

$$x=\frac{-4+(2k-2)}{2}=\frac{2k-6}{2}=k-3$$

Taking the $-$ sign:

$$x=\frac{-4-(2k-2)}{2}=\frac{-2k-2}{2}=-k-1$$

So the two roots are $x=k-3$ or $x=-k-1$, which is option C.

Hint: only option C keeps the signs consistent with both factors: $x-(k-3)=0\Rightarrow x=k-3$.

Answer:  $x=k-3$  or  $x=-k-1$  —  Option C

Question. Let \(k\) be a constant. Solve the equation \(x^{2}+6x=k^{2}+2k-8\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Bring all terms to the left: \(x^{2}+6x-(k^{2}+2k-8)=0\), and \(k^{2}+2k-8=(k+4)(k-2)\).
Step 2. Since \((k+4)-(k-2)=6\), the expression factorises as \(\left[x-(k-2)\right]\left[x+(k+4)\right]=0\).
Step 3. Hence \(x=k-2\) or \(x=-k-4\).
Step 4. Check with \(k=3\): \(x^{2}+6x-7=0\) gives \(x=1\) or \(x=-7\), matching \(k-2=1\) and \(-k-4=-7\).
Answer: \(x=k-2\) or \(x=-k-4\)
