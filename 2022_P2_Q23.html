Trigonometry — Rhombus Diagonals

It is given that $PQRS$ is a rhombus. Let $T$ be the point of intersection of $PR$ and $QS$.

If $\angle QRT=\theta$, then $\dfrac{PQ}{ST}=$

A.  $\sin\theta$
B.  $\cos\theta$
C.  $\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$
D.  $\dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}$
Step 1 · Properties of a rhombus
Diagonals of a Rhombus \(PR\perp QS,\quad QT=ST\) The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, so \(\triangle QRT\) is right-angled at \(T\).

In a rhombus the diagonals bisect each other at right angles:

$$QT=ST,\qquad PR\perp QS$$

So $\triangle QRT$ is a right-angled triangle at $T$, and since $PQ=QR$ (all sides equal), $PQ = QR$.

Step 2 · Set up the right triangle $\triangle QRT$
Sine Ratio \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\) In \(\triangle QRT\), \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{QT}{QR}\), and \(QR=PQ\), \(QT=ST\).

Right angle at $T$. With $\angle QRT=\theta$:

$$\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}=\frac{QT}{QR}$$

Equivalently, $\dfrac{QT}{QR}=\sin\theta$.

Step 3 · Convert to the requested ratio

Since $QT=ST$ and $QR=PQ$:

$$\sin\theta=\frac{QT}{QR}=\frac{ST}{PQ}$$

Hence, taking reciprocals,

$$\frac{PQ}{ST}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$$

💡 Exam tip: The two half-diagonals of a rhombus form a right-angled triangle with a side. Sketch $\triangle QRT$ separately — the needed ratio follows immediately from SOH CAH TOA.

Answer: C  —  $$\boxed{\dfrac{PQ}{ST}=\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}}$$

Question. \(PQRS\) is a rhombus whose diagonals meet at \(T\). If \(\angle QRT=\theta\), find \(\dfrac{PR}{QS}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(PR\perp QS\) and the diagonals bisect each other,
so \(QT=ST\) and \(PT=RT\).
Step 2. \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{QT}{RT}\) in the right triangle \(QRT\).
Step 3. \(PR=2RT\) and \(QS=2QT\), so \(\dfrac{PR}{QS}=\dfrac{RT}{QT}=\dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{PR}{QS}=\dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}\)
