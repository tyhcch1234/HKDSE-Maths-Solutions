Coordinate Geometry — mid-point of the chord of intersection
Equation of Circle Line–Circle Intersection Roots & Coefficients Mid-point of a Chord

Q Question Multiple Choice

The straight line \(hx+ky=6\) and the circle \(x^2+y^2-8x-4y-18=0\) intersect at the points \(M\) and \(N\), where \(h\) and \(k\) are constants. If the coordinates of the mid-point of \(MN\) are \((1,\,0)\), find \(k\).

A. \(4\)     B. \(6\)     C. \(9\)     D. \(12\)

1 Find \(h\) from the mid-point condition Setup

Drag the slider: the chord \(MN\) always contains the fixed mid-point \((1,0)\), so the line must pass through it.

STEP 1Reasoning
\[\text{The mid-point of } MN \text{ lies on the line } hx+ky=6.\]
Hint: The mid-point of a chord always lies on the chord itself — so substitute the mid-point into the line equation.
STEP 21M
\[\text{Put }(x,y)=(1,0):\quad h(1)+k(0)=6\]
Hint: Only \(x=1\) appears because the \(y\)-term vanishes when \(y=0\).
STEP 31A
\[h=6\]
\(h=6\). The line is \(6x+ky=6\), i.e. \(x=\dfrac{6-ky}{6}\).

2 Substitute the line into the circle Algebra

As \(k\) changes, the chord pivots about \((1,0)\). Watch the mid-point \(P\) of \(MN\): only at \(k=4\) does it sit exactly on \((1,0)\).

STEP 11M
\[\text{Eliminate }x:\quad x=\frac{6-ky}{6}\]
Hint: With \(h=6\), the line \(6x+ky=6\) rearranges neatly to make \(x\) the subject.
STEP 21M
\[\left(\frac{6-ky}{6}\right)^{2}+y^{2}-8\left(\frac{6-ky}{6}\right)-4y-18=0\]
Hint: Both \(x^{2}\) and the \(-8x\) term must be replaced by \(\frac{6-ky}{6}\).
STEP 31M
\[\text{Multiply by }36:\quad (6-ky)^{2}+36y^{2}-48(6-ky)-144y-648=0\]
Hint: Clearing the denominator makes the arithmetic manageable — multiply the whole equation by \(36\).
STEP 41A
\[36-12ky+k^{2}y^{2}+36y^{2}-288+48ky-144y-648=0\] \[(k^{2}+36)y^{2}+36(k-4)y-900=0\quad\cdots(*) \]
The \(y\)-coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\) are the roots of \((*)\).
0 / 4

3 Use the sum of roots and solve for \(k\) Final

\(k=4\) makes the mid-point exactly \((1,0)\).  |  Current mid-point: (1.00, 0.00)

STEP 11M
\[\text{Let } m,\,n \text{ be the } y\text{-coordinates of } M \text{ and } N.\]
Hint: From \((*)\), \(m+n=-\dfrac{36(k-4)}{k^{2}+36}\) by the relations between roots and coefficients.
STEP 21M
\[y\text{-coordinate of the mid-point}=\frac{m+n}{2}=0\]
Hint: Mid-point \(y\)-coordinate is the average of the \(y\)-coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\), and it is given as \(0\).
STEP 31A
\[\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{36(k-4)}{k^{2}+36}\right)=0\] \[-36(k-4)=0\] \[k=4\]
\(k=4\), so the answer is A.

✓ Answers at a Glance

  • Mid-point of \(MN=(1,0)\) lies on \(hx+ky=6\):  \(h(1)+k(0)=6\)
  • So \(h=\mathbf{6}\), and the line is \(6x+ky=6\;\Rightarrow\;x=\dfrac{6-ky}{6}\)
  • Substituting into \(x^{2}+y^{2}-8x-4y-18=0\) gives \((k^{2}+36)y^{2}+36(k-4)y-900=0\)
  • Sum of roots: \(m+n=-\dfrac{36(k-4)}{k^{2}+36}\)
  • Mid-point \(y\)-coordinate \(=\dfrac{m+n}{2}=0\;\Rightarrow\;-36(k-4)=0\)
  • Therefore \(\boxed{k=4}\) — the answer is A
  • Quick check: the intersection points are \((6,\pm 3\sqrt{2})\) with mid-point \((1,0)\). ✓
