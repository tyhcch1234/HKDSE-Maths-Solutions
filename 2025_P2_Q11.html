Q Question

If $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are non-zero constants such that \[(\alpha+2\beta):(\beta+2\gamma):(\gamma+2\alpha)=4:9:5,\] then $\alpha:\beta=\;?$
A. 2 : 5
B. 5 : 2
C. 128 : 149
D. 149 : 128

✎ Step-by-step Solution

1Write the ratio as an equation
A chain ratio $a:b:c=p:q:r$ means there is a common unit $k$ with \[a=kp,\quad b=kq,\quad c=kr.\] So from $(\alpha+2\beta):(\beta+2\gamma):(\gamma+2\alpha)=4:9:5$ we may write \[\alpha+2\beta=4k,\qquad \beta+2\gamma=9k,\qquad \gamma+2\alpha=5k .\]
💡 Introduce the common factor $k\ne0$ — the unknowns' values scale with $k$.
Chain Ratio \(a:b:c=p:q:r\ \to\ a=kp,\ b=kq,\ c=kr\) So \(\alpha+\beta=3k\), \(\beta+\gamma=5k\) and \(\gamma+\alpha=4k\) for some \(k\neq0\).
2Form the ratio $\dfrac{\alpha+2\beta}{\gamma+2\alpha}$
The first and third terms give \[\frac{\alpha+2\beta}{\gamma+2\alpha}=\frac{4k}{5k}=\frac{4}{5}.\] Multiply across: \[5(\alpha+2\beta)=4(\gamma+2\alpha)\;\Rightarrow\;5\alpha+10\beta=4\gamma+8\alpha\] \[\Rightarrow\;4\gamma=10\beta-3\alpha \quad\cdots(1)\]
💡 Cancel $k$ first — this is why $k$ never appears again.
Ratio as an Equation \(\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{p}{q}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ aq=bp\) Cross multiplying the ratios keeps the common factor \(k\) cancelling out.
3Form the ratio $\dfrac{\alpha+2\beta}{\beta+2\gamma}$
The first and second terms give \[\frac{\alpha+2\beta}{\beta+2\gamma}=\frac{4k}{9k}=\frac{4}{9}.\] Multiply across: \[9(\alpha+2\beta)=4(\beta+2\gamma)\;\Rightarrow\;9\alpha+18\beta=4\beta+8\gamma\] \[\Rightarrow\;8\gamma=9\alpha+14\beta \quad\cdots(2)\]
💡 Now we have two linear equations linking $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$.
Solving for One Quantity from a Sum \(\alpha=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)-(\beta+\gamma)\) Adding the three sums gives \(2(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)=12k\), so \(\alpha=6k-5k=k\).
4Eliminate $\gamma$
From (1): $\;4\gamma=10\beta-3\alpha$, so $8\gamma=20\beta-6\alpha$.
Substitute into (2): \[20\beta-6\alpha=9\alpha+14\beta\] \[20\beta-14\beta=9\alpha+6\alpha\] \[6\beta=15\alpha .\]
💡 Match the coefficients of $\gamma$ (multiply (1) by 2) before subtracting.
5Solve for $\alpha:\beta$
\[6\beta=15\alpha\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{6}{15}=\frac{2}{5}\] Hence \[\alpha:\beta=2:5 .\]
💡 Divide both sides by $15\beta$ to isolate $\alpha/\beta$.
Answer: $\alpha:\beta=2:5$  (Option A)

Question. It is given that \((\alpha+\beta):(\beta+\gamma):(\gamma+\alpha)=3:5:4\), where \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\). Find \(\alpha:\beta:\gamma\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(\alpha+\beta=3k\), \(\beta+\gamma=5k\) and \(\gamma+\alpha=4k\).
Step 2. Adding: \(2(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)=12k\), so \(\alpha+\beta+\gamma=6k\).
Step 3. \(\alpha=6k-5k=k\), \(\beta=6k-4k=2k\), \(\gamma=6k-3k=3k\).
Step 4. Hence \(\alpha:\beta:\gamma=1:2:3\).
Answer: \(\alpha:\beta:\gamma=1:2:3\)
