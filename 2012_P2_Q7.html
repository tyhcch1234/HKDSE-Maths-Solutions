Question The solution of \(15+4x<3\)  or  \(9-2x>1\) is
A. \(x<-3\)
B. \(x>4\)
C. \(x<-3\) or \(x>4\)
D. \(-3<x<4\)
1Read the key word: “or”.
Solving a Linear Inequality \(ax0);\quad ax>b \Rightarrow x>\dfrac{b}{a}\ (a>0)\) Isolating \(x\) in \(15+4x<3\) gives \(4x<-12\).
The two inequalities are joined by or. We solve each one separately and then take the union of their solution sets — a value is acceptable if it satisfies at least one of them.
“or” ⇒ union of solution sets (that is why option C is possible).
2Solve the first inequality.
Rule for Reversing an Inequality \(-ax-\dfrac{b}{a}\) Dividing \(9-2x>1\) by \(-2\) reverses the sign, giving \(x<4\).
\[ 15+4x<3 \] Subtract \(15\) from both sides: \[ 4x<3-15=-12 \] Divide both sides by \(4\) (positive, so the sign is unchanged): \[ x<-3 \] On the number line: an open circle at \(-3\) with an arrow to the left.
Dividing by a positive number never flips the inequality sign.
3Solve the second inequality.
\[ 9-2x>1 \] Subtract \(9\) from both sides: \[ -2x>1-9=-8 \] Divide both sides by \(-2\). Because we divide by a negative number, the sign flips: \[ x<\frac{-8}{-2}=4 \] On the number line: an open circle at \(4\) with an arrow to the left.
Careful! Dividing by a negative number reverses \(>\) into \(<\).
4Combine with “or” (union).
Union of Solution Sets ("or") \(A \text{ or } B \iff x\in A\cup B\) The word "or" takes the union, so \(x<-3\) is already inside \(x<4\).
First set: \(x<-3\). Second set: \(x<4\). Their union is everything that lies in at least one of them: \[ \{x<-3\}\cup\{x<4\}\ =\ \{x<4\} \] because every \(x<-3\) is already contained in \(x<4\).
Union = “either one”; it does not require both to be true.
5Check against the options and verify.
\(x<4\) matches option B.
Quick test with \(x=0\): \(15+0=15\not<3\), but \(9-0=9>1\) ✓ (satisfies the “or”).
Test with \(x=-4\): \(-4<4\) ✓ and indeed \(15-16=-1<3\) ✓.
Substituting a sample value is a fast way to eliminate wrong options.
Answer: \(x<4\)  →  Option B

Question. Solve the compound inequality \(7+3x<1\) or \(11-4x>3\), and represent the solution on a number line.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. First part: \(7+3x<1\Rightarrow 3x<-6\Rightarrow x<-2\).
Step 2. Second part: \(11-4x>3\Rightarrow -4x>-8\Rightarrow x<2\) (sign reverses).
Step 3. The word \"or\" means union: \(x<-2\) or \(x<2\), and every \(x<-2\) already satisfies \(x<2\).
Step 4. So the solution set is simply \(x<2\).
Answer: \(x<2\)
