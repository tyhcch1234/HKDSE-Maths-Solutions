Question

Consider the compound inequality \[ (*)\qquad \begin{cases} \dfrac{6x+1}{2} < x-8 \\[6pt] 3x \le -21 \end{cases} \]

(a) Solve (*).

(b) Write down the greatest integer satisfying (*).

(4 marks)

Step-by-step solution

Step 1 1M
Solve the first inequality (clear the fraction). Multiply both sides by 2 (positive, so the inequality sign is unchanged):
\[ \frac{6x+1}{2} < x-8 \]
\[ 6x+1 < 2(x-8) \]
\[ 6x+1 < 2x-16 \]
Hint: never forget to multiply every term on the right-hand side by 2.
Step 2 1A
Collect like terms. Bring the \(x\)-terms to the left and the constants to the right:
\[ 6x-2x < -16-1 \]
\[ 4x < -17 \]
\[ x < -\frac{17}{4} \]
Hint: \(-\tfrac{17}{4} = -4.25\), so the boundary sits between \(-5\) and \(-4\).
Step 3
Solve the second inequality.
\[ 3x \le -21 \]
\[ x \le -\frac{21}{3} \]
\[ x \le -7 \]
Hint: dividing by the positive number 3 keeps the direction of \(\le\).
Step 4 1A
Form the intersection of the two conditions. We need BOTH
\[ x < -\frac{17}{4} \quad \text{and} \quad x \le -7 \]
Since \(-7 < -\tfrac{17}{4}\), the condition \(x \le -7\) is the stricter one — it already forces \(x < -\tfrac{17}{4}\). Hence the compound inequality is equivalent to just
\[ \boxed{\,x \le -7\,} \]
Hint: on a number line, the solution of a compound “and” inequality is where the two rays overlap — here the whole ray to the left of \(-7\).
Step 5 1A
(b) Find the greatest integer satisfying (*). The integers satisfying \(x \le -7\) are
\[ \dots,\,-9,\,-8,\,-7 \]
Reading from the right, the largest one is \(-7\).
Hint: \(-7\) itself is allowed because the inequality is \(\le\) (closed endpoint).
Answer: (a) \(x \le -7\)   (equivalently, \(x \le -\tfrac{21}{3}\), i.e. the stricter of \(x<-\tfrac{17}{4}\) and \(x\le -7\))
(b) The greatest integer satisfying (*) is \(\;-7\).
