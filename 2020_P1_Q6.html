Number Line: Solution Set of (*)

First inequality: \(x < -1\) Second inequality: \(x > -1\) Excluded point \(x = -1\) Greatest negative integer \(x=-2\)

Step 1 — read the two given inequalities from the question.

HKDSE 2020 · Paper 1 · Q6

Consider the compound inequality

\(3-x > \dfrac{7-x}{2}\)   or   \(5+x > 4\)     …… (*) .

(a) Solve (*) . 4 marks

(b) Write down the greatest negative integer satisfying (*) .

1Understand what “or” means

\(3-x > \dfrac{7-x}{2}\)   or   \(5+x>4\)
For a compound inequality joined by or, the solution set is the union of the two separate solution sets. Solve each one, then combine.

2Solve the first inequality

Multiplication Property of Inequalities \(a0)\) Multiplying by the positive number 2 clears the fraction without changing the sign.
\(3-x>\dfrac{7-x}{2}\)
\(2(3-x)>7-x\)
\(6-2x>7-x\)
\(-2x+x>7-6\)
\(x<-1\)
Multiply both sides by \(2\) (positive, so the sign stays). Then collect like terms. (1M for the method, 1A for the answer.)

3Solve the second inequality

\(5+x>4\)
\(x>4-5\)
\(x>-1\)
Simple rearrangement — subtract \(5\) from both sides.

4Combine: what is excluded?

Union of Solution Sets \(xa\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\neq a\) The two branches together cover every real number except the single point \(x=a\).
\(x<-1\)   or   \(x>-1\)
\(\Rightarrow\ x \ne -1\)
The only real number not covered by either branch is \(x=-1\) itself (it is excluded by both). So the combined solution is all real numbers except \(-1\). (1A)
Answer to (a):   \(x<-1\) or \(x>-1\), i.e. \(x \ne -1\).

5Find the greatest negative integer

Negative integers: \(\;-1,\,-2,\,-3,\dots\)
\(-1\) is excluded, but \(-2\) satisfies (*).
The greatest (closest to zero) negative integer that is allowed is \(-2\). (1A)
Answers (a) \(x<-1\) or \(x>-1\)  (equivalently \(x\ne -1\))     (b) \(-2\)
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. Solve the inequality \(4-x>\dfrac{6-x}{2}\) or \(3+x>5\). Hence find the greatest negative integer satisfying the inequality.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Multiply the first inequality by the positive number \(2\): \(8-2x>6-x\).
Step 2. Collect terms: \(8-6>2x-x\), i.e. \(2>x\), so \(x<2\).
Step 3. The second inequality gives \(x>2\).
Step 4. The union is \(x<2\) or \(x>2\), i.e. \(x\neq2\).
Step 5. The negative integers allowed include \(-1\), so the greatest negative integer is \(-1\).
Answer: \(x\neq2\); the greatest negative integer is \(-1\).
