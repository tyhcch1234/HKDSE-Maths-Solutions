Compound Inequality with "or"

Consider the compound inequality

\(3 - x > \dfrac{7 - x}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad 5 + x > 4\) …… (*)

(a) Solve (*).

(b) Write down the greatest negative integer satisfying (*).

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 0 of 6
1

Understand the structure of (*)

A compound inequality joined by "or" means a value satisfies (*) if it satisfies at least one of the two inequalities. We solve each part separately, then combine the results.

💡 Hint: For "or", the solution is the union of the two solution sets.
2

Solve the first inequality

Start with the first part of (*):

\(3 - x > \dfrac{7 - x}{2}\)
💡 Hint: Multiply both sides by 2 to remove the fraction. Remember: multiplying by a positive number does not reverse the inequality sign.
3

Simplify the first inequality

\(2(3 - x) > 7 - x\)
\(6 - 2x > 7 - x\)
\(-2x + x > 7 - 6\)
\(-x > 1\)
\(x < -1\)
💡 Hint: Dividing or multiplying by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. Here we divided by −1, so \( > \) becomes \( < \).

Result: \(x < -1\)

4

Solve the second inequality

Now the second part of (*):

\(5 + x > 4\)
\(x > 4 - 5\)
\(x > -1\)

Result: \(x > -1\)

💡 Hint: Simply subtract 5 from both sides — no sign reversal needed.
5

Combine with "or"

We have

\(x < -1\)   or   \(x > -1\)

Together, this means every real number except \(x = -1\).

\(x \neq -1\)
💡 Hint: The union of \(x < -1\) and \(x > -1\) is all real numbers except the boundary point −1.
6

Answer part (b)

We need the greatest negative integer satisfying (*).

Negative integers: −1, −2, −3, …

But −1 is excluded (since \(x \neq -1\)). So the greatest negative integer is −2.

Answer: −2
