Rotation & Polar Coordinates

Question

The rectangular coordinates of the point \( P \) are \( \left(\sqrt{2},\,-\sqrt{2}\right) \). If \( P \) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin through \( 90^{\circ} \), then the polar coordinates of its image are

  1. \( \left(\sqrt{2},45^{\circ}\right) \)
  2. \( \left(\sqrt{2},225^{\circ}\right) \)
  3. \( \left(2,45^{\circ}\right) \)
  4. \( \left(2,225^{\circ}\right) \)

Solution

STEP 1 · Polar form of \( P \) \[ r = \sqrt{(\sqrt{2})^{2}+(-\sqrt{2})^{2}} = \sqrt{2+2} = 2. \] \( P \) lies in the fourth quadrant with reference angle \( 45^{\circ} \), so its polar angle is \( 315^{\circ} \).
STEP 2 · Rotate anticlockwise by \( 90^{\circ} \) An anticlockwise rotation adds to the angle: \[ 315^{\circ} + 90^{\circ} = 405^{\circ} \equiv 405^{\circ} - 360^{\circ} = 45^{\circ}. \] The distance from the origin is unchanged (\( r = 2 \)), so the image is at \( (2,\,45^{\circ}) \).
Final Answer: C. \( \left(2,\,45^{\circ}\right) \)
