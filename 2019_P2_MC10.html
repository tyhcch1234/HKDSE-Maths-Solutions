Functions & Graphs — properties of a quadratic function

Quadratic Function Opening Direction Point Substitution x-intercepts Factor Form

The QuestionWhich statement(s) is/are true?

Consider the graph of \[ y=(3-x)(x+2)+6 .\] Which of the following statements about the graph is/are true?

I.  The graph opens downwards.

II.  The graph passes through the point \((1,10)\).

III.  The \(x\)-intercepts of the graph are \(-2\) and \(3\).

A. I only    B. II only    C. I and III only    D. II and III only

All parts carry equal weight in the MCQ — a total of 3 statements must be tested independently.

Part I · Statement IDoes the graph open downwards?

Step 1

Expand the function into the standard form \(y=ax^2+bx+c\).

\[ y=(3-x)(x+2)+6 = 3x+6-x^2-2x+6 = -x^2+x+12 \]
Hint: Careful with signs: \((3-x)(x+2)=3x+6-x^2-2x = -x^2+x+6\), then add the constant \(6\).
Step 2

Read off the coefficient of \(x^2\):

\[ a=-1<0 .\]

A negative leading coefficient means the parabola opens downwards.

Hint: If \(a<0\) the curve is “sad”; if \(a>0\) the curve is “happy”. Here it is sad.
Statement I is TRUE.
0 / 2

Part II · Statement IIDoes it pass through \((1,10)\)?

Step 1

Substitute \(x=1\) into \(y=-x^2+x+12\):

\[ y=-1^2+1+12=-1+1+12=12 .\]
Hint: \((-1)^2\) is not needed here — only \(1^2=1\). The parabola value is \(12\), not \(10\).
Step 2

The point \((1,12)\) lies on the graph, but \((1,10)\) does not, since \(12\ne 10\).

Hint: A point lies on the curve only when its coordinates satisfy the equation exactly.
Statement II is FALSE.
0 / 2

Part III · Statement IIIAre the x-intercepts \(-2\) and \(3\)?

Step 1

Put \(y=0\) and solve the quadratic equation:

\[ -x^2+x+12=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2-x-12=0 .\]
Hint: Multiplying throughout by \(-1\) makes the leading coefficient positive and easier to factorise.
Step 2

Factorise:

\[ x^2-x-12=(x-4)(x+3)=0 .\]

Hence \(x=4\) or \(x=-3\). The \(x\)-intercepts are \(-3\) and \(4\), not \(-2\) and \(3\).

Hint: Check: \((-3)\times 4=-12\) and \((-3)+4=1\) ✓. The numbers \(-2\) and \(3\) would give \(x^2-x-6\), which is wrong.
Statement III is FALSE.
0 / 2

Summary of Results

Expanded form of the function:

\[ y=(3-x)(x+2)+6=-x^2+x+12 \]

  • I. Graph opens downwards — TRUE since \(a=-1<0\).
  • II. Passes through \((1,10)\) — FALSE; at \(x=1\), \(y=12\).
  • III. \(x\)-intercepts \(-2\) and \(3\) — FALSE; they are \(-3\) and \(4\).
  • Only statement I is true → the correct option is A. I only.
