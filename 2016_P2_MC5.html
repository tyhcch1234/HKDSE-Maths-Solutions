HKDSE Math 2016 Paper 2 Q5

Simultaneous Equations · Elimination

Question

If $4\alpha + \beta = 5$ and $7\alpha + 3\beta = 5$, then $\beta =$

A. $-3$
B. $-2$
C. $2$
D. $3$

(Answer will be revealed after step 5)

🔍 Step-by-Step Solution

1

Write down the system of equations

$(1)\quad 4\alpha + \beta = 5$

$(2)\quad 7\alpha + 3\beta = 5$

💡 Hint: We have two linear equations with two unknowns, $\alpha$ and $\beta$. We can solve them by elimination.
2

Eliminate $\alpha$ by making coefficients equal

Multiply equation (1) by 7 and equation (2) by 4:

$(1)\times 7:\quad 28\alpha + 7\beta = 35$

$(2)\times 4:\quad 28\alpha + 12\beta = 20$

💡 Hint: We choose multipliers so that the coefficient of $\alpha$ becomes the same in both equations. Then we can subtract to eliminate $\alpha$.
3

Subtract to eliminate $\alpha$

Perform $(2)\times 4 - (1)\times 7$:

$(28\alpha + 12\beta) - (28\alpha + 7\beta) = 20 - 35$

$28\alpha - 28\alpha + 12\beta - 7\beta = -15$

$5\beta = -15$

💡 Hint: Always subtract the smaller multiple from the larger to keep the arithmetic clean. Here $12\beta - 7\beta = 5\beta$.
4

Solve for $\beta$

$5\beta = -15$

$\beta = \dfrac{-15}{5}$

$\beta = -3$

💡 Hint: Divide both sides by 5. The negative sign stays with the result.
5

Conclusion & Answer

Therefore, $\beta = -3$.

Checking the options: A. $-3$ is correct.

✅ The correct answer is A.
