Statistics — Mean, Median & Unknown Data Values
Mean Median Ordering Data Unknown Values m, n Multiple Choice

Question

Consider the following data:

\[ 19 \quad 10 \quad 12 \quad 12 \quad 13 \quad 13 \quad 14 \quad 15 \quad 16 \quad m \quad n \]

If both the mean and the median of the above data are 14, which of the following are true?

  • I. \(\; m \ge 14\)
  • II. \(\; n \le 16\)
  • III. \(\; m + n = 30\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

Part A — Using the Mean to Relate \(m\) and \(n\)

The mean of the 11 numbers being \(14\) immediately gives a linear relation between \(m\) and \(n\). Drag the slider to explore the line \(m+n=30\).

Step 1Understanding

There are \(11\) numbers in total. If the mean is \(14\), the sum of all data values must be:

\[ 11 \times 14 = 154 \]
Hint: Mean \(\times\) number of data = total sum. Always set up the total sum equation first.
Step 2

Add the eight known numbers:

\[ 19+10+12+12+13+13+14+15+16 = 124 \]
Hint: Add carefully — 19 + 10 = 29, grow the running total to 124. The two unknown values \(m\) and \(n\) are not yet included.
Step 3

So the total sum is \(124 + m + n\), and this must equal \(154\):

\[ 124 + m + n = 154 \implies m+n = 30 \]
Hence statement III is TRUE: \(m+n = 30\).
Hint: The mean condition gives exactly ONE equation. We still need the median condition to bound \(m\) and \(n\) individually.
Step 4Conclusion

This step is quick but important: the mean alone already eliminates any option that does not contain III.

\[ \Rightarrow m+n = 30 \quad \checkmark \text{ (III)} \]
Hint: Since mean = 14 is GIVEN, \(m+n=30\) is guaranteed. Only options B, C, D contain III.

Part B — Using the Median to Bound \(m\) and \(n\)

Step 1Understanding

The 11 numbers, sorted, are:

\[ a_1 \le a_2 \le \cdots \le a_{11} \]

Since \(n=11\) is odd, the median is the 6th smallest value, i.e. \(a_6\).

Hint: For an odd number \(2k+1\) of data, the median is the \((k+1)\)-th value in order. Here \(k+1 = 6\).
Step 2

List the eight known numbers in ascending order and mark their positions:

\[ 10,\ 12,\ 12,\ 13,\ 13,\ 14,\ 15,\ 16,\ 19 \]

These occupy some of the 11 positions, but the unknown \(m\) and \(n\) may be inserted anywhere.

Hint: \(19\) is the largest known value; make sure your sorted list has all nine known numbers in the correct order.
Step 3

We require \(a_6 = 14\). Consider the known data around 14:

There are five known values less than or equal to 14: \(10,12,12,13,13\), plus one known \(14\), so \(14\) is already the 6th known value in the sorted list of known numbers — but only if both \(m\) and \(n\) are ≥ 14 or one of them sits exactly at the boundary.

Hint: Think of placing \(m\) and \(n\) into the sorted list. If either falls below 14, the count of values ≤ 14 increases and the 6th value would drop below 14.
Step 4

Step 5

From \(14 \le m \le 16\) we get \(m \ge 14\) — statement I is TRUE.
From \(14 \le n \le 16\) we get \(n \le 16\) — statement II is TRUE.

I ✓   II ✓   III ✓   \(\Rightarrow\) Answer: D
Hint: All three statements follow, so the correct option is D (I, II and III).

Summary — Answers at a Glance

  • Mean condition: \(\dfrac{124+m+n}{11}=14 \;\Rightarrow\; m+n=30\)  (III ✓)
  • Median condition (11 numbers \(\Rightarrow\) median is the 6th value): both unknowns must be at least 14 \(\Rightarrow m \ge 14\) (I ✓) and \(n=30-m \le 16\) (II ✓).
  • Full range: \(14 \le m \le 16\) and \(14 \le n \le 16\), with \(m+n=30\).
  • Correct option: D — I, II and III
