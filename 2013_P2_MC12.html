HKDSE Math 2013 Paper 2 Q12

Topic: Rate, Ratio and Variations · Scale of a Map

Ratio Area Scale Unit Conversion

The actual area of a playground is 900 m². If the area of the playground on a map is 36 cm², then the scale of the map is

A. 1 : 25
B. 1 : 50
C. 1 : 500
D. 1 : 250 000

Correct answer: C

1 Step-by-step solution

A map scale (e.g. \(1:n\)) compares a length on the map to the corresponding actual length.

If the linear scale is \(1:n\), then the area scale is:

Area scale = \(1 : n^2\)

💡 Hint: Always check that both areas are in the same unit before comparing.

Actual area = \(900 \text{ m}^2\). Since \(1 \text{ m} = 100 \text{ cm}\):

\(900 \text{ m}^2 = 900 \times (100 \text{ cm})^2 = 900 \times 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2 = 9\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^2\)

💡 Key idea: \(1 \text{ m}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2\), not \(100 \text{ cm}^2\).

Map area = \(36 \text{ cm}^2\) · Actual area = \(9\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^2\).

\(\displaystyle \frac{\text{Map area}}{\text{Actual area}} = \frac{36}{9\,000\,000}\)

Simplify the fraction:

\(\displaystyle \frac{36}{9\,000\,000} = \frac{1}{250\,000}\)

Area scale = \(1 : 250\,000\). The linear scale is the square root of the area scale:

\(\displaystyle \text{Linear scale} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{250\,000}}\)
\(\displaystyle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{250\,000}} = \frac{1}{500}\)

Thus the scale of the map is \(1 : 500\) — option C.

A linear scale of \(1:500\) gives an area scale of \(1:250\,000\).

Check: \(\displaystyle 900 \text{ m}^2 \times \frac{1}{250\,000} = 0.0036 \text{ m}^2 = 36 \text{ cm}^2\). ✅

✅ Final answer: C. \(1:500\)

📌 Key takeaway

  • Linear scale \(1:n\) → Area scale \(1:n^2\).
  • Always convert areas to the same unit before taking the ratio.
  • To find \(n\) from an area ratio, take the square root.
