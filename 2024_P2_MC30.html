Statistics — Mean, Mode, Median & Standard Deviation of a Data Set
Mean Mode Median Standard Deviation Multiple Choice

QQuestion

Consider the following positive integers:

\[ 5 \quad 5 \quad 5 \quad 6 \quad 9 \quad 9 \quad 11 \quad 13 \quad m \quad n \]

Let \(p\), \(q\) and \(r\) be the standard deviation, the mode and the median of the above positive integers respectively. If the mean of the above positive integers is \(7\), which of the following must be true?

  • I. \(\; p > 3\)
  • II. \(\; q = 5\)
  • III. \(\; r < 7\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

1The Mean ConditionKey Step

Use the given mean \(=7\) to find a relation between \(m\) and \(n\).

Mean of 10 numbers = 7.00 Sum \(= 5+5+5+6+9+9+11+13 = \mathbf{63}\) \(m+n = \) 7
Bar chart of the ten numbers, with the mean line at 7.
Step 1

Write the mean equation and multiply both sides by \(10\):

\[ \frac{5+5+5+6+9+9+11+13+m+n}{10}=7 \] \[ 63+m+n=70 \]
Hint: Sum the ten known-and-unknown numbers, then equate to \(10\times 7 = 70\).
Step 2

Hence

\[ m+n = 70-63 = 7. \]
\(m+n=7\)  (and \(m,n\) are positive integers, so \(1\le m,n\le 6\)).

2Finding the Mode \(q\)Statement II

Explore how the frequency of each value changes as \(m\) and \(n\) vary with \(m+n=7\).

1 6
Data set: 5 5 5 6 9 9 11 13 1 6 Max frequency = 3 (value 5) Mode \(q = \) 5
Frequency chart — bars for \(5,6,9,11,13\) plus the chosen \(m\) and \(n\). The tallest bar determines the mode.
Step 1

Since \(m,n\le 6\), neither \(m\) nor \(n\) can equal \(11\) or \(13\), and at most one of them can equal \(6\) or \(9\). So no value besides \(5\) can reach a frequency of 3.

Hint: Initially \(5\) appears three times. For another value to become the mode it would need 4 occurrences — impossible here.
Step 2

Therefore \(5\) appears most often, so

\[ q = 5. \]
Statement II (\(q=5\)) is true.

With 10 numbers, the median is the mean of the 5th and 6th values after sorting. Slide to see where \(m\) and \(n\) land.

Number line of the sorted data — the 5th and 6th positions (highlighted) always lie below 7.
Step 1

Arrange the data in ascending order. Because \(m,n\le 6\), they can only sit among the small values, so the sorted sequence starts with the four values from \(\{5,5,5\}\cup\{m,n\}\) that are \(\le 5\), then \(6\)'s, etc. The 5th and 6th values are therefore at most \(6\) and \(9\).

Hint: The largest possible 6th value occurs when both \(m\) and \(n\) equal \(6\); even then the 6th value is \(6\), giving \(r=6\).
Step 2

Checking the extreme case \(m=n=6\) (not allowed, since \(m+n=7\), but it bounds the median) as well as the valid cases \((m,n)=(1,6),(2,5),(3,4)\):

4Checking the Standard Deviation \(p\)Statement I

Compute \(p\) for each possible pair \((m,n)\) and test whether \(p>3\).

(m,n) = (1,6)
Mean \(=7\) \(\sum(x-\bar x)^2 = \) 100.5 \(p=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sum(x-\bar x)^2}{10}} = \) 3.17 \(p>3\) ?
Deviation bar chart: each \((x-7)\) for the chosen \((m,n)\). The spread shrinks as \(m\) and \(n\) move toward 5.
Step 1

Use \(p=\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{\sum (x-\bar x)^2}{10}}\) with \(\bar x = 7\). The fixed part of the sum of squares is

\[ (5-7)^2\!\times\!3+(6-7)^2+(9-7)^2\!\times\!2+(11-7)^2+(13-7)^2 = 12+1+8+16+36 = 73. \]
Hint: Only the terms for \(m\) and \(n\) change; everything else is constant at \(73\).
Step 2

Since \(m+n=7\), we have \(n=7-m\), so \((m-7)^2+(n-7)^2 = (m-7)^2+(m)^2\) upon simplification. Evaluate:

✓Summary & Final Answer

  • Mean condition gives \(\;m+n = 7\;\) (so \(1\le m,n\le 6\)).
  • Regularity of the mode: 5 occurs three times and cannot be overtaken ⇒ \(q = 5\). Statement II ✓
  • Regularity of the median: the 5th and 6th sorted values are at most \(6\) ⇒ \(r\le 5.5 < 7\). Statement III ✓
  • Standard deviation: \(p\approx 2.98\) for \((m,n)=(3,4)\), so \(p>3\) fails. Statement I ✗
Answer: C (II and III only)
