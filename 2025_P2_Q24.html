HKDSE 2025 · Paper 2 · MC24 — Equilateral Triangle in Polar Coordinates

X (1, 20°) Y (2, 80°) Z (r, θ) OX OY OZ XY / XZ / YZ
QUESTION

The polar coordinates of the points $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ are $\left(1,20^{\circ}\right)$, $\left(2,80^{\circ}\right)$ and $\left(r,\theta\right)$ respectively, where $20^{\circ}<\theta<80^{\circ}$ and $r>0$. If $XYZ$ is an equilateral triangle and $O$ is the pole, find $r$.

A. $\sqrt{3}$
B. $\sqrt{5}$
C. $\sqrt{7}$
D. $\sqrt{10}$
STEP 1 · SIDE LENGTH OF THE EQUILATERAL TRIANGLE
Cosine Formula \(XY^{2}=OX^{2}+OY^{2}-2(OX)(OY)\cos\angle XOY\) The two radii and the included angle \(60^{\circ}\) give the side of the equilateral triangle.

$\angle XOY = 80^{\circ}-20^{\circ}=60^{\circ}$. By the cosine rule in $\triangle XOY$:

$$XY^{2}=1^{2}+2^{2}-2(1)(2)\cos 60^{\circ}=1+4-2=3 \implies XY=\sqrt{3}.$$

So $XZ=YZ=\sqrt{3}$ as well.

STEP 2 · SET UP EQUATIONS FOR Z = (r, θ)

Let $u=\theta-20^{\circ}$;
then $\theta-80^{\circ}=u-60^{\circ}$.

$$XZ^{2}:\quad r^{2}+1-2r\cos u=3 \implies r^{2}-2=2r\cos u \quad (1)$$

$$YZ^{2}:\quad r^{2}+4-4r\cos(u-60^{\circ})=3 \implies r^{2}+1=4r\cos(u-60^{\circ}) \quad (2)$$

STEP 3 · ELIMINATE cos u
Compound Angle Formula \(\cos(u-60^{\circ})=\tfrac{1}{2}\cos u+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin u\) Expanding the shifted cosine lets the two distance equations be combined.

Expand (2) using $\cos(u-60^{\circ})=\tfrac{1}{2}\cos u+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin u$:

$$r^{2}+1=2r\cos u+2\sqrt{3}\,r\sin u.$$

Substitute (1) ($2r\cos u=r^{2}-2$):

$$r^{2}+1=r^{2}-2+2\sqrt{3}\,r\sin u \implies 2\sqrt{3}\,r\sin u=3 \implies \sin u=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2r}.$$

From (1): $\cos u=\dfrac{r^{2}-2}{2r}$.

STEP 4 · USE sin²u + cos²u = 1
Trigonometric Identity \(\sin^{2}u+\cos^{2}u=1\) Eliminating \(u\) leaves a polynomial equation in \(r\) alone.

$$\frac{3}{4r^{2}}+\frac{(r^{2}-2)^{2}}{4r^{2}}=1 \implies 3+(r^{2}-2)^{2}=4r^{2}.$$

Expanding:

$$r^{4}-4r^{2}+4+3-4r^{2}=0 \implies r^{4}-8r^{2}+7=0$$

$$(r^{2}-1)(r^{2}-7)=0 \implies r=1\ \text{or}\ r=\sqrt{7}.$$

STEP 5 · APPLY THE RESTRICTION 20° < θ < 80°

If $r=1$: $\cos u=\frac{1-2}{2}=-\tfrac{1}{2}$, $\sin u=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\implies u=120^{\circ}\implies\theta=140^{\circ}$ — rejected.

If $r=\sqrt{7}$: $\cos u=\frac{5}{2\sqrt{7}}$, $\sin u=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{7}}\implies u\approx 19.1^{\circ}\implies\theta\approx 39.1^{\circ}$ — accepted.

💡 Key ideas:
(i) the angle between the two given radii is the difference of the polar angles;
(ii) an equilateral triangle makes all three chords equal, so $XZ=YZ=XY$;
(iii) using $\sin^2u+\cos^2u=1$ converts the system into a quadratic in $r^2$.
Final Answer: C. $\;r=\sqrt{7}$  (with $\theta\approx 39.1^{\circ}$)

Question. The polar coordinates of \(X\) and \(Y\) are \(\left(2,0^{\circ}\right)\) and \(\left(3,60^{\circ}\right)\). The point \(Z\) has polar coordinates \(\left(r,\theta\right)\), where \(0^{\circ}<\theta<60^{\circ}\) and \(r>0\). If \(XYZ\) is an equilateral triangle and \(O\) is the pole, find \(r\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(XY^{2}=2^{2}+3^{2}-2(2)(3)\cos 60^{\circ}=4+9-6=7\), so \(XZ=YZ=\sqrt{7}\).
Step 2. \(XZ^{2}\): \(r^{2}+4-4r\cos\theta=7\), i.e. \(r^{2}-3=4r\cos\theta\).
Step 3. \(YZ^{2}\): \(r^{2}+9-6r\cos(\theta-60^{\circ})=7\), i.e. \(r^{2}+2=6r\cos(\theta-60^{\circ})\). Expand: \(r^{2}+2=3r\cos\theta+3\sqrt{3}\,r\sin\theta\).
Step 4. Substitute \(3r\cos\theta=\tfrac{3}{4}\left(r^{2}-3\right)\): \(\dfrac{r^{2}+17}{4}=3\sqrt{3}\,r\sin\theta\), while \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{r^{2}-3}{4r}\).
Step 5. Use \(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta=1\): \(\dfrac{(r^{2}+17)^{2}}{432r^{2}}+\dfrac{(r^{2}-3)^{2}}{16r^{2}}=1\), giving \(28r^{4}-560r^{2}+532=0\), i.e. \(r^{4}-20r^{2}+19=0\).
Step 6. \((r^{2}-1)(r^{2}-19)=0\). If \(r=1\), then \(\cos\theta=-\tfrac12\) and \(\theta=120^{\circ}\), rejected.
Hence \(r=\sqrt{19}\) (with \(\theta\approx 23.4^{\circ}\)).
Answer: \(r=\sqrt{19}\)
