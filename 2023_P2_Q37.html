Question

Consider the following system of inequalities:

$x+3 \ge 0$
$2x+3y-12 \le 0$
$5x-3y+12 \le 0$

Let $D$ be the region which represents the solution of the above system of inequalities. Find the range of values of $\beta$ such that the greatest value of $\beta x + 6y$ is $24$, where $(x,y)$ is a point lying in $D$.

A. $\beta \ge 4$
B. $\beta \ge -10$
C. $\beta \le 4$
D. $\beta \le -10$
Step 1 · Find the vertices of D

Rewrite the three boundary lines and intersect them:

$L_1:\;x=-3$,
  $L_2:\;2x+3y=12$,   $L_3:\;5x-3y=-12$.

$L_1\cap L_2:\;2(-3)+3y=12 \Rightarrow y=6$  $\Rightarrow P(-3,\,6)$.
$L_2\cap L_3$: adding, $7x=0 \Rightarrow x=0,\; y=4$  $\Rightarrow Q(0,\,4)$.
$L_1\cap L_3:\;5(-3)-3y=-12 \Rightarrow -3y=3 \Rightarrow y=-1$  $\Rightarrow R(-3,\,-1)$.

Testing a point inside (e.g. $(-2,0)$) satisfies all three,
so $D$ is the triangle with vertices $P(-3,6)$, $Q(0,4)$, $R(-3,-1)$.

Hint: The feasible region of three linear inequalities is always a polygon — here a triangle.
Step 2 · Evaluate the objective at each vertex
Vertex Principle of Linear Programming \(\max Z=\max\left\{Z(P),\ Z(Q),\ Z(R)\right\}\) A linear function on a polygon attains its greatest value at a vertex, so only three values matter.

For $Z=\beta x+6y$:

$Z(P)=\beta(-3)+6(6)=-3\beta+36$
$Z(Q)=\beta(0)+6(4)=24$
$Z(R)=\beta(-3)+6(-1)=-3\beta-6$

The maximum of a linear function over a polygon occurs at a vertex,
so $\max Z=\max\{-3\beta+36,\;24,\;-3\beta-6\}$.

Hint: at $Q(0,4)$ the value is $24$ regardless of $\beta$ — that is exactly why the question sets the maximum equal to $24$.
Step 3 · Require the maximum to equal 24
Solving a Linear Inequality \(-3\beta\le-12\ \Longrightarrow\ \beta\ge 4\) Dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

We need $\max\{-3\beta+36,\,24,\,-3\beta-6\}=24$, i.e. both other vertices must not exceed $24$:

$-3\beta+36 \le 24 \;\Rightarrow\; -3\beta \le -12 \;\Rightarrow\; \beta \ge 4$.
$-3\beta-6 \le 24 \;\Rightarrow\; -3\beta \le 30 \;\Rightarrow\; \beta \ge -10$.

Both conditions must hold, so $\beta \ge 4$ (the stronger one).

Hint: the value $24$ at $Q$ must be tied for maximum — every other vertex value must be $\le 24$.
Step 4 · Geometry check & conclusion

The level lines $\beta x+6y=c$ have slope $-\beta/6$. The edge $PR$ (the line $x=-3$) is vertical,
so raising $\beta$ makes the objective direction more like the $x$-direction and pushes the optimum from $P$ to $Q$.

For $\beta=4$: $Z(P)=-12+36=24=Z(Q)$ — the level line $4x+6y=24$ passes through the whole edge $PQ$,
still giving maximum $24$.

Hint: the answer is the range of $\beta$ for which $Q(0,4)$ attains the greatest value $24$.
Answer $\beta \ge 4$  —  Option A.
