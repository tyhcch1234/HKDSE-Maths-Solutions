HKDSE Math 2012 Paper 2 Q31

Polynomials · HCF & LCM

The H.C.F. and the L.C.M. of three expressions are $ab^2$ and $4a^4b^5c^6$ respectively. If the first expression and the second expression are $2a^2b^4c$ and $4a^4b^2c^6$ respectively, then the third expression is

A. $ab^2$
B. $ab^5$
C. $2ab^2c$
D. $2ab^5c$

Key concepts: HCF, LCM, prime factorisation, powers (min/max).

Step-by-step guided solution

1 Write prime factorisation of H.C.F. and L.C.M.

Express H.C.F. and L.C.M. in factorised form:

$$ \text{H.C.F.} = a^1 b^2 c^0 \quad\text{(so $c$ does not appear)} $$ $$ \text{L.C.M.} = 4a^4 b^5 c^6 = 2^2 \cdot a^4 \cdot b^5 \cdot c^6 $$
💡 Hint: The H.C.F. is the product of the lowest powers of each prime factor present in all expressions. The L.C.M. is the product of the highest powers.
2 Factorise the two given expressions

First expression: $E_1 = 2a^2b^4c = 2^1\,a^2\,b^4\,c^1$

Second expression: $E_2 = 4a^4b^2c^6 = 2^2\,a^4\,b^2\,c^6$

We will use these to determine the powers of $E_3$.

3 Set up the third expression

Let the third expression be $E_3 = 2^p \, a^q \, b^r \, c^s$, where $p,q,r,s$ are non-negative integers.

💡 Hint: We don't know the exponents yet — we'll determine them using the definition of H.C.F. (minimum) and L.C.M. (maximum).
4 Use H.C.F. to constrain powers (lower bounds)

The H.C.F. of the three expressions is $a^1 b^2 c^0$ (no $c$).
This means the minimum exponent among the three expressions for each factor is:

$$ \min(1,2,p)=1 \quad\text{? Wait! Check carefully:} $$

Actually, for factor $a$: exponents from $E_1,E_2,E_3$ are $2,4,q$. The minimum is $1$ (from H.C.F. $a^1$).
So $\min(2,4,q) = 1$. Since $\min(2,4)=2$, the only way the overall min is 1 is if $q \le 1$ and the minimum becomes 1. But $q$ must be $\ge 0$. So $q$ could be 0 or 1? Let's check: if $q=0$, min(2,4,0)=0 → H.C.F. would have $a^0$ (i.e., no $a$). If $q=1$, min(2,4,1)=1. So $q$ must be 1.
Similarly, for $b$: $\min(4,2,r)=2$ (since H.C.F. has $b^2$). We know $\min(4,2)=2$, so any $r \ge 2$ works. So $r \ge 2$.
For $c$: $\min(1,6,s)=0$ (H.C.F. has no $c$). Thus $s=0$.

💡 Result from H.C.F.: $q=1$, $r\ge 2$, $s=0$. Also $p$ not yet determined (since $2$ is a constant, H.C.F. has no factor of 2 → $\min(1,2,p)=0$? Wait check: H.C.F. is $ab^2$ — it has no factor 2. So the minimum power of 2 among the three expressions must be 0. The powers are $1,2,p$. For min to be 0, we need $p=0$. So $p=0$.)
5 Use L.C.M. to fix the remaining powers (upper bounds)

L.C.M. $= 4a^4 b^5 c^6 = 2^2 a^4 b^5 c^6$.

So the maximum exponent among the three expressions is:

  • For factor 2: $\max(1,2,p) = 2$. Since $\max(1,2)=2$, any $p \le 2$ works. But from H.C.F. we already got $p=0$ (which satisfies $0 \le 2$). So $p=0$.
  • For factor $a$: $\max(2,4,q) = 4$. Since $\max(2,4)=4$, $q \le 4$. We already have $q=1$ from H.C.F., which satisfies $1 \le 4$.
  • For factor $b$: $\max(4,2,r) = 5$. We know $\max(4,2)=4$, so we need $r=5$ to reach the maximum 5.
  • For factor $c$: $\max(1,6,s) = 6$. Since $\max(1,6)=6$, $s \le 6$. We already have $s=0$, which satisfies.
💡 Conclusion: $p=0$, $q=1$, $r=5$, $s=0$.
6 Write the third expression

Substitute $p=0, q=1, r=5, s=0$ into $E_3 = 2^p a^q b^r c^s$:

$$ E_3 = 2^0 \cdot a^1 \cdot b^5 \cdot c^0 = ab^5 $$
✅ Answer: B. $ab^5$
