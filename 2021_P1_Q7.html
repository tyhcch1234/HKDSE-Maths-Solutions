HKDSE 2021 · Paper 1 · Q7

Polar Coordinates

Question

In a polar coordinate system, \( O \) is the pole. The polar coordinates of the points \( P \) and \( Q \) are \( \left(r,80^{\circ}\right) \) and \( \left(r,140^{\circ}\right) \) respectively, where \( r \) is a positive constant. It is given that the distance between \( P \) and \( Q \) is 21. Find

  1. \( \angle POQ \),
  2. \( r \),
  3. the perimeter of \( \triangle OPQ \). (4 marks)

Solution

STEP 1 · Part (a) — the angle at the pole The two position vectors make angles \( 80^{\circ} \) and \( 140^{\circ} \) with the initial line, so \[ \angle POQ = 140^{\circ}-80^{\circ} = 60^{\circ}. \]
STEP 2 · Part (b) — find \( r \) Since \( OP = OQ = r \), \( \triangle OPQ \) is isosceles. By the cosine rule on \( \triangle OPQ \): \[ PQ^{2} = r^{2}+r^{2}-2r^{2}\cos 60^{\circ} = 2r^{2}-r^{2} = r^{2}, \] so \( PQ = r \). Given \( PQ = 21 \), \[ r = 21. \] (Equivalently: an isosceles triangle with vertex angle \( 60^{\circ} \) is equilateral.)
STEP 3 · Part (c) — the perimeter All three sides of \( \triangle OPQ \) equal 21, so the perimeter is \[ 21+21+21 = 63. \]
Final Answer: (a) \( 60^{\circ} \)  |  (b) \( r = 21 \)  |  (c) \( 63 \)
