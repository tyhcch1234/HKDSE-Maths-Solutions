Topic 15 · Statistics (mean, mode, median, range)

Consider the following positive integers:

2   3   4   6   7   9   10   m   n

Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be the mode, the median and the range of the above positive integers respectively. If the mean of the above positive integers is 5, which of the following must be true?

I.   $a = 2$
II.   $b = 4$
III.   $c = 8$

A. I only    B. II only    C. I and III only    D. II and III only

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Use the mean to relate $m$ and $n$.
Mean of a Data Set \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum x}{n}\) With \(9\) values and mean \(5\) the total must be \(45\); the seven known numbers add to \(41\), so \(m+n=4\).
$$\frac{2+3+4+6+7+9+10+m+n}{9}=5$$ $$41 + m + n = 45 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m+n = 4$$
Hint: sum the seven known numbers first ($=41$), then solve for $m+n$.
Step 2List all positive-integer pairs with $m+n=4$.
$(m,n)=(1,3),\ (2,2),\ (3,1)$
Hint: $m,n$ are positive integers, so only these three cases exist — test each one.
Step 3Compute $a$ (mode), $b$ (median), $c$ (range) for each case.
Range of a Data Set \(\text{range}=\text{maximum}-\text{minimum}\) Here the minimum is \(1\) and the maximum is \(10\), so the range is \(9\) in the cases \((1,3)\) and \((3,1)\).
Median of an Odd Number of Data \(\text{median}=x_{\frac{n+1}{2}}\) With \(9\) values the median is always the 5th value in ascending order, whichever pair \((m,n)\) is used.

Question. The eleven numbers \(5,\ 9,\ 10,\ 10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13,\ 14,\ 15,\ m,\ n\) have mean \(12\) and median \(12\), where \(m\) and \(n\) are integers.
(a) Find \(m+n\).
(b) Show that \(m\ge12\) and \(n\ge12\).
(c) Find the least possible value of the range of the data.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) There are \(11\) values, so the total is \(12\times11=132\). The nine known values add to \(99\), hence \(99+m+n=132\), i.e. \(m+n=33\).
Step 2. (b) With \(11\) values the median is the 6th value. Exactly five of the known values are below \(12\) (\(5,9,10,10,11\)), so if either \(m\) or \(n\) were below \(12\) the 6th value would be less than \(12\). Hence \(m\ge12\) and \(n\ge12\).
Step 3. (c) The minimum is \(5\), so the range is \(\text{maximum}-5\). Since \(m+n=33>2\times16\), at least one of \(m,n\) exceeds \(16\), so the maximum is at least \(17\).
Step 4. Taking \(m=16,\ n=17\) gives maximum \(17\) and range \(17-5=12\), so the least possible range is \(12\).
Step 5. Check: \(5,9,10,10,11,12,13,14,15,16,17\) has mean \(\dfrac{132}{11}=12\) ✓ and median \(12\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(m+n=33\) (b) shown (c) least range \(=12\)
