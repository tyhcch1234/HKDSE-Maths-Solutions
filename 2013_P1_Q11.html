HKDSE 2013 P1 Q11

The weight of a tray of perimeter $\ell$ metres is $W$ grams. It is given that $W$ is the sum of two parts, one part varies directly as $\ell$ and the other part varies directly as $\ell^{2}$. When $\ell=1$, $W=181$ and when $\ell=2$, $W=402$.

(a) Find the weight of a tray of perimeter $1.2$ metres. [4 marks]

(b) If the weight of a tray is $594$ grams, find the perimeter of the tray. [2 marks]

1Set up the variation model.
Partial Variation \(z=k_{1}x+k_{2}x^{2}\) The weight is a sum of one part varying directly as \(\ell\) and one varying directly as \(\ell^{2}\).

"Sum of two parts": one varies directly as $\ell$, the other directly as $\ell^{2}$:

$$W=h\ell+k\ell^{2},\qquad h\text{ and }k\text{ are nonzero constants.}$$

Hint: "sum of two parts" means you ADD the two direct variations together.
2Substitute the two given data pairs.

Put $(\ell,W)=(1,181)$ and $(2,402)$ into the model:

$$\begin{cases} h(1)+k(1^{2})=181\\ h(2)+k(2^{2})=402\end{cases} \;\Longrightarrow\; \begin{cases} h+k=181 \quad \cdots(1)\\ 2h+4k=402 \;\Rightarrow\; h+2k=201 \quad \cdots(2)\end{cases}$$

Hint: simplify equation (2) by dividing every term by 2 before solving.
3Solve the simultaneous equations.
Elimination Method \(a_{1}x+b_{1}y=c_{1},\quad a_{2}x+b_{2}y=c_{2}\) The \(h\)-terms already match, so subtracting \((1)\) from \((2)\) leaves \(k\) alone.

$(2)-(1)$: $\;k = 201-181 = 20$.

Substitute $k=20$ into $(1)$: $\;h = 181-20 = 161$.

$$\boxed{\,W=161\ell+20\ell^{2}\,}$$

Hint: eliminating $h$ is fastest here — the $h$ terms are already matched.
4(a) Weight when $\ell=1.2$ m.

$$W=161(1.2)+20(1.2)^{2}=193.2+20(1.44)=193.2+28.8=222$$

The required weight is $\mathbf{222}$ grams.

Hint: do not drop the units — the answer is a weight in grams.
5(b) Solve $W=594$ for $\ell$.
Cross Method \(acx^{2}+(ad+bc)x+bd=(ax+b)(cx+d)\) For \(20\ell^{2}+161\ell-594\) the cross terms \(216\ell-55\ell\) give \(161\ell\).

$$20\ell^{2}+161\ell=594$$

$$20\ell^{2}+161\ell-594=0$$

Factorise: $\;20\ell^{2}+161\ell-594=(4\ell-11)(5\ell+54)$.

Hint: for $20\ell^{2}+161\ell-594$, try $(4\ell-11)(5\ell+54)$: cross terms give $216\ell-55\ell=161\ell$. ✓
6Reject the invalid root and conclude.

$$(4\ell-11)(5\ell+54)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \ell=\frac{11}{4}\quad\text{or}\quad \ell=-\frac{54}{5}$$

Since $\ell$ is a perimeter (a length), $\ell>0$, so $\ell=-\dfrac{54}{5}$ is rejected.

Thus the perimeter of the tray is $\dfrac{11}{4}$ metres (i.e. $2.75$ m).

Hint: always check the physical meaning — a perimeter cannot be negative.
Answer:  (a) $222$ grams   |   (b) $\dfrac{11}{4}$ metres ($=2.75$ m)

Question. The cost \(\$C\) of printing a banner is the sum of a part varying directly as its width \(w\) metres and a part varying directly as \(w^{2}\). When \(w=1\), \(C=32\); when \(w=2\), \(C=104\). (a) Find \(C\) when \(w=1.5\). (b) Find \(w\) when \(C=560\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write \(C=hw+kw^{2}\) with \(h,k\) nonzero constants.
Step 2. Substituting \((1,32)\) and \((2,104)\): \(h+k=32\) and \(2h+4k=104\), i.e. \(h+2k=52\).
Step 3. Subtracting gives \(k=20\), then \(h=12\); so \(C=12w+20w^{2}\).
Step 4. When \(w=1.5\): \(C=12(1.5)+20(2.25)=18+45=63\).
Step 5. When \(C=560\): \(20w^{2}+12w-560=0\), i.e. \(5w^{2}+3w-140=0\), so \((w-5)(5w+28)=0\). Since \(w>0\), \(w=5\).
Answer: (a) \(\$63\) (b) \(w=5\)
