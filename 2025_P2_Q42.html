HKDSE Math 2025 Paper 2 Q42

Probability Combinations Counting

Question

A committee is formed by 2 managers, 4 officers and 12 clerks. If 7 members are selected from the committee to form a working group consisting of at least 1 manager, how many different working groups can be formed?

A. 16016
B. 20384
C. 22880
D. 31824
1

Understand the Counting Problem

We have a committee with 2 managers, 4 officers, and 12 clerks — a total of 18 people.

We need to select 7 members such that the group contains at least 1 manager.

Key idea: Use the complement method — count all possible groups, then subtract the groups with no manager.

2

Count ALL possible 7-member groups

Choose any 7 people from the 18 committee members.

\[ \text{Total groups} = C^{18}_{7} = C^{18}_{7} \]

💡 Hint: The formula for combinations is \( C^n_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \). Here \(n = 18\), \(r = 7\).

\( C^{18}_{7} = 31\,824 \)

3

Count groups with NO managers (the complement)

If there are no managers, we must choose all 7 from the remaining 16 people (4 officers + 12 clerks).

\[ \text{No-manager groups} = C^{16}_{7} = C^{16}_{7} \]

💡 Hint: Why 16? Because we exclude the 2 managers entirely: \(18 - 2 = 16\).

\( C^{16}_{7} = 11\,440 \)

4

Subtract to find the final answer

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Required groups} &= \text{Total groups} - \text{No-manager groups} \\ &= C^{18}_{7} - C^{16}_{7} \\ &= 31\,824 - 11\,440 \\ &= \mathbf{20\,384} \end{aligned} \]

The answer is B. 20384.
