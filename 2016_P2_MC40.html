1 Step 1 — The Isosceles Triangle \(PBD\)

Equal tangents from an external point create an isosceles triangle.

Step 1 Tangents from an external point are equal
\[ PB = PD \qquad (\text{tangent properties}) \]
Hint: Two tangents drawn from the same external point \(P\) to a circle are equal in length. So \(\triangle PBD\) is isosceles with apex at \(P\).
Step 2 Base angles of an isosceles triangle
\[ \therefore \angle PBD = \angle PDB \qquad (\text{base }\angle\text{s, isos. } \triangle) \]
Hint: The equal sides \(PB\) and \(PD\) means the angles opposite them are equal — the base angles sit on the chord \(BD\).
Step 3 Angle sum of \(\triangle PBD\)
\[ \begin{aligned} \angle PBD + \angle PDB &= 180^\circ - \angle BPD \qquad (\angle \text{sum of } \triangle)\\ &= 180^\circ - 68^\circ\\ &= 112^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Subtract the given vertex angle from \(180^\circ\) to get the sum of the two equal base angles.
Step 4 Divide equally
\[ \therefore \angle PBD = 112^\circ \div 2 = 56^\circ \]
Result: \(\angle PBD = \angle PDB = 56^\circ\)
Hint: Since the two base angles are equal, each is half of \(112^\circ\).

Hint: The angle between the tangent \(PB\) and the chord \(BD\) equals the angle subtended by that chord in the opposite (alternate) segment — that is \(\angle BAD\).

3 Step 3 — Angle in Semi-circle & Final Angle

Use the diameter \(AC\) to get a right angle, then finish in \(\triangle ABQ\).

Step 1 Angle in a semi-circle
\[ \angle ABC = 90^\circ \qquad (\angle \text{ in semi-circle}) \]
Hint: Since \(AC\) is a diameter, the angle subtended by it at any point on the circle — here at \(B\) — is a right angle.
Step 2 Set up \(\triangle ABQ\)
Since \(Q\) lies on \(AD\) produced and \(BC\) produced: \[ \angle BAQ = \angle BAD = 56^\circ,\qquad \angle ABQ = \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]
Hint: \(AQ\) is simply \(AD\) extended, and \(BQ\) is \(BC\) extended — so the angles at \(A\) and \(B\) do not change.
Step 3 Angle sum of \(\triangle ABQ\)
\[ \begin{aligned} \angle AQB &= 180^\circ - \angle ABQ - \angle BAQ \qquad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle)\\ &= 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 56^\circ\\ &= 34^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Answer: \(\angle AQB = 34^\circ\) — option D.
Hint: Everything collapses neatly: the whole question reduces to \(180^\circ - 90^\circ - 56^\circ\).

📌 Summary of Answers

  • Given: \(AC\) is a diameter, \(PB\) & \(PD\) are tangents, \(\angle BPD = 68^\circ\).
  • \(PB = PD\) (tangent properties) \(\Rightarrow \triangle PBD\) is isosceles.
  • \(\angle PBD = \angle PDB = (180^\circ - 68^\circ)\div 2 = 56^\circ\)
  • \(\angle BAD = \angle PBD = 56^\circ\) (angle in alternate segment).
  • \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\) (angle in semi-circle).
  • In \(\triangle ABQ\): \(\angle AQB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 56^\circ = \mathbf{34^\circ}\) D
