HKDSE 2018 · Paper 2 · Q20

Geometry · Similar triangles & Pythagoras in a square with an extended side

Interactive Diagram

Square ABCD Line DE & segment DF EF = 5 cm BE = 4 cm Intersection F

A–B–E are collinear; BC and DE meet at F. Triangle EBF is similar to triangle DCF (rotated).

QUESTION

In the figure, $ABCD$ is a square. $E$ is a point lying on $AB$ produced such that $BE = 4\text{ cm}$. $BC$ and $DE$ intersect at the point $F$. If $EF = 5\text{ cm}$, then $DF =$

A. 9 cm
B. 10 cm
C. 12 cm
D. 15 cm
Step 1 · Identify the similar triangles

Since $ABCD$ is a square, $DC \parallel AB$, so $DC \parallel AE$. Consider the two triangles sharing the vertex $F$:

$$ \triangle EBF \quad\text{and}\quad \triangle DCF $$

Because $DC \parallel AB$ (i.e. $DC \parallel EB$), the corresponding angles are equal:

$$ \angle BEF = \angle CDF \quad (\text{alternate angles}), \qquad $$ $$ \angle BFE = \angle CFD \quad (\text{vertically opposite angles}) $$

So $\triangle EBF \sim \triangle DCF$ (AA).

Step 2 · Set up the ratio of corresponding sides

The correspondence is $E \leftrightarrow D$, $B \leftrightarrow C$, $F \leftrightarrow F$. Writing $s$ for the side of the square:

$$ \frac{EF}{DF} = \frac{EB}{DC} = \frac{BF}{CF} $$

Hence

$$ \frac{5}{DF} = \frac{4}{s} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad DF = \frac{5s}{4} $$

Step 3 · Use $\triangle EBF$ with $\angle B = 90^\circ$ to find $BF$

Because $ABCD$ is a square, $BC \perp AB$, so $\angle EBF = 90^\circ$ and $\triangle EBF$ is right-angled at $B$. By Pythagoras' theorem:

$$ BF^2 + BE^2 = EF^2 $$

$$ BF^2 + 4^2 = 5^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad BF^2 = 25 - 16 = 9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad BF = 3\text{ cm} $$

Since $BC = s$ and $F$ lies on $BC$, we get $FC = s - 3$.

Step 4 · Use the ratio $\dfrac{BF}{CF}=\dfrac{EB}{DC}$ to solve for $s$

$$ \frac{3}{s-3} = \frac{4}{s} $$

$$ 3s = 4(s-3) = 4s - 12 $$

$$ s = 12\text{ cm} $$

So the side of the square is $12\text{ cm}$ (and $FC = 12-3 = 9\text{ cm}$).

Step 5 · Find $DF$

From Step 2, $DF = \dfrac{5s}{4}$ with $s = 12$:

$$ DF = \frac{5 \times 12}{4} = 15\text{ cm} $$

Check: $\triangle DCF$ is right-angled at $C$ with $DC = 12$, $FC = 9$, so $DF = \sqrt{12^2+9^2} = \sqrt{144+81} = \sqrt{225} = 15$. ✓

Answer
D.  15 cm
Key idea: extending a side of a square creates a pair of similar triangles. Locate the right angle ($\angle EBF = 90^\circ$), use Pythagoras to get a short length, then apply the similarity ratio.
