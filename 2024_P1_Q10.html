HKDSE 2024 · P1 Q10 Topic 2 · Rate, Ratio and Variations

It is given that $g(x)$ is partly constant and partly varies as $x$. Suppose that $g(-3)=-21$ and $g(7)=9$.

(a) Find $g(x)$. (3 marks)

(b) Let $h(x)=x\,g(x)+k$, where $k$ is a real constant. If all the roots of the equation $h(x)=0$ are real numbers, find the range of values of $k$. (3 marks)

Step-by-step Solution
Step 1 · Translate the wording into a model

"Partly constant and partly varies as $x$" means a constant term plus a term proportional to $x$:

$g(x)=a+bx$, where $a$ and $b$ are non-zero constants.

So the graph of $g$ is a straight line with $y$-intercept $a$ and slope $b$.

Hint: “partly constant” → constant term; “varies as $x$” → $bx$.
Step 2 · Substitute the two given points

Using $g(-3)=-21$:   $a-3b=-21$    … (1)

Using $g(7)=9$:   $a+7b=9$    … (2)

These are the two red points shown on the left: $(-3,\,-21)$ and $(7,\,9)$.

Hint: each given value of $x$ gives one equation — two points determine the line.
Step 3 · Solve the simultaneous equations (part a)

$(2)-(1)$:   $10b=30 \Rightarrow b=3$.

Substitute $b=3$ into (2):   $a+21=9 \Rightarrow a=-12$.

Hence   $g(x)=-12+3x$, i.e. $g(x)=3x-12$.

Check: $g(-3)=-9-12=-21$ ✓   $g(7)=21-12=9$ ✓

Hint: eliminate $a$ by subtracting to get $b$ first.
Step 4 · Form the equation $h(x)=0$ (part b)

$h(x)=x\,g(x)+k=0$

$x(3x-12)+k=0$

$3x^{2}-12x+k=0$    — a quadratic in $x$.

Hint: expand $x\,g(x)$ then move everything to one side.
Step 5 · “All roots are real” ⇒ discriminant $\geq 0$

For a quadratic $Ax^{2}+Bx+C=0$, the roots are all real when the discriminant $\Delta=B^{2}-4AC\ge 0$.

Here $A=3$, $B=-12$, $C=k$:

$(-12)^{2}-4(3)(k)\ge 0$

$144-12k\ge 0$

$12k\le 144 \Rightarrow k\le 12$

Hint: “all roots are real” ⇒ $\Delta \geq 0$; remember to divide by a positive number without flipping the inequality.
Step 6 · Interpret the answer graphically

For $k\le 12$, the parabola $y=3x^{2}-12x+k$ meets the $x$-axis (two real roots, or one repeated root when $k=12$).

For $k>12$ there are no real roots, since the whole parabola lies above the $x$-axis.

The green double arrow on the left shows the range $k\le 12$ on the number line.

Hint: $k=12$ is included because a repeated (double) real root still counts as a real root.
(a)  $g(x)=3x-12$
(b)  $k\le 12$ Both parts are fully justified: (a) by solving two simultaneous equations, (b) by the discriminant condition $\Delta\ge 0$.
