Estimation, Approximation and Error

Consider the number $534.7698$.

  1. Round up $534.7698$ to the nearest hundred.
  2. Round down $534.7698$ to $2$ decimal places.
  3. Round off $534.7698$ to $2$ significant figures.

(3 marks)

1Read the digits of the number
$534.7698 = 5\cdot10^{2} + 3\cdot10^{1} + 4\cdot10^{0} + 7\cdot10^{-1} + 6\cdot10^{-2} + 9\cdot10^{-3} + 8\cdot10^{-4}$
Digit positions (left → right):
$5$ (hundreds), $3$ (tens), $4$ (ones), $.7$ (1 d.p.), $6$ (2 d.p.), $9$ (3 d.p.), $8$ (4 d.p.)
💡 Write the number with all digit positions labelled — this tells you which digit each part cares about.
2(a) Round up to the nearest hundred 1A
Rounding Up \(x\ \text{rounded up to the nearest }h=h\left\lceil \dfrac{x}{h}\right\rceil\) Any non-zero tail pushes \(534.7698\) up to the next hundred, \(600\).
The hundreds digit is $5$ (value $500$). The next hundred up is $600$.
"Round up" always moves away from zero to the next hundred, whatever the tail is:
$534.7698 \;\longrightarrow\; \text{next hundred above} \;=\; \boxed{600}$
Check: $500 < 534.7698 < 600$, so rounding up gives $600$.
💡 "Round up" ignores the usual “look at the next digit” rule — you simply jump to the higher target.
3(b) Round down to 2 decimal places 1A
Rounding Down (Truncation) \(x\ \text{rounded down}=\lfloor 10^{d}x\rfloor/10^{d}\) Rounding down simply cuts the digits after the second decimal place, even though the next digit is 9.
Keep only 2 digits after the decimal point:
$534.76\;\underline{9}\;8$ → drop the digits after the 2nd d.p.
"Round down" ignores the tail digits ($9,8$) and keeps the truncated value:
$534.7698 \;\longrightarrow\; \boxed{534.76}$
💡 With “round down” you never look at the $9$ — if you had used normal rounding, $534.77$ would be wrong here.
4(c) Round off to 2 significant figures 1A
Rounding Off to Significant Figures \(\text{next digit}<5\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{keep the digit unchanged}\) To 2 significant figures the first two digits are 5 and 3, and the next digit 4 keeps the 3.
First significant figure is $5$ (hundreds), second is $3$ (tens).
The next digit is $4$, and $4 < 5$, so the $3$ stays unchanged (round down in the significant-figure sense):
$5\;\underline{3}\;4.7698 \;\longrightarrow\; 53 \times 10^{1} \;=\; \boxed{530}$
The trailing zero is required — it holds the place value so that there are exactly 2 significant figures.
💡 “Round off” = ordinary rounding to a given number of significant figures.
Answer: (a) 600
(b) 534.76
(c) 530

Step 2. (b) Rounding down keeps two decimal places and drops the rest: \(728.46\).
