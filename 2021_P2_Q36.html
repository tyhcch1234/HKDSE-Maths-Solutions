HKDSE 2021 Paper 2 — Q36

Question. If $p,\;q,\;r,\;s$ is a geometric sequence, which of the following must be true?

I.   $ps = qr$
II.  $p + s = q + r$
III. $p < q < r < s$

A. I only
B. II only
C. I and III only
D. II and III only
Step 1 · Set up the common ratio

Let $u$ be the common ratio of the geometric sequence. Since $p$ is the first term,

$q = pu,\qquad r = pu^{2},\qquad s = pu^{3}.$

Every term is the previous term multiplied by the same number $u$ — this is the definition of a geometric sequence.
General Term of a Geometric Sequence \(T(n)=pu^{\,n-1}\) With first term \(p\) and common ratio \(u\), the next three terms are \(pu\), \(pu^{2}\), \(pu^{3}\).
Step 2 · Test statement I

Compute the left-hand side and the right-hand side of $ps = qr$:

$\text{L.H.S.} = ps = p\,(pu^{3}) = p^{2}u^{3}$

$\text{R.H.S.} = qr = (pu)(pu^{2}) = p^{2}u^{3}$

Since L.H.S. $=$ R.H.S., statement I is always true ✓.

This holds for every choice of $p$ and $u$ — it is an identity for geometric sequences.
Product of Corresponding Terms \(ps=p^{2}u^{3}=qr\) Both \(p\cdot pu^{3}\) and \(pu\cdot pu^{2}\) equal \(p^{2}u^{3}\), so statement I holds for all \(u\).
Step 3 · Test statement II

Try a concrete case: let $p = 1$ and $u = -2$. Then

$q = -2,\quad r = 4,\quad s = -8.$

$\text{L.H.S.} = p + s = 1 + (-8) = -7$

$\text{R.H.S.} = q + r = (-2) + 4 = 2$

Since $-7 \ne 2$, statement II is not always true ✗.

One counter-example is enough to disprove a “must be true” claim. Note: $p+s=q+r$ only holds when $u=1$ or $u=-1$.
Disproof by Counter-example \(\text{one counter-example is enough to disprove a must-be-true claim}\) With \(p=1\) and \(u=-2\), \(p+s=-7\) while \(q+r=2\), so statement II fails.
Step 4 · Test statement III

Using the same counter-example, $p = 1,\; q = -2,\; r = 4,\; s = -8$:

$1 < -2$ is false,

so the chain $p < q < r < s$ is not always true ✗.

A negative common ratio makes the terms alternate in sign — the sequence is not monotonic.
Step 5 · Conclude

Only statement I is necessarily true. Hence the answer is A. I only.

Key idea: for a geometric sequence with common ratio $u$, the middle term is the geometric mean of its neighbours: $q^{2}=pr$, and in fact $ps=qr$ (both equal $p^{2}u^{3}$).

Check each option: II and III fail for $u=-2$; I survives for all $u$.
Answer: A. I only
