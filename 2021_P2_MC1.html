Laws of Indices · Exponential Functions

Learn to simplify exponential expressions by writing everything in the same base.

Laws of Indices Exponential Functions Base Change Power of a Power

Question

Simplify:

$$ \frac{(2^n)(8^{3n})}{64^{n}} = $$
A.   \(4^{n}\)
B.   \(4^{2n}\)
C.   \(4^{3n}\)
D.   \(4^{-4n}\)

Core Idea

Every number here — \(2\), \(8\), \(64\), and \(4\) — is a power of 2. If we rewrite all bases as powers of 2, the whole expression collapses into a single exponent.

\(8 = 2^3\) \(64 = 2^6\) \(4 = 2^2\)

Try It Yourself — Base-2 Power Slider

Drag the slider to change \(n\). Watch how the powers of 2 combine and the final result becomes \(4^{2n}\).

\(n =\) 1

Numerator \( (2^n)(8^{3n}) \)

\(2^{1} \cdot 2^{9} = 2^{10}\)

Denominator \(64^n\)

\(2^{6}\)

Result

\( \frac{2^{10}}{2^{6}} = 2^{4} = 4^{2} \)

Step-by-Step Solution

Notice that \(8 = 2^3\), \(64 = 2^6\), and \(4 = 2^2\).

Substitute these into the expression:

\[ \frac{(2^n)((2^3)^{3n})}{(2^6)^n} \]
💡 Hint

Use the fact that \(8 = 2^3\) and \(64 = 2^6\). Both are powers of 2.

Use \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\):

\[ (2^3)^{3n} = 2^{3 \cdot 3n} = 2^{9n} \] \[ (2^6)^n = 2^{6n} \]

Now the expression becomes:

\[ \frac{(2^n)(2^{9n})}{2^{6n}} \]
💡 Hint

Power of a power: multiply the indices. \((a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}\).

Use \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\):

\[ (2^n)(2^{9n}) = 2^{n + 9n} = 2^{10n} \]
💡 Hint

When multiplying powers of the same base, add the indices.

Use \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\):

\[ \frac{2^{10n}}{2^{6n}} = 2^{10n - 6n} = 2^{4n} \]
💡 Hint

When dividing powers of the same base, subtract the indices.

The options are written in base 4, so convert \(2^{4n}\) to a power of 4:

\[ 2 = 4^{1/2} \] \[ 2^{4n} = (4^{1/2})^{4n} = 4^{(1/2) \cdot 4n} = 4^{2n} \]

Answer: B   \(4^{2n}\)

💡 Hint

Since \(4 = 2^2\), we have \(2 = 4^{1/2}\). Alternatively, \(2^{4n} = (2^2)^{2n} = 4^{2n}\).
