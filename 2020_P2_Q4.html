HKDSE 2020 · Paper 2 · Q4

Polynomials — Factorisation by a Common Factor

$(3a+2b)(4a-5b)-a(6a+4b)=$
A. $(3a+2b)(2a-5b)$
B. $(3a+2b)(6a-5b)$
C. $(3a-2b)(2a+5b)$
D. $(3a-2b)(6a+5b)$
1Look for a hidden common factor

We are asked to factorise a difference of two terms. The first term already shows the factor $(3a+2b)$. Check the second term $a(6a+4b)$:

$$a(6a+4b)=a\cdot 2(3a+2b)=2a(3a+2b).$$

Hint: always try to pull out a common bracket before expanding everything.

2Rewrite the whole expression

Substitute the rewritten second term back:

$$(3a+2b)(4a-5b)-2a(3a+2b)$$

Now both terms visibly share the factor $(3a+2b)$ — a classic "common factor" set-up.

3Factor out $(3a+2b)$

Take $(3a+2b)$ out as a common factor and collect what remains:

$$(3a+2b)\big[(4a-5b)-2a\big]$$

Mentally check: expanding this back must give the original expression.

4Simplify the inner bracket

$$(4a-5b)-2a=4a-5b-2a=2a-5b$$

Only like terms combine: $4a-2a=2a$; $-5b$ stays as it is.

5Write the final factorised form

$$(3a+2b)(4a-5b)-a(6a+4b)=(3a+2b)(2a-5b)$$

This matches option A exactly.

Answer: Option A  —  $(3a+2b)(2a-5b)$
