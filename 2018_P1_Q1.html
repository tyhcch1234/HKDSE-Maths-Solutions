Question

Make $b$ the subject of the formula $$\frac{a+4}{3}=\frac{b+1}{2}.$$ [3 marks]
1

Clear the denominators — cross-multiply

Multiply both sides by $3\times 2=6$ (cross-multiplication):

$$2(a+4)=3(b+1)$$

Hint: the numerator of the left is multiplied by the denominator of the right, and vice-versa.

Cross-Multiplication \(\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}\ \Longrightarrow\ ad=bc\quad(b,d\neq0)\) Multiplying by \(3\times2\) clears both denominators of \(\dfrac{a+4}{3}=\dfrac{b+1}{2}\).
2

Expand the brackets

$$2a+8=3b+3$$

Hint: distribute the 2 into $(a+4)$ and the 3 into $(b+1)$. Keep the $b$-terms on the right.

Expanding Brackets \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) The 2 and the 3 are distributed into the brackets to give \(2a+8=3b+3\).
3

Collect the $b$-terms on one side

Subtract $3$ from both sides, then rearrange:

$$3b = 2a+5$$

Hint: move the constant $3$ across the equals sign — it changes sign. The $2a$ stays on the right.

4

Divide by the coefficient of $b$

Divide both sides by $3$:

$$b=\frac{2a+5}{3}$$

Hint: the result is a single fraction with $b$ as the subject — the $\frac{2a+5}{3}$ cannot be simplified further.

Answer:
\(\displaystyle b=\frac{2a+5}{3}\)

Question. Make \(b\) the subject of the formula \(\dfrac{a+3}{4}=\dfrac{b-2}{5}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Cross-multiply: \(5(a+3)=4(b-2)\).
Step 2. Expand both sides: \(5a+15=4b-8\).
Step 3. Collect the \(b\)-term: \(4b=5a+15+8=5a+23\).
Step 4. Divide by 4: \(b=\dfrac{5a+23}{4}\).
Answer: \(b=\dfrac{5a+23}{4}\)
