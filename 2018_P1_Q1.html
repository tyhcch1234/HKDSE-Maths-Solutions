Make \(b\) the subject of the formula

\[ \frac{a+4}{3} = \frac{b+1}{2} \quad \text{.} \]
[3 marks]
1 Clear the fractions by cross‑multiplying

Multiply both sides by \(3 \times 2 = 6\) to eliminate the denominators.

\[ \frac{a+4}{3} \times 6 = \frac{b+1}{2} \times 6 \]
\[ 2(a+4) = 3(b+1) \]
Hint: Cross‑multiplication: multiply the numerator of the left fraction by the denominator of the right, and vice versa. The equation becomes an equality of two products.
2 Expand both sides

Remove the brackets by distributing.

\[ 2a + 8 = 3b + 3 \]
Hint: Use the distributive law: \(2(a+4) = 2a + 8\) and \(3(b+1) = 3b + 3\).
3 Collect the \(b\)-terms on one side

Subtract \(3\) from both sides and then rearrange to isolate terms containing \(b\).

\[ 2a + 8 - 3 = 3b \]
\[ 2a + 5 = 3b \]
Hint: We want \(b\) alone. Move all other terms away from \(b\). Here we simply move the constant and \(a\)-term to the left.
4 Divide by the coefficient of \(b\)

Divide both sides by \(3\) to make \(b\) the subject.

\[ b = \frac{2a+5}{3} \]
Final answer: \(b = \dfrac{2a+5}{3}\). This matches the standard answer.
