Changing the Subject of a Formula

Topic 5 — Formulas and Sequences  •  Multiple Choice

Question
If \((3c+1)(d-4)=2d(5c-1)\), then \(c=\ ?\)
A\(\dfrac{3d-4}{7d+12}\)
B\(\dfrac{3d+4}{7d-12}\)
C\(\dfrac{7d-12}{3d+4}\)
D\(\dfrac{7d+12}{3d-4}\)
Change of subject Expand brackets Collect like terms Factorisation Formulas
Step-by-Step Solution 0 / 5 revealed

We are given a formula containing two variables, \(c\) and \(d\). We must rearrange it so that \(c\) appears alone on one side.

\[(3c+1)(d-4)=2d(5c-1)\]

Key idea: every term containing \(c\) should be moved to one side; everything else goes to the other side.

💡Hint — "subject": the subject must be a single \(c\), not \(5c\), \(3cd\) or \(c(7d+12)\). We will need to factorise \(c\) out at the end.

Expand the left-hand side by distributing each term:

\[(3c+1)(d-4)=3c\cdot d - 3c\cdot 4 + 1\cdot d - 1\cdot 4\]
\[=3cd-12c+d-4\]

Expand the right-hand side:

\[2d(5c-1)=10cd-2d\]

So the equation becomes:

\[3cd-12c+d-4=10cd-2d\]
💡Hint — Distribution law \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\). Keep the term \(3c\cdot d\) as \(3cd\); do not merge it with \(-12c\).

Move the \(cd\) term \(10cd\) from the right to the left, and move the plain \(d\) and constant from the left to the right:

\[3cd-12c+d-4=10cd-2d\]
\[3cd-10cd-12c=-2d-d+4\]
\[-7cd-12c=-3d+4\]
💡Hint — sign flips: changing sides means changing signs. \(-2d-d=-3d\) and the \(-4\) becomes \(+4\) on the right.

It is tidier to work with positive \(c\) terms. Multiply both sides by \(-1\):

\[7cd+12c=3d-4\]

Both terms on the left contain \(c\). Take \(c\) out as a common factor:

\[c(7d+12)=3d-4\]
💡Hint — common factor: \(7cd+12c=c\cdot 7d+c\cdot 12=c(7d+12)\). This is exactly the step most students forget!

Divide both sides by the whole bracket \((7d+12)\):

\[c=\frac{3d-4}{\,7d+12\,}\]

Compare with the four options. This matches option A.

\[\text{A: }\frac{3d-4}{7d+12}\quad\checkmark\]
✅ The answer is A.   Common trap: option D is the reciprocal — a sign that the student divided in the wrong direction.
