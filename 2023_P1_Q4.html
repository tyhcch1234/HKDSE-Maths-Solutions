Intersection of $x > -\tfrac{9}{2}$ and $x < 3$.

First inequality (lower bound) Second inequality (upper bound) Combined solution $-\tfrac{9}{2}<x<3$
HKDSE 2023 · Paper 1 · Q4  |  Inequalities

Question. Consider the compound inequality

$3x+2 > \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$   and   $3x-2 < 7$    …… (*)

(a) Solve (*).

(b) How many negative integers satisfy (*)?   [4 marks]

Step 1 · Set up

The compound inequality (*) means both conditions must hold at the same time:

$3x+2 > \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$   and   $3x-2 < 7$

Tip: Solve each inequality separately, then take the intersection of the two answers.
Step 2 · 1M · First inequality

Multiply both sides of $3x+2 > \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$ by $2$ to clear the fraction:

$2(3x+2) > 4x-5$

$6x+4 > 4x-5$

Multiplying by the positive number $2$ keeps the direction of the inequality sign.
Solving a Linear Inequality \(ax>b\ \Longrightarrow\ x>\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a>0)\) Terms are gathered on one side and divided by the coefficient of \(x\), keeping the same direction.
Step 3 · Collect like terms

Move the $x$-terms to the left and the constants to the right:

$6x-4x > -5-4$

$2x > -9$

Step 4 · 1A · First result

Divide both sides by $2$ (positive), so the sign stays:

$x > -\dfrac{9}{2}$

Keep the fraction form $-\tfrac{9}{2}$ — it makes part (b) easier to handle.
Intersection of Two Inequalities \(a The two separate conditions overlap only between the smaller and the larger endpoint.
Step 5 · Second inequality

Solve $3x-2 < 7$ :

$3x < 7+2$

$3x < 9$

$x < 3$

Step 6 · 1A · Combine

Take the overlap (intersection) of $x > -\dfrac{9}{2}$ and $x < 3$:

$-\dfrac{9}{2} < x < 3$

The lower bound comes from the first inequality; the upper bound comes from the second.
Step 7 · Part (b) · Look at the interval

We need negative integers $x$ with $-\dfrac{9}{2} < x < 3$, i.e. $-4.5 < x < 3$.

The integers in this open interval are:

$-4,\;-3,\;-2,\;-1,\;0,\;1,\;2$

Note $x=-5$ is too small ($-5 < -4.5$) and $x=3$ is excluded.
Step 8 · 1A · Count the negative ones

The negative integers among these are:

$-4,\;-3,\;-2,\;-1$

That is 4 negative integers.

Number of Integers in an Interval \(\#\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:a\le x Counting the whole numbers inside the final interval gives the required number of values.
(a)  $\boxed{-\dfrac{9}{2} < x < 3}$     (b)  $\boxed{4}$ negative integers
(The negative integers are $-4,\,-3,\,-2,\,-1$.)

Question. Solve the compound inequality \(5x-3<2x+9\) and \(4x+1\ge x-8\). Hence write down the number of integers that satisfy both inequalities.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(5x-3<2x+9\Rightarrow 3x<12\Rightarrow x<4\).
Step 2. \(4x+1\ge x-8\Rightarrow 3x\ge-9\Rightarrow x\ge-3\).
Step 3. Combining: \(-3\le x<4\).
Step 4. The integers are \(-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\), that is \(7\) values.
Answer: \(-3\le x<4\); \(7\) integers
