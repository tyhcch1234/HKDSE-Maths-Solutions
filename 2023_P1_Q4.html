QQuestion

Consider the compound inequality
$3x+2 \;>\; \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$   and   $3x-2 \;<\; 7$    $\cdots (*)$
(a) Solve $(*)$ . 3 marks
(b) How many negative integers satisfy $(*)$ ? 1 mark

Progress: 0 of 6 steps shown
1Read the compound inequality carefully

A compound inequality joined by "and" means we must satisfy both parts at the same time. It is usually written as a single double inequality:

$3x+2 \;>\; \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$   AND   $3x-2 \;<\; 7$

Strategy: solve each part separately first, then combine the two results.

Concept: Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number flips the inequality sign. Multiplying by a positive number (e.g. $2$) keeps the sign unchanged — that's what we use in Step 2.
2Solve the first inequality $3x+2 > \dfrac{4x-5}{2}$

Multiply both sides by $2$ (a positive number, so the sign stays the same):

$2(3x+2) > 4x-5$
$6x+4 > 4x-5$

Collect $x$-terms on the left, constants on the right:

$6x-4x > -5-4$
$2x > -9$
$\boxed{\,x > -\dfrac{9}{2}\,}$
Formula used: If $a > b$ and $c > 0$, then $ac > bc$.
Meaning of each part: $2$ = the denominator we clear; $3x+2$ and $\frac{4x-5}{2}$ are the two sides being compared. Dividing by the coefficient $2$ of $x$ gives $x > -\frac{9}{2}$.
3Solve the second inequality $3x-2 < 7$
$3x-2 < 7$
$3x < 7+2$
$3x < 9$
$\boxed{\,x < 3\,}$
Formula used: If $a < b$, then $a+c < b+c$ (adding the same number keeps the sign).
Here we add $2$ to both sides, then divide by $3$ (positive) so the sign stays "<".
4Combine both results

We need the values of $x$ that satisfy both conditions simultaneously — the overlap:

$x > -\dfrac{9}{2}$   and   $x < 3$
$\therefore\;\; -\dfrac{9}{2} \;<\; x \;<\; 3$
Concept: "and" ⇒ intersection of the two solution sets. On a number line, you shade where the two arrows overlap. Since $-\frac{9}{2} = -4.5 < 3$, the overlap is the open interval $\left(-\frac{9}{2},\,3\right)$.

The shaded region below is the solution set of $(*)$: $-\frac{9}{2} < x < 3$.

Open circles are used because the inequalities are strict ($>$, $<$), so $-\frac{9}{2}$ and $3$ are NOT included in the solution.

The negative integers satisfying $(*)$ are:

$x = -4,\,-3,\,-2,\,-1$

Therefore there are 4 such negative integers.

Method: First write the integer candidates in the interval: $-4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2$. Keep the negative ones only: $-4,-3,-2,-1$. Count = $4$.
Note $x = -5$ is not allowed because $-5 < -4.5$, which violates the lower bound.

✔ Standard Answer

(a)   $-\dfrac{9}{2} < x < 3$

(b)   $4$ negative integers  $\left(x=-4,\,-3,\,-2,\,-1\right)$
