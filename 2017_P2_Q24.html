\(L_1: 9x-5y+45=0\) \(L:\ 5x+9y+15=0\) x-intercept \((-3,0)\) y-intercept \(\left(0,-\tfrac53\right)\) Right-angle \(P\)
Uniform scale: 1 unit = same length on both axes, so the two lines really are perpendicular.
2017 P2 Q24
The straight line \(L\) is perpendicular to the straight line \(9x-5y+45=0\). If the \(x\)-intercept of \(L\) is \(-3\), then the equation of \(L\) is
  • A. \(5x+9y+15=0\)
  • B. \(5x+9y+27=0\)
  • C. \(9x-5y+15=0\)
  • D. \(9x-5y+27=0\)
STEP 1 · SLOPE OF THE GIVEN LINE
Slope of a Line in General Form \(Ax+By+C=0 \Rightarrow \text{slope}=-\dfrac{A}{B}\) For \(9x-5y+45=0\) the slope is \(-\dfrac{9}{-5}=\dfrac{9}{5}\).

Put \(9x-5y+45=0\) into slope-intercept form to read off its slope.
Denote it by \(L_1\):

\[9x-5y+45=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; 5y=9x+45 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=\frac{9}{5}x+9\]

So the slope of \(L_1\) is \(\dfrac{9}{5}\),
and its \(y\)-intercept is \(9\).

From \(Ax+By+C=0\), slope \(=-\dfrac{A}{B}=-\dfrac{9}{-5}=\dfrac{9}{5}\).
STEP 2 · SLOPE OF \(L\) FROM PERPENDICULARITY
Condition for Perpendicular Lines \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) The required line has slope \(-\dfrac{1}{9/5}=-\dfrac{5}{9}\).

For perpendicular lines, \(m_1m_2=-1\). Hence

\[m_L=-\frac{1}{m_{L_1}}=-\frac{1}{\dfrac{9}{5}}=-\frac{5}{9}\]

So \(L\) has slope \(-\dfrac{5}{9}\). (Notice this matches the red line,
sloping downwards as \(x\) increases.)

STEP 3 · POINT–SLOPE FORM WITH THE \(x\)-INTERCEPT
Point-Slope Form \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\) Using the point \((-3,0)\) with slope \(-\dfrac{5}{9}\) gives \(y=-\dfrac{5}{9}(x+3)\).

The \(x\)-intercept of \(L\) is \(-3\), so \(L\) passes through \((-3,\,0)\). Using \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\):

\[y-0=-\frac{5}{9}\bigl[x-(-3)\bigr] \;\Longrightarrow\; y=-\frac{5}{9}(x+3)\]

STEP 4 · SIMPLIFY TO THE REQUIRED FORM

Multiply both sides by \(9\):

\[9y=-5(x+3)=-5x-15\]

Bring everything to one side:

\[5x+9y+15=0\]

Quick check: slope \(=-\dfrac{5}{9}\) ✓ , and putting \(y=0\) gives \(5x+15=0\Rightarrow x=-3\) ✓
Answer:  A  —   \(5x+9y+15=0\)
