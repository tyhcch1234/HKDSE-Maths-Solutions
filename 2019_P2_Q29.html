Statistics — What a box-and-whisker diagram can (and cannot) tell us

Box-and-Whisker Diagram Range Inter-quartile Range Standard Deviation

Question Box-and-Whisker Diagram

Which of the following can be obtained from any box-and-whisker diagram?

\[ \begin{array}{ll} \text{I.} & \text{Range}\\[4pt] \text{II.} & \text{Standard deviation}\\[4pt] \text{III.} & \text{Inter-quartile range} \end{array} \]
\[ \begin{array}{ll} \text{A.} & \text{I and II only}\\ \text{B.} & \text{I and III only}\\ \text{C.} & \text{II and III only}\\ \text{D.} & \text{I, II and III} \end{array} \]
Note: This is a multiple-choice question. We must test whether each of the three quantities can always be read off any box-and-whisker diagram.

Part I Can the Range be obtained?

The range is \(\text{maximum} - \text{minimum}\). A box-and-whisker diagram marks the maximum and minimum with the two whisker ends.

Min = 1 Max = 14 Range = 13
1 What the two whisker ends represent
Every box-and-whisker diagram has:
\[ \text{left whisker end} = \text{minimum}, \qquad \text{right whisker end} = \text{maximum} \]
Hint: Move the sliders above and watch the two ends of the diagram move. They always mark the min and the max — no matter how the data are distributed.
2 Compute the range
\[ \text{Range} = \text{Maximum} - \text{Minimum} \]
With the current slider values, e.g. \(\text{Range} = 14 - 1 = 13\).
✓ Yes — the range can always be obtained from any box-and-whisker diagram.

Part II Can the Standard Deviation be obtained?

The mean is not marked on a box-and-whisker diagram. Two data sets can share the same box-and-whisker diagram yet have different standard deviations — so the standard deviation cannot always be read off.

Five-number summary: −3, −3, 0, 3, 3 s.d. = 2.449 mean = 0
1 Build two data sets sharing one diagram
Consider the family of data sets (with \(0 \le a \le 1\)):
\[ \{-3,\;-3,\;-3,\;-3a,\;0,\;3a,\;3,\;3,\;3\} \]
Sorting gives the five-number summary:
For \(0\le a\le 1\): \(-3 \le -3a \le 0 \le 3a \le 3\). So the ordered list is
\[ -3,\;-3,\;-3,\;-3a,\;0,\;3a,\;3,\;3,\;3 \]
\[ \Rightarrow\;\min=-3,\; Q_1=-3,\; \text{median}=0,\; Q_3=3,\; \max=3 \]
2 The diagram stays fixed as \(a\) changes
For every value of \(a \in [0,1]\), the five-number summary is the same:
\[ \min=-3,\quad Q_1=-3,\quad \text{median}=0,\quad Q_3=3,\quad \max=3 \]
Hint: Drag the \(a\)-slider. The box-and-whisker diagram does not move at all — but the individual points (the dots) spread out.
3 Compute the standard deviation as a function of \(a\)
Mean: \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{-3-3-3-3a+0+3a+3+3+3}{9} = \dfrac{0}{9} = 0\).
\[ \sum x^2 = 3(-3)^2 + (-3a)^2 + 0^2 + (3a)^2 + 3(3)^2 = 27 + 9a^2 + 9a^2 + 27 = 54 + 18a^2 \]
\[ \sigma^2 = \frac{54+18a^2}{9} - 0^2 = 6 + 2a^2 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{2a^2+6} \]
4 Conclusion — s.d. is NOT determined by the diagram
When \(a = 0\): \(\sigma = \sqrt{6} \approx 2.449\).
When \(a = 1\): \(\sigma = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.828\).
The diagram is identical in both cases, but the standard deviations differ.
Key insight: A box-and-whisker diagram only stores the five-number summary — it throws away how the data are spread between the quartiles.
✗ No — the standard deviation cannot be obtained from a box-and-whisker diagram in general.

Part III Can the Inter-quartile Range be obtained?

The inter-quartile range is \(Q_3 - Q_1\) — and \(Q_1, Q_3\) are exactly the two ends of the box.

Q₁ = 2 Q₃ = 10 IQR = 8
1 Locate the quartiles on the diagram
The left edge of the rectangle is \(Q_1\), the right edge is \(Q_3\).
Hint: Move the sliders — the box width is the inter-quartile range. It is directly visible.
2 Compute the inter-quartile range
\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 10 - 2 = 8 \]
✓ Yes — the inter-quartile range can always be obtained from any box-and-whisker diagram.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • I. Range — ✓ Obtainable. Equal to \(\text{max} - \text{min}\), both endpoints of the whiskers.
  • II. Standard deviation — ✗ Not obtainable in general. Two data sets with the same five-number summary can have different \(\sigma\).
  • III. Inter-quartile range — ✓ Obtainable. Equal to the width of the box, \(Q_3 - Q_1\).
  • Hence only I and III can be obtained.
Answer: B. I and III only
