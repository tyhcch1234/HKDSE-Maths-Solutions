Question

There are 21 boys and 11 girls in a class. If 5 students are selected from the class to form a committee consisting of at least 1 boy, how many different committees can be formed?

Probability & Counting Combinations \(C^n_r\) Complementary Counting "at least 1"

Core Idea

When a question says "at least 1 boy", it is usually much faster to subtract the unwanted case from the total.

\[ \text{(At least 1 boy)} \;=\; \text{(All cases)} \;-\; \text{(No boys at all)} \]

"No boys" means the committee is made up entirely of girls — and there are only 11 girls, so we choose 5 of them.

Step-by-Step Solution

Tap each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to answer before you open it!

1
Understand the constraint: "at least 1 boy"
▼

The committee has 5 members. The allowed compositions are:

  • 1 boy + 4 girls
  • 2 boys + 3 girls
  • 3 boys + 2 girls
  • 4 boys + 1 girl
  • 5 boys + 0 girls

These are 5 separate cases. Counting them one by one works — but it is slow.

Hint: The only forbidden composition is 0 boys + 5 girls. That's just one case to exclude!
2
Recall the combination formula \(C^n_r\)
▼

When order does not matter (a committee is just a group of people), we use combinations:

\[ C^n_r \;=\; \binom{n}{r} \;=\; \frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!} \]
  • \(n\) = total number of items available to choose from
  • \(r\) = number of items we actually pick
Why not permutations? Because selecting \(\{A,B\}\) is the same committee as \(\{B,A\}\) — order does not create a new committee.
3
Count the TOTAL number of committees (no restriction)
▼

Total students \(= 21 + 11 = 32\).

We simply choose any 5 from these 32 students:

\[ \text{Total} \;=\; C^{32}_{5} \;=\; \binom{32}{5} \]

Compute it:

\[ \binom{32}{5} = \frac{32 \times 31 \times 30 \times 29 \times 28}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{24\,165\,120}{120} = 201\,376 \]
Calculator tip: On many calculators, key in 32 → nCr → 5 → =.
4
Count the UNWANTED case: committees with NO boys
▼

"No boys" means all 5 members are girls. There are 11 girls available.

\[ \text{No boys} \;=\; C^{11}_{5} \;=\; \binom{11}{5} \]
\[ \binom{11}{5} = \frac{11 \times 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{55\,440}{120} = 462 \]
Important: Always check \(r \le n\). Here \(5 \le 11\), so this is valid.
5
Subtract to get the required number
▼
\[ \text{Required} \;=\; C^{32}_{5} - C^{11}_{5} \]
\[ = 201\,376 - 462 \;=\; \boxed{200\,914} \]

So the number of valid committees is 200 914.

Exam marking (3 marks): usually
• 1M for using \(C^{32}_{5}\) (total)
• 1M for using \(C^{11}_{5}\) (unwanted, all girls)
• 1A for the correct answer \(200\,914\)
6
Double-check with the direct case-by-case method
▼

Let's verify by adding all allowed cases (\(k\) boys, \(5-k\) girls):

Boys (\(k\))Girls (\(5-k\))Count \(= C^{21}_k \cdot C^{11}_{5-k}\)Value

Both methods give the same answer — but the complement method takes only two combinations instead of five. Use the complement when you see "at least".

Solution Summary

\[ \text{Required} = C^{32}_{5} - C^{11}_{5} \] \[ = 201\,376 - 462 = \mathbf{200\,914} \]
Key technique: For "at least one" questions, use the complement method:
\(\text{Required} = \text{Total} - \text{Unwanted}\)
It saves time and avoids summing several cases.
