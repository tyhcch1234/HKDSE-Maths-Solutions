HKDSE 2016 · Paper 2 · Q31

Least Common Multiple of Monomials

Polynomials L.C.M. Powers & Exponents Algebraic Manipulation

Question: The L.C.M. of \(9a^2b\), \(12a^4b^3\) and \(15a^6\) is

A. \(3a^2\)
B. \(3a^2b\)
C. \(180a^6b^3\)
D. \(180a^{12}b^4\)

🔍 Step-by-step Solution

1 Prime factorise each coefficient ▾

Write each numerical coefficient as a product of prime numbers:

\(9 = 3^2\),   \(12 = 2^2 \cdot 3\),   \(15 = 3 \cdot 5\)

Hint: Breaking down numbers into primes helps us find the L.C.M. easily.
2 List the prime factorisation of each monomial ▾

Include the variables with their powers (and \(b^0\) if \(b\) is missing):

  • \(9a^2b = 3^2 \cdot a^2 \cdot b^1\)
  • \(12a^4b^3 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot a^4 \cdot b^3\)
  • \(15a^6 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot a^6 \cdot b^0\)
Hint: Remember \(b^0 = 1\), so we can still include \(b^0\) to compare powers of \(b\).
3 Identify the highest power of each prime factor ▾

For the L.C.M., we take the largest exponent for each prime base appearing in any of the monomials.

  • Prime \(2\): highest power is \(2^2\) (from 12)
  • Prime \(3\): highest power is \(3^2\) (from 9)
  • Prime \(5\): highest power is \(5^1\) (from 15)
  • Variable \(a\): highest power is \(a^6\) (from \(15a^6\))
  • Variable \(b\): highest power is \(b^3\) (from \(12a^4b^3\))
Hint: The L.C.M. of monomials takes the highest power of every factor present.
4 Multiply the highest powers together ▾

Now combine all the highest powers:

\[ \text{L.C.M.} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot a^6 \cdot b^3 \]

Compute the numerical part: \(4 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 = 180\).

So the L.C.M. is \(180a^6b^3\).

Hint: Double-check: \(180a^6b^3\) is divisible by each of the original monomials.
5 Match with the options ▾

The result \(180a^6b^3\) corresponds to option C.

✅ Answer: C. \(180a^6b^3\)
