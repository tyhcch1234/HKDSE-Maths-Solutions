📋 Question

Figure 6(a) shows a solid pyramid \(VABCD\) with a rectangular base, where \(AB = 18\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 10\text{ cm}\), \(VB = VC = 30\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle VAB = \angle VDC = 110^\circ\).

(a) Find \(\angle VBA\). 2 marks
(b) \(P\), \(Q\), \(M\) and \(N\) are the mid-points of \(AB\), \(CD\), \(VB\) and \(VC\) respectively. A geometric model is made by cutting off \(PBCQNM\) from \(VABCD\) as shown in Figure 6(b). A craftsman claims that the area of the trapezium \(PQNM\) is less than \(70\text{ cm}^2\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. 5 marks

Part (a) Finding \(\angle VBA\)

Apply the sine formula to triangle \(VAB\). Explore the triangle below — drag the slider to change the assumed value of \(\angle VBA\) and watch how the required side \(VB\) changes. The correct answer makes \(VB = 30\) exactly.

35.7°
∠AVB = 34.3° Implied VB = 30.0 cm Target VB = 30 cm
Step 11M
Apply the sine formula in \(\triangle VAB\): \[ \frac{\sin\angle AVB}{AB}=\frac{\sin\angle VAB}{VB} \] \[ \frac{\sin\angle AVB}{18}=\frac{\sin 110^\circ}{30} \] \[ \sin\angle AVB=\frac{18\sin110^\circ}{30}=0.563815\ldots \] \[ \angle AVB \approx 34.32008291^\circ \]
Hint: The sine formula links an angle to the side opposite it. Side \(AB = 18\) is opposite \(\angle AVB\), and side \(VB = 30\) is opposite \(\angle VAB = 110^\circ\).
Step 21A
Angles in a triangle sum to \(180^\circ\): \[ \angle VBA = 180^\circ - 110^\circ - 34.32008291^\circ \] \[ \angle VBA \approx 35.67991709^\circ \approx 35.7^\circ \]
Hint: Double-check the angle sum: \(110^\circ+34.320^\circ+35.680^\circ=180^\circ\). ✓
Answer (a): \(\angle VBA \approx 35.7^\circ\)

Part (b) Area of the trapezium \(PQNM\)

Find both slant edges \(MP\) and \(NQ\) (equal by symmetry), then the height and area of the trapezium. Use the slider to see how the trapezium area depends on \(\angle VBA\).

35.7°
MP = NQ = 9.31 cm height h = 8.97 cm Area = 67.3 cm²
Step 11M
In \(\triangle ABM\) (or \(\triangle BPM\)), use the cosine formula. Since \(M\) is the mid-point of \(VB\), \[ BM=\frac{VB}{2}=\frac{30}{2}=15\text{ cm},\qquad PB=\frac{AB}{2}=\frac{18}{2}=9\text{ cm}. \] \[ MP^2 = BP^2 + BM^2 - 2(BP)(BM)\cos\angle VBA \] \[ MP^2 \approx 9^2+15^2-2(9)(15)\cos 35.67991709^\circ \] \[ MP^2 \approx 306-221.3796168 = 86.7102\ldots \] \[ MP \approx 9.310329519\text{ cm} \]
Hint: The angle between \(BP\) and \(BM\) is \(\angle VBA\) because \(P\) lies on \(BA\) and \(M\) lies on \(BV\) — the sides \(BP\) and \(BM\) are just scaled copies of \(BA\) and \(BV\).
Step 21M
In \(\triangle VBC\), \(M\) and \(N\) are mid-points of \(VB\) and \(VC\), so by the mid-point theorem: \[ MN=\frac{BC}{2}=\frac{10}{2}=5\text{ cm} \] Also \(PQ = AB = 18\ldots\) wait — actually \(P,Q\) lie on the rectangular base, and \(PQ\) is parallel to \(AD\)–\(BC\), so the trapezium has parallel sides \(MN=5\) and \(PQ=10\).
Hint: In the trapezium \(PQNM\), the two parallel sides are \(MN\) (across the top of the pyramid) and \(PQ\) (across the base of the cut region). Here \(MN=5\) cm and \(PQ=10\) cm (half of \(BC\) scaled — see Figure 6(b): \(PQ\) joins mid-points of \(AD\)-parallel edges, giving \(PQ=BC=10\) cm). Thus \(PQ=10\) cm and \(MN=5\) cm.
Step 31M
The trapezium is isosceles: \(MP=NQ\approx 9.310329519\text{ cm}\). Let \(h\) be its height. Using the right-angle relationship (drop perpendiculars from \(M,N\) to \(PQ\)): \[ h=\sqrt{MP^2-\left(\frac{PQ-MN}{2}\right)^2} \] \[ h\approx\sqrt{9.310329519^2-\left(\frac{10-5}{2}\right)^2} \] \[ h\approx 8.968402074\text{ cm} \]
Hint: Since the trapezium is isosceles, the horizontal offset on each side is \(\frac{PQ-MN}{2}=\frac{10-5}{2}=2.5\text{ cm}\). Then Pythagoras gives the height.
Step 41M
Trapezium area: \[ \text{Area}=\frac{h(MN+PQ)}{2} \approx \frac{8.968402074\,(5+10)}{2} \approx 67.26301555\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: The area of a trapezium is (average of parallel sides) × height.
Step 51A
Since \[ 67.26301555\text{ cm}^2 < 70\text{ cm}^2, \] the craftsman's claim is agreed.
Hint: The comparison is the final conclusion — state clearly whether you agree or not.
Answer (b): Yes, the claim is agreed. The area of \(PQNM\approx 67.3\text{ cm}^2<70\text{ cm}^2\).
