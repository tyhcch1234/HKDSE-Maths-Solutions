Question

In Figure 3(a), $ABCDB'$ is a pentagonal paper card. It is given that $AB = AB' = 40\text{ cm}$,  $BC = B'D = 24\text{ cm}$ and $\angle ABC = \angle AB'D = 80^\circ$.

(a) Suppose that $105^\circ \le \angle BCD \le 145^\circ$. [7 marks]

  1. Find the distance between $A$ and $C$.
  2. Find $\angle ACB$.
  3. Describe how the area of the paper card varies when $\angle BCD$ increases from $105^\circ$ to $145^\circ$. Explain your answer.

(b) Suppose that $\angle BCD = 132^\circ$. The paper card in Figure 3(a) is folded along $AC$ and $AD$ such that $AB$ and $AB'$ join together to form a pyramid $ABCD$ as shown in Figure 3(b). Find the volume of the pyramid $ABCD$. [6 marks]

STEP 1 · (a)(i) Distance AC — Cosine Formula
Cosine Formula \(b^{2}=a^{2}+c^{2}-2ac\cos B\) In \(\triangle ABC\) the sides \(AB=40\) and \(BC=24\) enclose the known angle \(80^{\circ}\), so \(AC\) follows directly.

In $\triangle ABC$, the two sides $AB=40$ and $BC=24$ enclose the given angle $\angle ABC = 80^\circ$.

$$AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 - 2(AB)(BC)\cos\angle ABC$$ $$AC^2 = 40^2 + 24^2 - 2(40)(24)\cos 80^\circ$$ $$AC^2 = 1600 + 576 - 1920(0.173648\ldots) \approx 1842.9256$$ $$AC \approx 42.9256 \text{ cm}$$
$AC \approx 42.9$ cm  (the distance between $A$ and $C$)
STEP 2 · (a)(ii) Angle ACB — Sine Formula
Sine Formula \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}\) With \(\dfrac{\sin\angle ACB}{40}=\dfrac{\sin 80^{\circ}}{42.9256}\) the angle at \(C\) can be found.

Apply the sine formula in $\triangle ABC$:

$$\frac{\sin\angle ACB}{AB}=\frac{\sin\angle ABC}{AC}$$ $$\frac{\sin\angle ACB}{40}=\frac{\sin 80^\circ}{42.9256}$$ $$\sin\angle ACB = \frac{40\sin 80^\circ}{42.9256} \approx 0.9182$$

The candidates are $\angle ACB \approx 66.59^\circ$ or $113.41^\circ$.

Since $\angle BCD \ge 105^\circ$ and $\triangle ABC$ already uses $80^\circ$,
the obtuse value would force $\angle BAC$ to be tiny and contradict the geometry ($\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 80^\circ - \angle ACB < 0$ for the obtuse case). Hence the obtuse value is rejected.

$\angle ACB \approx 66.6^\circ$
STEP 3 · (a)(iii) Area of the card as a function of ∠BCD
Area of a Triangle \(\text{Area}=\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C\) Each of the two congruent triangles uses \(\tfrac{1}{2}(40)(24)\sin80^{\circ}\), giving the constant part \(960\sin80^{\circ}\).

By symmetry the card is made of two congruent triangles: $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle AB'D$,
plus the central triangle $\triangle ACD$.

$$\text{Area} = 2\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(40)(24)\sin 80^\circ\right) + \tfrac{1}{2}AC^2\sin\angle CAD$$ $$\text{Area} = 960\sin 80^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}AC^2 \sin\angle CAD$$

The constant part is $960\sin 80^\circ$;
the variable part depends only on $\sin\angle CAD$.

Also $\angle CAD = 180^\circ - 2(\angle BCD - \angle ACB) = 180^\circ - 2(\angle BCD - 66.59^\circ)$.

So $\angle CAD$ ranges from $23.18^\circ$ (when $\angle BCD = 105^\circ$) down to $-46.36^\circ$… more carefully: as $\angle BCD$ goes from $105^\circ$ to $145^\circ$, $\angle CAD$ goes from $23.18^\circ$ to $-46.36^\circ$,
passing through $90^\circ$… but the geometry only allows $\angle CAD \ge 0$;
the maximum $\sin\angle CAD$ occurs when $\angle CAD = 90^\circ$.

Set $\angle CAD = 90^\circ$: $\;180^\circ - 2(\angle BCD - 66.59^\circ) = 90^\circ \Rightarrow \angle BCD = 111.59^\circ$. Let this threshold be $\alpha$.
The area increases as $\angle BCD$ goes from $105^\circ$ to $\alpha \approx 111.6^\circ$,
then decreases as $\angle BCD$ goes from $\alpha \approx 111.6^\circ$ to $145^\circ$.
STEP 4 · (b) Folding data — ∠ACD and CD

Given $\angle BCD = 132^\circ$:

$$\angle ACD = \angle BCD - \angle ACB = 132^\circ - 66.5908^\circ \approx 65.4092^\circ$$

In $\triangle ACD$ use the cosine formula (or the perpendicular drop) to get $CD$:

$$\cos\angle ACD = \frac{CD/2}{AC}\;\Rightarrow\;CD = 2AC\cos\angle ACD$$ $$CD = 2(42.9256)\cos 65.4092^\circ \approx 35.7258 \text{ cm}$$
$CD \approx 35.73$ cm
STEP 5 · (b) Height of the pyramid

Let $M$ be the mid-point of $CD$. Then $AM \perp CD$ and $BM \perp CD$ (isosceles triangles),
so the dihedral angle between faces $ACD$ and $BCD$ is $\angle AMB$.

$$AM^2 = AC^2 - CM^2 = 42.9256^2 - 17.8629^2 \approx 1523.16$$ $$BM^2 = BC^2 - CM^2 = 24^2 - 17.8629^2 \approx 256.92$$ $$\cos\angle AMB = \frac{AM^2 + BM^2 - AB^2}{2\,AM\cdot BM} = \frac{1523.16 + 256.92 - 1600}{2\sqrt{1523.16}\sqrt{256.92}} \approx 0.3183$$ $$\angle AMB \approx 81.71^\circ$$

The height of the pyramid is the perpendicular distance from $B$ to the plane $ACD$:

$$h = BM\sin\angle AMB \approx 16.028\,(\sin 81.71^\circ) \approx 15.86 \text{ cm}$$
STEP 6 · (b) Area of base and Volume
Volume of a Pyramid \(V=\dfrac{1}{3}(\text{base area})(\text{height})\) The base is \(\triangle ACD\) with area \(697.2\text{ cm}^{2}\) and the height is \(15.86\text{ cm}\).

Base $\triangle ACD$ has base $CD$ and height $AM$:

$$\text{Area}_{\triangle ACD} = \tfrac{1}{2}(CD)(AM) = \tfrac{1}{2}(35.7258)(39.028) \approx 697.2 \text{ cm}^2$$

Finally the volume of the pyramid:

$$V = \frac{1}{3}(\text{base area})(\text{height}) = \frac{1}{3}(697.2)(15.8612) \approx 3686.28 \text{ cm}^3$$
Volume $\approx 3690$ cm³  (3 s.f.)

Question. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB=30\text{ cm}\), \(BC=18\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle ABC=70^{\circ}\).
(a) Find \(AC\).
(b) Find \(\angle ACB\).
(c) Find the area of \(\triangle ABC\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) By the cosine formula, \(AC^{2}=AB^{2}+BC^{2}-2(AB)(BC)\cos\angle ABC=30^{2}+18^{2}-2(30)(18)\cos70^{\circ}\).
Step 2. \(AC^{2}=900+324-1080(0.342020\ldots)\approx854.62\), so \(AC\approx29.23\text{ cm}\) (\(\approx29.2\text{ cm}\)).
Step 3. (b) Use the cosine formula at \(C\): \(\cos\angle ACB=\dfrac{BC^{2}+AC^{2}-AB^{2}}{2(BC)(AC)}=\dfrac{324+854.62-900}{2(18)(29.2335)}\approx0.2647\).
Step 4. Hence \(\angle ACB\approx74.6^{\circ}\) (the cosine is positive, so the angle is acute and there is no ambiguity).
Step 5. (c) Area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(30)(18)\sin70^{\circ}=270(0.939693\ldots)\approx253.7\text{ cm}^{2}\) (\(\approx254\text{ cm}^{2}\)).
Answer: (a) \(AC\approx29.2\text{ cm}\) (b) \(\angle ACB\approx74.6^{\circ}\) (c) area \(\approx254\text{ cm}^{2}\)
