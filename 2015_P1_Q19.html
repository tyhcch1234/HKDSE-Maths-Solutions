Trigonometry — Cosine Rule, Sine Rule, Optimising Area & Volume of a Pyramid

Cosine Rule Sine Rule Angle Ambiguity Maximum Area Volume of Pyramid

📄 The Question 13 marks total

Figure 3(a) shows a pentagonal paper card \(ABCDB'\\). It is given that \[AB = AB' = 40\text{ cm},\quad BC = B'D = 24\text{ cm},\quad \angle ABC = \angle AB'D = 80^\circ.\]

(a) Suppose that \(105^\circ \le \angle BCD \le 145^\circ\).

  • (i) Find the distance between \(A\) and \(C\). 2 marks
  • (ii) Find \(\angle ACB\). 2 marks
  • (iii) Describe how the area of the paper card varies when \(\angle BCD\) increases from \(105^\circ\) to \(145^\circ\). Explain your answer. 7 marks

(b) Suppose that \(\angle BCD = 132^\circ\). The paper card in Figure 3(a) is folded along \(AC\) and \(AD\) such that \(AB\) and \(AB'\\) join together to form a pyramid \(ABCD\) as shown in Figure 3(b). Find the volume of the pyramid \(ABCD\). 6 marks

Part (a)(i) — Finding the distance \(AC\) Cosine Rule

AB = 40 cm BC = 24 cm ∠ABC = 80° AC = — cm
0 / 3
Step 11M
Identify the triangle and the included angle. In \(\triangle ABC\), we know two sides and the included angle between them: \[AB = 40,\quad BC = 24,\quad \angle ABC = 80^\circ.\] The side \(AC\) is opposite the known angle, so the Cosine Rule applies.
Hint: \(a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A\) — pick the formula with the angle between the two given sides.
Step 21M
Apply the Cosine Rule: \[ AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 - 2(AB)(BC)\cos\angle ABC \] \[ AC^2 = 40^2 + 24^2 - 2(40)(24)\cos 80^\circ \] \[ AC^2 = 1600 + 576 - 1920\cos 80^\circ \approx 2176 - 333.43 = 1842.57 \]
Hint: Keep full precision until the very last step — rounding early can cost accuracy marks.
Step 31A
Take the square root: \[ AC = \sqrt{1842.57\ldots} \approx 42.92546\ \text{cm} \]
Answer: \(AC \approx 42.9\ \text{cm}\) (correct to 3 significant figures)

Part (a)(ii) — Finding \(\angle ACB\) Sine Rule

∠ACB (acute) = ≈ 66.6° ∠ACB (obtuse alt.) = ≈ 113.4° ✗ rejected
0 / 3
Step 11M
Use the Sine Rule in \(\triangle ABC\). The side \(AB = 40\) is opposite \(\angle ACB\), and \(AC \approx 42.925\) is opposite \(\angle ABC = 80^\circ\): \[ \frac{\sin\angle ACB}{AB} = \frac{\sin\angle ABC}{AC} \]
Hint: Match each side with the angle opposite it. \(AB\) is opposite \(\angle ACB\) (the angle at \(C\)).
Step 21M
Substitute the values: \[ \frac{\sin\angle ACB}{40} = \frac{\sin 80^\circ}{42.92546446} \;\Longrightarrow\; \sin\angle ACB = \frac{40\sin 80^\circ}{42.92546446} \approx 0.9179 \]
Hint: \(\sin\theta = 0.9179\) has two solutions in \(0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\): one acute, one obtuse.
Step 31A
Solving: the acute solution is \[ \angle ACB \approx 66.59081487^\circ \approx 66.6^\circ, \] while the obtuse solution is \(\angle ACB \approx 113.4091851^\circ\).

Why reject the obtuse value? In \(\triangle ABC\), the angles must sum to \(180^\circ\). If \(\angle ACB \approx 113.4^\circ\), then \(\angle ACB + \angle ABC \approx 193.4^\circ > 180^\circ\), which is impossible.

Answer: \(\angle ACB \approx 66.6^\circ\) (correct to 3 significant figures)

Part (a)(iii) — How the area varies 7 marks · Optimisation

Area △ABC = — cm² Area △ACD = — cm² Total card area = — cm² AC = — cm
0 / 5
Step 11M
Split the pentagonal card \(ABCDB'\\) along the line of symmetry \(AC\) (and \(AD\)). The card is made of two congruent halves \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle AB'D\), and the central triangle \(\triangle ACD\). So \[ \text{Area of card} = 2\times[\triangle ABC] + [\triangle ACD]. \]
Hint: By symmetry about the vertical axis, \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AB'D\) — work with one half only.
Step 21M
The area of the fixed triangle \(\triangle ABC\) is constant: \[ 2\cdot\tfrac12 (40)(24)\sin 80^\circ = 960\sin 80^\circ \approx 945.43\ \text{cm}^2. \] The variable part comes from \(\triangle ACD\): \[ [\triangle ACD] = \tfrac12\cdot AC\cdot CD\cdot\sin\angle ACD \quad\text{and}\quad AC\cdot CD = 2[\triangle ACD]/\sin\angle ACD. \] More directly, the only changing quantity is the angle \(\angle CAD\): \[ [\,\text{card}\,] = 960\sin 80^\circ + 2\cdot\tfrac12 AC\cdot AD \sin\angle CAD. \]
Hint: Since \(900\sin 80^\circ\) is fixed, the card's area depends only on \(\sin\angle CAD\).
Step 31M
Express \(\angle CAD\) in terms of \(\angle BCD\): \[ \angle CAD = 180^\circ - 2(\angle BCD - \angle ACB). \] With \(\angle ACB \approx 66.59^\circ\), as \(\angle BCD\) runs from \(105^\circ\) to \(145^\circ\), \(\angle CAD\) runs from about \(23.18^\circ\) up to about \(103.18^\circ\).
Hint: At \(\angle BCD = 105^\circ\): \(\angle CAD = 180^\circ - 2(105^\circ - 66.59^\circ) \approx 23.18^\circ\). Check the upper end yourself.
Step 41M
The area is largest when \(\sin\angle CAD = 1\), i.e. when \[ 180^\circ - 2(\angle BCD - 66.59081487^\circ) = 90^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; \angle BCD = 45^\circ + 66.59081487^\circ \approx 111.59^\circ. \] Note \(111.59^\circ\) lies between \(105^\circ\) and \(145^\circ\), so this maximum is attainable.
Hint: \(\sin\theta\) increases to a maximum of 1 at \(\theta = 90^\circ\), then decreases.
Step 51A
Therefore, with \(\alpha = 111.59^\circ\):
  • As \(\angle BCD\) increases from \(105^\circ\) to \(\alpha \approx 111.6^\circ\), the area of the card increases.
  • As \(\angle BCD\) increases from \(\alpha \approx 111.6^\circ\) to \(145^\circ\), the area of the card decreases.
Answer: The area first increases, reaches a maximum at \(\angle BCD \approx 111.6^\circ\), then decreases. (Drag the slider below to see this!)

Part (b) — Volume of the pyramid \(ABCD\) 6 marks

∠ACD = ≈ 65.41° CD = ≈ 35.73 cm AM = ≈ 39.03 cm BM = ≈ 16.03 cm Angle ∠AMB = ≈ 81.71°
0 / 6
Step 11M
First find the geometry of the base \(\triangle ACD\). With \(\angle BCD = 132^\circ\), \[ \angle ACD = \angle BCD - \angle ACB \approx 132^\circ - 66.59081487^\circ \approx 65.40918513^\circ. \]
Hint: At \(C\), the diagonal \(AC\) splits \(\angle BCD\) into \(\angle ACB\) and \(\angle ACD\).
Step 21M
In \(\triangle ACD\), use the right angle at \(D\) (from the given \(\angle AB'D = 80^\circ\) symmetric construction the folded edge \(CD\) is perpendicular to \(AD\) in the base), or use the Cosine Rule. We have \[ \cos\angle ACD = \frac{CD/2}{AC} \;\Longrightarrow\; CD = 2\cdot AC\cos\angle ACD \approx 2(42.92546446)\cos 65.40918513^\circ \] \[ CD \approx 35.72557859\ \text{cm}. \]
Hint: Alternatively use the Cosine Rule in \(\triangle ACD\) with \(AD = 40\). Both routes give the same \(CD\).
Step 31M
When \(AB\) and \(AB'\\) join, the apex \(B\) sits above the foot \(M\), the midpoint of the base edge \(CD\). Compute \(AM\) and \(BM\) from right triangles: \[ AM^2 = AC^2 - CM^2, \qquad BM^2 = BC^2 - CM^2, \] where \(CM = CD/2 \approx 17.86278930\ \text{cm}\). \[ AM^2 \approx 1842.57 - 319.08 \approx 1523.52 \;\Longrightarrow\; AM \approx 39.03236\ \text{cm}, \] \[ BM^2 \approx 576 - 319.08 \approx 256.92 \;\Longrightarrow\; BM \approx 16.02871\ \text{cm}. \]
Hint: \(AC \approx 42.925\), \(BC = 24\). Both \(AM\) and \(BM\) are heights to the same midpoint \(M\) of \(CD\).
Step 41M
In \(\triangle AMB\), apply the Cosine Rule to find \(\angle AMB\): \[ \cos\angle AMB = \frac{AM^2 + BM^2 - AB^2}{2(AM)(BM)} \approx \frac{1523.52 + 256.92 - 1600}{2(39.03236)(16.02871)} \] \[ \cos\angle AMB \approx 0.14416 \;\Longrightarrow\; \angle AMB \approx 81.70890517^\circ. \]
Hint: Triangle \(AMB\) is the "cross-section" of the pyramid — \(AB = 40\) cm is the original card edge.
Step 51M
The height of the pyramid is the perpendicular height from \(B\): \[ h = BM\sin\angle AMB \approx 16.02871 \times \sin 81.70890517^\circ \approx 15.86121883\ \text{cm}. \] The base area \([\triangle ACD]\) is \[ [\triangle ACD] = \tfrac12 (CD)(AM) \approx \tfrac12 (35.72557859)(39.03236) \approx 697.2247927\ \text{cm}^2. \]
Hint: Height = \(BM \times \sin(\angle AMB)\). Base area = ½ × base edge × height-to-base.
Step 61A
Finally, the volume of the pyramid: \[ V = \frac{1}{3} \times [\triangle ACD] \times h \approx \frac{1}{3}(697.2247927)(15.86121883) \approx 3686.278338\ \text{cm}^3. \]
Answer: \(V \approx 3690\ \text{cm}^3\) (correct to 3 significant figures)

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a)(i)   \(AC \approx 42.9\ \text{cm}\)
  • (a)(ii)   \(\angle ACB \approx 66.6^\circ\)   (the obtuse solution \(113.4^\circ\) is rejected)
  • (a)(iii)   As \(\angle BCD\) increases from \(105^\circ\) to \(145^\circ\), the area first increases and then decreases. The maximum occurs when \(\angle CAD = 90^\circ\), i.e. at \(\angle BCD \approx 111.6^\circ\).
  • (b)   Volume of pyramid \(ABCD \approx 3690\ \text{cm}^3\)
