Rhombus with equal segments — finding an angle via congruent triangles

Rhombus Congruent Triangles (SAS) Isosceles Triangle Angle Sum of Triangle

Question MC · 4 options

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rhombus. \(E\) and \(F\) are points lying on \(AB\) and \(AD\) respectively such that \(AE = AF\) and \(\angle ECF = 42^\circ\). If \(\angle BAD = 110^\circ\), then \(\angle BEC =\)

  • A. \(70^\circ\)
  • B. \(76^\circ\)
  • C. \(80^\circ\)
  • D. \(84^\circ\)

Part (a) Set up the rhombus

First find the angles of the rhombus and the key symmetry. Drag the slider to rotate the figure and see that \(E\) and \(F\) are always mirror images about the diagonal \(AC\).

∠BAD = 110°, ∠ABC = 70°, ∠BCD = 110°, ∠ADC = 70°
1Rhombus angles1M

In a rhombus, opposite angles are equal and adjacent angles sum to \(180^\circ\). Given \(\angle BAD = 110^\circ\):

\[ \angle BCD = 110^\circ, \qquad \angle ABC = \angle ADC = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ. \]
Hint: A rhombus is a parallelogram, so opposite angles are equal. Also \(AB=BC=CD=DA\).
2Symmetry about \(AC\)1M

Diagonal \(AC\) bisects \(\angle BAD\) and \(\angle BCD\). Since \(AE = AF\) and \(AB = AD\), points \(E\) and \(F\) are symmetric about \(AC\).

\[ \angle BAC = \angle DAC = 55^\circ, \qquad \angle BCA = \angle DCA = 55^\circ. \] \[ AC \text{ bisects } \angle ECF \;\Rightarrow\; \angle ECA = \angle ACF = 21^\circ. \]
Hint: The symmetry guarantees \(\triangle AEC \cong \triangle AFC\).
\(\angle ECA = 21^\circ\) and \(\angle ACF = 21^\circ\).

Part (b) Find \(\angle BEC\)

Now use the angle sum of \(\triangle BEC\) and the straight angle at \(E\).

—
1Congruent triangles1M

Since \(AE = AF\), \(AC\) is common, and \(\angle EAC = \angle FAC = 55^\circ\):

\[ \triangle AEC \cong \triangle AFC \quad (\text{SAS}). \] Hence \(EC = FC\), so \(\triangle ECF\) is isosceles with vertex angle \(42^\circ\): \[ \angle CEF = \angle CFE = \frac{180^\circ - 42^\circ}{2} = 69^\circ. \]
Hint: Equal sides \(AE=AF\) plus a shared side \(AC\) and included angle gives SAS congruence.
2Angle in \(\triangle AEC\)1M

In \(\triangle AEC\), we have \(\angle EAC = 55^\circ\) and \(\angle ECA = 21^\circ\). Therefore:

\[ \angle AEC = 180^\circ - 55^\circ - 21^\circ = 104^\circ. \]
Hint: \(\angle ECA = \angle ECF / 2 = 21^\circ\) by the diagonal symmetry found in Part (a).
3Straight angle at \(E\)1A

Points \(A\), \(E\), \(B\) are collinear ( \(E\) lies on \(AB\) ). So \(\angle AEC\) and \(\angle BEC\) form a straight angle:

\[ \angle BEC = 180^\circ - \angle AEC = 180^\circ - 104^\circ = 76^\circ. \]
Hint: Angles on a straight line sum to \(180^\circ\).
Answer: B. \(76^\circ\)

Summary of answers

  • \(\angle BAD = 110^\circ \Rightarrow \angle ABC = \angle ADC = 70^\circ\), \(\angle BCD = 110^\circ\).
  • Diagonal \(AC\) bisects \(\angle ECF\), so \(\angle ECA = \angle ACF = 21^\circ\).
  • \(\triangle AEC \cong \triangle AFC\) (SAS) \(\Rightarrow \angle AEC = 104^\circ\).
  • \(\angle BEC = 180^\circ - 104^\circ = \mathbf{76^\circ}\).
  • Correct option: B
