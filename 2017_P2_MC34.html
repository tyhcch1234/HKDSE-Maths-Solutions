HKDSE Math 2017 Paper 2 Q34

Laws of Indices, Exponential and Logarithmic Functions
📐 Logarithms 🔁 Simultaneous Equations 🧩 Substitution ✖️ Quadratic in disguise
Question

If \[ \begin{cases} \log_9 y = x - 3 \\[4pt] 2(\log_9 y)^2 = 4 - x \end{cases} \] then \(y =\)

A. \(-1\) or \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)
B. \(1\) or \(\dfrac{1}{3}\)
C. \(2\) or \(\dfrac{7}{2}\)
D. \(3\) or \(\dfrac{1}{9}\)
Step-by-step Solution
0%
1
Identify the common quantity
▼

Both equations contain the expression \(\log_9 y\). Let's set

\[ u = \log_9 y \]

This substitution will turn the system into a simpler pair of equations.

💡 Hint: When you see the same complicated expression in multiple equations, substituting a single variable for it often simplifies everything.
2
Rewrite the system with \(u\)
▼

Substituting \(u = \log_9 y\) into the original system:

\[ \begin{cases} u = x - 3 \quad \cdots (1) \\[4pt] 2u^2 = 4 - x \quad \cdots (2) \end{cases} \]

Now we have two equations with two unknowns \(u\) and \(x\).

3
Eliminate \(x\) by adding the equations
▼

From (1), we can write \(x = u + 3\). Substitute this into (2)…

Or even faster — add the two equations directly:

\[ (u) + (2u^2) = (x - 3) + (4 - x) \]
\[ u + 2u^2 = 1 \]
💡 Hint: Adding (1) and (2) kills the \(x\) terms because \(x - x = 0\). This is a common trick for simultaneous equations.
4
Form and solve the quadratic equation
▼

Rearrange \(2u^2 + u = 1\) into standard quadratic form:

\[ 2u^2 + u - 1 = 0 \]

Factorise:

\[ (2u - 1)(u + 1) = 0 \]

So the two possible values are:

\[ u = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad u = -1 \]
Graph of \(y = 2u^2 + u - 1\) — roots at \(u = -1\) and \(u = \tfrac{1}{2}\)
5
Convert back to \(y\)
▼

Recall \(u = \log_9 y\). Use the definition of logarithm: if \(\log_9 y = u\), then \(y = 9^u\).

Case 1: \(u = \dfrac{1}{2}\)

\[ y = 9^{1/2} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \]

Case 2: \(u = -1\)

\[ y = 9^{-1} = \frac{1}{9} \]
💡 Hint: \(9^{1/2}\) means the square root of 9. A negative exponent means reciprocal: \(9^{-1} = \frac{1}{9}\).
6
Final answer
▼

The possible values of \(y\) are:

\[ y = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad y = \frac{1}{9} \]

This matches Option D.

✅ Check: If \(y = 3\): \(\log_9 3 = \frac{1}{2}\), and \(2(\frac{1}{2})^2 = \frac{1}{2}\). Does \(\frac{1}{2} = 4 - x\)? Then \(x = \frac{7}{2}\), and \(x - 3 = \frac{1}{2}\) ✓. If \(y = \frac{1}{9}\): \(\log_9 \frac{1}{9} = -1\), and \(2(-1)^2 = 2\). Then \(2 = 4 - x \Rightarrow x = 2\), and \(x - 3 = -1\) ✓.
