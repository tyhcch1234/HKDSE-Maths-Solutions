Coordinate Geometry — circle equation, perpendicular-distance locus, and angle comparison

Circle Equation Parallel Lines Angle Bisector Locus Tangent of Angle Coordinate Geometry

📋 The Question

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-10,\,0)\) and \((30,\,0)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). It is given that the \(y\)-coordinate of \(G\) is \(-15\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). 3 marks

(b) The straight line \(L\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\). Another straight line \(\ell\) is parallel to \(L\). Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(\ell\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A\).

(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\). 1 mark

(ii) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). 2 marks

(iii) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at another point \(H\). Someone claims that \(\angle GAH < 70^\circ\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. 3 marks

[6 marks]

Part (a) Finding the equation of circle \(C\)

r = 25.00 G = (10.00, −15.00) (x−10)² + (y+15)² = 625

Move the slider: the centre \(G\) slides along the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\), and the radius changes while the circle keeps passing through \(A\) and \(B\). Notice that only when \(G_y=-15\) does the radius equal \(25\).

Step 11M
Since the circle passes through \(A(-10,0)\) and \(B(30,0)\), the centre lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\): \[x\text{-coordinate of }G = \frac{-10+30}{2} = 10.\]
Hint: Equal distances from the centre to \(A\) and \(B\) force \(G\) onto the perpendicular bisector of the chord \(AB\).
Step 21M
The radius is the distance from \(G(10,-15)\) to \(A(-10,0)\): \[r=\sqrt{(-10-10)^2+(0-(-15))^2}=\sqrt{400+225}=\sqrt{625}=25.\]
Hint: Use the distance formula between two points. Here the horizontal difference is \(20\) and the vertical difference is \(15\), a \(3\!-\!4\!-\!5\) triangle scaled by \(5\).
Step 31A
Hence the equation of \(C\) is \[(x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=25^2.\]
Answer: \((x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=625\)
Hint: Standard form of a circle with centre \((a,b)\) and radius \(r\) is \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2\).

Part (b)(i) Relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\)

d(P, L) = — d(P, ℓ) = — Equal? —

Line \(L\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\); line \(\ell\) is parallel to \(L\). The red dashed line is the locus \(\Gamma\) (the mid-parallel line between \(L\) and \(\ell\)). Drag the slider so that \(P\) lies on \(\Gamma\): the two perpendicular distances become equal, showing that \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\).

Step 11M
A point equidistant from two parallel lines \(L\) and \(\ell\) lies on the line exactly halfway between them. That line is parallel to both \(L\) and \(\ell\).
Hint: The set of points equidistant from two given parallel lines is the mid-parallel line — not an angle bisector, because the lines are parallel rather than intersecting.
Step 21A
Therefore \(\Gamma\) is a straight line parallel to \(L\).
Answer: \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\).

Part (b)(ii) Equation of \(\Gamma\)

slope of L = 0.75 Γ: 3x − 4y + 30 = 0

Line \(L\) has slope \(\frac{3}{4}\). Whichever \(\ell\) you choose, \(\Gamma\) stays parallel to \(L\). The line through \(A\) with slope \(\frac{3}{4}\) is \(\Gamma\), giving \(3x-4y+30=0\).

Step 11M
Slope of \(L\) (through \(B(30,0)\) and \(G(10,-15)\)): \[\text{slope of }L=\frac{0-(-15)}{30-10}=\frac{15}{20}=\frac{3}{4}.\]
Hint: Slope \(=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\). Here rise \(=15\), run \(=20\), so slope \(=\frac34\).
Step 21A
Since \(\Gamma\parallel L\), the slope of \(\Gamma\) is also \(\frac34\). Given \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A(-10,0)\): \[y-0=\frac{3}{4}\bigl(x-(-10)\bigr)\] \[3x-4y+30=0.\]
Answer: \(\Gamma:\;3x-4y+30=0\)
Hint: Use the point–slope form \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) with the point \(A(-10,0)\) and slope \(\frac34\).

Part (b)(iii) Is \(\angle GAH < 70^\circ\)? Does \(\Gamma\) cut \(C\)?

∠ABG = 36.87° ∠BAH = — ∠GAH = —

Locus \(\Gamma\) (red dashed) and circle \(C\) (blue) intersect at \(A\) and a second point \(H\). Use the slider to slide \(H\) to its true position. Then \(\angle GAH = 2\angle ABG \approx 73.74^\circ > 70^\circ\), so the claim is disagreed.

Step 11M
For \(\angle ABG\), in right triangle with \(\tan\angle ABG=\frac{15}{20}=\frac34\): \[\tan\angle ABG=\frac{3}{4}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \angle ABG\approx 36.86989765^\circ.\]
Hint: The slope of \(L\) is the tangent of the angle \(L\) makes with the positive \(x\)-axis. Since \(AB\) is horizontal, \(\angle ABG\) is exactly this slope angle.
Step 21M
Note that \(\angle BAH=\angle ABG\) (alternate angles, \(\Gamma\parallel L\)). Also \(\angle BAG=\angle ABG\) (base angles of isosceles \(\triangle ABG\), since \(GA=GB=r\)).
Hint: \(GA\) and \(GB\) are both radii, so \(\triangle ABG\) is isosceles with base \(AB\).
Step 31A
Since \(A\) and \(H\) both lie on \(\Gamma\), \[\angle GAH=\angle BAH+\angle BAG=2\angle ABG.\] As \(\angle ABG>35^\circ\), we have \(\angle GAH>70^\circ\), so the claim is disagreed.
Conclusion: No — \(\angle GAH\approx 73.74^\circ>70^\circ\). The claim is disagreed.
Hint: Use the fact that both \(A\) and \(H\) lie on \(\Gamma\) to write \(\angle GAH\) as a sum, then compare with \(2\angle ABG\).

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(C:\;(x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=625\)  (centre \(G(10,-15)\), radius \(25\))
  • (b)(i) \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\).
  • (b)(ii) \(\Gamma:\;3x-4y+30=0\)
  • (b)(iii) \(\angle ABG\approx 36.87^\circ\), so \(\angle GAH=2\angle ABG\approx 73.74^\circ>70^\circ\). The claim is disagreed.

Key ideas: perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre · mid-parallel line is the locus equidistant from two parallel lines · slope \(=\) tangent of the inclination angle.
