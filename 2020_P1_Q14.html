HKDSE 2020 #14

The coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$ are $(-10,\,0)$ and $(30,\,0)$ respectively. The circle $C$ passes through $A$ and $B$. Denote the centre of $C$ by $G$. It is given that the $y$-coordinate of $G$ is $-15$.

(a) Find the equation of $C$. [3 marks]

(b) The straight line $L$ passes through $B$ and $G$. Another straight line $\ell'$ is parallel to $L$. Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $L$ is equal to the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $\ell'$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$. It is given that $\Gamma$ passes through $A$.

(i) Describe the geometric relationship between $\Gamma$ and $L$.
(ii) Find the equation of $\Gamma$.
(iii) Suppose that $\Gamma$ cuts $C$ at another point $H$. Someone claims that $\angle GAH < 70^\circ$. Do you agree? Explain your answer. [6 marks]

💡 Hint: The centre $G$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. Since $A$ and $B$ are symmetric about $x=10$, the $x$-coordinate of $G$ is $10$. Then use $GA = GB = r$.
Step 1 · Find the centre G of circle C

$G$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, and $A(-10,0),\,B(30,0)$ are symmetric about $x=10$.

$$x_G = \frac{-10+30}{2} = 10$$

Given $y_G = -15$, so $\boxed{G(10,\,-15)}$.

Step 2 · Radius of C and its equation (part a)

$r = GA = GB = \sqrt{(-10-10)^2 + (0+15)^2} = \sqrt{400+225} = \sqrt{625} = 25$

Equation of $C$: $$\boxed{(x-10)^2 + (y+15)^2 = 25^2 = 625}$$

Step 3 · Slope of L and relation to Γ (part b i & ii)

$L$ passes through $B(30,0)$ and $G(10,-15)$:

$$m_L = \frac{0-(-15)}{30-10} = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}$$

The locus $\Gamma$ is the mid-line between $L$ and $\ell'$, so $\Gamma \parallel L$ and $\Gamma$ lies midway between them. Since $\Gamma$ passes through $A(-10,0)$:

$$y - 0 = \tfrac{3}{4}(x+10) \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{3x - 4y + 30 = 0}$$

Step 4 · Find H, the second intersection of Γ and C

Substitute $y = \tfrac{3}{4}(x+10)$ into $(x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=625$:

After simplification: $25x^2 - 280x - 1400 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 - 11.2x - 56 = 0$, giving $x = -10$ (=$A$) or $x = 21.2$.

At $x = 21.2$: $y = \tfrac{3}{4}(31.2) = 23.4$.

So $\boxed{H\,(21.2,\;23.4)}$.

Step 5 · Compute ∠GAH (part b iii)

$$\tan\angle ABG = \frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4} \;\Rightarrow\; \angle ABG \approx 36.87^\circ$$

Since $\Gamma$ is the perpendicular-bisector-style mid-line between $L$ and $\ell'$, reflection across $\Gamma$ maps $L$ to $\ell'$; also $A$ lies on $\Gamma$ and $H$ is $A$ reflected across $G$, giving $\angle BAH = \angle ABG$ and $\angle BAG = \angle ABG$.

$$\angle GAH = \angle BAH + \angle BAG = 2\angle ABG \approx 73.74^\circ$$

Step 6 · Conclusion

Since $\angle ABG > 35^\circ$, we have $\angle GAH = 2\angle ABG > 70^\circ$.

Answers:
(a) $(x-10)^2 + (y+15)^2 = 625$
(b)(i) $\Gamma$ is parallel to $L$ (it is the mid-line between $L$ and $\ell'$).
(b)(ii) $3x - 4y + 30 = 0$
(b)(iii) $\angle GAH \approx 73.74^\circ > 70^\circ$. The claim is disagreed.
