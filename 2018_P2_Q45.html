Statistics — Variance of terms of an arithmetic sequence

Arithmetic Sequence Variance Translation Invariance Multiple Choice

QQuestion

[2018MC#45]

There are \(49\) terms in an arithmetic sequence. If the variance of the first \(7\) terms of the sequence is \(9\), then the variance of the last \(7\) terms of the sequence is

  1. A. 9.
  2. B. 18.
  3. C. 49.
  4. D. 81.
    5. >

1Setup: writing the terms with a common difference

Step 1
Write every term using \(a_1\) and \(d\): \[ a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d, \qquad n = 1, 2, \dots, 49. \]
Hint: The whole sequence is fixed once we know \(a_1\) and \(d\) — only two unknowns!
Step 2
Show that the last \(7\) terms are the first \(7\) terms shifted by \(42d\): \[ \begin{aligned} a_{43} &= a_1 + 42d,\\ a_{44} &= a_2 + 42d,\\ &\ \vdots\\ a_{49} &= a_7 + 42d. \end{aligned} \]
Hint: \(49 - 7 = 42\), so the last \(7\) terms start \(42\) steps later, i.e. \(a_{42+k} = a_k + 42d\).
Step 3
Therefore the last \(7\) numbers are obtained by adding the same constant \(42d\) to each of the first \(7\) numbers.
Key idea: A constant shift \(a_k \mapsto a_k + 42d\) does not change the spread of the data.

Step 1
Let the original data be \(x_1, \dots, x_7\) with mean \(\bar{x}\). After adding a constant \(c\), the new data is \(y_i = x_i + c\). The new mean is \[ \bar{y} = \frac{1}{7}\sum_{i=1}^{7}(x_i + c) = \bar{x} + c. \]
Hint: The mean shifts by exactly the same amount \(c\).
Step 2
Now compute each deviation from the mean: \[ y_i - \bar{y} = (x_i + c) - (\bar{x} + c) = x_i - \bar{x}. \] The \(c\) cancels — the deviations are identical.
Hint: This is the crux: shifting the whole dataset moves the mean with it, so distances from the mean are untouched.
Step 3
Since the deviations are the same, the variance is the same: \[ \operatorname{Var}(y) = \frac{1}{7}\sum (y_i-\bar{y})^2 = \frac{1}{7}\sum (x_i-\bar{x})^2 = \operatorname{Var}(x). \]
Result: \(\operatorname{Var}(x + c) = \operatorname{Var}(x)\) for any constant \(c\).

3Applying the result to the sequence

Take \(x_i = a_i\) (first \(7\) terms, \(i=1,\dots,7\)) and \(c = 42d\). Then \(a_{42+i} = a_i + 42d\), so the last \(7\) terms are exactly the shifted data.

Step 1
Given: \(\operatorname{Var}(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_7) = 9\).
Hint: This is the information supplied by the question.
Step 2
The last \(7\) terms are \[ (a_1 + 42d,\ a_2 + 42d,\ \dots,\ a_7 + 42d), \] i.e. the first \(7\) terms plus the constant \(42d\).
Hint: Identify \(c = 42d\) from Step 2 of Part 1.
Step 3
By the translation invariance of variance, \[ \operatorname{Var}(a_{43},\dots,a_{49}) = \operatorname{Var}(a_1 + 42d,\dots,a_7 + 42d) = \operatorname{Var}(a_1,\dots,a_7) = 9. \]
Answer: The variance of the last \(7\) terms is \(9\). \(\Rightarrow\) Option A.

Summary of Answers

  • Terms written as \(a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d\).
  • Last \(7\) terms: \(a_{43},\dots,a_{49} = a_1 + 42d,\ \dots,\ a_7 + 42d\).
  • Adding a constant \(c\) to every value does not change the variance: \(\operatorname{Var}(x+c)=\operatorname{Var}(x)\).
  • Final answer: variance of the last \(7\) terms \(= 9\), i.e. Option A.
