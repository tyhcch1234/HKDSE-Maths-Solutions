Question

[2018 MC #12]

Let $a_n$ be the $n$th term of a sequence. If $a_3=21$, $a_6=89$ and $a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}$ for any positive integer $n$, then $a_1=$

A. 8
B. 13
C. 34
D. 55

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1
Write down what is given: $a_3=21$, $a_6=89$, and the recurrence $a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}$.
Tip: rearrange the relation to go backwards — $a_n=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}$ and $a_{n+1}=a_{n+2}-a_n$.
Recurrence Relation\(a_{n+2}=a_{n}+a_{n+1}\)Each term is the sum of the two before it, so the given \(a_{3}=21\) and \(a_{6}=89\) are linked through \(a_{4}\) and \(a_{5}\).
STEP 2
Find the term in the middle, $a_4$.
Using $a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}$ with $n=4$: $a_6=a_4+a_5 \Rightarrow a_5=a_6-a_4$.
Using it again with $n=3$: $a_5=a_3+a_4$.
Equate the two expressions for $a_5$:
$$a_3+a_4=a_6-a_4$$
$$2a_4=a_6-a_3=89-21=68$$
$$\boxed{a_4=34}$$
Key idea: the middle term is the average of its two neighbours, $a_4=\dfrac{a_6-a_3}{2}$.
Elimination Method\(a_{3}+a_{4}=a_{6}-a_{4}\ \Rightarrow\ 2a_{4}=a_{6}-a_{3}\)Equating the two expressions for \(a_{5}\) eliminates it, giving \(2a_{4}=89-21=68\).
STEP 3
Now find $a_2$ using $n=2$ in $a_{n+2}=a_n+a_{n+1}$:
$$a_4=a_2+a_3 \Rightarrow a_2=a_4-a_3=34-21=13$$
Each new term equals the sum of the two before it — so subtract backwards.
STEP 4
Finally find $a_1$ using $n=1$:
$$a_3=a_1+a_2 \Rightarrow a_1=a_3-a_2=21-13=8$$
Alternative combined form: $a_1=2a_3-a_4=2(21)-34=8$.
Working Backwards with a Recurrence\(a_{n}=a_{n+2}-a_{n+1}\)Rearranged, the rule gives \(a_{2}=a_{4}-a_{3}=13\) and then \(a_{1}=a_{3}-a_{2}=8\).
ANSWER
The sequence begins $a_1=8,\ a_2=13,\ a_3=21,\ a_4=34,\ a_5=55,\ a_6=89$ — the famous Fibonacci-type pattern.
Answer: A  ($a_1=8$)

Question. Let \(b_{n}\) be the \(n\)th term of a sequence. If \(b_{3}=13\), \(b_{6}=55\) and \(b_{n+2}=b_{n}+b_{n+1}\) for any positive integer \(n\), find \(b_{1}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. From \(b_{6}=b_{4}+b_{5}\) we get \(b_{5}=b_{6}-b_{4}\); from \(n=3\), \(b_{5}=b_{3}+b_{4}\).
Step 2. Equate them: \(b_{3}+b_{4}=b_{6}-b_{4}\), so \(2b_{4}=55-13=42\) and \(b_{4}=21\).
Step 3. \(b_{4}=b_{2}+b_{3}\) gives \(b_{2}=21-13=8\).
Step 4. \(b_{3}=b_{1}+b_{2}\) gives \(b_{1}=13-8=5\). The sequence is \(5,8,13,21,34,55\) ✓.
Answer: \(b_{1}=5\)
