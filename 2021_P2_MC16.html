Mensuration of Solids — Total Surface Area & Volume of Hemispheres

Total Surface Area Volume of Hemisphere Ratio 2 : 3 Algebraic Substitution

Question Full Question Text Answer: 1 mark

The sum of the total surface areas of two solid hemispheres is \(351\pi\text{ cm}^2\). If the ratio of the radius of the smaller hemisphere to the radius of the larger hemisphere is \(2:3\), then the difference of the volumes of the two hemispheres is

A. \(342\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
B. \(630\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
C. \(684\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
D. \(1260\pi\text{ cm}^3\)

A solid hemisphere has a curved surface plus one flat circular base. Its total surface area is \(3\pi r^2\) and its volume is \(\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3\).

Part (a) Set up the radii and the surface-area equation

Smaller radius \(r_1 = 2a\): 6 cm
Larger radius \(r_2 = 3a\): 9 cm
TSA sum \(= 39\pi a^2\): 351π cm²
1Introduce a common unit \(a\)1M

Since the radii are in the ratio \(2:3\), let

\[ r_1 = 2a \text{ cm (smaller)}, \qquad r_2 = 3a \text{ cm (larger)} \]
Hint: Using a single unknown \(a\) turns a ratio problem into a one-variable equation.
2Surface area of each solid hemisphere1M

Each hemisphere has a curved surface \(2\pi r^2\) plus a flat circular base \(\pi r^2\):

\[ \text{TSA} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2 \]

So for the two hemispheres:

\[ 3\pi(2a)^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 3\pi(3a)^2 \]
Hint: Do not forget the flat base — the question says solid hemispheres, so the surface area is \(3\pi r^2\), not \(2\pi r^2\).
3Form the equation from the given sum1M

The sum of the total surface areas is \(351\pi\text{ cm}^2\), therefore

\[ 3\pi(2a)^2 + 3\pi(3a)^2 = 351\pi \]
Hint: Cancel \(\pi\) on both sides before expanding — it makes the arithmetic much cleaner.
0 / 3

Part (b) Solve for \(a\) and find the radii

Equation: \(39a^2 = 351\)
\(a^2 =\) 9
\(a =\) 3  (reject \(-3\))
1Expand and simplify1M
\[ 3\pi(4a^2) + 3\pi(9a^2) = 351\pi \] \[ 12\pi a^2 + 27\pi a^2 = 351\pi \] \[ 39\pi a^2 = 351\pi \]
Hint: Combine the \(\pi a^2\) terms first; you get a single simple equation.
2Solve for \(a\)1A
\[ a^2 = \frac{351}{39} = 9 \] \[ a = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad a = -3 \]

Since a radius must be positive, \(a = 3\).

Hint: A square root gives two signs — always reject the negative value when it represents a length or radius.
Answer: \(a = 3\), so \(r_1 = 2(3) = 6\text{ cm}\) and \(r_2 = 3(3) = 9\text{ cm}\).
0 / 2

Part (c) Difference of the volumes

Volume of smaller \(=\tfrac{2}{3}\pi(6)^3\): 144π cm³
Volume of larger \(=\tfrac{2}{3}\pi(9)^3\): 486π cm³
Difference: 342π cm³
1Volume formula for a hemisphere1M
\[ V = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3 \]
Hint: A hemisphere is half a sphere, so its volume is half of \(\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\).
2Substitute \(r_1=6\) and \(r_2=9\)1M
\[ V_{\text{small}} = \frac{2}{3}\pi(6)^3 = \frac{2}{3}\pi(216) = 144\pi \] \[ V_{\text{large}} = \frac{2}{3}\pi(9)^3 = \frac{2}{3}\pi(729) = 486\pi \]
Hint: Keep the answers in the form \(k\pi\) until the very end to avoid rounding errors.
3Find the difference1A
\[ \text{Difference} = 486\pi - 144\pi = 342\pi\text{ cm}^3 \]
Difference of volumes \(= 342\pi\text{ cm}^3\) — Answer: A

Summary of Answers

  • Let \(r_1 = 2a\) cm and \(r_2 = 3a\) cm.
  • Total surface area of a solid hemisphere: \(3\pi r^2\).
  • Equation: \(3\pi(2a)^2 + 3\pi(3a)^2 = 351\pi \;\Rightarrow\; 39a^2 = 351\).
  • Solution: \(a = 3\) (reject \(a = -3\)), giving \(r_1 = 6\) cm, \(r_2 = 9\) cm.
  • Volumes: \(V_{\text{small}} = 144\pi\text{ cm}^3\), \(V_{\text{large}} = 486\pi\text{ cm}^3\).
  • Difference: \(\boxed{342\pi\text{ cm}^3}\)   Option A
