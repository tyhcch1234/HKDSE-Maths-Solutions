Probability & Counting Combinations \(C^n_r\) Multiplication Principle
Paper 2 · MC
Q

There are 13 boys and 17 girls in a class. If a team of 2 boys and 3 girls is selected from the class to participate in a voluntary service, how many different teams can be formed?

A. 38 896
B. 53 040
C. 142 506
D. 636 480

·

1

Understand the task

We need to choose 2 boys out of 13 and 3 girls out of 17. The selections are independent, so we multiply the number of ways for each group.

💡 Hint: "Choose" means order does not matter → use combinations \(C^n_r\).
2

Ways to choose the boys

From 13 boys, choose 2. The number of combinations is:

\( \displaystyle C^{13}_2 = \frac{13 \times 12}{2} = 78 \)
3

Ways to choose the girls

From 17 girls, choose 3. The number of combinations is:

\( \displaystyle C^{17}_3 = \frac{17 \times 16 \times 15}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 680 \)
4

Multiply the independent choices

Since the team must contain both selections, we use the multiplication principle:

\( C^{13}_2 \times C^{17}_3 = 78 \times 680 = 53\,040 \)

✅ Therefore, the answer is B. 53 040.

When selecting from separate groups, multiply the number of combinations from each group. Use \(C^n_r\) because order does not matter.
