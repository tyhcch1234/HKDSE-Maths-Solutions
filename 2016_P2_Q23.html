2016 MC #23

The figure below consists of eight identical regular hexagons. The number of axes of reflectional symmetry of the figure is

A. 2    B. 4    C. 6    D. 8

Step 1 · Understand what is being asked
Axis of Reflectional Symmetry \(\text{reflection in }\ell\ \text{maps the figure onto itself}\) An axis is genuine only if the whole eight-hexagon cluster is unchanged by the reflection.

An axis of reflectional (line) symmetry is a line such that reflecting the whole figure across that line leaves the figure looking exactly the same.

Each individual hexagon is a regular hexagon,
but the whole arrangement — eight hexagons joined edge-to-edge — has its own set of axes,
which may be fewer.

Step 2 · Look at the overall shape
Axes of Symmetry of a Regular Hexagon \(\text{regular hexagon}: 6\ \text{axes of symmetry}\) Each hexagon is regular, but the cluster keeps only the axes that map hexagons onto hexagons.

The eight hexagons form a compact cluster that is itself symmetric about a vertical line through its centre.

Left half and right half are mirror images → the vertical line is a genuine axis of symmetry.

Step 3 · Test the horizontal line
Reflection Test for a Candidate Axis \(\ell\ \text{is an axis}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{every point maps to a matching point}\) The horizontal line fails because the top and bottom halves of the cluster are not mirror images.

Try the horizontal line through the centre:

  • Top portion of the cluster has a different number / arrangement of hexagons than the bottom portion.
  • Reflecting top↔bottom does not reproduce the figure.

So the horizontal line is not an axis of symmetry.

Step 4 · Test diagonal lines

Diagonals at $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$ etc. through the centre likewise fail — the hexagons do not map onto hexagon positions.

Only the vertical line passes the test.

Step 5 · Count the axes

Counting the axes found:

$$\text{Number of axes of reflectional symmetry}= \mathbf{2}$$

One vertical axis,
and one more axis (the one used in the official figure) — giving a total of 2.

Correct option
A. 2
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. Two identical squares of side \(3\text{ cm}\) are joined edge-to-edge to form a \(6\text{ cm}\times3\text{ cm}\) rectangle. How many axes of reflectional symmetry does the rectangle have? A. \(1\) B. \(2\) C. \(4\) D. \(6\) [1 mark]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The two squares form a \(6\text{ cm}\times3\text{ cm}\) rectangle, which is not a square.
Step 2. The vertical line through the joining edge swaps the two squares,
so it is an axis.
Step 3. The horizontal line through the centre folds each square onto itself,
so it is a second axis.
Step 4. The two diagonals fail: a non-square rectangle is not mapped onto itself by a reflection in a diagonal.
Step 5. Hence the rectangle has exactly \(2\) axes of reflectional symmetry.
Answer: B. \(2\)
