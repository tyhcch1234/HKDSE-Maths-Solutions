Probability Combinatorics Sample Space

Q Question Overview

Two numbers are randomly drawn at the same time from six cards numbered \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5\) and \(6\) respectively. Find the probability that the product of the numbers drawn is not less than 12.

A. \( \frac{1}{3} \)
B. \( \frac{2}{3} \)
C. \( \frac{7}{15} \)
D. \( \frac{8}{15} \)

Step-by-Step Solution

We are drawing two cards simultaneously from a set of 6 distinct cards (numbered 1 to 6).

The total number of ways to choose 2 cards from 6 is given by the combination formula:

\(C^{6}_{2} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2 \times 1} = 15\)

So, there are 15 equally likely outcomes in total.

Let's list all possible products. Since order doesn't matter, we only consider pairs \((a, b)\) where \(a < b\).

Pair Product $\ge 12$ ?

Note: The table lists all 15 possible combinations.

We need the product to be not less than 12 (i.e., \(\ge 12\)).

By checking the table in Step 2, we can count the favorable outcomes:

(2,6) → 12
(3,4) → 12
(3,5) → 15
(3,6) → 18
(4,5) → 20
(4,6) → 24
(5,6) → 30

There are 7 favorable outcomes.

The probability is calculated as:

\(\text{Probability} = \frac{\text{Number of Favorable Outcomes}}{\text{Total Number of Outcomes}}\)

\(= \frac{7}{15}\)

✅ The correct answer is C. \( \frac{7}{15} \)
