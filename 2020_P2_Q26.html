The equation of the straight line \(L\) is \[kx + 4y - 2k = 0,\] where \(k\) is a constant. If \(L\) is perpendicular to the straight line \(6x - 9y + 4 = 0\), find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\).

A.  \(-3\)
B.  \(-2\)
C.  \(2\)
D.  \(3\)

1 Step 1 — Find the slope of the given line \(L_1\)

Rewrite \(6x - 9y + 4 = 0\) in slope-intercept form and read off its slope.

Diagram 1 · The line \(L_1\) and its slope 0 / 3 steps
Slope of \(L_1\) = 0.667 Angle with x-axis = 33.7°

The slider moves the line \(L_1\) up and down — notice that the slope never changes, because the slope depends only on the coefficients of \(x\) and \(y\), not on the constant term.

Step 1

Make \(y\) the subject of \(6x - 9y + 4 = 0\):

\[ 6x - 9y + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 9y = 6x + 4 \;\Rightarrow\; y = \frac{6x+4}{9} = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{4}{9}. \]
Hint: In the form \(y = mx + c\), the coefficient of \(x\) is the slope \(m\). Focus only on the \(x\) and \(y\) coefficients — the constant \(+4\) does not affect the slope.
Slope of \(L_1\) \(= m_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
Step 2

Equivalently, use the shortcut for a line \(Ax+By+C=0\): slope \(=-\dfrac{A}{B}\).

\[ m_1 = -\frac{6}{-9} = \frac{2}{3}. \]
Hint: The rise–run triangle on the diagram has vertical side \(2\) and horizontal side \(3\), confirming \(m_1=\frac{2}{3}\).
Confirmed: slope of \(L_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
Step 3check

The line rises to the right (positive slope). Entering \(x=2\) gives \(y=\frac{2}{3}(2)+\frac{4}{9}=\frac{16}{9}\), while \(x=-2\) gives \(y=-\frac{8}{9}\) — the values increase with \(x\).

Hint: A positive slope always means the line goes up as we move right.
\(m_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}\) (positive)

2 Step 2 — Use the perpendicular condition to find \(k\)

Two lines are perpendicular exactly when the product of their slopes equals \(-1\).

Diagram 2 · Line \(L\) rotating with the slider — perpendicular only at \(k=6\) 0 / 4 steps
Slope of \(L\): \(m_L = -\dfrac{k}{4}\) = −0.75 \(m_L \times m_1\) = −0.5000 not perpendicular

Drag the slider: \(L\) is drawn for the chosen \(k\). The orange line \(L_1\) has fixed slope \(\frac{2}{3}\). The two lines only cross at a right angle when the product of slopes is \(-1\), i.e. when \(k=6\).

Step 1

Read the slope of \(L: kx+4y-2k=0\) using \(m=-\dfrac{A}{B}\):

\[ m_L = -\frac{k}{4}. \]
Hint: Here \(A=k\) and \(B=4\). Keep \(k\) as an unknown — do not substitute a number yet.
\(m_L = -\dfrac{k}{4}\)
Step 2

Apply the perpendicular condition \(m_L \times m_1 = -1\):

\[ \left(-\frac{k}{4}\right)\times\left(-\frac{6}{-9}\right) = -1 \]
Hint: \(-\dfrac{6}{-9}=+\dfrac{2}{3}\), so this is \(\left(-\dfrac{k}{4}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)=-1\).
\(\left(-\dfrac{k}{4}\right)\left(-\dfrac{6}{-9}\right) = -1\)
Step 3

Solve for \(k\):

\[ -\frac{k}{4}\cdot\frac{2}{3} = -1 \;\Rightarrow\; -\frac{k}{6} = -1 \;\Rightarrow\; k = 6. \]
Hint: Both signs are negative, so the product is positive \(\frac{k}{6}\); setting it equal to \(1\) gives \(k=6\).
\(k = 6\)  ⟹  \(L:\; 6x+4y-12=0\)
Step 4

Check with the slider: at \(k=6\), \(m_L=-\frac{6}{4}=-\frac{3}{2}\) and \(-\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{2}{3}=-1\).

Hint: Negative reciprocal — the slope of a perpendicular line is the negative reciprocal of the original slope: \(-\dfrac{1}{2/3}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\).
\(m_L = -\dfrac{3}{2}\), which is the negative reciprocal of \(\dfrac{2}{3}\)

3 Step 3 — Find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\)

Substitute \(k=6\), then set \(x=0\).

Diagram 3 · Line \(L: 6x+4y-12=0\) with its \(y\)-intercept marked 0 / 3 steps
\(y\)-intercept = \(-\dfrac{-2k}{4}\) at \(k=6\) = 3 \(x\)-intercept = 2 Perpendicular? yes ✓
Step 1

With \(k=6\), the equation of \(L\) becomes

\[ 6x + 4y - 12 = 0. \]
Hint: Replace every \(k\) by \(6\). Remember \(2k = 12\).
\(L:\; 6x+4y-12=0\)
Step 2

The \(y\)-intercept is the value of \(y\) when \(x=0\):

\[ 4y - 12 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 4y = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 3. \]
Hint: Any point on the \(y\)-axis has \(x=0\). Substituting \(x=0\) kills the \(x\)-term.
\(y\)-intercept \(= 3\)
Step 3

Shortcut: from \(kx+4y-2k=0\), setting \(x=0\) gives \(y=\dfrac{2k}{4}=\dfrac{k}{2}\). With \(k=6\), \(y=\dfrac{6}{2}=3\).

\[ \boxed{\,y\text{-intercept} = 3\,} \]
Hint: Sanity check against the options: 3 is option D, matching the answer key.
Answer: D   (\(y\)-intercept \(=3\))
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • Slope of \(L_1: 6x-9y+4=0\)  ⟹  \(m_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
  • Slope of \(L: kx+4y-2k=0\)  ⟹  \(m_L = -\dfrac{k}{4}\)
  • Perpendicular condition: \(\left(-\dfrac{k}{4}\right)\left(-\dfrac{6}{-9}\right) = -1\)  ⟹  \(k = 6\)
  • \(L\) becomes \(6x+4y-12=0\); putting \(x=0\) gives \(y = 3\).
  • Final answer: \(y\)-intercept \(= 3\), i.e. option D.
