[2020MC#26]
The equation of the straight line \(L\) is \[ kx+4y-2k=0, \] where \(k\) is a constant. If \(L\) is perpendicular to the straight line \[ 6x-9y+4=0, \] find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\).
A. \(-3\)
B. \(-2\)
C. \(2\)
D. \(3\)
Step 1 · Read off the slope of \(L\)
Slope of a Line in General Form \(ax+by+c=0\ \Rightarrow\ m=-\dfrac{a}{b}\) For \(kx+4y-2k=0\), \(m_L=-\dfrac{k}{4}\); for \(6x-9y+4=0\), \(m=\dfrac{2}{3}\).
Rearrange \(kx+4y-2k=0\) into slope-intercept form: \[ 4y=-kx+2k \;\Longrightarrow\; y=-\frac{k}{4}x+\frac{2k}{4}. \] So the slope of \(L\) is \[ m_L=-\frac{k}{4}. \]
Step 2 · Slope of the given line \(L_1\) Let \(L_1\) denote \(6x-9y+4=0\). Rearranging: \[ 9y=6x+4 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=\frac{6}{9}x+\frac{4}{9}=\frac{2}{3}x+\frac{4}{9}. \] So \[ m_{L_1}=\frac{2}{3}\qquad\Big(\text{equivalently } -\frac{6}{-9}=\frac{2}{3}\Big). \]
Step 3 · Apply the perpendicular condition
Condition for Perpendicular Lines \(m_1m_2=-1\) \(\left(-\dfrac{k}{4}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)=-1\) gives \(k=6\).
Since \(L\perp L_1\), \[ m_L\times m_{L_1}=-1 \] \[ \Big(-\frac{k}{4}\Big)\times\Big(-\frac{6}{9}\Big)=-1 \] \[ \Big(-\frac{k}{4}\Big)\times\Big(\frac{2}{3}\Big)=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; -\frac{k}{6}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; k=6. \]
Step 4 · Find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\) With \(k=6\), the equation of \(L\) becomes \[ 6x+4y-12=0. \] Put \(x=0\): \[ 4y-12=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=3. \] Hence the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\) is \(3\).
Quick Check / Exam Tip In the general form \(ax+by+c=0\), the slope is \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \(-\dfrac{c}{b}\).
• For \(L\): slope \(=-\dfrac{k}{4}\), \(y\)-intercept \(=\dfrac{2k}{4}\).
• For \(L_1\): slope \(=-\dfrac{6}{-9}=\dfrac{2}{3}\).
Perpendicular condition: product of slopes \(=-1\). Never forget to substitute \(k\) back before reading off the intercept.
Answer \(k=6\), so \(L:\;6x+4y-12=0\), giving \(y\)-intercept \(\;3\).
D. \(3\)

Question. The line \(L: kx+2y-3k=0\) is perpendicular to \(3x-6y+7=0\). Find the \(y\)-intercept of \(L\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Slope of \(L\): \(m_L=-\dfrac{k}{2}\).
Step 2. \(3x-6y+7=0\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{1}{2}x+\dfrac{7}{6}\), so \(m=\dfrac{1}{2}\).
Step 3. \(m_Lm=-1\): \(\left(-\dfrac{k}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=-1\Rightarrow k=4\).
Step 4. \(L: 4x+2y-12=0\). Put \(x=0\): \(2y=12\Rightarrow y=6\).
Answer: \(6\)
