Trigonometry (59) · Sub-Topic: Trigonometric Identities

T13 Q31

Write down the fundamental trigonometric identity relating $\sin^2\theta$ and $\cos^2\theta$.

Solution

Recall the unit circle equation: for any angle $\theta$, the point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ lies on $x^2+y^2=1$.
Substitute $x=\cos\theta$ and $y=\sin\theta$ into $x^2+y^2=1$.
This gives $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.
The fundamental trigonometric identity is $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.
