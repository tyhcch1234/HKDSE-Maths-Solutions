In the figure, the equation of the straight line \(L\) is \(px+qy=7\). Which of the following is/are true?

  • I. \(p>7\)
  • II. \(q>7\)
  • III. \(q>p\)
  • A. I only   B. II only   C. I and III only   D. II and III only
STEP 1 · REWRITE IN SLOPE–INTERCEPT FORM

Make \(y\) the subject of the given equation \(px+qy=7\):

$$qy=-px+7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac{p}{q}x+\frac{7}{q}$$

So the slope is \(-\dfrac{p}{q}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \(\dfrac{7}{q}\).

STEP 2 · \(x\)-INTERCEPT OF \(L\)

Put \(y=0\) into \(px+qy=7\):

$$px=7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{7}{p}$$

So \(L\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis at \(\left(\dfrac{7}{p},0\right)\).

STEP 3 · READ THE FIGURE: \(0<\dfrac{7}{p}<1\)

From the diagram, the \(x\)-intercept lies strictly between \(O\) and \(1\):

$$0<\frac{7}{p}<1$$

Since \(p>0\) (it is positive from the position of the intercept) and \(\dfrac{7}{p}<1\):

$$\boxed{\,p>7\,}$$

Therefore statement I is true.

💡 The \(x\)-intercept being just to the left of \(1\) means the divisor must be larger than \(7\).
STEP 4 · READ THE \(y\)-INTERCEPT: \(\dfrac{7}{q}>1\)

From the diagram, \(L\) crosses the \(y\)-axis above \(1\):

$$\frac{7}{q}>1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q<7$$

So statement II (\(q>7\)) is NOT true.

STEP 5 · COMPARE \(p\) AND \(q\)

We have found \(p>7\) and \(q<7\). Hence:

$$q<7

So statement III (\(q>p\)) is NOT true.

💡 Only statement I holds — check the options carefully.
ANSWER

\(p>7\) and \(q<7\), so only I is true.

➜ Option A · I only
