Perpendicular Distance in Polar Coordinates

Question

The polar coordinates of the points \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are \((3,\,160^\circ)\), \((4,\,280^\circ)\) and \((6,\,340^\circ)\) respectively. The perpendicular distance from \(Q\) to \(PR\) is
A. \(2\)
B. \(3\)
C. \(2\sqrt{3}\)
D. \(3\sqrt{3}\)

Solution

Step 1 · Line PR passes through the pole \[ \angle POR = 340^\circ - 160^\circ = 180^\circ. \] So \(P\) and \(R\) lie on opposite rays from the pole \(O\); the line \(PR\) therefore passes through \(O\).
Key idea: \(P\), \(O\), \(R\) are collinear — the "line \(PR\)" is the diameter line through the pole.
Step 2 · Angle between \(OQ\) and line \(PR\)
Angle between Two Rays in Polar Coordinates \(\theta=\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}\quad(\theta_{2}\ge\theta_{1})\) \(OQ\) points at \(280^{\circ}\) and \(PR\) lies along \(160^{\circ}\), so the angle between them is \(120^{\circ}\).
Direction of \(OQ\) is \(280^\circ\), and the direction of line \(PR\) is \(160^\circ\) (equivalently \(340^\circ\)). The angle between them is \[ 280^\circ - 160^\circ = 120^\circ. \]
Step 3 · Perpendicular distance
Perpendicular Distance from a Point to a Line \(d=OQ\sin\theta\) The perpendicular from \(Q\) to the line through \(O\) is the opposite side of a right triangle with hypotenuse \(OQ=4\).
Dropping a perpendicular from \(Q\) to line \(PR\) (which passes through \(O\)) gives a right triangle with hypotenuse \(OQ = 4\): \[ d = OQ \cdot \sin 120^\circ = 4 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3}. \]
Step 4 · Verify foot lies on segment \(PR\)
Projection onto a Line \(\text{projection}=OQ\cos\theta\) \(4\cos120^{\circ}=-2\) puts the foot \(2\) units from \(O\) on the \(R\)-side, and \(OR=6>2\) confirms it is on \(PR\).
The projection of \(OQ\) onto line \(PR\) is \(4\cos 120^\circ = -2\); that is, the foot is \(2\) units from \(O\) on the \(R\)-side of \(O\). Since \(OR = 6 > 2\), the foot lies on the segment \(PR\).
Final Answer: C. \(2\sqrt{3}\)
