Mensuration — Spheres melted and recast into right circular cones

Surface Area Ratio → Length Ratio Volume of a Sphere Volume of a Cone Similar Solids & the \(r^3\) Argument Volume Conservation

The Question Full Question Text 7 marks

Read carefully: two spheres first, then two cones after melting.

There are two solid metal spheres. The ratio of the surface area of the smaller sphere to the surface area of the larger sphere is \(4:9\). The radius of the larger sphere is \(9\) cm.

(a) Express, in terms of \(\pi\), the volume of the smaller sphere.  [3 marks]

(b) The two spheres are melted and recast into two solid right circular cones. Denote these two circular cones by \(A\) and \(B\). It is given that the height and the base radius of \(A\) are \(10\) cm and \(6\) cm respectively. A student finds that the base radius of \(B\) is \(12\) cm. The student claims that \(A\) and \(B\) are similar. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.  [4 marks]

Sphere (radius \(r\))Surface area \(=4\pi r^{2}\)
Sphere (radius \(r\))Volume \(=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\)
Cone (base \(r\), height \(h\))Volume \(=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\)

Key ratio rule for similar solids: area ratio \(=(k)^2\) and volume ratio \(=(k)^3\), where \(k\) is the ratio of corresponding lengths.

Part (a) Volume of the smaller sphere 3 marks

Use the surface-area ratio to find the radius first, then apply the sphere volume formula.

Area ratio \(S_{\text{small}}:S_{\text{large}} = 4:9\)
Length ratio \(r_{\text{small}}:r_{\text{large}} = 2:3\)
\(r_{\text{small}} = 6.00\) cm
\(V_{\text{small}} = 288\pi\) cm³
9 cm

Move the slider: even when the larger radius changes, the ratio \(r_{\text{small}}:r_{\text{large}}\) stays \(2:3\) — that is the whole point of the surface-area ratio.

Step 11M

For similar solids (spheres are all similar), the surface-area ratio is the square of the ratio of corresponding lengths (radii). So with the area ratio \(4:9\), \[\frac{r_{\text{small}}}{r_{\text{large}}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{9}} = \frac{2}{3}.\]

Hint: Area ratio \(=(k)^2\), volume ratio \(=(k)^3\), where \(k\) is the length ratio. To undo a square, take the square root.
Step 21M

The radius of the larger sphere is \(9\) cm, so \[r_{\text{small}} = \frac{2}{3}\times 9 = 6 \text{ cm}.\]

Hint: Cross-multiply: \(r_{\text{small}}=\dfrac{2}{3}(9)\). Check it is smaller than \(9\) — a sanity check.
Step 31A

Apply the sphere volume formula with \(r=6\): \[V_{\text{small}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi (6)^{3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi \times 216 = 288\pi \text{ cm}^{3}.\]

Hint: \(6^3 = 216\), and \(\dfrac{4}{3}\times 216 = 4\times 72 = 288\). Do not cube the radius before substituting — cube \(r\), not \(3r\).
Answer: The volume of the smaller sphere is \(\boxed{288\pi \text{ cm}^{3}}\).

Part (b) Are cones \(A\) and \(B\) similar? 4 marks

Recast = the total volume of metal is unchanged. Compare the actual volumes with what similarity would demand.

\(V_{\text{metal}} = V_{\text{sph small}} + V_{\text{sph large}} = 1512\pi\) cm³
\(V_A = \frac{1}{3}\pi(6)^2(10) = 120\pi\) cm³
\(V_B = 1392\pi\) cm³
\(V_B:V_A = 11.6\)  vs  required \(8\)
10 cm

Slide the axis height to “see through” the two cones. \(A\) and \(B\) share the same base radius (blue lines) — a similarity would demand proportional bases and heights, and volumes in the ratio \(1^3:2^3 = 1:8\).

Step 11M

Volume of cone \(A\) (base radius \(6\) cm, height \(10\) cm): \[V_A = \frac{1}{3}\pi (6)^{2}(10) = \frac{1}{3}\pi (36)(10) = 120\pi \text{ cm}^{3}.\]

Hint: Square the base radius first: \(6^2=36\); then \(\dfrac{1}{3}(36)(10)=120\).
Step 21M

Recasting conserves volume. Volume of the larger sphere: \[\frac{4}{3}\pi(9)^{3} = \frac{4}{3}\pi(729) = 972\pi \text{ cm}^{3}.\] So the total metal is \[V_{\text{small}} + V_{\text{large}} = 288\pi + 972\pi = 1260\pi \text{ cm}^{3},\] and the volume of cone \(B\) is \[V_B = 1260\pi - V_A = 1260\pi - 120\pi = 1140\pi \text{ cm}^{3}.\]

Hint: “Melted and recast” almost always signals volume is conserved. The two spheres become exactly the two cones, so \(V_A + V_B = V_{\text{small sphere}} + V_{\text{large sphere}}\).
Intermediate answers: \(V_A = 120\pi\ \text{cm}^3\) and \(V_B = 1140\pi\ \text{cm}^3\).
Step 31M

Now test the similarity claim. For similar solids the ratio of corresponding lengths is the same in every direction. The base radii of \(A\) and \(B\) are \(6\) and \(12\), so the length ratio would be \[k = \frac{12}{6} = 2.\] If \(A\) and \(B\) were similar with \(k=2\), their volumes would be in the ratio \[\frac{V_B}{V_A} = k^{3} = 2^{3} = 8.\] But from the actual volumes, \[\frac{V_B}{V_A} = \frac{1140\pi}{120\pi} = \frac{19}{2} = 9.5 \neq 8.\]

Hint: Volume ratio of similar solids \(=(k)^3\), not \((k)^2\). A common trap is to use the area ratio \(k^2=4\). Also simplify \(1140/120\) fully: divide top and bottom by \(60\).
Step 41A

Equivalently, if \(A\) and \(B\) were similar with base-radius ratio \(2\), their heights would also have to be in the ratio \(2:1\), i.e. the height of \(B\) would be \(2\times 10 = 20\) cm; this would give \[V_B = \frac{1}{3}\pi(12)^{2}(20) = 960\pi \neq 1140\pi.\] Whatever route is used, the actual volume ratio \(19:2\) disagrees with the value \(8\) required by similarity. Hence \(A\) and \(B\) are not similar, and the student’s claim is incorrect.

Hint: You only need one inconsistency to disprove similarity. A fully correct answer must state the conclusion clearly: “So \(A\) and \(B\) are not similar. Thus the claim is not correct.”
Answer: The claim is not correct — \(A\) and \(B\) are not similar, since \(\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}=\dfrac{19}{2}\neq 8=\left(\dfrac{12}{6}\right)^{3}\).

Summary Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Radius: \(\sqrt{\dfrac{4}{9}}=\dfrac{2}{3}\), so \(r_{\text{small}} = \dfrac{2}{3}\times 9 = 6\) cm.
  • (a) Volume: \(V_{\text{small}} = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi(6)^{3} = \boxed{288\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}}\).
  • (b) Cone A: \(V_A=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(6)^{2}(10)=120\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}\).
  • (b) Total metal / Cone B: \(288\pi+972\pi=1260\pi\), so \(V_B=1260\pi-120\pi=\boxed{1140\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}}\).
  • (b) Conclusion: \(\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}=\dfrac{1140\pi}{120\pi}=\dfrac{19}{2}\), but similarity with base-radius ratio \(2\) would require \(\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}=2^{3}=8\). Since \(\dfrac{19}{2}\neq 8\), \(A\) and \(B\) are not similar.

Remember the three-scale rule for similar solids: lengths \(\times k\), areas \(\times k^{2}\), volumes \(\times k^{3}\).
