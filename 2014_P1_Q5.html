Question

Consider the formula

$$2(3m+n)=m+7.$$

(a) Make \(n\) the subject of the above formula.

(b) If the value of \(m\) is increased by \(2\), write down the change in the value of \(n\). [4 marks]

Solution

Step 1

Start from the given formula

\[2(3m+n)=m+7\]

The variable \(n\) is trapped inside the bracket.
We must first remove the bracket.

Hint: expanding the bracket is always the first move when the subject is inside it.
Step 2

Expand the left-hand side

Distributive Law \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\) Multiply each term inside the bracket by \(2\): \(2(3m+n)=6m+2n\).
\[2\cdot 3m+2\cdot n = m+7\] \[6m+2n = m+7\]

1M — expanding correctly scores the method mark.

Hint: \(2(3m+n)=6m+2n\). Multiply each term inside the bracket by 2.
Step 3

(a) Make \(n\) the subject

Balancing Method for a Linear Equation \(ax+b=c\;\Longrightarrow\;x=\dfrac{c-b}{a}\quad(a\neq 0)\) Move \(6m\) to the other side, then divide both sides by the coefficient \(2\) of \(n\).

Collect all \(n\)-terms on one side;
move everything else to the other side:

\[2n = m+7-6m\] \[2n = 7-5m\]

Divide both sides by \(2\):

\[\boxed{\,n=\frac{7-5m}{2}\,}\]

1A — the correct subject scores the answer mark.

Hint: subtract \(6m\) from both sides, then divide by the coefficient of \(n\), which is \(2\).
Step 4

(b) Replace \(m\) by \(m+2\)

The new value of \(n\) is

\[n_{\text{new}}=\frac{7-5(m+2)}{2}=\frac{7-5m-10}{2}=\frac{-5m-3}{2}\]

1M — substituting \(m+2\) correctly.

Hint: put brackets around \(m+2\) so the \(-5\) multiplies the whole expression \(-5(m+2)\).
Step 5

Find the change in \(n\)

Change in the Subject \(\text{change}=n_{\text{new}}-n_{\text{old}}\) Subtracting the two expressions cancels every \(m\)-term, leaving the constant \(-5\).

Change \(=n_{\text{new}}-n_{\text{old}}\):

\[\frac{-5m-3}{2}-\frac{7-5m}{2}=\frac{-5m-3-7+5m}{2}=\frac{-10}{2}=-5\]

So the value of \(n\) decreases by 5.  
1M

Quick check with \(m=0\): old \(n=3.5\);
new \(n=\dfrac{7-5(2)}{2}=-1.5\). Indeed \(3.5-(-5)=8.5-... \) → difference \(=-5\). ✔

Hint: the \(m\)-terms cancel, so the change does not depend on \(m\) at all — it is a constant, \(-5\).
Answer:
(a)  \(n=\dfrac{7-5m}{2}\)
(b)  \(n\) decreases by \(5\) (change \(=-5\)).

Question. Consider the formula \(2(5m+n)=3m+8\).
(a) Make \(n\) the subject of the formula.
(b) If the value of \(m\) is increased by \(4\), write down the change in the value of \(n\). [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand the bracket: \(10m+2n=3m+8\).
Step 2. Collect the \(n\)-terms: \(2n=8-7m\), so \(n=\dfrac{8-7m}{2}\).
Step 3. Replace \(m\) by \(m+4\): \(n_{\text{new}}=\dfrac{8-7(m+4)}{2}=\dfrac{8-7m-28}{2}=\dfrac{-7m-20}{2}\).
Step 4. Change \(=\dfrac{-7m-20-8+7m}{2}=\dfrac{-28}{2}=-14\), so \(n\) decreases by \(14\). The \(m\)-terms cancel,
so the change does not depend on \(m\).
Answer: (a) \(n=\dfrac{8-7m}{2}\) (b) \(n\) decreases by \(14\)
