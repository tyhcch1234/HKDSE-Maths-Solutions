Step 1 · Read the figure

The centre $C(h,k)$ lies in Quadrant II, i.e. to the left of the $y$-axis and above the $x$-axis. Also the radius is positive.

$$\Rightarrow\quad h<0,\qquad k>0,\qquad r>0$$

This is the key information — every claim below is decided by this one fact plus the fact that the circle does not cross the axes.

Hint: in the figure the whole circle stays to the left of the $y$-axis and above the $x$-axis.
Distance from a Point to a Coordinate Axis \(d\big((h,k),\,y\text{-axis}\big)=-h,\qquad d\big((h,k),\,x\text{-axis}\big)=k\) For \(C(h,k)\) the gap to \(x=0\) is \(-h\) (since \(h<0\)) and the gap to \(y=0\) is \(k\) (since \(k>0\)).

Step 4 · Test claim II: $r-h>0$

Since $r>0$ and $h<0$, we have $-h>0$. Hence

Claim II is TRUE ✓ — a positive number minus a negative number is always positive.

Question. The circle \(C:(x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\) has its centre in Quadrant IV and does not cut either coordinate axis. Which of the following must be true? I. \(h>r\) II. \(k<-r\) III. \(h+k<0\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The centre \((h,k)\) lies in Quadrant IV, so \(h>0\), \(k<0\) and \(r>0\).
Step 2. The circle does not cut the \(y\)-axis, so the horizontal gap \(h\) from the centre to \(x=0\) exceeds the radius: \(h>r\). Statement I is true.
Step 3. The circle does not cut the \(x\)-axis, so the vertical gap \(-k\) exceeds the radius: \(-k>r\), i.e. \(k<-r\). Statement II is true.
Step 4. For III take \(h=10\), \(k=-2\), \(r=1\): then \(h>r\) and \(k<-r\), but \(h+k=8>0\). So III need not hold.
Step 5. Only I and II must be true.
Answer: I and II only.
