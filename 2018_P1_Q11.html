Statistics — Mode, Median & Mean of a Discrete Distribution
Mode Median Arithmetic Mean Frequency Table Inequalities

Q The Question

The following table shows the distribution of the numbers of children of some families:

Number of children01234
Number of families\(k\)2967

It is given that \(k\) is a positive integer.

(a)  If the mode of the distribution is 2, write down
   (i) the least possible value of \(k\);
   (ii) the greatest possible value of \(k\).  [2 marks]
(b)  If the median of the distribution is 2, write down
   (i) the least possible value of \(k\);
   (ii) the greatest possible value of \(k\).  [2 marks]
(c)  If the mean of the distribution is 2, find the value of \(k\).  [2 marks]

a Part (a) — Mode is 2 2 marks · 2A

9
STEP 11M

Write down the frequencies of each number of children:

\[ \begin{array}{c|ccccc} \text{children }x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ \hline \text{families }f & k & 2 & 9 & 6 & 7 \end{array} \]
Hint: The mode is the value with the largest frequency. Here \(f(2)=9\), so the mode is 2 whenever no other group reaches 9 or more.
STEP 21A

For the mode to be 2, the frequency of 2 must be strictly greater than every other group:

\[ 9 > k, \qquad 9 > 2,\; 9 > 6,\; 9 > 7 \]

The only real restriction is \(k < 9\). Since \(k\) is a positive integer (\(k \ge 1\)):

\[ 1 \le k \le 8 \]
Careful: if \(k=9\), then the value 0 would tie with 2 — and then 2 would not be the (unique) mode. So we need \(k<9\), i.e. \(k\le 8\).
STEP 31A
(a)(i) Least \(k = 1\)   |   (a)(ii) Greatest \(k = 8\)
0 / 3

b Part (b) — Median is 2 2 marks · 2A

9
STEP 11M

Find the total number of families and the cumulative frequencies:

\[ N = k + 2 + 9 + 6 + 7 = k + 24 \]

The sorted data must have the median at the middle. For the median to equal 2, the middle position must fall inside the block of "2"s.

Key idea: The cumulative frequency before the 2s is \(k+2\); the cumulative frequency up to and including the 2s is \(k+11\).
STEP 21A

For \(N=k+24\) an odd number, the median is the \(\left(\tfrac{N+1}{2}\right)\)-th value; for even \(N\), the median is the average of the \(\tfrac{N}{2}\)-th and \(\left(\tfrac{N}{2}+1\right)\)-th values.

Both middle positions must lie within the 2s, which happens exactly when

\[ k+2 < \frac{N+1}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{N}{2}+1 \le k+11 \]

Substituting \(N=k+24\):

\[ \frac{k+25}{2} > k+2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k+25 > 2k+4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k < 21 \] \[ \frac{k+24}{2}+1 \le k+11 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{k+26}{2} \le k+11 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k+26 \le 2k+22 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k \ge 4 \]
Both conditions at once: \(4 \le k \le 20\). The lower bound \(k\ge4\) stops the 1s (only 2 families) from pulling the median below 2; the upper bound \(k\le20\) stops the 0s from pulling the median above 2.
STEP 31A
(b)(i) Least \(k = 4\)   |   (b)(ii) Greatest \(k = 20\)
Check at the ends: \(k=4\Rightarrow N=28\), median \(=\frac{x_{14}+x_{15}}{2}=2\) ✓. \(k=20\Rightarrow N=44\), median \(=\frac{x_{22}+x_{23}}{2}=2\) ✓. At \(k=3\), the 13th/14th values are 1s → median 1 ✗; at \(k=21\), the 23rd value is a 0 → median 1 ✗.
0 / 3

c Part (c) — Mean is 2 2 marks · 1M + 1A

STEP 11M

Use the definition of the arithmetic mean:

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f} = \frac{0(k) + 1(2) + 2(9) + 3(6) + 4(7)}{k + 2 + 9 + 6 + 7} = 2 \]
Hint: the \(0(k)\) term contributes nothing to the numerator — this is why \(k\) only appears in the denominator at first.
STEP 21A

Evaluate the numerator and simplify the denominator:

\[ \frac{0 + 2 + 18 + 18 + 28}{k + 24} = 2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{66}{k+24} = 2 \]

Cross-multiply and solve:

\[ 66 = 2(k+24) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2k + 48 = 66 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2k = 18 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k = 9 \]
(c) \(k = 9\)
STEP 3CHECK

Verify: with \(k=9\), \(N=33\) and \(\sum fx = 66\), so

\[ \bar{x} = \frac{66}{33} = 2 \;\checkmark \]
Interesting note: \(k=9\) from part (c) would not give mode 2 (it gives a tie between 0 and 2), showing that the three conditions (a), (b), (c) are independent constraints.

📋 Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a)(i) Least possible value of \(k = 1\)
  • (a)(ii) Greatest possible value of \(k = 8\)
  • (b)(i) Least possible value of \(k = 4\)
  • (b)(ii) Greatest possible value of \(k = 20\)
  • (c) \(k = 9\)  (from \(\dfrac{66}{k+24}=2\))
