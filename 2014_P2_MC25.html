Question MC

In the figure, the two straight lines intersect at a point on the positive \(y\)-axis. Which of the following are true?

I. \(a < 0\)
II. \(c > 0\)
III. \(b = d\)

  • A. I and II only
  • B. I and III only
  • C. II and III only
  • D. I, II and III ✓

The two lines are \(ax+by=1\) (upper right) and \(cx+dy=1\) (upper left), meeting on the positive \(y\)-axis. Explore the geometry below, then check each statement.

Step 11M

Find the \(x\)-intercept of \(ax+by=1\) by putting \(y=0\):

\[ ax + b(0) = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = \dfrac{1}{a} \]
Hint: the \(x\)-intercept of a line written as \((x\text{-part})+(y\text{-part})=1\) is simply the number dividing \(x\).
Step 21M

From the figure, the upper-right line \(ax+by=1\) cuts the negative \(x\)-axis (its \(x\)-intercept lies to the left of \(O\)). Hence

\[ \dfrac{1}{a} < 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a < 0 \qquad (\text{since } a\neq 0). \]
Hint: be careful — \(1/a<0\) implies \(a<0\) because \(a\) must be negative for its reciprocal to be negative.
Step 31A
Statement I is TRUE: \(a<0\). ✓

Step 11M

Find the \(x\)-intercept of \(cx+dy=1\) by putting \(y=0\):

\[ cx + d(0) = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = \dfrac{1}{c} \]
Hint: same idea as before — isolate the \(x\)-term and divide.
Step 21M

From the figure, the upper-left line \(cx+dy=1\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis, i.e. \(1/c>0\):

\[ \dfrac{1}{c} > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; c > 0 \qquad (\text{since } c\neq 0). \]
Hint: positive \(x\)-intercept means the line crosses the axis to the right of the origin.
Step 31A
Statement II is TRUE: \(c>0\). ✓

Statement III — Is \(b = d\)? Reasoning

Step 11M

Both lines pass through the same point \(P\) on the \(y\)-axis. On the \(y\)-axis \(x=0\), so substitute \(x=0\) into each equation:

\[ by = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=\tfrac{1}{b}, \qquad dy = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=\tfrac{1}{d}. \]
Hint: a point on the \(y\)-axis has \(x\)-coordinate \(0\).
Step 21M

Since \(P\) is the same point for both lines, its \(y\)-coordinate must be the same:

\[ \dfrac{1}{b} = \dfrac{1}{d}. \]
Hint: the two lines share one intersection point — one \(x\)-value, one \(y\)-value.
Step 31M

Taking reciprocals of both sides (valid since \(b,d\neq 0\)):

\[ b = d. \]
Hint: \(1/b=1/d\) with \(b,d\neq0\) gives \(b=d\).
Step 41M

Geometric reading: the common point \(P\) is on the positive \(y\)-axis, so

\[ \dfrac{1}{b}=\dfrac{1}{d} > 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; b = d > 0 . \]
Hint: “positive \(y\)-axis” means \(y>0\) at the intersection, so the common \(y\)-intercept is positive.
Step 51A
Statement III is TRUE: \(b=d\). ✓

Summary

  • I. \(a<0\) — the line \(ax+by=1\) has a negative \(x\)-intercept \(1/a\), so \(a<0\). TRUE
  • II. \(c>0\) — the line \(cx+dy=1\) has a positive \(x\)-intercept \(1/c\), so \(c>0\). TRUE
  • III. \(b=d\) — both lines pass through the same point on the \(y\)-axis, hence \(1/b=1/d\) and \(b=d\). TRUE
All three statements are true → Answer: D
