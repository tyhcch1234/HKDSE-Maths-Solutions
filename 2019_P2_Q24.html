2019MC#24

Find the constant $k$ such that the straight lines

$3x + 2y + k = 0$   and   $kx + 12y - 6 = 0$

are perpendicular to each other.

  • A. −8
  • B. −4
  • C. 4
  • D. 8
Step 1 · Label the two lines
Let $L_1$ : $3x+2y+k=0$ and $L_2$ : $kx+12y-6=0$.
Step 2 · Find slope of $L_1$
Rearrange $L_1$ into the form $y = mx+c$:
$$2y = -3x - k \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\tfrac{3}{2}x - \tfrac{k}{2}$$ Hence the slope of $L_1$ is $$m_1 = -\frac{3}{2}.$$
Slope of a Line in General Form \(Ax+By+C=0\ \Longrightarrow\ m=-\dfrac{A}{B}\) For \(L_{1}:3x+2y+k=0\) the slope is \(m_{1}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\).
Step 3 · Find slope of $L_2$
Rearrange $L_2$:
$$12y = -kx + 6 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\tfrac{k}{12}x + \tfrac{1}{2}$$ Hence the slope of $L_2$ is $$m_2 = -\frac{k}{12}.$$
Step 4 · Apply the perpendicular condition
For $L_1$ $\perp$ $L_2$, their slopes satisfy $$m_1 \times m_2 = -1.$$ Substitute $m_1$ and $m_2$: $$\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{k}{12}\right) = -1.$$
Perpendicular Slopes \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) With \(m_{1}=-\tfrac{3}{2}\) and \(m_{2}=-\dfrac{k}{12}\), the product equals \(-1\) and gives \(k=-8\).
Step 5 · Solve for $k$
$$\frac{3k}{24} = -1 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{k}{8} = -1 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{k = -8}.$$
∴ The answer is k = −8 — option A.

Question. If the straight lines \(L_{1}:2x+3y+k=0\) and \(L_{2}:kx-8y+5=0\) are perpendicular, find \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. For \(L_{1}:2x+3y+k=0\), the slope is \(m_{1}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\).
Step 2. For \(L_{2}:kx-8y+5=0\), the slope is \(m_{2}=-\dfrac{k}{-8}=\dfrac{k}{8}\).
Step 3. Perpendicular lines satisfy \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\): \(\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{k}{8}\right)=-1\).
Step 4. So \(-\dfrac{2k}{24}=-1\), i.e. \(\dfrac{k}{12}=1\), giving \(k=12\).
Step 5. Check: \(m_{1}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\) and \(m_{2}=\dfrac{12}{8}=\dfrac{3}{2}\), and \(-\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{3}{2}=-1\) ✓.
Answer: \(k=12\)
