Remainder Theorem

Question

Let \(g(x)=hx^3+2x^2+kx-5\), where \(h\) and \(k\) are constants. If \(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x-4\), find the remainder when \(g(x)\) is divided by \(x+4\).

  • A. \(-27\)
  • B. \(-10\)
  • C. \(0\)
  • D. \(54\)

Solution

Use the factor theorem
Factor Theorem \((x-c)\ \text{is a factor of}\ g(x)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ g(c)=0\) Divisibility by \(x-4\) gives \(64h+4k+27=0\), so \(8h+2k=-5\).

\(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x-4\), so \(g(4)=0\):

\[ 64h+2(4)^2+4k-5=0\ \Rightarrow\ 64h+4k+27=0\ \Rightarrow\ 16h+k=-\frac{27}{4} \]
Set up the required remainder
Remainder Theorem \(\text{remainder on division by }(x-a)=g(a)\) The remainder on division by \(x+4\) is \(g(-4)=-(8h+2k)+5\).

By the remainder theorem, the remainder on division by \(x+4\) is \(g(-4)\):

\[ g(-4)=h(-64)+2(16)+k(-4)-5=-64h-4k+27=-4(16h+k)+27 \]
Substitute and evaluate \[ g(-4)=-4\left(-\frac{27}{4}\right)+27=27+27=54 \]
Final Answer: D
