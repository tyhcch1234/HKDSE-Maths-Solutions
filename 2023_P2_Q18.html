Question

2023 MC #18. According to the figure, which of the following must be true?

I. $\;a+b=90^\circ$
II. $\;c+d=180^\circ$
III. $\;a+b+c=d$

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

Corrected figure (the three parallel lines $AD // GH // BC$, transversals $EG$ and $CD$):
  • $AD // GH // BC$ (shown on the parallel lines).
  • $E$, $B$, $G$ are collinear (one transversal through $B$); $A$, $F$ and $C$, $D$ lie on the other transversal.
  • $a=\angle GAD$,   $b=\angle FBG$,   $c=\angle ECB$,   $d=\angle ADC$.

Solution

STEP 0 · Understand the figure

The lines $AD$, $GH$, $BC$ are parallel. The line $EG$ is a transversal cutting $GH$ at $B$, and $CD$ is a transversal joining $BC$ to $AD$.

Angles: $a=\angle GAD$, $b=\angle FBG$, $c=\angle ECB$, $d=\angle ADC$.

Tip: whenever two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, use corresponding angles and co-interior (interior) angles.
STEP 1 · Test statement I

Draw the auxiliary line through $B$ parallel to $AD$ and $BC$ — this is the given line $GH$.

Since $GH // AD$ and $EG$ is a transversal: $$\angle AGH=\angle GAD=a \quad \text{(corresponding } \angle\text{s, } GH // AD)$$

Since $GH // BC$ and $FB$ is a transversal: $$\angle BGH=\angle FBG=b \quad \text{(corresponding } \angle\text{s, } GH // BC)$$

At point $G$, the straight angle gives $$\angle AGH+\angle BGH=\angle AGB$$ and since $A$ is on line $BC$ (with $G$ above it), $\angle AGB=90^\circ$. Hence $$a+b=90^\circ.$$ Statement I is true.

Corresponding Angles of Parallel Lines \(GH // AD\ \Longrightarrow\ \angle AGH=\angle GAD\) The transversal \(EG\) cuts the parallel lines \(GH\) and \(AD\), giving equal corresponding angles.
STEP 2 · Test statement II

Consider the transversal $CD$ joining the parallel lines $AD$ and $BC$, with the point $B$ between them.

Since $AD // BC$, $$\angle BCD+\angle ADC=180^\circ \quad \text{(interior } \angle\text{s, } AD // BC)$$

But $\angle BCD=c$ and $\angle ADC=d$, therefore $$c+d=180^\circ.$$ Statement II is true.

Interior Angles of Parallel Lines \(AD // BC\ \Longrightarrow\ \angle BCD+\angle ADC=180^{\circ}\) The transversal \(CD\) makes the two co-interior angles supplementary, so \(c+d=180^{\circ}\).
STEP 3 · Test statement III

Is $a+b+c=d$ always true? Use a counter-example: fix the line $BC$ and $GH$ and keep the transversal $FB$ fixed, then move $D$ only along the direction of the arrow (i.e. slide the line $AD$).

When $AD$ moves upward:

$a$ and $b$ stay the same (the transversal $EG$ doesn't change), while $$c \text{ increases and } d \text{ decreases.}$$

Numerical check with a valid configuration: take $a=50^\circ$, $b=40^\circ$, then from statement II $c+d=180^\circ$. Take $c=100^\circ$, $d=80^\circ$: $$a+b+c=50^\circ+40^\circ+100^\circ=190^\circ\neq 80^\circ=d.$$

So the L.H.S. increases while the R.H.S. decreases — the equality can fail. Statement III is not necessarily true.

STEP 4 · Choose the answer

Only statements I and II must be true, so the answer is A. I and II only

Final answer:   A. I and II only

Question. In the figure, \(AD // BC\). The line \(AB\) meets them at \(A\) and \(B\) with \(\angle DAB=68^{\circ}\), and the line \(CD\) meets them at \(D\) and \(C\) with \(\angle BCD=112^{\circ}\). Find \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle ADC\), and state whether \(\angle DAB+\angle ABC=180^{\circ}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(AD // BC\) with transversal \(AB\): the co-interior angles are supplementary, so \(\angle DAB+\angle ABC=180^{\circ}\).
Step 2. Hence \(\angle ABC=180^{\circ}-68^{\circ}=112^{\circ}\).
Step 3. \(AD // BC\) with transversal \(CD\): \(\angle ADC+\angle BCD=180^{\circ}\), so \(\angle ADC=180^{\circ}-112^{\circ}=68^{\circ}\).
Step 4. Since the two angles are co-interior angles between parallel lines, \(\angle DAB+\angle ABC=180^{\circ}\) is indeed always true.
Answer: \(\angle ABC=112^{\circ}\), \(\angle ADC=68^{\circ}\); yes, the sum is \(180^{\circ}\)
