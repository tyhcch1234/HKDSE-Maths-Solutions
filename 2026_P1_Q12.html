Line L : y = −2x − 9 Perp. bisector of HK H K P Q
QUESTION

The straight line \(L\) passes through the point \((-10,11)\). The slope of \(L\) is \(-2\).

(a)  Find the equation of \(L\). (2 marks)

(b)  The coordinates of the points \(H\) and \(K\) are \((2,5)\) and \((-4,-7)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(L\) such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(H\) and \(K\).

   (i)  Find the coordinates of \(P\).

   (ii)  Let \(Q\) be a point such that \(HPKQ\) is a quadrilateral, where \(HP=HQ=KQ\). Find the area of the quadrilateral \(HPKQ\). (5 marks)

Step 1 · Equation of L

Using the point–slope form with point \((-10,11)\) and slope \(-2\):

\[ y-11=-2(x+10)\ \Rightarrow\ y=-2x-9 \]
💡 Notice the slope is negative, so the line goes steeply down to the right.
Step 2 · Equidistance condition

Let \(P=(x,y)\). From \(PH^2=PK^2\):

\[ (x-2)^2+(y-5)^2=(x+4)^2+(y+7)^2 \]

Expanding and cancelling \(x^2+y^2\):

\[ -4x-10y+29=8x+14y+65 \] \[ 12x+24y=-36\ \Rightarrow\ x+2y=-3 \]

This is the perpendicular bisector of \(HK\).

Step 3 · Intersect with L

Substitute \(y=-2x-9\) into \(x+2y=-3\):

\[ x+2(-2x-9)=-3 \] \[ -3x-18=-3\ \Rightarrow\ x=-5 \] \[ y=-2(-5)-9=1 \]

So \(P=(-5,\,1)\).  Check: \(PH^2=49+16=65=PK^2\) ✓

Step 4 · Locate Q

\(Q\) is also at distance \(HP=\sqrt{65}\) from both \(H\) and \(K\), so \(Q\) also lies on \(x+2y=-3\). Let \(Q=(x,y)\) with \(x=-3-2y\). From \(HQ^2=65\):

\[ (-5-2y)^2+(y-5)^2=65 \] \[ 5y^2+10y+50=65 \] \[ y^2+2y-3=0\ \Rightarrow\ (y+3)(y-1)=0 \]

\(y=1\) gives \(Q=P\) (rejected); \(y=-3\) gives \(x=3\).

So \(Q=(3,\,-3)\).

Step 5 · Area (rhombus shortcut)

All four sides equal \(\sqrt{65}\), so \(HPKQ\) is a rhombus; its diagonals \(HK\) and \(PQ\) are perpendicular.

\[ HK=\sqrt{6^2+12^2}=6\sqrt5,\qquad PQ=\sqrt{8^2+4^2}=4\sqrt5 \] \[ \text{Area}=\tfrac12\times HK\times PQ=\tfrac12(6\sqrt5)(4\sqrt5)=60 \]
Shoelace check with \((2,5),(-5,1),(-4,-7),(3,-3)\) gives \(\tfrac12|64-(-56)|=60\) ✓
Answers:
(a)   \(y=-2x-9\)
(b)(i)   \(P=(-5,\,1)\)
(b)(ii)   Area \(=60\)
