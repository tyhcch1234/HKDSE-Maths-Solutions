HKDSE 2015 P2Q24 · Coordinate Geometry — Locus of a Point Equidistant from A and B

A (2, 0) B (1, 5) Segment AB & midpoint M Moving point P Locus (perpendicular bisector) PA = PB

Question. The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((2,0)\) and \((1,5)\) respectively. If \(P\) is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\), then the locus of \(P\) is

  • A. the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
  • B. the circle with \(AB\) as a diameter.
  • C. the straight line which passes through \(A\) and \(B\).
  • D. the angle bisector of \(\angle AOB\), where \(O\) is the origin.
STEP 1 · Translate the condition

\(P\) is equidistant from the two fixed points \(A\) and \(B\) means exactly:

$$PA = PB$$

So every position of \(P\) must satisfy this single distance equation.

💡 The locus is the set of all points satisfying the given condition — not just one point.
STEP 2 · Use the distance formula
Distance Formula \(PA=\sqrt{(x-x_{A})^{2}+(y-y_{A})^{2}}\) The condition \(PA=PB\) is written with the distance formula for \(A(2,0)\) and \(B(1,5)\).

Let \(P = (x,y)\). With \(A(2,0)\) and \(B(1,5)\):

$$\sqrt{(x-2)^2+(y-0)^2}=\sqrt{(x-1)^2+(y-5)^2}$$

Squaring both sides (both sides are non-negative):

$$(x-2)^2+y^2=(x-1)^2+(y-5)^2$$

STEP 3 · Expand and simplify

$$x^2-4x+4+y^2=x^2-2x+1+y^2-10y+25$$

Cancel \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) on both sides:

$$-4x+4=-2x-10y+26$$

$$\Rightarrow\ 2x-10y+22=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x-5y+11=0$$

📐 A linear equation in \(x\) and \(y\) ⟹ the locus is a straight line.
STEP 4 · Identify the line geometrically
Perpendicular Bisector \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) The locus has slope \(\tfrac{1}{5}\) while \(AB\) has slope \(-5\), so the two are perpendicular.

Slope of \(AB\):

$$m_{AB}=\frac{5-0}{1-2}=\frac{5}{-1}=-5$$

Slope of the locus line \(x-5y+11=0\):

$$y=\tfrac{1}{5}x+\tfrac{11}{5}\ \Rightarrow\ m=\tfrac{1}{5}$$

Since \(m_{AB}\cdot m=-5\times\tfrac{1}{5}=-1\), the locus is perpendicular to \(AB\).

STEP 5 · Check it passes through the midpoint
Midpoint Formula \(M=\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2}\right)\) The midpoint \(\left(\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{5}{2}\right)\) lies on the locus, confirming it is the perpendicular bisector.

Midpoint of \(AB\):

$$M=\left(\frac{2+1}{2},\frac{0+5}{2}\right)=\left(\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{5}{2}\right)$$

Substitute into \(x-5y+11\):

$$\tfrac{3}{2}-5\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)+11=\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{25}{2}+\tfrac{22}{2}=0\ \checkmark$$

The locus is the line through \(M\), perpendicular to \(AB\) — the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

STEP 6 · Conclusion

By the standard locus result: the locus of a point equidistant from two fixed points is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them.

Option B would require \(P\) on a circle (only some points satisfy it); Option C gives points that are not equidistant (except the midpoint); Option D gives points equidistant from the two lines \(OA\) and \(OB\), not from the points \(A\) and \(B\).

ANSWERA · the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\)

Question. \(A(3,1)\) and \(B(-1,5)\) are two fixed points. \(P\) moves so that \(PA=PB\). Find the equation of the locus of \(P\) and describe it.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(P=(x,y)\). \(PA=PB\) gives \(\sqrt{(x-3)^{2}+(y-1)^{2}}=\sqrt{(x+1)^{2}+(y-5)^{2}}\).
Step 2. Squaring and expanding: \(x^{2}-6x+9+y^{2}-2y+1=x^{2}+2x+1+y^{2}-10y+25\).
Step 3. Simplifying: \(-8x+8y-16=0\), i.e. \(x-y+2=0\).
Step 4. Slope of \(AB\) is \(\dfrac{5-1}{-1-3}=-1\) and the slope of the locus is \(1\);
since \(-1\times1=-1\), the locus is perpendicular to \(AB\).
Step 5. The midpoint of \(AB\) is \((1,3)\), which satisfies \(1-3+2=0\). Hence the locus is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
Answer: \(x-y+2=0\), the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\)
