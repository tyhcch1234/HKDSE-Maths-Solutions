HKDSE 2017 Paper 2 — Question 31

The figure shows the graph of $y=f(x)$. The curve cuts the $x$-axis at $x=-12$ and $x=4$, and its $y$-intercept is $10$.

If $g(x)=f\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$, which of the following may represent the graph of $y=g(x)$?

A. roots $-6,\;2$; $y$-int $5$
B. roots $-6,\;2$; $y$-int $10$
C. roots $-24,\;8$; $y$-int $5$
D. roots $-24,\;8$; $y$-int $10$
Step 1 · Read the given graph

From $y=f(x)$:   $f(-12)=0$,   $f(4)=0$,   $f(0)=10$.

The $x$-intercepts tell us the zeros of $f$; the $y$-intercept gives one known value of $f$.

Step 2 · Interpret $g(x)=f(x/2)$

Replacing $x$ by $x/2$ is a horizontal stretch by a factor of 2 about the $y$-axis (each point moves to double its horizontal distance from the $y$-axis).

Horizontal changes inside the bracket act opposite to intuition — dividing the input stretches the graph sideways.

Horizontal Stretch of a Graph \(y=f\!\left(\dfrac{x}{k}\right)\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{stretch by a factor }k\text{ parallel to the }x\text{-axis}\) Replacing \(x\) by \(\dfrac{x}{2}\) doubles every horizontal distance from the \(y\)-axis, so the graph becomes twice as wide.
Step 3 · Find the new $x$-intercepts

$g(x)=0 \iff f\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)=0 \iff \dfrac{x}{2}=-12 \text{ or } 4$.

So $x=-24$ or $x=8$.

Every zero of $f$ doubles: $-12 \to -24$ and $4 \to 8$.

x-intercepts of a Transformed Graph \(f\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \dfrac{x}{2}=-12\ \text{ or }\ 4\) Each zero of \(f\) is doubled: \(-12\to-24\) and \(4\to8\).
Step 4 · Find the new $y$-intercept

Put $x=0$:   $g(0)=f\!\left(\dfrac{0}{2}\right)=f(0)=10$.

The point on the $y$-axis does not move, because $x=0$ satisfies $x/2=0$.

Invariance of the y-intercept \(g(0)=f\!\left(\dfrac{0}{2}\right)=f(0)\) Because \(x=0\) maps to \(x=0\), the \(y\)-intercept stays at \(10\).
Step 5 · Choose the matching option

So $y=g(x)$ has $x$-intercepts $-24$ and $8$, and $y$-intercept $10$ — the same shape, twice as wide.

This matches option D.

Options A, B keep the old intercepts $-6,2$; option C moves the $y$-intercept to $5$ — all wrong.

Answer:  D

Question. The graph of \(y=f(x)\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(x=-9\) and \(x=6\), and its \(y\)-intercept is \(12\). If \(g(x)=f\!\left(\dfrac{x}{3}\right)\), find the \(x\)-intercepts and the \(y\)-intercept of the graph of \(y=g(x)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Replacing \(x\) by \(\dfrac{x}{3}\) is a horizontal stretch by a factor of \(3\) about the \(y\)-axis.
Step 2. The zeros satisfy \(\dfrac{x}{3}=-9\) or \(\dfrac{x}{3}=6\), so \(x=-27\) or \(x=18\).
Step 3. The \(y\)-intercept: \(g(0)=f\!\left(\dfrac{0}{3}\right)=f(0)=12\), so it stays at \(12\).
Step 4. Hence \(y=g(x)\) has \(x\)-intercepts \(-27\) and \(18\), and \(y\)-intercept \(12\) — the same shape, three times as wide.
Answer: \(x\)-intercepts \(-27\) and \(18\); \(y\)-intercept \(12\)
