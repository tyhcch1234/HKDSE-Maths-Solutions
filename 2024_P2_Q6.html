If $a$, $b$ and $c$ are non-zero constants such that $$4x^{2}+2ax+3a \equiv x(4x+b)+2c,$$ then $a:b:c=\;?$
  • A. $2:4:3$
  • B. $3:4:2$
  • C. $4:6:3$
  • D. $6:4:3$
Step 1 · Expand the right-hand side Expand $x(4x+b)+2c$: $$x(4x+b)+2c = 4x^{2}+bx+2c.$$ The identity becomes $$4x^{2}+2ax+3a \equiv 4x^{2}+bx+2c.$$
The $4x^{2}$ term matches automatically ✓ — we now compare the $x$ term and the constant term.
Step 2 · Compare the $x$ terms Coefficient of $x$ on the left is $2a$; on the right is $b$. $$\therefore\; 2a = b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a}{b}=\frac{1}{2}$$ So $$a:b = 1:2 = 2:4.$$
Scale $1:2$ up by 2 so that $b$ matches a $b$-value we can combine later.
Equating Coefficients \(Ax^{2}+Bx+C\equiv A_{1}x^{2}+B_{1}x+C_{1}\ \Longrightarrow\ A=A_{1},\ B=B_{1},\ C=C_{1}\) Comparing the \(x\)-terms gives \(2a=b\), i.e. \(a:b=1:2\).
Step 3 · Compare the constant terms Constant term on the left is $3a$; on the right is $2c$. $$\therefore\; 3a = 2c \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a}{c}=\frac{2}{3}$$ So $$a:c = 2:3.$$
Both conditions give a ratio to $a$ — this is the key of the whole question.
Step 4 · Combine into $a:b:c$ We have $a:b = 2:4$ and $a:c = 2:3$.
Choose $a = 2$ (the common value — it already matches in both ratios): $$b = 4,\qquad c = 3.$$ $$\therefore\; a:b:c = 2:4:3.$$
If the two ratios disagree on the value of $a$, rescale one of them (LCM of the $a$-values) before combining.
Combining Two Ratios \(a:b=m:n\ \text{and}\ a:c=p:q\ \Longrightarrow\ a:b:c=mp:nq:qm\) With \(a:b=2:4\) and \(a:c=2:3\) the common value \(a=2\) gives \(b=4\) and \(c=3\).
Step 5 · Check & pick the option Check e.g. $a=\tfrac{1}{2},\,b=1,\,c=\tfrac{3}{4}$: $$4x^{2}+2\!\left(\tfrac12\right)\!x+3\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=4x^{2}+x+\tfrac32,$$ $$x(4x+1)+2\!\left(\tfrac34\right)=4x^{2}+x+\tfrac32. \checkmark$$ This matches $a:b:c = 2:4:3$, i.e. option A.
Any non-zero multiple of $(a,b,c)$ works, since the identity only fixes the ratios.
Answer: A  ($a:b:c = 2:4:3$)

Question. If \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are non-zero constants such that \(6x^{2}+3ax+4a\equiv x(6x+b)+2c\), find \(a:b:c\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand the right side: \(x(6x+b)+2c=6x^{2}+bx+2c\), so the identity is \(6x^{2}+3ax+4a\equiv6x^{2}+bx+2c\).
Step 2. Compare the \(x\)-terms: \(3a=b\), so \(a:b=1:3\).
Step 3. Compare the constant terms: \(4a=2c\), so \(c=2a\) and \(a:c=1:2\).
Step 4. Take \(a=1\): then \(b=3\) and \(c=2\), giving \(a:b:c=1:3:2\).
Step 5. Check with \(a=2,\ b=6,\ c=4\): LHS \(=6x^{2}+6x+8\); RHS \(=x(6x+6)+8=6x^{2}+6x+8\) ✓.
Answer: \(a:b:c=1:3:2\)
