Tangent–Chord Angle, Alternate Segment & Equal Chords in a Circle

Tangent to Circle Alternate Segment Theorem Equal Chords ⇒ Equal Angles Angle Sum of Triangle Multiple Choice

Question Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(TA\) is the tangent to the circle \(ABCD\) at the point \(A\). \(CD\) produced and \(TA\) produced meet at the point \(E\). It is given that \(AB=CD\), \(\angle BAT=24^\circ\) and \(\angle AED=72^\circ\). Find \(\angle ABC\).

A. \(60^\circ\)    B. \(66^\circ\)    C. \(72^\circ\)    D. \(78^\circ\)

Answer: B  ·  2 marks

Part (a) Toolbox — Tangent–Chord Angle & Alternate Segment

Two circle theorems do almost all of the work here. Drag the slider to change the tangent–chord angle \(\angle BAT\) and watch every dependent angle update live on the circle.

24°
∠BAT = ∠ACB = 24° ∠CBD = ∠ACB = 24° ∠CAE = ∠ABC = 66°
Step 11M

Since \(TA\) is a tangent at \(A\) and \(AC\) is a chord, the tangent–chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment:

\[ \angle ACB = \angle BAT = 24^\circ \]
Hint: The tangent–chord angle uses the chord \(AC\) — the intercepted arc is the arc \(AC\) not containing \(B\), and the angle standing on that arc at \(B\) is exactly \(\angle ABC\)… careful! It is the alternate segment angle that matters, i.e. \(\angle ACB\) waits on arc \(AB\).
Step 21M

Given \(AB=CD\), the chords are equal, so the angles in the alternate segments are equal (equal chords subtend equal angles at the circumference):

\[ \angle CBD = \angle ACB = 24^\circ \]
Hint: Equal chords \(AB\) and \(CD\) subtend equal angles at the circle: \(\angle ACB\) (on \(AB\)) equals \(\angle CBD\) (on \(CD\)).
Step 31M

Apply the alternate segment theorem to the tangent \(TA\) with chord \(AC\) at the other side:

\[ \angle CAE = \angle ABC \quad\text{(angle in alternate segment)} \]

Then in \(\triangle ACE\), use the angle sum:

\[ \angle AEC + \angle ACE + \angle CAE = 180^\circ \]
This sets up the equation for \(\angle ABC = x\) in Part (b).
0 / 3

Part (b) Solve — Find \(\angle ABC\)

Let \(\angle ABC = x\). Use the map below to follow the chain of angle equalities and finish with \(\triangle ACE\).

66°
∠ACD = x − 24° ∠CAE = x 72° + (x−24°) + x = 180°
Step 11M

Let \(\angle ABC = x\). We already know \(\angle ABD\) and \(\angle ACD\) stand on the same chord \(AD\), so they are equal (angles in the same segment). Also \(\angle ABD = x - 24^\circ\).

\[ \angle ACD = \angle ABD = x - 24^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle ABD = \angle ABC - \angle CBD = x - 24^\circ\), because \(BD\) splits \(\angle ABC\).
Step 21M

Use the alternate segment theorem once more for the tangent–chord angle at \(A\) with chord \(AB\):

\[ \angle CAE = \angle ABC = x \]
Hint: \(\angle CAE\) is the angle between the tangent \(EA\) and the chord \(AC\) measured in the alternate segment, which equals \(\angle ABC\).
Step 31A

In \(\triangle ACE\), \(\angle AEC = 72^\circ\) is given. Apply the angle sum of a triangle:

\[ 72^\circ + (x-24^\circ) + x = 180^\circ \] \[ 2x + 48^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ x = 66^\circ \]
\[ \angle ABC = 66^\circ \quad \boxed{\text{B}} \]
0 / 3

Answer at a Glance

  • Tangent–chord angle: \(\angle ACB = \angle BAT = 24^\circ\)
  • Equal chords \(AB=CD\): \(\angle CBD = \angle ACB = 24^\circ\)
  • Then \(\angle ABD = \angle ABC - \angle CBD = x - 24^\circ\)
  • Same segment: \(\angle ACD = \angle ABD = x - 24^\circ\)
  • Alternate segment: \(\angle CAE = \angle ABC = x\)
  • Angle sum in \(\triangle ACE\): \(72^\circ + (x-24^\circ) + x = 180^\circ\)
  • 🔷 \(\angle ABC = 66^\circ\) → Option B
