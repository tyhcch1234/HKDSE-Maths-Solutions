HKDSE 2025 · Paper 2 · Q9

Question

Let \(p(x)=nx^{3}-3nx+36\), where \(n\) is a constant. If \(x+3\) is a factor of \(p(x)\), then \(p(3)=\ ?\)
A. \(-2\)
B. \(0\)
C. \(2\)
D. \(72\)
1
Apply the Factor Theorem.
If \(x+3\) is a factor of \(p(x)\), then \(p(-3)=0\).
Factor Theorem: \((x-a)\) is a factor \(\iff p(a)=0\). Here \(a=-3\).
2
Substitute \(x=-3\) into \(p(x)=nx^{3}-3nx+36\).
\[n(-3)^{3}-3n(-3)+36=0\] \[-27n+9n+36=0\]
Careful with signs: \((-3)^3=-27\) and \(-3n(-3)=+9n\).
3
Solve for \(n\).
\[-18n+36=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 18n=36 \;\Rightarrow\; n=2\]
So \(p(x)=2x^{3}-6x+36\).
4
Evaluate \(p(3)\).
\[p(3)=2(3)^{3}-6(3)+36\] \[=54-18+36=72\]
Substituting back into the polynomial with \(n=2\).
Answer: D. \(72\)

Check: \(p(-3)=2(-27)-6(-3)+36=-54+18+36=0\) ✓ so \(x+3\) is indeed a factor.
