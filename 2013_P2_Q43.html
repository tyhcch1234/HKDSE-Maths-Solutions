Finding the \(y\)-coordinate of the circumcentre of a triangle

Coordinate Geometry Circumcentre Perpendicular Bisector Equidistant Points

Q The Question

Let \(O\) be the origin. If the coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,\,12)\) and \((30,\,12)\) respectively, then the \(y\)-coordinate of the circumcentre of \(\triangle OAB\) is

A. \(6\).
B. \(8\).
C. \(12\).
D. \(15\).

A Part (a) — Key Idea: Perpendicular Bisector of \(OA\)

The circumcentre is equidistant from \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\).

Triangle \(OAB\) Perpendicular bisector of \(OA\) Circumcentre & circle
0 / 3
Step 1
The circumcentre \(C\) is equidistant from \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\), so it lies on the perpendicular bisector of every side.
Hint: \(C\) is the centre of the circle passing through \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\).
Step 2
\(OA\) is a vertical segment (from \((0,0)\) to \((0,12)\)), so the perpendicular bisector of \(OA\) is a horizontal line.
Hint: A vertical segment has a horizontal perpendicular bisector.
Step 3
Since \(C\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(OA\), its \(y\)-coordinate is the midpoint of the \(y\)-coordinates of \(O\) and \(A\): \[ y_C = \frac{0+12}{2} = 6. \]
Answer: Option A — the \(y\)-coordinate of the circumcentre is \(\boxed{6}\).

B Part (b) — Check with the Perpendicular Bisector of \(AB\)

An alternative view using the \(x\)-coordinate.

0 / 3
Step 1
\(AB\) is a horizontal segment, so its perpendicular bisector is a vertical line through the midpoint of \(AB\): \[ x_C = \frac{0+30}{2} = 15. \]
Hint: The midpoint of \(AB\) is \((15,12)\).
Step 2
Let the circumcentre be \(C=(15,\,y)\). Equate the distances \(CO\) and \(CA\): \[ 15^2 + y^2 = 15^2 + (y-12)^2. \]
Hint: Both sides share \(15^2\), so they cancel.
Step 3
\[ y^2 = (y-12)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; y^2 = y^2 - 24y + 144 \;\Rightarrow\; 24y = 144 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 6. \]
Answer: \(y\)-coordinate \(=\boxed{6}\), consistent with Method 1.

★ Summary of Results

  • Circumcentre \(C\) is equidistant from \(O(0,0)\), \(A(0,12)\), \(B(30,12)\).
  • Perpendicular bisector of \(OA\) is the horizontal line \(y=6\) ⇒ \(y\)-coordinate \(= \frac{0+12}{2} = 6\).
  • Perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) gives \(x=15\); solving \(CO=CA\) yields \(y=6\).
  • Distance from \(C(15,6)\) to \(O\): \(CO = \sqrt{15^2+6^2} = \sqrt{261}\).
  • Final answer: Option A — the \(y\)-coordinate of the circumcentre is \(6\).
