Functions and Graphs — Vertex & Circumcentre

Let $g(x)=x^{2}-2kx+2k^{2}+4$, where $k$ is a real constant.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, find, in terms of $k$, the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$.2 marks

(b) On the same rectangular coordinate system, let $D$ and $E$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$ and the vertex of the graph of $y=-g(x-2)$ respectively. Is there a point $F$ on this rectangular coordinate system such that the coordinates of the circumcentre of $\triangle DFE$ are $(0,3)$? Explain your answer.4 marks

1(a) Completing the square
Completing the Square\(x^{2}+bx=\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{b^{2}}{4}\)\(x^{2}-2kx=(x-k)^{2}-k^{2}\), so \(g(x)=(x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+4\) and the vertex is \((k,\,k^{2}+4)\).
$$g(x)=x^{2}-2kx+2k^{2}+4$$ $$=x^{2}-2kx+k^{2}+k^{2}+4$$ $$=(x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+4$$

So the vertex of $y=g(x)$ is $\;(k,\;k^{2}+4)$.

Hint: $-2kx$ tells you the square is $(x-k)^2$; split $2k^2$ into $k^2+k^2$, use one $k^2$ inside the bracket and keep the other outside.
2(b) Vertices $D$ and $E$
Translation and Reflection of a Graph\(y=-g(x-2)\ \Rightarrow\ \text{shift right by }2,\ \text{then reflect in the }x\text{-axis}\)\(E\) is the image of \((k,\,k^{2}+4)\), so \(E=(k+2,\,-(k^{2}+4))\).

From (a), $\;D=(k,\;k^{2}+4)$.

Now $g(x+2)=(x+2-k)^{2}+k^{2}+4$, whose vertex is $(k-2,\;k^{2}+4)$. Replacing $x$ by $x-2$ shifts the graph 2 units to the right:

$$\text{vertex of } y=g(x-2) \;=\; (k+2,\;k^{2}+4)$$ $$y=-g(x-2)\;\Rightarrow\;\text{reflect in the }x\text{-axis}$$ $$\therefore\; E=(k+2,\; -(k^{2}+4))$$
Hint: $y=-g(x-2)$ means shift right by 2, then flip vertically — so the $y$-coordinate changes sign.
3(b) Let the circumcentre be $C(0,3)$

Denote the point $(0,3)$ by $C$. If $C$ were the circumcentre of $\triangle DFE$,
then it must be equidistant from $D$, $E$ and $F$;
in particular $CD=CE$.

$$CD^{2}=\bigl((k-2)-0\bigr)^{2}+\bigl((k^{2}+4)-3\bigr)^{2}$$ $$=(k-2)^{2}+(k^{2}+1)^{2}$$ $$=k^{2}-4k+4+k^{4}+2k^{2}+1$$ $$=k^{4}+3k^{2}-4k+5$$
Hint: use the distance formula with the squared form $d^2=(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2$ to avoid square roots.
4(b) Compare $CD^{2}$ and $CE^{2}$
$$CE^{2}=\bigl((k+2)-0\bigr)^{2}+\bigl(-(k^{2}+4)-3\bigr)^{2}$$ $$=(k+2)^{2}+(k^{2}+7)^{2}$$ $$=k^{2}+4k+4+k^{4}+14k^{2}+49$$ $$=k^{4}+15k^{2}+4k+53$$

Set $CD^{2}=CE^{2}$:

$$k^{4}+3k^{2}-4k+5=k^{4}+15k^{2}+4k+53$$ $$3k^{2}+2k+12=0$$
Hint: the $k^4$ terms cancel — you are left with a quadratic in $k$.
5(b) Discriminant test → conclusion
Discriminant\(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\)\(\Delta=2^{2}-4(3)(12)=-140<0\), so \(CD^{2}=CE^{2}\) has no real solution in \(k\).
$$\Delta = 2^{2}-4(3)(12)=4-144=-140<0$$

So $3k^{2}+2k+12=0$ has no real roots: for every real $k$, $CD^{2}\neq CE^{2}$,
i.e. $CD\neq CE$. Hence $C(0,3)$ can never be equidistant from $D$ and $E$,
so it cannot be the circumcentre — whatever $F$ is chosen.

Therefore no such point $F$ exists. $\;\blacksquare$

Hint: a circumcentre must satisfy $CD=CE=CF$; failing $CD=CE$ already kills the possibility regardless of $F$.
Answer: (a) $\;(k,\;k^{2}+4)$.   (b) No — $CD^{2}=CE^{2}$ leads to $3k^{2}+2k+12=0$, whose discriminant is $-140<0$, so the equation has no real roots.

Question. Let \(g(x)=x^{2}-2px+2p^{2}+1\), where \(p\) is a real constant.
(a) By completing the square, find the coordinates of the vertex of \(y=g(x)\).
(b) A student claims that the equation \(g(x)=0\) has real roots for some value of \(p\). Do you agree? Explain.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(g(x)=x^{2}-2px+p^{2}+p^{2}+1=(x-p)^{2}+p^{2}+1\).
Step 2. So the vertex is \((p,\,p^{2}+1)\), and \(p^{2}+1>0\) for every real \(p\).
Step 3. Discriminant of \(x^{2}-2px+(2p^{2}+1)=0\): \(\Delta=(-2p)^{2}-4(2p^{2}+1)=4p^{2}-8p^{2}-4=-4p^{2}-4<0\).
Step 4. Since \(\Delta<0\) for all real \(p\), there are no real roots — I disagree with the student.
This agrees with the vertex always lying above the \(x\)-axis.
Answer: (a) \((p,\,p^{2}+1)\) (b) No — \(\Delta=-4p^{2}-4<0\) for all real \(p\)
