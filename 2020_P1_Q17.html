Question Full Question Text 6 marks

Let \(g(x)=x^{2}-2kx+2k^{2}+4\), where \(k\) is a real constant.

  1. (a) Using the method of completing the square, find, in terms of \(k\), the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\). 2 marks
  2. (b) On the same rectangular coordinate system, let \(D\) and \(E\) be the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) and the vertex of the graph of \(y=-g(x-2)\) respectively. Is there a point \(F\) on this rectangular coordinate system such that the coordinates of the circumcentre of \(\triangle DFE\) are \((0,3)\)? Explain your answer. 4 marks

Part (a) Vertex by Completing the Square 2 marks

vertex = (1.00, 5.00)
y-intercept = 6.00
a = 1, b = -2.00, c = 6.00
1Write down the function and prepare to complete the square1M

\[g(x)=x^{2}-2kx+2k^{2}+4\]

We want to express it in the form \((x-h)^{2}+t\). The coefficient of \(x\) is \(-2k\), so we use \(\left(\dfrac{-2k}{2}\right)^{2}=k^{2}\).

Hint: Split \(2k^{2}\) into \(k^{2}+k^{2}\) so that one \(k^{2}\) can complete the square with \(x^{2}-2kx\).
2Complete the square1M

\[\begin{aligned} g(x) &= x^{2}-2kx+k^{2}+k^{2}+4 \\ &= (x-k)^{2}+k^{2}+4 \end{aligned}\]

Hint: \(x^{2}-2kx+k^{2}=(x-k)^{2}\). The leftover constant is \(k^{2}+4\).
3Read off the vertex1A

For \(g(x)=(x-k)^{2}+(k^{2}+4)\), the vertex occurs when the squared term is zero:

\[x-k=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=k,\qquad y=k^{2}+4\]

Answer: The coordinates of the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) are \(\;(k,\;k^{2}+4)\).

Part (b)(i) Locate \(D\) and \(E\) 1A

D = ( -1.00 , 5.00 )
E = ( 3.00 , -5.00 )
C = ( 0 , 3 )
1Vertex of \(y=g(x)\) — this is \(D\)1A

From part (a):

\[D=(k,\;k^{2}+4)\]

Hint: The vertex of \(y=g(x)\) was found in part (a).
2Vertex of \(y=-g(x-2)\) — this is \(E\)1A

Replace \(x\) by \(x-2\) in \(g(x)\):\(\;g(x-2)=(x-2-k)^{2}+k^{2}+4\).

So \(\;y=-g(x-2)=-\big[(x-(k+2))^{2}+k^{2}+4\big]=-(x-(k+2))^{2}-k^{2}-4\).

This downward-opening parabola has vertex at \(x=k+2,\; y=-k^{2}-4\).

\[E=(k+2,\;-k^{2}-4)\]

Hint: The minus sign reflects the parabola downwards; the shift \(x\to x-2\) moves the vertex 2 units to the right.
Answer: \(D=(k,\;k^{2}+4)\), \(\quad E=(k+2,\;-k^{2}-4)\).

Part (b)(ii) Circumcentre Condition: \(CD^{2}=CE^{2}\) 1M

CD² = 17.00
CE² = 33.00
Difference CD² − CE² = -16.00
1Condition for \(C=(0,3)\) to be the circumcentre of \(\triangle DFE\)1M

If \(C\) is the circumcentre of \(\triangle DFE\), then \(C\) must be equidistant from the three vertices:

\[CD=CE=CF\]

In particular, we must have \(CD^{2}=CE^{2}\).

Hint: The circumcentre is the point equidistant from all three vertices. Start by forcing \(CD=CE\) and see whether a real \(k\) exists.
2Compute \(CD^{2}\)1M

\(C=(0,3)\), \(D=(k,\,k^{2}+4)\).

\[\begin{aligned} CD^{2} &= (k-0)^{2}+\big[(k^{2}+4)-3\big]^{2} \\ &= k^{2}+(k^{2}+1)^{2} \\ &= k^{2}+k^{4}+2k^{2}+1 \\ &= k^{4}+3k^{2}+1 \end{aligned}\]

Hint: Distance formula: \(CD^{2}=(x_{D}-x_{C})^{2}+(y_{D}-y_{C})^{2}\).
So: \(CD^{2}=k^{4}+3k^{2}+1\).
3Compute \(CE^{2}\)1M

\(C=(0,3)\), \(E=(k+2,\,-k^{2}-4)\).

\[\begin{aligned} CE^{2} &= (k+2-0)^{2}+\big[(-k^{2}-4)-3\big]^{2} \\ &= (k+2)^{2}+(-k^{2}-7)^{2} \\ &= (k^{2}+4k+4)+(k^{4}+14k^{2}+49) \\ &= k^{4}+15k^{2}+4k+53 \end{aligned}\]

Hint: Careful with signs: \((-k^{2}-7)^{2}=(k^{2}+7)^{2}=k^{4}+14k^{2}+49\).
So: \(CE^{2}=k^{4}+15k^{2}+4k+53\).
4Set \(CD^{2}=CE^{2}\) and simplify1M

\[k^{4}+3k^{2}+1=k^{4}+15k^{2}+4k+53\]

Cancel \(k^{4}\):

\[3k^{2}+1=15k^{2}+4k+53\]

\[0=12k^{2}+4k+52\]

Divide by 4:

\[3k^{2}+k+13=0\]

Hint: Bring all terms to one side to form a quadratic equation in \(k\).
Condition: \(3k^{2}+k+13=0\).

Part (b)(iii) Conclusion via the Discriminant 1A

h(k) = 13.00
Discriminant Δ = 1 − 4(3)(13) = -155
Roots? None (no real k)
1Compute the discriminant1M

For the quadratic equation \(3k^{2}+k+13=0\):

\[a=3,\quad b=1,\quad c=13\]

\[\Delta=b^{2}-4ac=1^{2}-4(3)(13)=1-156=-155\]

Hint: \(\Delta<0\) means no real roots; \(\Delta=0\) one repeated root; \(\Delta>0\) two distinct real roots.
2Interpret the result1M

Since \(\Delta=-155<0\), the equation \(3k^{2}+k+13=0\) has no real roots.

Therefore there is no real value of \(k\) such that \(CD^{2}=CE^{2}\), which means \(C=(0,3)\) cannot be equidistant from \(D\) and \(E\).

Hint: If \(C\) cannot be equidistant from \(D\) and \(E\), it certainly cannot be the circumcentre of \(\triangle DFE\).
3Conclude about the existence of \(F\)1A

For any choice of \(k\), the point \(C=(0,3)\) fails the necessary condition \(CD=CE\). Since the circumcentre must satisfy \(CD=CE=CF\), no such circumcentre at \((0,3)\) can exist.

Answer: There is no point \(F\) on this rectangular coordinate system such that the coordinates of the circumcentre of \(\triangle DFE\) are \((0,3)\).
