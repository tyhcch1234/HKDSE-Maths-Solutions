HKDSE 2022 · Paper 2 · Q22 Cyclic Quadrilateral in a Right-Angled Triangle

Diagram is drawn to scale: $AB=660$, $AD=572$, $BE=275$ (all cm).

22. In the figure, $ABC$ is a right-angled triangle with $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$. Let $D$ and $E$ be points lying on $AC$ and $BC$ respectively such that $ABED$ is a cyclic quadrilateral. If $AB = 660\text{ cm}$, $AD = 572\text{ cm}$ and $BE = 275\text{ cm}$, then $CD =$
A. 429 cm
B. 715 cm
C. 728 cm
D. 845 cm
STEP 1 · AE is a diameter
Angle in a Semicircle \(\angle ABE=90^\circ\ \Rightarrow\ AE\text{ is a diameter}\) \(E\) is on \(BC\), so \(\angle ABE=\angle ABC=90^\circ\) and therefore \(\angle ADE=90^\circ\).

Since \(E\) lies on \(BC\), $\angle ABE=\angle ABC=90^\circ$. In the cyclic quadrilateral \(ABED\), this right angle stands on the chord \(AE\), so \(AE\) is a diameter:

$$\angle ABE=90^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; AE\text{ is a diameter} \;\Rightarrow\; \angle ADE=90^\circ$$

Hence \(DE\perp AC\).

Hint: a right angle standing on a chord marks that chord as a diameter; the angle in the opposite arc is then \(90^\circ\) too.
STEP 2 · Lengths AE and DE
Pythagoras Theorem \(c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}\) \(AE^2=660^2+275^2=511225\) gives \(AE=715\), and \(DE^2=715^2-572^2\) gives \(DE=429\).

In right triangle \(ABE\) (right angle at \(B\), \(AB=660\), \(BE=275\)):

$$AE=\sqrt{660^2+275^2}=\sqrt{511\,225}=715$$

In right triangle \(AED\) (right angle at \(D\), \(AD=572\)):

$$DE=\sqrt{715^2-572^2}=\sqrt{184\,041}=429$$

\((660,275,715)=55\times(12,5,13)\) and \((572,429,715)=143\times(4,3,5)\), so both lengths are exact.
STEP 3 · Similar triangles
Similar Triangles (AAA) \(\dfrac{CD}{CB}=\dfrac{CE}{CA}=\dfrac{DE}{AB}\) Both triangles are right-angled and share \(\angle C\), and \(\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\dfrac{429}{660}=\dfrac{13}{20}\).

\(DE\perp AC\) and \(AB\perp BC\), and the angle at \(C\) is shared, so

$$\triangle CDE\;\sim\;\triangle CBA\quad(\text{A.A.A.})$$

The correspondence is \(C\leftrightarrow C\), \(D\leftrightarrow B\), \(E\leftrightarrow A\), so

$$\frac{CD}{CB}=\frac{CE}{CA}=\frac{DE}{AB}=\frac{429}{660}=\frac{13}{20}$$

Small triangle first, big triangle second — the right angles sit at \(D\) and \(B\), so \(CD\) pairs with \(CB\), not with \(CA\).
STEP 4 · One equation in CA

Write \(k=\dfrac{13}{20}\), \(CA=w\) and \(CB=y\). From step 3, \(CE=kw\) and \(CD=ky\), while the figure gives

$$CA=CD+572 \;\Rightarrow\; w=ky+572,\qquad CB=CE+275 \;\Rightarrow\; y=kw+275$$

Substituting the second into the first:

$$w=k(kw+275)+572 \;\Rightarrow\; w\left(1-k^{2}\right)=275k+572$$

Both unknowns collapse into a single equation because \(CD\) and \(CE\) are both measured by the same scale factor \(k\).
STEP 5 · Solve for CA

$$1-k^{2}=1-\frac{169}{400}=\frac{231}{400},\qquad 275k+572=\frac{3575}{20}+\frac{11\,440}{20}=\frac{3003}{4}$$

$$w\cdot\frac{231}{400}=\frac{3003}{4}\;\Rightarrow\;w=\frac{3003}{4}\times\frac{400}{231}=1300$$

Check: \(1300^2-660^2=1\,254\,400\), and \(\sqrt{1\,254\,400}=1120=CB\), as required.
STEP 6 · Find CD

$$CD=CA-AD=1300-572=728$$

Consistency check: \(CB=1120\), \(CE=1120-275=845\), and \(\dfrac{728}{1120}=\dfrac{845}{1300}=\dfrac{13}{20}\).

$$\boxed{CD=728\text{ cm}}$$

Answer: C  ·  \(CD = 728\) cm

Question. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle ABC=90^\circ\), \(AB=12\text{ cm}\) and \(BC=16\text{ cm}\). \(E\) lies on \(BC\) with \(BE=7\text{ cm}\), and \(D\) lies on \(AC\) such that \(ABED\) is a cyclic quadrilateral. Find \(CD\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\angle ABE=90^\circ\), so \(AE\) is a diameter and \(\angle ADE=90^\circ\); hence \(DE\perp AC\).
Step 2. \(\triangle CDE\sim\triangle CBA\) (AAA: shared angle at \(C\), right angles at \(D\) and \(B\)).
Step 3. \(AC=\sqrt{12^{2}+16^{2}}=20\text{ cm}\) and \(CE=BC-BE=16-7=9\text{ cm}\).
Step 4. \(\dfrac{CD}{CB}=\dfrac{CE}{CA}\Rightarrow CD=\dfrac{16\times9}{20}=7.2\text{ cm}\).
Answer: \(CD=7.2\ \text{cm}\)
