Question 1 — Change of Subject of a Formula

Make \(y\) the subject of the formula \(k=\dfrac{3x-y}{y}\). (3 marks)

Change of Subject Algebraic Manipulation Fractions & Equations Factorisation

Q The Question

Make \(y\) the subject of the formula
\(k=\dfrac{3x-y}{y}\)
3 marks
Big picture: \(y\) appears in the numerator and the denominator. Our job is to isolate every \(y\) on one side, then factorise it out and divide.

1 Step-by-Step Solution

0 of 4 steps revealed
1 Clear the fraction — multiply both sides by \(y\) ▾

Where does \(y\) first block us? It sits in the denominator. Multiply both sides by \(y\) to remove it.

\(k=\dfrac{3x-y}{y}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; ky=\left(\dfrac{3x-y}{y}\right)y\)
\(\Rightarrow\quad ky=3x-y\) 1M
Hint: On the right, the \(y\)'s cancel because \(\dfrac{y}{y}=1\). Multiplying an equation by the same non-zero quantity keeps it balanced — remember \(y\neq 0\).
2 Collect all \(y\) terms on one side ▾

There is a \(y\) on the left and a \(y\) on the right. Add \(y\) to both sides so that both \(y\) terms live on the left.

\(ky+y=3x\) 1M
Hint: \(-y+y=0\) on the right-hand side. On the left we now have two terms containing \(y\): \(ky\) and \(y\).
3 Factorise \(y\) out of the left-hand side ▾

\(ky\) and \(y\) share the common factor \(y\). Taking \(y\) out leaves us with a single \(y\).

\(y(k+1)=3x\)
Hint: \(ky+y = y\cdot k + y\cdot 1 = y(k+1)\).
This is exactly the distributive law read backwards: \(ab+ac=a(b+c)\).
4 Divide both sides by \((k+1)\) to finish ▾

Now \(y\) is multiplied by \((k+1)\). Divide the whole equation by \((k+1)\).

\(y=\dfrac{3x}{k+1}\) 1A
Hint: Require \(k+1\neq 0\), i.e. \(k\neq -1\), so the division is valid.

✓ Final Answer

Answer
\(y=\dfrac{3x}{k+1}\)
for \(k\neq -1\)

2 Interactive Check — Does Your Formula Really Work?

Put in any \(x\) and \(k\), compute \(y=\dfrac{3x}{k+1}\) using the formula you derived, then substitute it back into the original equation \(k=\dfrac{3x-y}{y}\) to see if the left-hand side matches \(k\).
