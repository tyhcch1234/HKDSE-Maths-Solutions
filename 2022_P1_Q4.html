The Question

Factorize

(a)
$$9c^{2}-6c+1,$$
(b)
$$(4c+d)^{2}-9c^{2}+6c-1.$$
[4 marks]
i

Why does the question split into (a) and (b)?

Part (a) is a warm-up: it is a perfect square. Part (b) contains the expression from (a) side by side with a squared bracket — so after rewriting, (b) becomes the difference of two squares. Spotting this link is the whole trick.

Formula 1 — Perfect Square

$$(x-y)^{2}=x^{2}-2xy+y^{2}$$

Used in (a): identify \(x=3c\), \(y=1\).

Formula 2 — Difference of Two Squares

$$X^{2}-Y^{2}=(X+Y)(X-Y)$$

Used in (b): \(X=4c+d\), \(Y=3c-1\).
