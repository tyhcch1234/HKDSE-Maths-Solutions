Question 38. In the figure, \(AC\) is a diameter of the circle. The point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\) is \(E\). It is given that \(DT\) is the tangent to the circle at \(D\). If \(\angle BEC = 96^\circ\) and \(\angle CDT = 41^\circ\), then \(\angle CDE =\)
A. \(35^\circ\)
B. \(41^\circ\)
C. \(43^\circ\)
D. \(47^\circ\)
💡 Hints. (1) An angle in a semi-circle is a right angle — so \(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\). (2) The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment. (3) An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
STEP 1 · Angle in a semi-circle

\(AC\) is a diameter, so the angle subtended by \(AC\) at the circumference is a right angle:

$$\angle ADC = 90^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in semi-circle})$$

Join \(AD\) to form triangle \(ADC\). On the diagram, \(AD\) is drawn dashed and the right angle at \(D\) is marked in orange.

STEP 2 · Alt. segment theorem
Tangent-Chord (Alternate Segment) \(\angle DAC=\angle CDT\) The tangent \(DT\) and chord \(DC\) give \(\angle DAC=41^\circ\).

\(DT\) is the tangent at \(D\) and \(DC\) is a chord. By the alternate segment theorem:

$$\angle DAC = \angle CDT = 41^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in alt. segment})$$

The green tangent \(DT\) and the \(41^\circ\) angle between \(CD\) and \(DT\) are highlighted.

STEP 3 · Angle sum of △ACD
Angle in a Semicircle \(AC\text{ is a diameter}\ \Rightarrow\ \angle ADC=90^\circ\) So \(\angle ACD=180^\circ-90^\circ-41^\circ=49^\circ\).

In triangle \(ACD\):

$$\angle ACD + \angle DAC + \angle ADC = 180^\circ$$

$$\angle ACD + 41^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$$

$$\angle ACD = 49^\circ$$

Triangle \(ACD\) is shaded; the two known angles are \(41^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\).

STEP 4 · Exterior angle of △CDE
Exterior Angle of a Triangle \(\angle BEC=\angle CDE+\angle DCE\) \(96^\circ=\angle CDE+49^\circ\), so \(\angle CDE=47^\circ\).

Consider triangle \(CDE\). Its exterior angle at \(E\) is

$$\angle BEC = 96^\circ = \angle CDE + \angle DCE \quad (\text{ext. } \angle \text{ of } \Delta)$$

Since \(DCE\) is the same angle as \(DCA=\angle ACD = 49^\circ\):

$$\angle CDE + 49^\circ = 96^\circ$$

$$\angle CDE = 47^\circ$$

Answer:   \(\angle CDE = 47^\circ\)   →   Option D
