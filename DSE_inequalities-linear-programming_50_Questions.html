Type 1

Linear Inequalities in One Unknown · 7 questions

Solve and show the solution on a number line

Core idea: An inequality is solved exactly like an equation — add, subtract, multiply or divide both sides by the same thing — with one extra rule: multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. On a number line use an open circle for \(<\) or \(>\) and a solid dot for \(\le\) or \(\ge\).

\(a\(abc\)\(x>k\): open circle at \(k\), arrow right
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \(3x-5>7\) and describe the solution on a number line.

Answer\(x>4\)
  1. Add 5 to both sides: \(3x>7+5=12\).
  2. Divide by 3 (positive, so the sign is unchanged): \(x>4\).
  3. On a number line: an open circle at 4 with an arrow pointing to the right.
  4. Check a value inside the solution, e.g. \(x=5\): \(3(5)-5=10>7\). ✓
Common mistake

Using a solid dot for \(x>4\). A solid dot means the endpoint is included, which is only right for \(\le\) or \(\ge\).

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \(2(x-3)\le 5x\).

Answer\(x\ge -2\)
  1. Expand the bracket: \(2x-6\le 5x\).
  2. Subtract \(2x\) from both sides: \(-6\le 3x\).
  3. Divide by 3 (positive): \(-2\le x\), i.e. \(x\ge -2\).
  4. Check \(x=0\): \(2(0-3)=-6\le 0\). ✓
Q3★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

Solve the inequality \(\frac{2x-1}{3}\ge\frac{x+4}{2}\).

Answer\(x\ge 14\)
  1. Multiply both sides by 6 (the L.C.M. of 3 and 2, and it is positive, so the sign stays): \(2(2x-1)\ge 3(x+4)\).
  2. Expand both sides: \(4x-2\ge 3x+12\).
  3. Subtract \(3x\): \(x-2\ge 12\), so \(x\ge 14\).
  4. Check \(x=14\): left \(=\frac{27}{3}=9\), right \(=\frac{18}{2}=9\). ✓
Common mistake

Multiplying by 6 but forgetting to multiply both terms inside a bracket, giving \(4x-1\ge 3x+4\). Multiply the whole numerator.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

(a) Solve \(5-3x<2x+20\).

(b) Write down the smallest integer satisfying the inequality.

Answer(a) \(x>-3\)　(b) \(-2\)
  1. Collect the \(x\) terms on the right to keep them positive: \(5-20<2x+3x\).
  2. \(-15<5x\), so dividing by 5 gives \(-3-3\).
  3. (b) The integers greater than \(-3\) are \(-2,-1,0,1,\dots\), so the smallest is \(-2\).
  4. Check \(x=-2\): \(5+6=11<16\). ✓
Q5★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the inequality \(\frac{3x-2}{4}-1\le\frac{x}{2}\).

Answer\(x\le 6\)
  1. Multiply every term by 4: \((3x-2)-4\le 2x\).
  2. Simplify: \(3x-6\le 2x\).
  3. Subtract \(2x\) and add 6: \(x\le 6\).
  4. Check \(x=6\): \(\frac{16}{4}-1=3\) and \(\frac{6}{2}=3\). ✓
Q6★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

Solve the inequality \(0.4x-1.5>0.7x+0.6\).

Answer\(x<-7\)
  1. Multiply the whole inequality by 10 to clear the decimals: \(4x-15>7x+6\).
  2. Subtract \(4x\): \(-15>3x+6\).
  3. Subtract 6: \(-21>3x\).
  4. Divide by 3: \(-7>x\), i.e. \(x<-7\).
  5. Check \(x=-8\): left \(=-3.2-1.5=-4.7\), right \(=-5.6+0.6=-5.0\), and \(-4.7>-5.0\). ✓
Common mistake

Dividing by 3 and keeping the sign the same is fine, but dividing by \(-3\) (a negative) would require reversing it. Also check the answer: \(x<-7\) is a ray to the left.

Q7★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

(a) Solve the inequality \(\frac{3x+8}{2}\le 2(x+1)\).

(b) Solve the inequality \(36-4x\ge 0\).

(c) Hence find the number of integers satisfying both inequalities.

Answer(a) \(x\ge 4\)　(b) \(x\le 9\)　(c) 6 integers
  1. (a) Multiply by 2 (positive): \(3x+8\le 4x+4\).
  2. Subtract \(3x\): \(8\le x+4\), so \(x\ge 4\).
  3. (b) \(36\ge 4x\), so \(x\le 9\).
  4. (c) Both inequalities hold when \(4\le x\le 9\).
  5. The integers are \(4,5,6,7,8,9\), a total of \(9-4+1=6\).
  6. Check the ends: \(x=4\) gives \(\frac{20}{2}=10=2(5)\) ✓; \(x=9\) gives \(36-36=0\ge 0\) ✓.
Common mistake

Counting \(9-4=5\) integers by subtracting the endpoints. When both ends are included the count is \(b-a+1\).

Compound Inequalities and Reversing the Sign · 7 questions

“and” / “or” statements; multiplying by a negative

Core idea: A double inequality \(a<\cdotsoverlap of the two solution sets; for “or”, take the union. Every time you multiply or divide by a negative number, the inequality sign flips.

\(abc\)“and” = intersection“or” = union
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \(-7x>21\).

Answer\(x<-3\)
  1. Divide both sides by \(-7\).
  2. Because \(-7\) is negative, the sign must be reversed: \(x<\frac{21}{-7}\).
  3. So \(x<-3\).
  4. Check \(x=-4\): \(-7(-4)=28>21\). ✓
Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \(-4<3x+2\le 11\).

Answer\(-2
  1. Subtract 2 from all three parts: \(-6<3x\le 9\).
  2. Divide all three parts by 3 (positive, so both signs stay): \(-2
  3. Check the ends: \(x=-2\) gives \(3(-2)+2=-4\), which is not \(>-4\), so \(-2\) is excluded ✓; \(x=3\) gives 11, which is allowed ✓.
Common mistake

Doing the operation to only the middle part. In a double inequality every part must receive the same treatment.

Solve the inequality \(-2\le\frac{4-x}{3}<5\).

  2. Subtract 4 from all parts: \(-10\le -x<11\).
  3. Multiply all parts by \(-1\), reversing both signs: \(10\ge x>-11\).
  4. Rewrite with \(x\) in the middle: \(-11
  5. Check \(x=0\): \(\frac{4}{3}\approx 1.33\), which lies between \(-2\) and 5 ✓.

Solve the compound inequality \(2x-1>5\) or \(3x+2<-7\).

Answer\(x>3\) or \(x<-3\)
  1. Solve the first: \(2x>6\), so \(x>3\).
  2. Solve the second: \(3x<-9\), so \(x<-3\).
  3. The word is “or”, so the answer is the union: \(x>3\) or \(x<-3\).
  4. On a number line: an open circle at 3 with an arrow right, and an open circle at \(-3\) with an arrow left; the middle gap is not shaded.
  5. Check \(x=-5\): \(3(-5)+2=-13<-7\) ✓.

Q12★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 1 Q4 ↗

Solve the compound inequality \(3x-4<2x+1\) and \(2x+5\ge x-1\). Hence write down the number of integers satisfying both inequalities.

Answer\(-6\le x<5\); 11 integers
  1. First inequality: \(3x-2x<1+4\), so \(x<5\).
  2. Second inequality: \(2x-x\ge -1-5\), so \(x\ge -6\).
  3. “and” means overlap: \(-6\le x<5\).
  4. The integers are \(-6,-5,\dots,4\). The count is \(4-(-6)+1=11\).
  5. Check the boundary \(x=-6\): \(2(-6)+5=-7\) and \(-6-1=-7\), so \(\ge\) holds ✓.

Q14★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

(a) Solve the compound inequality.

(b) Write down the greatest negative integer satisfying it.

Answer(a) \(x>-11\) or \(x\le -7\), i.e. all real numbers　(b) \(-1\)

  3. Divide by \(-3\) and reverse the sign: \(x>-11\).

Type 3

Quadratic Inequalities · 7 questions

Sign analysis, test values, graph reading

Core idea: Move everything to one side, factorise, and mark the two roots on a number line. A parabola that opens upwards is below the axis between its roots and above it outside them — so “\(<0\)” gives the interval between the roots and “\(>0\)” gives the two outer rays. Always test one value to confirm.

\(a>0,\ (x-r_{1})(x-r_{2})<0\Rightarrow r_{1}\((x-r_{1})(x-r_{2})>0\Rightarrow xr_{2}\)\(x^{2}\ge k\Rightarrow x\le-\sqrt{k}\ \text{or}\ x\ge\sqrt{k}\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \((x-2)(x-5)<0\).

Answer\(2
  1. The roots are \(x=2\) and \(x=5\). The coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is positive, so the parabola opens upwards.
  2. A product of two factors is negative between the roots.
  3. So \(2
  4. Check \(x=3\): \((1)(-2)=-2<0\) ✓. Check \(x=6\): \((4)(1)=4>0\), outside the interval ✓.
Common mistake

Writing \(x<2\) or \(x>5\). That is the solution of \((x-2)(x-5)>0\), not \(<0\).

Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the inequality \(x^{2}-9\ge 0\).

Answer\(x\le -3\) or \(x\ge 3\)
  1. Factorise as a difference of squares: \(x^{2}-9=(x-3)(x+3)\).
  2. The roots are \(-3\) and \(3\), and the parabola opens upwards.
  3. The expression is non-negative outside the roots, so \(x\le -3\) or \(x\ge 3\).
  4. Check \(x=4\): \(16-9=7\ge 0\) ✓; \(x=0\): \(-9<0\), so 0 is not in the solution ✓.
Q17★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the inequality \(x^{2}-5x+6\le 0\).

Answer\(2\le x\le 3\)
  1. Factorise: \(x^{2}-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)\).
  2. The roots are 2 and 3 and the parabola opens upwards, so the expression is \(\le 0\) between the roots.
  3. Both ends are included because of \(\le\), so \(2\le x\le 3\).
  4. Check \(x=2.5\): \((0.5)(-0.5)=-0.25\le 0\) ✓.
Common mistake

Using open endpoints for a \(\le\) inequality. When the sign includes equality the roots themselves are part of the solution.

Q18★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the inequality \(x^{2}+2x-15>0\).

Answer\(x<-5\) or \(x>3\)
  1. Factorise: look for two numbers with product \(-15\) and sum \(2\), namely \(5\) and \(-3\).
  2. \(x^{2}+2x-15=(x+5)(x-3)\), so the roots are \(-5\) and \(3\).
  3. The parabola opens upwards, so it is positive outside the roots: \(x<-5\) or \(x>3\).
  4. Check \(x=0\): \(-15>0\) is false, so 0 is correctly excluded.
Q19★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the inequality \(2x^{2}-7x+3\ge 0\).

Answer\(x\le\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x\ge 3\)
  1. Factorise: \(2x^{2}-7x+3=(2x-1)(x-3)\).
  2. The roots are \(x=\frac{1}{2}\) and \(x=3\).
  3. The coefficient of \(x^{2}\) is positive, so the expression is \(\ge 0\) outside the roots.
  4. Hence \(x\le\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x\ge 3\).
  5. Check \(x=0\): \(3\ge 0\) ✓; \(x=1\): \(2-7+3=-2<0\), so 1 is excluded ✓.
Common mistake

Forgetting to divide both roots by the leading coefficient when the quadratic does not factorise with a unit leading coefficient — here the roots are \(\frac{1}{2}\) and \(3\), not \(1\) and \(3\).

Q20★★★☆4 marks

Solve the inequality \(x^{2}-6x+9>0\).

AnswerAll real \(x\) except \(x=3\)
  1. Recognise a perfect square: \(x^{2}-6x+9=(x-3)^{2}\).
  2. A square is never negative, so \((x-3)^{2}\ge 0\) for every real \(x\).
  3. The only value making it equal to 0 is \(x=3\), and the inequality is strict, so \(x=3\) must be excluded.
  4. Therefore the solution is all real numbers except \(3\).
  5. Check \(x=0\): \(9>0\) ✓; \(x=3\): \(0>0\) is false ✓.
Q21★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 2 Q7 ↗

(a) Solve the inequality \(x^{2}-6x+5<0\).

(b) Hence find the number of integers satisfying the inequality.

Answer(a) \(1
  1. (a) Factorise: \(x^{2}-6x+5=(x-1)(x-5)\).
  2. The roots are 1 and 5 and the parabola opens upwards, so the expression is negative between the roots: \(1
  3. (b) The integers strictly between 1 and 5 are \(2,3,4\).
  4. There are 3 such integers.
  5. Check \(x=1\): \(0<0\) is false, so the endpoints are correctly excluded ✓.
Common mistake

Including 1 and 5 in the count because they are the roots. A strict inequality excludes both endpoints.

Type 4

Linear Inequalities in Two Unknowns and Half-planes · 7 questions

Test a point; describe the shaded region

Core idea: The equation \(ax+by=c\) is a straight line; the inequality \(ax+by\le c\) is the whole half-plane on one side of it, boundary included. To decide which side, substitute a convenient test point (the origin is best when the line misses it). If the test point satisfies the inequality, shade that side.

\(ax+by\(ax+by\le c\): solid line, boundary includedTest \((0,0)\): does \(0\le c\) hold?
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

Does the point \((3,1)\) satisfy the inequality \(2x+3y\le 10\)?

AnswerYes
  1. Substitute \(x=3\), \(y=1\): left side \(=2(3)+3(1)=6+3=9\).
  2. Compare with 10: \(9\le 10\) is true.
  3. So \((3,1)\) lies in the region, on the boundary side of the line.
Q23★☆☆☆2 marks

Does the point \((1,-2)\) satisfy the inequality \(y>2x-3\)?

AnswerNo
  1. Substitute: left side \(y=-2\), right side \(2(1)-3=-1\).
  2. Is \(-2>-1\)? No, \(-2\) is smaller.
  3. So \((1,-2)\) does not satisfy the inequality; the point is below the line.
Q24★★☆☆3 marks

Consider the region defined by \(3x+2y<12\). Determine, with reasons, whether each of the points \((0,0)\) and \((5,0)\) lies inside the region.

Answer\((0,0)\): yes; \((5,0)\): no
  1. Substitute \((0,0)\): \(3(0)+2(0)=0<12\) is true, so the origin is inside the region.
  2. Substitute \((5,0)\): \(3(5)+2(0)=15\) and \(15<12\) is false, so \((5,0)\) is outside.
  3. Interpretation: the boundary line is \(3x+2y=12\), cutting the axes at \((4,0)\) and \((0,6)\). The region is the triangle below it containing the origin; \((5,0)\) is beyond the \(x\)-intercept.
Common mistake

Testing only one point and assuming the whole line is inside. Each point must be tested separately.

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

The line \(x+2y=6\) divides the plane into two half-planes.

(a) Write down the inequality describing the half-plane that contains the origin.

(b) Write down the inequality describing the half-plane that contains \((4,4)\).

Answer(a) \(x+2y\le 6\)　(b) \(x+2y\ge 6\)
  1. (a) Test \((0,0)\): \(0+0=0<6\), so the origin's half-plane is \(x+2y<6\), i.e. \(x+2y\le 6\) if the boundary is included.
  2. (b) Test \((4,4)\): \(4+2(4)=12>6\), so this half-plane is \(x+2y>6\), i.e. \(x+2y\ge 6\).
  3. The two half-planes together make up the whole plane, and the line itself belongs to both \(\le\) and \(\ge\) descriptions.
Common mistake

Choosing the sign from the slope of the line. The sign is decided only by a test point.

Q26★★☆☆3 marks

A region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\) and \(4x+5y\le 20\). Find the coordinates of its three vertices.

Answer\((0,0)\), \((5,0)\), \((0,4)\)
  1. The line \(4x+5y=20\) meets the \(x\)-axis where \(y=0\): \(4x=20\), so \(x=5\); vertex \((5,0)\).
  2. It meets the \(y\)-axis where \(x=0\): \(5y=20\), so \(y=4\); vertex \((0,4)\).
  3. The axes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) meet at the origin \((0,0)\), the third vertex.
  4. Check \((0,4)\): \(0+20=20\le 20\) ✓, so the boundary is included.
Q27★★★☆4 marks

The region \(R\) is defined by \(1\le x\le 4\), \(y\ge 2\) and \(x+y\le 7\).

(a) Find the coordinates of the four vertices of \(R\).

(b) Find the number of points with integer coordinates that lie in \(R\).

Answer(a) \((1,2),(1,6),(4,3),(4,2)\)　(b) 14 points
  1. (a) Combine the boundary lines pairwise. \(x=1\) with \(y=2\) gives \((1,2)\); \(x=1\) with \(x+y=7\) gives \((1,6)\); \(x=4\) with \(x+y=7\) gives \((4,3)\); \(x=4\) with \(y=2\) gives \((4,2)\).
  2. (b) For each integer \(x\) from 1 to 4, \(y\) runs from 2 up to \(7-x\).
  3. \(x=1\): \(y=2,3,4,5,6\) → 5 points; \(x=2\): \(y=2,3,4,5\) → 4 points.
  4. \(x=3\): \(y=2,3,4\) → 3 points; \(x=4\): \(y=2,3\) → 2 points.
  5. Total \(=5+4+3+2=14\) points.
  6. Check the vertex \((1,6)\): \(1+6=7\le 7\) ✓, and \((4,3)\): \(4+3=7\le 7\) ✓.
Q28★★★☆4 marks

A region is bounded by \(y\ge 0\), \(y\le 2x\) and \(x+y\le 6\).

(a) Find the coordinates of its three vertices.

(b) Find the area of the region.

Answer(a) \((0,0),(6,0),(2,4)\)　(b) 12 square units
  1. \(y=0\) with \(y=2x\): \(2x=0\), so \((0,0)\).
  2. \(y=0\) with \(x+y=6\): \(x=6\), so \((6,0)\).
  3. \(y=2x\) with \(x+y=6\): \(x+2x=6\), so \(x=2\) and \(y=4\); vertex \((2,4)\).
  4. (b) The base on the \(x\)-axis is from \((0,0)\) to \((6,0)\), length 6; the height is the \(y\)-coordinate of \((2,4)\), namely 4.
  5. Area \(=\frac{1}{2}\times 6\times 4=12\) square units.
  6. Check that \((2,4)\) satisfies all three: \(4\ge 0\) ✓, \(4\le 4\) ✓, \(2+4=6\le 6\) ✓.
Common mistake

Treating \((2,4)\) as if the base were the slanted side \(y=2x\); the easiest base is the horizontal side on the \(x\)-axis.

Type 5

Systems of Inequalities and the Feasible Region · 7 questions

Intersecting regions; finding vertices

Core idea: A system of inequalities describes the intersection of several half-planes — the feasible region. Its corners (vertices) are found by solving the boundary lines in pairs. Only keep the intersection points that satisfy all the inequalities of the system.

Vertices = intersections of boundary linesKeep only points satisfying every inequalityInclude \(x\ge 0,\ y\ge 0\) when the quantities are counts
Q29★☆☆☆2 marks

Does the point \((2,3)\) satisfy both \(x+y\le 6\) and \(y\le 2x\)?

AnswerYes
  1. First inequality: \(2+3=5\le 6\) ✓.
  2. Second inequality: \(y=3\) and \(2x=4\), so \(3\le 4\) ✓.
  3. Both hold, so \((2,3)\) lies in the feasible region.
Q30★☆☆☆2 marks

Does the point \((4,1)\) satisfy the system \(x+y\le 4\), \(y\ge 2x-1\)?

AnswerNo
  1. First inequality: \(4+1=5\), and \(5\le 4\) is false.
  2. So the point fails the system already; there is no need to test the second inequality.
  3. (Indeed \(1\ge 2(4)-1=7\) is also false.)
Common mistake

Ignoring the equality case: a point exactly on a boundary is feasible for \(\le\), but \((4,1)\) is strictly outside.

Q31★★☆☆3 marks

Find the coordinates of the vertices of the feasible region defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(x+y\le 6\) and \(y\le x\).

Answer\((0,0)\), \((6,0)\), \((3,3)\)
  1. \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) meet at \((0,0)\), which satisfies both other inequalities.
  2. \(y=0\) with \(x+y=6\) gives \((6,0)\); check \(0\le 6\) ✓.
  3. \(y=x\) with \(x+y=6\) gives \(2x=6\), so \(x=3,y=3\); vertex \((3,3)\).
  4. \(y=x\) with \(x=0\) gives \((0,0)\), already listed.
  5. Check \((3,3)\): \(3+3=6\le 6\) ✓ and \(3\le 3\) ✓.
Common mistake

Including the intersection of \(y=x\) with \(y=0\), which is \((0,0)\) — the same point, not a new vertex.

Q32★★☆☆3 marks

Find the coordinates of the vertices of the region defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(2x+y\le 10\) and \(x+y\le 6\).

Answer\((0,0)\), \((5,0)\), \((4,2)\), \((0,6)\)
  1. On the \(x\)-axis (\(y=0\)): \(2x\le 10\) gives \(x\le 5\) and \(x\le 6\) gives \(x\le 6\); the tighter bound is \(x=5\), so \((5,0)\).
  2. On the \(y\)-axis (\(x=0\)): \(y\le 10\) and \(y\le 6\); the tighter bound is \(y=6\), so \((0,6)\).
  3. The two slant lines meet where \(2x+y=10\) and \(x+y=6\). Subtracting: \(x=4\), then \(y=2\). Vertex \((4,2)\).
  4. Add the origin \((0,0)\) from the two axes.
  5. Check \((4,2)\) in both: \(8+2=10\le 10\) ✓ and \(4+2=6\le 6\) ✓.
Q33★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

Find the coordinates of the vertices of the region defined by \(y\ge 1\), \(x+y\le 8\) and \(y\le x+2\).

Answer\((-1,1)\), \((7,1)\), \((3,5)\)
  1. \(y=1\) with \(x+y=8\): \(x=7\), vertex \((7,1)\).
  2. \(y=1\) with \(y=x+2\): \(1=x+2\), so \(x=-1\), vertex \((-1,1)\).
  3. \(x+y=8\) with \(y=x+2\): \(x+(x+2)=8\), so \(x=3\) and \(y=5\), vertex \((3,5)\).
  4. Check \((3,5)\): \(5\ge 1\) ✓, \(3+5=8\le 8\) ✓, \(5\le 5\) ✓.
Q34★★★☆4 marks

A feasible region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(x+y\le 7\) and \(y\le 2x+1\).

(a) Find the coordinates of its four vertices.

(b) Determine whether \((3,3)\) lies inside the region.

Answer(a) \((0,0),(7,0),(2,5),(0,1)\)　(b) Yes
  1. (a) \(x=0\): the bounds are \(y\le 7\) and \(y\le 1\), so the vertices on the \(y\)-axis are \((0,0)\) and \((0,1)\).
  2. \(y=0\): \(x\le 7\) and \(0\le 2x+1\) (always true for \(x\ge 0\)), giving \((7,0)\).
  3. The slant lines meet where \(x+y=7\) and \(y=2x+1\): \(x+2x+1=7\), so \(x=2\), \(y=5\). Vertex \((2,5)\).
  4. (b) Test \((3,3)\): \(3+3=6\le 7\) ✓ and \(3\le 2(3)+1=7\) ✓, so the point is inside the region.
  5. Check \((2,5)\): \(2+5=7\) ✓ and \(5=2(2)+1\) ✓.
Common mistake

Listing \((0,7)\) as a vertex. The line \(y\le 2x+1\) cuts the \(y\)-axis at \((0,1)\), so \((0,7)\) is not feasible.

Q35★★★☆4 marks

A region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(3x+2y\le 18\) and \(x+y\le 8\).

(a) Find the coordinates of its four vertices.

(b) A student claims that \((4,4)\) lies in the region. Do you agree?

Answer(a) \((0,0),(6,0),(2,6),(0,8)\)　(b) No — \(3(4)+2(4)=20>18\)
  1. (a) On \(y=0\): \(3x\le 18\) gives \(x\le 6\) and the other bound gives \(x\le 8\); vertex \((6,0)\).
  2. On \(x=0\): \(2y\le 18\) gives \(y\le 9\) and \(y\le 8\); vertex \((0,8)\).
  3. The slant lines meet where \(3x+2y=18\) and \(y=8-x\): \(3x+16-2x=18\), so \(x=2\) and \(y=6\). Vertex \((2,6)\).
  4. Together with \((0,0)\) the vertices are \((0,0),(6,0),(2,6),(0,8)\).
  5. (b) At \((4,4)\): \(3(4)+2(4)=12+8=20\) and \(20\le 18\) is false, so \((4,4)\) is outside the region. The claim is wrong.
Common mistake

Testing only \(x+y\le 8\) for the point \((4,4)\) (which passes) and forgetting to test the other inequality.

Type 6

Linear Programming: Optimising at the Vertices · 7 questions

Corner test for max / min of an objective

Core idea: A linear objective \(P=ax+by\) over a polygonal feasible region always takes its greatest and least values at a vertex. So the method is short: find every vertex, substitute each one into \(P\), and compare. Never test only one vertex — the answer must be justified by a complete table.

\(P=ax+by\)Corner test: evaluate \(P\) at every vertexState the maximum/minimum and where it occurs
Q36★☆☆☆2 marks

The feasible region has vertices \((0,0)\), \((4,0)\) and \((0,3)\). Find the maximum value of \(P=3x+2y\).

AnswerMaximum \(P=12\) at \((4,0)\)
  1. At \((0,0)\): \(P=3(0)+2(0)=0\).
  2. At \((4,0)\): \(P=3(4)+2(0)=12\).
  3. At \((0,3)\): \(P=3(0)+2(3)=6\).
  4. The largest value is 12, occurring at \((4,0)\).
Q37★☆☆☆2 marks

The feasible region has vertices \((1,2)\), \((3,1)\) and \((4,4)\). Find the minimum value of \(C=5x+4y\).

AnswerMinimum \(C=13\) at \((1,2)\)
  1. At \((1,2)\): \(C=5(1)+4(2)=13\).
  2. At \((3,1)\): \(C=15+4=19\).
  3. At \((4,4)\): \(C=20+16=36\).
  4. The smallest value is 13, at \((1,2)\).
Q38★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2012 Paper 2 Q36 ↗

A feasible region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\) and \(x+y\le 6\). Find the maximum value of \(P=4x+5y\).

AnswerMaximum \(P=30\) at \((0,6)\)
  1. The vertices are \((0,0)\), \((6,0)\) and \((0,6)\) (the axes meet the line \(x+y=6\), which cuts them at 6).
  2. Corner test: \((0,0)\to 0\); \((6,0)\to 24\); \((0,6)\to 30\).
  3. The maximum is 30 at \((0,6)\).
  4. Check with an interior point \((3,3)\): \(P=12+15=27<30\), so the maximum really is on the boundary. ✓
Common mistake

Assuming the maximum must be at \((6,0)\) because \(x\) has the larger coefficient 4. Here \(y\)'s coefficient 5 is larger, so the \(y\)-vertex wins.

Q39★★☆☆3 marks

The feasible region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(2x+y\le 10\) and \(x+y\le 6\). Find the maximum value of \(P=5x+3y\).

AnswerMaximum \(P=26\) at \((4,2)\)
  1. Vertices: \((0,0)\), \((5,0)\) from \(2x=10\), \((0,6)\) from \(y=6\), and the intersection of the two slant lines.
  2. Solving \(2x+y=10\) and \(x+y=6\): subtract to get \(x=4\), then \(y=2\), giving \((4,2)\).
  3. Corner test: \((0,0)\to 0\); \((5,0)\to 25\); \((4,2)\to 20+6=26\); \((0,6)\to 18\).
  4. The maximum is 26 at \((4,2)\).
Q40★★☆☆3 marks

The feasible region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(x+2y\le 12\) and \(2x+y\le 12\). Find the maximum value of \(P=3x+5y\).

AnswerMaximum \(P=32\) at \((4,4)\)
  1. Vertices on the axes: \((6,0)\) from \(2x\le 12\) and \((0,6)\) from \(2y\le 12\), together with \((0,0)\).
  2. Intersection: \(x+2y=12\) and \(2x+y=12\). Subtract the first from twice the second: \(3x=12\), so \(x=4\) and \(y=4\). Vertex \((4,4)\).
  3. Corner test: \((0,0)\to 0\); \((6,0)\to 18\); \((4,4)\to 12+20=32\); \((0,6)\to 30\).
  4. The maximum is 32 at \((4,4)\).

Using \((6,0)\) as the \(x\)-intercept of both lines. \(x+2y=12\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at 12, but the bound \(2x+y\le 12\) cuts it down to 6.

Q41★★★☆4 marks

The feasible region is the quadrilateral with vertices \((0,2)\), \((3,0)\), \((6,4)\) and \((2,6)\).

(a) Find the maximum value of \(P=4x+3y\).

(b) Find the minimum value of \(C=2x+5y\).

Answer(a) 36 at \((6,4)\)　(b) 6 at \((3,0)\)
  1. (a) \(P\) at \((0,2)=6\); at \((3,0)=12\); at \((6,4)=24+12=36\); at \((2,6)=8+18=26\). Maximum 36 at \((6,4)\).
  2. (b) \(C\) at \((0,2)=10\); at \((3,0)=6\); at \((6,4)=12+20=32\); at \((2,6)=4+30=34\). Minimum 6 at \((3,0)\).
  3. Present the results as a table with one row per vertex so no vertex is skipped.
  4. Check (a) with the midpoint of the side joining \((6,4)\) and \((2,6)\), i.e. \((4,5)\): \(P=16+15=31<36\), consistent with the maximum being at a vertex. ✓
Q42★★★☆4 marks

A feasible region is defined by \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), \(x+y\le 7\) and \(y\le 2x+1\).

(a) Show that its vertices are \((0,0)\), \((7,0)\), \((2,5)\) and \((0,1)\).

(b) Hence find the maximum value of \(P=2x+3y\).

Answer(a) shown　(b) Maximum \(P=19\) at \((2,5)\)

Exam-level Word Problems: Production, Budget, Transport · 8 questions

Model, solve, interpret

Core idea: Every linear-programming word problem follows the same four steps: (1) define the variables and their units, (2) translate each limit into an inequality, (3) find the vertices of the feasible region, (4) run the corner test on the profit or cost function. Since the variables are counts, they must be non-negative integers.

Limit: resource used \(\le\) resource availableRequirement: output \(\ge\) targetProfit \(=ax+by\); test every vertex
Q43★☆☆☆3 marks

Ken buys \(x\) apples and \(y\) oranges. Each apple costs $3 and each orange costs $4. He spends at most $48 and buys at least 5 oranges. Write down a system of inequalities that describes the possible values of \(x\) and \(y\).

Answer\(3x+4y\le 48\), \(y\ge 5\), \(x,y\) are non-negative integers
  1. Let \(x\) = number of apples and \(y\) = number of oranges (both non-negative integers).
  2. Cost condition: \(3x+4y\le 48\).
  3. Orange condition: \(y\ge 5\).
  4. Apple condition: \(x\ge 0\), and of course \(y\ge 0\) is already implied by \(y\ge 5\).
  5. Check the extreme case \(x=0,y=5\): cost \(=\) $20 \(\le 48\) ✓, so a feasible purchase exists.
Q44★☆☆☆3 marks

A hotel lift can carry at most 15 people and has a maximum load of 800 kg. An adult weighs 70 kg and a child weighs 40 kg. Let \(x\) and \(y\) be the numbers of adults and children in the lift. Write down the constraints on \(x\) and \(y\).

Answer\(x+y\le 15\), \(70x+40y\le 800\), \(x,y\ge 0\) integers
  1. People constraint: \(x+y\le 15\).
  2. Weight constraint: \(70x+40y\le 800\), which simplifies to \(7x+4y\le 80\).
  3. Numbers of people cannot be negative or fractional, so \(x,y\ge 0\) and both are integers.
  4. Check \(x=8,y=5\): \(13\) people ✓ and \(560+200=760\le 800\) ✓, so this loading is allowed.
Q45★★☆☆4 marks

A small factory makes \(x\) tables and \(y\) chairs each day. Each table needs 3 units of wood and each chair needs 2 units; only 60 units of wood are available per day. The factory must make at least 4 tables and at least 6 chairs daily.

(a) Write down the system of inequalities.

(b) The manager plans to make 10 tables and 10 chairs. Is this plan feasible? Explain.

Answer(a) \(3x+2y\le 60\), \(x\ge 4\), \(y\ge 6\), \(x,y\) integers　(b) Yes
  1. (a) Wood: \(3x+2y\le 60\). Minimum output: \(x\ge 4\) and \(y\ge 6\). Also \(x,y\) are integers.
  2. (b) Substitute \(x=10,y=10\) into the wood constraint: \(3(10)+2(10)=30+20=50\).
  3. \(50\le 60\) ✓, and \(10\ge 4\), \(10\ge 6\) ✓.
  4. So the plan uses 50 of the 60 units and is feasible — 10 units of wood are left over.
Q46★★☆☆4 marks

A delivery company uses \(x\) vans and \(y\) lorries. A van carries 8 boxes and a lorry carries 20 boxes. The company has at most 6 vehicles available and must deliver at least 80 boxes.

(a) Write down the constraints.

(b) Find the number of possible combinations of vans and lorries.

Answer(a) \(x+y\le 6\), \(8x+20y\ge 80\)　(b) 7 combinations
  1. (a) Vehicles: \(x+y\le 6\). Boxes: \(8x+20y\ge 80\), i.e. \(2x+5y\ge 20\). Both \(x,y\) are non-negative integers.
  2. (b) Try each possible \(y\): \(y=0,1,2\) need \(2x\ge 20,15,10\), i.e. \(x\ge 10,7.5,5\), all bigger than the \(x\le 6-y\) allowed — none work.
  3. \(y=3\): \(2x\ge 5\) and \(x\le 3\) → \(x=3\) only.
  4. \(y=4\): \(x\le 2\) and \(2x\ge 0\) → \(x=0,1,2\); \(y=5\): \(x=0,1\); \(y=6\): \(x=0\).
  5. Counting: \(1+3+2+1=7\) combinations.
  6. Check \((4,4)\) is rejected: \(8(4)+20(4)=112\ge 80\) ✓ but \(4+4=8>6\) ✗.
Common mistake

Counting only the boundary solutions. Every integer point inside the region counts, so the answer is 7, not 3.

Q47★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2025 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

A shop buys \(x\) tables at $400 each and \(y\) chairs at $300 each. The budget is $4800 and the storeroom holds at most 14 items. The profit is $90 per table and $80 per chair.

(a) Write down the constraints.

(b) Find the number of tables and chairs that give the maximum profit.

Answer(a) \(4x+3y\le 48\), \(x+y\le 14\)　(b) 6 tables and 8 chairs, profit $1180
  1. (a) Budget: \(400x+300y\le 4800\), i.e. \(4x+3y\le 48\). Space: \(x+y\le 14\). Integers, non-negative.
  2. Vertices: \((0,0)\); from \(4x\le 48\), \((12,0)\); from \(y\le 14\), \((0,14)\).
  3. Intersection of \(4x+3y=48\) and \(x+y=14\): substituting \(y=14-x\) gives \(4x+42-3x=48\), so \(x=6\), \(y=8\).
  4. (b) Profit \(P=90x+80y\): \((0,0)\to 0\); \((12,0)\to 1080\); \((6,8)\to 540+640=1180\); \((0,14)\to 1120\).
  5. The maximum profit is $1180, from 6 tables and 8 chairs.
  6. Check the budget: \(400(6)+300(8)=2400+2400=4800\) ✓ and \(6+8=14\) ✓.
Common mistake

Forgetting to convert the budget to \(4x+3y\le 48\). Mixing dollars with hundreds in the same inequality makes the numbers unmanageable.

Q48★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

A factory produces \(x\) units of product A and \(y\) units of product B each week. Each unit of A needs 2 machine-hours and 4 labour-hours; each unit of B needs 3 machine-hours and 2 labour-hours. There are 36 machine-hours and 40 labour-hours available per week. The profit is $300 per unit of A and $250 per unit of B.

(a) Write down the constraints.

(b) Find the vertices of the feasible region.

(c) Find the maximum weekly profit.

Answer(a) \(2x+3y\le 36\), \(2x+y\le 20\)　(b) \((0,0),(10,0),(6,8),(0,12)\)　(c) $3800
  1. (a) Machine-hours: \(2x+3y\le 36\). Labour-hours: \(4x+2y\le 40\), i.e. \(2x+y\le 20\). Also \(x,y\) are non-negative integers.
  2. (b) On \(y=0\): \(2x\le 20\) gives \((10,0)\). On \(x=0\): \(3y\le 36\) gives \((0,12)\).
  3. Solve \(2x+3y=36\) and \(2x+y=20\): subtracting gives \(2y=16\), so \(y=8\) and \(x=6\). Vertex \((6,8)\).
  4. Together with \((0,0)\) the vertices are \((0,0),(10,0),(6,8),(0,12)\).
  5. (c) Profit \(P=300x+250y\): \((0,0)\to 0\); \((10,0)\to 3000\); \((6,8)\to 1800+2000=3800\); \((0,12)\to 3000\).
  6. The maximum profit is $3800 per week, from 6 units of A and 8 units of B.
  7. Check the hours for \((6,8)\): machine \(12+24=36\) ✓, labour \(24+16=40\) ✓ — both fully used.
Q49★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 1 Q16 ↗

A removal company hires \(x\) five-tonne trucks and \(y\) two-tonne trucks to move 30 tonnes of goods. It can hire at most 9 trucks in total. A five-tonne truck costs $1500 and a two-tonne truck costs $500 per day.

(a) Write down the constraints.

(b) Find the vertices of the feasible region.

(c) Find the minimum daily cost, and the number of each type of truck hired.

Answer(a) \(x+y\le 9\), \(5x+2y\ge 30\)　(b) \((6,0),(9,0),(4,5)\)　(c) $8500, with 4 five-tonne and 5 two-tonne trucks
  1. (a) Vehicles: \(x+y\le 9\). Capacity: \(5x+2y\ge 30\). Also \(x,y\) are non-negative integers.
  2. (b) With \(y=0\): \(5x\ge 30\) gives \(x\ge 6\), and \(x\le 9\), so the edge runs from \((6,0)\) to \((9,0)\).
  3. With \(x=0\): \(2y\ge 30\) needs \(y\ge 15\), which exceeds 9 — impossible, so the \(y\)-axis meets no vertex.

Q50★★★★8 marksPast paper · 2014 Paper 1 Q18 ↗

A canteen prepares \(x\) set meals of type A and \(y\) set meals of type B each day. Each set A uses 3 kg of rice and 1 kg of beans; each set B uses 2 kg of rice and 2 kg of beans. The canteen has 60 kg of rice and 40 kg of beans available daily. The profit is $50 per set A and $40 per set B.

(a) Write down the system of inequalities.

(b) Find the coordinates of the vertices of the feasible region.

(c) The manager claims that the daily profit can exceed $1200. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

Answer(a) \(3x+2y\le 60\), \(x+2y\le 40\), \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\)　(b) \((0,0),(20,0),(10,15),(0,20)\)　(c) No: the maximum profit is $1100
  1. (a) Rice: \(3x+2y\le 60\). Beans: \(x+2y\le 40\). Counts: \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), both integers.
  2. (b) On \(y=0\): \(3x\le 60\) gives \(x\le 20\) and \(x\le 40\) → \((20,0)\). On \(x=0\): \(2y\le 60\) gives \(y\le 30\) and \(2y\le 40\) gives \(y\le 20\) → \((0,20)\).
  3. Solve \(3x+2y=60\) and \(x+2y=40\): subtract, \(2x=20\), so \(x=10\), and then \(y=15\). Vertex \((10,15)\).
  4. Together with \((0,0)\): vertices \((0,0),(20,0),(10,15),(0,20)\).
  5. (c) Profit \(P=50x+40y\). Corner test: \((0,0)\to 0\); \((20,0)\to 1000\); \((10,15)\to 500+600=1100\); \((0,20)\to 800\).
  6. The maximum profit is $1100, at 10 sets of A and 15 sets of B.
  7. Since $1100 < $1200, the manager's claim is not agreed. Check the resources at \((10,15)\): rice \(30+30=60\) ✓, beans \(10+30=40\) ✓ — both fully used, so nothing more can be produced.
Common mistake

Agreeing on the strength of one corner such as \((20,0)\), where the profit is only $1000. The claim can only be judged after every vertex has been tested.
