2019 Paper 2 — Question 5

Change of Subject · Formulas and Sequences

QQuestion

If  \( h = 3 - \dfrac{5}{k+4} \),   then   \( k = \)
Change of Subject Algebraic Manipulation Common Denominator
A.\(\dfrac{4h-7}{3-h}\)
B.\(\dfrac{4h-17}{3-h}\)
C.\(\dfrac{4h-7}{3+h}\)
D.\(\dfrac{4h-17}{3+h}\)

1Guided Solution — Step by Step

1 Isolate the term that contains \(k\) ▶

We want \(k\) on its own. First move the term carrying \(k\) to one side.

\[ h = 3 - \frac{5}{k+4} \]

Add \(\dfrac{5}{k+4}\) to both sides and subtract \(h\) from both sides:

\[ \frac{5}{k+4} = 3 - h \]
Rearranging rule: whatever operation you apply to one side, apply to the other. Here we move \(-\frac{5}{k+4}\) across the equals sign (it becomes \(+\frac{5}{k+4}\)) and move \(h\) across (it becomes \(3-h\)).
2 Cross-multiply to remove the fraction ▶

Now \(k\) sits in the denominator. Cross-multiply:

\[ \frac{5}{k+4} = 3-h \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{5}{3-h} = k+4 \]
Cross-multiplication: \(\dfrac{a}{b}=c \;\Rightarrow\; a = b\,c\). Rearranged for the denominator: \(b = \dfrac{a}{c}\).
Here \(a=5,\; b=k+4,\; c=3-h\). Condition: \(3-h \neq 0\) and \(k+4 \neq 0\).
3 Make \(k\) the subject ▶

Subtract \(4\) from both sides:

\[ k = \frac{5}{3-h} - 4 \]

This is already a valid answer, but the options are single fractions — so we combine next.

4 Combine over a common denominator ▶

Write \(4\) as \(\dfrac{4(3-h)}{3-h}\) so both terms share the denominator \((3-h)\):

\[ k = \frac{5}{3-h} - \frac{4(3-h)}{3-h} = \frac{5 - 4(3-h)}{3-h} \]
Common denominator: a whole number \(n\) can be written as \(\dfrac{n\cdot D}{D}\).
Here \(4 = \dfrac{4(3-h)}{3-h}\), so we can subtract the numerators directly.
5 Expand and simplify the numerator ▶

Watch the sign carefully — the minus sign applies to the whole bracket:

\[ \begin{aligned} 5 - 4(3-h) &= 5 - 12 + 4h \\ &= 4h - 7 \end{aligned} \]

Therefore:

\[ \boxed{\,k = \frac{4h-7}{3-h}\,} \]
Answer: Option A
Common mistake: writing \(-4(3-h) = -12 - 4h\) — forgetting that \(-4 \times (-h) = +4h\). Be careful with the double negative!

Option A
\[ k = \frac{4h-7}{3-h} \]
Valid for \(h \neq 3\) (and \(k \neq -4\)).
