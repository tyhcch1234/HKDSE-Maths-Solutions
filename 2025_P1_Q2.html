Simplify the algebraic fraction

Topic 4 · Polynomials — addition & subtraction of algebraic fractions

Algebraic Fractions LCM of Denominators Subtraction of Polynomials 3 marks

Question

Simplify $$ \frac{1}{\,3d-4\,} \;-\; \frac{2}{\,6d+5\,} $$ 3 marks

Key idea: to subtract two fractions, first write them over a common denominator, then combine the numerators — and remember the minus sign applies to the whole second numerator.

Explore it — is the denominator zero?

Before we simplify, note that the original expression is undefined when a denominator equals 0, i.e. when $3d-4=0$ or $6d+5=0$. Drag the slider to test values of $d$.

Notice: wherever it is defined, the value of the simplified expression equals the value of the original one. Equivalence is only broken at the excluded values of $d$, where the original expression is undefined.

Step-by-step solution

1
Read the question — what are we adding/subtracting?

We must simplify the difference of two algebraic fractions. A fraction can only be combined with another fraction when they share the same denominator.

Recap: for numbers, $\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{3-2}{6}$. The denominators $2$ and $3$ are replaced by their LCM, $6$. We use exactly the same idea with algebra.
2
Find the common denominator

The denominators are $3d-4$ and $6d+5$. These are two different linear expressions and neither divides the other, so the lowest common multiple is simply their product:

$$ \text{LCD} = (3d-4)(6d+5) $$
Hint: multiply each fraction's numerator and denominator by the other denominator — this does not change the value of the fraction.
3
Rewrite each fraction with the common denominator

Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by the missing factor:

$$ \frac{1}{3d-4}\cdot\frac{(6d+5)}{(6d+5)} \;-\; \frac{2}{6d+5}\cdot\frac{(3d-4)}{(3d-4)} $$ $$ =\; \frac{\,6d+5\,}{\,(3d-4)(6d+5)\,} \;-\; \frac{\,2(3d-4)\,}{\,(3d-4)(6d+5)\,} $$
Careful: bracket the second numerator as $2(3d-4)$. Without the bracket you will forget to multiply the whole numerator by $2$.
4
Combine the numerators over one denominator

Now the denominators match, so subtract the numerators — keep the minus sign in front of the whole second numerator:

$$ =\; \frac{\,(6d+5)\;-\;2(3d-4)\,}{\,(3d-4)(6d+5)\,} $$
Hint: the minus sign belongs to the entire group $2(3d-4)$, so it will flip both terms inside that bracket when we expand.
5
Expand the numerator and collect like terms
$$ 2(3d-4) = 6d-8 $$
$$ (6d+5)-(6d-8) = 6d+5-6d+8 $$
$$ = 13 $$

The $6d$ and $-6d$ cancel, leaving the constant $5+8=13$.

Common mistake: writing $(6d+5)-6d-8 = 5-8=-3$. You must distribute the negative sign into the bracket: $-2(3d-4) = -6d+8$.
6
State the final answer (and check for cancellation)
$$ =\; \frac{13}{\,(3d-4)(6d+5)\,} $$

The numerator $13$ is prime, so it shares no common factor with either bracket — nothing cancels. This is the answer required by the question.

Answer: $\;\dfrac{13}{(3d-4)(6d+5)}$   (valid for $d\neq\frac{4}{3}$ and $d\neq-\frac{5}{6}$)

How the 3 marks are awarded

Mark What the marker looks for
1M Correct use of the common denominator: writing $\dfrac{(6d+5)-2(3d-4)}{(3d-4)(6d+5)}$ (the numerator must be bracketed as one group).
1M Correct expansion and simplification of the numerator to $6d+5-6d+8$.
1A Answer accuracy: the fully simplified fraction $\dfrac{13}{(3d-4)(6d+5)}$.

Tip: the final answer may also be left with the denominator expanded as $18d^2-9d-20$. Either form is acceptable as long as it is fully simplified.
