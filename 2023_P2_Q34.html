HKDSE 2023 · Paper 2 · Q34

If $k$ is a real number, then the real part of $\displaystyle \frac{i}{k-i}+\frac{2}{k+i}$ is

A. $\dfrac{k+2}{k^{2}-1}$
B. $\dfrac{2k-1}{k^{2}+1}$
C. $\dfrac{k-2}{k^{2}+1}$
D. $\dfrac{k+2}{k^{2}+1}$

Step-by-step solution

Step 1Read the expression and the two denominators
$$E=\frac{i}{k-i}+\frac{2}{k+i},\qquad k\in\mathbb{R}$$
Hint: $k-i$ and $k+i$ are conjugates.
Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by the conjugate of its own denominator,
so every denominator becomes real.
Difference of Two Squares \((k-i)(k+i)=k^{2}-i^{2}=k^{2}+1\) Multiplying by the conjugate makes the denominator real, because \(i^{2}=-1\).
Step 2Rationalise each denominator
$$\frac{i}{k-i}=\frac{i(k+i)}{(k-i)(k+i)},\qquad \frac{2}{k+i}=\frac{2(k-i)}{(k-i)(k+i)}$$
Hint: $(k-i)(k+i)=k^{2}-i^{2}=k^{2}+1$,
a positive real number.
Step 3Combine over the common real denominator $k^{2}+1$
$$E=\frac{i(k+i)+2(k-i)}{(k-i)(k+i)}=\frac{i(k+i)+2(k-i)}{k^{2}+1}$$
Hint: Once the denominators agree,
only the numerators need to be added.
Powers of the Imaginary Unit \(i^{2}=-1\) Replacing \(i^{2}\) by \(-1\) separates the numerator into a real part and a multiple of \(i\).
Step 4Expand the numerator, using $i^{2}=-1$
$$i(k+i)=ki+i^{2}=ki-1,\qquad 2(k-i)=2k-2i$$ $$i(k+i)+2(k-i)=(2k-1)+(k-2)i$$
Hint: Group the terms: real numbers together,
and multiples of $i$ together.
Real and Imaginary Parts \(\dfrac{a+bi}{c}=\dfrac{a}{c}+\dfrac{b}{c}i\) With a real denominator \(k^{2}+1\), the real part is simply the coefficient of the non-\(i\) term.
Step 5Split $E$ into real part + imaginary part
$$E=\frac{(2k-1)+(k-2)i}{k^{2}+1} =\underbrace{\frac{2k-1}{k^{2}+1}}_{\text{real part}} +\underbrace{\frac{k-2}{k^{2}+1}}_{\text{imaginary part}}i$$
Hint: Since $k$ is real, $k^{2}+1\neq 0$,
so the split into real and imaginary parts is valid.
Step 6State the real part and pick the option
$$\operatorname{Re}(E)=\frac{2k-1}{k^{2}+1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\text{B}}$$
Hint: The imaginary denominator $k^{2}+1$ appears in option B;
watch out for traps using $k^{2}-1$ (option A) — that would require a real denominator,
which does not occur here.
Answer: Option B — the real part is $\displaystyle \frac{2k-1}{k^{2}+1}$.

Question. If \(k\) is a real number, find the real part of \(\dfrac{3}{k-i}+\dfrac{i}{k+i}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The denominators are conjugates,
and \((k-i)(k+i)=k^{2}+1\).
Step 2. \(\dfrac{3}{k-i}=\dfrac{3(k+i)}{k^{2}+1}\) and \(\dfrac{i}{k+i}=\dfrac{i(k-i)}{k^{2}+1}\).
Step 3. Numerator: \(3(k+i)+i(k-i)=3k+3i+ki-i^{2}=(3k+1)+(3+k)i\).
Step 4. So the expression is \(\dfrac{(3k+1)+(3+k)i}{k^{2}+1}\), whose real part is \(\dfrac{3k+1}{k^{2}+1}\).
Step 5. Check with \(k=2\): the expression becomes \(\dfrac{6+3i}{5}+\dfrac{2i+1}{5}=\dfrac{7+5i}{5}\), real part \(\dfrac{7}{5}\),
and the formula gives \(\dfrac{7}{5}\) ✓
Answer: \(\dfrac{3k+1}{k^{2}+1}\)
