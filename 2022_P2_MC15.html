Mensuration — Surface areas of a hemisphere & a right circular cylinder
Hemisphere Right Circular Cylinder Curved Surface Area Ratio

Q15 The Question

Multiple Choice · 1 mark
The radius of a solid hemisphere and the base radius of a solid right circular cylinder are equal. If the height of the circular cylinder is equal to its base diameter, then the ratio of the total surface area of the hemisphere to the total surface area of the circular cylinder is
  • A. \(1:2\)
  • B. \(1:3\)
  • C. \(2:3\)
  • D. \(2:5\) ✓
Key idea: "Total surface area" means every exposed face — the hemisphere includes its flat circular base, and the cylinder includes both circular ends.

(a) Set up the variables

STEP 1 · 1M
hemisphere r 3.0 cm cylinder r 3.0 cm cylinder h 6.0 cm h = 2r = diameter ✓
Step 1 1M

Let the common radius be \(r\) cm. Since the cylinder's height equals its base diameter,

\[ r_{\text{hemi}} = r_{\text{cyl}} = r, \qquad h = 2r. \]
Hint: Choosing one variable \(r\) for both solids is the trick — every other length can be written as a multiple of \(r\). Notice \(r\) cancels at the end, so the ratio is a pure number.
Set-up: radius of hemisphere \(= r\), base radius of cylinder \(= r\), height of cylinder \(= 2r\).
0 / 1

(b) Total surface area of the hemisphere

STEP 2 · 1A
curved \(2\pi r^2\) 56.55 cm² base \(\pi r^2\) 28.27 cm² total \(3\pi r^2\) 84.82 cm²
Step 2 1A

A solid hemisphere has two surfaces: the curved dome and the flat circular base.

\[ \text{Curved surface} = 2\pi r^2, \qquad \text{Flat base} = \pi r^2 \] \[ \text{Total S.A. of hemisphere} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2. \]
Hint: A "solid" hemisphere is closed — students often forget the flat circular base and lose the mark. The formula \(\tfrac12(4\pi r^2)\) only gives the curved dome.
Total S.A. of hemisphere \(= 3\pi r^2\)
0 / 1

(c) Total surface area of the cylinder

STEP 3 · 1M
lateral \(2\pi rh\) 113.10 cm² 2 ends \(2\pi r^2\) 56.55 cm² total \(6\pi r^2\) 169.65 cm²
Step 3 1M

The cylinder has a curved lateral surface and two circular ends. With \(h = 2r\):

\[ \text{Lateral surface} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi r (2r) = 4\pi r^2 \] \[ \text{Two ends} = 2\pi r^2 \] \[ \text{Total S.A. of cylinder} = 4\pi r^2 + 2\pi r^2 = 6\pi r^2. \]
Hint: Substitute \(h = 2r\) before adding. A very common slip is to write the lateral surface as \(2\pi r \cdot 2r = 4\pi r\) and drop the extra \(r\). Keep track of the units: surface area must have \(r^2\).
Total S.A. of cylinder \(= 6\pi r^2\)
0 / 1

(d) Form the ratio & simplify

STEP 4 · 1A
\(3\pi r^2 : 6\pi r^2\) 3 : 6 simplified 1 : 2 check \(\tfrac{3}{6} = 0.5\)
Step 4 1A

Now divide the two results:

\[ \frac{\text{S.A. of hemisphere}}{\text{S.A. of cylinder}} = \frac{3\pi r^2}{6\pi r^2} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}. \]

Hence the ratio is

\[ \boxed{1:2} \]
Hint: Both \(\pi\) and \(r^2\) cancel — the answer is a pure number, confirming the ratio does not depend on the size of the solids. Always double-check the order: the question asks hemisphere to cylinder, so the hemisphere's value goes first.
Answer: \(1:2\), i.e. option A.
