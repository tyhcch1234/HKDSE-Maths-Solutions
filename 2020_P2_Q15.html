Question

[2020MC#15]
The angle of a sector is decreased by \(60\%\) but its radius is increased by \(k\%\). If the arc length of the sector remains unchanged, find the value of \(k\).
A. 40
B. 60
C. 67
D. 150 ✓

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Write the arc-length formula

For a sector with radius \(r\) and angle \(\theta^\circ\):

\[ l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta^\circ}{360^\circ} \]

The full circumference \(2\pi r\) is split in the ratio \(\theta : 360\).

STEP 2 · Rearrange to a constant ratio

\[ l = \frac{\pi r \theta}{180} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{r\theta}{l} = \frac{180}{\pi} \]

Since \(\dfrac{180}{\pi}\) is a fixed number, the product \(r\theta\) must be proportional to \(l\) — and \(l\) stays unchanged.

So the condition is simply:\[ r\theta = \text{constant} \]

STEP 3 · Apply the changes

Angle decreased by \(60\%\): new angle \(= (1-60\%)\theta = 0.4\,\theta\).

Radius increased by \(k\%\): new radius \(= \left(1+\dfrac{k}{100}\right)r\).

Arc length unchanged, so the products of old and new must be equal:

\[ r\theta = \left[\left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)r\right]\bigl[(1-60\%)\theta\bigr] \]

STEP 4 · Cancel and solve for \(k\)

Cancel \(r\theta\) on both sides:

\[ 1 = \left(1+\frac{k}{100}\right)(0.4) \]

\[ 1+\frac{k}{100} = 2.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{k}{100} = 1.5 \]

\[ \boxed{k = 150} \]

Check: \(r\) becomes \(2.5r\), \(\theta\) becomes \(0.4\theta\); product \(= r\theta\). ✓

Answer: D · \(k = 150\)
