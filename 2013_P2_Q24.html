HKDSE 2013 Paper 2 · Q24 — Locus (Perpendicular Bisector)

Point A (2, 5) Point B (4, −1) Mid-point M (3, 2) Moving point P Locus: x − 3y + 3 = 0 Segment AB / distances
[2013 MC #24]

The coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$ are $(2,\ 5)$ and $(4,\ -1)$ respectively. Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that $AP = BP$. Find the equation of the locus of $P$.

  • A. $x - 3y + 3 = 0$
  • B. $x - 3y - 7 = 0$
  • C. $x - 3y + 13 = 0$
  • D. $3x + y - 11 = 0$
STEP 1 · INTERPRET THE CONDITION

We are given $AP = BP$, i.e. $P$ is always equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

Recall the standard result: the set of all points equidistant from two fixed points $A$ and $B$ is the perpendicular bisector of the segment $AB$.

So the locus of $P$ is a straight line — and we expect it to be perpendicular to $AB$ and to pass through the mid-point of $AB$.

STEP 2 · FIND THE MID-POINT OF $AB$

Mid-point formula:

$$M = \left(\frac{x_A+x_B}{2},\ \frac{y_A+y_B}{2}\right)$$ $$M = \left(\frac{2+4}{2},\ \frac{5+(-1)}{2}\right) = (3,\ 2)$$

So the locus line must pass through $(3,\ 2)$.

STEP 3 · FIND THE SLOPE OF $AB$

Slope of $AB$:

$$m_{AB} = \frac{-1 - 5}{4 - 2} = \frac{-6}{2} = -3$$

Since the locus is perpendicular to $AB$,

$$m_{\text{locus}} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}} = -\frac{1}{-3} = \frac{1}{3}$$
STEP 4 · EQUATION OF THE LOCUS

Use point-slope form with $M(3,2)$ and slope $\tfrac13$:

$$y - 2 = \tfrac{1}{3}(x - 3)$$ $$3(y - 2) = x - 3$$ $$3y - 6 = x - 3$$ $$x - 3y + 3 = 0$$
Shortcut check: the mid-point $(3,2)$ must satisfy the answer. Substituting into option A: $3 - 3(2) + 3 = 3 - 6 + 3 = 0$ ✓. Option B gives $3-6-7=-10\neq0$; option C gives $3-6+13=10\neq0$; option D gives $9+2-11=0$ but its slope is $-3$ (parallel to $AB$), so it is not perpendicular.
Locus of $P$: $x - 3y + 3 = 0$  →  Answer: A
