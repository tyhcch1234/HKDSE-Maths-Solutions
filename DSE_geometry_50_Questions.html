Type 1

Angles, Parallel Lines, Angle Sum and the Exterior Angle · 7 questions

Corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles with reasons

Core idea: When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal, alternate angles are equal, and co-interior (consecutive interior) angles add to \(180^{\circ}\). Inside any triangle the three angles sum to \(180^{\circ}\), and producing one side gives an exterior angle equal to the sum of the two interior opposite angles. Every line of working must name the reason — that is where the marks are.

\(a+b=180^{\circ}\)\(A+B+C=180^{\circ}\)\(\text{ext. }\angle=A+B\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

In the figure, \(AB\parallel CD\). A straight line cuts \(AB\) at \(P\) and \(CD\) at \(Q\), with \(B\) to the right of \(P\) on \(AB\) and \(D\) to the left of \(Q\) on \(CD\). It is given that \(\angle BPQ=65^{\circ}\). Find (a) \(\angle PQD\), (b) \(\angle PQC\).

Answer(a) \(65^{\circ}\)　(b) \(115^{\circ}\)
  1. \(\angle BPQ\) and \(\angle PQD\) lie on opposite sides of the transversal and between the parallels \(AB\) and \(CD\), so they are alternate angles.
  2. Hence \(\angle PQD=\angle BPQ=65^{\circ}\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(AB\parallel CD\)).
  3. \(PQD\) and \(PQC\) are adjacent angles on the straight line \(CD\), so they add to \(180^{\circ}\).
  4. \(\angle PQC=180^{\circ}-65^{\circ}=115^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
Q2★☆☆☆3 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(\angle A=x\), \(\angle B=2x\) and \(\angle C=3x\). (a) Find \(x\). (b) State, with a reason, what kind of triangle \(ABC\) is.

Answer(a) \(x=30^{\circ}\)　(b) A right-angled triangle (angles \(30^{\circ},60^{\circ},90^{\circ}\))
  1. Angle sum of a triangle: \(x+2x+3x=180^{\circ}\).
  2. \(6x=180^{\circ}\), so \(x=30^{\circ}\).
  3. The angles are \(30^{\circ}\), \(60^{\circ}\) and \(90^{\circ}\).
  4. Since \(\angle C=3x=90^{\circ}\), triangle \(ABC\) is right-angled at \(C\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
Common mistake

\(x+2x+3x=6x\) — do not write \(6x=360^{\circ}\); \(360^{\circ}\) is the sum of angles at a point, not in a triangle.

Q3★★☆☆4 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(\angle BAC=48^{\circ}\) and \(\angle ABC=67^{\circ}\). The side \(BC\) is produced to \(D\). Find (a) \(\angle ACB\), (b) \(\angle ACD\), giving a reason for each answer.

Answer(a) \(65^{\circ}\)　(b) \(115^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) In \(\triangle ABC\): \(\angle ACB=180^{\circ}-48^{\circ}-67^{\circ}=65^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  2. (b) \(ACB\) and \(ACD\) are adjacent angles on the straight line \(BCD\).
  3. \(\angle ACD=180^{\circ}-65^{\circ}=115^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
  4. Check with the exterior angle: \(\angle ACD=\angle BAC+\angle ABC=48^{\circ}+67^{\circ}=115^{\circ}\) (ext. \(\angle\) of \(\triangle\)). ✓
Q4★★☆☆4 marks

In the figure, \(AB\parallel CD\) and \(BC\) is a transversal. It is given that \(\angle ABC=3x\) and \(\angle BCD=5x\), where the two angles lie on the same side of \(BC\). (a) Find \(x\). (b) Find the size of each of the two angles.

Answer(a) \(x=22.5^{\circ}\)　(b) \(67.5^{\circ}\) and \(112.5^{\circ}\)
  1. \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle BCD\) are co-interior angles between the parallels \(AB\) and \(CD\).
  2. Co-interior angles are supplementary: \(3x+5x=180^{\circ}\) (int. \(\angle\)s, \(AB\parallel CD\)).
  3. \(8x=180^{\circ}\), so \(x=22.5^{\circ}\).
  4. \(\angle ABC=3(22.5^{\circ})=67.5^{\circ}\) and \(\angle BCD=5(22.5^{\circ})=112.5^{\circ}\). Check: \(67.5+112.5=180\). ✓
Common mistake

Co-interior angles add to \(180^{\circ}\); they are NOT equal. Setting \(3x=5x\) gives \(x=0\), which shows the error immediately.

Q5★★☆☆5 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC\). The side \(BC\) is produced to \(D\) so that \(\angle ACD=124^{\circ}\). Find (a) \(\angle ACB\), (b) \(\angle ABC\), (c) \(\angle BAC\), giving a reason for each step.

Answer(a) \(56^{\circ}\)　(b) \(56^{\circ}\)　(c) \(68^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(ACB\) and \(ACD\) are adjacent angles on the straight line \(BCD\): \(\angle ACB=180^{\circ}-124^{\circ}=56^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
  2. (b) \(AB=AC\), so the base angles at \(B\) and \(C\) are equal: \(\angle ABC=\angle ACB=56^{\circ}\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  3. (c) In \(\triangle ABC\): \(\angle BAC=180^{\circ}-56^{\circ}-56^{\circ}=68^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  4. Check with the exterior angle: \(\angle ACD=\angle BAC+\angle ABC=68^{\circ}+56^{\circ}=124^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

\(AB=AC\) means the equal angles are \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle ACB\) (the base), not \(\angle BAC\). Matching the equal sides to the wrong pair of angles is the usual slip.

Q6★★★☆5 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(\angle ABC=64^{\circ}\) and \(\angle ACB=52^{\circ}\). The bisector of \(\angle ABC\) and the bisector of \(\angle ACB\) meet at the point \(X\) inside the triangle. Find (a) \(\angle BAC\), (b) \(\angle XBC\), (c) \(\angle BXC\).

Answer(a) \(64^{\circ}\)　(b) \(32^{\circ}\)　(c) \(122^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(\angle BAC=180^{\circ}-64^{\circ}-52^{\circ}=64^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  2. (b) \(BX\) bisects \(\angle ABC\), so \(\angle XBC=\frac{1}{2}\times 64^{\circ}=32^{\circ}\).
  3. Similarly \(CX\) bisects \(\angle ACB\), so \(\angle XCB=\frac{1}{2}\times 52^{\circ}=26^{\circ}\).
  4. (c) In \(\triangle BXC\): \(\angle BXC=180^{\circ}-32^{\circ}-26^{\circ}=122^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  5. Check with the half-angle formula: \(\angle BXC=90^{\circ}+\frac{1}{2}\angle BAC=90^{\circ}+32^{\circ}=122^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

Do not use the full angles \(64^{\circ}\) and \(52^{\circ}\) at \(B\) and \(C\) inside \(\triangle BXC\) — the bisectors halve them first.

Q7★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q17 ↗

In the figure, \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) lie on a straight line in that order, and \(A\), \(D\), \(E\) lie on a straight line in that order, with \(D\) between \(A\) and \(E\). It is given that \(AB=BD\), \(BC=CD\) and \(\angle CDE=72^{\circ}\). Let \(\angle ACD=x\). (a) Find \(\angle CAD\) in terms of \(x\). (b) Find \(\angle CDB\) in terms of \(x\). (c) Hence find \(x\).

Answer(a) \(72^{\circ}-x\)　(b) \(144^{\circ}-2x\)　(c) \(x=36^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) In \(\triangle ACD\), \(\angle CDE\) is an exterior angle at \(D\): \(\angle CDE=\angle CAD+\angle ACD\) (ext. \(\angle\) of \(\triangle\)).
  2. So \(72^{\circ}=\angle CAD+x\), giving \(\angle CAD=72^{\circ}-x\).
  3. (b) In \(\triangle ABD\), \(AB=BD\), so \(\angle BDA=\angle BAD=72^{\circ}-x\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  4. The exterior angle of \(\triangle ABD\) at \(B\): \(\angle CBD=\angle BAD+\angle BDA=(72^{\circ}-x)+(72^{\circ}-x)=144^{\circ}-2x\) (ext. \(\angle\) of \(\triangle\)).
  5. In \(\triangle BCD\), \(BC=CD\), so \(\angle CDB=\angle CBD=144^{\circ}-2x\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  6. (c) \(A\), \(D\), \(E\) are collinear, so \(\angle BDA+\angle CDB+\angle CDE=180^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
  7. \((72^{\circ}-x)+(144^{\circ}-2x)+72^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\), i.e. \(288^{\circ}-3x=180^{\circ}\).
  8. \(3x=108^{\circ}\), so \(x=36^{\circ}\).
  9. Check: \(\angle BDA=36^{\circ}\), \(\angle CDB=72^{\circ}\), and \(36^{\circ}+72^{\circ}+72^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

In (c) the three angles at \(D\) must be added along the straight line \(ADE\); omitting \(\angle CDE=72^{\circ}\) gives the wrong equation.

Type 2

Congruent Triangles and Isosceles-Triangle Properties · 7 questions

S.S.S., S.A.S., A.S.A., R.H.S. and base angles

Core idea: Two triangles are congruent if S.S.S., S.A.S., A.S.A., A.A.S. or R.H.S. holds, and then all corresponding sides and angles are equal. An isosceles triangle has two equal base angles; conversely, two equal angles mean two equal sides. Every proof needs three statements with three reasons, then the conclusion with its test named.

\(\text{AB}=\text{AC}\Rightarrow\angle B=\angle C\)\(\text{S.A.S.}\Rightarrow\cong\)\(\text{corr. }\angle\text{s of }\cong\triangle\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

It is given that \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle PQR\), where \(AB=7\) cm, \(BC=9\) cm and \(CA=6\) cm. Write down (a) \(PQ\), (b) \(QR\), (c) the angle of \(\triangle PQR\) that equals \(\angle BCA\).

Answer(a) \(7\) cm　(b) \(9\) cm　(c) \(\angle QRP\)
  1. In the congruence \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle PQR\) the order of the letters gives the matching vertices: \(A\leftrightarrow P\), \(B\leftrightarrow Q\), \(C\leftrightarrow R\).
  2. So \(PQ\) corresponds to \(AB=7\) cm and \(QR\) corresponds to \(BC=9\) cm (corr. sides of \(\cong\triangle\)).
  3. \(\angle BCA\) has its vertex at \(C\), so it matches the angle at \(R\), i.e. \(\angle QRP\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)).
Common mistake

\(QR\) matches \(BC\), not \(AC\): read the letter order in the congruence statement, not the position in the drawing.

Q9★☆☆☆3 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC\), \(\angle ABC=3x\) and \(\angle BAC=4x\). (a) Find \(x\). (b) Find the size of each angle of the triangle.

Answer(a) \(x=18^{\circ}\)　(b) \(54^{\circ},54^{\circ},72^{\circ}\)
  1. \(AB=AC\), so \(\angle ACB=\angle ABC=3x\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  2. Angle sum of \(\triangle ABC\): \(3x+3x+4x=180^{\circ}\).
  3. \(10x=180^{\circ}\), so \(x=18^{\circ}\).
  4. \(\angle ABC=\angle ACB=54^{\circ}\) and \(\angle BAC=4(18^{\circ})=72^{\circ}\). Check: \(54+54+72=180\). ✓
Q10★★☆☆4 marks

In quadrilateral \(ABCD\), \(AB=AD\) and \(CB=CD\). (a) Prove that \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle ADC\). (b) Hence prove that \(\angle ABC=\angle ADC\).

Answer(a) \(\triangle ABC\cong\triangle ADC\) (S.S.S.)　(b) \(\angle ABC=\angle ADC\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\))
  1. (a) In \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle ADC\):
  2. \(AB=AD\) (given),
  3. \(CB=CD\) (given),
  4. \(AC=AC\) (common side).
  5. \(\therefore\triangle ABC\cong\triangle ADC\) (S.S.S.).
  6. (b) Corresponding angles of congruent triangles are equal, with \(B\leftrightarrow D\), so \(\angle ABC=\angle ADC\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)).
Common mistake

The common side \(AC\) must be listed — without the third pair of sides, S.S.S. cannot be claimed.

Q11★★☆☆4 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC\) and \(D\) is the mid-point of \(BC\). (a) Prove that \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\). (b) Hence prove that \(\angle ADB=90^{\circ}\), that is, \(AD\perp BC\).

Answer(a) \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\) (S.S.S.)　(b) \(\angle ADB=\angle ADC=90^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(AB=AC\) (given), \(BD=DC\) (\(D\) is the mid-point of \(BC\)), \(AD=AD\) (common side).
  2. \(\therefore\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\) (S.S.S.).
  3. (b) Hence \(\angle ADB=\angle ADC\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)).
  4. \(B\), \(D\), \(C\) are collinear, so \(\angle ADB+\angle ADC=180^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
  5. Each is therefore \(\frac{180^{\circ}}{2}=90^{\circ}\), so \(AD\perp BC\).
Common mistake

In (b) the two equal angles \(ADB\) and \(ADC\) are also supplementary because \(BDC\) is a straight line; both facts are needed to get \(90^{\circ}\).

Q12★★☆☆5 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC\). \(D\) is a point on \(AB\) and \(E\) is a point on \(AC\) such that \(BD=CE\). (a) Prove that \(\triangle BCD\cong\triangle CBE\). (b) Hence prove that \(CD=BE\). (c) If \(CD\) and \(BE\) meet at \(O\), prove that \(\triangle BOC\) is isosceles.

Answer(a) \(\triangle BCD\cong\triangle CBE\) (S.A.S.)　(b) \(CD=BE\)　(c) \(\angle OBC=\angle OCB\), so \(OB=OC\)
  1. (a) In \(\triangle BCD\) and \(\triangle CBE\):
  2. \(BD=CE\) (given),
  3. \(\angle DBC=\angle ECB\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\) with \(AB=AC\)),
  4. \(BC=CB\) (common side).
  5. \(\therefore\triangle BCD\cong\triangle CBE\) (S.A.S.).
  6. (b) Hence \(CD=BE\) (corr. sides of \(\cong\triangle\)).
  7. (c) Also \(\angle BCD=\angle CBE\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)), i.e. \(\angle OCB=\angle OBC\).
  8. Therefore \(OB=OC\) (sides opp. equal \(\angle\)s), so \(\triangle BOC\) is isosceles.
Common mistake

The included angle must be between the two listed sides: \(\angle DBC\) lies between \(BD\) and \(BC\), and \(\angle ECB\) lies between \(CE\) and \(CB\) — those are the base angles, equal because \(AB=AC\).

Q13★★★☆6 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(AB=AC\). \(D\) lies on \(AB\) and \(E\) lies on \(AC\) such that \(AD=AE\). The line \(BE\) and the line \(CD\) meet at \(P\). (a) Prove that \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle ACD\). (b) Hence prove that \(BE=CD\) and \(\angle ABE=\angle ACD\). (c) Prove that \(PB=PC\).

Answer(a) \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle ACD\) (S.A.S.)　(b) \(BE=CD\), \(\angle ABE=\angle ACD\)　(c) \(PB=PC\)
  1. (a) In \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle ACD\):
  2. \(AB=AC\) (given),
  3. \(\angle BAE=\angle CAD\) (common angle \(A\)),
  4. \(AE=AD\) (given).
  5. \(\therefore\triangle ABE\cong\triangle ACD\) (S.A.S.).
  6. (b) Hence \(BE=CD\) and \(\angle ABE=\angle ACD\) (corr. sides and corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)).
  7. (c) Since \(AB=AC\) and \(AE=AD\), \(AB-AD=AC-AE\), that is \(BD=CE\).
  8. \(\angle ABC=\angle ACB\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)). Subtracting the equal angles in (b): \(\angle PBC=\angle ABC-\angle ABE=\angle ACB-\angle ACD=\angle PCB\).
  9. Therefore \(PB=PC\) (sides opp. equal \(\angle\)s).
Common mistake

In (c) do not assume \(P\) is the mid-point or that \(PB=PC\) from the picture: it follows from \(\angle PBC=\angle PCB\), which needs the subtraction of the two equal angles.

Q14★★★☆7 marksPast paper · 2018 Paper 2 Q18 ↗

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rhombus with \(\angle BAD=120^{\circ}\). \(E\) and \(F\) are points lying on \(AB\) and \(AD\) respectively such that \(AE=AF\), and \(\angle ECF=40^{\circ}\). (a) Show that \(\angle CAE=\angle CAF=60^{\circ}\). (b) Prove that \(\triangle ACE\cong\triangle ACF\) and hence find \(\angle ACE\). (c) Find \(\angle BEC\).

Answer(a) \(60^{\circ}\) each　(b) S.A.S., \(\angle ACE=20^{\circ}\)　(c) \(\angle BEC=80^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) In a rhombus the diagonal \(AC\) bisects \(\angle BAD\) (prop. of rhombus), so \(\angle CAE=\angle CAF=\frac{120^{\circ}}{2}=60^{\circ}\).
  2. (b) In \(\triangle ACE\) and \(\triangle ACF\): \(AC=AC\) (common side), \(\angle CAE=\angle CAF=60^{\circ}\) (from (a)), \(AE=AF\) (given).
  3. \(\therefore\triangle ACE\cong\triangle ACF\) (S.A.S.).
  4. Hence \(\angle ACE=\angle ACF\) (corr. \(\angle\)s of \(\cong\triangle\)), so \(\angle ACE=\frac{40^{\circ}}{2}=20^{\circ}\).
  5. (c) \(E\) lies on \(AB\), so \(AE\) produced to \(B\) makes \(\angle BEC\) an exterior angle of \(\triangle ACE\).
  6. \(\angle BEC=\angle ACE+\angle CAE=20^{\circ}+60^{\circ}=80^{\circ}\) (ext. \(\angle\) of \(\triangle\)).
  7. Check: \(\angle AEC=180^{\circ}-80^{\circ}=100^{\circ}\), and in \(\triangle ACE\), \(100^{\circ}+20^{\circ}+60^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

In (c) the exterior angle uses the two interior opposite angles \(\angle ACE\) and \(\angle CAE\), not \(\angle AEC\).

Type 3

Polygons: Interior and Exterior Angles and the Number of Sides · 7 questions

\((n-2)180^{\circ}\) and the constant exterior sum

Core idea: A polygon with \(n\) sides has interior angle sum \((n-2)\times 180^{\circ}\). If the polygon is regular, each exterior angle is \(\frac{360^{\circ}}{n}\) and each interior angle is \(180^{\circ}-\frac{360^{\circ}}{n}\). The exterior angles of any convex polygon always add to \(360^{\circ}\), which is the fastest route to \(n\).

\(\text{sum}=(n-2)180^{\circ}\)\(\text{ext.}=\frac{360^{\circ}}{n}\)\(\text{int.}+\text{ext.}=180^{\circ}\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the sum of the interior angles of a polygon with 10 sides.

Answer\(1440^{\circ}\)
  1. A polygon with \(n\) sides has interior angle sum \((n-2)\times 180^{\circ}\).
  2. With \(n=10\): \((10-2)\times 180^{\circ}=8\times 180^{\circ}=1440^{\circ}\).
Common mistake

Using \(n\times 180^{\circ}=1800^{\circ}\) forgets to subtract the two triangles that a polygon of \(n\) sides does not have: the answer is \((n-2)180^{\circ}\).

Q16★☆☆☆3 marks

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is \(156^{\circ}\). Find the number of sides of the polygon.

Answer\(15\) sides
  1. Interior and exterior angle at a vertex are adjacent angles on a straight line: exterior \(=180^{\circ}-156^{\circ}=24^{\circ}\).
  2. For a regular polygon, exterior angle \(=\frac{360^{\circ}}{n}\).
  3. \(n=\frac{360^{\circ}}{24^{\circ}}=15\), so the polygon has 15 sides.
Common mistake

Dividing \(360^{\circ}\) by the interior angle \(156^{\circ}\) gives 2.3 — always convert to the exterior angle first.

Q17★★☆☆4 marks

A regular polygon has 20 sides. Find (a) the size of each exterior angle, (b) the size of each interior angle, (c) the sum of the interior angles.

Answer(a) \(18^{\circ}\)　(b) \(162^{\circ}\)　(c) \(3240^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) Exterior angle \(=\frac{360^{\circ}}{20}=18^{\circ}\).
  2. (b) Interior angle \(=180^{\circ}-18^{\circ}=162^{\circ}\).
  3. (c) Sum \(=(20-2)\times 180^{\circ}=18\times 180^{\circ}=3240^{\circ}\).
  4. Check: \(20\times 162^{\circ}=3240^{\circ}\). ✓
Q18★★☆☆4 marks

In a regular polygon, each interior angle is 3 times each exterior angle. (a) Find each exterior angle. (b) Find the number of sides of the polygon.

Answer(a) \(45^{\circ}\)　(b) \(8\) sides
  1. Let the exterior angle be \(e\). Then the interior angle is \(3e\).
  2. Interior and exterior angles are supplementary: \(3e+e=180^{\circ}\).
  3. \(4e=180^{\circ}\), so \(e=45^{\circ}\).
  4. \(n=\frac{360^{\circ}}{45^{\circ}}=8\), so the polygon is a regular octagon.
Common mistake

Setting \(3e=360^{\circ}\) mixes up the interior angle with the exterior sum; the \(360^{\circ}\) belongs to the sum of all exterior angles, not to one interior angle.

Q19★★☆☆5 marks

A polygon has 9 sides. Five of its interior angles are each \(130^{\circ}\) and the remaining four interior angles are all equal. Find the size of each of the remaining four angles.

Answer\(152.5^{\circ}\) each
  1. Interior angle sum \(=(9-2)\times 180^{\circ}=7\times 180^{\circ}=1260^{\circ}\).
  2. The five given angles total \(5\times 130^{\circ}=650^{\circ}\).
  3. The four equal angles total \(1260^{\circ}-650^{\circ}=610^{\circ}\).
  4. Each is \(610^{\circ}\div 4=152.5^{\circ}\).
  5. Check: \(650^{\circ}+4(152.5^{\circ})=650^{\circ}+610^{\circ}=1260^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

This polygon is not regular, so \(\frac{360^{\circ}}{9}\) is irrelevant here — use the interior angle sum only.

Q20★★★☆6 marks

In the figure, \(ABCDEF\) is a regular hexagon. (a) Find \(\angle ABC\), giving a reason. (b) Prove that \(\angle BCA=30^{\circ}\). (c) Hence find \(\angle ACD\) and state what this tells you about the lines \(AC\) and \(CD\).

Answer(a) \(120^{\circ}\)　(b) \(30^{\circ}\)　(c) \(\angle ACD=90^{\circ}\), so \(AC\perp CD\)
  1. (a) Exterior angle of a regular hexagon \(=\frac{360^{\circ}}{6}=60^{\circ}\), so each interior angle \(=180^{\circ}-60^{\circ}=120^{\circ}\); hence \(\angle ABC=120^{\circ}\) (int. \(\angle\) of regular polygon).
  2. (b) In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB=BC\) (sides of a regular hexagon), so it is isosceles with base \(AC\).
  3. \(\angle BCA=\angle BAC=\frac{180^{\circ}-120^{\circ}}{2}=30^{\circ}\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  4. (c) The whole interior angle at \(C\) is \(\angle BCD=120^{\circ}\), and \(\angle BCA=30^{\circ}\) lies inside it.
  5. \(\angle ACD=120^{\circ}-30^{\circ}=90^{\circ}\), so \(AC\perp CD\).
Common mistake

\(\angle BCA\) is not half of \(120^{\circ}\); it is half of \(180^{\circ}-120^{\circ}\), because \(120^{\circ}\) is the vertex angle of the isosceles triangle \(ABC\).

Q21★★★☆7 marks

In a regular polygon, each interior angle is 5 times each exterior angle. (a) Find the number of sides \(n\). (b) Find the sum of the interior angles. (c) Given that each side is 3.5 cm long, find the perimeter of the polygon.

Answer(a) \(n=12\)　(b) \(1800^{\circ}\)　(c) \(42\) cm
  1. (a) Let the exterior angle be \(e\), so the interior angle is \(5e\).
  2. \(5e+e=180^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line), giving \(6e=180^{\circ}\) and \(e=30^{\circ}\).
  3. \(n=\frac{360^{\circ}}{e}=\frac{360^{\circ}}{30^{\circ}}=12\).
  4. (b) Sum of interior angles \(=(12-2)\times 180^{\circ}=10\times 180^{\circ}=1800^{\circ}\).

Circle Properties: Chords, Angles at the Centre and at the Circumference · 7 questions

Perpendicular from centre, semicircle, same segment

Core idea: A perpendicular drawn from the centre to a chord bisects that chord, which turns every chord question into a right-angled triangle with the radius as hypotenuse. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc, the angle in a semicircle is \(90^{\circ}\), and angles in the same segment are equal.

\(\angle AOB=2\angle ACB\)\(r^{2}=d^{2}+\left(\frac{AB}{2}\right)^{2}\)\(\angle\text{ in semicircle}=90^{\circ}\)

Doubling instead of halving is the usual slip: the angle at the centre is the larger one, so the angle at the circumference is \(\frac{1}{2}\times 130^{\circ}\).

Tangents, Cyclic Quadrilaterals and Concyclic Points · 7 questions

Tangent perpendicular to radius, opposite angles, alternate segment

Core idea: A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, and the two tangents drawn from an external point are equal in length. A quadrilateral is cyclic exactly when its opposite angles are supplementary, and then an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle. The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

\(\angle OAT=90^{\circ}\)\(\angle A+\angle C=180^{\circ}\)\(TA=TB\)

The line \(XY\) passes through \(A\) and is parallel to \(BC\), with \(X\) on the same side of \(A\) as \(B\) and \(Y\) on the same side of \(A\) as \(C\). Find (a) \(\angle XAB\), (b) \(\angle YAC\), (c) \(\angle BAC\), giving reasons.

Answer(a) \(70^{\circ}\)　(b) \(50^{\circ}\)　(c) \(60^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(XY\parallel BC\) and \(AB\) is a transversal, so \(\angle XAB=\angle ABC=70^{\circ}\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(XY\parallel BC\)).
  2. (b) Similarly, with transversal \(AC\): \(\angle YAC=\angle ACB=50^{\circ}\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(XY\parallel BC\)).
  3. (c) \(X\), \(A\), \(Y\) are collinear, so \(\angle XAB+\angle BAC+\angle YAC=180^{\circ}\) (adj. \(\angle\)s on st. line).
  4. \(\angle BAC=180^{\circ}-70^{\circ}-50^{\circ}=60^{\circ}\), which agrees with the angle sum of \(\triangle ABC\). ✓
Common mistake

The alternate angles are \(\angle XAB\) with \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle YAC\) with \(\angle ACB\): pairing them the other way round gives \(50^{\circ}\) and \(70^{\circ}\).

Q44★☆☆☆4 marks

\(ABCDE\) is a regular pentagon. (a) Find \(\angle BCD\). (b) Prove that \(\angle BCA=36^{\circ}\). (c) Hence find \(\angle ACD\).

Answer(a) \(108^{\circ}\)　(b) \(36^{\circ}\)　(c) \(72^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) Exterior angle \(=\frac{360^{\circ}}{5}=72^{\circ}\), so each interior angle \(=180^{\circ}-72^{\circ}=108^{\circ}\); hence \(\angle BCD=108^{\circ}\) (int. \(\angle\) of regular polygon).
  2. (b) \(AB=BC\) (sides of a regular pentagon), so \(\triangle ABC\) is isosceles with base \(AC\).
  3. \(\angle BCA=\angle BAC=\frac{180^{\circ}-108^{\circ}}{2}=36^{\circ}\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  4. (c) \(\angle ACD=\angle BCD-\angle BCA=108^{\circ}-36^{\circ}=72^{\circ}\).
Common mistake

The vertex angle of the isosceles triangle \(ABC\) is the interior angle \(108^{\circ}\), so each base angle is \(36^{\circ}\), not \(54^{\circ}\).

Q45★★☆☆5 marks

\(ABCD\) is a square. \(ABE\) is an equilateral triangle drawn inside the square, so that \(E\) lies inside \(ABCD\). (a) Find \(\angle EAB\). (b) Find \(\angle DAE\). (c) Prove that \(\angle AED=75^{\circ}\) and hence find \(\angle EDC\).

Answer(a) \(60^{\circ}\)　(b) \(30^{\circ}\)　(c) \(\angle AED=75^{\circ}\), \(\angle EDC=15^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(\triangle ABE\) is equilateral, so each of its angles is \(60^{\circ}\); in particular \(\angle EAB=60^{\circ}\).
  2. (b) \(\angle DAB=90^{\circ}\) (angle of a square), so \(\angle DAE=90^{\circ}-60^{\circ}=30^{\circ}\).
  3. (c) \(AD=AB\) (sides of the square) and \(AE=AB\) (equilateral triangle), so \(AD=AE\) and \(\triangle ADE\) is isosceles.
  4. \(\angle ADE=\angle AED=\frac{180^{\circ}-30^{\circ}}{2}=75^{\circ}\) (base \(\angle\)s, isos. \(\triangle\)).
  5. \(\angle EDC=\angle ADC-\angle ADE=90^{\circ}-75^{\circ}=15^{\circ}\).
Common mistake

In (c) the vertex angle of the isosceles triangle is \(\angle DAE=30^{\circ}\), so the base angles are \(\frac{180^{\circ}-30^{\circ}}{2}=75^{\circ}\); using \(60^{\circ}\) as the vertex angle gives the wrong answer.

Q46★★☆☆5 marks

In quadrilateral \(ABCD\), \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\), \(AB=8\) cm, \(BC=6\) cm, \(CD=24\) cm and \(DA=26\) cm. (a) Find \(AC\). (b) Prove that \(\angle ACD=90^{\circ}\). (c) Find the area of \(ABCD\).

Answer(a) \(10\) cm　(b) converse of Pythagoras　(c) \(144\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  1. (a) \(\triangle ABC\) is right-angled at \(B\), so \(AC^{2}=8^{2}+6^{2}=64+36=100\) and \(AC=10\) cm (Pythagoras).
  2. (b) In \(\triangle ACD\): \(AC^{2}+CD^{2}=10^{2}+24^{2}=100+576=676\).
  3. Also \(DA^{2}=26^{2}=676\). Since \(AC^{2}+CD^{2}=DA^{2}\), \(\angle ACD=90^{\circ}\) (converse of Pythagoras).
  4. (c) Area \(=[ABC]+[ACD]=\frac{1}{2}\times 8\times 6+\frac{1}{2}\times 10\times 24\).
  5. \(=24+120=144\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Common mistake

The converse of Pythagoras must be tested with the longest side on its own: check \(AC^{2}+CD^{2}\) against \(DA^{2}=676\), not against \(CD^{2}\).

Q47★★☆☆6 marks

In triangle \(ABC\), \(\angle ACB=90^{\circ}\). \(D\) is a point on \(AB\) such that \(CD\perp AB\). (a) Prove that \(\triangle ACD\sim\triangle ABC\). (b) Hence prove that \(AC^{2}=AD\cdot AB\). (c) Given that \(AD=4\) cm and \(DB=5\) cm, find \(AC\) and \(CD\).

Answer(c) \(AC=6\) cm, \(CD=2\sqrt{5}\approx 4.47\) cm
  1. (a) In \(\triangle ACD\) and \(\triangle ABC\): \(\angle ADC=90^{\circ}=\angle ACB\) (given) and \(\angle CAD=\angle CAB\) (common angle).
  2. \(\therefore\triangle ACD\sim\triangle ABC\) (A.A.A.), with \(A\leftrightarrow A\), \(D\leftrightarrow C\), \(C\leftrightarrow B\).
  3. (b) Corresponding sides: \(\frac{AD}{AC}=\frac{AC}{AB}\), so \(AC^{2}=AD\cdot AB\).
  4. (c) \(AB=AD+DB=4+5=9\) cm, so \(AC^{2}=4\times 9=36\) and \(AC=6\) cm.
  5. For \(CD\): \(\triangle ACD\sim\triangle CBD\), so \(\frac{CD}{BD}=\frac{AD}{CD}\), giving \(CD^{2}=AD\cdot DB=4\times 5=20\).
  6. \(CD=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}\approx 4.47\) cm.
  7. Check: \(BC^{2}=AB^{2}-AC^{2}=81-36=45\), so \(BC=3\sqrt{5}\), and \(AC^{2}+BC^{2}=36+45=81=AB^{2}\). ✓
Common mistake

In (b) the ratio is \(\frac{AD}{AC}=\frac{AC}{AB}\) — the side \(AC\) appears in both triangles, which is what produces the square \(AC^{2}\).

Q48★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2023 Paper 1 Q8 ↗

In the figure, the lines \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at the point \(E\), and \(AC\parallel DB\). It is given that \(AB=20\) cm, \(AC=10\) cm, \(BD=15\) cm and \(CE=6\) cm. (a) Prove that \(\triangle ACE\sim\triangle BDE\). (b) Find \(AE\) and \(BE\). (c) Is \(\triangle BDE\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer.

Answer(b) \(AE=8\) cm, \(BE=12\) cm　(c) Yes — \(\angle BED=90^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(AC\parallel DB\) with transversal \(AB\): \(\angle CAE=\angle DBE\) (alt. \(\angle\)s, \(AC\parallel DB\)).
  2. \(\angle AEC=\angle BED\) (vert. opp. \(\angle\)s).
  3. \(\therefore\triangle ACE\sim\triangle BDE\) (A.A.A.), with \(A\leftrightarrow B\), \(C\leftrightarrow D\), \(E\leftrightarrow E\).
  4. (b) Corresponding sides: \(\frac{AE}{BE}=\frac{AC}{BD}=\frac{10}{15}=\frac{2}{3}\).
  5. Let \(AE=2k\) and \(BE=3k\). Since \(AE+BE=AB=20\): \(5k=20\), so \(k=4\).
  6. \(AE=8\) cm and \(BE=12\) cm.
  7. (c) In \(\triangle ACE\): \(AE^{2}+CE^{2}=8^{2}+6^{2}=64+36=100\) and \(AC^{2}=10^{2}=100\).
  8. Since \(AE^{2}+CE^{2}=AC^{2}\), \(\angle AEC=90^{\circ}\) (converse of Pythagoras).
  9. From the similarity in (a), the corresponding angle \(\angle BED=90^{\circ}\), so \(\triangle BDE\) is right-angled. ✓
Common mistake

Corresponding sides must match the similarity order: \(AE\) with \(BE\) and \(AC\) with \(BD\). Pairing \(AE\) with \(BD\) gives nonsense.

Q49★★★☆7 marks

\(AB\) is a diameter of a circle with centre \(O\), and \(C\) is a point on the circle. The tangent to the circle at \(C\) meets \(AB\) produced at \(T\), with \(B\) between \(A\) and \(T\). It is given that \(\angle CAB=34^{\circ}\). (a) Find \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle ACB\). (b) Find \(\angle BCT\), giving a reason. (c) Find \(\angle ATC\). (d) Find \(\angle OTC\) and check your answer with triangle \(OTC\).

Answer(a) \(\angle ABC=56^{\circ}\), \(\angle ACB=90^{\circ}\)　(b) \(34^{\circ}\)　(c) \(22^{\circ}\)　(d) \(22^{\circ}\)
  1. (a) \(AB\) is a diameter, so \(\angle ACB=90^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) in semicircle).
  2. In \(\triangle ABC\): \(\angle ABC=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}-34^{\circ}=56^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  3. (b) \(TC\) is a tangent at \(C\) and \(CB\) is a chord, so the angle between them equals the angle in the alternate segment: \(\angle BCT=\angle CAB=34^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) in alt. segment).
  4. (c) In \(\triangle ACT\): \(\angle CAT=34^{\circ}\) and \(\angle ACT=\angle ACB+\angle BCT=90^{\circ}+34^{\circ}=124^{\circ}\).
  5. \(\angle ATC=180^{\circ}-34^{\circ}-124^{\circ}=22^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  6. (d) \(\angle COB=2\angle CAB=68^{\circ}\) (\(\angle\) at centre twice \(\angle\) at circumference on arc \(CB\)).
  7. \(\angle OCT=90^{\circ}\) (tangent \(\perp\) radius), so in right-angled \(\triangle OCT\): \(\angle OTC=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}-68^{\circ}=22^{\circ}\). ✓
Common mistake

In (c) \(\angle ACT\) is \(90^{\circ}+34^{\circ}=124^{\circ}\), not \(90^{\circ}-34^{\circ}\): the tangent lies outside the triangle, so the two angles at \(C\) add.

Q50★★★☆8 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 2 Q18 ↗

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rectangle with perimeter 170 cm and \(BC=34\) cm. \(E\) is a point outside the rectangle on the side of \(AB\) opposite to \(C\), \(F\) is a point outside the rectangle on the side of \(BC\) opposite to \(A\), and \(E\), \(B\), \(F\) lie on a straight line. It is given that \(\angle AEB=\angle BFC=90^{\circ}\) and \(AE=24\) cm. (a) Find \(AB\). (b) Find \(BE\). (c) Prove that \(\triangle ABE\sim\triangle BCF\). (d) Find \(EF\).

Answer(a) \(51\) cm　(b) \(45\) cm　(c) A.A.A.　(d) \(61\) cm
  1. (a) In a rectangle \(AB=CD\) and \(AD=BC=34\) cm. Perimeter: \(2\,AB+2(34)=170\).
  2. \(2\,AB=170-68=102\), so \(AB=51\) cm.
  3. (b) \(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled at \(E\), so \(BE^{2}=AB^{2}-AE^{2}=51^{2}-24^{2}=2601-576=2025\) (Pythagoras).
  4. \(BE=45\) cm.
  5. (c) \(\angle AEB=\angle BFC=90^{\circ}\) (given).
  6. \(\angle ABE=90^{\circ}-\angle BAE\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle ABE\)) and, since \(E\), \(B\), \(F\) are collinear and \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\), \(\angle CBF=90^{\circ}-\angle ABE=\angle BAE\).
  7. Hence \(\angle BAE=\angle CBF\), and the third angles are equal too: \(\triangle ABE\sim\triangle BCF\) (A.A.A.), with \(A\leftrightarrow B\), \(E\leftrightarrow F\), \(B\leftrightarrow C\).
  8. (d) Corresponding sides: \(\frac{AE}{BF}=\frac{AB}{BC}\), so \(\frac{24}{BF}=\frac{51}{34}=\frac{3}{2}\).
  9. \(3\,BF=48\), giving \(BF=16\) cm.
  10. \(E\), \(B\), \(F\) are collinear with \(B\) between \(E\) and \(F\), so \(EF=BE+BF=45+16=61\) cm.
Common mistake

In (d) \(B\) lies between \(E\) and \(F\), so the lengths add; and the ratio is \(\frac{AE}{BF}=\frac{AB}{BC}\) — matching \(AE\) with \(AB\) would give the wrong \(BF\).
