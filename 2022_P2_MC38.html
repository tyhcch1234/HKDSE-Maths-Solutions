Circle Geometry — Alternate Segment Theorem & Angles in the Same Segment

Tangent at a Point Alternate Segment Theorem Angles in the Same Segment Angle Sum

Q The Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a circle. \(PA\) and \(QB\) are the tangents to the circle at \(A\) and \(B\) respectively.

If \(\angle ADC = 79^\circ\), \(\angle CBQ = 39^\circ\) and \(\angle DAP = 42^\circ\), then \(\angle BCD =\)

  • A. \(76^\circ\)
  • B. \(79^\circ\)
  • C. \(81^\circ\)
  • D. \(82^\circ\)
Key idea: The tangent at a point cuts off a chord; the angle between the tangent and the chord equals the angle that chord subtends in the alternate segment. We will use this twice — at \(B\) and at \(A\).

a Part (a) — Use the tangent at \(B\) to find \(\angle BDC\)

Slide the tangency point \(B\) around the circle and toggle the tangent line. Watch how the angle between the tangent \(QB\) and the chord \(BD\) always equals the angle that chord subtends in the opposite arc.

\(\angle CBQ =\) 39°
\(\angle BDC =\) 39°
\(\angle ADC =\) 79°
\(\angle ADB = \angle ADC-\angle BDC =\) 40°
Step 11M

Identify the tangent at \(B\) and the chord \(BD\). The angle between them, \(\angle CBQ\), lies in the alternate segment to \(\angle BDC\).

Hint: The tangent–chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment (tangent properties / alternate segment theorem).
\(\angle BDC = \angle CBQ = 39^\circ\)  (∠ in alt. segment)
Step 21A

At \(D\), the ray \(DB\) splits \(\angle ADC\) into \(\angle ADB\) and \(\angle BDC\). Subtract to isolate \(\angle ADB\).

Hint: \(\angle ADB = \angle ADC - \angle BDC\). Make sure \(DB\) lies inside \(\angle ADC\).
\(\angle ADB = 79^\circ - 39^\circ = 40^\circ\)

b Part (b) — Use the tangent at \(A\) to find \(\angle ACD\), then add

Now bring in the second tangent \(PA\) at \(A\). Drag the slider to see the tangent–chord angle \(\angle DAP\) and the alternate-segment angle \(\angle ACD\) stay equal, then combine \(\angle ACB + \angle ACD\).

\(\angle ACD = \angle DAP =\) 42°
\(\angle ACB = \angle ADB =\) 40°
\(\angle BCD =\) 82°
Step 11M

Using the tangent \(PA\) at \(A\) with chord \(AD\), the angle \(\angle DAP\) lies in the alternate segment to \(\angle ACD\).

Hint: Again apply the alternate segment theorem, this time at point \(A\).
\(\angle ACD = \angle DAP = 42^\circ\)  (∠ in alt. segment)
Step 21M

\(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\) are concyclic. The angles \(\angle ACB\) and \(\angle ADB\) stand on the same chord \(AB\), so they are equal (same segment).

Hint: Angles in the same segment of a circle are equal — both subtend chord \(AB\).
\(\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 40^\circ\)  (∠s in the same segment)
Step 31A

The ray \(CA\) splits \(\angle BCD\) into \(\angle ACB\) and \(\angle ACD\). Add them.

Hint: \(\angle BCD = \angle ACB + \angle ACD\) — check that \(CA\) lies inside \(\angle BCD\).
\(\angle BCD = 40^\circ + 42^\circ = 82^\circ\)
Step 41A

Compare \(82^\circ\) with the options.

Hint: \(76^\circ,\ 79^\circ,\ 81^\circ\) do not match; only D gives \(82^\circ\).
Answer: D. \(82^\circ\)

✓ Summary of the Solution

  • \(\angle BDC = \angle CBQ = 39^\circ\)  (∠ in alt. segment at \(B\))
  • \(\angle ADB = \angle ADC - \angle BDC = 79^\circ - 39^\circ = 40^\circ\)
  • \(\angle ACB = \angle ADB = 40^\circ\)  (∠s in the same segment on chord \(AB\))
  • \(\angle ACD = \angle DAP = 42^\circ\)  (∠ in alt. segment at \(A\))
  • \(\angle BCD = \angle ACB + \angle ACD = 40^\circ + 42^\circ = 40^\circ + 42^\circ = \mathbf{82^\circ}\)
Final answer: D. \(82^\circ\)
