1 Introduce the auxiliary line \(GH /\!/ AD\) 1M
Draw a line \(GH\) through \(B\) with \(GH /\!/ AD /\!/ BC\). The line \(AB\) cuts it, producing corresponding angles: \[\angle AGH=\angle EAD=a \quad (\text{corr. }\angle\text{s, }GH/\!/ AD)\] \[\angle BGH=\angle FBC=b \quad (\text{corr. }\angle\text{s, }GH/\!/ BC)\]
Hint: Corresponding angles are equal only when the two lines cut by the transversal are parallel — that's exactly the given condition.
2 Form the straight angle \(\angle AGB\) 1M
At the point \(G\) on the straight line \(GH\), \(\angle AGB\) is a straight angle: \[\angle AGH+\angle BGH=\angle AGB=180^\circ\] \[a+b=180^\circ\]
Hint: Angles on a straight line sum to \(180^\circ\). Careful — the figure's \(GH\) is not vertical here, so \(a+b\) is not \(90^\circ\) in general.
3 Conclusion for statement I 1A
We obtain \(a+b=180^\circ\), not \(90^\circ\). Statement I is false in general.
Statement I: \(\;a+b=180^\circ\;\Rightarrow\;a+b=90^\circ\) is NOT true.

1 Apply interior angles on the same side 1M
Since \(AD /\!/ BC\) and \(CD\) is a transversal: \[\angle BCD+\angle ADC=180^\circ\quad(\text{int. }\angle\text{s, }AD/\!/ BC)\] \[\therefore\;c+d=180^\circ\]
Hint: Interior angles on the same side of a transversal between parallel lines are supplementary — they always add to \(180^\circ\), no matter how \(CD\) is slanted.
2 Conclusion for statement II 1A
The identity \(c+d=180^\circ\) holds for every configuration.
Statement II: TRUE — \(c+d=180^\circ\).

1 Entertain the claim \(a+b+c=d\) 1M
Statement III claims the four angles satisfy \(a+b+c=d\). To disprove a "must be true" claim we only need one valid configuration where it fails.
Hint: Move \(D\) further along \(AD\) (extending the top side). Angles \(a\) and \(b\) are locked by the fixed transversal \(AB\), but \(c\) and \(d\) react to the slanted line \(CD\).
2 Counter-example: expand \(AD\) by moving \(D\) only 1A
With \(D\) pushed along \(AD\): \(a\) and \(b\) are unchanged, \(c\) increases and \(d\) decreases. Hence the L.H.S. \(a+b+c\) increases while the R.H.S. \(d\) decreases, so \(a+b+c=d\) fails for that case.
Statement III: NOT necessarily true — a counter-example exists.

Summary of Results

  • I. \(\;a+b=180^\circ\) (corresponding angles on parallels) — so \(a+b=90^\circ\) is false.
  • II. \(\;c+d=180^\circ\) (interior angles, \(AD/\!/ BC\)) — true.
  • III. \(\;a+b+c=d\) — false; move \(D\) to extend \(AD\) and the equality breaks.
Answer:  A. I and II only
Exam tip: For "must be true" geometry questions, prove the true statements from parallel-line angle facts, and disprove a suspect statement with a quick counter-example — here, simply stretching \(AD\) breaks statement III while leaving I's actual relation and II intact.
