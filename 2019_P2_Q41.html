Orthocentre, Centroid and In-centre of a Right-Angled Triangle

Interactive Diagram

Question

If $\triangle ABC$ is a right-angled triangle with $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$, which of the following is/are true?

  1. The orthocentre of $\triangle ABC$ lies on $AC$.
  2. The centroid of $\triangle ABC$ lies inside $\triangle ABC$.
  3. The in-centre of $\triangle ABC$ lies outside $\triangle ABC$.
  • A.  I only
  • B.  II only
  • C.  I and III only
  • D.  II and III only

Solution

Step 1 · Check statement I (orthocentre)

The altitude from $A$ is the side $AB$ (since $AB \perp BC$), and the altitude from $B$ is the side $BC$ (since $BC \perp AB$). These two altitudes meet at the right-angle vertex $B$.

So the orthocentre is at vertex $B$, not on $AC$. Statement I is false.

Orthocentre of a Right-Angled Triangle \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{orthocentre}=B\) The two legs \(AB\) and \(BC\) are themselves altitudes, so they meet at the right-angle vertex.
Step 2 · Check statement II (centroid)

The centroid is the intersection of the medians. For every triangle — acute, right or obtuse — the centroid lies inside the triangle.

Statement II is true.

Centroid of a Triangle \(G=\left(\dfrac{x_{A}+x_{B}+x_{C}}{3},\dfrac{y_{A}+y_{B}+y_{C}}{3}\right)\) The centroid is always the average of the vertices, so it lies inside every triangle.
Step 3 · Check statement III (in-centre)

The in-centre is the intersection of the internal angle bisectors; it is the centre of the inscribed circle, which fits inside the triangle. For every triangle the in-centre lies inside the triangle.

Statement III is false.

Recall: In an obtuse triangle the orthocentre and circumcentre lie outside, but the centroid and in-centre always lie inside.
In-centre of a Triangle \(d(\text{in-centre},\ \text{side})=r\) The in-centre is the centre of the inscribed circle, which lies inside the triangle.
Step 4 · Conclusion

Only statement II is true.

Final Answer B. II only
