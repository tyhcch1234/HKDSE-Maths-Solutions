[2016 MC #2]
If $\dfrac{a}{x}+\dfrac{b}{y}=3$, then $x=$
  • A. $\dfrac{ay}{3y-b}$
  • B. $\dfrac{ay}{b-3y}$
  • C. $\dfrac{by}{3y-a}$
  • D. $\dfrac{by}{a-3y}$
1Start from the given equation
$$\frac{a}{x}+\frac{b}{y}=3$$
We must make $x$ the subject. Since $x$ appears in a denominator, first isolate the term $\dfrac{a}{x}$.
2Move $\dfrac{b}{y}$ to the right-hand side
$$\frac{a}{x}=3-\frac{b}{y}$$
Subtract $\dfrac{b}{y}$ from both sides — keep the term containing $x$ alone on the left.
3Combine into a single fraction
Adding Algebraic Fractions \(\dfrac{a}{b}\pm\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ad\pm bc}{bd}\) Writing \\(3\\) as \\(\dfrac{3y}{y}\\) puts \\(3-\\dfrac{b}{y}\\) over the single denominator \\(y\\).
$$\frac{a}{x}=\frac{3y}{y}-\frac{b}{y}=\frac{3y-b}{y}$$
Common denominator $y$: multiply $3$ by $\dfrac{y}{y}$. This avoids a nested fraction later.
4Reciprocate both sides
Reciprocal of an Equation \(\dfrac{a}{x}=\dfrac{c}{d}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \dfrac{x}{a}=\dfrac{d}{c}\) Once the left side is \\(\dfrac{a}{x}\\), taking reciprocals moves \\(x\\) into the numerator.
$$\frac{x}{a}=\frac{y}{3y-b}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{ay}{3y-b}$$
If $\dfrac{a}{x}=\dfrac{c}{d}$ then $\dfrac{x}{a}=\dfrac{d}{c}$; then multiply both sides by $a$.
5Check against the options
$$x=\frac{ay}{3y-b}$$
Compare with the choices — the denominators $b-3y$, $3y-a$, $a-3y$ do not match, so the answer is option A.
Answer: A    $x=\dfrac{ay}{3y-b}$
