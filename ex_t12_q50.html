Coordinate Geometry (117) · Sub-Topic: Locus

T12 Q50

Find the equation of the locus of a point equidistant from the lines \(x=5\) and \(x=5\).

Solution

The two given lines are $x=5$ and $x=5$, so they coincide.
For any point $(x_0,y_0)$, its distance from the line $x=5$ is $|x_0-5|$.
The distance from $(x_0,y_0)$ to the second line is also $|x_0-5|$, so the two distances are always equal.
Therefore every point in the plane satisfies the condition, with no restriction on $x$ or $y$.
The locus is the whole plane, represented by an identity such as $0=0$; there is no unique line equation.
