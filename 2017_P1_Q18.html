Functions and Graphs — Parabola & Line Intersection
Quadratic Equation Discriminant Sum & Product of Roots Distance Between Points

Question

The equation of the parabola \(\Gamma\) is \[ y = 2x^{2} - 2kx + 2x - 3k + 8 \] where \(k\) is a real constant. Denote the straight line \(y = 19\) by \(L\).

(a) Prove that \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) intersect at two distinct points. 3 marks

(b) The points of intersection of \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) are \(A\) and \(B\).

(i) Let \(a\) and \(b\) be the \(x\)-coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\) respectively. Prove that \((a-b)^{2} = k^{2} + 4k + 23\). 3 marks

(ii) Is it possible that the distance between \(A\) and \(B\) is less than \(4\)? Explain your answer. 2 marks

[Total: 8 marks]

Part (a) — Two Distinct Intersections

\(\Delta = 4k^2+16k+92\) Δ = 112.00 > 0 Two distinct real roots
11M

Substitute \(y = 19\) into the equation of \(\Gamma\):

\[ 2x^{2} - 2kx + 2x - 3k + 8 = 19 \]

Rearranging into standard quadratic form in \(x\):

\[ 2x^{2} + (2 - 2k)x - (3k + 11) = 0 \]
Hint: Intersections of a line and a curve correspond to real solutions of the resulting equation.
21M

Compute the discriminant of this quadratic:

\[ \Delta = (2 - 2k)^{2} - 4(2)\bigl(-(3k+11)\bigr) \] \[ = 4k^{2} + 16k + 92 \]

Complete the square:

\[ = 4(k^{2} + 4k + 2^{2} - 2^{2}) + 92 = 4(k+2)^{2} + 76 \]
Hint: \(4(k+2)^2 \ge 0\), so the smallest possible value of \(\Delta\) is \(76\).
31A

Since \(4(k+2)^{2} \ge 0\) for all real \(k\), we get

\[ \Delta = 4(k+2)^{2} + 76 > 0 \]

Therefore the quadratic equation has two distinct real roots, meaning:

Answer: \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) intersect at two distinct points. ✓
Hint: \(\Delta > 0 \;\Leftrightarrow\;\) two distinct real roots \(\;\Leftrightarrow\;\) two distinct intersection points.

Part (b)(i) — Proving \((a-b)^{2} = k^{2}+4k+23\)

\(a+b = k-1\) \(ab = -\dfrac{3k+11}{2}\) \((a-b)^2 = k^2+4k+23\)

Move the \(k\)-slider in Part (a) — the diagram above updates automatically.

11M

The roots of \(2x^{2} + (2-2k)x - (3k+11) = 0\) are exactly \(a\) and \(b\). By the sum and product of roots:

\[ a + b = -\frac{2 - 2k}{2} = k - 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad ab = \frac{-(3k+11)}{2} \]
Hint: For \(px^{2}+qx+r=0\): sum \(=-\dfrac{q}{p}\), product \(=\dfrac{r}{p}\).
21M

Expand \((a-b)^{2}\) using a standard identity:

\[ (a-b)^{2} = a^{2} - 2ab + b^{2} = (a+b)^{2} - 4ab \]
Hint: We use \((a+b)^2\) and \(ab\) because those are what Vieta's formulas give us directly.
31A

Substitute the obtained expressions:

\[ (a-b)^{2} = (k-1)^{2} - 4\left(\frac{-(3k+11)}{2}\right) \] \[ = k^{2} - 2k + 1 + 2(3k+11) \] \[ = k^{2} + 4k + 23 \]
Answer: \((a-b)^{2} = k^{2} + 4k + 23\) ✓
Hint: \(4 \times \dfrac{-(3k+11)}{2} = -2(3k+11)\), and subtracting gives \(+2(3k+11)\).

Part (b)(ii) — Can \(AB < 4\)?

\(AB = \sqrt{k^2+4k+23}\) minimum \(AB = \sqrt{19} \approx 4.359\)

The curve \(AB(k) = \sqrt{k^{2}+4k+23}\) is plotted against the dashed line \(AB = 4\).

11M

Since \(A\) and \(B\) lie on the horizontal line \(y = 19\), their \(y\)-coordinates are equal. Therefore:

\[ AB = \sqrt{(a-b)^{2} + (19-19)^{2}} = \sqrt{(a-b)^{2}} \]

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(AB < 4\). Then:

\[ \sqrt{(a-b)^{2}} < 4 \;\Longrightarrow\; (a-b)^{2} < 16 \]
Hint: Because \(A\) and \(B\) are on \(y=19\), the distance \(AB\) equals the horizontal gap \(|a-b|\).
21M

Using the result of part (b)(i):

\[ k^{2} + 4k + 23 < 16 \;\Longrightarrow\; k^{2} + 4k + 7 < 0 \]
Hint: Substitute the proven identity to turn the inequality into a condition on \(k\) alone.
31A

Examine the discriminant of \(k^{2} + 4k + 7 = 0\):

\[ 4^{2} - 4(1)(7) = 16 - 28 = -12 < 0 \]

Also the coefficient of \(k^{2}\) is positive (\(1 > 0\)), so \(k^{2}+4k+7 > 0\) for all real \(k\).

Hence \(k^{2}+4k+7 < 0\) has no real solution — a contradiction.

Answer: It is not possible for \(AB\) to be less than \(4\). The minimum distance is \(\sqrt{19} \approx 4.36\). ✓
Hint: Negative discriminant \(+\) positive leading coefficient \(\Rightarrow\) the expression is always positive, so it can never be \(< 0\).
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Substituting \(y=19\) gives \(2x^{2}+(2-2k)x-(3k+11)=0\). The discriminant \(\Delta = 4(k+2)^{2}+76 > 0\), so \(L\) and \(\Gamma\) intersect at two distinct points.
  • (b)(i) Roots \(a,b\) satisfy \(a+b = k-1\) and \(ab = -\dfrac{3k+11}{2}\). Hence \((a-b)^{2} = (a+b)^{2} - 4ab = k^{2}+4k+23\).
  • (b)(ii) \(AB = \sqrt{(a-b)^{2}} = \sqrt{k^{2}+4k+23}\). Assuming \(AB<4\) leads to \(k^{2}+4k+7<0\), whose discriminant is \(-12<0\). No real solution exists, so \(AB<4\) is impossible.
  • Minimum value of \(AB\) is \(\sqrt{19} \approx 4.36\), attained at \(k=-2\).
