HKDSE 2012 · P2 · MC23Rotation and Polar Coordinates

Point \(P(-3,-3\sqrt3)\) Image \(P'(3\sqrt3,-3)\) Origin Radius \(r=6\) Angle

The rectangular coordinates of the point \(P\) are \((-3,\,-3\sqrt{3})\). If \(P\) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin through \(90^\circ\), then the polar coordinates of its image are

A\((3,\,150^\circ)\)
B\((3,\,330^\circ)\)
C\((6,\,150^\circ)\)
D\((6,\,330^\circ)\)
Step 1 · Apply the rotation

A \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise rotation about the origin sends \((x,\,y)\mapsto(-y,\,x)\). Hence

\[ P(-3,\,-3\sqrt{3}) \;\longmapsto\; P'(3\sqrt{3},\,-3). \]

Step 2 · Find the radius \(r\)

\[ r=\sqrt{(3\sqrt{3})^2+(-3)^2}=\sqrt{27+9}=\sqrt{36}=6. \]

Step 3 · Find the angle \(\theta\)

The reference angle satisfies \(\tan\alpha=\dfrac{3}{3\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\), so \(\alpha=30^\circ\).

\(P'\) has \(x>0\) and \(y<0\), so it lies in quadrant IV and

\[ \theta = 360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ. \]

Step 4 · Write the polar coordinates

The image is therefore \((6,\,330^\circ)\).

Check: \(P=(-3,-3\sqrt3)\) is \((6,240^\circ)\). A \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise rotation adds \(90^\circ\): \(240^\circ+90^\circ=330^\circ\), with the radius unchanged at \(6\). ✔

💡 Hint: A rotation about the origin changes the polar angle by the rotation amount, but keeps the distance \(r\) from the origin the same.
Final Answer: D. \((6,\,330^\circ)\) Radius 6, direction \(330^\circ\) (quadrant IV)
