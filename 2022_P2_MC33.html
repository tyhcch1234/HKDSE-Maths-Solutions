Laws of Indices, Exponential & Logarithmic Functions

Logarithms Change of Base Quadratic in log
Q33

If $\log_4 y = 2x - 1$ and $(\log_4 y)^2 = 20x - 31$, then

$\log_2 y =$

A. 1 or 2
B. 2 or 4
C. 3 or 7
D. 6 or 14 ✓ answer
💡

Key insight

Both equations involve $\log_4 y$. Treat it as a single unknown, eliminate $x$, and solve a quadratic. Then use the base‑change rule: $\log_2 y = \frac{\log_4 y}{\log_4 2} = 2\log_4 y$.

Step-by-step solution

1 Given equations ▾

We have:

(1)   $\log_4 y = 2x - 1$
(2)   $(\log_4 y)^2 = 20x - 31$

Let $u = \log_4 y$ to simplify the algebra.

2 Eliminate $x$ ▾

From (1):

$x = \dfrac{\log_4 y + 1}{2}$

Substitute into (2):

$(\log_4 y)^2 = 20 \cdot \dfrac{\log_4 y + 1}{2} - 31$

Simplify the right‑hand side:

$(\log_4 y)^2 = 10(\log_4 y + 1) - 31$
$(\log_4 y)^2 = 10\log_4 y + 10 - 31$
$(\log_4 y)^2 = 10\log_4 y - 21$

3 Form a quadratic in $u = \log_4 y$ ▾

Bring all terms to one side:

$u^2 - 10u + 21 = 0$

Factorise:

$(u - 3)(u - 7) = 0$

So $u = 3$ or $u = 7$.

i.e. $\log_4 y = 3$ or $\log_4 y = 7$.

4 Convert to $\log_2 y$ ▾

Use the base‑change rule:

$\log_2 y = \dfrac{\log_4 y}{\log_4 2} = \dfrac{\log_4 y}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2\log_4 y$

Therefore:

If $\log_4 y = 3$, then $\log_2 y = 2 \times 3 = 6$.
If $\log_4 y = 7$, then $\log_2 y = 2 \times 7 = 14$.

Hence $\log_2 y = 6$ or $14$. ⇒ Option D

When you see $\log_4 y$ appearing in both equations, substitute it with a single variable. After finding $u = \log_4 y$, remember to convert to the required base using $\log_2 y = \frac{\log_4 y}{\log_4 2}$. This avoids solving for $y$ directly.
