3-D Trigonometry — angles, distances and the dihedral angle in a tetrahedron

Sine Formula Cosine Formula Tetrahedron Dihedral Angle 3-D Visualisation

📄 The Question 7 marks

Figure 3 shows a tetrahedron \(ABCD\). Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(AD\) such that \(BP\) is perpendicular to \(AD\). A craftsman finds that \(AC = AD = CD = 13\) cm, \(BC = 8\) cm, \(BD = 12\) cm and \(\angle ABD = 72^\circ\).

(a) Find
(i) \(\angle BAD\),
(ii) \(CP\).   [5 marks]
(b) The craftsman claims that \(\angle BPC\) is the angle between the face \(ABD\) and the face \(ACD\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.   [2 marks]

(a)(i) Find \(\angle BAD\) 2 marks

Use the sine formula in triangle \(ABD\). We know \(BD = 12\), \(AD = 13\) and the angle opposite \(AD\) is \(\angle ABD = 72^\circ\).

Known sides Unknown angle target Given angle 72°
72°
BD = 12 cm
AD = 13 cm
∠BAD ≈ ?
Step 11M
Apply the sine formula in \(\triangle ABD\): \[ \frac{\sin \angle BAD}{BD} = \frac{\sin \angle ABD}{AD} \]
Hint: Match each side with the angle opposite to it. \(BD\) is opposite \(\angle BAD\); \(AD\) is opposite \(\angle ABD\).
Step 21A
Substitute the values: \[ \frac{\sin \angle BAD}{12} = \frac{\sin 72^\circ}{13} \] \[ \sin \angle BAD = \frac{12\sin 72^\circ}{13} \approx 0.8779 \] \[ \angle BAD \approx 61.39^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad 118.61^\circ \]
Hint: \(\sin \theta\) has two solutions in \(0^\circ \!-\!180^\circ\). Reject \(118.61^\circ\) because the sum of angles in \(\triangle ABD\) would exceed \(180^\circ\) (\(118.61^\circ + 72^\circ > 180^\circ\)).
Answer (a)(i): \(\angle BAD \approx 61.4^\circ\)
0 / 2

(a)(ii) Find \(CP\) 3 marks

Work in \(\triangle ABD\) first to get \(AP\), then use \(\triangle ACD\) (equilateral!) to get the angle \(\angle CAP = 60^\circ\), and finally apply the cosine formula in \(\triangle ACP\).

△ABD (finding AP) △ACD (equilateral) △ACP (finding CP)
AP
AP = ?
∠CAP = 60°
CP = ?
Step 11M
In \(\triangle ABD\), find \(\angle ADB\) using the angle sum: \[ \angle ADB \approx 180^\circ - 72^\circ - 61.39^\circ \approx 46.61^\circ \] Since \(BP \perp AD\), \(\triangle ABP\) is right-angled at \(P\): \[ \cos \angle ADB = \frac{AP}{AD} \;\Rightarrow\; AP = 13\cos 46.61^\circ \approx 8.93 \]
Hint: \(P\) lies on \(AD\) and \(BP \perp AD\), so in the right triangle \(ABP\) the side adjacent to \(\angle ADB\) is \(AP\).
Step 21M
In \(\triangle ACD\): \(AC = AD = CD = 13\), so it is equilateral and \[ \angle CAP = 60^\circ. \] Apply the cosine formula in \(\triangle ACP\): \[ CP^2 = AC^2 + AP^2 - 2(AC)(AP)\cos \angle CAP \]
Hint: The included angle between \(AC\) and \(AP\) is \(\angle CAP\), which is exactly the \(60^\circ\) angle of the equilateral triangle.
Step 31A
Substitute: \[ CP^2 \approx 13^2 + 8.93^2 - 2(13)(8.93)\cos 60^\circ \] \[ CP^2 \approx 169 + 79.74 - 116.09 \approx 132.65 \] \[ CP \approx 11.5 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: Keep the full-precision value of \(AP\) (do not round early) to avoid a final answer that is off by 0.1 cm. Using \(AP \approx 8.927\) cm gives \(CP \approx 11.52\) cm.
Answer (a)(ii): \(CP \approx 11.4\) cm  (accept \(11.4 - 11.5\))
0 / 3

(b) Is \(\angle BPC\) the angle between the faces? 2 marks

The angle between two planes is measured by the angle between two lines, one in each plane, both perpendicular to the line of intersection. Here the intersection of faces \(ABD\) and \(ACD\) is the line \(AD\).

\(BP \perp AD\) (true) \(CP\) (not \(\perp AD\)) Correct angle needs \(CP' \perp AD\)
\(AP^2 + CP^2 = ?\)
\(AC^2 = 169\)
Is \(\angle APC = 90^\circ\)?
Step 11M
Test whether \(CP \perp AD\) — i.e. whether \(\angle APC = 90^\circ\). Use the converse of Pythagoras: \[ AP^2 + CP^2 \approx 8.93^2 + 11.5^2 \approx 79.7 + 132.3 \approx 212 \] But \(AC^2 = 13^2 = 169\).
Hint: If \(\angle APC = 90^\circ\), then \(AP^2 + CP^2\) must equal \(AC^2\). Here \(AP^2 + CP^2 \neq AC^2\), so the right angle fails.
Step 21A
Since \(\angle APC\) is not a right angle, \(CP\) is not perpendicular to \(AD\).

Therefore \(BP\) and \(CP\) are not both perpendicular to the common line \(AD\), so \(\angle BPC\) is not the angle between face \(ABD\) and face \(ACD\).
Hint: The correct dihedral angle would use \(BP\) and the foot of the perpendicular from \(C\) to \(AD\), say \(P'\) (where \(P'\) is the midpoint of \(AD\) since \(\triangle ACD\) is equilateral).
Answer (b): The claim is not correct. \(\angle APC \neq 90^\circ\), so \(CP\) is not perpendicular to \(AD\); the angle between the two faces requires both lines to be perpendicular to the line of intersection \(AD\).
0 / 2

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a)(i)   \(\angle BAD \approx 61.4^\circ\)
  • (a)(ii)   \(AP \approx 8.93\) cm, \(\angle CAP = 60^\circ\), \(CP \approx 11.4\) cm
  • (b)   The claim is not correct. \(AP^2 + CP^2 \approx 212 \neq 169 = AC^2\), so \(\angle APC \neq 90^\circ\); hence \(CP\) is not perpendicular to \(AD\) and \(\angle BPC\) is not the angle between the two faces.
