[2019 #18] Trigonometry — Tetrahedron \(ABCD\)

HKDSE Mathematics (Compulsory Part) Paper 1

Figure 3 shows a tetrahedron \(ABCD\). Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(AD\) such that \(BP\) is perpendicular to \(AD\). A craftsman finds that \(AC=AD=CD=13\text{ cm}\), \(BC=8\text{ cm}\), \(BD=12\text{ cm}\) and \(\angle ABD=72^\circ\).
(a) Find
  1. \(\angle BAD\),
  2. \(CP\).  [5 marks]
(b) The craftsman claims that \(\angle BPC\) is the angle between the face \(ABD\) and the face \(ACD\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.  [2 marks]
Step 1 · Set up △ABD

Consider triangle \(ABD\). We know \(BD=12\), \(\angle ABD=72^\circ\) and, since \(AD=13\),
we have \(AD=13\).

To use the sine formula we first need the angle opposite \(BD\),
i.e. \(\angle BAD\), but \(AD\) is the side opposite \(\angle ABD\). Use:

$$\frac{\sin \angle BAD}{BD}=\frac{\sin \angle ABD}{AD}$$

This is a good target: one unknown angle,
one known side pair.

First obtain \(AB\) by the cosine formula (Step 2), then use the sine formula for \(\angle BAD\).
Step 2 · Find \(\angle BAD\) by the sine formula
Sine Formula \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}\) \(BD=12\) is opposite \(\angle BAD\) and \(AD=13\) is opposite \(72^{\circ}\), so \(\sin\angle BAD\approx 0.879\).

Apply the sine formula with \(BD=12\), \(AD=13\), \(\angle ABD=72^\circ\):

$$\frac{\sin \angle BAD}{12}=\frac{\sin 72^\circ}{13}$$
$$\sin \angle BAD=\frac{12\sin 72^\circ}{13}\approx 0.8790869$$

So \(\angle BAD\approx 61.38986936^\circ\) or \(180^\circ-61.38986936^\circ=118.6101306^\circ\).

In \(\triangle ABD\),
the remaining angle is \(\angle ADB=180^\circ-72^\circ-\angle BAD\approx 46.61^\circ>0\), so the obtuse solution is rejected (it would make \(\angle ADB<0\) together with \(72^\circ\)).

\(\angle BAD\approx 61.4^\circ\) (to 1 d.p.)
Step 3 · Find \(AP\)

We now have all angles of \(\triangle ABD\): \(\angle ABD=72^\circ\), \(\angle BAD\approx 61.3899^\circ\), so

$$\angle ADB\approx 180^\circ-72^\circ-61.3899^\circ\approx 46.6101^\circ$$

In the right-angled triangle formed by dropping the perpendicular from \(B\) to \(AD\),
i.e. in \(\triangle BPD\) (since \(BP\perp AD\)),

$$\cos \angle ADB=\frac{DP}{BD}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad DP=BD\cos\angle ADB$$
$$DP\approx 12\cos 46.61013064^\circ\approx 8.245028386$$

Since \(AD=13\) and \(P\) lies on \(AD\):

$$AP=AD-DP\approx 13-8.245028386=4.754971614\text{ cm}$$
\(AP\approx 4.7549716\text{ cm}\)
Step 4 · Find \(\angle CAP\)

Triangle \(ACD\) is equilateral because \(AC=AD=CD=13\). Hence every interior angle is \(60^\circ\),
and in particular, since \(P\) lies on \(AD\):

$$\angle CAP=\angle CAD=60^\circ$$

Note: \(\angle CAP\) is the angle at \(A\) in \(\triangle ACD\),
between \(AC\) and \(AD\).

Step 5 · Find \(CP\) by the cosine formula
Cosine Formula \(a^{2}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos A\) With \(AC=13\), \(AP\approx 4.755\) and \(\angle CAP=60^{\circ}\), we get \(CP^{2}\approx 129.8\).

In \(\triangle ACP\), we know \(AC=13\), \(AP\approx 4.754971614\) and \(\angle CAP=60^\circ\). By the cosine formula:

$$CP^2=AC^2+AP^2-2(AC)(AP)\cos\angle CAP$$
$$CP^2\approx 13^2+4.754971614^2-2(13)(4.754971614)\cos 60^\circ$$
$$CP^2\approx 169+22.60975503-61.81463098$$
$$CP^2\approx 129.7951241\quad\Longrightarrow\quad CP\approx 11.39276539\text{ cm}$$
\(CP\approx 11.4\text{ cm}\) (to 3 s.f.)
Step 6 · (b) Test whether \(\angle APC=90^\circ\)
Converse of Pythagoras' Theorem \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\iff\text{the angle opposite } c \text{ is } 90^{\circ}\) Here \(AP^{2}+CP^{2}\approx 152.4\neq AC^{2}=169\), so \(\angle APC\) is not a right angle.

The angle between the two faces \(ABD\) and \(ACD\) lies along their common edge \(AD\). It is the angle between two lines drawn in each face perpendicular to \(AD\) at a common point.

We are told \(BP\perp AD\),
so the relevant perpendicular in face \(ABD\) is \(BP\) (meeting \(AD\) at \(P\)). The angle between the faces is therefore \(\angle BPC\) only if \(CP\perp AD\),
i.e. only if \(\angle APC=90^\circ\).

Test with the converse of Pythagoras in \(\triangle ACP\):

$$AP^2+CP^2\approx 4.754971614^2+11.39276539^2\approx 152.4140341$$
$$AC^2=169$$

Since \(AP^2+CP^2\neq AC^2\), \(\angle APC\) is not a right angle.

Step 7 · Conclusion for (b)

Because \(\angle APC\neq 90^\circ\), the line \(CP\) is not perpendicular to \(AD\). Hence \(\angle BPC\) is not the angle between the planes \(ABD\) and \(ACD\).

Answer: The claim is not correct.
\(\angle BPC\) is merely the angle between \(BP\) and \(CP\); it is not the angle between the faces \(ABD\) and \(ACD\) because \(CP\) is not perpendicular to the common edge \(AD\).
Rule to remember: the angle between two planes is the angle between lines drawn in each plane perpendicular to the line of intersection at the same point. Here \(BP\perp AD\) but \(CP\not\perp AD\).

Question. In the figure, \(BD=10\), \(AD=13\), \(\angle ABD=65^{\circ}\), and \(BP\perp AD\) with \(P\) on \(AD\). Triangle \(ACD\) is equilateral.
(a) Find \(\angle BAD\).
(b) Find \(AP\).
(c) Find \(CP\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) By the sine formula, \(\dfrac{\sin\angle BAD}{10}=\dfrac{\sin 65^{\circ}}{13}\), so \(\sin\angle BAD\approx 0.6972\) and \(\angle BAD\approx 44.2^{\circ}\).
Step 2. The obtuse value \(180^{\circ}-44.2^{\circ}=135.8^{\circ}\) is rejected because \(135.8^{\circ}+65^{\circ}>180^{\circ}\).
Step 3. \(\angle ADB=180^{\circ}-65^{\circ}-44.2^{\circ}=70.8^{\circ}\), so \(DP=10\cos 70.8^{\circ}\approx 3.29\).
Step 4. (b) \(AP=AD-DP\approx 13-3.29=9.71\text{ cm}\).
Step 5. (c) Since \(\triangle ACD\) is equilateral, \(\angle CAP=60^{\circ}\), so \(CP^{2}=13^{2}+9.71^{2}-2(13)(9.71)\cos 60^{\circ}\approx 137.1\).
Step 6. Hence \(CP\approx 11.7\text{ cm}\).
Answer: (a) \(44.2^{\circ}\); (b) \(AP\approx 9.71\text{ cm}\); (c) \(CP\approx 11.7\text{ cm}\)
