Step 1: The upper cone removed is similar to the whole cone — start there.
[2015 MC #16]

The height and the base radius of a right circular cone are 12 cm and 9 cm respectively. The figure shows a frustum which is made by cutting off the upper part of the circular cone. The height of the frustum is 8 cm. Find the volume of the frustum.

  • A. 210π cm³
  • B. 312π cm³
  • C. 324π cm³
  • D. 936π cm³
Step 1 · Height of the removed cone

Total cone height = 12 
cm, frustum height = 8 
cm, so the small cone cut off on top has height

\[ h_{\text{top}} = 12 - 8 = 4\ \text{cm} \]
Step 2 · Use similarity of the two cones
Similar Solids \(\dfrac{l_{1}}{l_{2}}=\dfrac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\) The small cone and the whole cone are similar, so \(\dfrac{r_{\text{top}}}{9}=\dfrac{4}{12}\).

The small cone and the whole cone are similar solids (same vertex angle),
so corresponding lengths are in a fixed ratio:

\[ \frac{r_{\text{top}}}{9} = \frac{h_{\text{top}}}{12} = \frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3} \]
Step 3 · Radius of the upper cone
\[ r_{\text{top}} = 9 \times \frac{4}{12} = 3\ \text{cm} \]

So the removed cone has radius 3 
cm and height 4 
cm.

Step 4 · Volume of the frustum
Volume of a Cone \(V=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\) The frustum is the whole cone minus the \(3\ \text{cm}\times4\ \text{cm}\) top cone.

Volume of frustum = Volume of whole cone − Volume of upper cone:

\[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{frustum}} &= \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(9)^2(12) - \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(3)^2(4) \\[4pt] &= \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(81)(12) - \tfrac{1}{3}\pi(9)(4) \\[4pt] &= 324\pi - 12\pi \\[4pt] &= 312\pi\ \text{cm}^3 \end{aligned} \]
Answer: B  ·  Volume of the frustum = 312π cm³
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. A solid cone of base radius \(12\ \text{cm}\) and height \(18\ \text{cm}\) is cut by a plane parallel to its base, leaving a frustum of height \(12\ \text{cm}\). Find the volume of the frustum in terms of \(\pi\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The cone removed from the top has height \(18-12=6\ \text{cm}\).
Step 2. The two cones are similar,
so \(\dfrac{r_{\text{top}}}{12}=\dfrac{6}{18}=\dfrac{1}{3}\), giving \(r_{\text{top}}=4\ \text{cm}\).
Step 3. Volume of the whole cone \(=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi(12)^{2}(18)=864\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}\).
Step 4. Volume of the removed cone \(=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi(4)^{2}(6)=32\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}\).
Step 5. Volume of the frustum \(=864\pi-32\pi=832\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}\).
Answer: \(832\pi\ \text{cm}^{3}\)
Exam tip: For a frustum, always work with the whole cone minus the small similar cone. Use the height ratio to find the small cone's radius, then subtract the two volumes.
