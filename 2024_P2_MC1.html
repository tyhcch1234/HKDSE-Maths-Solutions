🔑 Difference of Two Squares 🧮 Factorisation ⚡ Identity: \(a^2-b^2\)

HKDSE Math 2024 Paper 2 · Question 1

Multiple Choice · Polynomials

\[ (x+3y)^2-(x-3y)^2 = ? \]

💡 Key Hint — Formula first, numbers later

Difference of Two Squares Identity

\[ a^2-b^2 = (a+b)(a-b) \]

Here \(a = x+3y\) and \(b = x-3y\). Note the middle term \(xy\) appears when adding/subtracting these two branches.

Step-by-Step Solution

1

Recognise the pattern

It is of the form \(a^2-b^2\)

\[ \underbrace{(x+3y)^2}_{a^2} - \underbrace{(x-3y)^2}_{b^2} \]

So \(a = x+3y\) and \(b = x-3y\).

2

Apply the identity

\(a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)\)

\[ = \big[(x+3y)+(x-3y)\big]\big[(x+3y)-(x-3y)\big] \]
3

Simplify each bracket

Notice that the \(\pm 3y\) terms cancel out

\[ = (2x)(6y) \]

The \(y\)-terms vanish when added/subtracted — only \(2x\) and \(6y\) remain.

4

Final result

Multiply the two remaining terms

\[ = 12xy \quad \Rightarrow \quad \boxed{\text{C}} \]

⚠️ Watch out for these mistakes

  • ✗ Expanding directly as \((x+3y)^2-(x-3y)^2 = x^2+6xy+9y^2 - x^2+6xy-9y^2\). This gives \(12xy\), but many students forget the sign and write \(6xy\) or \(18y^2\).
  • ✗ Choosing A (\(2x^2\)): this ignores the cross terms and assumes squaring just doubles the square.
  • ✗ Choosing B (\(6xy\)): only counting one cross term instead of both.
  • ✗ Choosing D (\(2x^2+18y^2\)): this is the answer to \((x+3y)^2+(x-3y)^2\), not the difference.
