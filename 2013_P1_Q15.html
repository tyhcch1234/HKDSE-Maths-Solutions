Box-and-Whisker Diagram, Mean & Standard Score

Statistics Box-and-Whisker Diagram Median & Mean Standard Score \( z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \)

Question 15 Total 4 marks

Full question text

The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the scores (in marks) of the students of a class in a test. Susan gets the highest score while Tom gets \(65\) marks in the test. The standard scores of Susan and Tom in the test are \(3\) and \(0.5\) respectively.

(a) Find the mean of the distribution. 2 marks

(b) Susan claims that the standard scores of at least half of the students in the test are negative. Do you agree? Explain your answer. 2 marks

Step 11M

Let \(\mu\) marks and \(\sigma\) marks be the mean and the standard deviation of the distribution respectively. The standard score of a score \(x\) is

\[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}. \]
Hint: Susan has the highest score. From the box-and-whisker diagram the right whisker ends at \(90\), so Susan's score is \(90\).
Step 21M

Form one equation for each student:

\[ \text{Susan: } \frac{90-\mu}{\sigma}=3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 90-\mu=3\sigma \] \[ \text{Tom: } \frac{65-\mu}{\sigma}=0.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 65-\mu=0.5\sigma \]
Hint: Subtract the two equations to eliminate \(\mu\).
Step 31A

Subtracting:

\[ (90-\mu)-(65-\mu)=3\sigma-0.5\sigma \] \[ 25=2.5\sigma \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sigma=10. \]

Substituting \(\sigma=10\) back into \(90-\mu=3\sigma\):

\[ 90-\mu=30 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mu=60. \]
Answer (a): The mean of the distribution is \(\boxed{60}\) marks (and \(\sigma=10\)).
Hint: Use the sliders above and set \(\mu=60,\ \sigma=10\) — the two readouts should turn to exactly \(3\) and \(0.5\).

Step 11M

A standard score is negative exactly when the raw score is less than the mean:

\[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}<0 \iff x<\mu . \]
Hint: \(\sigma>0\) always, so the sign of \(z\) is decided by \(x-\mu\) alone.
Step 21M

Read the box-and-whisker diagram: the median (line inside the box) is \(55\), while from part (a) the mean is \(\mu=60\). Hence

\[ \text{median }55 < \text{mean }60. \]
Hint: By definition of the median, at least half of the students score \(\le 55\) (i.e. at most the median).
Step 31A

At least half of the students have scores \(\le 55 < 60=\mu\). For all these students, \(x<\mu\), so their standard scores are negative.

Answer (b): Yes, we agree. Since the median \(55\) is less than the mean \(60\), at least half of the students score below the mean, so the standard scores of at least half of the students are negative.
Hint: Tick "Highlight negative z-scores" above: everything to the left of the mean line lies at or below the median region, so it is negative.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Let \(\mu\) be the mean and \(\sigma\) the standard deviation. From Susan (\(x=90,\ z=3\)) and Tom (\(x=65,\ z=0.5\)): \[ 90-\mu=3\sigma,\qquad 65-\mu=0.5\sigma \;\Longrightarrow\; \sigma=10,\ \mu=60. \] The mean of the distribution is \(60\) marks.
  • (b) From the box-and-whisker diagram, the median is \(55\), which is less than the mean \(60\). A standard score is negative \(\iff\) the score is below the mean. Since at least half of the students have scores \(\le 55 < 60\), at least half of the standard scores are negative. So we agree with Susan's claim.
  • Key idea: \( z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \), and \(z<0 \iff x<\mu\).
