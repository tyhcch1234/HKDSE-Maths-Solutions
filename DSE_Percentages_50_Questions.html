Type 1

Basic Percentage Changes · 7 questions

Increase, decrease, find the original, find the % change

Core idea: a percentage is just a fraction with denominator 100. So "increase by \(r\%\)" means multiply by \(\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)\), and "decrease by \(r\%\)" means multiply by \(\left(1-\frac{r}{100}\right)\). Before every calculation, ask one question: what is the base?

Increase by \(r\%\): \(\;\times\left(1+\dfrac{r}{100}\right)\) Decrease by \(r\%\): \(\;\times\left(1-\dfrac{r}{100}\right)\) Percentage change: \(\;\dfrac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\times100\%\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Increase 45 by 20%.

Answer54
  1. Increasing by 20% keeps the original 100% and adds 20%, so the new value is \(100\%+20\%=120\%\) of the original.
  2. As a multiplier: \(45\times\left(1+\frac{20}{100}\right)=45\times1.2\).
  3. \(45\times1.2=54\).
Common mistake

Do not write \(45+20\). The 20% is a proportion, not 20 units.

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Decrease 80 by 35%.

Answer52
  1. Decreasing by 35% leaves \(100\%-35\%=65\%\) of the original.
  2. \(80\times\left(1-\frac{35}{100}\right)=80\times0.65\).
  3. \(80\times0.65=52\).
Common mistake

"Decrease by 35%" is not "decrease to 35%". The first multiplies by 0.65; the second by 0.35.

Q3★☆☆☆2 marks

The marked price of an item is $250. It is now sold at a discount of 18%. Find the selling price.

Answer$205
  1. After an 18% discount the selling price is \(100\%-18\%=82\%\) of the marked price.
  2. Selling price \(=250\times0.82\).
  3. \(250\times0.82=205\), so the selling price is $205.
Q4★★☆☆2 marks

12% of a number is 54. Find the number.

Answer450
  1. Let the number be \(x\). Then \(12\%\times x=54\), i.e. \(0.12x=54\).
  2. So \(x=\frac{54}{0.12}\).
  3. \(x=450\).
  4. Check: \(450\times12\%=54\) ✓
Common mistake

Finding the original value is the inverse operation — divide by the percentage, do not multiply.

Q5★★☆☆3 marks

The number of students in a class rises from 40 to 46. Find the percentage increase.

Answer15%
  1. The base of a percentage change is the original value, i.e. 40 students.
  2. Increase \(=46-40=6\) students.
  3. Percentage increase \(=\frac{6}{40}\times100\%\).
  4. \(=\frac{600}{40}\%=15\%\).
Common mistake

The denominator is the old value 40, not the new value 46. Using 46 gives \(\frac{6}{46}\approx13\%\), which is wrong.

Q6★★☆☆3 marks

A student's weight drops from 64 kg to 56 kg. Find the percentage decrease.

Answer12.5%
  1. The base is the original weight, 64 kg.
  2. Decrease in weight \(=64-56=8\) kg.
  3. Percentage decrease \(=\frac{8}{64}\times100\%\).
  4. \(=\frac{1}{8}\times100\%=12.5\%\).
Q7★★★☆4 marks

The price of an item is first reduced by 30%. The new price is then increased by 30%. Find the percentage change from the original price.

Answera decrease of 9%
  1. Take the original price as 100 (setting the base to 100 is the safest habit).
  2. After the 30% cut: \(100\times0.7=70\).
  3. Increase the new price by 30%: \(70\times1.3=91\).
  4. Relative to the original 100, the change is \(\frac{91-100}{100}\times100\%=-9\%\).
  5. So the final price is 9% lower than the original price.
Common mistake

A 30% cut followed by a 30% rise does not cancel out: the two percentages use different bases (100 and 70).

Type 2

Comparing Two Quantities · 8 questions

"A is r% more / less than B" and working backwards

Core idea: in "A is \(r\%\) more than B", the base is B, so \(A=B\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)\). The quantity right after "than" is always the base. The same difference gives different percentages depending on the base.

From B to A: \(\;A=B\left(1\pm\dfrac{r}{100}\right)\) From A back to B: \(\;B=\dfrac{A}{1\pm\frac{r}{100}}\) "\(r\)% more / less": \(\;\dfrac{|A-B|}{B}\times100\%\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

A has $500 and B has $400. By what percentage is A's amount more than B's?

Answer25%
  1. "A is more than B" uses B ($400) as the base.
  2. Difference \(=500-400=100\).
  3. Percentage \(=\frac{100}{400}\times100\%=25\%\).
Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

Using the same amounts (A has $500, B has $400), by what percentage is B's amount less than A's?

Answer20%
  1. Now "B is less than A" makes A ($500) the base.
  2. The difference is still \(500-400=100\).
  3. Percentage \(=\frac{100}{500}\times100\%=20\%\).
  4. So the same $100 gap can be described as "25% more" or "20% less".
Common mistake

Reverse the direction and you reverse the base, so the answers are not symmetric. This is the most common source of lost marks.

Q10★★☆☆2 marks

The number of boys in a school is 20% less than the number of girls. If there are 250 girls, how many boys are there?

Answer200
  1. "20% less than the girls" uses the 250 girls as the base.
  2. Boys \(=250\times\left(1-\frac{20}{100}\right)=250\times0.8\).
  3. \(250\times0.8=200\), so there are 200 boys.
Q11★★☆☆4 marks

The number of boys in a school is 25% more than the number of girls. The school has 540 students in total. Find the number of boys and the number of girls.

Answer300 boys and 240 girls
  1. Let the number of girls be \(x\). Since boys are 25% more, there are \(1.25x\) boys.
  2. Total: \(x+1.25x=540\).
  3. \(2.25x=540\Rightarrow x=\frac{540}{2.25}=240\).
  4. Girls \(=240\); boys \(=1.25\times240=300\).
  5. Check: \(300+240=540\) ✓ and \(\frac{300-240}{240}=25\%\) ✓
Common mistake

Do not compute \(540\times0.25\). The base of the 25% is the number of girls, not the whole school.

Q12★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2012 Paper 1 Q4 ↗

Ada's daily wage is 20% higher than Billy's, while Billy's daily wage is 20% lower than Christine's. Given that Billy's daily wage is $480,

(a) find Ada's daily wage;
(b) who has the highest daily wage? Explain your answer.

Answer(a) $576　(b) Christine
  1. (a) Ada is 20% above Billy, so the base is Billy's $480:
  2. \(480\times1.2=576\). Ada earns $576 per day.
  3. (b) Billy is 20% below Christine, so \(480=0.8\times(\text{Christine})\).
  4. Christine \(=\frac{480}{0.8}=600\).
  5. Comparing: Christine $600 > Ada $576 > Billy $480, so Christine earns the most.
Common mistake

"Billy is 20% below Christine" must be undone by dividing by 0.8, not by multiplying by 1.2.

Q13★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

Donald's monthly salary is 25% higher than Peter's, and Peter's monthly salary is 25% lower than Teresa's. If Donald's monthly salary is $33 360, find Teresa's monthly salary.

Answer$35 584
  1. Donald \(=1.25\times\) Peter, so Peter \(=\frac{33\,360}{1.25}=26\,688\).
  2. Peter \(=0.75\times\) Teresa, so Teresa \(=\frac{26\,688}{0.75}\).
  3. \(26\,688\div0.75=35\,584\).
  4. Teresa's monthly salary is $35 584.
Common mistake

Both steps are inverse operations (divide by 1.25, then by 0.75). Multiplying forwards gives the trap answer $31 275.

Q14★★★☆4 marks

The cost of an item is $250 and its selling price is 20% above cost. The selling price is then reduced by 15%. Find the new percentage profit.

Answer2%
  1. Original selling price \(=250\times1.2=300\).
  2. Reduce by 15%: new selling price \(=300\times0.85=255\).
  3. Profit \(=255-250=5\), measured against the cost of $250.
  4. Percentage profit \(=\frac{5}{250}\times100\%=2\%\).
Common mistake

The 15% cut is taken on the selling price 300, but the percentage profit is measured against the cost 250. Two different bases.

Q15★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 2 Q9 ↗

The cost of a toy is \(x\%\) lower than its selling price. After selling the toy, the percentage profit is 25%. Find \(x\).

Answer20
  1. Percentage profit uses the cost as base: \(\frac{\text{selling price}-\text{cost}}{\text{cost}}=25\%\).
  2. Hence selling price \(=1.25\times\) cost, i.e. cost \(=\frac{\text{selling price}}{1.25}=0.8\times\) selling price.
  3. The cost is 80% of the selling price, so it is \(100\%-80\%=20\%\) lower.
  4. Therefore \(x=20\).
Common mistake

Trap: answering \(x=25\) on sight of "25%". The 25% is measured on the cost, while the 20% is measured on the selling price — they are reciprocals of each other.

Type 3

Marked Price, Discount, Cost and Profit · 9 questions

Cost → marked price → selling price → profit

Core idea: a "price chain" of three quantities. Write the links down before touching the numbers:

Selling price \(=\) marked price \(\times(1-\text{discount rate})\) Profit \(=\) selling price \(-\) cost Percentage profit \(=\dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost}}\times100\%\)

Remember: a discount is always a percentage of the marked price, while the percentage profit is always a percentage of the cost. When a loss is made the same formulas apply, with a negative profit.

Q16★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2014 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

The marked price of a toy is $255. It is sold at a discount of 40% on its marked price.

(a) Find the selling price of the toy.
(b) If the percentage profit is 2%, find the cost of the toy.

Answer(a) $153　(b) $150
  1. (a) After a 40% discount the selling price is 60% of the marked price: \(255\times0.6=153\).
  2. (b) Percentage profit is measured on the cost \(C\): \(\frac{153-C}{C}=2\%\).
  3. So \(153=1.02C\).
  4. \(C=\frac{153}{1.02}=150\). The cost is $150.
Common mistake

"40% discount" means the buyer pays 60%. Do not multiply by 0.4.

Q17★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

The cost of a book is $250 and the percentage profit is 20%.

(a) Find the selling price of the book.
(b) If the book is sold at a discount of 25% on its marked price, find the marked price.

Answer(a) $300　(b) $400
  1. (a) Selling price \(=250\times(1+20\%)=250\times1.2=300\).
  2. (b) The selling price is 75% of the marked price \(M\): \(300=0.75M\).
  3. \(M=\frac{300}{0.75}=400\). The marked price is $400.
Q18★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

A wallet is sold at a discount of 25% on its marked price for $690.

(a) Find the marked price of the wallet.
(b) The percentage profit is 15%. Find the cost of the wallet.

Answer(a) $920　(b) $600
  1. (a) \(690=0.75M\Rightarrow M=\frac{690}{0.75}=920\).
  2. (b) Selling price \(=1.15\times\) cost.
  3. Cost \(=\frac{690}{1.15}=600\).
Q19★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

A fan is sold at a discount of 30% on its marked price. The profit is $78 and the percentage profit is 26%. Find the marked price of the fan.

Answer$540
  1. From the percentage profit: \(\frac{78}{C}=26\%\Rightarrow C=\frac{78}{0.26}=300\).
  2. Selling price \(=\) cost \(+\) profit \(=300+78=378\).
  3. The selling price is 70% of the marked price: \(378=0.7M\).
  4. \(M=\frac{378}{0.7}=540\).
Common mistake

The percentage profit is measured on the cost, so the first step must be \($78\div0.26\), not treating $78 as 26% of the marked price.

Q20★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

The marked price of a shirt is $80 more than its cost. The shirt is sold at a 10% discount on its marked price and the percentage profit is 30%. Find the marked price of the shirt.

Answer$260
  1. Let the cost be \(C\). Then the marked price is \(C+80\).
  2. After a 10% discount the selling price is \(0.9(C+80)\).
  3. Percentage profit 30% means the selling price is \(1.3C\).
  4. So \(0.9(C+80)=1.3C\).
  5. Expand: \(0.9C+72=1.3C\), hence \(0.4C=72\) and \(C=180\).
  6. Marked price \(=180+80=260\), i.e. $260.
  7. Check: selling price \(=260\times0.9=234\); profit \(=234-180=54\); \(\frac{54}{180}=30\%\) ✓
Q21★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

The marked price of a calculator is 40% higher than its cost. The calculator is sold at a discount of 25% on its marked price and the profit is $13. Find the marked price of the calculator.

Answer$364
  1. Let the cost be \(C\). Then the marked price is \(1.4C\).
  2. Selling price \(=1.4C\times0.75=1.05C\).
  3. Profit \(=1.05C-C=0.05C\).
  4. Given the profit is $13: \(0.05C=13\Rightarrow C=260\).
  5. Marked price \(=1.4\times260=364\).
Common mistake

Introduce the cost as \(C\) and express the marked price, selling price and profit all in terms of \(C\). Substitute numbers only at the end.

Q22★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2018 Paper 1 Q7 ↗

The marked price of a vase is 30% above its cost. A loss of $88 is made by selling the vase at a discount of 40% on its marked price. Find the marked price of the vase.

Answer$520
  1. Let the cost be \(C\). Marked price \(=1.3C\).
  2. Selling price \(=1.3C\times0.6=0.78C\).
  3. Since \(0.78C\lt C\) there is a loss, and the loss is \(C-0.78C=0.22C\).
  4. \(0.22C=88\Rightarrow C=400\).
  5. Marked price \(=1.3\times400=520\).
  6. Check: selling price \(=520\times0.6=312\); loss \(=400-312=88\) ✓
Q23★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2023 Paper 2 Q11 ↗

The marked price of a jacket is 60% above its cost. A profit of $104 is made by selling the jacket at a discount of 25% on its marked price. Find the cost of the jacket.

Answer$520
  1. Let the cost be \(C\). Marked price \(=1.6C\).
  2. Selling price \(=1.6C\times0.75=1.2C\).
  3. Profit \(=1.2C-C=0.2C\).
  4. \(0.2C=104\Rightarrow C=520\).
Q24★★★★7 marksPast paper · 2025 Paper 1 Q7 ↗

The selling price of a souvenir is $378. The souvenir is sold at a discount of 60% on its marked price, and the marked price is 75% above its cost.

(a) Find the marked price of the souvenir.
(b) Find the cost of the souvenir.
(c) Is there a gain or a loss after selling the souvenir? Explain your answer.

Answer(a) $630　(b) $360　(c) a gain of $18
  1. (a) \(378=0.6M\Rightarrow M=\frac{378}{0.6}=630\).
  2. (b) The marked price is 75% above cost: \(630=1.75C\).
  3. \(C=\frac{630}{1.75}=360\).
  4. (c) Selling price $378 \(\gt \) cost $360, so there is a gain.
  5. Gain \(=378-360=18\), i.e. a profit of $18 (percentage profit \(=\frac{18}{360}=5\%\)).

Type 4

The "One Gain, One Loss" Trap · 6 questions

Two items sold at the same price, one at +x% and one at -x%

Core idea: a gain of \(x\%\) and a loss of \(x\%\) are measured on the two different costs, never on the common selling price. Two items sold at the same price therefore have different costs, so the money gained and the money lost cannot be equal.

Gain of \(x\%\): \(\;C=\dfrac{S}{1+\frac{x}{100}}\) Loss of \(x\%\): \(\;C=\dfrac{S}{1-\frac{x}{100}}\) Same price \(S\), \(+x\%\) and \(-x\%\): overall loss \(=\dfrac{x^{2}}{100}\%\)

The last line is the quick formula: one gain and one loss of the same percentage on the same selling price always produce a loss of \(\frac{x^2}{100}\%\).

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

Two items are each sold for $600. One is sold at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. Find the overall percentage profit or loss.

Answeran overall loss of 4%
  1. Cost of the item sold at +20%: \(\frac{600}{1.2}=500\).
  2. Cost of the item sold at -20%: \(\frac{600}{0.8}=750\).
  3. Total cost \(=500+750=1250\); total selling price \(=1200\).
  4. Loss \(=1250-1200=50\).
  5. Percentage loss \(=\frac{50}{1250}\times100\%=4\%\).
Common mistake

"+20% then -20%, so break even" is wrong: the item sold at a loss had the higher cost, so more money was lost than gained.

Q26★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

Susan sells two cars for $80 080 each. She gains 30% on one and loses 30% on the other. After the two transactions, Susan

A. loses $15 840.　B. gains $5 544.　C. gains $10 296.　D. has no gain and no loss.

AnswerA (a loss of $15 840)
  1. Cost of the car sold at +30%: \(\frac{80\,080}{1.3}=61\,600\).
  2. Cost of the car sold at -30%: \(\frac{80\,080}{0.7}=114\,400\).
  3. Total cost \(=61\,600+114\,400=176\,000\).
  4. Total selling price \(=80\,080\times2=160\,160\).
  5. Loss \(=176\,000-160\,160=15\,840\).
  6. The answer is A.
Common mistake

Formula check: \(\frac{30^2}{100}\%=9\%\), and \(176\,000\times9\%=15\,840\) ✓

Q27★★★☆4 marks

Two items are each sold for $2 000. One is sold at a profit of 25% and the other at a loss of 25%. Find the overall percentage loss.

Answera loss of 6.25%
  1. Item sold at +25%: cost \(=\frac{2000}{1.25}=1600\).
  2. Item sold at -25%: cost \(=\frac{2000}{0.75}=\frac{8000}{3}\approx2666.67\).
  3. Total cost \(=\frac{12\,800}{3}\); total selling price \(=4000=\frac{12\,000}{3}\).
  4. Loss \(=\frac{800}{3}\approx266.67\).
  5. Percentage loss \(=\frac{800/3}{12\,800/3}=\frac{800}{12\,800}=6.25\%\).
  6. Formula check: \(\frac{25^{2}}{100}\%=6.25\%\) ✓
Common mistake

Never "(25 - 25)% = 0": percentages may only be added or subtracted when they share the same base.

Q28★★★☆4 marks

Two items are each sold for $3 000. One is sold at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. Find the amount of the overall loss and the percentage loss.

Answera loss of $250, i.e. 4%
  1. Item sold at +20%: cost \(=\frac{3000}{1.2}=2500\).
  2. Item sold at -20%: cost \(=\frac{3000}{0.8}=3750\).
  3. Total cost \(=6250\); total selling price \(=6000\).
  4. Loss \(=6250-6000=250\).
  5. Percentage loss \(=\frac{250}{6250}\times100\%=4\%\), matching \(\frac{20^2}{100}\%=4\%\).
Q29★★★☆5 marks

Item A is sold for $1 380 at a profit of 15%. Item B is sold for $960 at a loss of 20%. Find the overall percentage profit or loss.

Answeran overall loss of 2.5%
  1. Cost of A \(=\frac{1380}{1.15}=1200\).
  2. Cost of B \(=\frac{960}{0.8}=1200\).
  3. Total cost \(=2400\); total selling price \(=1380+960=2340\).
  4. Loss \(=2400-2340=60\).
  5. Percentage loss \(=\frac{60}{2400}\times100\%=2.5\%\).
Common mistake

Here the two costs happen to be equal, but the percentage must still be taken over the total cost.

Q30★★★★5 marks

A trader marks the price of an item at 40% above its cost, then sells it at a 30% discount, claiming that he still makes a 2% profit. Is his claim correct? Find the actual percentage profit or loss.

AnswerThe claim is wrong; the actual result is a 2% loss
  1. Let the cost be \(C\). Marked price \(=1.4C\).
  2. Selling price \(=1.4C\times0.7=0.98C\).
  3. Since \(0.98C\lt C\), he actually makes a loss.
  4. Loss \(=C-0.98C=0.02C\), so the percentage loss is \(\frac{0.02C}{C}=2\%\).
  5. He used the right number with the wrong direction: the selling price is 98% of the cost, a 2% loss rather than a 2% profit.
Common mistake

Instant check: \(1.4\times0.7=0.98\lt 1\), so it must be a loss. A markup and a discount never simply cancel.

Type 5

Compound Interest, Growth and Depreciation · 8 questions

Amount, interest, depreciation, back to the principal

Core idea: compound interest is "interest on interest". Each period takes the previous amount as its base, so the growth is exponential.

Amount: \(\;A=P\left(1+\dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\) Interest: \(\;I=A-P\) Growth / decay: \(\;A=P(1\pm r)^{t}\)

where \(P\) is the principal, \(r\) the annual rate (as a decimal), \(n\) the number of compounding periods per year and \(t\) the number of years. Rate per period \(=\frac{r}{n}\), total number of periods \(=nt\).

Q31★★☆☆3 marks

$10 000 is deposited at 5% per annum, compounded yearly. Find the amount and the interest after 2 years.

Answeramount $11 025, interest $1 025
  1. \(P=10\,000\), \(r=0.05\), \(n=1\), \(t=2\), so there are 2 periods.
  2. \(A=10\,000(1+0.05)^{2}=10\,000\times1.1025\).
  3. \(A=11\,025\).
  4. Interest \(=11\,025-10\,000=1\,025\).
Common mistake

Simple interest would give only \(10\,000\times5\%\times2=1\,000\). The extra $25 is "interest on interest".

Q32★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

$2 000 is deposited at 5% per annum, compounded half-yearly. Find the interest after 4 years, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer$437
  1. Compounded twice a year for 4 years gives \(2\times4=8\) periods.
  2. Rate per period \(=\frac{5\%}{2}=2.5\%\).
  3. \(A=2000(1.025)^{8}=2000\times1.218403\ldots\)
  4. \(A\approx2436.81\).
  5. Interest \(=2436.81-2000=436.81\approx437\).
Common mistake

"Half-yearly" does not double the rate: it halves the rate and doubles the number of periods.

Q33★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 2 Q11 ↗

$50 000 is deposited at 8% per annum for 1 year, compounded monthly. Find the interest, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer$4 150
  1. Monthly compounding gives 12 periods in a year, with rate per period \(\frac{0.08}{12}\).
  2. \(A=50\,000\left(1+\frac{0.08}{12}\right)^{12}\).
  3. \(A\approx54\,149.98\).
  4. Interest \(=54\,149.98-50\,000=4\,149.98\approx4\,150\).
Common mistake

Simple interest gives \(50\,000\times8\%=4\,000\) — that is the distractor answer.

Q34★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

$50 000 is deposited at 6% per annum for 3 years, compounded quarterly. Find the amount, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer$59 781
  1. 4 quarters per year for 3 years gives \(4\times3=12\) periods.
  2. Rate per period \(=\frac{6\%}{4}=1.5\%\).
  3. \(A=50\,000(1.015)^{12}\).
  4. \(A\approx59\,780.91\), i.e. about $59 781.
Q35★★☆☆3 marks

A machine is worth $80 000 and depreciates by 15% each year. Find its value after 4 years, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer$41 761
  1. Depreciating by 15% a year means multiplying by \(1-0.15=0.85\) each year.
  2. Value after 4 years \(=80\,000\times0.85^{4}\).
  3. \(0.85^{2}=0.7225\), so \(0.85^{4}=0.52200625\).
  4. \(80\,000\times0.52200625=41\,760.5\approx41\,761\).
Common mistake

Depreciation compounds: multiply by 0.85 four times, do not subtract \(4\times15\%\).

Q36★★☆☆3 marks

The population of a city is 250 000 and grows by 3% each year. Find the population after 3 years, correct to the nearest hundred.

Answer273 200
  1. A 3% annual increase means multiplying by 1.03 each year.
  2. Population after 3 years \(=250\,000\times1.03^{3}\).
  3. \(1.03^{3}=1.092727\).
  4. \(250\,000\times1.092727=273\,181.75\).
  5. Correct to the nearest hundred: 273 200.
Q37★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 2 Q11 ↗

$60 000 is deposited at 4% per annum, compounded quarterly, for 2 years 6 months. Find the amount, correct to the nearest dollar.

Answer$66 277
  1. 2 years 6 months \(=2.5\) years, so the number of periods is \(2.5\times4=10\).
  2. Rate per period \(=\frac{4\%}{4}=1\%\).
  3. \(A=60\,000(1.01)^{10}\).
  4. \(1.01^{10}\approx1.104622\).
  5. \(A\approx66\,277.33\), i.e. about $66 277.
Common mistake

Convert 2 years 6 months into 10 quarterly periods, not 2.5 or 3 periods.

Q38★★★☆4 marks

A sum of money is deposited at 4% per annum, compounded yearly. After 2 years the amount is $54 080. Find the principal.

Answer$50 000
  1. Let the principal be \(P\). Then \(P(1.04)^{2}=54\,080\).
  2. \((1.04)^{2}=1.0816\).
  3. \(P=\frac{54\,080}{1.0816}\).
  4. \(P=50\,000\).
  5. Check: \(50\,000\times1.04^{2}=54\,080\) ✓
Common mistake

Finding the principal means dividing by \((1+r)^{t}\), exactly like finding an original value from a percentage.

Type 6

Part and Whole (Weighted Percentages) · 6 questions

Mixing two groups or two batches

Core idea: an overall percentage is not the simple average of the group percentages; it is a weighted average. The most reliable trick is to take the total as 100, which turns percentages into head counts.

Overall percentage \(=\dfrac{\text{sum of the parts}}{\text{total}}\times100\%\) Two batches combined: \(\;\dfrac{a\cdot p_1+b\cdot p_2}{a+b}\)
Q39★★☆☆3 marks

Alloy A has mass 200 g and contains 30% silver; alloy B has mass 300 g and contains 50% silver. If the two pieces are melted together, what percentage of the mixture is silver?

Answer42%
  1. Silver in A: \(200\times30\%=60\) g.
  2. Silver in B: \(300\times50\%=150\) g.
  3. Total silver \(=210\) g; total mass \(=500\) g.
  4. Percentage \(=\frac{210}{500}\times100\%=42\%\).
Common mistake

It is not \(\frac{30\%+50\%}{2}=40\%\): the heavier piece carries more weight.

Q40★★☆☆3 marks

400 g of salt solution contains 12% salt. After adding 100 g of pure water, what is the concentration of the solution?

Answer9.6%
  1. Salt present: \(400\times12\%=48\) g, unchanged by adding water.
  2. New total mass \(=400+100=500\) g.
  3. New concentration \(=\frac{48}{500}\times100\%=9.6\%\).
Common mistake

Adding water only increases the denominator, so the concentration must fall.

Q41★★☆☆3 marks

In a batch of 500 items, the first 150 items have a defective rate of 4% and the remaining 350 items have a defective rate of 2%. Find the defective rate of the whole batch.

Answer2.6%
  1. Defectives in the first group: \(150\times4\%=6\).
  2. Defectives in the second group: \(350\times2\%=7\).
  3. Total defectives \(=13\).
  4. Defective rate \(=\frac{13}{500}\times100\%=2.6\%\).
Q42★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2012 Paper 2 Q8 ↗

In a company, 37.5% of the employees are female. If 60% of the male employees and 80% of the female employees are married, find the percentage of married employees in the company.

Answer67.5%
  1. Males make up \(100\%-37.5\%=62.5\%\) of the staff.
  2. Married males as a share of the company: \(62.5\%\times60\%=0.625\times0.6=0.375=37.5\%\).
  3. Married females as a share of the company: \(37.5\%\times80\%=0.375\times0.8=0.30=30\%\).
  4. Married employees \(=37.5\%+30\%=67.5\%\).
  5. Alternative: take 1000 employees — 625 male, 375 female; married \(=625\times0.6+375\times0.8=375+300=675\), i.e. 67.5%.
Q43★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 2 Q9 ↗

In a school, 33% of the students are overweight. It is given that 60% of the students are girls and 45% of the girls are overweight. If \(x\%\) of the boys are overweight, find \(x\).

Answer15
  1. Boys make up \(100\%-60\%=40\%\) of the school.
  2. Overweight girls as a share of the school: \(60\%\times45\%=27\%\).
  3. Overweight boys as a share of the school: \(33\%-27\%=6\%\).
  4. That 6% is a share of the whole school, while boys are 40% of it, so the boys' rate is \(\frac{6\%}{40\%}=\frac{6}{40}=15\%\).
  5. Hence \(x=15\).
Common mistake

The last step is where most marks are lost: 6% is 6% of the school, not of the boys.

Q44★★★★4 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 2 Q11 ↗

In a school, 40% of the students are girls, and \(\beta\%\) of the girls are foreign students. 30% of the boys are foreign students. If the number of foreign students equals the number of girls, find \(\beta\).

A. 20　B. 45　C. 55　D. 80

AnswerC (55)
  1. Take the school to have 100 students: 40 girls and 60 boys.
  2. The number of girls is 40, and this equals the number of foreign students.
  3. Foreign girls \(=40\times\frac{\beta}{100}=0.4\beta\); foreign boys \(=60\times30\%=18\).
  4. So \(0.4\beta+18=40\).
  5. \(0.4\beta=22\Rightarrow\beta=55\).
  6. The answer is C.
Common mistake

Taking the total as 100 turns three percentages into plain numbers and removes the algebra.

Type 7

Percentages in Formulas, Geometry and Variation · 6 questions

Area, volume and direct/inverse variation

Core idea: if \(y\propto x^{n}\) and \(x\) changes by \(r\%\), then \(y\) changes by \(\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)^{n}-1\). Area scales as the square of a length, volume as the cube.

length \(\times k\) \(\Rightarrow\) area \(\times k^{2}\) length \(\times k\) \(\Rightarrow\) volume \(\times k^{3}\) variation \(z=\dfrac{kx^{2}}{y}\): replace each variable by its new multiple and simplify

Standard method: write "an increase of \(r\%\)" as "multiply by \(1+\frac{r}{100}\)", substitute into the formula, and read off the final multiple.

Q45★★☆☆3 marks

The radius of a circle is increased by 20%. By what percentage does its area increase?

Answer44%
  1. Area \(=\pi r^{2}\), proportional to the square of the radius.
  2. New radius \(=1.2r\).
  3. New area \(=\pi(1.2r)^{2}=1.44\pi r^{2}\).
  4. The area becomes 1.44 times the original, an increase of \((1.44-1)\times100\%=44\%\).
Common mistake

The area grows by 44%, not 20%: the square applies to the multiplier, not to the percentage.

Q46★★☆☆3 marks

The length of a rectangle is increased by 15% while its width is decreased by 10%. By what percentage does its area change?

Answeran increase of 3.5%
  1. Let the original length be \(L\) and width \(W\); original area \(=LW\).
  2. New length \(=1.15L\), new width \(=0.9W\).
  3. New area \(=1.15L\times0.9W=1.035LW\).
  4. The area becomes 1.035 times the original, an increase of \(3.5\%\).
Q47★★★☆3 marks

The area of a square is increased by 69%. By what percentage is its side increased?

Answer30%
  1. Let the original side be \(a\); original area \(=a^{2}\).
  2. New area \(=1.69a^{2}\).
  3. New side \(=\sqrt{1.69a^{2}}=1.3a\).
  4. The side becomes 1.3 times the original, an increase of \(30\%\).
  5. Check: \(1.3^{2}=1.69\) ✓
Common mistake

This is an inverse problem: take the square root of the area multiple, do not halve the percentage.

Q48★★★☆4 marksPast paper · 2014 Paper 2 Q10 ↗

If the radius of a sector is decreased by 50% and its angle is decreased by \(x\%\) so that its area is decreased by 90%, find \(x\).

A. 20　B. 40　C. 60　D. 80

AnswerC (60)
  1. Area of a sector \(=\pi r^{2}\times\frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}}\), proportional to \(r^{2}\theta\).
  2. New radius \(=0.5r\); new angle \(=\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)\theta\).
  3. New area \(=0.5^{2}\times\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)\times\) original area.
  4. The area falls by 90%, so the new area is 10% of the original: \(0.25\left(1-\frac{x}{100}\right)=0.1\).
  5. \(1-\frac{x}{100}=0.4\Rightarrow\frac{x}{100}=0.6\).
  6. \(x=60\), so the answer is C.
Q49★★★☆3 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 2 Q13 ↗

It is given that \(z\) varies directly as the square of \(x\) and inversely as \(y\). If \(x\) is increased by 20% and \(y\) is decreased by 20%, then \(z\)

A. is increased by 20%.　B. is decreased by 20%.　C. is increased by 80%.　D. is decreased by 80%.

AnswerC (increased by 80%)
  1. From the description, \(z=\frac{kx^{2}}{y}\).
  2. New \(x=1.2x\); new \(y=0.8y\).
  3. New \(z=\frac{k(1.2x)^{2}}{0.8y}=\frac{1.44}{0.8}\cdot\frac{kx^{2}}{y}=1.8z\).
  4. \(z\) becomes 1.8 times the original, an increase of \(80\%\).
Common mistake

A smaller \(y\) makes \(z\) larger (inverse variation). Decide the direction first, then compute the multiple.

Q50★★★☆4 marks

The base radius of a cylinder is increased by 10% and its height is decreased by 10%. By what percentage does its volume change?

Answeran increase of 8.9%
  1. Volume \(=\pi r^{2}h\).
  2. New radius \(=1.1r\); new height \(=0.9h\).
  3. New volume \(=\pi(1.1r)^{2}(0.9h)=\pi r^{2}h\times1.21\times0.9\).
  4. \(1.21\times0.9=1.089\).
  5. Volume increases by \((1.089-1)\times100\%=8.9\%\).
Common mistake

The 10% on the radius becomes 21% after squaring, which more than cancels the 10% drop in height.
