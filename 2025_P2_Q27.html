Circle $C$ $x$-axis Chord $PQ$ Points $P,\,M,\,Q$ Centre $G$
[2025MC#27]
The coordinates of the centre of the circle $C$ are $(7,-5)$. If $C$ cuts the $x$-axis at the points $P$ and $Q$ such that $PQ=24$, then the equation of $C$ is
  • A. $x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0$.
  • B. $x^{2}+y^{2}+14x-10y-95=0$.
  • C. $x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-119=0$.
  • D. $x^{2}+y^{2}+14x-10y-119=0$.
Step 1 · Set up the right triangle
Perpendicular from the Centre to a Chord \(PM=MQ=\dfrac{PQ}{2}\) The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so the foot \(M\) is the mid-point of \(PQ\).

Let $M$ be the mid-point of chord $PQ$. The perpendicular from the centre $G$ to a chord bisects the chord, so $M$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $G$ to the $x$-axis.

Hence $M$ has the same $x$-coordinate as $G$: $M=(7,0)$.

So the vertical distance is the larger $y$-value minus the smaller one, and $0>-5$: $$MG = 0-(-5) = 5 .$$

Step 2 · Half the chord

Since $M$ bisects $PQ$ and $PQ=24$, $$PM = MQ = \tfrac{24}{2} = 12 .$$

Step 3 · Pythagoras for the radius
Pythagoras' Theorem \(r=\sqrt{MG^{2}+PM^{2}}\) The radius, half the chord and the centre-to-chord distance form a right triangle.

Triangle $GMP$ is right-angled at $M$ (radius $GP$ is the hypotenuse), so $$r = GP = \sqrt{MG^{2}+PM^{2}} = \sqrt{5^{2}+12^{2}} = \sqrt{169} = 13 .$$

Check $5$–$12$–$13$ is a Pythagorean triple — a classic HKDSE shortcut.
Step 4 · Write the equation of $C$
Centre-Radius Form of a Circle \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\) The centre and radius are now both known, so the equation follows directly.

With centre $G(7,-5)$ and radius $13$, the standard form is $$(x-7)^{2}+\bigl[y-(-5)\bigr]^{2}=13^{2}$$ $$(x-7)^{2}+(y+5)^{2}=169 .$$

Expanding: $$x^{2}-14x+49+y^{2}+10y+25=169$$ $$x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y+74-169=0$$ $$x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0 .$$

Answer: A  —  $x^{2}+y^{2}-14x+10y-95=0$.
Quick check: centre $=\bigl(-\tfrac{-14}{2},-\tfrac{10}{2}\bigr)=(7,-5)$ ✓, and radius $=\sqrt{7^{2}+(-5)^{2}-(-95)}=\sqrt{49+25+95}=\sqrt{169}=13$ ✓.

Question. A circle \(C\) has centre \(G(3,4)\) and cuts the \(x\)-axis at two points \(P\) and \(Q\) such that \(PQ=6\). Find the equation of \(C\) in the form \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The perpendicular from \(G\) to the \(x\)-axis meets it at \(M(3,0)\), so \(MG=4\).
Step 2. \(M\) bisects \(PQ\), hence \(PM=MQ=3\).
Step 3. Radius \(r=\sqrt{MG^{2}+PM^{2}}=\sqrt{4^{2}+3^{2}}=5\).
Step 4. \((x-3)^{2}+(y-4)^{2}=25\), i.e. \(x^{2}+y^{2}-6x-8y+0=0\).
Step 5. Check: \(9+16-25=0\), so the centre is \((3,4)\) with radius \(\sqrt{9+16}=5\) ✓
Answer: \(x^{2}+y^{2}-6x-8y=0\)
