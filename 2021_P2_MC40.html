Circle & straight line — finding the circle with chord \(MN\) as a diameter.

Coordinate Geometry Circle Equation Line–Circle Intersection Mid-point Diameter Form

Question Multiple Choice

If the straight line \(4x = 3y\) and the circle

\[ x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 22y + 75 = 0 \]

intersect at the points \(M\) and \(N\), then the equation of the circle with \(MN\) as a diameter is

  • A. \((x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = 25\)
  • B. \((x-8)^2 + (y-6)^2 = 25\)
  • C. \((x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = 100\)
  • D. \((x-8)^2 + (y-6)^2 = 100\)

Drag the slider: when the slope equals \(4/3\), the line meets the circle exactly at the two black points \(M(3,4)\) and \(N(9,12)\). Notice how the discriminant (shown above) is positive exactly when there are two intersection points, and how it drops toward 0 as the line drifts.

Step 11M Make \(y\) the subject

From \(4x = 3y\) we get

\[ y = \frac{4x}{3} \]
Hint: The line passes through the origin with slope \(\tfrac{4}{3}\). Substituting this into the circle gives a quadratic in \(x\) only.
Step 21M Substitute into the circle

Put \(y = \dfrac{4x}{3}\) into \(x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 22y + 75 = 0\):

\[ x^2 + \left(\frac{4x}{3}\right)^2 - 4x - 22\left(\frac{4x}{3}\right) + 75 = 0 \] \[ x^2 + \frac{16x^2}{9} - 4x - \frac{88x}{3} + 75 = 0 \] \[ \frac{25x^2}{9} - \frac{100x}{3} + 75 = 0 \]
Hint: Multiply throughout by \(9/25\) to clear the fractions — the numbers become the friendly \(x^2 - 12x + 27 = 0\).
Step 31A Solve the quadratic
\[ x^2 - 12x + 27 = 0 \] \[ (x-3)(x-9) = 0 \] \[ x = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 9 \]
Hint: Two distinct real roots \(\Rightarrow\) the line cuts the circle at two points, so \(M\) and \(N\) are separate points (a genuine chord).
Step 41A Back-substitute to get \(M\) and \(N\)

Using \(y = \dfrac{4x}{3}\):

\[ x=3:\quad y=\frac{4(3)}{3}=4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad M(3,4) \] \[ x=9:\quad y=\frac{4(9)}{3}=12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad N(9,12) \]
Answer: \(M(3,4)\) and \(N(9,12)\).

Move the slider to slide \(P\) around the green circle. By Thales' theorem the angle \(\angle MPN\) stays a right angle for every position — a visual proof that \(MN\) really is a diameter of \(C_2\).

Step 11M Mid-point of \(MN\)

The centre of a circle is the mid-point of any diameter:

\[ \left(\frac{3+9}{2},\ \frac{4+12}{2}\right) = (6,\ 8) \]
Hint: A standard trap is swapping the coordinates to \((8,6)\) — that is option B. Always pair \(x\) with \(x\) and \(y\) with \(y\).
Step 21M Radius of the new circle

The radius is half the diameter \(MN\):

\[ r \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}|MN| \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(9-3)^2+(12-4)^2} \] \[ = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{36+64} = \tfrac{1}{2}(10) = 5 \]
Hint: \(r=5\) gives \(r^2 = 25\), not \(100\). A radius of \(10\) would be the whole diameter — that mistake leads to options C/D.
Step 31A Write the equation

Alternatively, use the diameter form directly:

\[ (x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = (3-6)^2 + (4-8)^2 \] \[ (x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 \]
Answer: \((x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = 25\)  →  Option A
0 / 3

Check — verify \(M\) and \(N\) really lie on \(C_2\) Verification

Substituting each point into \((x-6)^2 + (y-8)^2 = 25\):

  • \(M(3,4)\): \((3-6)^2+(4-8)^2 = 9+16 = 25\) ✓
  • \(N(9,12)\): \((9-6)^2+(12-8)^2 = 9+16 = 25\) ✓

Also check the distractors: \(M\) in option B gives \((3-8)^2+(4-6)^2 = 25+4 = 29 \neq 25\), so B is ruled out immediately.

Summary of Answers

  • Line: \(4x=3y \Rightarrow y = \tfrac{4}{3}x\)
  • Substituting gives \(x^2 - 12x + 27 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3,\ 9\)
  • Intersection points: \(M(3,4)\) and \(N(9,12)\)
  • Mid-point of \(MN\) (centre of required circle): \((6,8)\)
  • Radius \(=\tfrac{1}{2}|MN| = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(9-3)^2+(12-4)^2} = \) \(5\)
  • Equation: \((x-6)^2+(y-8)^2 = 25\)
Correct option: A — \((x-6)^2+(y-8)^2=25\)
