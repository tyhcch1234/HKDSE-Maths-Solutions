QUESTION

When Susan sells $n$ handbags in a month, her income in that month is $\$S$. It is given that $S$ is a sum of two parts, one part is a constant and the other part varies as $n$. When $n=10$, $S=10600$; when $n=6$, $S=9000$.

(a) When Susan sells 20 handbags in a month, find her income in that month. [4 marks]

(b) Is it possible that when Susan sells a certain number of handbags in a month, her income in that month is $\$18000$? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

1Set up the variation model

“One part is a constant and the other part varies as $n$” means

$$S = a + b\,n$$

where $a$ is the constant part (base salary) and $b$ is a non-zero constant.

Hint: “varies as $n$” $\Rightarrow$ a term of the form $b\,n$ (a direct variation part).

2Substitute the two given data points

Putting $n=10,\ S=10600$: $\quad a + 10b = 10600$  …(1)

Putting $n=6,\ S=9000$: $\quad a + 6b = 9000$  …(2)

These are the two red points plotted on the left.

Hint: each condition gives one linear equation in $a$ and $b$.
3Solve for $a$ and $b$

$(1)-(2):\quad 4b = 1600$

$$b = 400$$

Substitute into (2): $\quad a + 400(6) = 9000 \Rightarrow a = 9000 - 2400$

$$a = 6600$$

Hint: subtracting eliminates the constant $a$, leaving a single unknown $b$.
4Income when $n = 20$ (part a)

$$S = 6600 + 400n$$

At $n=20$:

$$S = 6600 + 400(20) = 6600 + 8000 = 14600$$

So her income in that month is $\$14600$.

Hint: the green point on the diagram lies exactly on the line at $n=20$.
5Test the target income $\$18000$ (part b)

Set $S = 18000$:

$$6600 + 400n = 18000$$

$$400n = 11400$$

$$n = 28.5$$

Hint: $n$ must be a whole number — Susan cannot sell half a handbag.
6Conclusion

Since $28.5$ is not an integer, there is no whole number of handbags giving an income of $\$18000$.

Therefore it is not possible.

Hint: the purple line $S=18000$ meets the red line at $n=28.5$, which lies strictly between the integers 28 and 29.
Answer: (a) $\$14600$   |   (b) Not possible — it would require $n = 28.5$, which is not an integer.
