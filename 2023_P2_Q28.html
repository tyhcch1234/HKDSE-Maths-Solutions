QUESTION

A box contains five cards numbered $1,2,3,4$ and $5$ respectively, while another box contains four cards numbered $6,7,8$ and $9$ respectively. If a number is randomly drawn from each box, find the probability that the product of the two numbers drawn is divisible by $4$.

A. $\dfrac{1}{5}$
B. $\dfrac{3}{10}$
C. $\dfrac{7}{20}$
D. $\dfrac{9}{20}$

1Identify the sample space.

Box A $=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ (5 cards) and Box B $=\{6,7,8,9\}$ (4 cards). One card is drawn from each box, independently.

Each ordered pair is equally likely — this is a uniform sample space, so probability $=\dfrac{\text{no. of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total no. of outcomes}}$.

2Count the total number of outcomes.

By the multiplication rule for independent choices: $$\text{Total outcomes} = 5 \times 4 = 20.$$

For each of the 5 cards from Box A there are 4 cards from Box B.

3Divide the favourable pairs into two cases.

A product is divisible by $4$ when the power of $2$ in the product is at least $2^2$. Check the cards:

  • Box A: $4=2^2$ gives two factors of 2; $2=2^1$ gives one factor of 2; others are odd.
  • Box B: $8=2^3$ gives three factors of 2; $6=2^1$ gives one factor of 2; $7,9$ are odd.

Case (i): Box A draws $4$ (has $2^2$). Then any card from Box B works: $$(4,6),\ (4,7),\ (4,8),\ (4,9) \Rightarrow 4 \text{ outcomes.}$$ Case (ii): Box A does not draw $4$, so we need Box B to supply the missing factor of 2, i.e. Box B draws $8$ (or $6$ only with $4$, already counted). Here Box B draws $8$ with Box A $\in\{1,2,3,5\}$ — but $2\times 8$ already works; check each: $$(1,8),\ (2,8),\ (3,8),\ (5,8) \Rightarrow 4 \text{ outcomes.}$$

Careful: $(4,8)$ is in Case (i); $(2,8)$ needs a factor $2^2$ — $2 \times 8 = 16 = 2^4$ ✓.

4List all favourable outcomes and count them.

The $9$ favourable outcomes are exactly: $$(1,8),\ (2,6),\ (2,8),\ (3,8),\ (4,6),\ (4,7),\ (4,8),\ (4,9),\ (5,8).$$

Quick check of the pattern: the only impossible case is “odd × odd”, which gives an odd product ($3\times 4 = 12$ such pairs).

5Compute the required probability.

$$P = \frac{9}{20}.$$

Sanity check with cases: $P(\text{A draws }4)=\frac15$ gives all 4; $P(\text{B draws }8 \text{ and A} \neq 4)=\frac{4}{5}\times\frac14=\frac15$ gives 4 more, but $(4,8)$ overlaps — combined count is $4+4=8$? Re-count carefully: Case (i) 4 outcomes; Case (ii) 4 outcomes; overlap $(4,8)$ counted in both, so favourable $=4+4-1+2=9$. The table above confirms it directly.

Answer: D  $\dfrac{9}{20}$
