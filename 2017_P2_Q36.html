5. Formulas and Sequences  •  Arithmetic Sequences

Interactive term-plot of each sequence

Step 1 — Given information: examine each sequence I, II, III.

Sequence I: $\pi^{30},\pi^{45},\pi^{60}$ (geometric) Sequence II: $30\pi,45\pi,60\pi$ (arithmetic) Sequence III: $\pi-30,\pi-45,\pi-60$ (arithmetic)
[2017MC#36]

Which of the following are arithmetic sequences?

I. $\quad \pi^{30},\ \pi^{45},\ \pi^{60}$
II. $\quad 30\pi,\ 45\pi,\ 60\pi$
III. $\quad \pi-30,\ \pi-45,\ \pi-60$
  • A. I and II only
  • B. I and III only
  • C. II and III only
  • D. I, II and III

Step-by-step Solution

1Recall the definition

A sequence is arithmetic if the difference of consecutive terms $d = T_{n+1}-T_n$ is the same for every pair of consecutive terms. We test each sequence's common difference.

Key test: $T_2-T_1 \;=\; T_3-T_2$ ?
2Test sequence I: $\pi^{30},\ \pi^{45},\ \pi^{60}$

$$T_2-T_1=\pi^{45}-\pi^{30}=\pi^{30}(\pi^{15}-1)$$ $$T_3-T_2=\pi^{60}-\pi^{45}=\pi^{45}(\pi^{15}-1)$$

Since $\pi^{30}(\pi^{15}-1)\neq \pi^{45}(\pi^{15}-1)$ (the factor $\pi^{15}\neq 1$), the differences are not equal.

✗Sequence I is not arithmetic — it is geometric with common ratio $\pi^{15}$.

Because the terms grow by a fixed multiplier, the differences grow too.
3Test sequence II: $30\pi,\ 45\pi,\ 60\pi$

$$T_2-T_1 = 45\pi - 30\pi = 15\pi$$ $$T_3-T_2 = 60\pi - 45\pi = 15\pi$$

The two differences are equal ($d=15\pi$).

✓Sequence II is arithmetic.

Constant common difference $d = 15\pi$.
4Test sequence III: $\pi-30,\ \pi-45,\ \pi-60$

$$(\pi-45)-(\pi-30) = -15$$ $$(\pi-60)-(\pi-45) = -15$$

The two differences are equal ($d=-15$).

✓Sequence III is arithmetic.

Constant common difference $d = -15$ (subtracting 15 each time).
5Conclusion & select the option

Only sequences II and III are arithmetic.

Among the options, this corresponds to C. II and III only.

I fails, II and III pass → option C.
Answer: C — II and III only Sequence II has $d=15\pi$; sequence III has $d=-15$; sequence I is geometric, not arithmetic.
