Weighted Average Rate Typing Speed

Mary performs a typing task for 7 hours. Her average typing speeds for the first 3 hours and the last 4 hours are 63 words per minute and 56 words per minute respectively. Find her average typing speed for the 7 hours.

Step-by-Step Solution

Click each step to reveal the full working.

Mary types for a total of 7 hours. This is split into two parts:

  • First 3 hours → average speed = 63 wpm
  • Last 4 hours → average speed = 56 wpm

💡 Hint

The intervals have different lengths (3 hours vs 4 hours), so we cannot simply average 63 and 56. We must account for the time spent.

Since speed is given in words per minute, convert the hours to minutes:

3 hours = 3 × 60 = 180 minutes

4 hours = 4 × 60 = 240 minutes

Now calculate total words in each period:

Words in first 3 hours = 63 × 180 = 11,340

Words in last 4 hours = 56 × 240 = 13,440

💡 Hint

Speed × Time = Total Words. Since the speeds are per minute, the time must be in minutes too.

Total words typed over the 7 hours:

Total words = 11,340 + 13,440 = 24,780

Total time in minutes:

7 hours = 7 × 60 = 420 minutes

Average typing speed:

Average = 24,780 ÷ 420 = 59 words per minute

💡 Hint

The average is always total words ÷ total time, not the average of the two speeds.

For an MCQ, you can use the weighted average formula directly without converting to minutes:

\( \text{Average} = \frac{63 \times 3 + 56 \times 4}{3 + 4} \)

\( = \frac{189 + 224}{7} \)

\( = \frac{413}{7} = 59 \)

💡 Hint

The units of hours cancel out because both the numerator and denominator use the same time unit. This is the fastest method for MCQ.

Correct Answer: C

\( \frac{63 \times 3 + 56 \times 4}{7} = \frac{413}{7} = 59 \) words per minute

⚠️ Common Mistake

Simply averaging the speeds: \( \frac{63 + 56}{2} = 59.5 \). This is wrong because the time periods are not equal. This would only be correct if both periods were the same length.

Key Takeaway

When averaging rates (or speeds) over unequal time periods, always use the weighted average: Total Amount ÷ Total Time. Never just average the two rates together.
