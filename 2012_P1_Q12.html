Question & Solution

12. (a) Figure 3(a) shows a solid metal right circular cone of base radius \(48\text{ cm}\) and height \(96\text{ cm}\). Find the volume of the circular cone in terms of \(\pi\). 2 marks

(b) A hemispherical vessel of radius \(60\text{ cm}\) is held vertically on a horizontal surface. The vessel is fully filled with milk.

(i) Find the volume of the milk in the vessel in terms of \(\pi\). 2 marks

(ii) The circular cone is now held vertically in the vessel as shown in Figure 3(b). A craftsman claims that the volume of the milk remaining in the vessel is greater than \(0.3\text{ m}^3\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. 5 marks

STEP 1 · (a) Volume of the cone

For a right circular cone,

$$V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h=\tfrac13\pi(48)^2(96)$$

$$=\tfrac13\pi(2304)(96)=73728\pi\ \text{cm}^3$$

Radius \(48\) cm, height \(96\) cm — just substitute directly.
Volume of a Cone \(V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\) One third of the cylinder with the same base and height; here \(r=48\), \(h=96\).
STEP 2 · (b)(i) Volume of milk in the full vessel

Volume of a hemisphere \(=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac43\pi r^3=\tfrac23\pi r^3\)

$$V=\tfrac23\pi(60)^3=\tfrac23\pi(216000)=144000\pi\ \text{cm}^3$$

The vessel is completely full, so the milk volume equals the vessel capacity.
Volume of a Sphere / Hemisphere \(V_{\text{sphere}}=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3},\qquad V_{\text{hemisphere}}=\dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^{3}\) A hemisphere is half a sphere, so \(V=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\).
STEP 3 · (b)(ii) How deep does the cone go?

The cone stands on the flat base of the hemisphere, apex up. The submerged part of the cone is cut by the flat milk surface (a circle of radius \(48\) cm at the top of the hemisphere).

By Pythagoras in the vertical cross-section through the axis, let \(h\) cm be the height of the frustum under the milk surface:

$$h=\sqrt{60^2-48^2}=\sqrt{3600-2304}=\sqrt{1296}=36\ \text{cm}$$

The cone is \(96\) cm tall, so \(36\) cm is inside the vessel — the remaining \(96-36=60\) cm sticks out above the milk.
Pythagoras' Theorem \(R^{2}=r^{2}+h^{2}\) In the axial cross-section the hemisphere radius \(60\) is the hypotenuse and \(48\) the horizontal leg, so the submerged depth is \(h=\sqrt{60^{2}-48^{2}}\).
STEP 4 · Radius of the cone above the milk surface

Let \(r\) cm be the radius of the small similar cone above the milk surface. Similar triangles:

$$\frac{r}{48}=\frac{96-36}{96}=\frac{60}{96}=\frac58$$

$$r=48\times\tfrac58=30\ \text{cm}$$

The part of the cone above the surface is a similar cone of height \(60\) cm.
Similar Figures — Length Ratio \(\dfrac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}=\dfrac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\) The small cone above the milk is similar to the whole cone, so radii and heights share the same ratio.
STEP 5 · Milk remaining in the vessel

Milk remaining = (vessel capacity) − (volume of cone submerged in the milk).

The submerged part is a cone of height \(36\) cm, i.e. the whole cone minus the small cone of height \(60\) cm:

$$V_{\text{sub}}=73728\pi-\tfrac13\pi(30)^2(60)$$

$$=73728\pi-18000\pi=55728\pi\ \text{cm}^3$$

$$\text{Milk remaining}=144000\pi-55728\pi=88272\pi\ \text{cm}^3$$

STEP 6 · Compare with 0.3 m³

Since \(1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}\), \(\;1\text{ m}^3=1000000\text{ cm}^3\).

$$88272\pi\ \text{cm}^3=\frac{88272\pi}{10^6}\ \text{m}^3\approx 0.2773\ \text{m}^3$$

$$0.2773\ \text{m}^3<0.3\ \text{m}^3$$

Conclusion: The volume of milk remaining is not greater than \(0.3\text{ m}^3\) — it is only about \(0.277\text{ m}^3\). So the craftsman's claim is disagreed.

(a) \(73728\pi\ \text{cm}^3\)  ·  (b)(i) \(144000\pi\ \text{cm}^3\)  ·  (b)(ii) \(88272\pi\ \text{cm}^3\approx0.277\text{ m}^3<0.3\text{ m}^3\)

Question. A solid right circular cone of base radius \(48\text{ cm}\) and height \(48\text{ cm}\) stands on the flat base of a hemispherical vessel of radius \(60\text{ cm}\), with its apex pointing up. The vessel is then completely filled with water. (a) Find the volume of the cone. (b)(i) Find the capacity of the vessel. (b)(ii) Find the volume of water remaining, and determine whether it is greater than \(0.33\text{ m}^{3}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(48)^{2}(48)=36\,864\pi\approx115\,812\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Step 2. (b)(i) \(V=\dfrac{2}{3}\pi(60)^{3}=144\,000\pi\approx452\,389\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Step 3. The cone is cut by the water surface at the rim of the hemisphere: by Pythagoras the submerged depth is \(\sqrt{60^{2}-48^{2}}=36\text{ cm}\).
Step 4. The part of the cone above the surface is a similar cone of height \(48-36=12\text{ cm}\), so its radius is \(48\times\dfrac{12}{48}=12\text{ cm}\). Its volume is \(\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(12)^{2}(12)=576\pi\).
Step 5. Cone under water \(=36\,864\pi-576\pi=36\,288\pi\). Water remaining \(=144\,000\pi-36\,288\pi=107\,712\pi\approx338\,387\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Step 6. Since \(1\text{ m}^{3}=10^{6}\text{ cm}^{3}\), the water left is about \(0.338\text{ m}^{3}\), which is greater than \(0.33\text{ m}^{3}\).
Answer: (a) \(36\,864\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\) (b)(i) \(144\,000\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\) (b)(ii) \(107\,712\pi\approx0.338\text{ m}^{3}>0.33\text{ m}^{3}\)
Exam tips: keep \(\pi\) symbolic until the final comparison; convert units at the very end to avoid rounding errors; always state the comparison explicitly to earn the A mark.
