Mensuration — Volume of a Cone & a Hemisphere, Frustum of a Cone

Volume of Cone Volume of Hemisphere Similar Triangles Frustum Unit Conversion

Q12 Question

Figure 3(a) shows a solid metal right circular cone of base radius \(48\text{ cm}\) and height \(96\text{ cm}\).

(a) Find the volume of the circular cone in terms of \(\pi\). 2 marks

(b) A hemispherical vessel of radius \(60\text{ cm}\) is held vertically on a horizontal surface. The vessel is fully filled with milk.

(i) Find the volume of the milk in the vessel in terms of \(\pi\). 2 marks

(ii) The circular cone is now held vertically in the vessel as shown in Figure 3(b). A craftsman claims that the volume of the milk remaining in the vessel is greater than \(0.3\text{ m}^3\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. 5 marks

Original Figure

a Volume of the Circular Cone 2 marks

96 cm
Base radius r = 48 cm Height h = 96 cm Volume \(V=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2h\) = 73728π cm³
Step 11M
Apply the volume formula of a right circular cone: \[V=\frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\] with \(r=48\text{ cm}\) and \(h=96\text{ cm}\).
Hint: In HKDSE, the formula \(V=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\) is provided on the formula sheet — just substitute correctly. Keep \(\pi\) in the answer since "in terms of \(\pi\)" is required.
Step 21A
\[ \begin{aligned} V&=\frac{1}{3}\pi(48)^{2}(96)\\[2pt] &=\frac{1}{3}\pi(2304)(96)\\[2pt] &=73728\pi \end{aligned} \] (numerically \(\approx 231\,623\text{ cm}^3\))
Volume of the cone \(=73728\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)

b(i) Volume of Milk in the Vessel 2 marks

60 cm
Radius R = 60 cm Hemisphere volume \(V=\tfrac{2}{3}\pi R^{3}\) = 144000π cm³ ≈ 452389 cm³
Step 11M
A hemisphere of radius \(R\) has half the volume of a sphere: \[V=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}=\frac{2}{3}\pi R^{3}\] Put \(R=60\text{ cm}\).
Hint: The vessel is fully filled, so the milk volume equals the volume of the hemisphere. Do not forget the \(2/3\) factor — a whole sphere is \(4/3\pi R^3\).
Step 21A
\[ \begin{aligned} V&=\frac{2}{3}\pi(60)^{3}\\[2pt] &=\frac{2}{3}\pi(216000)\\[2pt] &=144000\pi \end{aligned} \] (numerically \(\approx 452\,389\text{ cm}^3\))
Volume of milk in the vessel \(=144000\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)

b(ii) Is the Claim Valid? 5 marks

60 cm
Immersion depth \(d\) = 96 cm Cone height immersed 60 cm Frustum height \(h\)cone = 36 cm Small-cone radius \(r\) = 30 cm Milk remaining = 88272π cm³ ≈ 0.2773 m³
Step 11M
When the cone rests in the vessel, its apex is at the bottom and the base is level with the rim. The cone is \(96\text{ cm}\) tall, so it penetrates the vessel.

The cone enters the vessel by \[96-60=36\text{ cm}\] so the height of the frustum under the milk surface is \(h=36\text{ cm}\).
Hint: Picture the vessel: it is a hemisphere of radius \(60\text{ cm}\), depth \(60\text{ cm}\). The cone is taller than the vessel is deep, hence the bottom part of the cone is buried inside the milk.
Step 21M
Let \(r\) be the base radius of the small circular cone above the milk surface. By similar triangles (take a vertical cross-section through the axis): \[ \frac{r}{48}=\frac{96-36}{96}=\frac{60}{96} \] Thus \[ r=48\times\frac{60}{96}=30\text{ cm} \]
Hint: The full cone and the "top" small cone share the same apex angle, so they are similar. The ratio of radii always equals the ratio of the corresponding heights measured from the apex.
Step 31M
Milk remaining \(=\) (volume of vessel) − (volume of cone below the milk surface).

The part of the cone inside the milk is the frustum: \[V_{\text{frustum}}=V_{\text{full cone}}-V_{\text{small cone}}\] \[V_{\text{small cone}}=\frac{1}{3}\pi(30)^{2}(60)\] So \[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{milk}}&=144000\pi-\left(73728\pi-\frac{1}{3}\pi(30)^{2}(60)\right)\\[3pt] &=144000\pi-(73728\pi-18000\pi)\\[3pt] &=144000\pi-55728\pi\\[3pt] &=88272\pi \end{aligned} \]
Hint: The cone displaces the milk — the remaining milk is the vessel volume minus the volume of the cone that lies under the milk surface. That buried part is a frustum, so subtract the small cone from the big cone.
Volume of milk remaining \(=88272\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)
Step 41A
Convert to cubic metres: \[1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}\;\Rightarrow\;1\text{ m}^{3}=10^{6}\text{ cm}^{3}\] \[ \begin{aligned} V_{\text{milk}}&=88272\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\\[2pt] &=\frac{88272\pi}{10^{6}}\text{ m}^{3}\\[2pt] &\approx 0.2773146667\text{ m}^{3} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Always convert units before comparing. The claim is in \(\text{m}^3\) but the geometry answer is in \(\text{cm}^3\) — divide by \(10^{6}\), not \(100\).
Step 51A
Compare: \[0.2773146667\text{ m}^{3}<0.3\text{ m}^{3}\] Therefore the volume of milk remaining is not greater than \(0.3\text{ m}^3\).
Hint: The question asks "Do you agree? Explain your answer." — a valid full-mark answer must (1) compute the value, (2) compare with \(0.3\), and (3) explicitly state the conclusion "disagree / the claim is not correct".
\(V_{\text{milk}}\approx0.2773\text{ m}^{3}<0.3\text{ m}^{3}\).  So I disagree with the craftsman — the claim is not correct.

  • (b)(ii) Frustum height under milk surface \(h=36\text{ cm}\); small-cone radius \(r=30\text{ cm}\) (similar triangles)
