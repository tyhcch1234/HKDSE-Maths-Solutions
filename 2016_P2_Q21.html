HKDSE 2016 · Paper 2 · Q21 (Trigonometry)

$AD$ $AB$ $BC$ Angle $\alpha$ / $c$ Right angle Helping arc
2016MC#21
In the figure, $ABCD$ is a quadrilateral in which $\angle ADC = 90^\circ$.
The angle $\angle BAE = \alpha$ and the angle $\angle BCF = c$ are given.
Then $AD =$
A. $AB\cos\alpha + BC\cos c$ B. $AB\cos\alpha + BC\sin c$ C. $AB\sin\alpha + BC\cos c$ D. $AB\sin\alpha + BC\sin c$
💡 Key idea: $AD$ is vertical. Drop perpendiculars from $B$ onto $AD$ and $CD$ to slice $AD$ into two pieces, each found by right-triangle trigonometry on $AB$ and $BC$.
Step 1 · Construct the perpendiculars

Let $E$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $B$ to $AD$, and $F$ the foot of the perpendicular from $B$ to $CD$.

So $BE \perp AD$ and $BF \perp CD$ → $E$ lies on $AD$, $F$ lies on $CD$.

Step 2 · Split $AD$ using the rectangle $BEDF$

Since $\angle EDF = \angle DEB = \angle DFB = 90^\circ$, the quadrilateral $BEDF$ is a rectangle.

Hence $ED = BF$, and $$AD = AE + ED = AE + BF.$$

Step 3 · Find $AE$ from triangle $ABE$

In right triangle $ABE$ (right angle at $E$), the hypotenuse is $AB$ and the angle at $A$ is $\alpha$, so $$\cos\alpha = \frac{AE}{AB} \;\Longrightarrow\; AE = AB\cos\alpha.$$

Step 4 · Find $BF$ from triangle $BCF$

In right triangle $BCF$ (right angle at $F$), the angle at $C$ is $c$, so $$\sin c = \frac{BF}{BC} \;\Longrightarrow\; BF = BC\sin c.$$

Step 5 · Combine

$$AD = AE + BF = AB\cos\alpha + BC\sin c.$$

So the answer is option B.

ANSWER $\;AD = AB\cos\alpha + BC\sin c$  (B)
