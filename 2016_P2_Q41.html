Question

The straight line $2x-y-6=0$ and the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}-8y-14=0$ intersect at $P$ and $Q$. Find the $y$-coordinate of the mid-point of $PQ$.
A. $-4$
B. $-2$
C. $2$
D. $4$

Solution

STEP 1 · REWRITE THE LINE
Making a Variable the Subject \(2x-y-6=0\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\dfrac{y+6}{2}\) Expressing \(x\) in terms of \(y\) keeps the working in \(y\), which is the coordinate asked for.

From the line $2x-y-6=0$ we get $y=2x-6$, or equivalently

$$x=\frac{y+6}{2}$$

(This form is convenient because the required answer is a $y$-coordinate.)

STEP 2 · SUBSTITUTE INTO THE CIRCLE

Let $p$ and $q$ be the $y$-coordinates of $P$ and $Q$ respectively. Substitute $x=\dfrac{y+6}{2}$ into $x^{2}+y^{2}-8y-14=0$:

$$\left(\frac{y+6}{2}\right)^{2}+y^{2}-8y-14=0$$

STEP 3 · CLEAR FRACTIONS & SIMPLIFY

Multiply the whole equation by $4$:

$$(y+6)^{2}+4(y^{2}-8y-14)=0$$

$$(y^{2}+12y+36)+(4y^{2}-32y-56)=0$$

$$5y^{2}-20y-20=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^{2}-4y-4=0 \quad\ldots(*)$$

A quadratic in $y$ means $p$ and $q$ are exactly its two roots.
STEP 4 · SUM OF ROOTS
Sum of Roots of a Quadratic Equation \(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{b}{a}\ \ \text{for}\ \ ax^{2}+bx+c=0\) \(p\) and \(q\) are the roots of \(y^{2}-4y-4=0\), so \(p+q=-\dfrac{-4}{1}=4\).

Since $p,q$ are the roots of $(*)$ with coefficients $a=1,\;b=-4$:

$$p+q=-\frac{b}{a}=-\frac{-4}{1}=4$$

Only the SUM of the roots is needed — we never need $p$ and $q$ individually.
STEP 5 · MID-POINT $y$-COORDINATE
Mid-point Formula \(\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2}\right)\) The mid-point of \(PQ\) has \(y\)-coordinate \(\dfrac{p+q}{2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2\).

The mid-point $M$ of $PQ$ has $y$-coordinate $\dfrac{p+q}{2}$:

$$\frac{p+q}{2}=\frac{4}{2}=2$$

So $M=(x_M,\,2)$ — the $y$-value $2$ is fixed regardless of the $x$-value.

Answer:  $y$-coordinate of the mid-point $=2$  ⟹  Option C
STEP 6 · CHECK (GEOMETRIC VIEW)

The circle is $x^{2}+(y-4)^{2}=30$, centre $C(0,4)$, radius $\sqrt{30}\approx5.48$.

Solving $(*)$: $y=2\pm2\sqrt{2}$, so $p\approx4.83$ and $q\approx-0.83$ — both symmetric about $y=2$.

The mid-point $M$ lies on the line and is the foot of the perpendicular from $C$ to the chord $PQ$, confirming the $y$-coordinate $2$.

A. \(-3\)    B. \(1\)    C. \(3\)    D. \(5\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. From the line, \(x=\dfrac{y+5}{2}\). Substituting into the circle: \(\left(\dfrac{y+5}{2}\right)^{2}+y^{2}-10y-11=0\).
Step 2. Multiply by \(4\): \((y+5)^{2}+4y^{2}-40y-44=0\), i.e. \(y^{2}+10y+25+4y^{2}-40y-44=0\).
Step 3. Simplify: \(5y^{2}-30y-19=0\), so \(y^{2}-6y-\dfrac{19}{5}=0\) has roots \(p\) and \(q\) with \(p+q=6\).
Step 4. Mid-point \(y\)-coordinate \(=\dfrac{p+q}{2}=\dfrac{6}{2}=3\). Option C.
Answer: C. \(3\)
