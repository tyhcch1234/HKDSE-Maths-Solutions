2020MC#28 14. Probability

Two numbers are randomly drawn at the same time from four cards numbered $3$, $5$, $7$ and $9$ respectively. Find the probability that the product of the numbers drawn is greater than $33$.

  • A. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  • B. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
  • C. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
  • D. $\dfrac{3}{8}$
SOLUTION
1

Count the total number of outcomes

Two cards are drawn at the same time, so the order does not matter (a combination, not a permutation).

$$n={}^{4}C_{2}=\frac{4\times 3}{2}=6$$

The $6$ possible pairs are: $\{3,5\}$, $\{3,7\}$, $\{3,9\}$, $\{5,7\}$, $\{5,9\}$, $\{7,9\}$.

Hint: "at the same time" ⇒ use ${}^{4}C_{2}$, do not double-count.
Combination \({}^{n}C_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\) Two cards are drawn at the same time, so there are \({}^{4}C_{2}=6\) equally likely pairs.
2

Compute the product of each pair

$\{3,5\}: 15$    $\{3,7\}: 21$    $\{3,9\}: 27$
$\{5,7\}: 35$    $\{5,9\}: 45$    $\{7,9\}: 63$

Hint: only pairs containing at least two "large" cards can exceed $33$.
3

Find the favourable outcomes (product > 33)

$15,\ 21,\ 27$ are not greater than $33$.

$35>33$, $45>33$ and $63>33$, so exactly the three pairs $\{5,7\}$, $\{5,9\}$ and $\{7,9\}$ are favourable.

Favourable outcomes: $3$ (out of $6$).

Hint: compare each product with the threshold $33$.
4

Apply the probability formula

$$P=\frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$$

Hint: always simplify the fraction at the end.
Probability of Equally Likely Outcomes \(P=\dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}\) \(3\) of the \(6\) pairs give a product above \(33\), so \(P=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}\).
Answer: A  $\dfrac{1}{2}$ 3 favourable pairs out of 6 equally likely pairs

Question. Two numbers are drawn at the same time from four cards numbered \(2\), \(4\), \(6\) and \(8\). Find the probability that the product of the numbers drawn is greater than \(24\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total outcomes: \({}^{4}C_{2}=6\) pairs: \(\{2,4\},\{2,6\},\{2,8\},\{4,6\},\{4,8\},\{6,8\}\).
Step 2. The products are \(8,12,16,24,32,48\).
Step 3. Only \(32\) and \(48\) exceed \(24\), so there are \(2\) favourable outcomes.
Step 4. \(P=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{3}\)
