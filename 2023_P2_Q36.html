HKDSE 2023 · Paper 2 · Q36
Let \(T(n)\) be the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence. If \(T(11)=83\) and \(T(25)+T(30)=463\), find the least value of \(k\) such that \[T(1)+T(2)+T(3)+\cdots+T(k) > 4\times 10^{5}.\]
  • A. 299
  • B. 300
  • C. 944
  • D. 945
STEP 1
nth Term of an Arithmetic Sequence \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\) Writing each given term in terms of \(a\) and \(d\) turns the data into two linear equations.
Set up the two equations. Let \(d\) be the common difference and write each term as \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\) with \(a=T(1)\).
\[T(11)=83 \;\Rightarrow\; a+10d=83 \quad\cdots(1)\]
\[T(25)+T(30)=463\;\Rightarrow\;(a+24d)+(a+29d)=463\]
\[\Rightarrow\; 2a+53d=463 \quad\cdots(2)\]

Hint: two conditions ⇒ two linear equations in \(a\) and \(d\).

STEP 2
Solve for \(a\) and \(d\). From \((1)\): \(a=83-10d\). Substitute into \((2)\):
\[2(83-10d)+53d=463\]
\[166-20d+53d=463 \;\Rightarrow\; 33d=297 \;\Rightarrow\; d=9\]
\[a = 83-10(9) = -7\]

Hint: back-substitute to find the first term \(a=-7\).

STEP 3
Sum of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\left[\,2a+(n-1)d\,\right]\) The sum of the first \(k\) terms is a quadratic in \(k\), which the inequality is built on.
Write the sum formula. Using \(S(k)=\dfrac{k}{2}\big[\,2a+(k-1)d\,\big]\):
\[S(k)=\frac{k}{2}\big[\,2(-7)+(k-1)9\,\big]=\frac{k}{2}(9k-23)=\frac{9k^2-23k}{2}\]

Hint: this is the condition \(T(1)+T(2)+\cdots+T(k)\) expressed as a function of \(k\).

STEP 4
Form the inequality.
\[\frac{9k^2-23k}{2} > 4\times 10^{5} \;\Longrightarrow\; 9k^2-23k-8\times 10^{5} > 0\]

Hint: \(4\times10^{5}\times 2 = 8\times10^{5}\).

STEP 5
Quadratic Formula \(k=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\) The two roots of \(9k^{2}-23k-8\times 10^{5}=0\) locate where the sum first exceeds \(4\times 10^{5}\).
Solve the quadratic inequality. The roots of \(9k^2-23k-8\times10^5=0\) are
\[k=\frac{23\pm\sqrt{(-23)^2-4(9)(-8\times10^5)}}{18}=\frac{23\pm\sqrt{28\,800\,529}}{18}\]
\[k\approx -296.867\ \text{ or }\ k\approx 299.423\]
Since the parabola opens upward, \(9k^2-23k-8\times10^5>0\) when \(k<-296.867\) or \(k>299.423\).

Hint: keep the out-of-range root for completeness, but only the positive one is meaningful for a term index.

STEP 6
Take the least integer \(k\). Since \(k\) is a positive integer, \(k\ge 300\). Checking:
\[S(299)=\frac{9(299)^2-23(299)}{2}\approx 399\,173 < 4\times10^{5}\]
\[S(300)=\frac{9(300)^2-23(300)}{2}=400\,050 > 4\times10^{5}\]

Hint: verify the neighbouring integers to justify "least".

Answer: B  ( \(k=300\) )

Question. Let \(T(n)\) be the \(n\)th term of an arithmetic sequence. If \(T(8)=52\) and \(T(20)+T(24)=272\), find the least value of \(k\) such that \(T(1)+T(2)+\cdots+T(k)>50000\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. With \(T(n)=a+(n-1)d\): \(a+7d=52\ \cdots(1)\) and \((a+19d)+(a+23d)=272\), i.e. \(2a+42d=272\ \cdots(2)\).
Step 2. From \((1)\), \(a=52-7d\). Substituting into \((2)\): \(104-14d+42d=272\), so \(28d=168\), \(d=6\) and \(a=52-42=10\).
Step 3. \(S(k)=\dfrac{k}{2}\left[\,2(10)+(k-1)6\,\right]=\dfrac{k}{2}(6k+14)=k(3k+7)\).
Step 4. Solve \(3k^{2}+7k-50000>0\): \(k>\dfrac{-7+\sqrt{49+600000}}{6}\approx\dfrac{-7+774.58}{6}\approx 127.9\).
Step 5. Since \(k\) is an integer, \(k\ge 128\): \(S(127)=127\times 388=49\,276<50\,000\) and \(S(128)=128\times 391=50\,048>50\,000\).
Answer: \(k=128\)
