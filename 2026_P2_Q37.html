Linear Programming

Question

Consider the following system of inequalities:
\[ y\le 7,\qquad 3x-4y\le 2,\qquad 6x+13y\ge 25 \]
Let \(R\) be the region which represents the solution of the above system of inequalities. If \((x,y)\) is a point lying in \(R\), then the least value of \(5x+4y+28\) is

  • A. \(1\)
  • B. \(19\)
  • C. \(42\)
  • D. \(106\)

Solution

Vertices
Vertices of a Feasible Region \(\text{vertex}=\text{intersection of two boundary lines}\) Each corner of the polygonal region is where two of the boundary lines cross, so the three vertices \((10,7)\), \((-11,7)\) and \((2,1)\) are found by solving pairs of line equations.
\(R\) is the triangle with vertices \((10,7),\ (-11,7),\ (2,1)\) (intersections of the boundary lines).
Evaluate
Corner-Point Principle \(f(x,y)=ax+by+c\ \text{attains its extreme values at a vertex}\) A linear function on a polygonal region is optimised at a corner, so it is enough to evaluate \(f=5x+4y+28\) at the three vertices.
\(f=5x+4y+28\): at \((10,7)\to106\);
at \((-11,7)\to1\);
at \((2,1)\to42\).
Minimum
Comparing Values at the Vertices \(\min\{106,\ 1,\ 42\}=1\) The smallest of the three values is \(1\), attained at \((-11,7)\), so the least value of \(f\) on \(R\) is \(1\).
Least value \(=1\) (at \((-11,7)\)).
Answer: A — 1

Question. \(R\) is the triangular region with vertices \((2,1)\), \((11,1)\) and \((5,6)\). Find the least value of \(f=2x+3y+7\) for \((x,y)\) in \(R\). (3 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(f\) is linear, so by the corner-point principle its extreme values occur at the vertices of \(R\).
Step 2. At \((2,1)\): \(f=2(2)+3(1)+7=4+3+7=14\).
Step 3. At \((11,1)\): \(f=22+3+7=32\).
Step 4. At \((5,6)\): \(f=10+18+7=35\).
Step 5. The smallest of \(14,\ 32,\ 35\) is \(14\), so the least value is \(14\) at \((2,1)\).
Answer: \(14\) (attained at \((2,1)\))
