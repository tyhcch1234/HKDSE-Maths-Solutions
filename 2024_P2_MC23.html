In the figure, \(ABC\) is a straight line. \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\,?\)

A. \(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\)
B. \(\dfrac{\tan\phi}{\sin\theta}\)

Part (a) — Relating \(DC\) to \(AD\) 1M

Work inside the right-angled triangle \(ADC\) to express \(DC\) in terms of \(AD\) and \(\theta\).

Step 11M
Triangle \(ADC\) has a right angle at \(C\) (given). Taking the angle \(\angle DAC=\theta\), we have: \[ \sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}=\frac{DC}{AD} \]
Hint: In \(\triangle ADC\), the side opposite \(\theta\) is \(DC\) and the hypotenuse is \(AD\).
\[ DC = AD\sin\theta \tag{1} \]

Part (b) — Relating \(DC\) to \(BC\) 1M

Now work inside the right-angled triangle \(BDC\) using the angle \(\phi\) at \(B\).

Step 11M
Triangle \(BDC\) has a right angle at \(C\). With \(\angle DBC=\phi\): \[ \tan\phi=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}=\frac{DC}{BC} \]
Hint: Here \(DC\) is opposite \(\phi\) and \(BC\) is adjacent to \(\phi\).
\[ DC = BC\tan\phi \tag{2} \]

Part (c) — Combining (1) and (2) 1A

Equate the two expressions for the common side \(DC\) and solve for \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}\).

Step 11A
From (1) and (2), the side \(DC\) is the same: \[ AD\sin\theta = BC\tan\phi \] Rearranging to make \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}\) the subject: \[ \frac{BC}{AD}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi} \]
Hint: Divide both sides by \(AD\tan\phi\). Then the extra factor \(\cos\theta\) is not needed — the ratio involves only \(\sin\theta\) and \(\tan\phi\).
Comparing with the options, the ratio \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}\) does not simplify further just by combining the two equations alone — one must be careful! The correct manipulation follows below.
Step 21A
Wait — re-check by eliminating \(DC\) in a different way. From (1): \[ AD=\frac{DC}{\sin\theta} \] From (2): \[ BC=\frac{DC}{\tan\phi} \] Now form the ratio directly: \[ \frac{BC}{AD}=\frac{DC/\tan\phi}{DC/\sin\theta}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi} \]
Hint: This is the textbook "eliminate the common length" technique — the unknown \(DC\) cancels.
So \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\), which matches option A.
Step 31A
To confirm with actual measurements, move the sliders above and note that the numeric value of \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}\) always equals \(\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\).
Check: With \(\theta=30^\circ,\ \phi=35^\circ\), \(\dfrac{\sin 30^\circ}{\tan 35^\circ}\approx 0.714\), matching the canvas readout.
The correct choice is A.

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(DC = AD\sin\theta\)
  • Part (b): \(DC = BC\tan\phi\)
  • Part (c): \(\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\tan\phi}\) — Option A
  • Key idea: the common side \(DC\) links the two right-angled triangles \(ADC\) and \(BDC\).
