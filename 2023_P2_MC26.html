Locus of a point equidistant from two parallel lines

Coordinate Geometry Equation of Straight Lines Locus Perpendicular Bisector Angle in Semi-circle

QQuestion

The equation of the straight lines \(\ell\) and \(L\) are \(9x+12y-37=0\) and \(12x+16y+85=0\) respectively. \(\ell\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at the point \(A\) while \(L\) cuts the \(y\)-axis at the point \(B\). Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(\ell\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). Which of the following are true?

  1. \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\).
  2. \(\Gamma\) is perpendicular to \(AB\).
  3. \(\Gamma\) passes through the mid-point of \(AB\).

A.  I and II only     B.  I and III only     C.  II and III only     D.  I, II and III

1Step 1 — Identify the two lines and their slopes

slope of \(\ell\): –
slope of \(L\): –
dist(\(P,\ell\)): –
dist(\(P,L\)): –

Step 11M

Write each line in slope-intercept form \(y=mx+c\).

\[ \ell:\ 9x+12y-37=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 12y=-9x+37 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+\tfrac{37}{12} \] \[ L:\ 12x+16y+85=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 16y=-12x-85 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x-\tfrac{85}{16} \]
Hint: The slope of a line \(ax+by+c=0\) is \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\). Here both give \(-\dfrac{9}{12}=-\dfrac{12}{16}=-\dfrac{3}{4}\).
Slope of \(\ell\) = slope of \(L\) = \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\ell /\!/ L\).
Step 21M

Since \(\ell /\!/ L\), the locus of points equidistant from two parallel lines is the line parallel to both and lying exactly half-way between them.

\[ \Gamma:\ y=-\tfrac{3}{4}x+c \quad\text{where } c=\frac{\tfrac{37}{12}+\left(-\tfrac{85}{16}\right)}{2} \] \[ c=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{148}{48}-\frac{255}{48}\right)=-\frac{107}{96} \]
Hint: For parallel lines \(y=mx+c_1\) and \(y=mx+c_2\), the locus is \(y=mx+\dfrac{c_1+c_2}{2}\) — the "mid-parallel" line.
\(\Gamma:\ y=-\dfrac{3}{4}x-\dfrac{107}{96}\), i.e. \(72x+96y+107=0\). Since \(\Gamma\) has the same slope as \(L\), \(\Gamma /\!/ L\).
\(\Rightarrow\) Statement I is TRUE.

2Step 2 — Check whether \(\Gamma \perp AB\)

slope \(AB\): –
slope \(\Gamma\): –
product: –
mid-point \(M\) of \(AB\): –
Step 11M

Find \(A\) (where \(\ell\) cuts the \(x\)-axis, put \(y=0\)) and \(B\) (where \(L\) cuts the \(y\)-axis, put \(x=0\)).

\[ A:\ 9x-37=0 \;\Rightarrow\; A\left(\tfrac{37}{9},\,0\right) \] \[ B:\ 16y+85=0 \;\Rightarrow\; B\left(0,\,-\tfrac{85}{16}\right) \]
Hint: \(x\)-intercept: set \(y=0\).  \(y\)-intercept: set \(x=0\).
\(A\left(\dfrac{37}{9},0\right)\),   \(B\left(0,-\dfrac{85}{16}\right)\).
Step 21M

Compute the slope of \(AB\) and compare the product of slopes with \(-1\):

\[ m_{AB}=\frac{-\tfrac{85}{16}-0}{0-\tfrac{37}{9}}=\frac{-\tfrac{85}{16}}{-\tfrac{37}{9}}=\frac{85}{16}\times\frac{9}{37}=\frac{765}{592} \] \[ m_\Gamma \times m_{AB}=\left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)\left(\frac{765}{592}\right)=-\frac{2295}{2368}\approx-0.969 \neq -1 \]
Hint: Two lines are perpendicular \(\iff m_1 m_2=-1\) (when both slopes are finite). \(-0.969\) is close to \(-1\), but not equal — a classic distractor!
Since \(m_\Gamma \times m_{AB} \neq -1\), \(\Gamma\) is NOT perpendicular to \(AB\).
\(\Rightarrow\) Statement II is FALSE.

3Step 3 — Does \(\Gamma\) pass through the mid-point of \(AB\)?

Step 11M

Let \(C\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(L\). Then \(AC\perp L\) and \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AC\) (because \(\Gamma\) is the set of points equidistant from \(\ell\) and \(L\)).

Since \(\Gamma\) is not perpendicular to \(AB\), line \(L\) is not perpendicular to \(AB\), so \(C\) does not coincide with \(B\).

Hint: \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining any point on \(\ell\) to its foot on \(L\). Take the point \(A\) on \(\ell\).
\(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AC\).
Step 21M

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AC \perp L\) and \(BC\subset L\), so \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\).

By the converse of the angle in a semi-circle, \(AB\) is a diameter of the circle passing through \(A\), \(B\), \(C\).

Hint: If \(\angle ACB = 90^\circ\), then the circle with \(AB\) as diameter passes through \(C\).
\(AB\) is a diameter of the circle \(ABC\).
Step 31A

The centre of circle \(ABC\) is the mid-point of \(AB\). But \(\Gamma\), being the perpendicular bisector of the chord \(AC\), must pass through the centre of the circle.

\[ \text{(perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre)} \]

Verify numerically: mid-point \(M\) of \(AB\) is

\[ M\left(\frac{\tfrac{37}{9}+0}{2},\ \frac{0-\tfrac{85}{16}}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{37}{18},\ -\frac{85}{32}\right)\approx(2.056,\,-2.656) \]

Check \(M\) on \(\Gamma: 72x+96y+107=0\):

\[ 72\left(\tfrac{37}{18}\right)+96\left(-\tfrac{85}{32}\right)+107 = 148-255+107=0\ \checkmark \]
Hint: Substituting the mid-point satisfies the equation — a nice algebraic confirmation of the geometric argument.
\(\Gamma\) passes through the mid-point of \(AB\).
\(\Rightarrow\) Statement III is TRUE.

✓Summary of Results

  • I. TRUE — Slopes of \(\ell\) and \(L\) both equal \(-\tfrac{3}{4}\), so \(\ell/\!/ L\) and \(\Gamma/\!/ L\).
  • II. FALSE — \(\Gamma\) is not perpendicular to \(AB\): \(m_\Gamma m_{AB}=-\tfrac{2295}{2368}\neq-1\).
  • III. TRUE — \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of a chord of the circle \(ABC\), so it passes through the centre, i.e. the mid-point of \(AB\).
Only I and III are true → the answer is B.
