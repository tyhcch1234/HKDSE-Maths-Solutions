Polar Coordinates and the Equilateral Triangle

Question

In a polar coordinate system, \(O\) is the pole. The polar coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((12,\,75^\circ)\) and \((12,\,135^\circ)\) respectively.

  1. Find \(\angle AOB\). (1 mark)
  2. Find the perimeter of \(\triangle AOB\). (2 marks)
  3. Write down the number of folds of rotational symmetry of \(\triangle AOB\). (1 mark)

Solution

Step 1 · Part (a): the angle at the pole \[ \angle AOB = 135^\circ - 75^\circ = 60^\circ. \]
Step 2 · Identify the type of triangle

Since \(OA = OB = 12\), \(\triangle AOB\) is isosceles with vertex angle \(\angle AOB = 60^\circ\). Its base angles are equal:

\[ \angle OAB = \angle OBA = \frac{180^\circ - 60^\circ}{2} = 60^\circ. \]

All three angles are \(60^\circ\), so \(\triangle AOB\) is equilateral, and therefore \(AB = OA = OB = 12\).

Step 3 · Part (b): the perimeter \[ \text{Perimeter} = OA + OB + AB = 12 + 12 + 12 = 36. \]
Step 4 · Part (c): rotational symmetry

An equilateral triangle is mapped onto itself by rotations of \(120^\circ\), \(240^\circ\) and \(360^\circ\) about its centre, so it has 3 folds of rotational symmetry.

Final Answer: (a) \(\angle AOB = 60^\circ\)   (b) perimeter \(= 36\)   (c) \(3\) folds of rotational symmetry.
