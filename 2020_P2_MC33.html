Topic 8 · Laws of Indices, Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

indices exponential reflection

The figure shows the graph of \( y = a^x \) and the graph of \( y = b^x \) on the same rectangular coordinate system, where \(a\) and \(b\) are positive constants. If the graph of \( y = a^x \) is the reflection image of the graph of \( y = b^x \) with respect to the \(y\)-axis, which of the following are true?

I. \( a < 1 \) II. \( b > 1 \) III. \( ab = 1 \)
A. I and II only B. I and III only C. II and III only D. I, II and III

🧩 Step-by-step reasoning

The graph of \(y = a^x\) is the reflection image of \(y = b^x\) across the \(y\)-axis.

A reflection across the \(y\)-axis replaces \(x\) with \(-x\). So the equation of the reflected curve is:

\( y = b^{-x} \)

But this reflected curve is exactly \(y = a^x\). Hence

\( a^x = b^{-x} \)

This must hold for all real \(x\). Setting \(x = 1\) gives the key relationship \( a = b^{-1} \) or \( ab = 1 \).

From the graph, \(y=b^x\) is upward sloping (increasing). For an exponential function \(y = b^x\) with \(b>0\), it is increasing if and only if \(b > 1\).

Thus \(b > 1\).

Since \(ab = 1\) and \(b > 1\), we have \(a = \frac{1}{b}\). Because \(b>1\), \(\frac{1}{b} < 1\), so \(a < 1\). Also \(a\) is positive, so \(0 < a < 1\).

  • I. \(a < 1\) — We deduced \(a = 1/b\) with \(b>1\), so \(a < 1\). ✅ True.
  • II. \(b > 1\) — The graph of \(y=b^x\) is increasing, so \(b > 1\). ✅ True.
  • III. \(ab = 1\) — Directly from the reflection condition. ✅ True.

All three statements I, II and III are true.

Therefore the correct option is D (I, II and III).

💡

Key hint

For an exponential function \(f(x) = k^x\) with \(k>0\):
• If \(k > 1\), the graph is increasing (rises to the right).
• If \(0 < k < 1\), the graph is decreasing (falls to the right).
Reflection across \(y\)-axis swaps \(x\) with \(-x\), leading to \(a = b^{-1}\).
