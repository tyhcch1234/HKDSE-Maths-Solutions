Question [2015 MC #33]

\[ 11 + 2^{6} + 2^{10} + 2^{11} = \;? \]
A.\(110001001011_{2}\)
B.\(110100100011_{2}\)
C.\(110000100101_{2}\)
D.\(110100100001_{2}\)

💡 Idea: A number written in base 2 is just a sum of powers of 2 — each power that appears gives a "1", and each power that is missing gives a "0".

Step-by-step Solution

Split the whole number \(11\) into powers of 2. Remember \(11 = 8 + 2 + 1\).

\[ 11 = 2^3 + 2^1 + 2^0 \]
Hint: \(2^0=1,\;2^1=2,\;2^3=8\). Check: \(8+2+1=11\). ✔

Substitute Step 1 back and simply list all powers of 2 present:

\[ 11 + 2^{6} + 2^{10} + 2^{11} = \bigl(2^{0}+2^{1}+2^{3}\bigr) + 2^{6} + 2^{10} + 2^{11} \]
Hint: Unlike the multiplication law \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\), addition of powers cannot be combined. Here the powers are all different, so no term simplifies further.

Arrange the exponents from 11 down to 0 to prepare the binary digits:

\[ = 2^{11} + 2^{10} + 2^{6} + 2^{3} + 2^{1} + 2^{0} \]

The exponents appearing are: \(11,\;10,\;6,\;3,\;1,\;0\).

For each exponent from 11 down to 0, write 1 if the power is present, and 0 if it is not:

\(2^{11}\)\(2^{10}\) \(2^{9}\)\(2^{8}\) \(2^{7}\)\(2^{6}\) \(2^{5}\)\(2^{4}\) \(2^{3}\)\(2^{2}\) \(2^{1}\)\(2^{0}\)
11 00 01 00 10 11
\[ = 110001001011_{2} \]

Compare with the options. The result \(110001001011_{2}\) matches option A.

Answer: A
