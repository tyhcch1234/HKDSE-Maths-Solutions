Coordinate Geometry — Completing the Square, Orthocentre & Concyclic Points

Completing the Square Vertex Form Translation of Graphs Orthocentre Perpendicular Slopes Concyclic Points Angle in Semicircle

📘 The Question

Let \(g(x)=3x^{2}-6kx+24x+3k^{2}-24k+55\), where \(k\) is a positive constant. Denote the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) by \(R\).

(a) Using the method of completing the square, express the coordinates of \(R\) in terms of \(k\). 2 marks

(b) The graph of \(y=h(x)\) is obtained by translating the graph of \(y=g(x)\) rightwards by 6 units and then downwards by 10 units. Denote the vertex of the graph of \(y=h(x)\) by \(S\). Let \(T\) be a point such that the orthocentre of \(\triangle RST\) is the origin.

(i) Express the coordinates of \(T\) in terms of \(k\). 4 marks

(ii) The coordinates of the point \(U\) are \((-5,5)\). Let \(V\) be a point lying on \(RS\) such that \(TV\) is perpendicular to \(RS\). It is given that \(S\), \(T\), \(U\) and \(V\) are concyclic. Find \(k\). 3 marks

Step 11M

Group the \(x\)-terms and factor out the leading coefficient \(3\):

\[ g(x)=3x^{2}-6kx+24x+3k^{2}-24k+55 \] \[ g(x)=3\Big(x^{2}-2kx+8x\Big)+3k^{2}-24k+55 \]
Hint
Collect the coefficient of \(x\): \(-6k+24=-2k+8\) after dividing by \(3\). Inside the bracket we have \(x^{2}+(-2k+8)x\).
Step 21M

Complete the square on \(x^{2}-2kx+8x = x^{2}+2(-k+4)x\):

\[ x^{2}+2(-k+4)x = \big(x+(-k+4)\big)^{2}-(-k+4)^{2} \] \[ = (x-k+4)^{2}-(k-4)^{2} \]

Substitute back:

\[ g(x)=3\Big[(x-k+4)^{2}-(k-4)^{2}\Big]+3k^{2}-24k+55 \] \[ g(x)=3(x-k+4)^{2}-3(k-4)^{2}+3k^{2}-24k+55 \]
Hint
\(-3(k-4)^{2}+3k^{2}-24k+55=-3(k^{2}-8k+16)+3k^{2}-24k+55=7\). The \(k^{2}\) and \(k\) terms cancel!
Step 31A

Hence:

\[ g(x)=3\big(x-(k-4)\big)^{2}+7 \]

Compare with the vertex form \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+c\), so \(h=k-4\) and the minimum value is \(7\).

Answer \[ R=\big(k-4,\;7\big) \]

Step 11M

The graph of \(h\) is \(g\) translated 6 units right and 10 units down. Each translation shifts the vertex the same way:

\[ S = \big((k-4)+6,\;7-10\big) \]
Coordinate of S \[ S=\big(k+2,\;-3\big) \]
Step 21M

Since \(O\) is the orthocentre of \(\triangle RST\), the line from a vertex to the opposite side is an altitude. Thus:

\[ RS \perp OT \qquad\text{and}\qquad RT \perp OS \]

Slope of \(RS\):

\[ m_{RS}=\frac{-3-7}{(k+2)-(k-4)}=\frac{-10}{6}=-\frac{5}{3} \]

Hence the slope of the altitude \(OT\) must satisfy \(m_{OT}\cdot m_{RS}=-1\):

\[ m_{OT}=\frac{3}{5} \]
Hint
Notice \(m_{RS}\) is a constant \(-\tfrac{5}{3}\), independent of \(k\) — because \(R\) and \(S\) always differ by the fixed vector \((6,-10)\).
Step 31M

Let the coordinates of \(T\) be \((5t,\,3t)\), because \(OT\) passes through the origin with slope \(\tfrac{3}{5}\).

Now use the other altitude condition \(RT\perp OS\):

\[ \text{slope of } OS = \frac{-3-0}{(k+2)-0} = \frac{-3}{k+2} \] \[ \text{slope of } RT = \frac{3t-7}{5t-(k-4)} \]

Perpendicularity gives the product \(-1\):

\[ \left(\frac{3t-7}{5t-(k-4)}\right)\left(\frac{-3}{k+2}\right)=-1 \]
Hint
This is exactly the relation in the official solution: \(\left(\dfrac{3t-7}{5t-(k-4)}\right)\left(\dfrac{-3}{k+2}\right)=-1\).
Step 41A

Solve for \(t\). Multiply both sides by \(-(k+2)\):

\[ \frac{3t-7}{5t-k+4}=\frac{1}{3}(k+2) \] \[ t=\frac{k^{2}-2k-29}{5k+1} \]

Substituting into \(T=(5t,3t)\):

Answer \[ T=\left(\frac{5(k^{2}-2k-29)}{5k+1},\;\frac{3(k^{2}-2k-29)}{5k+1}\right) \]
Hint
Move the \(k\)-slider — the diagram confirms \(RT\perp OS\) and \(RS\perp OT\) always hold, i.e. \(O\) is the orthocentre.

Step 1—

First find the slopes needed. Since \(V\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(T\) to \(RS\), and \(U=(-5,5)\):

\[ m_{SU}=\frac{5-(-3)}{-5-(k+2)}=\frac{8}{-k-7}=-\frac{8}{k+7} \]

Also, from part (b)(i) with \(T=(5t,3t)\) and \(t=\dfrac{k^{2}-2k-29}{5k+1}\):

\[ m_{TU}=\frac{3t-5}{5t+5}=\frac{3k^{2}-31k-92}{5k^{2}+15k-140} \]
Hint
Simplify \(m_{TU}\) by substituting \(t=\dfrac{k^{2}-2k-29}{5k+1}\) and combining fractions.
Step 21M

Since \(TV\perp RS\) and \(V\) lies on \(RS\), we have \(\angle SVT = 90^{\circ}\).

Because \(S,T,U,V\) are concyclic, opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so:

\[ \angle SUT = 180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}=90^{\circ} \]

Therefore \(SU\perp TU\), giving:

\[ m_{SU}\cdot m_{TU}=-1 \] \[ \left(-\frac{8}{k+7}\right)\left(\frac{3k^{2}-31k-92}{5k^{2}+15k-140}\right)=-1 \]
Hint
This is exactly the relation in the official solution: \(\left(\dfrac{-8}{k+7}\right)\left(\dfrac{3k^{2}-31k-92}{5k^{2}+15k-140}\right)=-1\). Move the slider to \(k=1\) and observe \(\angle SUT\) becomes a right angle.
Step 31M

Clear the denominator and simplify:

\[ 8(3k^{2}-31k-92)=(k+7)(5k^{2}+15k-140) \] \[ 24k^{2}-248k-736=5k^{3}+50k^{2}-35k-980 \] \[ 5k^{3}+26k^{2}+213k-244=0 \]
Hint
Bring everything to one side — the cubic in the readout shows the LHS. It should equal \(0\) at the answer.
Step 41A

Test \(k=1\): \(5+26+213-244=0\) ✓. So \((k-1)\) is a factor:

\[ (k-1)(5k^{2}+31k+244)=0 \]

The quadratic has discriminant \(31^{2}-4(5)(244)=961-4880=-3919<0\), so it has no real roots (and \(k>0\)).

Answer \[ k=1 \]
Hint
Set the slider to \(k=1\): the circle through \(S,T,U,V\) closes up and \(\angle SUT=\angle SVT=90^{\circ}\), exactly as required.

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a) Completing the square: \(g(x)=3\big(x-(k-4)\big)^{2}+7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\) vertex \(R=\big(k-4,\;7\big)\).
  • (b)(i) Translating \(R\) by \((+6,-10)\): \(S=\big(k+2,\;-3\big)\). With \(O\) the orthocentre, \(RS\perp OT\) and \(RT\perp OS\), giving \(T=\left(\dfrac{5(k^{2}-2k-29)}{5k+1},\;\dfrac{3(k^{2}-2k-29)}{5k+1}\right)\).
  • (b)(ii) Since \(TV\perp RS\), \(\angle SVT=90^{\circ}\). Concyclic \(\Rightarrow \angle SUT=90^{\circ}\), so \(SU\perp TU\): \(5k^{3}+26k^{2}+213k-244=0\), i.e. \((k-1)(5k^{2}+31k+244)=0\). Hence \(k=1\).
