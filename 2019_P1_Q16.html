Question

Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be real numbers such that

$\beta = 5\alpha - 18 \quad \text{and} \quad \beta = \alpha^{2} - 13\alpha + 63$.

(a) Find $\alpha$ and $\beta$. [2 marks]

(b) The 1st term and the 2nd term of an arithmetic sequence are $\log \alpha$ and $\log \beta$ respectively. Find the least value of $n$ such that the sum of the first $n$ terms of the sequence is greater than $888$. [4 marks]

Step 1Set up the quadratic equation.
Solving by Substitution \(\beta=5\alpha-18,\quad \beta=\alpha^{2}-13\alpha+63\) Equating the two expressions for \(\beta\) leaves one quadratic in \(\alpha\), namely \(\alpha^{2}-18\alpha+81=0\).

Putting $\beta = 5\alpha - 18$ into $\beta = \alpha^{2} - 13\alpha + 63$:

$5\alpha - 18 = \alpha^{2} - 13\alpha + 63$

$\alpha^{2} - 18\alpha + 81 = 0$

Equalise the two expressions for $\beta$ — a quadratic in $\alpha$ appears.
Step 2Solve for $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Recognising a perfect square: $\alpha^{2} - 18\alpha + 81 = (\alpha - 9)^{2} = 0$, so

$\alpha = 9$

Then $\beta = 5(9) - 18 = 45 - 18 = 27$.

Answer (a): $\alpha = 9$, $\beta = 27$.
$\alpha = 9 = 3^{2}$ and $\beta = 27 = 3^{3}$ — powers of 3 will simplify the logs.
Step 3Identify the arithmetic sequence.

Let $T(n)$ be the $n$th term.

$T(1) = \log 9 = \log 3^{2} = 2\log 3$

$T(2) = \log 27 = \log 3^{3} = 3\log 3$

Hence the common difference is $d = T(2) - T(1) = \log 3$.

Write all terms in the form $(n+1)\log 3$.
Step 4Write down the sum of the first $n$ terms.
Sum of the First n Terms of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl[2a+(n-1)d\bigr]\) With \(a=2\log 3\) and \(d=\log 3\), the sum is \(\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl[4\log 3+(n-1)\log 3\bigr]\).

Sum of the first $n$ terms $> 888$:

$T(1) + T(2) + T(3) + \cdots + T(n) > 888$

$2\log 3 + 3\log 3 + 4\log 3 + \cdots + (n+1)\log 3 > 888$

Using the arithmetic sum formula $S(n) = \dfrac{n}{2}\big[2a + (n-1)d\big]$ with $a = 2\log 3$, $d = \log 3$:

$\dfrac{n}{2}\big[2(2\log 3) + (n-1)\log 3\big] > 888$

Factor out $\log 3$ before expanding.
Step 5Simplify to a quadratic inequality in $n$.

$(\log 3)\,n^{2} + (3\log 3)\,n - 1776 > 0$

(Multiplying both sides by 2 and dividing by $\log 3 > 0$.)

$\log 3$ is positive, so the inequality direction is unchanged.
Step 6Solve and interpret.
Solving a Quadratic Inequality \(n^{2}+3n-\dfrac{1776}{\log 3}>0\) The positive root \(59.53\) is the threshold, so the least integer \(n\) is \(60\).

Solving $(\log 3)n^{2} + (3\log 3)n - 1776 = 0$ gives

$n \approx -62.52928981 \quad \text{or} \quad n \approx 59.52928981$

Since $n$ is a positive integer, we need $n > 59.52928981$, so $n \ge 60$.

Sum exceeds 888 only after $n$ passes the positive root.
Answer: (a) $\alpha = 9$, $\beta = 27$  |  (b) The least value of $n$ is $60$.

Question. Let \(\beta=5\alpha-24\) and \(\beta=\alpha^{2}-11\alpha+40\).
(a) Find \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).
(b) An arithmetic sequence has first term \(T(1)=\log\alpha\) and second term \(T(2)=\log\beta\). Find the least value of \(n\) such that the sum of the first \(n\) terms exceeds \(900\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) \(5\alpha-24=\alpha^{2}-11\alpha+40\), so \(\alpha^{2}-16\alpha+64=0\), i.e. \((\alpha-8)^{2}=0\). Hence \(\alpha=8\).
Step 2. Then \(\beta=5(8)-24=16\); note \(\alpha=8=2^{3}\) and \(\beta=16=2^{4}\).
Step 3. (b) \(T(1)=3\log 2\), \(T(2)=4\log 2\), so \(d=\log 2\) and \(T(n)=(n+2)\log 2\).
Step 4. \(S(n)=\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl[6\log 2+(n-1)\log 2\bigr]=\dfrac{n(n+5)}{2}\log 2\).
Step 5. Require \(\dfrac{n(n+5)}{2}\log 2>900\), i.e. \(n(n+5)>\dfrac{1800}{\log 2}\approx 5979.4\). Since \(74\times 79=5846\) and \(75\times 80=6000\), the least value is \(n=75\).
Answer: (a) \(\alpha=8,\ \beta=16\); (b) \(n=75\)
