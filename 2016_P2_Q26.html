Question

[2016MC#26]
The coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$ are $(9,-2)$ and $(-1,8)$ respectively. If $C$ is a point lying on the straight line $x-2y=0$ such that $AC=BC$, then the $x$-coordinate of $C$ is
A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Solution

Step 1 · Parametrise point C

Point $C$ lies on the straight line $x-2y=0$, i.e. $x=2y$.

Write $y=c$ (a real number). Then

$$C=(2c,\;c)$$
Parametric Form of a Line \(x=2y:\quad \text{put }y=c\ \Longrightarrow\ C=(2c,\ c)\) Every point of \(x-2y=0\) is written with one letter \(c\), reducing the problem to one unknown.
Step 2 · Use the condition AC = BC

Given $A(9,-2)$, $B(-1,8)$ and $C(2c,c)$, the condition $AC=BC$ gives

$$AC^{2}=BC^{2}$$
$$(2c-9)^{2}+\bigl(c-(-2)\bigr)^{2}=\bigl(2c-(-1)\bigr)^{2}+(c-8)^{2}$$
Distance Formula \(d^{2}=(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}\) Squaring \(AC=BC\) avoids square roots: \(AC^{2}=BC^{2}\) with \(A(9,-2)\) and \(B(-1,8)\).
Step 3 · Expand both sides
$$(4c^{2}-36c+81)+(c^{2}+4c+4)=(4c^{2}+4c+1)+(c^{2}-16c+64)$$
Step 4 · Solve for c

Cancel the common terms $4c^2$ and $c^2$ from both sides:

$$-36c+81+4c+4=4c+1-16c+64$$
$$-32c+85=-12c+65$$
$$-32c+12c=65-85$$
$$-20c=-20 \quad\Rightarrow\quad c=1$$
Solving a Linear Equation in One Unknown \(ac+b=0\ \Longrightarrow\ c=-\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a\neq 0)\) The quadratic terms cancel, leaving \(-20c=-20\), so \(c=1\).
Step 5 · Conclusion

Since $C=(2c,c)$ and $c=1$, we get $C=(2,1)$.

Therefore the $x$-coordinate of $C$ is

$$x_C = 2c = 2(1)=2$$
Hint / shortcut: The condition $AC=BC$ means $C$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. The midpoint of $AB$ is $(4,3)$ and the slope of $AB$ is $\frac{-2-8}{9-(-1)}=\frac{-10}{10}=-1$, so the perpendicular bisector is $y-3=1\,(x-4)$, i.e. $y=x-1$. Intersecting with $x=2y$: $2y=y-1 \Rightarrow$ wait, use $x=2y$ in $x=y+1$: $2y=y+1\Rightarrow y=1$, $x=2$.
Final Answer
The $x$-coordinate of $C$ is $2$  →  Option B
