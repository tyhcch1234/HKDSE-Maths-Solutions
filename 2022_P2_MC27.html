Coordinate geometry of a circle passing through the origin \(O\), \(P(10,-24)\) and \(Q(17,-7)\).
Circle through 3 points Diameter · angle in semicircle Centre = Midpoint Point inside a circle Circles \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\)

Q The Question

The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((10,-24)\) and \((17,-7)\) respectively. Let \(C\) be the circle which passes through the origin, \(P\) and \(Q\). Which of the following is true?
  1. \(PQ\) is a diameter of \(C\).
  2. The area of \(C\) is \(196\pi\).
  3. The point \((16,-9)\) lies inside \(C\).
  4. The centre of \(C\) lies on the straight line \(5x+12y=0\).

Step 11M
Compute the slope of \(OQ\) and of \(PQ\): \[ m_{OQ}=\frac{-7-0}{17-0}=-\frac{7}{17},\qquad m_{PQ}=\frac{-7-(-24)}{17-10}=\frac{17}{7} \]
Hint: To test for a diameter, check whether the two lines from \(O\) meet the chord at \(90^\circ\) — a right angle needs \(m_1 m_2=-1\).
Step 21M
Test the perpendicularity of \(OQ\) and \(PQ\): \[ m_{OQ}\cdot m_{PQ}=\left(-\frac{7}{17}\right)\left(\frac{17}{7}\right)=-1 \] So \(\angle OQP = 90^\circ\).
Hint: Note this shows the angle at \(Q\), not at \(O\). A diameter condition requires the angle at the third point \(O\) to be a right angle.
Step 3Conclusion
Check the slopes at the origin instead: \[ m_{OP}\cdot m_{OQ}=\left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)\left(-\frac{7}{17}\right)=\frac{84}{85}\neq -1 \] So \(\angle POQ\neq 90^\circ\), and by the converse of the angle in a semicircle, \(PQ\) is not a diameter of \(C\).
Option A is FALSE ✗ — \(PQ\) is not a diameter.

Step 11M
Because \(\angle OQP = 90^\circ\), \(OP\) is a diameter of \(C\). Hence \[ r=\frac{OP}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{(10-0)^2+(-24-0)^2}}{2}=\frac{\sqrt{100+576}}{2}=\frac{26}{2}=13. \]
Hint: \(OP\) is the hypotenuse of the right triangle with legs \(10\) and \(24\) — a \(5\text{-}12\text{-}13\) triangle scaled by 2, so \(OP=26\).
Step 21M
Compute the actual area: \[ \text{Area of } C = \pi(13)^2 = 169\pi. \]
Hint: The option says \(196\pi = \pi(14)^2\), i.e. it assumes radius \(14\). But the true radius is \(13\).
Step 3Conclusion
\[ 169\pi \neq 196\pi. \]
Option B is FALSE ✗ — the area is \(169\pi\), not \(196\pi\).

Step 11M
Since \(OP\) is a diameter of \(C\), the centre of \(C\) is the midpoint of \(OP\): \[ \text{Centre}=\left(\frac{0+10}{2},\ \frac{0+(-24)}{2}\right)=(5,-12). \]
Hint: The centre of a circle is always the midpoint of any diameter — here the diameter is the segment \(OP\).
Step 21M
Find the distance from the centre \((5,-12)\) to the point \((16,-9)\): \[ d=\sqrt{(16-5)^2+(-9-(-12))^2}=\sqrt{11^2+3^2}=\sqrt{121+9}=\sqrt{130}. \]
Hint: Don't forget to keep the sign in \(y-(-12)\), which gives \(+3\) under the square root.
Step 31M
Compare with the radius: \[ \sqrt{130}\approx 11.40 < 13 = r. \]
Hint: A point is inside the circle if its distance to the centre is less than the radius.
Step 4Conclusion
Since \(\sqrt{130} < 13\), the point \((16,-9)\) lies strictly inside \(C\).
Option C is TRUE ✓ — \((16,-9)\) lies inside \(C\).

Step 11M
Substitute \((x,y)=(5,-12)\) into \(5x+12y=0\): \[ \text{L.H.S.}=5(5)+12(-12)=25-144=-119,\qquad \text{R.H.S.}=0. \]

★ Summary of the whole solution

  • Slopes: \(m_{OP}=-12/5\), \(m_{OQ}=-7/17\) and \(m_{PQ}=17/7\). Since \(m_{OQ}\cdot m_{PQ}=-1\), \(\angle OQP=90^\circ\).
  • By the converse of the angle in a semicircle, \(OP\) is a diameter of \(C\). (So A is false.)
  • Radius: \(r=\dfrac{OP}{2}=\dfrac{26}{2}=13\), so area of \(C=\pi(13)^2=169\pi\neq 196\pi\). (So B is false.)
  • Centre of \(C\) = midpoint of \(OP\) = \(\left(\dfrac{0+10}{2},\dfrac{0-24}{2}\right)=\left(5,-12\right)\).
  • Distance from \((5,-12)\) to \((16,-9)\): \(\sqrt{11^2+3^2}=\sqrt{130}\approx 11.40<13\).
  • Therefore the point \((16,-9)\) lies inside \(C\). The answer is C.
  • Also \(5(5)+12(-12)=-119\neq 0\), confirming D is false.
