Question. The solution of \(7-3x>1\) or \(x>3\) is

  • A. \(x<2\)
  • B. \(x>3\)
  • C. \(x<2\) or \(x>3\)
  • D. \(2
1 Start with the given inequality

\[ 7-3x>1 \]

This is the first condition of the compound statement.

Hint: isolate the \(x\)-term first — subtract \(7\) from both sides.
2 Rearrange the constant

\[ 7-3x>1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad -3x>1-7=-6 \]

So we get the intermediate inequality \(-3x>-6\).

Hint: subtracting the same number from both sides keeps the inequality direction unchanged.
Adding or Subtracting on Both Sides \(a>b \Rightarrow a-c>b-c\) Subtracting \(7\) from both sides of \(7-3x>1\) gives \(-3x>-6\), keeping the direction.
3 Divide by a negative number (reverse the sign)

\[ -3x>-6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x<\frac{-6}{-3}=2 \]

Dividing by \(-3\) flips the inequality sign: \(x<2\).

Hint: multiplying/dividing by a negative number reverses \(>\) and \(<\).
Reversing the Inequality Sign \(-ax>-b \Rightarrow x<\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a>0)\) Dividing \(-3x>-6\) by the negative number \(-3\) turns \(>\) into \(<\), so \(x<2\).
4 Combine with the second condition

The full statement is \(7-3x>1\) or \(x>3\).

From step 3, \(7-3x>1 \iff x<2\). Hence the statement becomes:

\[ x<2 \quad\text{or}\quad x>3 \]

Hint: with “or”, the solution set is the union of the two regions.
Union of Solution Sets for "or" \(x<2 \text{ or } x>3 \iff x\in(-\infty,2)\cup(3,\infty)\) The word "or" keeps both rays, so the gap \(2

Answer: \(x<2\) or \(x>3\) — Option C.
