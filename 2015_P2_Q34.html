Quadratic Equations — Relations of Roots

QUESTION

Let $k$ be a constant. If the roots of the quadratic equation $x^{2}+kx-2=0$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=$

A.   $k^{2}+4$
B.   (this is the answer)
C.   $k^{2}-4$
D.   $(k-2)^{2}$
1 Identify the coefficients

Compare $x^{2}+kx-2=0$ with the standard form $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$:

$$a=1,\qquad b=k,\qquad c=-2.$$

Hint: read $a$, $b$, $c$ straight off the equation — the coefficient of $x$ is the whole $k$.

2 Apply relations of roots (sum)

For roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$: $\;\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{b}{a}$.

$$\alpha+\beta=-\frac{k}{1}=-k.$$

Hint: the sum of roots is MINUS b over a — don't forget the minus sign.

Sum of Roots of a Quadratic Equation\(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{b}{a}\)For \(x^{2}+kx-2=0\) we have \(a=1\) and \(b=k\), so \(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{k}{1}=-k\).
3 Apply relations of roots (product)

The product of roots is $\alpha\beta=\dfrac{c}{a}$.

$$\alpha\beta=\frac{-2}{1}=-2.$$

Hint: the product needs no extra sign change.

Product of Roots of a Quadratic Equation\(\alpha\beta=\dfrac{c}{a}\)Here \(c=-2\) and \(a=1\), so the product is \(-2\) with no extra sign change.
4 Use the identity

We want $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$, so use the standard identity

$$\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta.$$

Hint: this identity converts an expression in $\alpha^2,\beta^2$ into the sum and product you already know.

Sum of Squares of Roots\(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta\)It converts \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\) into the sum \(-k\) and product \(-2\) already found.
5 Substitute and simplify

$$\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta$$

$$=(-k)^{2}-2(-2)$$

$$=k^{2}+4.$$

Hint: $(-k)^2=k^2$ and $-2(-2)=+4$.

Answer: $\;\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=k^{2}+4\;$  →  Option A
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the roots of \(x^{2}-7x+3=0\), find \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Match with \(ax^{2}+bx+c=0\): \(a=1\), \(b=-7\), \(c=3\).
Step 2. Sum of roots: \(\alpha+\beta=-\dfrac{-7}{1}=7\).
Step 3. Product of roots: \(\alpha\beta=\dfrac{3}{1}=3\).
Step 4. \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta=7^{2}-2(3)=49-6=43\).
Answer: \(43\)
