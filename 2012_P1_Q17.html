Coordinate Geometry — Equation of a Circle & Mid-point of a Chord

Circle Equation Tangency Condition Line ∩ Circle Sum of Roots Mid-point of Chord

Question 7 marks

The coordinates of the centre of the circle \(C\) are \((6,\,10)\). It is given that the \(x\)-axis is a tangent to \(C\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). 2 marks

(b) The slope and the \(y\)-intercept of the straight line \(L\) is \(-1\) and \(k\) respectively. If \(L\) cuts \(C\) at \(A\) and \(B\), express the coordinates of the mid-point of \(AB\) in terms of \(k\). 5 marks

Part (a) — Find the equation of \(C\) 2 marks

10
Centre = (6, 10) Distance centre→x-axis = 10 Tangency ⟺ r = 10 Equation: (x−6)²+(y−10)²=100

Drag the slider: the circle is tangent to the \(x\)-axis only when the radius equals the vertical distance from the centre to the \(x\)-axis, i.e. \(r=10\).

1Read off the radius from the tangency condition1M

Since the \(x\)-axis is a tangent to \(C\), the perpendicular distance from the centre \((6,10)\) to the \(x\)-axis equals the radius.

\[ r = 10 - 0 = 10 \]
Hint: A tangent line touches the circle at exactly one point; the radius drawn to that point is perpendicular to the tangent. For the \(x\)-axis, that perpendicular is vertical.
2Write the centre–radius form1A

With centre \((6,10)\) and radius \(10\):

\[ (x-6)^2+(y-10)^2 = 10^2 \]
Hint: Standard form: \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\) with centre \((h,k)\).
3Simplify & check the tangency1A

Expanding gives the general form \(x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0\). Check tangency by substituting \(y=0\):

\[ (x-6)^2+100=100 \;\Rightarrow\; x=6 \]

Only one intersection point \((6,0)\) — so the \(x\)-axis is indeed a tangent.

Equation of \(C:\; (x-6)^2+(y-10)^2 = 100\)  (i.e. \(x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0\))

Part (b) — Mid-point of chord \(AB\) 5 marks

14
L: y = −x + 14 A, B real ⟺ 0 ≤ k ≤ 20 M = (5, 9) Mid-point formula: ((k−4)/2, (k+4)/2)

The line \(L\) always has slope \(-1\). Moving the slider changes its \(y\)-intercept \(k\): the chord \(AB\) slides, and its mid-point \(M\) traces the straight line \(y=-x+10\) (the perpendicular from the centre).

0 / 5
1Write the equation of \(L\)1M

Slope \(=-1\), \(y\)-intercept \(=k\):

\[ y = -x+k \]
Hint: Slope–intercept form \(y=mx+c\) with \(m=-1\), \(c=k\).
2Substitute into the circle equation1M

Put \(y=-x+k\) into \(x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0\):

\[ x^2+(-x+k)^2-12x-20(-x+k)+36=0 \]
Hint: The two intersection points \(A\) and \(B\) correspond to the two roots of this quadratic in \(x\).
3Collect terms into a quadratic1M
\[ 2x^2+(8-2k)x+(k^2-20k+36)=0 \]

Divide by 2 (optional): \(x^2+(4-k)x+\dfrac{k^2-20k+36}{2}=0\).

Hint: Need real intersections: discriminant \(\ge 0\). Here \(\Delta = (8-2k)^2 - 8(k^2-20k+36) = -4(k^2-20k+36)\), so \(0\le k\le 20\) (check with the slider).
4Mid-point \(x\)-coordinate: use the sum of roots1M + 1A

If the roots are \(x_A,x_B\), then \(x_A+x_B=-\dfrac{8-2k}{2}=k-4\). Hence

\[ x_M=\frac{x_A+x_B}{2}=\frac{k-4}{2} \]
Hint: The mid-point of a chord has \(x\)-coordinate equal to the average of the two intersection \(x\)-coordinates — no need to solve the quadratic!
5Mid-point \(y\)-coordinate & final answer1A

Since \(M\) lies on \(L\), \(y_M=-x_M+k\):

\[ y_M=-\frac{k-4}{2}+k=\frac{k+4}{2} \]
Mid-point of \(AB\): \(\;\left(\dfrac{k-4}{2},\;\dfrac{k+4}{2}\right)\)

Sanity check: \(y_M+x_M=\frac{k+4}{2}+\frac{k-4}{2}=k\) ✓ (lies on \(L\)), and \(y_M-x_M=4\), i.e. \(M\) lies on the fixed line \(y=x+4\)? — better: from \(L\), \(M\) traces \(y=-x+10\) when \(x_M,y_M\) are expressed with a common parameter. Substituting \(k=2x_M+4\) into \(y_M\) gives \(y_M=-x_M+10\), the line through the centre with slope \(-1\). ✓

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Radius \(=10\); equation of \(C\): \(\;(x-6)^2+(y-10)^2=100\), i.e. \(x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0\).
  • (b) \(L:\; y=-x+k\). Substituting gives \(2x^2+(8-2k)x+(k^2-20k+36)=0\).
  • Sum of roots \(= k-4\), so mid-point \(x\)-coordinate \(=\dfrac{k-4}{2}\).
  • Mid-point \(y\)-coordinate \(=-\dfrac{k-4}{2}+k=\dfrac{k+4}{2}\).
  • (b) Answer: Mid-point of \(AB=\left(\dfrac{k-4}{2},\;\dfrac{k+4}{2}\right)\).
