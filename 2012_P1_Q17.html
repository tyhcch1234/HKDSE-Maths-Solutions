QUESTION

The coordinates of the centre of the circle $C$ are $(6,10)$. It is given that the $x$-axis is a tangent to $C$.

(a) Find the equation of $C$. [2 marks]

(b) The slope and the $y$-intercept of the straight line $L$ is $-1$ and $k$ respectively. If $L$ cuts $C$ at $A$ and $B$, express the coordinates of the mid-point of $AB$ in terms of $k$. [5 marks]

STEP 1 · RADIUS FROM THE TANGENT
Radius Perpendicular to Tangent \(OT\perp \text{tangent at }T\) So the perpendicular distance from the centre to the tangent \(y=0\) equals the radius.

The centre is $C(6,10)$. Since the $x$-axis (i.e. $y=0$) is a tangent to the circle, the perpendicular distance from the centre to the tangent equals the radius.

Radius $=\;$ (vertical distance from $y=10$ down to $y=0$) $=10$.

The tangent point is $(6,0)$ — it is directly below the centre, so the radius is vertical here.

Key idea: the radius is always perpendicular to a tangent at the point of contact.
STEP 2 · EQUATION OF $C$  —  PART (a)
Standard Equation of a Circle \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\) Centre \((h,k)\), radius \(r\). Expanded form: \(x^{2}+y^{2}-2hx-2ky+h^{2}+k^{2}-r^{2}=0\).

With centre $(6,10)$ and radius $10$, the standard form $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$ gives

$$(x-6)^2+(y-10)^2=10^2$$

Answer (a): $\;(x-6)^2+(y-10)^2=100$

(Expanded form: $x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0$.)

Question. The circle \(C\) has centre \((4,6)\) and is tangent to the \(x\)-axis. \(L\) is the line with slope \(-1\) and \(y\)-intercept \(k\), meeting \(C\) at \(A\) and \(B\). (a) Find the equation of \(C\). (b) Find the coordinates of the mid-point of \(AB\) in terms of \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Since the \(x\)-axis is a tangent, the radius equals the vertical distance from the centre to \(y=0\), i.e. \(r=6\).
Step 2. (a) \((x-4)^{2}+(y-6)^{2}=36\), i.e. \(x^{2}+y^{2}-8x-12y+16=0\).
Step 3. (b) \(L:y=-x+k\). Substituting: \(x^{2}+(x^{2}-2kx+k^{2})-8x+12x-12k+16=0\), i.e. \(2x^{2}+(4-2k)x+(k^{2}-12k+16)=0\).
Step 4. Sum of roots \(=-\dfrac{4-2k}{2}=k-2\), so \(x_{M}=\dfrac{k-2}{2}\).
Step 5. \(y_{M}=-x_{M}+k=\dfrac{k+2}{2}\). The mid-point is \(\left(\dfrac{k-2}{2},\dfrac{k+2}{2}\right)\).
Answer: (a) \((x-4)^{2}+(y-6)^{2}=36\). (b) \(\left(\dfrac{k-2}{2},\dfrac{k+2}{2}\right)\).
STEP 3 · WRITE DOWN LINE $L$

Line $L$ has slope $-1$ and $y$-intercept $k$, so its equation is

$$y=-x+k$$

STEP 4 · SUBSTITUTE INTO THE CIRCLE

Use the expanded circle $x^2+y^2-12x-20y+36=0$ and put $y=-x+k$:

$$x^2+(-x+k)^2-12x-20(-x+k)+36=0$$

$$x^2+(x^2-2kx+k^2)-12x+20x-20k+36=0$$

$$2x^2+(8-2k)x+(k^2-20k+36)=0$$

The roots of this quadratic are the $x$-coordinates of $A$ and $B$.

STEP 5 · $x$-COORDINATE OF THE MID-POINT
Sum of Roots of a Quadratic \(x_{1}+x_{2}=-\dfrac{b}{a}\) The roots are the \(x\)-coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\), so this gives \(x_{M}\) without solving.

For a quadratic $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with roots $x_1,x_2$: $\;x_1+x_2=-\dfrac{b}{a}$.

Here $a=2$, $b=8-2k$, so

$$x_M=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}=-\frac{b}{2a}=\frac{-(8-2k)}{2\cdot 2}=\frac{-(8-2k)}{4}=\frac{k-4}{2}$$

STEP 6 · $y$-COORDINATE OF THE MID-POINT
Mid-point Formula \(M=\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2}\right)\) Use \(x_{M}\) from the sum of roots, then find \(y_{M}\) from the equation of \(L\).

The mid-point lies on $L$, so its $y$-coordinate is $-x_M+k$:

$$y_M=-\frac{k-4}{2}+k=\frac{-(k-4)+2k}{2}=\frac{k+4}{2}$$

Alternatively: $y_M=\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}$ using the same root-sum idea — both give $\dfrac{k+4}{2}$.
STEP 7 · FINAL ANSWER  —  PART (b)

The mid-point of $AB$ is

$$\left(\frac{k-4}{2},\;\frac{k+4}{2}\right)$$

Notice the mid-point always lies on the line through the centre perpendicular to $L$ — a neat geometric check.
