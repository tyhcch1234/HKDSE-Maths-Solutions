QUESTION The Craftman's Solids

The base radius of the solid right circular cylinder \(X\) and the base radius of the solid right circular cone \(Y\) are equal. The heights of \(X\) and \(Y\) are \(20\text{ cm}\) and \(24\text{ cm}\) respectively. The volume of the solid right circular cone \(Z\) is equal to the sum of the volume of \(X\) and the volume of \(Y\). The base radius of \(Z\) is equal to the base diameter of \(X\). A craftsman finds that the volume of \(Y\) is \(800\pi\text{ cm}^3\).

(a) Find the base radius of \(Y\). [2 marks]
(b) Are \(Y\) and \(Z\) similar? Explain your answer. [3 marks]
(c) The craftsman claims that the sum of the curved surface area of \(X\) and the curved surface area of \(Y\) is greater than the curved surface area of \(Z\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

PART (a) Find the base radius of \(Y\)

r = 10 cm
🔎 Drag the slider: only \(r = 10\) makes the cone volume exactly \(800\pi\text{ cm}^3\).
Step 11M

Let \(r\text{ cm}\) be the base radius of \(Y\). Using the cone volume formula:

\[ V_Y = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 (24) = 8\pi r^2 \]
Hint: The slant height is not used for volume — only the vertical height \(24\) cm is needed.
Step 21M

Given the volume of \(Y\) is \(800\pi\text{ cm}^3\), set up the equation:

\[ 8\pi r^2 = 800\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad r^2 = 100 \]
Hint: Divide both sides by \(8\pi\). Radius must be positive, so take the positive root.
Step 31A

Since \(r>0\), we take the positive root:

\[ r = 10 \]
Answer: The base radius of \(Y\) is \(\boxed{10\text{ cm}}\).
Hint: Because \(X\) and \(Y\) have equal base radii, the base radius of \(X\) is also \(10\text{ cm}\).

PART (b) Are \(Y\) and \(Z\) similar?

🔎 Toggle the cones. For similarity of cones, the ratio of radii must equal the ratio of heights.
Step 11M

First find the volume of \(Z\), which equals \(V_X + V_Y\).

\[ V_X = \pi(10)^2(20) = 2000\pi\text{ cm}^3 \] \[ V_Z = 2000\pi + 800\pi = 2800\pi\text{ cm}^3 \]
Hint: \(X\) is a cylinder: \(V=\pi r^2 h\) (no \(\tfrac13\)).
Step 21M

Compare the base-radius ratio with the height ratio.

\[ \text{Base radius of }Z = \text{diameter of }X = 2\times 10 = 20\text{ cm} \] \[ \frac{\text{base radius of }Y}{\text{base radius of }Z} = \frac{10}{20} = \frac{1}{2} \] \[ \frac{h_Y}{h_Z} = \frac{24}{21} = \frac{8}{7} \]
Hint: Two cones are similar only if all corresponding linear dimensions are in the same ratio.
Step 31M

Use the volume–similarity test. If \(Y\) and \(Z\) were similar with linear ratio \(1:2\), then:

\[ \frac{V_Y}{V_Z} \overset{?}{=} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8} \] \[ \text{But } \frac{V_Y}{V_Z} = \frac{800\pi}{2800\pi} = \frac{2}{7} \neq \frac{1}{8} \]
Hint: For similar solids, volume ratio = (linear ratio)\(^3\).
Step 41A

The required condition fails, so the two cones are not similar.

Answer: \(Y\) and \(Z\) are not similar, because \(\dfrac{\text{base radius of }Y}{\text{base radius of }Z}=\dfrac12\) differs from \(\dfrac{h_Y}{h_Z}=\dfrac87\).
Hint: A complete answer must explain — quote both ratios or the failed volume test.

PART (c) Is the curved-surface-area claim true?

hZ = 21 cm
🔎 At \(h_Z = 21\), compare the two horizontal bars:
Step 11M

Curved surface area of the cylinder \(X\):

\[ \text{CSA}_X = 2\pi r h = 2\pi(10)(20) = 400\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: A cylinder unrolls into a rectangle of width \(2\pi r\) and height \(h\).
Step 21M

Curved surface area of cone \(Y\) needs its slant height \(l_Y=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}\):

\[ l_Y = \sqrt{10^2+24^2} = \sqrt{676} = 26 \] \[ \text{CSA}_Y = \pi r l = \pi(10)(26) = 260\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: CSA of a cone \(=\pi r l\), where \(l\) is the slant height, not the vertical height.
Step 31M

Find the height \(h\) of cone \(Z\) using its volume \(V_Z = 2800\pi\):

\[ \frac{\pi(20)^2 h}{3} = 2800\pi \] \[ \frac{400h}{3} = 2800 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h = 21 \]
Hint: Substitute \(V=2800\pi\) and \(r=20\) into \(V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h\).
Step 41M

Find the slant height and curved surface area of \(Z\):

\[ l_Z = \sqrt{20^2+21^2} = \sqrt{841} = 29 \] \[ \text{CSA}_Z = \pi(20)(29) = 580\pi\text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: \(20\!-\!21\!-\!29\) is a scaled Pythagorean triple (\(20,21,29\)).
Step 51A

Compare the sum for \(X\) and \(Y\) with the value for \(Z\):

\[ \text{CSA}_X + \text{CSA}_Y = 400\pi + 260\pi = 660\pi > 580\pi = \text{CSA}_Z \]
Answer: \(\text{CSA}_X+\text{CSA}_Y = 660\pi\text{ cm}^2 > 580\pi\text{ cm}^2 = \text{CSA}_Z\). The craftsman's claim is agreed.
Hint: Keep \(\pi\) in the answer — no need to multiply it out; compare coefficients \(660\) and \(580\) directly.

📌 Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Base radius of \(Y\) = \(\mathbf{10\text{ cm}}\)   (so \(r_X = 10\text{ cm}\)).
  • (b) \(Y\) and \(Z\) are not similar: \(\dfrac{r_Y}{r_Z}=\dfrac{1}{2}\) but \(\dfrac{h_Y}{h_Z}=\dfrac{8}{7}\), and \(\dfrac{V_Y}{V_Z}=\dfrac{2}{7}\neq\left(\dfrac12\right)^3\).
  • (c) \(V_Z = 2800\pi\text{ cm}^3\), so \(h_Z = 21\text{ cm}\) and \(\text{CSA}_Z = 580\pi\text{ cm}^2\).
  • (c) \(\text{CSA}_X = 400\pi\), \(\text{CSA}_Y = 260\pi\); sum \(= 660\pi > 580\pi\) → claim is agreed.
