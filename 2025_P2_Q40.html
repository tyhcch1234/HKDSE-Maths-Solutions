Angle between a line and a plane in a regular tetrahedron
3D Trigonometry Regular Tetrahedron Plane & Central Point Foot of Perpendicular Multiple Choice

Q The Question

\(PQRS\) is a regular tetrahedron. Find the angle between \(PQ\) and \(\triangle QRS\) correct to the nearest degree.

A. \(35^\circ\)
B. \(55^\circ\)
C. \(60^\circ\)
D. \(71^\circ\)

Setup: the base \(QRS\) is an equilateral triangle, \(P\) is the apex vertically above the centroid \(T\) of the base, and every edge has equal length.

1 Setting Up the Model Step-by-step

Drag the slider to change the edge length. Rotate the 3D view by dragging the canvas — the geometry stays fixed, only the viewpoint changes.

Edge \(=2x\)
Base altitude \(= x\sqrt{3}\)
\(QV = VS = x\)
\(\angle PQT \approx\) 55°
Step 1

Let every edge of the regular tetrahedron have length \(2x\). Label the apex \(P\) and the base \(\triangle QRS\).

Hint: "Regular" means all four faces are congruent equilateral triangles — every edge is equal, so we may use one symbol \(2x\).
Set each edge \(PQ=QR=RS=SQ=PR=PS=2x\). The angle to find is between the line \(PQ\) and the plane containing \(\triangle QRS\).
Step 2

By symmetry, the perpendicular from \(P\) to the base plane lands at the centroid \(T\) of \(\triangle QRS\). The angle between \(PQ\) and the plane \(QRS\) is the angle between \(PQ\) and its projection \(QT\), i.e. \(\angle PQT\).

\[ PQ \text{ and plane } QRS \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \angle PQT \]
Hint: The angle between a line and a plane is defined as the angle between the line and its perpendicular projection onto the plane. Since \(PT\perp\) plane, the projection of \(PQ\) is \(QT\).
Required angle \(= \angle PQT\).

2 Finding the Projection Length \(QT\) Step-by-step

Drag \(x\) and/or \(V\) along the median — check that \(QV = VS = x\) and that \(T\) always lies at the centroid.

\(QS = 2x =\) 4.00
\(QV =\) 2.00, \(VS =\) 2.00
\(QT =\) 2.31
\(\angle TQV =\) 30.0°
Step 3

Because \(\triangle QRS\) is equilateral, \(QT\) lies on the median from \(Q\), which bisects \(RS\) and also bisects \(\angle RQS = 60^\circ\). Hence \(\angle TQV = 30^\circ\) where \(V\) is the midpoint of \(QS\).

Hint: In an equilateral triangle the centroid lies on every median, and each median bisects the \(60^\circ\) vertex angle into two \(30^\circ\) angles.
\(\angle TQV = 30^\circ\), and \(QV = VS = x\) (half of the edge \(2x\)).
Step 4

In the right-angled triangle \(QVT\) (right angle at \(V\)):

\[ \cos 30^\circ = \frac{QV}{QT} \;\Longrightarrow\; QT = \frac{QV}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{x}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} \]
Hint: \(QT\) is the hypotenuse because the right angle at \(V\) is opposite to it. \(\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\), so \(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt3/2} = \dfrac{2x}{\sqrt3}\).
\(QT = \dfrac{2x}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}\,x}{3}\).

3 The Final Angle \(\angle PQT\) Step-by-step

Slide \(T\) along the base and watch the two lengths update. The ratio \(QT : PQ\) is always \(1 : \sqrt3\).

\(PQ = 2x =\) 3.00
\(QT = \dfrac{2x}{\sqrt3} =\) 1.73
\(\cos\angle PQT =\) 0.5774
\(\angle PQT =\) 54.7°
Step 5

In the right-angled triangle \(PQT\) (right angle at \(T\), because \(PT \perp\) base plane so \(PT\perp QT\)):

\[ \cos\angle PQT = \frac{QT}{PQ} = \frac{\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt3}}{2x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt3} \]
Hint: \(T\) is the foot of the perpendicular, so \(PT \perp QT\), making \(QT\) the adjacent side and \(PQ\) the hypotenuse. The \(2x\) cancels!
\(\cos \angle PQT = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.5774\).
Step 6

Therefore \(\angle PQT = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\right)\).

\[ \angle PQT \approx 54.7356^\circ \approx 55^\circ \]
Hint: \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} \approx 0.5774\), and \(\cos^{-1}0.5774 \approx 54.7^\circ\). Rounding to the nearest degree gives \(55^\circ\).
The angle between \(PQ\) and \(\triangle QRS\) is \(\boxed{55^\circ}\) — the answer is B.

Summary of the Solution

  • Let each edge of the regular tetrahedron be \(2x\).
  • Let \(T\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(P\) to the plane \(QRS\). By symmetry, \(T\) is the centroid of the equilateral base.
  • The required angle is \(\angle PQT\) — the angle between \(PQ\) and its projection \(QT\).
  • With \(V\), the midpoint of \(QS\): \(QV = x\) and \(\angle TQV = 30^\circ\), giving \[ QT = \frac{x}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{2x}{\sqrt3}. \]
  • Then \[ \cos\angle PQT = \frac{QT}{PQ} = \frac{\dfrac{2x}{\sqrt3}}{2x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}. \]
  • Hence \(\angle PQT = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\right) \approx 54.7^\circ \approx \mathbf{55^\circ}\).
  • Answer: B. \(55^\circ\)
