Estimation Approximation Error

Question

If \( 0.06557 < x < 0.06564 \), which of the following is true?

A. \( x = 0.065 \) (correct to 2 decimal places)
B. \( x = 0.065 \) (correct to 2 significant figures)
C. \( x = 0.0656 \) (correct to 3 decimal places)
D. \( x = 0.0656 \) (correct to 3 significant figures)

Step-by-step reasoning

\( x \) lies strictly between 0.06557 and 0.06564.

This means the possible values of \( x \) are all numbers from 0.06557 up to 0.06564 (not including the endpoints).

2 decimal places means we keep two digits after the decimal point: 0.06 | 557 (the third decimal digit is the first dropped digit).

The dropped digit is 5 (from 0.06557 → the thousandths digit is 5). Since it is ≥ 5, we round up: \( 0.06 \rightarrow 0.07 \).

So, correct to 2 d.p., \( x \approx 0.07 \).

Therefore A is false (it says 0.065).

Significant figures start from the first non-zero digit. For 0.06557, the first significant digit is 6 (the 2nd decimal place).

2 significant figures means we keep the digits 6 and 5: 0.065|57.

The next digit is 5 (the fourth decimal place). Since it is ≥ 5, we round the 5 up to 6: \( 0.065 \rightarrow 0.066 \).

So, correct to 2 s.f., \( x \approx 0.066 \).

Therefore B is false (it says 0.065).

3 decimal places means we keep three digits after the decimal point: 0.065 | 57.

The first dropped digit is the fourth decimal place, which is 5. Since it is ≥ 5, we round the 5 (in the third decimal place) up to 6: \( 0.065 \rightarrow 0.066 \).

So, correct to 3 d.p., \( x \approx 0.066 \).

Therefore C is false (it says 0.0656).

3 significant figures means we keep the first three non-zero digits: 6, 5, 5 in 0.06557.

The next digit after the third significant figure is 7 (the fifth decimal place). Since 7 ≥ 5, we round the last kept digit (5) up to 6: \( 0.0655 \rightarrow 0.0656 \).

So, correct to 3 s.f., \( x \approx 0.0656 \).

Therefore D is true.

Answer: D

\( x = 0.0656 \) (correct to 3 significant figures) is the only true statement.
