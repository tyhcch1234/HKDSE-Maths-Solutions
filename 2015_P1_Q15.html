Standard Score & Normal Distribution · Comparing Performance Across Examinations
Standard Score Normal Distribution Mean & Standard Deviation Comparing Performances

Question

The table below shows the means and the standard deviations of the scores of a large group of students in a Mathematics examination and a Science examination:

ExaminationMeanStandard deviation
Mathematics66 marks12 marks
Science52 marks10 marks

The standard score of David in the Mathematics examination is \(-0.5\).

(a) Find the score of David in the Mathematics examination.   [2 marks]

(b) Assume that the scores in each of the above examinations are normally distributed. David gets 49 marks in the Science examination. He claims that relative to other students, he performs better in the Science examination than in the Mathematics examination. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.   [2 marks]

Part (a) — Finding David's Mathematics Score

Idea: The standard score (z-score) measures how many standard deviations a score is above or below the mean:

\[ z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \]

For Mathematics: \(\mu=66,\ \sigma=12,\ z=-0.5\). Drag the slider to see how David's score relates to the distribution.

x = 42
0 / 3
1 1M Set up the standard-score equation
\[ \frac{x-66}{12}=-0.5 \]
Hint: The standard score formula is \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\). Substitute \(\mu=66\), \(\sigma=12\) and \(z=-0.5\).
2 1A Solve for \(x\)
\[ \begin{aligned} x-66 &= -0.5 \times 12 \\ x-66 &= -6 \\ x &= 60 \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Multiply both sides by \(12\), then add \(66\) to both sides. David's score is 6 marks below the mean of 66.
3 1A State the conclusion
Answer: David's score in the Mathematics examination is 60 marks.

Part (b) — Is David's Claim Correct?

Idea: A raw score cannot be compared across two different examinations — we must standardise both scores. The larger standard score means a better relative performance.

David's Mathematics standard score is \(-0.5\). Compute his Science standard score using \(\mu=52,\ \sigma=10,\ x=49\).

x = 49
0 / 4
1 1M Apply the standard-score formula to Science
\[ \begin{aligned} z_{\text{Science}} &= \frac{49-52}{10} \\[2pt] &= \frac{-3}{10} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: David is 3 marks below the Science mean of 52. Divide by the standard deviation \(\sigma=10\).
2 1A Simplify the standard score
\[ z_{\text{Science}} = -0.3 \]
Hint: The standard score is negative, so David is below average in Science too — but how far below? Compare \(-0.3\) with \(-0.5\).
3 1A Compare the two standard scores
\[ -0.3 > -0.5 \]
Hint: A standard score closer to \(0\) (less negative) means a better relative performance. David's Science z-score is greater than his Maths z-score, so he did relatively better in Science.
Comparison: \(z_{\text{Science}}=-0.3\)  vs  \(z_{\text{Maths}}=-0.5\), and \(-0.3 > -0.5\).
4 1A Conclusion on the claim
Answer: Yes, the claim is correct. Relative to other students, David performs better in the Science examination than in the Mathematics examination.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) David's Mathematics score is 60 marks. (From \(\dfrac{x-66}{12}=-0.5 \Rightarrow x=60\).)
  • (b) Science standard score: \(\dfrac{49-52}{10} = -0.3\).
  • Since \(-0.3 > -0.5\), David's standard score is higher in Science than in Mathematics.
  • Conclusion: Yes — his claim is correct; relative to other students he performs better in the Science examination.
