Mathematics Science David’s position
HKDSE 2015 · Paper 1 · Q15

Normal Distribution & Standard Scores

The table below shows the means and the standard deviations of the scores of a large group of students in a Mathematics examination and a Science examination:

ExaminationMeanStandard deviation
Mathematics66 marks12 marks
Science52 marks10 marks

The standard score of David in the Mathematics examination is $-0.5$.

(a) Find the score of David in the Mathematics examination.  [2 marks]

(b) Assume that the scores in each of the above examinations are normally distributed. David gets $49$ marks in the Science examination. He claims that relative to other students, he performs better in the Science examination than in the Mathematics examination. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.  [2 marks]

1Set up the standard‑score formula

For a score $x$ with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, \[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}.\] Mathematics: $\mu=66$, $\sigma=12$. David’s standard score is $-0.5$.

A negative standard score means David scored below the Mathematics mean.
2(a) Solve for David’s Mathematics score

\[\frac{x-66}{12}=-0.5\] \[x-66=(-0.5)(12)\] \[x=66-6=60.\]

David scores $60$ marks in Mathematics (below the mean of $66$).
3(b) Find David’s Science standard score

Science: $\mu=52$, $\sigma=10$, and David’s score is $49$. \[z=\frac{49-52}{10}=\frac{-3}{10}=-0.3.\]

Both standard scores are negative, but $-0.3$ is larger than $-0.5$.
4Compare and conclude

For Mathematics $z=-0.5$; for Science $z=-0.3$. Since \[-0.3>-0.5,\] David’s result in Science is closer to (i.e. less far below) the mean, relative to the spread of that paper. He therefore performs better in Science than in Mathematics relative to other students.

The larger standard score always indicates the relatively better performance.

Answer:

(a) David’s Mathematics score is $60$ marks.

(b) Science standard score $=-0.3>-0.5$ (Mathematics), so the claim is correct — David performs better in the Science examination relative to other students.
