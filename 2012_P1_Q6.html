Question 6  ·  [4 marks]

(a) Find the range of values of $x$ which satisfy both $$\frac{4x+6}{7}>2(x-3) \quad\text{and}\quad 2x-10\le 0 .$$

(b) How many positive integers satisfy both the inequalities in (a)?

1 Solve inequality (1):   $\dfrac{4x+6}{7}>2(x-3)$
Multiplication Property of Inequality \(A>B\;\Longrightarrow\;kA>kB\ (k>0),\qquad A>B\;\Longrightarrow\;kA Multiplying by the positive number \(7\) keeps the direction of the inequality sign unchanged.
4x + 6 > 14(x − 3)    (× 7 on both sides) 4x + 6 > 14x − 42    (expand) 6 + 42 > 14x − 4x 48 > 10x
Multiplying by the positive number 7 does not change the inequality sign. Keep $x$ on the right to avoid sign errors.
2 Rearranging inequality (1)

$$x<\frac{48}{10}=\frac{24}{5}=4.8$$

So inequality (1) gives $\;x<4.8$, i.e. $x<4.8$ — a strict inequality, so 4.8 itself is not included.

Answer must be a single simplified fraction: $\dfrac{24}{5}$.
3 Solve inequality (2):   $2x-10\le 0$
2x − 10 ≤ 0 2x ≤ 10 x ≤ 5
This is a non-strict inequality — the endpoint 5 is included.
4 Combine the two inequalities (part (a))
Intersection of Two Solution Sets \(x Both conditions must hold at the same time, so the stricter bound controls the answer.

Required:  $x<\dfrac{24}{5}$  and  $x\le 5$.

Since $\dfrac{24}{5}=4.8<5$, the condition $x<4.8$ is the stricter one, so it controls the intersection:

$$\boxed{\,x<\frac{24}{5}\,}$$

The valid region is the part of the number line common to both — everything strictly left of 4.8.
5 Part (b): count the positive integers

Positive integers satisfying $x<\dfrac{24}{5}=4.8$ and $x\le 5$ are the positive integers less than 4.8:

$$x=1,\;2,\;3,\;4$$

Number of such integers $=4$.

$x=5$ satisfies (2) but fails (1) since $5\not<4.8$. So the count stops at 4.

Answer

(a)  The required range is $\;x<\dfrac{24}{5}$  (i.e. $x<4.8$).

(b)  There are $\;4\;$ positive integers (namely $1,2,3,4$).
