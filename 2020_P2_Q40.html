Coordinate Geometry — In-centre of a Triangle

Interactive Figure

\(L_1:4x+3y=24\) \(L_2:4x-3y=24\) \(L_3:x=a\) Vertices \(A,B,C\) In-centre \(I\) Inscribed circle

Question

[2020MC#40]
The equation of the three sides of a triangle are \[4x+3y=24,\qquad 4x-3y=24,\qquad x=a,\] where \(a\) is a constant. If the \(x\)-coordinate of the in-centre of the triangle is \(31\), then \(a=\)
A. 15
B. 31
C. 45
D. 51

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 · Find the vertex \(C\) on the \(x\)-axis

Solve \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) together, i.e. \(4x+3y=24\) and \(4x-3y=24\):

Adding: \(8x=48 \Rightarrow x=6\), then \(y=0\).

So the two lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) meet the \(x\)-axis at \(C(6,\,0)\).

Step 2 · Symmetry about the \(x\)-axis

Rewrite: \(L_1: y=-\tfrac43x+8\), \(\;L_2: y=\tfrac43x-8\). The slopes are \(\pm\tfrac43\) — opposite in sign.

Hence \(L_2\) is the reflection image of \(L_1\) in the \(x\)-axis, so the triangle (with vertical side \(L_3:x=a\)) is symmetric about the \(x\)-axis.

Therefore the \(x\)-axis bisects \(\angle ACB\) and passes through the in-centre \(I\).

So the \(y\)-coordinate of \(I\) is \(0\).

Step 3 · The in-radius

The in-centre \(I\) has \(x\)-coordinate \(31\), so \(I=(31,0)\).

The vertical side is \(L_3:x=a\). Since \(I\) lies inside the triangle, \(a>31\).

The distance from \(I\) to the line \(x=a\) equals the in-radius:

\[r=|a-31|=a-31.\]

Step 4 · Use right triangle \(CIP\)

Let \(P\) be the point where the inscribed circle touches \(AC\) (i.e. \(L_1\)). Then \(IP\perp AC\) (tangent \(\perp\) radius).

Slope of \(L_1\) is \(-\tfrac43\), so \(\tan\angle PCI=\tfrac43\), giving \(\sin\angle PCI=\tfrac45\).

In \(\triangle CIP\): \(\;IC=31-6=25\), \(IP=r=a-31\), and

\[\frac{IP}{IC}=\sin\angle PCI=\frac45.\]

Step 5 · Solve for \(a\)

\[\frac{a-31}{31-6}=\frac45 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{a-31}{25}=\frac45\]

\[a-31=20 \;\Rightarrow\; a=51.\]

Final Answer \(a=51\)  →  Option D
💡 Key idea: because \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) have slopes \(\pm\frac43\), the \(x\)-axis is the angle bisector of \(\angle ACB\); the in-centre lies on it — so we only need a right-triangle ratio.
