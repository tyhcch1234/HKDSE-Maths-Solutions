Step 1 — Reading the given ratio.

HKDSE 2016 Paper 2 · Q11

Topic: Rate, Ratio and Variations

If $x$ and $y$ are non-zero numbers such that

\[(3y-4x):(2x+y)=5:6,\]

then $x:y=$

  • A. $7:8$
  • B. $8:29$
  • C. $9:32$
  • D. $13:34$
1Write the given ratio as a fraction

A ratio $a:b = c:d$ means $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$. So the given ratio becomes

\[\frac{3y-4x}{2x+y}=\frac{5}{6}.\]

Turn the ratio notation into an equation between two fractions first.
2Cross-multiply

Multiply both sides by $6(2x+y)$ (valid since $x,y\neq 0$ and $2x+y\neq 0$):

\[6(3y-4x)=5(2x+y).\]

Multiply each denominator across; keep the brackets.
3Expand both sides

\[18y-24x=10x+5y.\]

Careful with the sign of $-4x$: $6(-4x)=-24x$.
4Collect like terms

Move the $x$-terms to the left and the $y$-terms to the right:

\[18y-5y=10x+24x\]

\[13y=34x.\]

Check: each term changes sign when it crosses the equals sign.
5Form the ratio $x:y$

Divide both sides by $34y$ (allowed as $y\neq 0$):

\[\frac{x}{y}=\frac{13}{34}\]

Hence

\[x:y=13:34.\]

$\dfrac{x}{y}=\dfrac{13}{34}$ reads directly as the ratio $x:y$.
Answer: D  —  $x:y=13:34$
