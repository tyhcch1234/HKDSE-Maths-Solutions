Solving Quadratic Equations

Question

Solve \((x+2s)(x+t)=sx+st\), where \(s\) and \(t\) are constants.

  • A. \(x=-s\)
  • B. \(x=-2s\)
  • C. \(x=-s\) or \(x=-t\)
  • D. \(x=-2s\) or \(x=-t\)

Solution

Expand the left-hand side \[ (x+2s)(x+t)=x^2+tx+2sx+2st=x^2+(2s+t)x+2st \]
Expanding a Product of Binomials \((x+p)(x+q)=x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq\) \((x+3s)(x+t)=x^{2}+(3s+t)x+3st\).
Bring everything to one side \[ x^2+(2s+t)x+2st=sx+st \] \[ x^2+(2s+t-s)x+(2st-st)=0\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+(s+t)x+st=0 \]
Bringing All Terms to One Side \((3s+t)-2s=s+t,\qquad 3st-2st=st\) Subtracting the right side leaves \(x^{2}+(s+t)x+st=0\).
Factor and solve \[ (x+s)(x+t)=0 \]

Therefore \(x=-s\) or \(x=-t\), which is option C.

Factorising a Quadratic \(x^{2}+(s+t)x+st=(x+s)(x+t)\) The two roots are \(x=-s\) and \(x=-t\).
Final Answer: C
