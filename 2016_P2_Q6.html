HKDSE 2016 Paper 2 — Q6

Let $f(x)=4x^{3}+kx+3$, where $k$ is a constant. If $f(x)$ is divisible by $2x+1$, find the remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $x+1$.

A. $-7$
B. $-6$
C. $0$
D. $5$

Step-by-step Solution

1Use the given condition (divisibility).
$f(x)$ is divisible by $2x+1$ $\Rightarrow$ the remainder is $0$.
By the Remainder Theorem, put $2x+1=0 \Rightarrow x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$:
$f\!\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=0$
Divisible means the remainder is zero — so $f$ evaluated at the root of $2x+1$ equals $0$.
2Substitute $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ into $f(x)$.
$4\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{3}+k\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)+3=0$
$4\left(-\dfrac{1}{8}\right)-\dfrac{k}{2}+3=0$
$-\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{k}{2}+3=0$
Handle the cube of a negative carefully: $\left(-\tfrac12\right)^3=-\tfrac18$.
3Solve for the constant $k$.
$-\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{k}{2}=-3$
$-\dfrac{k}{2}=-3+\dfrac{1}{2}=-\dfrac{5}{2}$
$\boxed{k=5}$
Multiply through by $-2$ to clear the fractions quickly.
4Find the required remainder using $x+1$.
Now $f(x)=4x^{3}+5x+3$. For division by $x+1$, put $x=-1$:
$f(-1)=4(-1)^{3}+5(-1)+3$
$=-4-5+3=-6$
Remainder Theorem again: the remainder equals $f$ at the root of the divisor.
5Conclusion.
The remainder when $f(x)$ is divided by $x+1$ is $-6$.
This corresponds to option B.
Answer: B   ($-6$)
