Properties of a Rhombus — Diagonals, Right Angles & Pythagoras

Rhombus Diagonals Perpendicular Bisectors Pythagoras' Theorem Multiple Choice

? The Question

It is given that \(ABCD\) is a rhombus. Denote the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\) by \(E\). Which of the following must be true?

  1. \(AE = BE\)
  2. \(AE^2 + BE^2 = CD^2\)
  3. \(\angle AEB = 90^\circ\)

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

★ Key Rhombus Facts

Before testing each statement, recall the defining properties of a rhombus:

  • All four sides are equal: \(AB = BC = CD = DA\).
  • A rhombus is a parallelogram, so its diagonals bisect each other: \(AE = CE\) and \(BE = DE\).
  • The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular: \(AC \perp BD\).
  • Each diagonal bisects a pair of opposite angles.
Hint: Only the perpendicularity of the diagonals gives a right-angled triangle at \(E\) — that is the bridge to Pythagoras.

Step 1Reasoning
In a rhombus, the diagonals bisect each other, so \[ AE = \tfrac{1}{2}AC, \qquad BE = \tfrac{1}{2}BD. \] For \(AE = BE\) we would need \(AC = BD\), i.e. the diagonals equal.
Hint: Equal diagonals happen only for a rectangle (or a square), not for every rhombus. A long, thin rhombus has very unequal diagonals.
Step 2Counter-example
Take a rhombus with diagonals \(AC = 16\), \(BD = 12\). Then \[ AE = 8, \quad BE = 6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad AE \neq BE. \] The slider above shows \(AE \neq BE\) for almost every shape — only the degenerate square gives equality.
Conclusion: Statement I is NOT always true. ✗

Step 1Proof
In \(\triangle ABD\), since \(AB = AD\) (rhombus), the triangle is isosceles with apex \(A\).
Since \(BE = DE\) (diagonals bisect), the median from \(A\) to \(BD\) is also the altitude: \[ AE \perp BD \quad\Rightarrow\quad \angle AEB = 90^\circ. \]

Step 11M
By Statement III, \(\angle AEB = 90^\circ\), so \(\triangle AEB\) is right-angled at \(E\). Applying Pythagoras' theorem: \[ AE^2 + BE^2 = AB^2. \]
Hint: This is the classic "perpendicular diagonals ⇒ Pythagoras" step. Look at the blue right triangle in the diagram.
Step 21A
Since \(ABCD\) is a rhombus, \(AB = CD\). Therefore \[ AB^2 = CD^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad AE^2 + BE^2 = CD^2. \]
Conclusion: Statement II is always true. ✓

✓ Summary of the Answer

  • Statement I \((AE = BE)\): Not always true — only if \(AC = BD\). ✗
  • Statement II \((AE^2 + BE^2 = CD^2)\): Always true — Pythagoras in \(\triangle AEB\) with \(AB = CD\). ✓
  • Statement III \((\angle AEB = 90^\circ)\): Always true — diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular. ✓

Therefore the correct choice is II and III only.

Answer: C
