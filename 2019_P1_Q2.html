Topic 4: Polynomials — Simplifying algebraic fractions

Q

Question

Simplify the following expression. [3 marks]

\[ \frac{3}{\,7x-6\,} \;-\; \frac{2}{\,5x-4\,} \]

What is being tested?

Adding / subtracting fractions whose denominators are linear polynomials. The key skill is finding a common denominator and expanding carefully.

Watch out for

When you multiply, the minus sign must go with the whole numerator \(2(7x-6)\), not only with \(2\).

→

Step-by-Step Solution

Tap a step to reveal its working. Each step also has a hint for the formula used.

The expression has two fractions with different denominators:

\[ \frac{3}{7x-6} - \frac{2}{5x-4} \]
1st denominator: \(7x-6\) 2nd denominator: \(5x-4\)

Hint 💡

These two denominators are linear expressions. They share no factor, so the simplest common denominator is just their product: \((7x-6)(5x-4)\).

Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by the other denominator:

\[ \frac{3\color{#1d4ed8}{(5x-4)}}{(7x-6)\color{#1d4ed8}{(5x-4)}} - \frac{2\color{#b45309}{(7x-6)}}{(5x-4)\color{#b45309}{(7x-6)}} \]

Hint 💡

Equivalent fractions rule: \(\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{a \times k}{b \times k}\). Here \(k = 5x-4\) for the first fraction, and \(k = 7x-6\) for the second.

Now both fractions share the same denominator, so subtract numerators:

\[ = \frac{3(5x-4) - 2(7x-6)}{(7x-6)(5x-4)} \]

Hint 💡

The minus sign in front belongs to the entire numerator: it applies to both \(2\) and \((7x-6)\). This is why brackets are kept around \(2(7x-6)\).

Expand each bracketed product:

\[ = \frac{15x-12 - 14x+12}{(7x-6)(5x-4)} \]

First term

\(3(5x-4) = 15x - 12\)

Second term

\(2(7x-6) = 14x - 12\)

Hint 💡

Distributive law: \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\). Remember the minus in front changes the sign of both \(14x\) and \(-12\): \(-14x+12\).

Collect like terms in the numerator:

\[ 15x - 12 - 14x + 12 = (15x-14x) + (-12+12) = x \]

Final Answer

\[ \frac{x}{(7x-6)(5x-4)} \]

Hint 💡

Combine the \(x\)-terms: \(15x-14x=x\). The constants: \(-12+12=0\). They cancel exactly, leaving only \(x\) on top.

Key Takeaways

🧩

Common denominator

For two different linear denominators, multiply them together.

⚠️

Watch the sign

A minus in front distributes over the whole numerator — keep brackets!

✅

Check the answer

Substitute a value of \(x\) into both versions — they should match.
