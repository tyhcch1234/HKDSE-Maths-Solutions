QQuestion

[2017 MC #33] The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between \(x\) and \(\sqrt{y}\). Which of the following must be true?

  • A. \(y = x^{2} - 4x + 8\)
  • B. \(y = x^{2} + 4x + 8\)
  • C. \(y = 4x^{2} - 32x + 64\)
  • D. \(y = 4x^{2} + 32x + 64\)

The graph is a straight line plotted with \(x\) on the horizontal axis and \(\sqrt{y}\) on the vertical axis. It cuts the vertical axis at \(8\) and the horizontal axis at \(4\).

Vertical intercept ( \(x=0\) ): √y = 8
Horizontal intercept ( \(\sqrt y =0\) ): x = 4
Gradient \(m = \dfrac{0-8}{4-0}=\) -2

Step 11M

Locate where the line meets the vertical axis (the \(\sqrt{y}\)-axis):

\[ x = 0,\qquad \sqrt{y} = 8 \]
Hint: The vertical axis of this graph is \(\sqrt{y}\), not \(y\). Reading “8” means \(\sqrt{y}=8\), so \(y = 8^2 = 64\).
Step 21M

Locate where the line meets the horizontal axis (the \(x\)-axis):

\[ \sqrt{y} = 0,\qquad x = 4 \]
Hint: A straight line is completely fixed by two pieces of information. These two intercepts are exactly that information.

2Part (ii): Write the linear relation

Using the two-point form of a straight line with the points \((0,8)\) and \((4,0)\) in the \((x,\sqrt{y})\)-plane.

Gradient \(m = \dfrac{8-0}{0-4} =\) -2
Equation: \(\sqrt{y}=\) -2x + 8
1.50
Step 11M

Find the gradient of the line using the two intercepts \((0,8)\) and \((4,0)\):

\[ m=\frac{8-0}{0-4}=\frac{8}{-4}=-2 \]
Hint: Always keep the vertical quantity on top. Here the vertical quantity is \(\sqrt{y}\), giving the gradient of the line in the \(x\)–\(\sqrt{y}\) plane.
Step 21M

Apply the point-slope form through \((4,0)\), which is the cleaner point to use:

\[ \sqrt{y}-0=\frac{8-0}{0-4}\,(x-4) \] \[ \boxed{\sqrt{y}=-2x+8} \]
Hint: Check with the other point: putting \(x=0\) gives \(\sqrt{y}=8\) ✓. Putting \(\sqrt{y}=0\) gives \(x=4\) ✓. The relation is linear in \(x\) and \(\sqrt{y}\), not in \(y\).
Step 31M

The relation is valid only when the right-hand side is non-negative, since \(\sqrt{y}\ge 0\):

\[ -2x+8\ge 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\le 4 \]
Hint: This confirms the domain \(0\le x\le 4\) seen in the graph, and it will be reflected in the range of \(y\) later.
0 / 3

3Part (iii): Square both sides to get \(y\) in terms of \(x\)

Squaring the linear relation converts it to a quadratic relation between \(x\) and \(y\), then we compare with the four options.

Option A: \(y=x^2-4x+8\), at \(x=0\) gives \(y=\) A
Option B: \(y=x^2+4x+8\), at \(x=0\) gives \(y=\) B
Option C: \(y=4x^2-32x+64\), at \(x=0\) gives \(y=\) C
Option D: \(y=4x^2+32x+64\), at \(x=0\) gives \(y=\) D
1.00
Step 11M

Square both sides of \(\sqrt{y}=-2x+8\):

\[ y=(-2x+8)^{2} \]
Hint: \((-2x+8)^2\) is a square of a binomial. Never expand it as \(-4x^2+64\) — that is the classic trap (missing the middle term).
Step 21M

Expand using \((a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2\) with \(a=8,\ b=2x\) (or \(a=-2x,\ b=8\)):

\[ (-2x+8)^2=(2x)^2-2(2x)(8)+8^2 \] \[ =4x^2-32x+64 \]
Hint: The coefficient of \(x^2\) becomes \((-2)^2=4\), not \(1\) — this immediately eliminates options A and B. The middle term is negative because the gradient is negative, eliminating option D.
Step 31A

Hence the relation must be:

\[ \boxed{\,y=4x^{2}-32x+64\,} \]

This matches Option C.

Hint: Quick check: at \(x=4\), \(y=4(16)-32(4)+64=64-128+64=0\). And from the graph, \(x=4\) gives \(\sqrt{y}=0\Rightarrow y=0\). ✓
Answer: \(\;y=4x^{2}-32x+64\;\)  ⟹  C

✓Summary of Answers

  • Two points on the \(\bigl(x,\sqrt{y}\bigr)\)-plane: \(\;(0,8)\;\) and \(\;(4,0)\).
  • Gradient: \(\;m=\dfrac{0-8}{4-0}=-2\).
  • Linear relation: \(\;\sqrt{y}=-2x+8\;\) for \(x\le 4\).
  • Squaring: \(\;y=(-2x+8)^2=4x^2-32x+64\).
  • Correct option: C
  • Key trap: the graph is linear in \(\sqrt{y}\), not in \(y\); do not read “8” as the \(y\)-intercept.
