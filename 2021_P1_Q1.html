Simplify $\;(\alpha\beta^{3})(\alpha^{-2}\beta^{4})^{5}\;$ and express your answer with positive indices. [3 marks]
1
Power of a Power \(\left(x^{m}\right)^{n}=x^{mn}\) \(\left(\alpha^{-2}\beta^{4}\right)^{5}=\alpha^{-10}\beta^{20}\): the outer power 5 multiplies both indices.
Expand the bracket with the Power Law
$(x^{m})^{n}=x^{mn}$. Apply the outer power $5$ to both $\alpha$ and $\beta$ inside the bracket: $$(\alpha^{-2}\beta^{4})^{5}=\alpha^{(-2)(5)}\,\beta^{(4)(5)}=\alpha^{-10}\beta^{20}$$
Raise every factor inside the bracket to the power 5 — don't forget the negative sign.
2
Product Law of Indices \(x^{m}\cdot x^{n}=x^{m+n}\) For \(\alpha\): \(1+(-10)=-9\); for \(\beta\): \(3+20=23\).
Multiply the two powers and collect like bases
Using $x^{m}\cdot x^{n}=x^{m+n}$: $$(\alpha^{1}\beta^{3})\times(\alpha^{-10}\beta^{20})$$ $$\alpha\text{-powers: } (1)+(-10)=-9,\qquad \beta\text{-powers: } (3)+(20)=23$$ $$=\alpha^{-9}\beta^{23}$$
Same base ⇒ add the exponents (positive + negative).
3
Negative Index Law \(x^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}\quad(x\neq0)\) \(\alpha^{-9}\) is moved to the denominator, giving \(\dfrac{\beta^{23}}{\alpha^{9}}\).
Rewrite with positive indices
Since $x^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{x^{n}}$, move $\alpha^{-9}$ to the denominator: $$\alpha^{-9}\beta^{23}=\frac{\beta^{23}}{\alpha^{9}}$$
Only the negative-index factor is flipped; $\beta^{23}$ stays in the numerator.
Answer: $\quad\dfrac{\beta^{23}}{\alpha^{9}}$ All indices positive — as required.

Question. Simplify \(\left(\alpha^{2}\beta^{-3}\right)\left(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{2}\right)^{4}\) and express your answer with positive indices. [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Power of a power: \(\left(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{2}\right)^{4}=\alpha^{-4}\beta^{8}\).
Step 2. Product law: \(\alpha\)-powers \(2+(-4)=-2\), \(\beta\)-powers \(-3+8=5\), so the expression is \(\alpha^{-2}\beta^{5}\).
Step 3. Negative index: \(\alpha^{-2}\beta^{5}=\dfrac{\beta^{5}}{\alpha^{2}}\), with all indices positive.
Answer: \(\dfrac{\beta^{5}}{\alpha^{2}}\)
