HKDSE Math 2021 Paper 1 Q1

Laws of Indices Exponential Functions Positive Indices Algebraic Simplification

Question

Simplify \((\alpha\beta^3)(\alpha^{-2}\beta^4)^5\) and express your answer with positive indices.

[3 marks]

Key Concepts: Power of a Product Rule, Product Rule for Exponents, Negative Exponents, Positive Indices.

Step-by-Step Solution

Apply the rule \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\) to the term \((\alpha^{-2}\beta^4)^5\).

\((\alpha\beta^3)(\alpha^{-2}\beta^4)^5 = (\alpha\beta^3)(\alpha^{-2})^5(\beta^4)^5\)

💡 Hint: The power \(5\) applies to every factor inside the bracket: both \(\alpha^{-2}\) and \(\beta^4\).

Use the rule \((a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}\) to simplify the exponents inside the brackets.

\((\alpha^{-2})^5 = \alpha^{-2 \times 5} = \alpha^{-10}\)

\((\beta^4)^5 = \beta^{4 \times 5} = \beta^{20}\)

💡 Hint: Multiply the exponents: \((-2) \times 5 = -10\) and \(4 \times 5 = 20\).

Now multiply the two simplified parts: \((\alpha\beta^3)(\alpha^{-10}\beta^{20})\).

Apply the rule \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\) for each variable.

\(\alpha^{1 + (-10)} \cdot \beta^{3 + 20} = \alpha^{-9} \cdot \beta^{23}\)

💡 Hint: Remember \(\alpha = \alpha^1\). Add the exponents: \(1 + (-10) = -9\) and \(3 + 20 = 23\).

Convert negative exponent to positive by using the rule \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\).

\(\alpha^{-9} \cdot \beta^{23} = \frac{\beta^{23}}{\alpha^9}\)

✅ Final Answer:

\(\displaystyle \frac{\beta^{23}}{\alpha^9}\)

💡 Hint: The final answer must have only positive indices. Move \(\alpha^{-9}\) to the denominator.

Key Takeaways

  • Power of a Product: \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\)
  • Power of a Power: \((a^m)^n = a^{m \times n}\)
  • Product Rule: \(a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}\)
  • Negative Exponent: \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\) (to express with positive indices)
