Question 12.

The circle \(C\) passes through the point \(A(6,11)\) and the centre of \(C\) is the point \(G(0,3)\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). [2 marks]

(b) \(P\) is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(AP = GP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).

(i) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\).

(ii) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and the line segment \(AG\).

(iii) If \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at \(Q\) and \(R\), find the perimeter of the quadrilateral \(AQGR\). [5 marks]

STEP 1 · RADIUS OF C
The radius is the distance from the centre \(G(0,3)\) to a point \(A(6,11)\) on the circle: $$r=\sqrt{(6-0)^2+(11-3)^2}=\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10$$
💡 Use the distance formula between the centre and any point on the circle.
STEP 2 · EQUATION OF C
A circle with centre \((a,b)\) and radius \(r\) has equation \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2\). With centre \(G(0,3)\) and \(r=10\): $$x^2+(y-3)^2=100$$
ANSWER (a)
\(C:\; x^2+(y-3)^2=100\)
STEP 3 · LOCUS Γ : METHOD 1
Let \(P=(x,y)\). The condition is \(AP=GP\): $$\sqrt{(x-6)^2+(y-11)^2}=\sqrt{(x-0)^2+(y-3)^2}$$ Squaring both sides: $$x^2-12x+36+y^2-22y+121 = x^2+y^2-6y+9$$ $$-12x-22y+157=-6y+9$$ $$-12x-16y+148=0$$ $$3x+4y-37=0$$
💡 Squaring is valid here because both sides are distances (non‑negative).
STEP 4 · LOCUS Γ : METHOD 2
Slope of \(AG\): \(\;m_{AG}=\dfrac{11-3}{6-0}=\dfrac{8}{6}=\dfrac{4}{3}\).
Midpoint of \(AG\): \(\;M=\left(\dfrac{6+0}{2},\dfrac{11+3}{2}\right)=(3,7)\).
Since \(AP=GP\), \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AG\), with slope \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\): $$y-7=-\tfrac{3}{4}(x-3)\;\Rightarrow\;4y-28=-3x+9$$ $$3x+4y-37=0 \quad\checkmark$$
ANSWER (b)(i)
\(\Gamma:\; 3x+4y-37=0\)
STEP 5 · GEOMETRIC RELATIONSHIP
From Step 4, \(\Gamma\) passes through the midpoint \(M(3,7)\) of \(AG\) and is perpendicular to \(AG\). $$AG\perp\Gamma,\qquad M\text{ is the midpoint of }AG$$
ANSWER (b)(ii)
\(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment \(AG\).
STEP 6 · PERIMETER OF AQGR
Since \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AG\) and \(Q,R\) lie on \(\Gamma\): $$QA=QG\quad\text{and}\quad RA=RG$$ Both \(Q\) and \(R\) lie on the circle \(C\) whose centre is \(G\) and radius \(10\), so $$QG=RG=10.$$ Hence \(QA=QG=10=RA=RG\), so each side of the quadrilateral \(AQGR\) has length \(10\): $$\text{Perimeter}=4\times 10=40$$
💡 Every side equals the radius — no coordinates of \(Q,R\) are needed!
ANSWER (b)(iii)
Perimeter of \(AQGR = 40\)
