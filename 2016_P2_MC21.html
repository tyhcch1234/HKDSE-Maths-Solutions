Drop perpendiculars from \(B\) to \(AD\) and to \(CD\) (extended). Let the feet be \(E\) and \(F\).

We want the vertical length \(AD\). Split the broken line \(A\to B\to C\) into its vertical pieces.

Hint: \(AD\) is vertical. Only the vertical component of every segment can contribute to it.
2
1M

In \(\triangle ABE\): \(\angle BAE = a\), the hypotenuse is \(AB\), and \(AE\) is adjacent to \(a\).

\[ AE = AB\cos \angle BAE = AB\cos a \]
Hint: Move the slider for \(a\) and watch \(AE\) change — it always equals \(AB\cos a\).
3
1M

In \(\triangle BCF\): the angle at \(C\) is \(c\), hypotenuse \(BC\), and \(BF\) is opposite to \(c\).

\[ BF = BC\sin c \]
Hint: \(BF\) is horizontal-to-D but vertical in direction — it is exactly the rise from \(B\) up to the level of \(A\).

1A

Hence

\[ AD = AE + ED = AE + BF = AB\cos a + BC\sin c \]
Answer: B \(\;AB\cos a + BC\sin c\)

Since \(E\) and \(F\) are feet of perpendiculars from \(B\), and \(AD\perp CD\):

\[ \angle EDF = \angle DEB = \angle DFB = 90^\circ \]
Hint: Three right angles is enough — a quadrilateral with three right angles is automatically a rectangle.

1A

Hence \(BEDF\) is a rectangle, so opposite sides are equal:

\[ ED = BF \]
Hint: Drag the height slider — \(ED\) and \(BF\) stay perfectly equal no matter how \(B\) moves vertically.

Therefore the whole vertical height is the sum of the two vertical pieces:

\[ AD = AE + ED = AE + BF = AB\cos a + BC\sin c \]

Summary — Answer at a glance

  • Key construction: drop perpendiculars from \(B\) onto \(AD\) (foot \(E\)) and onto \(CD\) (foot \(F\)).
  • Triangle \(ABE\): \(AE = AB\cos a\).
  • Triangle \(BCF\): \(BF = BC\sin c\).
  • Rectangle \(BEDF\): \(ED = BF\), so \(AD = AE + ED = AE + BF\).
  • Final result: \[ AD = AB\cos a + BC\sin c \]
  • Correct option: B
