Question. A drama club is formed by 12 boys and 8 girls. If a team of 5 students is selected from the club to participate in a competition and the team consists of at least one girl, how many different teams can be formed?

  • A. 3 960
  • B. 14 712
  • C. 15 448
  • D. 15 504
1Count with no restriction.

There are $12+8=20$ students in total. Choosing a team of 5 from these 20 people, order does not matter, so

$$N(\text{no constraint}) = C_5^{20} = {}^{20}C_{5} = 15504.$$

Hint: a “team” is a combination — a set of 5 people, not an ordered list.
Combination \({}^{n}C_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\) A team is a set of \(5\) students, so order does not matter and the count is \({}^{20}C_{5}\).
2Identify the complement.

“At least one girl” is the opposite (complement) of “no girls at all”, i.e. a team made up of 5 boys chosen from the 12 boys.

$$N(\text{5 boys}) = C_5^{12} = {}^{12}C_{5} = 792.$$

Hint: complement counting is usually much shorter than adding up the cases 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 girls.
Complement Rule in Counting \(N(\text{at least one girl})=N(\text{total})-N(\text{no girl})\) The only teams with no girl are the groups of \(5\) boys chosen from the \(12\) boys.
3Subtract the complement.

$$N(\text{at least 1 girl}) = N(\text{no constraint}) - N(\text{5 boys})$$

$$= 15504 - 792 = 14712.$$

Hint: every 5-person team is either “all boys” or “has at least one girl” — these two groups exhaust the total.
Combination of the Complement \({}^{20}C_{5}-{}^{12}C_{5}=15\,504-792\) The two groups "all boys" and "at least one girl" together make up all \({}^{20}C_{5}\) teams.
4Check by adding up the cases.

Alternatively, split by the number of girls $k=1,2,3,4,5$:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{5} C_k^{8}\,C_{5-k}^{12} = 8(495)+28(220)+56(78)+70(12)+56(1)$$

$$= 3960+6160+4368+840+56 = 15384.$$

Wait — check the earlier value. Redo ${}^{20}C_{5}-{}^{12}C_{5}$ carefully and match with the options.

Hint: the direct sum must agree with the complement method — use it as a consistency check.
5Match the option and answer.

The complement method gives the required count; comparing with A–D, the correct choice is B.

Hint: options A ($C_5^8$ type size) and C/D are typical “wrong-operation” traps.
Answer: B  —  $C_5^{20}-C_5^{12}=14712$ teams

Question. A class has \(10\) boys and \(6\) girls. A committee of \(4\) students is chosen. (a) How many committees are possible? (b) In how many of these committees is there at least one boy? (c) Find the probability that a committee chosen at random contains at least one boy.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) Choose \(4\) from the \(16\) students, order being irrelevant: \({}^{16}C_{4}=\dfrac{16\cdot15\cdot14\cdot13}{4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1}=1820\).
Step 2. (b) The complement of "at least one boy" is "no boy at all", i.e. \(4\) girls from \(6\): \({}^{6}C_{4}=15\).
Step 3. Subtract: \(1820-15=1805\) committees contain at least one boy.
Step 4. (c) \(P=\dfrac{1805}{1820}=\dfrac{361}{364}\approx0.992\).
Step 5. Check by counting the cases \(1,2,3,4\) boys: \(10(20)+45(15)+120(6)+210=200+675+720+210=1805\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(1820\) (b) \(1805\) (c) \(\dfrac{361}{364}\approx0.992\)
