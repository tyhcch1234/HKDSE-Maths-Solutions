Q43 · Probability

#Combination #Probability #At least one

A drama club is formed by 12 boys and 8 girls. If a team of 5 students is selected from the club to participate in a competition and the team consists of at least one girl, how many different teams can be formed?

A. 3 960 B. 14 712 C. 15 448 D. 15 504

Core idea: Use the complement method. Total teams of 5 from 20 students minus teams with no girls (i.e., all 5 boys). This avoids adding multiple cases (1 girl + 2 girls + …) and is a classic “at least one” combination problem.

Step-by-step breakdown

1

Total students & team size

Club size: 12 boys + 8 girls = 20 students.

We select a team of 5. Total possible teams without any restriction is:

\( C^{20}_{5} = 15\,504 \)
2

The “at least one girl” condition

“At least one girl” means the team can have 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 girls.

It is easier to count the complement: teams with no girls (i.e., all 5 members are boys).

Complement: all-boys team = 5 boys chosen from 12 boys.
3

Count the complement (all boys)

Number of ways to choose 5 boys from 12 boys:

\( C^{12}_{5} = 792 \)
4

Subtract to get the answer

Teams with at least one girl = Total teams − All-boys teams.

\( C^{20}_{5} - C^{12}_{5} = 15\,504 - 792 = 14\,712 \)

✔ Correct answer: B. 14 712

Key formula

\( C^{n}_{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \) — number of ways to choose \(r\) objects from \(n\).

Use the complement: “at least one” → 1 − P(none) or Total − None.
