Coordinate Geometry — Equation, Centre & Radius of a Circle

Circle Equation Completing the Square Centre & Radius Circumference Point–Circle Position

Q27 The Question Multiple Choice

The equation of the circle \(C\) is

\[ 5x^2+5y^2-30x+10y+6=0. \]

Which of the following is true?

  • A. The origin lies inside \(C\).
  • B. \(C\) lies in the second quadrant.
  • C. The circumference of \(C\) is less than \(20\).
  • D. The coordinates of the centre of \(C\) are \((15,-5)\).

Use the interactive panels below to explore the circle, then reveal the step-by-step reasoning.

Step A Simplify & Find the Centre and Radius

centre (3, −1), r = 2.966
Step 11M

Divide the whole equation by \(5\) so that the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) become \(1\):

\[ 5x^2+5y^2-30x+10y+6=0 \] \[ \Rightarrow x^2+y^2-6x+2y+\tfrac{6}{5}=0 \]
Hint: A circle must be in the form \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\) first — always divide out the common coefficient.
Step 21M

Complete the square in \(x\) and in \(y\):

\[ (x^2-6x)+(y^2+2y)+\tfrac{6}{5}=0 \] \[ (x-3)^2-9+(y+1)^2-1+\tfrac{6}{5}=0 \] \[ (x-3)^2+(y+1)^2=9+1-\tfrac{6}{5} \]
Hint: Half the coefficient of \(x\) gives \(3\); half the coefficient of \(y\) gives \(1\). Remember to subtract the squares you added.
Step 31A
\[ (x-3)^2+(y+1)^2=\frac{44}{5} \]

So the centre is \((3,-1)\) and

\[ r=\sqrt{\frac{44}{5}}=\frac{2\sqrt{55}}{5}\approx 2.966. \]
Hint: Centre \(=(-D/2,-E/2)\), radius \(=\sqrt{(D/2)^2+(E/2)^2-F}\). Here \(D=-6,\;E=2,\;F=\tfrac{6}{5}\).
Centre of \(C=(3,-1)\), radius \(=\dfrac{2\sqrt{55}}{5}\approx2.97\).  ⇒ Option D is false.
0 / 3

Step B Is the Origin Inside \(C\)? (Option A)

OC = 3.162 > r = 2.966 → outside
Step 11M

Distance from the centre \(C(3,-1)\) to the origin \(O(0,0)\):

\[ OC=\sqrt{(3-0)^2+(-1-0)^2}=\sqrt{9+1}=\sqrt{10}\approx3.162 \]
Hint: Use the distance formula. Compare this distance with the radius \(r\approx2.966\).
Step 21A

Since \(\sqrt{10}\approx3.162 > \dfrac{2\sqrt{55}}{5}\approx2.966\), the origin lies outside the circle.

\[ OC > r \;\Rightarrow\; O \text{ is outside } C \]
Hint: \(dr\): outside.
Option A is false — the origin lies outside \(C\).
0 / 2

Step C Quadrant Test (Option B) & Circumference (Option C)

C = 2πr ≈ 18.64 < 20
Step 11M

The centre \((3,-1)\) has \(x>0\) and \(y<0\), so the circle is centred in the fourth quadrant. Since the radius is only about \(2.97\), the circle stays near \(x\approx 0.03\) to \(5.97\) and \(y\approx -3.97\) to \(1.97\). It cannot lie entirely in the second quadrant.

\[ C=(3,-1)\quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{fourth-quadrant centre} \]
Hint: A circle "lies in a quadrant" only if every point of it is in that quadrant. Check the extreme \(x\)- and \(y\)-ranges.
Step 21M

Compute the circumference with the exact radius \(r=\sqrt{\tfrac{44}{5}}\):

\[ C_{\text{circ}}=2\pi r = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{44}{5}} \approx 2\pi(2.966)\approx 18.64 \]
Hint: Use \(r=\sqrt{44/5}\), NOT \(r=\sqrt{44/5}\) rounded too early. \(2\pi\sqrt{8.8}\) gives the value.
Option C is true — the circumference \(\approx18.64<20\). Option B is false.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • Standard form: \(x^2+y^2-6x+2y+\tfrac{6}{5}=0\)
  • Centre of \(C\): \((3,\,-1)\) — so D is false.
  • Radius: \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{44}{5}}=\dfrac{2\sqrt{55}}{5}\approx 2.966\)
  • \(OC=\sqrt{10}\approx3.162>r\) → origin is outside \(C\) — so A is false.
  • Centre is in the 4th quadrant → B is false.
  • Circumference \(=2\pi r\approx 18.64<20\) → C is true.
  • Correct choice: C
