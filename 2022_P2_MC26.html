Coordinate Geometry — Locus of a Moving Point & the Perpendicular Bisector
Equation of a Straight Line Intercepts Locus Perpendicular Bisector Distance Formula

📝 Question Multiple Choice

The straight line \(12x - 5y = 60\) cuts the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis at the points \(A\) and \(B\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(AP = BP\). Find the equation of the locus of \(P\).

A. \(10x + 24y + 119 = 0\)
B. \(15x + 36y + 179 = 0\)
C. \(x^2 + y^2 - 5x + 12y = 0\)
D. \(x^2 + y^2 + 12x - 133 = 0\)

Part (a) — Find the intercepts \(A\) and \(B\) Warm-up

First locate where the line \(12x-5y=60\) meets the two axes. Drag the slider to move a point along the line and watch the intercept labels.

1M

\[12x-5(0)=60 \;\Rightarrow\; 12x=60 \;\Rightarrow\; x=5.\]

Hint: The \(x\)-intercept is where the line cuts the \(x\)-axis, so \(y=0\).
Answer: \(A(5,\,0)\)
2
y-intercept: put \(x=0\)
1M

\[12(0)-5y=60 \;\Rightarrow\; -5y=60 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-12.\]

Hint: The \(y\)-intercept is where the line cuts the \(y\)-axis, so \(x=0\). Watch the sign!
Answer: \(B(0,\,-12)\)
3
Check with the graph
1A

The line passes through \(A(5,0)\) and \(B(0,-12)\). Its slope is \(\dfrac{-12-0}{0-5}=\dfrac{12}{5}\).

Hint: A positive slope rising to the right matches the picture — the line goes up as \(x\) increases.
Answer: \(A(5,0)\), \(B(0,-12)\), slope \(=\dfrac{12}{5}\)

Part (b) — The locus is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) Key idea

Because \(AP=BP\), the moving point \(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\). Drag \(P\) (or use the slider) and watch that the two distances always stay equal along the locus.

1
Interpret the condition \(AP=BP\)
1M

Let \((u,v)\) be the coordinates of \(P\). Then \(AP=BP\) means the point is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\).

Hint: "Equidistant from two fixed points" always describes the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them.
2
Use the squared distance formula
1M

Squaring avoids square roots: \(AP^2=BP^2\), i.e. \[(u-5)^2+(v-0)^2=(u-0)^2+(v+12)^2.\]

Hint: Since both sides are non-negative, \(AP=BP \iff AP^2=BP^2\). This is valid and cleaner.
Answer: \(AP^2=BP^2\)
3
Expand and simplify
1A

\[u^2-10u+25+v^2 \;=\; u^2 + v^2 +24v +144\] \[-10u-24v-119 = 0\] \[10u + 24v + 119 = 0.\]

Hint: All the \(u^2\) and \(v^2\) terms cancel — that is exactly why a straight line (not a circle) appears. Replace \((u,v)\) by \((x,y)\) for the final locus.
Answer: \(10x+24y+119=0\) — so option A.

Part (c) — Alternative method: perpendicular bisector Method 2

Find the midpoint \(M\) of \(AB\) and the slope of \(AB\), then write the perpendicular bisector. Hook the slider to the slope of \(AB\) and see the bisector swing.

Slope \(AB =\) 12/5
Perp. slope \(=\) −5/12
Midpoint \(M =\) (2.5, −6)

The perpendicular bisector always passes through \(M\) and is at right angles to \(AB\).

1
Midpoint of \(AB\)
1M

\[M=\left(\frac{5+0}{2},\;\frac{0+(-12)}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{5}{2},\,-6\right)\]

Hint: The perpendicular bisector must pass through the midpoint of the segment.
Answer: \(M\left(\dfrac{5}{2},-6\right)\)
2
Slope of \(AB\) and of the bisector
1M

Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{0-(-12)}{5-0}=\dfrac{12}{5}\).

Slope of perpendicular bisector \(=-\dfrac{1}{12/5}=-\dfrac{5}{12}\).

Hint: Perpendicular lines have slopes whose product is \(-1\): \(m_1 m_2=-1\).
Answer: Perp. slope \(=-\dfrac{5}{12}\)
3
Point–slope form
1A

\[y-(-6)=-\frac{5}{12}\left(x-\frac{5}{2}\right)\]

Multiply by 24: \(24y+144=-10x+25\), giving \(10x+24y+119=0\).

Hint: Multiplying through by 24 clears all fractions neatly. The answer matches Method 1 — a strong consistency check.
Answer: \(10x+24y+119=0\), matching option A.

✅ Summary of Answers

  • Intercepts: \(A(5,\,0)\), \(B(0,\,-12)\)
  • Slope of \(AB\): \(\dfrac{12}{5}\); perpendicular slope: \(-\dfrac{5}{12}\)
  • Midpoint: \(M\left(\dfrac{5}{2},\,-6\right)\)
  • Locus equation: \(10x+24y+119=0\)
  • Correct option: A
  • Key insight: \(AP=BP\) describes the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) — a straight line, not a circle.
