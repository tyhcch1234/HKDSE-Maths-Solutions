QUESTION

If $c$ and $d$ are constants such that

$(x+2)(x+c)+12 \equiv x(x+d)+6c(x+1)$,

then $d =$

A. −13
B. −3
C. 3
D. 17

⚙ Choose a Solving Strategy

Solution Progress 0 / 4 steps

Since the identity holds for all values of $x$, we can substitute any convenient value.

Hint — look at the factor $6c(x+1)$. Why would $x = -1$ be great?

It makes the term $6c(x+1)$ vanish! But here the official solution picks $x = -\frac{4}{5}$ — let's see why.

The official solution substitutes:

$x = -\dfrac{4}{5}$

💡 Why? The coefficients of $x$ on both sides are $1$ and $6c + d$. Choosing $x$ so that both factors become simple leads to cancellation. Let's follow through.

Substituting $x = -\frac{4}{5}$ into both sides:

Left-hand side (LHS):

$\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+2\right)\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+c\right)+12$

Right-hand side (RHS):

$\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}\right)\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+d\right)+6c\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+1\right)$

Simplify slowly:
$-\frac{4}{5}+2 = \frac{6}{5}$   |   $-\frac{4}{5}+1 = \frac{1}{5}$

Expanding the LHS:

$\dfrac{6}{5}\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+c\right)+12 = -\dfrac{24}{25}+\dfrac{6c}{5}+12 = \dfrac{276}{25}+\dfrac{6c}{5}$

Expanding the RHS:

$\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}\right)\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}+d\right)+\dfrac{6c}{5} = \dfrac{16}{25}-\dfrac{4d}{5}+\dfrac{6c}{5}$

Set LHS = RHS:

$\dfrac{276}{25}+\dfrac{6c}{5} = \dfrac{16}{25}-\dfrac{4d}{5}+\dfrac{6c}{5}$

✨ Notice the $\frac{6c}{5}$ on both sides — they cancel beautifully! This is why $x = -\frac{4}{5}$ was chosen.

After cancelling $\frac{6c}{5}$:

$\dfrac{276}{25} = \dfrac{16}{25} - \dfrac{4d}{5}$

Multiply through by $25$:

$276 = 16 - 20d$

Rearrange:

$20d = 16 - 276 = -260$

Final Answer

$d = -13$

→ Option A

Left-hand side:

$(x+2)(x+c)+12$
$= x^2 + cx + 2x + 2c + 12$
$= x^2 + (c+2)x + (2c+12)$

Right-hand side:

$x(x+d)+6c(x+1)$
$= x^2 + dx + 6cx + 6c$
$= x^2 + (6c+d)x + 6c$

Since the two polynomials are identical, corresponding coefficients are equal:

Coefficient of $x$

$c+2 = 6c+d$

(Equation 1)

Constant term

$2c+12 = 6c$

(Equation 2)

Key insight: The constant term equation only contains $c$, so solve it first!

From Equation 2:

$2c + 12 = 6c$
$\Rightarrow 12 = 4c$
$c = 3$

Substitute $c = 3$ into Equation 1:

$3 + 2 = 6(3) + d$
$5 = 18 + d$

Final Answer

$d = -13$

→ Option A

📝 Quick verification:

With $c = 3$ and $d = -13$, the LHS becomes $x^2 + 5x + 18$ and the RHS becomes $x^2 + 5x + 18$. ✓ Identical!

🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 1.A polynomial identity holds for all values of $x$ — you may substitute any convenient number.
  • 2.Choosing a smart value (like $x = -\frac{4}{5}$) can make terms cancel, simplifying the algebra.
  • 3.Alternatively, expand both sides and compare coefficients of like powers of $x$.
  • 4.The constant-term equation often reveals $c$ directly — solve it first, then back-substitute.
