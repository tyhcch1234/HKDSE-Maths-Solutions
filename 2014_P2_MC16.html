Square, Congruent Triangles & the Angle Sum of a Triangle

Q The Question

[2014 MC #16] In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a square. \(BC\) is produced to \(G\) such that \(\angle CDG = 25^\circ\). \(E\) is a point lying on \(AB\) such that \(AE = CG\). If \(F\) is a point lying on \(BC\) such that \(\angle CDF = 20^\circ\), then \(\angle DFE =\)

A. 60° B. 65° C. 70° D. 73°

(1 mark)

a Part (a) — Set up the figure & find the key lengths

AE = 0.00 CG = 0.00 DA = 0.00 DC = 0.00 ∠CDG = 25°
Step 11MUse the properties of a square

Since \(ABCD\) is a square, all four sides are equal and each interior angle is \(90^\circ\):

\[ AB = BC = CD = DA = s, \qquad \angle ADC = \angle BCD = 90^\circ . \]
Hint: A square is both a rectangle and a rhombus — equal sides and right angles. The diagonal \(AC\) splits it into two congruent right isosceles triangles.
Step 21MShow \(\triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG\)

Consider \(\triangle DAE\) and \(\triangle DCG\):

\[ DA = DC \ ( \text{sides of square} ), \quad \angle DAE = 90^\circ = \angle DCG \ ( \text{right angles} ), \quad AE = CG \ ( \text{given} ). \]

Therefore by SAS,

\[ \triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG. \]
Hint: Look at the slider — whenever you move \(F\) along \(BC\), the length \(CG\) always matches \(AE\). That is exactly the condition \(AE = CG\) that makes the two right triangles congruent.
\(\triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG\) (SAS)
Step 31AIdentify equal corresponding lengths

From the congruence, corresponding sides are equal:

\[ DE = DG, \qquad AE = CG. \]

Also, corresponding angles are equal:

\[ \angle ADE = \angle CDG = 25^\circ \quad (\text{corr. } \angle\text{s, } \cong \triangle\text{s}). \]
Hint: The congruence tells us \(\angle ADE = \angle CDG\). Since \(\angle CDG = 25^\circ\), we get \(\angle ADE = 25^\circ\), so \(\angle CDE = 90^\circ - 25^\circ = 65^\circ\).
\(DE = DG\), \(\angle ADE = 25^\circ\), \(\angle CDE = 65^\circ\)

b Part (b) — Find \(\angle DFE\)

∠CDF = 20° ∠CFD = 70° ∠DFE = 70°
Step 11MUse the angle sum of \(\triangle CDF\)

In \(\triangle CDF\), the angle at \(C\) is a right angle of the square, so \(\angle DCF = 90^\circ\). Hence

\[ \angle CFD = 180^\circ - \angle DCF - \angle CDF \quad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle) \] \[ = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 20^\circ = 70^\circ. \]
Hint: The three angles of any triangle add up to \(180^\circ\). With a \(90^\circ\) corner at \(C\) and \(20^\circ\) at \(D\), the remaining angle at \(F\) must be \(70^\circ\).
\(\angle CFD = 70^\circ\)
Step 21MRelate \(\angle DFE\) to \(\angle DFG\)

Because \(BC\) is produced to \(G\), the points \(B\), \(C\), \(G\) are collinear, so \(F\) lies on the straight line \(CG\) (extended). Thus \(E\), \(F\), \(G\) are collinear and the angles on the straight line at \(F\) give

\[ \angle DFG = 180^\circ - \angle CFD = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ. \]

But \(\angle DFE\) and \(\angle DFG\) are the same angle measured at \(F\) on the side of \(E\):

\[ \angle DFE = \angle DFG. \]
Hint: From the congruence \(\triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG\) we get \(DE = DG\), so \(\triangle DEF\) is a symmetric configuration around the line \(DG\). Watch the canvas: the red segment \(DE\) mirrors the green segment \(DG\), so the angles at \(F\) match.
Step 31AUse corresponding angles of the congruent triangles

From \(\triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG\), the corresponding angles are equal:

\[ \angle DFE = \angle DFG \quad (\text{corr. } \angle\text{s, } \cong \triangle\text{s}). \]

Since \(\angle DFG = \angle CFD = 70^\circ\) from the symmetric geometry, we obtain

\[ \angle DFE = 70^\circ. \]
Hint: Do not over-complicate: the congruence gives \(\angle DFE = \angle DFG\). And because \(CD = DA = s\) is fixed in the square, the calculation \(\angle CFD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 20^\circ = 70^\circ\) is all you need.
\(\angle DFE = \boxed{70^\circ}\)  →  Answer: C

Summary of the Solution

  • \(ABCD\) is a square \(\Rightarrow AB = BC = CD = DA = s\) and \(\angle ADC = \angle BCD = 90^\circ\).
  • \(AE = CG\), \(DA = DC\), \(\angle DAE = \angle DCG = 90^\circ\) \(\Rightarrow \triangle DAE \cong \triangle DCG\) (SAS).
  • Congruent triangles give \(DE = DG\) and \(\angle ADE = \angle CDG = 25^\circ\).
  • In \(\triangle CDF\): \(\angle DCF = 90^\circ\), \(\angle CDF = 20^\circ\).
  • \(\angle CFD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 20^\circ = 70^\circ\) (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  • By symmetry / corresponding angles, \(\angle DFE = \angle DFG = \angle CFD = 70^\circ\).
  • Final answer: \(\angle DFE = \span class="hi">70^\circ\), i.e. Option C.
