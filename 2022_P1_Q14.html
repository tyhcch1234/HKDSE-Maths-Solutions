Polynomials — division, factor theorem & nature of roots

Let $p(x)=2x^3+ax^2+bx-20$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. When $p(x)$ is divided by $x^2-2x+3$, the remainder is $x+13$.

(a) Find $a$ and $b$. [3 marks]

(b) Is $x-5$ a factor of $p(x)$? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

(c) Someone claims that the equation $p(x)=0$ has two irrational roots. Do you agree? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

1Step 1 — Set up the division identity

Write $p(x)$ as a quotient times the divisor plus the remainder:

$$p(x)=(mx+n)(x^2-2x+3)+x+13$$

Since $p(x)$ is degree 3 and the divisor is degree 2, the quotient must be linear, say $mx+n$.

Hint: The remainder has degree 1, so the quotient absorbs the leading term $2x^3$ — it must be $mx+n$.
Division Identity \(p(x)=(\text{divisor})\times(\text{quotient})+(\text{remainder})\) Dividing a cubic by \(x^{2}-2x+3\) leaves a linear quotient \(mx+n\) and remainder \(x+13\).
2Step 2 — Expand and compare coefficients

Expanding:

$$p(x)=mx^3+(n-2m)x^2+(3m-2n)x+(3n+13)$$

Compare with the coefficient of $x^3$: $m=2$.

Compare the constant term: $3n+13=-20$, so $n=-11$.

Hint: Matching the $x^3$ term gives $m$ immediately; the constant term gives $n$.
Equating Coefficients \(\text{identical polynomials}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{equal coefficients of each power}\) Matching \(x^{3}\) gives \(m=2\) and matching the constant gives \(3n+13=-20\), so \(n=-11\).
3Step 3 — Read off $a$ and $b$

Substituting $m=2,\ n=-11$:

$$p(x)=2x^3-15x^2+29x-20$$

Comparing with $p(x)=2x^3+ax^2+bx-20$ gives $a=-15$ and $b=29$.

Hint: The $x^3$ and $x^2$ coefficients fix $a$; the $x$ coefficient fixes $b$.
4Step 4 (b) — Apply the factor theorem

Compute $p(5)$:

$$\begin{aligned}p(5)&=2(5)^3-15(5)^2+29(5)-20\\&=250-375+145-20\\&=0\end{aligned}$$

Since $p(5)=0$, by the factor theorem $x-5$ is a factor of $p(x)$.

Hint: A linear expression $x-c$ is a factor of $p(x)$ exactly when $p(c)=0$.
5Step 5 (c) — Factorise and test the quadratic

Using the factor $x-5$:

$$p(x)=0 \Rightarrow (x-5)(2x^2-5x+4)=0$$

So $x=5$ or $2x^2-5x+4=0$.

Discriminant of $2x^2-5x+4$:

$$\Delta=(-5)^2-4(2)(4)=25-32=-7<0$$

So $2x^2-5x+4=0$ has no real roots (the two roots are complex conjugates, not irrational).

Hint: A negative discriminant means no real roots at all — not irrational ones.
Discriminant \(\Delta=B^{2}-4AC<0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{no real roots}\) For \(2x^{2}-5x+4\) the discriminant is \(25-32=-7<0\), so the quadratic contributes no real root.
6Step 6 (c) — Conclusion

The only real root is $x=5$, which is rational (not irrational).

Therefore the claim is disagreed with: $p(x)=0$ has exactly one real root, and it is rational.

Hint: "Irrational root" needs a real but non-rational root — here there are none.
Answer: (a) $a=-15,\ b=29$  ·  (b) Yes, $p(5)=0$ so $x-5$ is a factor  ·  (c) Disagree — $p(x)=(x-5)(2x^2-5x+4)$ and $2x^2-5x+4=0$ has $\Delta=-7<0$, so only the rational root $x=5$ exists.

Question. Let \(p(x)\) be a cubic polynomial. When \(p(x)\) is divided by \(x^{2}-3x+4\), the quotient is \(mx+n\) and the remainder is \(2x-7\). It is given that \(p(x)=2x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx-35\). (a) Find \(m\) and \(n\). (b) Find \(a\) and \(b\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write the division identity: \(p(x)=(mx+n)(x^{2}-3x+4)+(2x-7)\).
Step 2. Expand: \(mx^{3}+(n-3m)x^{2}+(4m-3n+2)x+(4n-7)\).
Step 3. Compare the \(x^{3}\) coefficients: \(m=2\). Compare the constant terms: \(4n-7=-35\), so \(n=-7\).
Step 4. Then \(a=n-3m=-7-6=-13\) and \(b=4m-3n+2=8+21+2=31\).
Step 5. So \(p(x)=2x^{3}-13x^{2}+31x-35\). Check with \(x=1\): \(2-13+31-35=-15\), and the identity gives \((2-7)(1-3+4)+(2-7)=(-5)(2)-5=-15\) ✓.
Answer: (a) \(m=2,\ n=-7\); (b) \(a=-13,\ b=31\)
