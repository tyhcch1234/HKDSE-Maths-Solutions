Similar triangles & the Pythagorean test — interactive worked solution

Figure 1 — Interactive Diagram

AC (25 cm) BC (20 cm) BD (12 cm) CD (16 cm) equal angles ∠BAC = ∠DBC common angle ∠ACB = ∠BCD

Question

In Figure 1, \(D\) is a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(\angle BAC = \angle DBC\).
(a) Prove that \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BDC\).  [2 marks]
(b) Suppose that \(AC = 25\) cm, \(BC = 20\) cm and \(BD = 12\) cm. Is \(\triangle BCD\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer.  [3 marks]
💡 Hints: For (a) look for two pairs of equal angles — one pair is given, the other is a common angle. For (b) use the similarity ratio to find the third side, then test the converse of the Pythagorean theorem.
STEP 1 · Two equal angles

In \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle BDC\):

$$\angle BAC = \angle DBC \qquad (\text{given})$$ $$\angle ACB = \angle BCD \qquad (\text{common } \angle)$$
STEP 2 · Conclusion by AAA

Two pairs of corresponding angles are equal, so the third pair is also equal. Hence the two triangles are equiangular and therefore similar (AAA):

$$\boxed{\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BDC}$$
STEP 3 · Set up the ratio (part b)

Since \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BDC\), corresponding sides are proportional:

$$\frac{CD}{BC}=\frac{BC}{AC}$$

(\(BC\) corresponds to \(AC\), and \(CD\) corresponds to \(BC\).)

STEP 4 · Find CD $$\frac{CD}{20}=\frac{20}{25}$$ $$CD = \frac{20\times 20}{25} = 16 \text{ cm}$$
STEP 5 · Pythagorean test

Check whether \(BD^2 + CD^2 = BC^2\):

$$BD^2+CD^2 = 12^2+16^2 = 144+256 = 400 = 20^2 = BC^2$$
STEP 6 · Final answer
(a) Since \(\angle BAC=\angle DBC\) (given) and \(\angle ACB=\angle BCD\) (common angle), \(\triangle ABC\sim\triangle BDC\) (AAA).

(b) Yes. From the similarity, \(CD=16\) cm, and \(12^2+16^2=20^2=BC^2\). By the converse of the Pythagorean theorem (with \(\angle BDC = 90^\circ\)), \(\triangle BCD\) is a right-angled triangle.
