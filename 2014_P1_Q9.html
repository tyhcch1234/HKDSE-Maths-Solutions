Similar Triangles & the Converse of Pythagoras’ Theorem

Similar Triangles AAA / Equiangular Ratio of Corresponding Sides Converse of Pythagoras Right-Angled Test

Q Question 5 marks

In Figure 1, \(D\) is a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(\angle BAC = \angle CBD\).

(a) Prove that \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta BDC\). 2 marks

(b) Suppose that \(AC = 25\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 20\text{ cm}\) and \(BD = 12\text{ cm}\). Is \(\Delta BCD\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer. 3 marks

Step 11M

Compare the two triangles \(\Delta ABC\) and \(\Delta BDC\) angle by angle.

\(\angle BAC = \angle CBD\)   (given)
Hint: This is the information handed to us in the question. Write the reason “given” every time you use it — reasons earn marks.
Step 21M

Since \(D\) lies on \(AC\), the points \(A\), \(D\), \(C\) are collinear, so the segment \(BC\) is shared as a side of both triangles.

\(\angle ACB = \angle BCD\)   (common angle)
Hint: \(C\) is a vertex of both triangles. The angle at \(C\) in \(\Delta ABC\) is literally the same angle as the angle at \(C\) in \(\Delta BCD\). This is the “common angle” reason.
Step 31A

Two pairs of equal corresponding angles are enough:

\(\therefore\ \Delta ABC \sim \Delta BDC\)   (AAA / equiangular)
\(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta BDC\)  —  proved by AAA (two pairs of equal angles implies the third pair is equal too).
Hint: The order of letters matters! \(\angle A \leftrightarrow \angle B\), \(\angle C \leftrightarrow \angle C\), \(\angle B \leftrightarrow \angle D\). So it must be written \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta BDC\), not \(\Delta ABC \sim \Delta CBD\).

Step 11M

From the similarity in (a), corresponding sides are in the same ratio. Matching \(CD \leftrightarrow BC\), \(BC \leftrightarrow AC\), \(BD \leftrightarrow AB\):

\[ \frac{CD}{BC}=\frac{BC}{AC} \]
\[ \frac{CD}{20}=\frac{20}{25} \]
Hint: Be careful with the order. \(\Delta ABC\sim\Delta BDC\) means \(AB\leftrightarrow BD\), \(BC\leftrightarrow DC\), \(AC\leftrightarrow BC\). So the side \(BC=20\) of the big triangle matches the side \(DC\) of the small one, and \(AC=25\) matches \(BC=20\)… read the ratio straight off the letter order.
Step 21A

Solve the ratio for \(CD\):

\[ CD=\frac{20\times 20}{25}=\frac{400}{25}=16\text{ cm} \]
\(CD = 16\text{ cm}\)
Hint: Cross-multiply: \(CD=\dfrac{BC^2}{AC}\). This is the “leg–hypotenuse” style relation produced by the similar triangles — a very common DSE setup.
Step 31M

Test the converse of Pythagoras’ theorem on \(\Delta BCD\), using the largest side \(BC=20\) as the possible hypotenuse:

\[ BD^2+CD^2 = 12^2+16^2 = 144+256 = 400 \] \[ BC^2 = 20^2 = 400 \]
Hint: Only the largest side could be the hypotenuse. If the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides equals the square of the longest side, the triangle is right-angled at the vertex opposite the longest side.
Step 41A

Since \(BD^2+CD^2=BC^2\), the converse of Pythagoras’ theorem applies:

Yes. \(\Delta BCD\) is a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(D\) (i.e. \(\angle BDC=90^\circ\)).
Hint: A complete answer needs three things: (i) the numerical comparison \(BD^2+CD^2=BC^2\), (ii) the statement that \(\Delta BCD\) is right-angled, and (iii) where the right angle is — at \(D\), the vertex opposite the hypotenuse \(BC\).

✓ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\angle BAC=\angle CBD\) (given) and \(\angle ACB=\angle BCD\) (common angle) \(\Rightarrow \Delta ABC\sim\Delta BDC\) (AAA).
  • (b) From similarity, \(\dfrac{CD}{BC}=\dfrac{BC}{AC}\), so \(\dfrac{CD}{20}=\dfrac{20}{25}\) giving \(CD=16\text{ cm}\).
  • (b) \(BD^2+CD^2=12^2+16^2=400=20^2=BC^2\).
  • (b) final Yes, \(\Delta BCD\) is a right-angled triangle, with the right angle at \(D\).
