Counting the roots of a quadratic in $\sin\theta$

Question

[2016MC#38]   For $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$, how many roots does the equation

$5\sin^2\theta + \sin\theta - 4 = 0$

have?

  • A. 2
  • B. 3
  • C. 4
  • D. 5

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 · Factorise as a quadratic in sin θ

Treat $\sin\theta$ as an unknown. Factorise by grouping / trial:

$$5\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta-4=0$$ $$(5\sin\theta-4)(\sin\theta+1)=0$$

Check: $5\sin^2\theta+5\sin\theta-4\sin\theta-4 = 5\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta-4$ ✓

So the equation splits into two simpler trigonometric equations. Each must be solved separately.
Zero Product Property \(AB=0\iff A=0\ \text{or}\ B=0\) \((5\sin\theta-4)(\sin\theta+1)=0\) splits the equation into \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{4}{5}\) and \(\sin\theta=-1\).
Step 2 · Case (a): sin θ = 4/5

From $5\sin\theta-4=0$ we get $\sin\theta=\dfrac{4}{5}=0.8$.

Since $0 < 0.8 < 1$, the reference angle is $\alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.8) \approx 53.13^\circ$.

In $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$, sine is positive in Quadrant I and Quadrant II:

$$\theta \approx 53.13^\circ \quad \text{or} \quad \theta \approx 180^\circ-53.13^\circ = 126.87^\circ$$

→ 2 roots from this case.

On the diagram, the horizontal line $y = 0.8$ cuts the unit circle at exactly these two points.
Sine in the Second Quadrant \(\sin\left(180^{\circ}-\theta\right)=\sin\theta\) Besides the reference angle \(53.13^{\circ}\), the solution \(180^{\circ}-53.13^{\circ}=126.87^{\circ}\) also satisfies \(\sin\theta=0.8\).
Step 3 · Case (b): sin θ = −1

From $\sin\theta+1=0$ we get $\sin\theta=-1$.

Within $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$ this occurs only at the bottom of the circle:

$$\theta = 270^\circ$$

→ 1 root from this case.

Here the horizontal line $y=-1$ is tangent to the unit circle, giving only one single point (a repeated value).
Sine of Special Angles \(\sin 270^{\circ}=-1,\qquad \sin 90^{\circ}=1\) \(\sin\theta=-1\) has the single solution \(\theta=270^{\circ}\) in \(0^{\circ}\le\theta\le360^{\circ}\).
Step 4 · Total number of roots

Add up the roots found in each case:

$$\underbrace{2}_{\sin\theta=4/5} \;+\; \underbrace{1}_{\sin\theta=-1} \;=\; 3$$

The three roots are:

$$\theta \approx 53.13^\circ,\quad 126.87^\circ,\quad 270^\circ$$
No value of $\theta$ is shared between the two cases (0.8 ≠ −1), so no double counting occurs.
Answer
B · 3 roots

A. \(2\)    B. \(3\)    C. \(4\)    D. \(5\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Factorise as a quadratic in \(\sin\theta\): \(2\sin^{2}\theta-3\sin\theta+1=(2\sin\theta-1)(\sin\theta-1)=0\), so \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\) or \(\sin\theta=1\).
Step 2. Case \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\): the reference angle is \(30^{\circ}\), giving \(\theta=30^{\circ}\) and \(\theta=180^{\circ}-30^{\circ}=150^{\circ}\) — two roots.
Step 3. Case \(\sin\theta=1\): the only solution in the range is \(\theta=90^{\circ}\) — one root.
Step 4. Total \(=2+1=3\) roots (no value is shared, since \(\dfrac{1}{2}\ne 1\)). Option B.
Answer: B. \(3\)
