Q Question

The sum of the volumes of two spheres is \(324\pi\text{ cm}^3\). The radius of the larger sphere is equal to the diameter of the smaller sphere. Express, in terms of \(\pi\),

  1. the volume of the larger sphere; 3 marks
  2. the sum of the surface areas of the two spheres. 3 marks
[5 marks]
Note: the paper total is printed as [5 marks] with (a) 3 marks (1M + 1M + 1A) and (b) 3 marks (1M + 1M + 1A); the marking scheme rounds the overall to 5 marks.

Step 11M

Let \(r\) be the radius of the smaller sphere. Since the radius of the larger sphere equals the diameter of the smaller sphere,

\[ R = 2r \]

So the ratio of the radii is \(R:r = 2:1\).

Hint: Read the sentence carefully — "the radius of the larger sphere is equal to the diameter of the smaller", i.e. \(R = 2r\), not \(R = r\).
Step 21M

For similar solids, volumes scale as the cube of the length ratio:

\[ \frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}} = \left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^3 = \frac{8}{1} \]
Hint: A sphere is described by one length (its radius), so a radius ratio of \(2:1\) gives a volume ratio of \(2^3:1^3 = 8:1\).
Step 31A

Total volume \(= 324\pi\text{ cm}^3\), shared in the ratio \(8:1\). Hence the larger part is

\[ V_{\text{large}} = 324\pi \times \frac{8}{1+8} = 324\pi \times \frac{8}{9} = 288\pi\text{ cm}^3 \]
Hint: The denominator is \(1+8 = 9\), the total number of equal parts — a very common slip is to write \(\frac{8}{1}\).
Answer (a): \(\;288\pi\text{ cm}^3\)

Use the slider to zoom both spheres — it illustrates that surface area scales as the square of the radius (not the cube).
Step 11M

In part (a) we found \(V_{\text{large}} = 288\pi\). Using \(V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\) with \(R\) the radius of the larger sphere:

\[ \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = 288\pi \]
Hint: Cancel \(\pi\) on both sides first, then multiply by \(\frac{3}{4}\).
Step 21M

Solving for \(R\):

\[ R^3 = 288 \times \frac{3}{4} = 216 \quad\Rightarrow\quad R = 6\text{ cm} \]

Hence the radius of the smaller sphere is \(r = \dfrac{R}{2} = 3\text{ cm}\).

Hint: \(6^3 = 216\), so \(R=6\). A quick sanity check: \(\frac43\pi(6)^3 = \frac43\pi(216) = 288\pi\) ✓.
Step 31A

Total surface area \(= 4\pi R^2 + 4\pi r^2\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 4\pi(6)^2 + 4\pi(3)^2 &= 4\pi(36) + 4\pi(9) \\ &= 144\pi + 36\pi \\ &= 180\pi\text{ cm}^2 \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Surface-area ratio \(= (2:1)^2 = 4:1\), so the areas split \(144\pi : 36\pi\) out of a total of \(180\pi\) — area scales with the square of the radius.
Answer (b): \(\;180\pi\text{ cm}^2\)

✓ Summary of Answers

  • Radius relation: \(R = 2r\)  ⟹  volume ratio \(= 8:1\), area ratio \(= 4:1\).
  • (a) Volume of larger sphere: \(288\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
  • (b) Sum of surface areas of both spheres: \(180\pi\text{ cm}^2\)
  • Radii used: \(R = 6\text{ cm}\), \(r = 3\text{ cm}\).
  • Key formulas: \(V=\frac43\pi r^3\),   surface area \(=4\pi r^2\).
