Question & Solution

[2019 #9]
The sum of the volumes of two spheres is $324\pi\text{ cm}^3$.
The radius of the larger sphere is equal to the diameter of the smaller sphere. Express, in terms of $\pi$,
(a) the volume of the larger sphere;
(b) the sum of the surface areas of the two spheres.
[5 marks]
STEP 1 · Set up the radii
Let $R$ cm be the radius of the larger sphere and $r$ cm be the radius of the smaller sphere.
Given: radius of larger = diameter of smaller, so $$R = 2r \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R:r = 2:1$$
Key idea: all lengths scale with the ratio $2:1$.
STEP 2 · Volumes ratio
Volume Ratio of Similar Solids \(\dfrac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=\left(\dfrac{L_{1}}{L_{2}}\right)^{3}\) Since \(R=2r\), the lengths are in the ratio \(2:1\) and the volumes in the ratio \(8:1\).
Volume ratio for similar solids = (length ratio)$^3$: $$\frac{V_R}{V_r}=\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^3=\left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^3=\frac{8}{1}$$ So $V_R : V_r = 8:1$, i.e. the larger sphere gets $\dfrac{8}{1+8}=\dfrac{8}{9}$ of the total volume.
STEP 3 · (a) Volume of larger sphere
Total volume $=324\pi\text{ cm}^3$, so $$V_R=324\pi\left(\frac{8}{1+8}\right)=324\pi\times\frac{8}{9}=288\pi\text{ cm}^3$$
(a) $\;288\pi\text{ cm}^3$
STEP 4 · Find the radii
Volume of a Sphere \(V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\) Setting \(\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}=288\pi\) gives \(R^{3}=216\), so \(R=6\) cm.
Let $R$ cm be the radius of the larger sphere. Then $$\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}=288\pi$$ $$R^{3}=288\times\frac{3}{4}=216 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R=6$$ Since $R=2r$, the smaller radius is $r=3\text{ cm}$.
STEP 5 · (b) Sum of surface areas
Surface Area of a Sphere \(A=4\pi r^{2}\) The two spheres of radii \(6\) cm and \(3\) cm have total surface area \(144\pi+36\pi=180\pi\) cm\(^{2}\).
Surface area of a sphere $=4\pi(\text{radius})^2$, so $$\text{Total SA}=4\pi(6)^{2}+4\pi(3)^{2}$$ $$=144\pi+36\pi=180\pi\text{ cm}^2$$
(b) $\;180\pi\text{ cm}^2$

Question. Two solid metal spheres are melted and recast from a total volume of \(2592\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\). The radius of the larger sphere is equal to the diameter of the smaller sphere.
(a) Find the volume of the larger sphere.
(b) Find the sum of the surface areas of the two spheres.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(R\) and \(r\) be the radii. Since \(R=2r\), the volumes are in the ratio \(2^{3}:1^{3}=8:1\).
Step 2. The larger sphere takes \(\dfrac{8}{1+8}=\dfrac{8}{9}\) of the total volume: \(V_{R}=2592\pi\times\dfrac{8}{9}=2304\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\).
Step 3. \(\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}=2304\pi\), so \(R^{3}=2304\times\dfrac{3}{4}=1728\) and \(R=12\) cm; hence \(r=6\) cm.
Step 4. Total surface area \(=4\pi(12)^{2}+4\pi(6)^{2}=576\pi+144\pi=720\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\).
Answer: (a) \(2304\pi\text{ cm}^{3}\)   (b) \(720\pi\text{ cm}^{2}\)
