In a test, the mean of the test scores is 68 marks. Peter gets 46 marks in the test and his standard score is −2.2. If Susan gets 52 marks in the test, then her standard score is

A. −2.5
B. −1.6
C. −0.6
D. 1.6
1Recall the standard score formula
Solving for the Standard Deviation \(\sigma=\dfrac{x-\mu}{z}\) Substituting \(x=46\), \(\mu=68\) and \(z=-2.2\) gives \(\sigma=\dfrac{-22}{-2.2}=10\).
Standard Score \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\) Peter scores \(x=46\) with \(\mu=68\) and \(z=-2.2\), which lets us find the common \(\sigma\).

For a value $x$ with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$,
the standard score is $z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$.

Hint: here $\mu=68$. The same $\sigma$ applies to everyone.
2Find the standard deviation σ from Peter

Peter: $x=46$, $z=-2.2$:

$\dfrac{46-68}{\sigma}=-2.2 \;\Rightarrow\; \sigma=\dfrac{-22}{-2.2}=10.$

Hint: 46 is 22 marks below the mean; a z of −2.2 means 22 marks $=2.2\sigma$, so $1\sigma=10$ marks.
3Compute Susan's standard score

Susan: $x=52$:

$z_S=\dfrac{52-68}{10}=\dfrac{-16}{10}=-1.6.$

Hint: 52 is 16 marks below the mean; $16/10=1.6$ standard deviations below.
4Verify the scale on the number line

Each $1\sigma = 10$ marks.
Peter: $68-2.2(10)=46$ ✓. Susan: $68-1.6(10)=52$ ✓.

Hint: always substitute back to check your value of $\sigma$.
Answer: B. −1.6

Question. In a test, the mean mark is \(72\) and Peter obtains \(48\) marks with a standard score of \(-1.6\). If Susan obtains \(57\) marks in the same test, her standard score is
A. \(-0.8\)   B. \(-1\)   C. \(-1.2\)   D. \(-1.5\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. For Peter: \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\) gives \(-1.6=\dfrac{48-72}{\sigma}\), so \(\sigma=\dfrac{-24}{-1.6}=15\).
Step 2. For Susan, \(x=57\): \(z=\dfrac{57-72}{15}=\dfrac{-15}{15}=-1\).
Step 3. Check the scale: \(72-1(15)=57\) ✓, so Susan is exactly one standard deviation below the mean.
The answer is B.
Answer: B   \(-1\)
