Factorization by Grouping

Polynomials Factorization Difference of Squares Grouping Terms
Q1

Multiple Choice — choose the correct answer.

Factorize: \(\; \alpha^2 - \alpha - \beta^2 + \beta\)

Step-by-Step Solution

Group the \(\alpha\)-terms together and the \(\beta\)-terms together.

\[ \alpha^2 - \beta^2 - \alpha + \beta \]

Why? It is easier to spot a difference of squares when the squared terms are adjacent: \(\alpha^2 - \beta^2\).

Apply the identity \(x^2 - y^2 = (x-y)(x+y)\) to \(\alpha^2 - \beta^2\).

\[ \alpha^2 - \beta^2 = (\alpha - \beta)(\alpha + \beta) \]

Now the expression becomes:

\[ (\alpha-\beta)(\alpha+\beta) - \alpha + \beta \]

Notice \(-\alpha + \beta = -(\alpha - \beta)\). This creates a common factor \((\alpha-\beta)\).

\[ (\alpha-\beta)(\alpha+\beta) - (\alpha-\beta) \]

Both terms now share \((\alpha-\beta)\) — this is the key insight!

Factor out \((\alpha-\beta)\) from both terms:

\[ (\alpha-\beta)\big[(\alpha+\beta) - 1\big] \]
\[ \boxed{(\alpha-\beta)(\alpha+\beta-1)} \]

Therefore, the answer is option D.

💡 Key Concept Reminder

  • Difference of squares: \(x^2 - y^2 = (x-y)(x+y)\)
  • Factorization by grouping: rearrange terms so a common factor appears.
  • Always check: \(-\alpha + \beta = -(\alpha - \beta)\) — the sign trap!
