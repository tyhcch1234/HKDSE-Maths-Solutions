Laws of Indices

Question

Simplify \(\dfrac{(uv^2)^5}{u^7 v^{-3}}\) and express your answer with positive indices. (3 marks)

Solution

STEP 1
Power of a Product & Power of a Power \((ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n},\qquad (a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}\) Apply the outer index to every factor inside the bracket.
Apply the power-of-a-product law \((ab)^n=a^n b^n\) and the power-of-a-power law \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\): \[ (uv^2)^5=u^5\,(v^2)^5=u^5v^{10} \]
STEP 2
Quotient Law of Indices \(\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{m-n}\quad (a\neq 0)\) Subtract the index of the denominator from that of the numerator.
Divide using \(\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\) for each base: \[ \frac{u^5v^{10}}{u^7v^{-3}}=u^{5-7}\,v^{10-(-3)}=u^{-2}v^{13} \]
STEP 3
Negative Index Law \(a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^{n}}\) Turn every negative index into a positive one by flipping the base.
Rewrite the negative index as a positive one, since \(a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}\): \[ u^{-2}v^{13}=\frac{v^{13}}{u^2} \]
\( \dfrac{v^{13}}{u^2} \)

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{(x^{2}y)^{3}}{x^{4}y^{-2}}\) and express your answer with positive indices.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Power of a product: \((x^{2}y)^{3}=x^{6}y^{3}\).
Step 2. Quotient law: \(\dfrac{x^{6}y^{3}}{x^{4}y^{-2}}=x^{6-4}y^{3-(-2)}=x^{2}y^{5}\).
Step 3. All indices are already positive, so the answer is \(x^{2}y^{5}\).
Answer: \(x^{2}y^{5}\)
