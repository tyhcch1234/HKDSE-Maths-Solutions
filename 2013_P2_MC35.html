HKDSE 2013 Paper 2 Q35 Quadratic Equations Roots of Equations

Sum and Product of Roots

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the distinct roots of the equation $3x = x^2 - 5$, then $\alpha\beta =$

A. $3$
B. $-3$
C. $5$
D. $-5$
1

Rewrite in Standard Form ▼

The given equation is:

$$3x = x^2 - 5$$

Bring all terms to one side to write it as a standard quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$:

$$x^2 - 3x - 5 = 0$$

Hint: The standard form is $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$. Here, $a = 1$, $b = -3$, $c = -5$.
2

Identify the Roots ▼

The problem states that $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the distinct roots of the equation.

This means:

$$\alpha^2 - 3\alpha - 5 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \beta^2 - 3\beta - 5 = 0$$

Why "distinct"? If $\alpha = \beta$, the equation would have a repeated root, and we would need to check for that. Here, the word "distinct" tells us $\alpha \neq \beta$, so the product of roots formula applies directly.
3

Use the Product of Roots Formula ▼

For a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$:

$$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$$

$$\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$$

We only need the product formula for this question:

$$\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$$

Where do these formulas come from? They come from expanding $a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = 0$ and comparing coefficients. The constant term $c$ equals $a\alpha\beta$, giving $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$.
4

Substitute and Compute ▼

From Step 1, we identified $a = 1$, $b = -3$, $c = -5$.

Substitute into the product formula:

$$\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{-5}{1} = -5$$

$\therefore \alpha\beta = -5$

The correct answer is D.
