Laws of Indices & Logarithms

Logarithms Quadratic in log y
Question Multiple Choice

If \( x - \log y = x^2 - \log y^2 - 10 = 2 \), then \( y = \)

A. \(100\)
B. \(2\) or \(-4\)
C. \(\dfrac{1}{100}\) or \(10000\)
D. \(\dfrac{1}{10000}\) or \(100\)
Logarithm Laws Substitution Quadratic Equation Factorization Exponential Form

Step-by-step Solution

The problem gives a chain of equalities:

\[ x - \log y = x^2 - \log y^2 - 10 = 2 \]

💡 Hint

Every part of the chain equals \(2\). So we can write two separate equations:

\[ x - \log y = 2 \quad \text{...(1)} \]
\[ x^2 - \log y^2 - 10 = 2 \quad \text{...(2)} \]

Equation (2) contains \( \log y^2 \). Use the power law of logarithms:

📘 Power Law

\[ \log y^2 = 2 \log y \]

Rewrite (2):

\[ x^2 - 2\log y - 10 = 2 \]

Bring terms together:

\[ x^2 - 2\log y - 12 = 0 \quad \text{...(2')} \]

From equation (1):

\[ x - \log y = 2 \]

Make \(x\) the subject:

\[ x = \log y + 2 \quad \text{...(3)} \]

💡 Why?

Substituting this into (2') removes \(x\) and leaves an equation in \(\log y\) only — a quadratic hidden behind logarithms.

Replace \(x\) in \(x^2 - 2\log y - 12 = 0\):

\[ (\log y + 2)^2 - 2\log y - 12 = 0 \]

Expand the square:

\[ (\log y)^2 + 4\log y + 4 - 2\log y - 12 = 0 \]

Combine like terms:

\[ (\log y)^2 + 2\log y - 8 = 0 \]

✅ Key Observation

This is a quadratic equation in \(\log y\). Let \(u = \log y\) and solve \(u^2 + 2u - 8 = 0\).

Factorize \(u^2 + 2u - 8 = 0\):

\[ (\log y + 4)(\log y - 2) = 0 \]

Therefore:

\[ \log y = -4 \]
\[ \log y = 2 \]

💡 Check

Sum of roots \(= -4 + 2 = -2\) ✓ (coefficient of \(\log y\) is \(2\), so sum \(= -2\)). Product \(= (-4)(2) = -8\) ✓.

Since the logarithm is base 10, use \( \log y = k \iff y = 10^k \).

Case 1:

\[ \log y = -4 \implies y = 10^{-4} = \frac{1}{10000} \]

Case 2:

\[ \log y = 2 \implies y = 10^{2} = 100 \]

🎯 Conclusion

\[ y = \frac{1}{10000} \quad \text{or} \quad y = 100 \]

This matches Option D.

Option D

Key Exam Skills

  • ▸ Break chained equalities into separate equations — each part equals the given constant.
  • ▸ Power law of logarithms: \(\log y^n = n\log y\). This turned \( \log y^2 \) into \(2\log y\).
  • ▸ Recognize hidden quadratics: an equation in \(\log y\) can be solved by factorization.
  • ▸ Remember base 10: \(\log y = k \iff y = 10^k\). Negative logs give fractions, don't discard them!
