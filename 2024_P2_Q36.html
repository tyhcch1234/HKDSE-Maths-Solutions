The sum of the first $n$ terms of a sequence is $S(n) = n(2n+3)$. Which of the following are true?
  1. I. 14 is a term of the sequence.
  2. II. The $n$th term of the sequence is $4n+1$.
  3. III. The sequence is an arithmetic sequence.
  • A. I and II only
  • B. I and III only
  • C. II and III only
  • D. I, II and III
Step 1 · Given
The sum of the first $n$ terms is $$S(n)=n(2n+3)=2n^{2}+3n.$$
Hint: always record $S(n)$ first — every other quantity is derived from it.
Step 2 · Find T(n)
For $n\ge 2$, $$T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$$ $$=n(2n+3)-(n-1)\bigl[2(n-1)+3\bigr]$$ $$=(2n^{2}+3n)-(n-1)(2n+1)$$ $$=(2n^{2}+3n)-(2n^{2}-n-1)=4n+1.$$ So statement II is TRUE.
Hint: subtract consecutive partial sums to isolate a single term.
Term from Partial Sums \(T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)\) \(S(n)=2n^{2}+3n\), so \(T(n)=(2n^{2}+3n)-(2n^{2}-n-1)=4n+1\).
Step 3 · Check common difference
$$T(n)-T(n-1)=4n+1-\bigl[4(n-1)+1\bigr]=4.$$ The difference is a constant, so the sequence is arithmetic with common difference $4$: $$T(1)=5,\;T(2)=9,\;T(3)=13,\;T(4)=17,\dots$$ Statement III is TRUE.
Hint: an arithmetic sequence needs a constant $T(n)-T(n-1)$.
Common Difference of an Arithmetic Sequence \(d=T(n)-T(n-1)\) \(d=(4n+1)-(4n-3)=4\), a constant, so the sequence is arithmetic.
Step 4 · Test 14 as a term
Suppose $T(n)=14$: $$4n+1=14 \;\Rightarrow\; 4n=13 \;\Rightarrow\; n=\frac{13}{4}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{+}.$$ Since $n$ must be a positive integer, $14$ is NOT a term. Statement I is FALSE.
Hint: a term index $n$ must be a positive integer.
Answer: C — II and III only.
