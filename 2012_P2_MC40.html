Angle between two planes of a regular tetrahedron
3D Geometry Regular Tetrahedron Angle between Planes Cosine Formula

Question

Multiple Choice

The figure shows a regular tetrahedron \(ABCD\). Find the angle between the plane \(ABC\) and the plane \(BCD\) correct to the nearest degree.

A. \(48^\circ\)     B. \(53^\circ\)     C. \(60^\circ\)     D. \(71^\circ\)

A regular tetrahedron has four faces, each an equilateral triangle of the same side length. The required angle is the dihedral angle between two adjacent faces — the angle you would "see" when looking straight down the common edge \(BC\).

Part (a) — Set up the model

Understanding

Let the side length of the regular tetrahedron be \(l\). We measure the dihedral angle along the common edge \(BC\) by dropping perpendiculars from \(A\) and \(D\) to \(BC\).

Side \(l\) = 1.000
Median \(AM\) = 0.866
Angle between planes = 70.53°
Step 11M

Each face of a regular tetrahedron is an equilateral triangle. In \(\triangle ABC\) with side \(l = AB = BC = CA\), the median from \(A\) to the midpoint \(M\) of \(BC\) is also an altitude.

\[ AM = \sqrt{l^2 - \left(\tfrac{l}{2}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{3l^2}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l \]

Hint: Use a slider above to change \(l\) — the median \(AM\) scales linearly, but the angle never changes. That is the signature of a shape-only (similar) problem.
Step 21M

By symmetry, in \(\triangle BCD\), the median from \(D\) to the same midpoint \(M\) of \(BC\) has the same length:

\[ DM = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l \]

The required dihedral angle between plane \(ABC\) and plane \(BCD\) is exactly the angle \(\angle AMD\).

Hint: \(AM \perp BC\) and \(DM \perp BC\). The angle between two planes is measured by the angle between the two lines that are perpendicular to the intersection line (here \(BC\)) at one point.
Key result: \(AM = DM = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\) and the required angle is \(\angle AMD\).

Part (b) — Apply the cosine formula in \(\triangle AMD\)

Calculation

Now look at the isosceles triangle formed by the two medians and the opposite edge \(AD = l\). We apply the cosine formula to find \(\cos\angle AMD\).

\(AM = DM\) = 0.866
\(AD = l\) = 1.000
\(\cos\angle AMD\) = 0.3333
\(\angle AMD\) = 70.53°
Step 11M

In \(\triangle AMD\) the three sides are known: \[ AM = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l,\qquad DM = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l,\qquad AD = l \]

Apply the cosine formula at vertex \(M\):

\[ \cos\angle AMD = \frac{AM^2 + DM^2 - AD^2}{2\cdot AM\cdot DM} \]

Hint: Since the two equal sides are \(AM\) and \(DM\), the angle at \(M\) is the one enclosed by them — exactly the dihedral angle we want.
Step 21A

Substitute the side lengths:

\[ \cos\angle AMD = \frac{\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\right)^2+\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\right)^2-l^2} {2\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l} = \frac{\dfrac{3}{4}l^2+\dfrac{3}{4}l^2-l^2}{\dfrac{3}{2}l^2} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}l^2}{\dfrac{3}{2}l^2} = \frac{1}{3} \]

Hint: The \(l^2\) cancels completely — another sign that only the shape matters, not the size.
So \(\cos\angle AMD = \dfrac{1}{3}\).
Step 31A

Take the inverse cosine and round to the nearest degree:

\[ \angle AMD = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) \approx 70.52877937^\circ \approx 71^\circ \]

Hint: A good sanity check — the value \(70.5^\circ\) lies slightly above \(60^\circ\), which makes sense: the two faces of a tetrahedron fold back a little more than the \(60^\circ\) of a flat triangular net.
Answer: \(\angle AMD \approx 71^\circ\), so the correct option is D.

Summary at a glance

  • Let the side of the regular tetrahedron be \(l\).
  • Every face is an equilateral triangle, so the median (altitude) from \(A\) to the midpoint \(M\) of \(BC\) is \(AM = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\).
  • By symmetry, \(DM = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l\) as well.
  • The angle between plane \(ABC\) and plane \(BCD\) equals the angle \(\angle AMD\).
  • Cosine formula in \(\triangle AMD\): \(\cos\angle AMD = \dfrac{AM^2+DM^2-AD^2}{2\cdot AM\cdot DM} = \dfrac{1}{3}\).
  • \(\angle AMD = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right) \approx 71^\circ\) — the answer is D.
