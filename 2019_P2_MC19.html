Q19 The Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a trapezium with \(AB /\!/ DC\) and \(\angle ABD = 90^\circ\). If \(AB = 18\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 26\text{ cm}\) and \(AD = 30\text{ cm}\), find the area of the trapezium \(ABCD\).

  • A. \(336\text{ cm}^2\)
  • B. \(400\text{ cm}^2\)
  • C. \(504\text{ cm}^2\)
  • D. \(552\text{ cm}^2\)
Hint: The key is to find the height of the trapezium, which is the diagonal \(BD\). Two right-angled triangles, \(\triangle ABD\) and \(\triangle BCD\), share it.

1 Find the Height \(BD\)

Step 1

In right-angled \(\triangle ABD\), the right angle is at \(B\), so \(AD\) is the hypotenuse:

\[ AD^2 = AB^2 + BD^2 \] \[ 30^2 = 18^2 + BD^2 \]
Hint: \(AB\) is a leg, not the hypotenuse — the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle.
Step 2

Rearrange and take the positive square root:

\[ BD^2 = 900 - 324 = 576 \] \[ BD = 24\text{ cm} \]
Hint: A length must be positive, so we take only \(BD = +24\).
Step 3
Height of trapezium: \(BD = 24\text{ cm}\).
Hint: \(BD \perp AB\) and \(AB /\!/ DC\), so \(BD\) is also the perpendicular distance between the parallel sides \(AB\) and \(DC\).

2 Find the Top Base \(CD\)

Because \(AB /\!/ DC\), the alternate angles give \(\angle CDB = \angle ABD = 90^\circ\). So \(\triangle BCD\) is also right-angled at \(D\). Move the slider to see how \(CD\) pairs with \(BD = 24\) to give \(BC = 26\).

\(CD = \) 13.0 cm \(BD = 24\) cm \(BC\) (this \(CD\)) = 27.3 cm Target \(BC = 26\) cm
0 / 3
Step 1

Since \(AB /\!/ DC\) (alternate angles):

\[ \angle CDB = \angle ABD = 90^\circ \]
Hint: \(BD\) acts as a transversal; interior alternate angles between parallel lines are equal.
Step 2

In right-angled \(\triangle BCD\), \(BC\) is the hypotenuse:

\[ BC^2 = BD^2 + CD^2 \] \[ 26^2 = 24^2 + CD^2 \] \[ CD^2 = 676 - 576 = 100 \]
Hint: Only 10 is possible for a length (\(CD > 0\)).
Step 3
Top base: \(CD = 10\text{ cm}\).
Hint: Now we know both bases: \(AB = 18\) and \(CD = 10\), with height \(BD = 24\).

3 Compute the Area of \(ABCD\)

The area of a trapezium is \(\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{sum of parallel sides}) \times \text{height}\). Adjust the two bases below and watch the shaded area update in real time (height fixed at \(24\text{ cm}\)).

Step 1

Apply the trapezium area formula with bases \(AB = 18\), \(CD = 10\), height \(= BD = 24\):

\[ \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(AB + CD)\times BD \] \[ = \frac{1}{2}(18 + 10)(24) \]
Hint: The height must be perpendicular to both bases — that is exactly \(BD\).
Step 2
\[ = \frac{1}{2}(28)(24) = 14 \times 24 = 336\text{ cm}^2 \]
Answer: Area of \(ABCD = 336\text{ cm}^2\) → choice A.
Hint: Sanity check via two triangles: \(\tfrac{1}{2}(18)(24) + \tfrac{1}{2}(10)(24) = 216 + 120 = 336\). ✓

✓ Summary of Answers

  • Height: \(BD = \sqrt{AD^2 - AB^2} = \sqrt{30^2 - 18^2} = 24\text{ cm}\)
  • Top base: \(CD = \sqrt{BC^2 - BD^2} = \sqrt{26^2 - 24^2} = 10\text{ cm}\)
  • Area: \(\dfrac{1}{2}(AB + CD)\cdot BD = \dfrac{1}{2}(18 + 10)(24) = 336\text{ cm}^2\)
  • Correct option: A. \(336\text{ cm}^2\)
