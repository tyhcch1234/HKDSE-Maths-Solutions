Question

Simplify \(\displaystyle \frac{m^{-12} n^8}{n^3}\) and express your answer with positive indices.

Step-by-step Solution

Start with the original expression:

\[ \frac{m^{-12} n^8}{n^3} \]

💡 The term \(n^8\) is in the numerator and \(n^3\) is in the denominator.

Group the powers with the same base:

\[ = m^{-12} \times \frac{n^8}{n^3} \]

💡 Now we can apply the quotient law of indices to the \(n\) terms.

Use the quotient law: \(\displaystyle \frac{a^x}{a^y} = a^{x-y}\)

\[ \begin{aligned} &= m^{-12} \times n^{8-3} \\ &= m^{-12} n^5 \end{aligned} \]

💡 Remember: \(8 - 3 = 5\). The \(n\) term now has a positive exponent.

The \(m\) term still has a negative exponent. Use \(\displaystyle a^{-k} = \frac{1}{a^k}\):

\[ \begin{aligned} &= \frac{1}{m^{12}} \times n^5 \\[4pt] &= \frac{n^5}{m^{12}} \end{aligned} \]
✅ Final answer: \(\displaystyle \frac{n^5}{m^{12}}\)

💡 Always check that all indices in the final answer are positive.
