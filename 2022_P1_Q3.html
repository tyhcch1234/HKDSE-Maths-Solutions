[2022 #3]

Simplify  \(\displaystyle \frac{3}{k-9}+\frac{2}{5k+6}\).

[3 marks]

Step-by-step solution

1

Identify the two denominators Given

First fraction: \(\dfrac{3}{k-9}\), denominator \(k-9\).
Second fraction: \(\dfrac{2}{5k+6}\), denominator \(5k+6\).

The two denominators \(k-9\) and \(5k+6\) are different and share no common factor, so the L.C.M. of the denominators is simply their product \((k-9)(5k+6)\).

💡 Two different linear denominators with no common factor → multiply them to get the common denominator.
2

Rewrite with the common denominator 1M

Adding Algebraic Fractions \(\dfrac{a}{b}+\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ad+bc}{bd}\) The denominators \(k-9\) and \(5k+6\) share no factor, so the common denominator is their product.

Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction so both have denominator \((k-9)(5k+6)\):

\[ \frac{3}{k-9}+\frac{2}{5k+6} =\frac{3(5k+6)+2(k-9)}{(k-9)(5k+6)} \]

💡 Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by the other denominator — this is the key mark (1M) for a correct common denominator.
3

Expand the numerator 1M

Distributive Law \(3(5k+6)=15k+18,\qquad 2(k-9)=2k-18\) Each numerator is multiplied by the other denominator, keeping the signs inside the brackets.

Expand each product carefully:

\[ 3(5k+6)=15k+18 \] \[ 2(k-9)=2k-18 \]

So the numerator becomes:

\[ 15k+18+2k-18 \]

💡 Distribute the coefficient over both terms inside the bracket — watch the signs, especially \(+2(k-9)\).
4

Collect like terms 1A

Collecting Like Terms \(15k+2k=17k,\qquad +18-18=0\) The constant terms cancel exactly, leaving the numerator \(17k\).

Add the \(k\)-terms and add the constant terms:

\[ 15k+2k=17k \qquad +18-18=0 \]

Numerator simplifies to \(17k\). The denominator is unchanged.

💡 The constant terms \(+18\) and \(-18\) cancel exactly — a very common place to lose the answer mark.
5

Write the final simplified form

\[ \frac{3}{k-9}+\frac{2}{5k+6} =\frac{17k}{(k-9)(5k+6)} \]

Check: the numerator \(17k\) shares no factor with \(k-9\) or \(5k+6\), so no further cancelling is possible.

💡 Always check whether the numerator can still cancel with the denominator — here it cannot.
Answer \(\displaystyle \frac{3}{k-9}+\frac{2}{5k+6}=\frac{17k}{(k-9)(5k+6)}\)

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{4}{k-7}+\dfrac{3}{2k+5}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The denominators \(k-7\) and \(2k+5\) have no common factor, so the common denominator is \((k-7)(2k+5)\).
Step 2. Rewrite: \(\dfrac{4(2k+5)+3(k-7)}{(k-7)(2k+5)}\).
Step 3. Expand: \(4(2k+5)=8k+20\) and \(3(k-7)=3k-21\).
Step 4. The numerator is \(8k+20+3k-21=11k-1\).
Step 5. Hence \(\dfrac{4}{k-7}+\dfrac{3}{2k+5}=\dfrac{11k-1}{(k-7)(2k+5)}\), and \(11k-1\) shares no factor with either denominator.
Answer: \(\dfrac{11k-1}{(k-7)(2k+5)}\)

Note: the expression is defined for \(k\neq 9\) and \(k\neq -\tfrac{6}{5}\).
