Question

Factorize  \( h^2 - 6h - 4k^2 - 12k \).

A. \((h-2k)(h-2k+6)\)
B. \((h-2k)(h+2k+6)\)
C. \((h+2k)(h-2k-6)\)
D. \((h+2k)(h+2k-6)\)

Step-by-Step Solution

We are asked to factorize:

\( h^2 - 6h - 4k^2 - 12k \)

There are 4 terms. When you see four terms, a common strategy is grouping.

Notice the terms involve two variables, \(h\) and \(k\).

💡 Hint — Grouping Strategy

For 4 terms, pair them so that each pair shares a common factor. Then look for a common bracket.

Pair the \(h^2\) with \(-4k^2\) and the \(-6h\) with \(-12k\):

\( (h^2 - 4k^2) + (-6h - 12k) \)

Why this pairing? Because \(h^2 - 4k^2\) is a difference of squares, and \(-6h - 12k\) has a common factor of \(-6\).

💡 Hint — Difference of Squares

\( a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b) \). Here \(a = h\) and \(b = 2k\), since \((2k)^2 = 4k^2\).

Group 1: \( h^2 - 4k^2 \)

\( h^2 - 4k^2 = (h - 2k)(h + 2k) \)

Group 2: \( -6h - 12k \)

\( -6h - 12k = -6(h + 2k) \)

So the expression becomes:

\( (h - 2k)(h + 2k) - 6(h + 2k) \)

💡 Hint — Common Factor in Group 2

Both \(-6h\) and \(-12k\) are divisible by \(-6\). Factoring out \(-6\) gives \(-6(h + 2k)\).

Now we have:

\( \underbrace{(h - 2k)(h + 2k)}_{\text{first term}} - \underbrace{6(h + 2k)}_{\text{second term}} \)

The bracket \((h + 2k)\) appears in both terms. This is the common factor!

💡 Hint — Factor out the bracket

Treat \((h+2k)\) as a single object. Just like \(ax - 6a = a(x - 6)\).

Factor out \((h + 2k)\):

\( (h + 2k)\big[(h - 2k) - 6\big] \)

Simplify the inner bracket:

\( (h + 2k)(h - 2k - 6) \)

✅ Final Answer

\( (h + 2k)(h - 2k - 6) \)

This matches option C.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ 4 terms → try grouping. Rearrange them to expose common factors.
  • ✓ Difference of squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)\).
  • ✓ Common bracket trick: when the same bracket appears in both terms, factor it out.
