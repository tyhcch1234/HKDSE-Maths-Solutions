Step 1 · Rewrite as slope-intercept form

Make $y$ the subject of $x+ay+b=0$:

$$ay=-x-b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac{1}{a}\,x-\frac{b}{a}$$

So the slope is $m=-\dfrac{1}{a}$ and the $y$-intercept is $-\dfrac{b}{a}$. The $x$-intercept comes from $y=0$: $x=-b$.

Hint: dividing by $a$ needs $a\neq 0$ — a straight line here always requires $a\neq0$.
Step 2 · I. $a<0$

From the graph, the line rises from left to right, so the slope is positive:

$$-\frac{1}{a}>0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{a}<0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a<0$$

✓ Statement I is true.

Watch the sign flip: multiplying an inequality by a negative quantity reverses the direction ($-1/a>0 \Rightarrow -1<0\cdot a$ is not valid — instead note $1/a$ must be negative).
