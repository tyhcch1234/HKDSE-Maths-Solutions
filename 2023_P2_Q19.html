Rhombus & its Diagonals

It is given that \(ABCD\) is a rhombus. Denote the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\) by \(E\). Which of the following must be true?
  1. \(AE=BE\)
  2. \(\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{BE}{BD}\)
  3. \(AE^{2}+BE^{2}=CD^{2}\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
STEP 1 · PROPERTIES OF A RHOMBUS

In a rhombus, all four sides are equal and the diagonals bisect each other at right angles.

\[AB=BC=CD=DA,\qquad \angle AEB=90^\circ,\qquad AE=EC,\quad BE=ED\]

Here \(E\) is the centre of the rhombus, so \(AE=\tfrac12 AC\) and \(BE=\tfrac12 BD\).

Properties of a Rhombus \(AB=BC=CD=DA,\qquad \angle AEB=90^{\circ},\qquad AE=EC,\ BE=ED\) The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, so \(AE=\tfrac12AC\) and \(BE=\tfrac12BD\).
STEP 2 · TEST STATEMENT I

Is \(AE=BE\) always true? Suppose \(AE=BE\). Then \(\triangle ABE\) is a right-angled isosceles triangle with \(\angle AEB=90^\circ\).

\[\angle ABE=\angle BAE=\frac{180^\circ-90^\circ}{2}=45^\circ\]

Since the diagonal \(BD\) bisects \(\angle ABC\), \(\angle CBE=\angle ABE=45^\circ\), so

\[\angle ABC=\angle ABE+\angle CBE=45^\circ+45^\circ=90^\circ\]

This forces \(ABCD\) to be a square. But a rhombus need not be a square, so \(AE=BE\) is not always true.

💡 Counter-example: in a rhombus with \(\angle ABC=60^\circ\), \(AE\ne BE\) — see the diagram.
STEP 3 · TEST STATEMENT II

Because the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other,

\[\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{BE}{BD}=\frac{1}{2}\]

Therefore

\[\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac{BE}{BD}\;\checkmark\]

Statement II is true for every rhombus.

Diagonals of a Parallelogram \(AE=\tfrac{1}{2}AC,\qquad BE=\tfrac{1}{2}BD\) Bisecting diagonals make both ratios equal to \(\tfrac12\), so statement II always holds.
STEP 4 · TEST STATEMENT III

Since \(\angle AEB=90^\circ\), Pythagoras' theorem in \(\triangle ABE\) gives

\[AE^{2}+BE^{2}=AB^{2}\]

All sides of a rhombus are equal, so \(AB=CD\). Hence

\[AE^{2}+BE^{2}=CD^{2}\;\checkmark\]

Statement III is true for every rhombus.

Pythagoras’ Theorem \(AE^{2}+BE^{2}=AB^{2}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle ABE\), and \(AB=CD\) in a rhombus, giving statement III.
STEP 5 · CONCLUSION

I  ✘ False  (only when the rhombus is a square)
II  ✔ True  (\(\frac{AE}{AC}=\frac{BE}{BD}=\frac12\))
III  ✔ True  (\(AE^{2}+BE^{2}=AB^{2}=CD^{2}\))

Answer: C  (II and III only)

Question. \(ABCD\) is a rectangle whose diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) meet at \(E\). Which of the following must be true? I. \(AE=BE\) II. \(\dfrac{AE}{AC}=\dfrac{BE}{BD}\) III. \(AE^{2}+BE^{2}=CD^{2}\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, so \(AC=BD\) and \(AE=EC=\tfrac12AC\), \(BE=ED=\tfrac12BD\).
Step 2. I: \(AE=\tfrac12AC=\tfrac12BD=BE\) — true.
Step 3. II: both ratios equal \(\tfrac12\) — true.
Step 4. III: AE=BE=\tfrac{1}{2}\\sqrt{AB^{2}+BC^{2}}, so AE^{2}+BE^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(AB^{2}+BC^{2}), while CD^{2}=AB^{2}; these agree only when AB=BC (a square), so III is not always true.
Step 5. Hence only I and II must be true.
Answer: I and II only
