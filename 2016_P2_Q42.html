Probability – Choosing Cans of Tea & Coffee

Probability Combination \(C_r^n\) "At least" Without Replacement

? Question

There are 9 cans of coffee and 3 cans of tea in a box. If 4 cans are randomly chosen from the box, find the probability that at least 2 cans of tea are chosen.

  • A.\(\dfrac{13}{55}\)
  • B.\(\dfrac{21}{55}\)
  • C.\(\dfrac{34}{55}\)
  • D.\(\dfrac{42}{55}\)

Tap a choice to check your instinct, then work through the steps below.

1 Explore the Box

The box holds 12 cans in total: 9 coffee (brown) and 3 tea (orange). Choose 4 of them and see what happens.

Darker cans are still in the box; faded cans have been taken out.

Pick the tea cans in your hand
0 tea chosen Number of ways: \(C_0^3 \times C_4^9\)

"At least 2 tea" means the 2-tea case or the 3-tea case.

2 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 Understand "at least 2 cans of tea" ▶

We choose 4 cans. "At least 2 are tea" means 2 or 3 (you cannot get 4 tea cans, since only 3 exist).

\[ P(\text{at least 2 tea}) = P(2\text{ tea}) + P(3\text{ tea}) \]

Hint: "At least" usually splits into separate exact cases that you add together (they are mutually exclusive).
Step 2 Count the total number of ways ▶

Total cans \( = 9 + 3 = 12 \). Choose any 4 of them:

\[ \text{Total ways} = C_4^{12} = 495 \]

Hint: \(C_r^n\) is the number of ways to choose \(r\) objects from \(n\) objects when order does not matter.
Step 3 Case: exactly 2 cans of tea ▶

Choose 2 tea from the 3 tea cans, and 2 coffee from the 9 coffee cans:

\[ C_2^{3} \times C_2^{9} = 3 \times 36 = 108 \]

\[ P(2\text{ tea}) = \frac{C_2^{3}\,C_2^{9}}{C_4^{12}} = \frac{108}{495} = \frac{12}{55} \]

Hint: The two selections are independent choices, so the counts are multiplied. The total goes in the denominator.
Step 4 Case: exactly 3 cans of tea ▶

Choose all 3 tea cans, and 1 coffee from the 9 coffee cans:

\[ C_3^{3} \times C_1^{9} = 1 \times 9 = 9 \]

\[ P(3\text{ tea}) = \frac{C_3^{3}\,C_1^{9}}{C_4^{12}} = \frac{9}{495} = \frac{1}{55} \]

Hint: \(C_3^{3}=1\) – there is only one way to take all three tea cans.
Step 5 Add the cases together ▶

\[ P(\ge 2\text{ tea}) = \frac{12}{55} + \frac{1}{55} = \frac{13}{55} \]

The two events are mutually exclusive, so we simply add the probabilities.

Step 6 Alternative check (complement) ▶

Instead of adding, subtract the unwanted cases (0 or 1 tea):

\[ P(\ge 2) = 1 - P(0\text{ tea}) - P(1\text{ tea}) \]

\[ P(0\text{ tea}) = \frac{C_4^{9}}{C_4^{12}} = \frac{126}{495},\qquad P(1\text{ tea}) = \frac{C_1^{3}C_3^{9}}{C_4^{12}} = \frac{252}{495} \]

\[ 1 - \frac{126+252}{495} = 1 - \frac{378}{495} = \frac{117}{495} = \frac{13}{55} \]

Hint: The complement method is a great way to double-check your answer.

3 The Answer

Required probability
\(\dfrac{13}{55}\)
\(\Rightarrow\) Option A
