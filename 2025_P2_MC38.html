Q38 The Question

Multiple-choice — circle geometry

In the figure, \(AB\) is a diameter of the circle \(ABCD\). Denote the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\) by \(E\). It is given that \(DT\) is the tangent to the circle at \(D\).

If \(\angle BEC = 96^\circ\) and \(\angle CDT = 41^\circ\), then \(\angle CDE =\)

A. \(43^\circ\)     B. \(45^\circ\)     C. \(\mathbf{47^\circ}\)     D. \(49^\circ\)

Key: \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral; the segment \(AD\) must be drawn as a construction.

Step 1

Label the figure correctly.

\(AB\) is a diameter, so the four points \(A, B, C, D\) all lie on the circle — i.e. \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral.

The two given angles are:

\[ \angle BEC = 96^\circ \quad\text{(at the intersection } E \text{ of } AC \text{ and } BD) \] \[ \angle CDT = 41^\circ \quad\text{(between chord } DC \text{ and tangent } DT) \]
Hint: Because \(AB\) is a diameter, joining \(AD\) will instantly give a right angle. This is the most useful construction in the whole question.
Step 2

Plan the chain of reasoning.

We want \(\angle CDE\). Notice that \(E\), \(C\), \(D\) form a triangle \(\triangle CDE\) containing the known angle \(\angle BEC\) as its exterior angle at \(E\). So we need \(\angle ACD\) — and that comes from \(\triangle ADC\) once \(AD\) is joined.

\[ \angle CDE + \angle ACD = \angle BEC \quad(\text{ext. }\angle\text{ of }\triangle) \]
Hint: The path is \(\;\angle CDT \to \angle DAC \to \angle ACD \to \angle CDE\). Each arrow uses exactly one circle theorem.

Part (b) Find \(\angle ACD\)

Focus on \(\triangle ADC\). Drag the slider to change the chord position and watch how the angles stay locked together.

41°
\(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\) — chord subtends semi-circle
\(\angle DAC = \angle CDT = 41° — ∠ in alt. segment
\(\angle ACD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \angle DAC = 49°\)
Step 1

Join \(AD\) and apply the angle in a semi-circle.

Since \(AB\) is a diameter, the angle subtended by \(AB\) at any point on the circle is a right angle. In particular, at \(D\):

\[ \angle ADC = 90^\circ \qquad (\angle \text{ in semi-circle}) \]
Hint: The angle mark at \(D\) inside \(\triangle ADC\) is exactly \(90^\circ\) — that is why joining \(AD\) is so powerful.
Step 2

Use the angle in the alternate segment.

\(DT\) is the tangent at \(D\), and \(DA\) is the chord forming the alternate segment. The angle between the tangent \(DT\) and the chord \(DC\) equals the angle in the alternate segment subtended by \(DC\):

\[ \angle DAC = \angle CDT = 41^\circ \qquad (\angle \text{ in alt. segment}) \]
Hint: The two orange arcs in the diagram are equal — the tangent-chord angle and the angle in the opposite arc.
Step 3

Angle sum of \(\triangle ADC\).

\[ \angle ACD + \angle DAC + \angle ADC = 180^\circ \qquad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle) \] \[ \angle ACD + 41^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ \boxed{\;\angle ACD = 49^\circ\;} \]
Answer of Part (b): \(\angle ACD = 49^\circ\)

Part (c) Find \(\angle CDE\)

Now use the exterior angle of \(\triangle CDE\) at \(E\).

96°
\(\angle ACD = 49^\circ\) (Part b)
\(\angle BEC = 96°\) — exterior angle of \(\triangle CDE\)
\(\angle CDE = \angle BEC - \angle ACD = 47°\)
Step 1

Exterior angle of a triangle.

In \(\triangle CDE\), the side \(CE\) is produced to \(A\), so \(\angle BEC\) (which equals \(\angle AED\) vertically opposite... more directly, the angle at \(E\) on the \(DB\)–\(EC\) side) is the exterior angle at \(E\):

\[ \angle CDE + \angle ACD = \angle BEC \qquad (\text{ext. } \angle \text{ of } \triangle) \] \[ \angle CDE + 49^\circ = 96^\circ \] \[ \boxed{\;\angle CDE = 47^\circ\;} \]
Hint: The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles. Here those are \(\angle CDE\) and \(\angle DCE = \angle ACD = 49^\circ\).
Answer: \(\angle CDE = 47^\circ\)  →  Option C

Summary of Answers

  • Construction: Join \(AD\).
  • \(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\)  (\(\angle\) in semi-circle)
  • \(\angle DAC = \angle CDT = 41^\circ\)  (\(\angle\) in alt. segment)
  • \(\angle ACD = 180^\circ - 41^\circ - 90^\circ = 49^\circ\)  (\(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\))
  • \(\angle CDE + \angle ACD = \angle BEC\)  (ext. \(\angle\) of \(\triangle\))
  • \(\angle CDE = 96^\circ - 49^\circ = 47^\circ\) → Option C
