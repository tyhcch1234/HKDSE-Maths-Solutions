HKDSE 2025 · Paper 2 · Q13

Formulas and Sequences — Recurrence Relation

Let $a_n$ be the $n$th term of a sequence. If $a_2 = 3$, $a_5 = 41$ and $a_{n+2} = 2a_{n+1} + a_n$ for any positive integer $n$, then $a_6 = \;?$

  • A. $99$
  • B. $101$
  • C. $239$
  • D. $243$

Step-by-step solution

1Write down the given information

We are told $a_2 = 3$ and $a_5 = 41$, with the recurrence $$a_{n+2} = 2a_{n+1} + a_n .$$

The unknowns are $a_3$ and $a_4$ — we need to pass through them from $a_2$ up to $a_5$.

Every term from the 3rd onward is "twice the previous term plus the one before that".

2Apply the rule with $n = 2$ and then $n = 3$

Put $n = 2$:   $a_4 = 2a_3 + a_2 = 2a_3 + 3$   $\cdots (1)$

Put $n = 3$:   $a_5 = 2a_4 + a_3$

Substituting $(1)$ into this:

$$a_5 = 2(2a_3 + 3) + a_3 = 4a_3 + 6 + a_3 = 5a_3 + 6 .$$

Chain the two equations so that everything is expressed in terms of the single unknown $a_3$.

3Solve for $a_3$, then find $a_4$

Use the known value $a_5 = 41$:

$$5a_3 + 6 = 41 \;\Rightarrow\; 5a_3 = 35 \;\Rightarrow\; a_3 = 7 .$$

Back-substitute into $(1)$:

$$a_4 = 2(7) + 3 = 14 + 3 = 17 .$$

Now the whole block $a_2, a_3, a_4, a_5$ is known — only $a_6$ is left.

4Use $n = 4$ to obtain $a_6$

The recurrence with $n = 4$ gives

$$a_6 = 2a_5 + a_4 .$$

Substitute $a_5 = 41$ and $a_4 = 17$:

$$a_6 = 2(41) + 17 = 82 + 17 = 99 .$$

Alternatively, first find $a_6$ from the relation directly; the arithmetic is exact and simple.

5Check and conclude

Sequence built up: $a_2 = 3 \to a_3 = 7 \to a_4 = 17 \to a_5 = 41 \to a_6 = 99$.

Re-check $a_5 = 2a_4 + a_3 = 2(17) + 7 = 41$ ✓.

Distractors: 101 and 243 come from mis-adding; 239 from doubling $a_5$ and adding $a_3$-type mistakes.

ANSWER
A  ·  $a_6 = 99$
