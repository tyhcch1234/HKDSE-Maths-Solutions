The equation of the straight lines $\ell$ and $L$ are $9x+12y-37=0$ and $12x+16y+85=0$ respectively. $\ell$ cuts the $x$-axis at the point $A$ while $L$ cuts the $y$-axis at the point $B$. Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $\ell$ is equal to the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $L$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$. Which of the following are true?
I.  $\Gamma$ is parallel to $L$.
II.  $\Gamma$ is perpendicular to $AB$.
III.  $\Gamma$ passes through the mid-point of $AB$.
A.  I and II only    B.  I and III only
C.  II and III only    D.  I, II and III
STEP 1 · SLOPES OF ℓ AND L For $\ell:\;9x+12y-37=0 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{9}{12}x+\tfrac{37}{12}$, so slope $=-\dfrac{9}{12}=-\dfrac34$.
For $L:\;12x+16y+85=0 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{12}{16}x-\tfrac{85}{16}$, so slope $=-\dfrac{12}{16}=-\dfrac34$.
Both slopes are equal $\Rightarrow$ $\ell // L$.
Condition for Parallel Lines \(m_{1}=m_{2}\) Both slopes equal \(-\tfrac34\), so \(\ell\) and \(L\) are parallel.
STEP 2 · FIND THE LOCUS Γ Since $\ell // L$, the locus of points equidistant from them is the line halfway between them, parallel to both:
Perpendicular Distance from a Point to a Line \(d=\dfrac{Ax_{0}+By_{0}+C}{\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}}\) If the numerator is negative, take its opposite, since a distance is positive. Equal distances from two parallel lines put \(P\) on the line halfway between them.
$$9x+12y-37=0,\qquad 12x+16y+85=0$$ Scale $\ell$ by $\tfrac43$: $\;12x+16y-\dfrac{148}{3}=0$. Average the constants: $$\Gamma:\;12x+16y+\frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{148}{3}\cdot(-1)+85\right)=0 \;\Rightarrow\;12x+16y+\frac{107}{6}=0.$$ So slope of $\Gamma=-\dfrac34$  $\Rightarrow$  I is TRUE (Γ // L).
STEP 3 · POINTS A AND B, SLOPE OF AB $A$ is the $x$-intercept of $\ell$: $\;y=0 \Rightarrow 9x=37 \Rightarrow A=\left(\dfrac{37}{9},0\right)$.
$B$ is the $y$-intercept of $L$: $\;x=0 \Rightarrow 16y=-85 \Rightarrow B=\left(0,-\dfrac{85}{16}\right)$.
Slope of $AB=\dfrac{-\frac{85}{16}-0}{0-\frac{37}{9}} =\dfrac{-85/16}{-37/9}=\dfrac{85\cdot 9}{16\cdot 37}=\dfrac{765}{592}\approx 1.292$.
Slope of $\Gamma \times$ slope of $AB=\left(-\tfrac34\right)\!\left(\tfrac{765}{592}\right) =-\dfrac{2295}{2368}\neq -1$. II is FALSE.
STEP 4 · MID-POINT OF AB
Midpoint Formula \(\left(\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},\ \dfrac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2}\right)\) The midpoint of \(AB\) is tested in the equation of \(\Gamma\) to settle statement III.
Mid-point of $AB$: $$M=\left(\frac{\frac{37}{9}+0}{2},\;\frac{0-\frac{85}{16}}{2}\right) =\left(\frac{37}{18},\;-\frac{85}{32}\right).$$ Check in $\Gamma:\;12x+16y+\dfrac{107}{6}=0$: $$12\cdot\frac{37}{18}+16\cdot\left(-\frac{85}{32}\right)+\frac{107}{6} =\frac{74}{3}-\frac{85}{2}+\frac{107}{6} =\frac{148-255+107}{6}=0.\;\checkmark$$ Alternatively: $\Gamma$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AC$ (foot $C$ of $\perp$ from $A$ to $L$), and $\angle ACB=90^\circ$ makes $AB$ the diameter, so the mid-point of $AB$ (the centre) lies on the $\perp$ bisector $\Gamma$. III is TRUE.
I   II ✗   III  $\Rightarrow$  Answer: B (I and III only)

Question. The equations of the straight lines \(\ell\) and \(L\) are \(6x+8y-11=0\) and \(3x+4y+7=0\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point such that its perpendicular distance from \(\ell\) equals its perpendicular distance from \(L\). Find the equation of the locus of \(P\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Slopes: \(\ell\) gives \(y=-\tfrac34x+\tfrac{11}{8}\) and \(L\) gives \(y=-\tfrac34x-\tfrac74\), so \(\ell // L\).
Step 2. Rewrite \(\ell\) with the same coefficients as \(L\): divide \(6x+8y-11=0\) by \(2\), giving \(3x+4y-\tfrac{11}{2}=0\).
Step 3. The locus is the line halfway between them,
so average the constants: \(\dfrac{1}{2}\left(-\dfrac{11}{2}+7\right)=\dfrac{3}{4}\).
Step 4. Hence the locus is \(3x+4y+\dfrac{3}{4}=0\), i.e. \(12x+16y+3=0\).
Step 5. Check: \(\ell\) has constant \(-\tfrac{11}{2}=-5.5\), \(L\) has \(7\), and the midpoint constant \(-0.75\) is exactly halfway ✓
Answer: \(12x+16y+3=0\)
Hint: equal perpendicular distances to two parallel lines means the locus is the mid-parallel line — same slope as both lines, and it is the perpendicular bisector of any segment joining the two lines.
