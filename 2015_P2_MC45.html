Statistics — Effect on Mean, Median and Variance when the largest datum is removed
Mean Median Variance Removing a Datum Counter-example

Q Question Multiple Choice

Let \(x_1\), \(y_1\) and \(z_1\) be the mean, the median and the variance of a group of numbers \(\{a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_{50}\}\) respectively, while \(x_2\), \(y_2\) and \(z_2\) be the mean, the median and the variance of the group of numbers \(\{a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_{49}\}\) respectively.

If \(x_1 = a_{50}\), which of the following must be true?

I. \(\;x_1 = x_2\)

II. \(\;y_1 \ge y_2\)

III. \(\;z_1 \le z_2\)

A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

1Write down the two means1M

\[x_1=\frac{a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{49}+a_{50}}{50},\qquad x_2=\frac{a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{49}}{49}\]

Hint: Both means share the same numerator \(a_1+\cdots+a_{49}\). Let \(S=a_1+\cdots+a_{49}\).
2Use the given condition \(x_1=a_{50}\)1M

\[\frac{S+a_{50}}{50}=a_{50}\;\Longrightarrow\;S+a_{50}=50a_{50}\;\Longrightarrow\;S=49a_{50}\]

Therefore \(x_2=\dfrac{S}{49}=\dfrac{49a_{50}}{49}=a_{50}\).

Hint: The condition "the mean equals the largest datum" forces the sum of the other 49 numbers to be exactly \(49a_{50}\).
3Conclude1A

Since \(x_1=a_{50}\) and \(x_2=a_{50}\), we get \(x_1=x_2\).

Statement I is TRUE — \(x_1 = x_2\). ✔

1Recall how each median is found1M

With 50 numbers in ascending order, \(y_1=\dfrac{a_{25}+a_{26}}{2}\).

With 49 numbers, \(y_2=a_{25}\).

Hint: Removing the largest datum deletes \(a_{50}\); the ordering of \(a_1,\dots,a_{49}\) is unchanged, so the "middle" position shifts from the 25.5-th to the 25-th value.
2Construct a counter-example1M

Take \(a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_{24}=0\), \(\;a_{25}=98\), \(\;a_{26}=a_{27}=\cdots=a_{49}=147\), and choose \(a_{50}\) so that \(x_1=a_{50}\):

\[a_{50}=x_1=x_2=\frac{0\times 24+98+147\times 24}{49}=74\]

Then \(y_1=\dfrac{74+98}{2}=86\) and \(y_2=98\).

Hint: Here the removed datum \(74\) is smaller than the old median \(86\). When a datum below the median is removed, the median rises — so \(y_1\ge y_2\) can fail.
3Check against the condition \(x_1=a_{50}\)1A

Verify: the mean of all 50 numbers is \(x_1=\frac{98+147\times24+74}{50}=\frac{3700}{50}=74=a_{50}\). ✔

But \(y_1=86 < 98=y_2\), so \(y_1\ge y_2\) is violated.

Statement II is NOT always true — a valid counter-example gives \(y_1=86<98=y_2\). ✘

1Write both variances about their own means1M

\[50z_1=(a_1-x_1)^2+(a_2-x_1)^2+\cdots+(a_{49}-x_1)^2+(a_{50}-x_1)^2 \tag{1}\]

\[49z_2=(a_1-x_2)^2+(a_2-x_2)^2+\cdots+(a_{49}-x_2)^2 \tag{2}\]

Hint: Use the "definition" form of variance: sum of squared deviations divided by the number of data. Multiply through to avoid fractions.
2Subtract (2) from (1) and use \(x_1=x_2\)1M

Since \(x_1=x_2\), the first 49 squared terms cancel:

\[50z_1-49z_2=(a_{50}-x_1)^2\]

so \[50z_1=49z_2+(a_{50}-x_1)^2=49z_2+0=49z_2\]

Hint: The condition \(x_1=a_{50}\) makes the extra squared deviation \((a_{50}-x_1)^2\) vanish — the outlier sits exactly on the mean.
3Compare \(z_1\) with \(z_2\)1A

\[z_1=\frac{49}{50}z_2\le z_2\qquad(\text{since } z_2\ge 0)\]

Statement III is TRUE — \(z_1=\tfrac{49}{50}z_2\le z_2\). ✔

✓ Answers at a Glance

  • I. \(x_1=x_2\)  — TRUE. From \(x_1=a_{50}\) we get \(a_1+\cdots+a_{49}=49a_{50}\), hence \(x_2=\frac{49a_{50}}{49}=a_{50}=x_1\).
  • II. \(y_1\ge y_2\)  — FAILS. Counter-example: \(a_1=\cdots=a_{24}=0,\ a_{25}=98,\ a_{26}=\cdots=a_{49}=147,\ a_{50}=74\) gives \(y_1=86\not\ge 98=y_2\).
  • III. \(z_1\le z_2\)  — TRUE. Since \(x_1=x_2\), subtraction of the variance equations gives \(50z_1-49z_2=(a_{50}-x_1)^2=0\), so \(z_1=\frac{49}{50}z_2\le z_2\).
Only I and III must be true → Answer: B
