Q The Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rectangle. \(E\) is a point lying on \(AD\). Find \(\dfrac{CE}{AC}\).

  • A. \(\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta}\)
  • B. \(\dfrac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}\)
  • C. \(\sin\alpha \sin\beta\)
  • D. \(\cos\alpha \cos\beta\)

1 Part (a) — Exploring the Figure

Drag the sliders to reshape the rectangle and move point \(E\). Watch how \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) change.

\(\beta\) = -
\(CE/AC\) = -
\(\cos\alpha/\cos\beta\) = -
Step 1 Understanding

Since \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, all four angles are \(90^\circ\). Look at the diagonal \(AC\) — it cuts the rectangle into two congruent right-angled triangles:

\[\triangle ABC \text{ (right angle at } B), \qquad \triangle CDA\]

Hint: In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AC\) is the hypotenuse. The angle marked \(\alpha\) is the angle between the base \(AB\) and the diagonal \(AC\), i.e. \(\angle BAC=\alpha\).
Step 2 Understanding

Now look at \(E\) on \(AD\). The line \(CE\) creates a second right-angled triangle:

\[\triangle CDE \text{ with right angle at } D.\]

The angle marked \(\beta\) is at vertex \(C\), so \(\angle DCE=\beta\), and \(CE\) is the hypotenuse of \(\triangle CDE\).

Hint: Both triangles share the same vertical side \(CD\) — this is the key link between \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

2 Part (b)(i) — Ratio from \(\triangle ABC\)

Express \(AC\) in terms of the horizontal side \(AB\) and \(\cos\alpha\).

\(\alpha\) = -
\(\cos\alpha\) = -
\(AC = AB/\cos\alpha\)
Step 1 Apply ratio

Consider \(\triangle ABC\) with the right angle at \(B\). The angle at \(A\) is \(\alpha\), the side \(AB\) is adjacent to \(\alpha\), and \(AC\) is the hypotenuse.

\[\cos\alpha = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{AC}\]

Hint: The word SOH-CAH-TOA helps remember: CAH → \(\cos = \text{Adj}/\text{Hyp}\).
Step 2 Rearrange

Rearranging for \(AC\):

\[AC = \frac{AB}{\cos\alpha} \qquad \cdots (1)\]

\(AC = \dfrac{AB}{\cos\alpha}\)
Hint: Keep this expression — we'll need it to divide the other one by it later.

3 Part (b)(ii) — Ratio from \(\triangle CDE\)

Now express \(CE\) in terms of the shared side \(CD\) and \(\cos\beta\).

\(\beta\) = -
\(\cos\beta\) = -
\(CE = CD/\cos\beta\)
Step 1 Apply ratio

Consider \(\triangle CDE\) with the right angle at \(D\). The angle at \(C\) is \(\beta\), the side \(CD\) is adjacent to \(\beta\), and \(CE\) is the hypotenuse.

\[\cos\beta = \frac{CD}{CE}\]

Hint: \(CD\) is a vertical side of the rectangle — the same length as \(AB\). Remember this!
Step 2 Rearrange

Rearranging for \(CE\):

\[CE = \frac{CD}{\cos\beta} \qquad \cdots (2)\]

\(CE = \dfrac{CD}{\cos\beta}\)
Hint: Since \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, \(AB = CD\). So the numerators of (1) and (2) are actually equal.

4 Part (b)(iii) — Combine to get \(CE/AC\)

Divide equation (2) by equation (1) and use \(AB = CD\).

\(CE/AC\) (measured) = -
\(\cos\alpha/\cos\beta\) = -
Step 1 Setup

Write down the two results from (b)(i) and (b)(ii):

\[(1):\ AC=\frac{AB}{\cos\alpha} \qquad\qquad (2):\ CE=\frac{CD}{\cos\beta}\]

Hint: We want \(\dfrac{CE}{AC}\), so we divide (2) by (1).
Step 2 Divide

Dividing (2) by (1):

\[\frac{CE}{AC}=\frac{\ \frac{CD}{\cos\beta}\ }{\ \frac{AB}{\cos\alpha}\ }=\frac{CD}{\cos\beta}\cdot\frac{\cos\alpha}{AB}=\frac{CD}{AB}\cdot\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}\]

Hint: Remember: dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.
Step 3 Use rectangle property

Since \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, opposite sides are equal, so \(CD = AB\). Hence \(\dfrac{CD}{AB}=1\):

\[\frac{CE}{AC}=\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}\]

Answer: B   \(\dfrac{CE}{AC}=\dfrac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}\)
Hint: The shared side \(CD=AB\) cancels out — that's the whole trick! Without it, the ratio would still contain \(AB\).

✓ Summary of the Solution

  • From \(\triangle ABC\) (right angle at \(B\)):  \(\cos\alpha = \dfrac{AB}{AC}\)  ⇒  \(\dfrac{AB}{AC}\) is fixed by \(\alpha\).
  • From \(\triangle CDE\) (right angle at \(D\)):  \(\cos\beta = \dfrac{CD}{CE}\)  ⇒  \(\dfrac{CD}{CE}\) is fixed by \(\beta\).
  • Since \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, \(AB=CD\), so the shared side cancels:
  • \[\frac{CE}{AC}=\frac{CD}{AB}\cdot\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}=\frac{\cos\alpha}{\cos\beta}\]
  • Correct option: B
