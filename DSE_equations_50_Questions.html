Type 1

Linear Equations in One Unknown · 7 questions

Brackets, fractions and decimal coefficients

Core idea: An equation is a balance. Do the same thing to both sides, clear brackets first, then multiply every term by the LCM of the denominators so that no fractions are left, and finally collect \(x\) on one side and numbers on the other.

\(ax+b=c\Rightarrow x=\frac{c-b}{a}\)\(\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\Rightarrow ad=bc\)\(0.4x=\frac{2}{5}x\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 2 Q5 ↗

Solve the equation \(4x-7=3x+5\).

Answer\(x=12\)
  1. Collect the \(x\)-terms on the left by subtracting \(3x\) from both sides: \(4x-3x-7=5\), i.e. \(x-7=5\).
  2. Add 7 to both sides: \(x=5+7\).
  3. \(x=12\).
  4. Check: LHS \(=4(12)-7=48-7=41\); RHS \(=3(12)+5=36+5=41\). Both sides agree.
Common mistake

Moving a term across the equals sign changes its sign: \(-7\) becomes \(+7\). Writing \(x=5-7\) gives the wrong answer \(-2\).

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the equation \(3(2x-5)=4(x+1)-3\).

Answer\(x=8\)
  1. Expand the left side: \(3(2x-5)=6x-15\).
  2. Expand the right side: \(4(x+1)-3=4x+4-3=4x+1\).
  3. So \(6x-15=4x+1\). Subtract \(4x\): \(2x-15=1\).
  4. Add 15: \(2x=16\), hence \(x=8\).
  5. Check: LHS \(=3(16-5)=3(11)=33\); RHS \(=4(9)-3=36-3=33\). Correct.
Q3★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2014 Paper 2 Q4 ↗

Solve the equation \(0.4x-1.2=0.25x+0.3\).

Answer\(x=10\)
  1. Multiply every term by 100 to remove the decimals: \(40x-120=25x+30\).
  2. Subtract \(25x\): \(15x-120=30\).
  3. Add 120: \(15x=150\), so \(x=10\).
  4. Check: LHS \(=0.4(10)-1.2=4-1.2=2.8\); RHS \(=0.25(10)+0.3=2.5+0.3=2.8\). Correct.
Common mistake

Multiplying by 100 must apply to every term, including the constant \(1.2\). Forgetting it gives \(40x-1.2=25x+0.3\), which is a different equation.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the equation \(\frac{x+5}{2}-\frac{x-1}{3}=4\).

Answer\(x=7\)
  1. The LCM of the denominators 2 and 3 is 6. Multiply every term by 6: \(3(x+5)-2(x-1)=24\).
  2. Expand: \(3x+15-2x+2=24\).
  3. Simplify: \(x+17=24\), so \(x=7\).
  4. Check: \(\frac{7+5}{2}-\frac{7-1}{3}=\frac{12}{2}-\frac{6}{3}=6-2=4\). Correct.
Common mistake

The minus sign in front of \(\frac{x-1}{3}\) multiplies both terms: \(-2(x-1)=-2x+2\), not \(-2x-2\).

Q5★★☆☆4 marks

Solve the equation \(2\left[3(x-2)-4(1-x)\right]=5(x+1)+11\).

Answer\(x=4\)
  1. Work inside the square bracket first: \(3(x-2)-4(1-x)=3x-6-4+4x=7x-10\).
  2. Multiply by the outer 2: \(2(7x-10)=14x-20\).
  3. Expand the right: \(5(x+1)+11=5x+5+11=5x+16\).
  4. So \(14x-20=5x+16\). Then \(9x=36\), giving \(x=4\).
  5. Check: bracket \(=3(2)-4(-3)=6+12=18\), LHS \(=2(18)=36\); RHS \(=5(5)+11=36\). Correct.
Q6★★★☆3 marks

Solve the equation \(\frac{x+7}{2}-\frac{2x-3}{5}=3\).

Answer\(x=-11\)
  1. LCM of 2 and 5 is 10. Multiply every term by 10: \(5(x+7)-2(2x-3)=30\).
  2. Expand: \(5x+35-4x+6=30\).
  3. Simplify: \(x+41=30\), so \(x=30-41=-11\).
  4. Check: \(\frac{-11+7}{2}-\frac{-22-3}{5}=\frac{-4}{2}-\frac{-25}{5}=-2+5=3\). Correct.
Common mistake

A negative answer is perfectly legal. Candidates often discard \(x=-11\) because it 'looks wrong' — always let the check, not the look, decide.

Q7★★★☆4 marks

Solve the equation \(0.6(x-2)+0.25=0.4x+1.35\).

Answer\(x=11.5\)
  1. Expand the bracket: \(0.6x-1.2+0.25=0.4x+1.35\).
  2. Combine the constants on the left: \(0.6x-0.95=0.4x+1.35\).
  3. Subtract \(0.4x\): \(0.2x-0.95=1.35\). Add 0.95: \(0.2x=2.3\).
  4. Divide by 0.2: \(x=\frac{2.3}{0.2}=11.5\).
  5. Check: LHS \(=0.6(9.5)+0.25=5.7+0.25=5.95\); RHS \(=0.4(11.5)+1.35=4.6+1.35=5.95\). Correct.

Type 2

Simultaneous Linear Equations · 7 questions

Elimination and substitution

Core idea: Two linear equations in two unknowns describe two straight lines; their solution is the single point where the lines meet. Elimination scales one or both equations until a variable cancels; substitution rewrites one equation as \(y=\dots\) and puts it into the other.

\(a_1x+b_1y=c_1\)\(a_2x+b_2y=c_2\)\(\text{unique solution}\iff a_1b_2\ne a_2b_1\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 2 Q3 ↗

Solve the simultaneous equations \[\begin{cases} x+y=13\\ x-y=5\end{cases}\]

Answer\(x=9,\ y=4\)
  1. Add the two equations so that \(y\) cancels: \((x+y)+(x-y)=13+5\), i.e. \(2x=18\).
  2. Hence \(x=9\).
  3. Substitute into \(x+y=13\): \(9+y=13\), so \(y=4\).
  4. Check with the second equation: \(9-4=5\). Correct.
Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve the simultaneous equations \(y=2x-1\) and \(3x+y=14\).

Answer\(x=3,\ y=5\)
  1. Substitute \(y=2x-1\) into \(3x+y=14\): \(3x+(2x-1)=14\).
  2. Simplify: \(5x-1=14\), so \(5x=15\) and \(x=3\).
  3. Then \(y=2(3)-1=5\).
  4. Check: \(3(3)+5=14\). Correct.
Common mistake

After finding \(x\), always go back to the simplest equation to get \(y\). Reporting \(x\) only loses half the marks.

Q10★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 1 Q2 ↗

Solve the simultaneous equations \[\begin{cases} 3x+4y=23\\ 5x-2y=21\end{cases}\]

Answer\(x=5,\ y=2\)
  1. Multiply the second equation by 2 so that the \(y\)-coefficients are \(+4\) and \(-4\): \(10x-4y=42\).
  2. Add this to the first equation: \((3x+4y)+(10x-4y)=23+42\), i.e. \(13x=65\).
  3. Hence \(x=5\). Substitute into \(3x+4y=23\): \(15+4y=23\), so \(4y=8\) and \(y=2\).
  4. Check in the second equation: \(5(5)-2(2)=25-4=21\). Correct.
Q11★★☆☆3 marks

Solve the simultaneous equations \[\begin{cases} \frac{x}{2}+\frac{y}{3}=4\\[4pt] \frac{x}{3}-\frac{y}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}\]

Answer\(x=6,\ y=3\)
  1. Clear the fractions in the first equation by multiplying by 6: \(3x+2y=24\).
  2. Clear the fractions in the second equation by multiplying by 6: \(2x-3y=3\).
  3. Eliminate \(y\): multiply the first by 3 and the second by 2: \(9x+6y=72\) and \(4x-6y=6\). Adding gives \(13x=78\), so \(x=6\).
  4. Substitute into \(3x+2y=24\): \(18+2y=24\), so \(y=3\).
  5. Check: \(\frac{6}{2}+\frac{3}{3}=3+1=4\) and \(\frac{6}{3}-\frac{3}{2}=2-1.5=0.5\). Correct.
Q12★★☆☆4 marks

Solve the simultaneous equations \[\begin{cases} 2(x+3y)=5(y+2)\\ 3(x-y)=10-x\end{cases}\]

Answer\(x=4,\ y=2\)
  1. Simplify the first equation: \(2x+6y=5y+10\), so \(2x+y=10\).
  2. Simplify the second equation: \(3x-3y=10-x\), so \(4x-3y=10\).
  3. From \(2x+y=10\) we get \(y=10-2x\). Substitute: \(4x-3(10-2x)=10\).
  4. Then \(4x-30+6x=10\), i.e. \(10x=40\), so \(x=4\) and \(y=10-8=2\).
  5. Check: \(3(4-2)=6\) and \(10-4=6\). Correct.
Common mistake

Do not substitute into an equation before simplifying it — the brackets hide the coefficients and errors creep in.

Q13★★★☆4 marks

Solve the simultaneous equations \[\begin{cases} 0.5x+0.8y=4.6\\ 0.3x-0.2y=1.4\end{cases}\]

Answer\(x=6,\ y=2\)
  1. Multiply both equations by 10 to clear the decimals: \(5x+8y=46\) and \(3x-2y=14\).
  2. Multiply the second by 4 so the \(y\)-coefficients match: \(12x-8y=56\).
  3. Add: \(17x=102\), so \(x=6\).
  4. Substitute into \(3x-2y=14\): \(18-2y=14\), so \(2y=4\) and \(y=2\).
  5. Check: \(0.5(6)+0.8(2)=3+1.6=4.6\). Correct.
Q14★★★☆5 marks

(a) Using the substitution \(a=\frac{1}{x}\) and \(b=\frac{1}{y}\), solve \[\begin{cases} 2a+3b=7\\ 4a-b=7\end{cases}\] (b) Hence solve \(\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{y}=7\) and \(\frac{4}{x}-\frac{1}{y}=7\).

Answer(a) \(a=2,\ b=1\)　(b) \(x=\frac{1}{2},\ y=1\)
  1. From \(4a-b=7\) make \(b\) the subject: \(b=4a-7\).
  2. Substitute into \(2a+3b=7\): \(2a+3(4a-7)=7\), i.e. \(14a-21=7\).
  3. Then \(14a=28\), so \(a=2\) and \(b=4(2)-7=1\). Check: \(2(2)+3(1)=7\). ✓
  4. Back-substitute: \(\frac{1}{x}=2\Rightarrow x=\frac{1}{2}\) and \(\frac{1}{y}=1\Rightarrow y=1\).
  5. Check in the original: \(\frac{2}{1/2}+\frac{3}{1}=4+3=7\) and \(\frac{4}{1/2}-1=8-1=7\). Correct.
Common mistake

Remember the reciprocal step at the end, and state \(x\ne 0,\ y\ne 0\). Writing \(x=2\) instead of \(x=\frac{1}{2}\) is the classic slip.

Type 3

Quadratic Equations by Factorisation · 7 questions

Zero product rule, cross method, difference of squares

Core idea: Rearrange the equation so that one side is exactly 0, factorise the other side, then use the zero product rule: if \(AB=0\) then \(A=0\) or \(B=0\). Never divide both sides by \(x\) — that throws away the root \(x=0\).

\(x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)\)\(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)\)\(AB=0\Rightarrow A=0\ \text{or}\ B=0\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 2 Q6 ↗

Solve \(x^{2}-7x+12=0\).

Answer\(x=3\ \text{or}\ x=4\)
  1. Look for two numbers whose product is \(+12\) and whose sum is \(-7\): they are \(-3\) and \(-4\).
  2. So \(x^{2}-7x+12=(x-3)(x-4)\) and the equation becomes \((x-3)(x-4)=0\).
  3. Hence \(x-3=0\) or \(x-4=0\), i.e. \(x=3\) or \(x=4\).
  4. Check: \(3^{2}-7(3)+12=9-21+12=0\) and \(4^{2}-28+12=0\). Correct.
Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve \(x^{2}=5x\).

Answer\(x=0\ \text{or}\ x=5\)
  1. Bring everything to one side: \(x^{2}-5x=0\).
  2. Factorise out the common factor \(x\): \(x(x-5)=0\).
  3. Hence \(x=0\) or \(x-5=0\), i.e. \(x=0\) or \(x=5\).
  4. Check: \(0=0\) and \(25=5(5)\). Correct.
Common mistake

Dividing both sides by \(x\) gives only \(x=5\) and loses the root \(x=0\). Always factorise, never cancel the unknown.

Q17★★☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 1 Q5 ↗

Solve \(2x^{2}+5x-3=0\).

Answer\(x=\frac{1}{2}\ \text{or}\ x=-3\)
  1. Try factors \((2x\ \ )(x\ \ )\). The product of the constants must be \(-3\) and the cross terms must add to \(+5x\).
  2. \((2x-1)(x+3)=2x^{2}+6x-x-3=2x^{2}+5x-3\). ✓
  3. \((2x-1)(x+3)=0\) gives \(2x-1=0\) or \(x+3=0\).
  4. So \(x=\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x=-3\).
  5. Check \(x=-3\): \(2(9)+5(-3)-3=18-15-3=0\). ✓
Q18★★☆☆3 marks

Solve \(3x^{2}-11x+6=0\).

Answer\(x=3\ \text{or}\ x=\frac{2}{3}\)
  1. The leading coefficient is 3, so start with \((3x\ \ )(x\ \ )\).
  2. \((3x-2)(x-3)=3x^{2}-9x-2x+6=3x^{2}-11x+6\). ✓
  3. Set each factor to zero: \(3x-2=0\) or \(x-3=0\).
  4. So \(x=\frac{2}{3}\) or \(x=3\).
  5. Check \(x=\frac{2}{3}\): \(3\left(\frac{4}{9}\right)-11\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)+6=\frac{4}{3}-\frac{22}{3}+\frac{18}{3}=0\). ✓
Common mistake

From \(3x-2=0\) the root is \(x=\frac{2}{3}\), not \(x=2\). Divide by the coefficient of \(x\), not by the constant.

Q19★★☆☆4 marks

Solve \((x+2)(x-5)=3x-10\).

Answer\(x=0\ \text{or}\ x=6\)
  1. Expand the left side: \((x+2)(x-5)=x^{2}-3x-10\).
  2. So \(x^{2}-3x-10=3x-10\). Subtract \(3x\) and add 10 to both sides: \(x^{2}-6x=0\).
  3. Factorise: \(x(x-6)=0\).
  4. Hence \(x=0\) or \(x=6\).
  5. Check \(x=6\): LHS \(=8\times 1=8\), RHS \(=18-10=8\). ✓
Q20★★★☆4 marks

Solve \(4(x-1)^{2}=25\).

Answer\(x=\frac{7}{2}\ \text{or}\ x=-\frac{3}{2}\)
  1. Bring everything to one side and treat it as a difference of two squares: \(4(x-1)^{2}-25=0\), i.e. \(\left[2(x-1)\right]^{2}-5^{2}=0\).
  2. Factorise: \(\left[2(x-1)-5\right]\left[2(x-1)+5\right]=0\), i.e. \((2x-7)(2x+3)=0\).
  3. Hence \(2x-7=0\) or \(2x+3=0\).
  4. So \(x=\frac{7}{2}\) or \(x=-\frac{3}{2}\).
  5. Check \(x=-\frac{3}{2}\): \(4\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)^{2}=4\left(\frac{25}{4}\right)=25\). ✓
Common mistake

Taking the square root of \(4(x-1)^{2}=25\) as \(2(x-1)=5\) only gives one root. Remember the \(\pm\): \(2(x-1)=\pm 5\).

Q21★★★☆5 marks

(a) Factorise \(6x^{2}+x-12\).
(b) Hence solve \(6x^{2}+x-12=0\).
(c) Hence solve \(6(y+1)^{2}+(y+1)-12=0\).

Answer(a) \((3x-4)(2x+3)\)　(b) \(x=\frac{4}{3}\ \text{or}\ x=-\frac{3}{2}\)　(c) \(y=\frac{1}{3}\ \text{or}\ y=-\frac{5}{2}\)
  1. (a) Product of constants \(=-12\), cross terms must total \(+x\): \((3x-4)(2x+3)=6x^{2}+9x-8x-12=6x^{2}+x-12\). ✓
  2. (b) \((3x-4)(2x+3)=0\Rightarrow 3x-4=0\) or \(2x+3=0\), so \(x=\frac{4}{3}\) or \(x=-\frac{3}{2}\).
  3. (c) The expression in (a) with \(x\) replaced by \((y+1)\) is exactly the left side, so \(y+1=\frac{4}{3}\) or \(y+1=-\frac{3}{2}\).
  4. Hence \(y=\frac{4}{3}-1=\frac{1}{3}\) or \(y=-\frac{3}{2}-1=-\frac{5}{2}\).
  5. Check \(y=\frac{1}{3}\): \(6\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{2}+\frac{4}{3}-12=\frac{32}{3}+\frac{4}{3}-12=12-12=0\). ✓
Common mistake

In (c) the unknown to solve for is \(y\), not \(y+1\). Subtracting 1 from each root is a compulsory final step.

Type 4

Quadratic Formula and the Discriminant · 7 questions

Using the formula and counting real roots

Core idea: When factorisation fails, use \(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\) on the standard form \(ax^{2}+bx+c=0\). The part under the root, \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\), tells you how many real roots exist before you solve anything.

\(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\)\(\Delta\gt 0\): 2 real roots\(\Delta=0\): 1 repeated root
Q22★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2018 Paper 2 Q36 ↗

Using the quadratic formula, solve \(x^{2}+3x-1=0\). Leave your answer in surd form.

Answer\(x=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{13}}{2}\)
  1. Identify \(a=1,\ b=3,\ c=-1\).
  2. Discriminant: \(b^{2}-4ac=3^{2}-4(1)(-1)=9+4=13\).
  3. Substitute into \(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\): \(x=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{13}}{2(1)}\).
  4. So \(x=\frac{-3+\sqrt{13}}{2}\) or \(x=\frac{-3-\sqrt{13}}{2}\).
  5. Check (approx.): \(\sqrt{13}\approx 3.606\), so the roots are about \(0.303\) and \(-3.303\); substituting \(0.303\) gives \(0.092+0.909-1\approx 0\). ✓
Q23★☆☆☆3 marks

Find the discriminant of \(2x^{2}-5x+3=0\) and hence state the number of real roots. Solve the equation to confirm.

Answer\(\Delta=1\), two distinct real roots; \(x=1\ \text{or}\ x=\frac{3}{2}\)
  1. Here \(a=2,\ b=-5,\ c=3\), so \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac=(-5)^{2}-4(2)(3)=25-24=1\).
  2. Since \(\Delta=1\gt 0\), the equation has two distinct real roots (and because 1 is a perfect square they are rational).
  3. Formula: \(x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{1}}{4}=\frac{5\pm 1}{4}\).
  4. So \(x=\frac{6}{4}=\frac{3}{2}\) or \(x=\frac{4}{4}=1\).
  5. Check: \(2(1)-5+3=0\). ✓
Common mistake

\(b=-5\) means \(b^{2}=(-5)^{2}=25\). Squaring only the 5 and forgetting that \(-b\) in the formula becomes \(+5\) causes most sign errors here.

Q24★★☆☆3 marks

Solve \(x^{2}-6x+4=0\), giving your answer in surd form.

Answer\(x=3\pm\sqrt{5}\)
  1. \(a=1,\ b=-6,\ c=4\).
  2. \(\Delta=(-6)^{2}-4(1)(4)=36-16=20\).
  3. \(x=\frac{6\pm\sqrt{20}}{2}=\frac{6\pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2}\).
  4. Cancel the common factor 2: \(x=3\pm\sqrt{5}\).
  5. Check: \((3+\sqrt{5})+(3-\sqrt{5})=6\) and \((3+\sqrt{5})(3-\sqrt{5})=9-5=4\), matching sum \(=6\) and product \(=4\). ✓
Q25★★☆☆4 marks

Solve \(3x^{2}+2x-4=0\), giving your answer in the form \(\frac{p\pm\sqrt{q}}{r}\) with integers \(p,q,r\).

Answer\(x=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{3}\)
  1. \(a=3,\ b=2,\ c=-4\).
  2. \(\Delta=2^{2}-4(3)(-4)=4+48=52\).
  3. \(x=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{52}}{2(3)}\). Since \(52=4\times 13\), \(\sqrt{52}=2\sqrt{13}\).
  4. \(x=\frac{-2\pm 2\sqrt{13}}{6}=\frac{2(-1\pm\sqrt{13})}{6}\).
  5. \(x=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{13}}{3}\).
Common mistake

Simplify the surd before cancelling. Leaving \(\frac{-2\pm 2\sqrt{13}}{6}\) unsimplified often loses the presentation mark.

Q26★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2025 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

It is given that the equation \(kx^{2}+4x+2=0\) (where \(k\ne 0\)) has equal roots.
(a) Find \(k\).
(b) Hence find the root.

Answer(a) \(k=2\)　(b) \(x=-1\)
  1. (a) Equal roots means \(\Delta=0\): \(4^{2}-4(k)(2)=0\).
  2. So \(16-8k=0\), giving \(k=2\).
  3. (b) The equation is \(2x^{2}+4x+2=0\), i.e. \(2(x^{2}+2x+1)=0\), so \(2(x+1)^{2}=0\).
  4. Hence \(x=-1\) (a repeated root).
  5. Check with \(x=-\frac{b}{2a}=-\frac{4}{2(2)}=-1\). ✓
Q27★★★☆5 marks

It is given that the equation \(x^{2}+(k+1)x+9=0\) has two distinct real roots. Find the range of values of \(k\).

Answer\(k\gt 5\ \text{or}\ k\lt -7\)
  1. Two distinct real roots requires \(\Delta\gt 0\).
  2. \(\Delta=(k+1)^{2}-4(1)(9)=(k+1)^{2}-36\).
  3. So \((k+1)^{2}-36\gt 0\), i.e. \((k+1)^{2}\gt 36\).
  4. Take square roots: \(k+1\gt 6\) or \(k+1\lt -6\). (Do not write \(-6\lt k+1\lt 6\) — that is the case \(\Delta\lt 0\).)
  5. Hence \(k\gt 5\) or \(k\lt -7\).
  6. Check \(k=6\): \(x^{2}+7x+9=0\), \(\Delta=49-36=13\gt 0\). ✓ Check \(k=0\): \(x^{2}+x+9=0\), \(\Delta=1-36\lt 0\). ✓
Common mistake

An inequality of the form \((k+1)^{2}\gt 36\) splits into two outer intervals, not one. Writing \(-7\lt k\lt 5\) answers the opposite question.

Q28★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

Consider the equation \(2x^{2}-6x+k=0\), where \(k\) is a constant.
(a) Find the range of \(k\) for which the equation has real roots.
(b) If the equation has equal roots, find \(k\) and the root.

Answer(a) \(k\le\frac{9}{2}\)　(b) \(k=\frac{9}{2}\), \(x=\frac{3}{2}\)
  1. (a) Real roots requires \(\Delta\ge 0\): \((-6)^{2}-4(2)(k)\ge 0\).
  2. \(36-8k\ge 0\Rightarrow 8k\le 36\Rightarrow k\le\frac{9}{2}\).
  3. (b) Equal roots means \(k=\frac{9}{2}\), giving \(2x^{2}-6x+\frac{9}{2}=0\).
  4. Multiply by 2: \(4x^{2}-12x+9=0\), i.e. \((2x-3)^{2}=0\).
  5. Hence \(x=\frac{3}{2}\) (repeated). Check: \(2\left(\frac{9}{4}\right)-9+\frac{9}{2}=\frac{9}{2}-9+\frac{9}{2}=0\). ✓

Type 5

Sum and Product of Roots · 7 questions

Building expressions and new equations from α and β

Core idea: For \(ax^{2}+bx+c=0\) with roots \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), you never need the roots themselves: \(\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}\) and \(\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}\). Any symmetric expression in \(\alpha,\beta\) can be rewritten using just these two quantities, and a quadratic is rebuilt from \(x^{2}-(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0\).

\(\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}\)\(\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}\)\(x^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)x+\alpha\beta=0\)
Q29★☆☆☆2 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

The equation \(2x^{2}-7x+3=0\) has roots \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Find \(\alpha+\beta\) and \(\alpha\beta\).

Answer\(\alpha+\beta=\frac{7}{2},\ \alpha\beta=\frac{3}{2}\)
  1. Identify \(a=2,\ b=-7,\ c=3\).
  2. \(\alpha+\beta=-\frac{b}{a}=-\frac{-7}{2}=\frac{7}{2}\).
  3. \(\alpha\beta=\frac{c}{a}=\frac{3}{2}\).
  4. Check by factorising: \(2x^{2}-7x+3=(2x-1)(x-3)\), so the roots are \(\frac{1}{2}\) and 3; their sum is \(\frac{7}{2}\) and product \(\frac{3}{2}\). ✓
Common mistake

The sum is \(-\frac{b}{a}\) with a minus sign in front. Since \(b=-7\), the sum is positive here; forgetting the minus gives \(-\frac{7}{2}\).

Q30★☆☆☆2 marks

Form a quadratic equation, with integer coefficients, whose roots are 3 and \(-5\).

Answer\(x^{2}+2x-15=0\)
  1. Sum of roots \(=3+(-5)=-2\).
  2. Product of roots \(=3\times(-5)=-15\).
  3. The equation is \(x^{2}-(\text{sum})x+(\text{product})=0\): \(x^{2}-(-2)x+(-15)=0\).
  4. So \(x^{2}+2x-15=0\).
  5. Check: \((x-3)(x+5)=x^{2}+2x-15\). ✓
Q31★★☆☆3 marks

If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the roots of \(x^{2}-4x+1=0\), find the value of \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\).

Answer\(14\)
  1. Here \(\alpha+\beta=4\) and \(\alpha\beta=1\).
  2. Use the identity \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta\).
  3. Substitute: \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=4^{2}-2(1)=16-2\).
  4. \(=14\).
  5. Check: the roots are \(2\pm\sqrt{3}\), and \((2+\sqrt{3})^{2}+(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}=(7+4\sqrt{3})+(7-4\sqrt{3})=14\). ✓
Common mistake

\(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\) is not \((\alpha+\beta)^{2}\). The cross term \(2\alpha\beta\) must be subtracted.

Q32★★☆☆3 marks

If \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the roots of \(2x^{2}+5x-3=0\), find the value of \(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}\).

Answer\(\frac{5}{3}\)
  1. From the equation: \(\alpha+\beta=-\frac{5}{2}\) and \(\alpha\beta=-\frac{3}{2}\).
  2. Combine the fractions: \(\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha\beta}\).
  3. Substitute: \(\frac{-5/2}{-3/2}=\frac{-5}{-3}\).
  4. \(=\frac{5}{3}\).
  5. Check: \(2x^{2}+5x-3=(2x-1)(x+3)\), roots \(\frac{1}{2},-3\); then \(2-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{5}{3}\). ✓
Q33★★☆☆4 marks

The roots of \(x^{2}-6x+4=0\) are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Form the quadratic equation whose roots are \(\alpha+1\) and \(\beta+1\), giving your answer with integer coefficients.

Answer\(x^{2}-8x+11=0\)
  1. From \(x^{2}-6x+4=0\): \(\alpha+\beta=6\), \(\alpha\beta=4\).
  2. New sum \(=(\alpha+1)+(\beta+1)=(\alpha+\beta)+2=6+2=8\).
  3. New product \(=(\alpha+1)(\beta+1)=\alpha\beta+\alpha+\beta+1=4+6+1=11\).
  4. Equation: \(x^{2}-8x+11=0\).
  5. Check: the roots are \(4\pm\sqrt{5}\); indeed \(\alpha=3\pm\sqrt{5}\) shifted up by 1. Sum \(8\), product \((4+\sqrt{5})(4-\sqrt{5})=16-5=11\). ✓
Q34★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 2 Q34 ↗

The roots of \(3x^{2}-2x-4=0\) are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). Form the quadratic equation whose roots are \(2\alpha\) and \(2\beta\), giving your answer with integer coefficients.

Answer\(3x^{2}-4x-16=0\)
  1. From \(3x^{2}-2x-4=0\): \(\alpha+\beta=\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\alpha\beta=-\frac{4}{3}\).
  2. New sum \(=2\alpha+2\beta=2(\alpha+\beta)=2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)=\frac{4}{3}\).
  3. New product \(=(2\alpha)(2\beta)=4\alpha\beta=4\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)=-\frac{16}{3}\).
  4. Equation: \(x^{2}-\frac{4}{3}x-\frac{16}{3}=0\). Multiply by 3: \(3x^{2}-4x-16=0\).
  5. Check with the formula: roots of \(3x^{2}-4x-16=0\) are \(\frac{4\pm\sqrt{16+192}}{6}=\frac{4\pm 4\sqrt{13}}{6}\), and the original roots are \(\frac{1\pm\sqrt{13}}{3}\); doubling matches. ✓
Common mistake

The product of the new roots is \((2\alpha)(2\beta)=4\alpha\beta\) — a factor of 4, not 2. Scaling roots by 2 scales their product by 4.

Q35★★★☆6 marks

The equation \(x^{2}-8x+k=0\) has roots \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), where \(k\) is a constant.
(a) Express \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}\) in terms of \(k\).
(b) Given that \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=40\), find \(k\) and hence solve the equation.

Answer(a) \(64-2k\)　(b) \(k=12\); \(x=2\ \text{or}\ x=6\)
  1. (a) \(\alpha+\beta=8\) and \(\alpha\beta=k\), so \(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2\alpha\beta=64-2k\).
  2. (b) Set \(64-2k=40\): \(2k=24\), so \(k=12\).
  3. The equation becomes \(x^{2}-8x+12=0\), which factorises as \((x-2)(x-6)=0\).
  4. Hence \(x=2\) or \(x=6\).
  5. Check: \(2^{2}+6^{2}=4+36=40\). ✓

Type 6

Fractional, Radical and Line–Curve Systems · 7 questions

Clear denominators, square both sides, substitute

Core idea: For a fractional equation, multiply through by the LCM of the denominators and then check that no solution makes a denominator zero. For a radical equation, isolate the surd, square both sides, solve, and test every root in the original equation because squaring can create false roots. A line meeting a curve gives a quadratic by substitution.

\(\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\Rightarrow ad=bc\)\(\sqrt{A}=B\Rightarrow A=B^{2},\ B\ge 0\)\(y=mx+k,\ y=ax^{2}+bx+c\Rightarrow ax^{2}+(b-m)x+(c-k)=0\)
Q36★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve \(\frac{2x+1}{x}=5\).

Answer\(x=\frac{1}{3}\)
  1. State the restriction \(x\ne 0\). Multiply both sides by \(x\): \(2x+1=5x\).
  2. Rearrange: \(1=3x\), so \(x=\frac{1}{3}\).
  3. Check: numerator \(=2\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)+1=\frac{5}{3}\), and \(\frac{5/3}{1/3}=5\). ✓
  4. The value \(x=\frac{1}{3}\) is not 0, so it is accepted.
Q37★☆☆☆2 marks

Solve \(\sqrt{2x+5}=3\).

Answer\(x=2\)
  1. The surd is already isolated, so square both sides: \(2x+5=3^{2}=9\).
  2. Then \(2x=4\), so \(x=2\).
  3. Check in the original: \(\sqrt{2(2)+5}=\sqrt{9}=3\). ✓
  4. Since the right side 3 is positive, no root is rejected.
Common mistake

Squaring must be applied to the whole side: \(3^{2}=9\), not \(3\). Also, \(\sqrt{A}=B\) needs \(B\ge 0\); a negative right side means no solution at all.

Q38★★☆☆3 marks

Solve \(\frac{5}{2x-1}=\frac{3}{x+3}\).

Answer\(x=18\)
  1. Cross-multiply (valid since \(2x-1\ne 0\) and \(x+3\ne 0\)): \(5(x+3)=3(2x-1)\).
  2. Expand: \(5x+15=6x-3\).
  3. Rearrange: \(15+3=6x-5x\), so \(x=18\).
  4. Check: LHS \(=\frac{5}{35}=\frac{1}{7}\), RHS \(=\frac{3}{21}=\frac{1}{7}\). ✓
  5. Neither denominator is zero at \(x=18\).
Q39★★☆☆4 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 1 Q7 ↗

Solve \(\frac{x+1}{x-1}-\frac{3}{x+1}=2\).

Answer\(x=2\ \text{or}\ x=-3\)
  1. Restrictions: \(x\ne 1,\ x\ne -1\). Multiply every term by \((x-1)(x+1)\): \((x+1)^{2}-3(x-1)=2(x-1)(x+1)\).
  2. Expand: \(x^{2}+2x+1-3x+3=2x^{2}-2\).
  3. Simplify the left: \(x^{2}-x+4=2x^{2}-2\).
  4. Bring everything to one side: \(0=x^{2}+x-6\), i.e. \((x+3)(x-2)=0\).
  5. So \(x=-3\) or \(x=2\), and neither value is \(\pm 1\). Check \(x=2\): \(\frac{3}{1}-\frac{3}{3}=3-1=2\). ✓
Common mistake

Multiply the middle term by the full LCM too: \(-\frac{3}{x+1}\times (x-1)(x+1)=-3(x-1)\). Forgetting the \((x-1)\) factor is the usual error.

Q40★★☆☆4 marks

Solve \(\sqrt{x+5}+x=7\).

Answer\(x=4\)
  1. Isolate the surd: \(\sqrt{x+5}=7-x\). This requires \(7-x\ge 0\), i.e. \(x\le 7\).
  2. Square both sides: \(x+5=(7-x)^{2}=49-14x+x^{2}\).
  3. Rearrange: \(0=x^{2}-15x+44\), i.e. \((x-4)(x-11)=0\), so \(x=4\) or \(x=11\).
  4. Check \(x=4\): \(\sqrt{9}+4=3+4=7\). ✓
  5. Check \(x=11\): \(\sqrt{16}+11=4+11=15\ne 7\), so reject it (it violates \(x\le 7\)).
  6. Answer: \(x=4\) only.
Common mistake

Squaring creates the extra root \(x=11\). Always test both roots in the original equation before writing the final answer.

Q41★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 2 Q35 ↗

The curve \(y=x^{2}-3x+1\) and the line \(y=2x-5\) intersect at two points. Find the coordinates of these points.

Answer\((2,-1)\) and \((3,1)\)
  1. At an intersection the \(y\)-values are equal: \(x^{2}-3x+1=2x-5\).
  2. Rearrange: \(x^{2}-5x+6=0\).
  3. Factorise: \((x-2)(x-3)=0\), so \(x=2\) or \(x=3\).
  4. Find \(y\) from the line \(y=2x-5\): at \(x=2\), \(y=-1\); at \(x=3\), \(y=1\).
  5. Check on the curve: \(2^{2}-3(2)+1=-1\) and \(3^{2}-9+1=1\). ✓
  6. The intersection points are \((2,-1)\) and \((3,1)\).
Q42★★★☆6 marks

The line \(y=2x+k\) is a tangent to the curve \(y=x^{2}-4x+7\).
(a) Find \(k\).
(b) Find the coordinates of the point of contact.

Answer(a) \(k=-2\)　(b) \((3,4)\)
  1. At the point of contact the two equations hold together: \(x^{2}-4x+7=2x+k\).
  2. Rearrange into a quadratic: \(x^{2}-6x+(7-k)=0\).
  3. (a) Tangency means exactly one solution, so \(\Delta=0\): \(36-4(7-k)=0\).
  4. Then \(36-28+4k=0\), i.e. \(4k=-8\), so \(k=-2\).
  5. (b) With \(k=-2\) the quadratic is \(x^{2}-6x+9=0\), i.e. \((x-3)^{2}=0\), so \(x=3\).
  6. Then \(y=2(3)-2=4\). Check on the curve: \(9-12+7=4\). ✓ The point is \((3,4)\).
Common mistake

A tangent means \(\Delta=0\) (one repeated root), not \(\Delta\gt 0\). Also substitute \(x=3\) back into either equation to get \(y\) — do not stop at \(x=3\).

Type 7

Exam-level Word Problems · 8 questions

Define the unknown, form the equation, reject impossible roots

Core idea: Every word problem follows the same four moves: (1) introduce a variable and say what it means, (2) translate each sentence into an equation, (3) solve, (4) reject any root that makes no physical sense (negative length, zero speed, too many people). Write the units in the final statement.

\(\text{distance}=\text{speed}\times\text{time}\)\(\text{work rate}=\frac{1}{\text{time taken}}\)\(\text{amount}=\text{concentration}\times\text{volume}\)
Q43★☆☆☆3 marks

The sum of three consecutive integers is 63. Find the three integers.

Answer\(20,\ 21,\ 22\)
  1. Let the smallest integer be \(n\). Then the next two are \(n+1\) and \(n+2\).
  2. Form the equation: \(n+(n+1)+(n+2)=63\).
  3. Simplify: \(3n+3=63\), so \(3n=60\) and \(n=20\).
  4. The integers are 20, 21 and 22.
  5. Check: \(20+21+22=63\). ✓
Common mistake

Writing the integers as \(n,\ n+1,\ n+3\) breaks the pattern. Consecutive integers differ by exactly 1.

Q44★☆☆☆3 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 1 Q7 ↗

A father is now three times as old as his son. In 12 years' time the father will be twice as old as his son. Find their present ages.

AnswerSon 12 years old, father 36 years old
  1. Let the son's present age be \(x\) years. Then the father is \(3x\) years old.
  2. In 12 years: son \(x+12\), father \(3x+12\).
  3. The condition gives \(3x+12=2(x+12)\).
  4. Expand: \(3x+12=2x+24\), so \(x=12\).
  5. The son is 12 and the father is \(3(12)=36\).
  6. Check: in 12 years they are 24 and 48, and \(48=2\times 24\). ✓
Q45★★☆☆4 marks

A rectangular noticeboard is 5 cm longer than it is wide and its area is 84 cm². Find its dimensions.

Answer7 cm by 12 cm
  1. Let the width be \(x\) cm; then the length is \((x+5)\) cm.
  2. Area: \(x(x+5)=84\).
  3. Expand and rearrange: \(x^{2}+5x-84=0\).
  4. Factorise: \((x+12)(x-7)=0\), so \(x=7\) or \(x=-12\).
  5. A width cannot be negative, so reject \(x=-12\); \(x=7\).
  6. The board is 7 cm wide and \(7+5=12\) cm long. Check: \(7\times 12=84\). ✓
Common mistake

Both roots of the quadratic must be examined. Keeping \(x=-12\) gives a 'length' of \(-7\) cm, which is meaningless.

Q46★★☆☆4 marks

The sum of a positive number and its reciprocal is \(\frac{10}{3}\). Find the number.

Answer\(3\) or \(\frac{1}{3}\)
  1. Let the number be \(x\) (\(x\ne 0\)). Then \(x+\frac{1}{x}=\frac{10}{3}\).
  2. Multiply every term by \(3x\): \(3x^{2}+3=10x\).
  3. Rearrange: \(3x^{2}-10x+3=0\).
  4. Factorise: \((3x-1)(x-3)=0\), so \(x=\frac{1}{3}\) or \(x=3\).
  5. Both are positive, and they are reciprocals of each other, so both answers are valid.
  6. Check \(x=3\): \(3+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{10}{3}\). ✓
Q47★★☆☆5 marks

A cyclist covers 60 km. If the speed had been 5 km/h greater, the journey would have taken 1 hour less. Find the actual speed.

Answer\(15\ \text{km/h}\)
  1. Let the actual speed be \(v\) km/h (\(v\gt 0\)), so the time taken is \(\frac{60}{v}\) hours.
  2. At \(v+5\) km/h the time is \(\frac{60}{v+5}\) hours, which is 1 hour less.
  3. Equation: \(\frac{60}{v}-\frac{60}{v+5}=1\).
  4. Multiply by \(v(v+5)\): \(60(v+5)-60v=v(v+5)\).
  5. So \(300=v^{2}+5v\), i.e. \(v^{2}+5v-300=0\).
  6. Factorise: \((v+20)(v-15)=0\), so \(v=15\) (reject \(v=-20\)).
  7. Check: \(\frac{60}{15}=4\) h and \(\frac{60}{20}=3\) h; the difference is 1 h. ✓
Common mistake

Time is \(\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}\), not speed divided by distance. Also state the rejection of the negative root explicitly for the method mark.

Q48★★★☆6 marks

A sum of $720 is to be shared equally among \(n\) students. If there were 3 more students, each would receive $8 less. Find \(n\).

Answer\(n=15\)
  1. Each student originally receives \(\frac{720}{n}\) dollars.
  2. With 3 more students each receives \(\frac{720}{n+3}\) dollars.
  3. The difference is $8: \(\frac{720}{n}-\frac{720}{n+3}=8\).
  4. Multiply by \(n(n+3)\): \(720(n+3)-720n=8n(n+3)\).
  5. So \(2160=8n^{2}+24n\). Divide by 8: \(n^{2}+3n-270=0\).
  6. Factorise: \((n+18)(n-15)=0\), so \(n=15\) (reject \(-18\)).
  7. Check: \(\frac{720}{15}=48\) and \(\frac{720}{18}=40\); the difference is $8. ✓
Q49★★★☆7 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 1 Q17 ↗

Pipe A alone can fill a tank in \(x\) hours, and pipe B alone takes 3 hours longer than pipe A. When both pipes are opened together, the tank is filled in 2 hours.
(a) Show that \(x^{2}-x-6=0\).
(b) Hence find \(x\).

Answer(a) shown　(b) \(x=3\)
  1. In 1 hour pipe A fills \(\frac{1}{x}\) of the tank and pipe B fills \(\frac{1}{x+3}\) of the tank.
  2. Together they fill \(\frac{1}{2}\) of the tank in 1 hour: \(\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x+3}=\frac{1}{2}\).
  3. (a) Multiply by \(2x(x+3)\): \(2(x+3)+2x=x(x+3)\).
  4. Expand: \(4x+6=x^{2}+3x\), so \(x^{2}-x-6=0\), as required.
  5. (b) Factorise: \((x-3)(x+2)=0\), so \(x=3\) or \(x=-2\). A time must be positive, so \(x=3\).
  6. Check: pipe A fills \(\frac{1}{3}\), pipe B fills \(\frac{1}{6}\); together \(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{2}\) per hour, so 2 hours. ✓
Q50★★★★8 marks

A chemist has 20 L of a solution that is 40% acid.
(a) How many litres of acid are in the solution?
(b) She adds \(x\) litres of pure acid so that the mixture becomes 60% acid. Show that \(0.4x=4\) and hence find \(x\).
(c) Verify the final concentration.

Answer(a) 8 L　(b) \(x=10\)　(c) \(\frac{18}{30}=60\%\), verified
  1. (a) Acid \(=40\%\times 20=0.4\times 20=8\) L (and water \(=12\) L).
  2. (b) After adding \(x\) L of pure acid: acid \(=8+x\) L, total volume \(=20+x\) L.
  3. The new concentration is 60%: \(\frac{8+x}{20+x}=0.6\).
  4. Multiply by \((20+x)\): \(8+x=12+0.6x\).
  5. Subtract \(0.6x\) and 8: \(0.4x=4\), as required. Hence \(x=10\).
  6. (c) Acid \(=8+10=18\) L, volume \(=20+10=30\) L, so the concentration is \(\frac{18}{30}=0.6=60\%\). ✓
  7. She must add 10 L of pure acid.
Common mistake

Adding pure acid changes both the amount of acid and the total volume. Using \(0.6\times 20\) for the new acid content forgets the added 10 L.
