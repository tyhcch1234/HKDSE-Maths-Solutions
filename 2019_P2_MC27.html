Coordinate Geometry — equation, centre and radius of a circle

Circle Equation Centre & Radius Completing the Square Point–Circle Position Quadrants

Q Question

Denote the circle \(2x^2+2y^2+4x-12y+15=0\) by \(C\).

Which of the following is/are true?

I.  The area of \(C\) is \(25\pi\).
II.  The point \((-3,\,3)\) lies outside \(C\).
III. The centre of \(C\) lies in the fourth quadrant.

A. I only    B. II only    C. I and III only    D. II and III only

I Statement I — Is the area \(25\pi\) ?

Centre(−1, 3)
Radius r√(5/2) ≈ 1.581
Area = πr²(5/2)π ≈ 7.854
Step 1

Divide the whole equation by 2 so that the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) become 1:

\[2x^2+2y^2+4x-12y+15=0\] \[x^2+y^2+2x-6y+\frac{15}{2}=0\]
Hint: A standard circle equation needs the \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) coefficients equal to \(1\).
Step 2

Compare with \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\), where \(D=2\), \(E=-6\), \(F=\frac{15}{2}\).

\(\textbf{Centre}=\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)\):

\[\text{Centre}=\left(-\frac{2}{2},\,-\frac{-6}{2}\right)=(-1,\,3)\]

\(\textbf{Radius}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^2-F}\):

\[r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{2}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{-6}{2}\right)^2-\frac{15}{2}}=\sqrt{1+9-\frac{15}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{20-15}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}\]
Hint: Always check \(r^2>0\). Here \(r^2=\frac{5}{2}>0\), so \(C\) is a genuine circle.
Step 3

Area of \(C=\pi r^2=\pi\cdot\frac{5}{2}=\frac{5}{2}\pi\approx7.85\)  and  \(\frac{5}{2}\pi\ne 25\pi\).

Statement I is false.

Conclusion\(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}\), Area \(=\dfrac{5}{2}\pi\), so I is FALSE. ✗

II Statement II — Does \((-3,3)\) lie outside \(C\)?

P (−3, 3)(−3, 3)
Distance CP2.000
Radius r1.581
VerdictOUTSIDE
Step 1

From Part I the centre is \(O(-1,\,3)\), and the radius is \(r=\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}\).

Hint: To locate a point relative to a circle, compare the distance from the centre with \(r\).
Step 2

Distance from the centre \(O(-1,3)\) to the point \(P(-3,3)\):

\[OP=\sqrt{(-3-(-1))^2+(3-3)^2}=\sqrt{(-2)^2+0^2}=\sqrt{4}=2\]
Hint: \(OP\) and \(r\) are both positive; compare \(OP^2\) with \(r^2\) to avoid sign errors.
Step 3

\(OP=2\) and \(r=\sqrt{2.5}\approx1.581\), so \(OP>r\).

Hence \(P(-3,\,3)\) lies outside \(C\).

Statement II is true.

Conclusion\(OP=2>r\approx1.581\), so II is TRUE.

III Statement III — Is the centre in the fourth quadrant?

Centre(−1, 3)
x-coordinate−1 < 0
y-coordinate3 > 0
QuadrantSECOND
Step 1

The centre of \(C\) is \((-1,\,3)\).

Since \(x=-1<0\) and \(y=3>0\), the centre lies in the second quadrant.

Hint: Quadrants: I \((+,+)\), II \((-,+)\), III \((-,-)\), IV \((+,-)\).
Step 2

The fourth quadrant requires \(x>0\) and \(y<0\). Here \(x<0\), so the centre is not in the fourth quadrant.

Statement III is false.

ConclusionCentre \((-1,3)\) is in the second quadrant, so III is FALSE. ✗

★ Summary — Final Answer

  • I. Area \(=\frac{5}{2}\pi\ne 25\pi\) → FALSE
  • II. \(OP=2>r=\sqrt{5/2}\) → TRUE
  • III. Centre \((-1,3)\) is in the 2nd quadrant → FALSE
  • Only II is true.
Correct OptionB. II only
