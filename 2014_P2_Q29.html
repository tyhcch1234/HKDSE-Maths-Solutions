Statistics — Reading a Pie Chart and Using Angles as Proportions

Pie Chart Angles at a Point Ratio & Proportion Multiple Choice

Question Full Statement

The pie chart below shows the expenditure of John in a certain week. John spends \(\$240\) on clothing that week. Find his expenditure on transportation that week.

The pie chart is divided into four sectors: Clothing \(160^\circ\), Others \(50^\circ\), Transportation (angle to be found, marked with a right angle to Meals), and Meals \(90^\circ\).

  • A \(\$40\)
  • B \(\$60\)
  • C \(\$90\)
  • D \(\$135\)
    • >

Part (a) Explore the Pie Chart

The whole pie represents the total expenditure of the week, i.e. \(360^\circ\). Drag the slider to rotate “Clothing” and see how every sector keeps its own angle — the angles are the key to comparing expenditures.

Clothing angle: 160°
Others angle: 50°
Meals angle: 90°
Transportation angle: —
Total = 360°
1 Use the angle at the centre of a circle.

The four sectors of a pie chart together form a complete circle. Hence

\[ \text{Clothing} + \text{Others} + \text{Meals} + \text{Transportation} = 360^\circ . \]

Substituting the known angles:

\[ 160^\circ + 50^\circ + 90^\circ + \angle T = 360^\circ . \]
Hint: The little square at the centre means the Meals sector subtends a right angle, so its angle is exactly \(90^\circ\) (∠s at a point).
2 Solve for the transportation angle.
\[ \angle T = 360^\circ - 160^\circ - 50^\circ - 90^\circ = 60^\circ . \]
Answer for Part (a): The angle of the sector for transportation is \(60^\circ\).

Part (b) Turn the Angle into Money

In a pie chart, the angle of a sector is proportional to the amount it represents. Move the slider to change the clothing spending given in the question and watch the transportation amount change — but the ratio \(\dfrac{60}{160}\) never changes.

Angle ratio \(\frac{60}{160}\) = 0.375
Transportation angle = 60°
Transportation spending = $90
1 Set up the proportion using the angles.

Since angle is proportional to expenditure,

\[ \frac{\text{Expenditure on transportation}}{\text{Expenditure on clothing}} = \frac{\text{Angle for transportation}}{\text{Angle for clothing}} = \frac{60^\circ}{160^\circ} = \frac{3}{8}. \]
Hint: Simplify \(\frac{60}{160}\) by dividing both the numerator and the denominator by \(20\): \(\frac{60}{160} = \frac{3}{8}\).
2 Compute the transportation expenditure.

John spends \(\$240\) on clothing, so

\[ \text{Expenditure on transportation} = \$240 \times \frac{3}{8} = \$90 . \]
Answer for Part (b): His expenditure on transportation that week is \(\$90\).
Therefore the correct option is C.
0 / 2

Summary — Answers at a Glance

  • Clothing Angle \(=160^\circ\), spending \(=\$240\) (given).
  • Meals Angle \(=90^\circ\) (marked with a right angle at the centre).
  • Others Angle \(=50^\circ\) (given).
  • Transportation Angle \(=360^\circ-160^\circ-50^\circ-90^\circ=60^\circ\).
  • Ratio \(\dfrac{\text{Transportation}}{\text{Clothing}}=\dfrac{60}{160}=\dfrac{3}{8}\).
  • Amount \(240\times\dfrac{3}{8}=\$90\).
Final Answer: \(\$90\) — Option C
