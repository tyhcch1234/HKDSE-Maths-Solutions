Coordinate Geometry — Equation of a Circle, Position of a Point, Collinearity & a Straight Line
Circle Equation Centre & Radius Distance > Radius Collinear Points Two-Point Form

QQuestion

Total: 7 marks

The coordinates of the points \(E\), \(F\) and \(G\) are \((-6,\,5)\), \((-3,\,11)\) and \((2,\,-1)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(E\) and the centre of \(C\) is \(G\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). [2 marks]

(b) Prove that \(F\) lies outside \(C\). [2 marks]

(c) Let \(H\) be a moving point on \(C\). When \(H\) is farthest from \(F\),

(i) describe the geometric relationship between \(F\), \(G\) and \(H\); [1 mark]

(ii) find the equation of the straight line which passes through \(F\) and \(H\). [2 marks]

aPart (a) — Finding the Equation of \(C\)

2 marks · centre \(G(2,-1)\), passing through \(E(-6,5)\)

Centre \(G=(2,-1)\) Radius \(r = 10\) \((x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=100\)
Step 11M

The centre of \(C\) is \(G(2,-1)\). Since \(C\) passes through \(E(-6,5)\), the radius is \(GE\). Apply the distance formula:

\[ r=GE=\sqrt{(2-(-6))^2+(-1-5)^2} \]
Hint: On the canvas the radius is the orange segment from \(G\) to \(E\). Watch how the circle must always pass through \(E\) — the radius is fixed by the two points.
Step 21A
\[ r=\sqrt{8^2+(-6)^2}=\sqrt{64+36}=\sqrt{100}=10 \]

So \(r^2=100\). The circle with centre \((h,k)\) and radius \(r\) has equation

\[ (x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2 \] \[\therefore\ (x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=100\]
Hint: Do not expand unless asked. The "centre–radius form" shows the centre and radius at once — exactly what part (b) needs.
Answer (a): \((x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=100\)  (centre \(G(2,-1)\), radius \(10\))

bPart (b) — Proving \(F\) Lies Outside \(C\)

2 marks · compare \(FG\) with the radius

\(FG = 13\) radius \(= 10\) \(FG > 10\) → \(F\) is outside \(C\)
Step 11M

From (a), the radius of \(C\) is \(10\). Compute the distance between \(F(-3,11)\) and the centre \(G(2,-1)\):

\[ FG=\sqrt{(2-(-3))^2+(-1-11)^2}=\sqrt{5^2+(-12)^2} \]
Hint: Drag the slider: as long as \(FG\) stays greater than the radius, the purple point \(F\) never touches the blue circle. Try pulling \(F\) inside to see the condition break.
Step 21
\[ FG=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13 \]

Since \(FG=13>10=\) radius of \(C\), the distance from \(F\) to the centre exceeds the radius.

Hint: The key criterion is a single comparison: distance from centre > radius. The \((5,12,13)\) triple makes this exact — no approximation needed.
Answer (b): \(FG=13>10=\) radius, therefore \(F\) lies outside \(C\). \(\blacksquare\)

ciPart (c)(i) — Relationship between \(F\), \(G\) and \(H\)

1 mark · \(H\) moves on \(C\), farthest from \(F\)

\(FH = 23.00\) \(H\) is not on line \(FG\) Maximum \(FH = FG + r = 23\)
Step 11M

For any point \(H\) on \(C\) with centre \(G\) and radius \(r=10\), the length \(FH\) is largest when \(H\) lies on the line through \(F\) and \(G\), on the far side of \(G\) (i.e. the ray from \(F\) through \(G\)). Then \(F\), \(G\), \(H\) are collinear with \(G\) between \(F\) and \(H\).

Hint: Slide \(H\) around. The readout \(FH\) is maximum (23) only when \(F,G,H\) line up. The triangle inequality gives \(FH\le FG+GH=FG+r\).
Answer (c)(i): \(F\), \(G\) and \(H\) are collinear (with \(G\) lying between \(F\) and \(H\) so that \(FH=FG+GH=13+10=23\)).

ciiPart (c)(ii) — Equation of the Line through \(F\) and \(H\)

2 marks · use two points on the line

Slope \(m = -\tfrac{12}{5}\) Line: \(12x+5y-19=0\) Check \(F\): \(12(-3)+5(11)-19=0\) ✓
Step 11M

The line passes through \(F\) and \(H\). Since \(F\), \(G\), \(H\) are collinear, its slope equals that of \(FG\):

\[ m=\frac{11-(-1)}{-3-2}=\frac{-12}{5} \]
Hint: You may use \(F\) and \(G\) instead of \(F\) and \(H\) — same line! The slope triangle on the canvas shows "rise \(-12\), run \(5\)".
Step 21A

Using the point–slope form with \(F(-3,11)\):

\[ y-11=-\frac{12}{5}\bigl(x-(-3)\bigr) \] \[ 5(y-11)=-12(x+3) \] \[ 5y-55=-12x-36 \] \[ 12x+5y-19=0 \]
Hint: The expected general form is \(12x+5y-19=0\). The green chip confirms \(F(-3,11)\) satisfies it. \(H\) would give the same result.
Answer (c)(ii): \(12x+5y-19=0\)

✓Summary of Answers

  • (a) Equation of \(C\): \((x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=100\)  (centre \(G(2,-1)\), radius \(10\))
  • (b) \(FG=\sqrt{(2+3)^2+(-1-11)^2}=13>10=\) radius of \(C\), so \(F\) lies outside \(C\).
  • (c)(i) When \(H\) is farthest from \(F\), \(F\), \(G\) and \(H\) are collinear (with \(G\) between \(F\) and \(H\)).
  • (c)(ii) Slope \(m=\dfrac{11-(-1)}{-3-2}=-\dfrac{12}{5}\); hence the line is \(12x+5y-19=0\).
