Coordinate Geometry — Circle through two points, centre & moving point

Question & Solution

The coordinates of the points $E$, $F$ and $G$ are $(-6,\,5)$, $(-3,\,11)$ and $(2,\,-1)$ respectively. The circle $C$ passes through $E$ and the centre of $C$ is $G$.

(a) Find the equation of $C$. [2 marks]

(b) Prove that $F$ lies outside $C$. [2 marks]

(c) Let $H$ be a moving point on $C$. When $H$ is farthest from $F$,

  • (i) describe the geometric relationship between $F$, $G$ and $H$;
  • (ii) find the equation of the straight line which passes through $F$ and $H$. [3 marks]
STEP 1 · Radius of C using E and G
Distance Formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) The radius is \(EG=\sqrt{(-6+3)^{2}+(5-11)^{2}}=\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt{5}\).

Since $C$ passes through $E$ and its centre is $G$,
the radius is

$$r = EG = \sqrt{(-6-(-3))^2+(5-11)^2}=\sqrt{9+36}=\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt5 .$$

So $r^2 = 45$ (equivalently, this is the same as distance $FG$ squared?
no — $EG^2=45$).

STEP 2 · Equation of circle C
Centre-Radius Form of a Circle \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\) The centre is \(G(-3,\ 11)\) and \(r^{2}=45\), so the circle is \((x+3)^{2}+(y-11)^{2}=45\).

Centre $G(-3,\,11)$ with radius $r=\sqrt{45}$:

$$(x+3)^2+(y-11)^2=45$$

Expanded form: $x^2+y^2+6x-22y+85=0$.

Answer (a): $\;(x+3)^2+(y-11)^2=45$
STEP 3 · Distance FG

$$FG=\sqrt{(2-(-3))^2+(-1-11)^2}=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13 .$$

Compare with the radius: $13>10=\sqrt{100}$;
since $r=\sqrt{45}\approx6.71$, indeed $FG>r$.

STEP 4 · Conclusion for (b)

Since $FG=13>$ radius $=\sqrt{45}$, the distance from $F$ to the centre exceeds the radius.

Answer (b): $F$ lies outside $C$.
STEP 5 · Farthest point H (part c(i))

The farthest point of a circle from an external point lies on the line joining that point to the centre,
on the far side of the circle.
Hence

$$F,\;G,\;H \text{ are collinear.}$$

Reason: $FH$ attains its maximum when the segment $FH$ passes through the centre $G$, i.e. $FH=FG+r$.
Answer (c)(i): $F$, $G$ and $H$ are collinear.
STEP 6 · Equation of line FH (part c(ii))
Point-Slope Form \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\) The line through \(F(2,\ -1)\) with slope \(-\dfrac{12}{5}\) is \(y+1=-\dfrac{12}{5}(x-2)\).

Since $FGH$ are collinear, the line through $F$ and $H$ is the same line through $F$ and $G$. Its slope is

$$m=\frac{11-(-1)}{-3-2}=\frac{12}{-5}=-\frac{12}{5}.$$

Using $F(2,\,-1)$:

$$y-(-1)=-\frac{12}{5}(x-2)$$

$$5y+5=-12x+24$$

$$12x+5y-19=0 .$$

Answer (c)(ii): $\;12x+5y-19=0$

Question. The coordinates of the points \(E\), \(F\) and \(G\) are \((-2,\ 8)\), \((9,\ 7)\) and \((1,\ 4)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(E\) and the centre of \(C\) is \(G\).
(a) Find the equation of \(C\). [2 marks] (b) Prove that \(F\) lies outside \(C\). [2 marks] (c) Let \(H\) be a moving point on \(C\). When \(H\) is farthest from \(F\),
(i) describe the geometric relationship between \(F\), \(G\) and \(H\);
(ii) find the equation of the straight line which passes through \(F\) and \(H\). [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(r=EG=\sqrt{(-2-1)^{2}+(8-4)^{2}}=\sqrt{9+16}=5\), so \(r^{2}=25\).
Step 2. The centre is \(G(1,\ 4)\), so \(C\) has equation \((x-1)^{2}+(y-4)^{2}=25\).
Step 3. \(FG=\sqrt{(9-1)^{2}+(7-4)^{2}}=\sqrt{64+9}=\sqrt{73}\approx8.54>5=r\), so \(F\) lies outside \(C\).
Step 4. For the greatest distance \(FH=FG+r\), the points \(F\), \(G\), \(H\) are collinear with \(G\) between \(F\) and \(H\).
Step 5. The line \(FH\) is the line \(FG\), whose slope is \(m=\dfrac{7-4}{9-1}=\dfrac{3}{8}\).
Step 6. Using \(F(9,\ 7)\): \(y-7=\dfrac{3}{8}(x-9)\), i.e. \(8y-56=3x-27\), so \(3x-8y+29=0\).
Answer: (a) \((x-1)^{2}+(y-4)^{2}=25\)   (b) \(FG=\sqrt{73}>5\), so \(F\) is outside   (c)(i) \(F\), \(G\), \(H\) are collinear   (c)(ii) \(3x-8y+29=0\)
