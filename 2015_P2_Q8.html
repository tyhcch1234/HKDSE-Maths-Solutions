Functions and Graphs — Quadratic in vertex form

The figure shows the graph of \(y = a(x+b)^2\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. Which of the following is true?

A. \(a>0\) and \(b<0\)
B. \(a<0\) and \(b<0\)
C. \(a>0\) and \(b>0\)
D. \(a<0\) and \(b>0\)

STEP 1Read the shape: which way does it open?

The given curve is a U-shaped parabola opening upwards.

In \(y=a(x+b)^2\) the coefficient \(a\) controls the opening:

\[ \text{opens upwards} \iff a>0 \qquad \text{opens downwards} \iff a<0 \]

Since it opens upwards, \[ \boxed{a>0} \]

Hint: think of \(y=x^2\) (\(a=1>0\)) vs \(y=-x^2\) (\(a=-1<0\)).

Sign of the Leading Coefficient \(a>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{the parabola opens upwards}\) The curve in the figure opens upwards, so \(a>0\).

STEP 2Locate the vertex and the x-intercept

Complete the square form \(y=a(x+b)^2\) is already in vertex form, so the vertex is at \[ (-b,\;0) \]

From the figure, the curve touches the x-axis once, and that touching point lies on the negative x-axis (to the left of the origin).

Hence \[ -b<0 \]

Hint: the point where the curve just touches the axis is the minimum point (turning point).

Vertex Form of a Quadratic Function \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{vertex }(h,k)\) Here \(y=a(x+b)^{2}=a\left(x-(-b)\right)^{2}+0\), so the vertex is \((-b,\,0)\) on the \(x\)-axis.

STEP 3Solve the inequality for \(b\)

From \(-b<0\), multiply both sides by \(-1\) (remember to reverse the inequality sign):

\[ -b<0 \;\Longrightarrow\; b>0 \]

So the vertex is at \((-b,0)\) with \(-b\) negative, i.e. the parabola sits on the left-hand side of the y-axis.

Hint: dividing or multiplying an inequality by a negative number flips the direction of the sign.

Reversing an Inequality \(-b<0\ \Longrightarrow\ b>0\) Multiplying by \(-1\) reverses the inequality, so the vertex lying left of the origin means \(b>0\).

STEP 4Combine both findings

From Step 1: \(a>0\). From Step 3: \(b>0\).

So \[ a>0 \quad\text{and}\quad b>0 \]

Compare with the options: this matches option C.

Hint: always check the y-intercept too — here \(y(0)=ab^2>0\), which agrees.

Answer: C. \(a>0\) and \(b>0\)

Question. The figure shows the graph of \(y=a(x+b)^{2}\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. The curve opens downwards and touches the \(x\)-axis at a point on the positive \(x\)-axis. Which of the following is true? A. \(a>0\) and \(b<0\) B. \(a>0\) and \(b>0\) C. \(a<0\) and \(b<0\) D. \(a<0\) and \(b>0\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The parabola opens downwards, so the coefficient \(a<0\).
Step 2. The equation is already in vertex form with vertex \((-b,\,0)\), and the curve touches the \(x\)-axis there.
Step 3. The touching point is on the positive \(x\)-axis, so \(-b>0\); multiplying by \(-1\) reverses the sign, giving \(b<0\).
Step 4. Hence \(a<0\) and \(b<0\), which is option C.
Answer: C: \(a<0\) and \(b<0\)
