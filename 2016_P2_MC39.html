3D Trigonometry — Finding \(\sin\angle PFQ\) in a Rectangular Block

Rectangular Block Space Diagonals Cosine Formula Pythagoras Theorem Sine–Cosine Identity

QQuestion

In the figure, \(ABCDEFGH\) is a rectangular block. \(AC\) and \(BD\) intersect at \(P\). \(Q\) is a point lying on \(CH\) such that \(CQ = 9\text{ cm}\) and \(QH = 15\text{ cm}\). Find \(\sin\angle PFQ\).
  • A. \(\dfrac{33}{65}\)
  • B. \(\dfrac{56}{65}\)
  • C. \(\dfrac{13}{5\sqrt{181}}\)
  • D. \(\dfrac{58}{13\sqrt{181}}\)

Original Figure

1Building the 3D Picture & Key Lengths

Before finding the angle, we must identify every relevant edge length of the block and locate the points \(P\) and \(Q\).

11MRead dimensions and locate points

The block has base \(AB = 16\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 12\text{ cm}\), and height \(CG = 15\text{ cm}\).

\(P\) is the centre of the base rectangle \(ABCD\) (where diagonals meet), and \(Q\) lies on \(CH\) with \(CQ = 9\text{ cm}\), \(QH = 15\text{ cm}\).

Hint: \(CG\) is vertical, so \(CH\) is a vertical edge of length \(9 + 15 = 24\text{ cm}\). Check: \(CG = 24\text{ cm}\)? — Actually \(CH\) is the vertical edge through \(C\), so \(CH = CG = 24\text{ cm}\). ✓
21MFind the base diagonal \(AC\) and \(PA\)

In right triangle \(ABC\) (base of block):

\[ AC=\sqrt{AB^{2}+BC^{2}}=\sqrt{16^{2}+12^{2}}=\sqrt{400}=20\text{ cm} \]

Since \(P\) is the midpoint of \(AC\):

\[ PA=PC=\frac{AC}{2}=10\text{ cm} \]
Hint: The base \(ABCD\) is a rectangle, so its diagonals bisect each other — \(P\) is the midpoint of both \(AC\) and \(BD\).
\(AC = 20\text{ cm},\quad PA = PC = 10\text{ cm}\)

2Find the Lengths \(FP\), \(PQ\), \(FQ\)

We need all three sides of \(\triangle PFQ\) to apply the cosine formula. Use a right triangle in space each time.

11MFind \(FP\) in right triangle \(AFP\)

\(AF\) is a vertical edge of the block. From the diagram, \(AF = 24\text{ cm}\). In right triangle \(AFP\) (right angle at \(A\)):

\[ FP=\sqrt{AP^{2}+AF^{2}}=\sqrt{10^{2}+24^{2}}=\sqrt{100+576}=\sqrt{676}=26\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(AF\) is vertical and \(AP\) lies in the base, so they are perpendicular — perfect for Pythagoras.
\(FP = 26\text{ cm}\)
21MFind \(PQ\) in right triangle \(CPQ\)

From Step 1, \(PC = 10\text{ cm}\). \(CQ = 9\text{ cm}\) is given on \(CH\). In right triangle \(CPQ\) (right angle at \(C\)):

\[ PQ=\sqrt{PC^{2}+CQ^{2}}=\sqrt{10^{2}+9^{2}}=\sqrt{100+81}=\sqrt{181}\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(CQ\) lies along the vertical edge \(CH\), and \(PC\) lies in the base — they are perpendicular at \(C\).
\(PQ = \sqrt{181}\text{ cm}\)
31MFind \(FQ\) in right triangle \(FHQ\)

\(FH = AB = 16\text{ cm}\) (top face edge), \(HQ = 15\text{ cm}\) is given. In right triangle \(FHQ\) (right angle at \(H\)):

\[ FQ=\sqrt{FH^{2}+HQ^{2}}=\sqrt{16^{2}+15^{2}}=\sqrt{256+225}=\sqrt{481}=25\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(FH\) lies in the top face and \(HQ\) is vertical — they meet at the corner \(H\) at a right angle. \(16\!-\!15\!-\!? \) gives \(25\) since \(256 + 225 = 481 = 25^2\)? Wait — re-check: \(16^2 = 256\), \(15^2 = 225\), sum \(= 481\). But the solution says \(25\) — let's verify with the given solution: \(FQ = \sqrt{FH^2 + HQ^2} = \sqrt{20^2 + 15^2} = 25\), so here \(FH = 20\text{ cm}\). The top face diagonal \(FH\) is not equal to \(AB\); \(FH\) is a face edge of length \(12\)? Let's trust the given working: \(FH = 20\text{ cm}\) is the diagonal of the top face. See next hint.

Careful: \(FH\) is a face diagonal of the top rectangle \(EFGH\), not an edge.

\[ FH = \sqrt{EF^2+EH^2} = \sqrt{16^2+12^2} = 20\text{ cm} \]
\(FQ = \sqrt{20^{2}+15^{2}} = 25\text{ cm}\)

3Apply the Cosine Formula to \(\triangle PFQ\)

Now that all three sides are known, we can find \(\cos\angle PFQ\), then turn it into \(\sin\angle PFQ\).

11MSet up the cosine formula

In \(\triangle PFQ\), the side opposite \(\angle PFQ\) is \(PQ\). So:

\[ \cos\angle PFQ=\frac{FP^{2}+FQ^{2}-PQ^{2}}{2\cdot FP\cdot FQ} \]
Hint: The cosine formula is \(a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A\). Here the angle at \(F\) is between \(FP\) and \(FQ\).
21ASubstitute values

\(FP = 26,\; FQ = 25,\; PQ = \sqrt{181}\):

\[ \cos\angle PFQ = \frac{26^{2}+25^{2}-(\sqrt{181})^{2}}{2\cdot 26\cdot 25} = \frac{676+625-181}{1300} = \frac{1120}{1300} = \frac{56}{65} \]
\(\cos\angle PFQ = \dfrac{56}{65}\)
31AUse the Pythagorean identity

Since \(\sin^{2}\theta + \cos^{2}\theta = 1\) and the angle is acute:

\[ \sin\angle PFQ = \sqrt{1-\cos^{2}\angle PFQ} = \sqrt{1-\left(\frac{56}{65}\right)^{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{65^{2}-56^{2}}{65^{2}}} \] \[ = \frac{\sqrt{4225-3136}}{65} = \frac{\sqrt{1089}}{65} = \frac{33}{65} \]
\(\boxed{\sin\angle PFQ = \dfrac{33}{65}}\)   → Answer A

📌 Summary of Results

  • Base diagonal: \(AC = \sqrt{16^2+12^2} = 20\text{ cm}\)
  • Half-diagonal: \(PA = PC = 10\text{ cm}\)
  • Space diagonal: \(FP = \sqrt{10^2+24^2} = 26\text{ cm}\)
  • Slant line: \(PQ = \sqrt{10^2+9^2} = \sqrt{181}\text{ cm}\)
  • Line: \(FQ = \sqrt{20^2+15^2} = 25\text{ cm}\)
  • Cosine formula: \(\cos\angle PFQ = \dfrac{56}{65}\)
  • Pythagorean identity: \(\sin\angle PFQ = \dfrac{33}{65}\)
  • Correct option: A
