Topic 12 · Coordinate Geometry — Rotation & Translation of Points

Original line \(PQ\) Image line \(P'Q'\) Original points \(P,Q\) Image points \(P',Q'\) Rotation arc (P→P') Translation vector (Q→Q')

[2014 #8]

The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((-3,\,5)\) and \((2,\,-7)\) respectively. \(P\) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin \(O\) through \(270^\circ\) to \(P'\). \(Q\) is translated leftwards by 21 units to \(Q'\).

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(P'\) and \(Q'\).

(b) Prove that \(PQ\) is perpendicular to \(P'Q'\).

[5 marks]

Solution

STEP 1 · Find P′ (rotation 270° anticlockwise)

A rotation of \(270^\circ\) anticlockwise about \(O\) is the same as \(90^\circ\) clockwise:

$$(x,\,y)\;\longrightarrow\;(y,\,-x)$$

With \(P(-3,\,5)\):

$$P' = (5,\,-(-3)) = (5,\,3)$$
\(P'(5,\,3)\)
STEP 2 · Find Q′ (translation left by 21 units)

“Leftwards by 21 units” subtracts 21 from the \(x\)-coordinate:

$$(x,\,y)\;\longrightarrow\;(x-21,\,y)$$

With \(Q(2,\,-7)\):

$$Q' = (2-21,\,-7) = (-19,\,-7)$$
\(Q'(-19,\,-7)\)
STEP 3 · Slope of PQ

Using the slope formula between \(P(-3,\,5)\) and \(Q(2,\,-7)\):

$$m_{PQ}=\frac{5-(-7)}{-3-2}=\frac{12}{-5}=-\frac{12}{5}$$
\(m_{PQ}=-\dfrac{12}{5}\)
STEP 4 · Slope of P′Q′

Between \(P'(5,\,3)\) and \(Q'(-19,\,-7)\):

$$m_{P'Q'}=\frac{3-(-7)}{5-(-19)}=\frac{10}{24}=\frac{5}{12}$$
\(m_{P'Q'}=\dfrac{5}{12}\)
STEP 5 · Conclude perpendicularity

Multiply the two slopes:

$$m_{PQ}\times m_{P'Q'}=-\frac{12}{5}\times\frac{5}{12}=-1$$

Since the product of the slopes is \(-1\), the two lines are perpendicular.

Answer:
(a) \(P'(5,\,3)\), \(Q'(-19,\,-7)\)
(b) \(m_{PQ}\times m_{P'Q'}=-1\), hence \(PQ\perp P'Q'\).
Exam tip: A \(270^\circ\) anticlockwise rotation = \(90^\circ\) clockwise: \((x,y)\to(y,-x)\). Two lines are perpendicular exactly when the product of their slopes is \(-1\) (provided neither is vertical).
