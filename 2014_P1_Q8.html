Coordinate Geometry — Rotation about the origin & translation

Rotation 270° about O Translation Slope of a Line Perpendicular Lines

Q8 The Question 5 marks

The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((-3,\,5)\) and \((2,\,-7)\) respectively. \(P\) is rotated anticlockwise about the origin \(O\) through \(270^\circ\) to \(P'\). \(Q\) is translated leftwards by \(21\) units to \(Q'\).

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(P'\) and \(Q'\). 2 marks

(b) Prove that \(PQ\) is perpendicular to \(P'Q'\). 3 marks

Part (a) Finding the coordinates of \(P'\) and \(Q'\)

How to read the diagram: drag the slider to watch \(P\) rotate anticlockwise about the origin about a circle of radius \(OP\). Red shows the rotation; violet shows the translation of \(Q\) leftwards.

Step 11A

Rotation of \(P\) through \(270^\circ\) anticlockwise about \(O\). Rotating a point \((x,\,y)\) anticlockwise about the origin through \(270^\circ\) gives \((y,\,-x)\):

\[ (x,\,y)\;\xrightarrow{\;270^\circ\;\text{acw}\;}\;(y,\,-x) \] \[ P(-3,\,5)\;\longrightarrow\;P'(5,\,-(-3))=(5,\,3) \]
Hint: A quarter-turn maps the \(x\)-axis to the \(y\)-axis. Check with a simple point: rotating \((1,0)\) by \(270^\circ\) anticlockwise should land on \((0,-1)\), and indeed \((y,-x)=(0,-1)\). ✓
Answer: \(P'=(5,\,3)\).
Step 21A

Translation of \(Q\) leftwards by \(21\) units. Moving a point leftwards by \(21\) units changes only the \(x\)-coordinate:

\[ Q(2,\,-7)\;\longrightarrow\;Q'(2-21,\,-7)=(-19,\,-7) \]
Hint: "Leftwards" means \(x\mapsto x-21\). The \(y\)-coordinate is untouched because the motion is horizontal.
Answer: \(Q'=(-19,\,-7)\).

Part (b) Proving \(PQ \perp P'Q'\)

How to read the diagram: the two segments are drawn as infinite lines (dashed) so you can see they cross at a right angle. Drag the slider to "squash" the picture vertically — notice the two slopes stay negative reciprocals of each other, which is exactly the perpendicular condition.

Step 11M

Slope of \(PQ\). Using \(P(-3,\,5)\) and \(Q(2,\,-7)\):

\[ m_{PQ}=\frac{5-(-7)}{-3-2}=\frac{12}{-5}=-\frac{12}{5} \]
Hint: Slope \(=\dfrac{y_1-y_2}{x_1-x_2}\). Keep the order of the points consistent in the numerator and the denominator.
Step 21A

Slope of \(P'Q'\). Using \(P'(5,\,3)\) and \(Q'(-19,\,-7)\):

\[ m_{P'Q'}=\frac{3-(-7)}{5-(-19)}=\frac{10}{24}=\frac{5}{12} \]
Hint: A simplified fraction is enough — but do not cancel the sign; \(m_{P'Q'}\) here is positive.
Step 31

Check the product of the slopes.

\[ m_{PQ}\times m_{P'Q'}=\left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)\times\left(\frac{5}{12}\right)=-1 \]

Since the product of the two slopes is \(-1\), the two lines are perpendicular:

\[ \therefore\; PQ \perp P'Q' \]
Hint: This is the standard criterion: two non-vertical lines are perpendicular \(\iff m_1 m_2 = -1\). State the conclusion clearly to earn the final mark.
Conclusion: \(m_{PQ}\times m_{P'Q'} = -1\), therefore \(PQ \perp P'Q'\). \(\blacksquare\)

Summary Answers at a glance

  • (a) \(P' = (5,\,3)\)  — rotation of \(P(-3,5)\) through \(270^\circ\) anticlockwise about \(O\).
  • (a) \(Q' = (-19,\,-7)\)  — translation of \(Q(2,-7)\) leftwards by \(21\) units.
  • (b) \(m_{PQ} = -\dfrac{12}{5} = -2.4\).
  • (b) \(m_{P'Q'} = \dfrac{5}{12} \approx 0.4167\).
  • (b) \(m_{PQ}\times m_{P'Q'} = -1 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{PQ \perp P'Q'}\).
  • Key idea: rotation through \(270^\circ\) maps \((x,y)\mapsto(y,-x)\); the perpendicular test is the negative-reciprocal slope product.
