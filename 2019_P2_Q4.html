2019MC#4  •  4. Polynomials
If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants such that \[(x-8)(x+\alpha)-6 \equiv (x-9)^2+\beta,\] then $\beta =$
A. $-26$
B. $-10$
C. $-7$
D. $-6$
1

Understand what an identity means

The symbol $\equiv$ means the two sides are equal for every value of $x$. So their $x^2$ coefficients, $x$ coefficients and constant terms must all match.
$(x-8)(x+\alpha)-6 \;\equiv\; (x-9)^2+\beta$
Key idea: an identity holds for all $x$ — you may either substitute a convenient value of $x$, or compare coefficients.
2

Method 1 — Substitute a convenient value of $x$

Choose $x=8$ so that the factor $(x-8)$ becomes $0$ and the left side simplifies instantly:
$(8-8)(8+\alpha)-6 = (8-9)^2+\beta$
$0-6 = (-1)^2+\beta$
$-6 = 1+\beta$
Why $x=8$? It kills the bracket $(x-8)$, and the right side uses $(x-9)$, which stays simple at $x=8$.
3

Solve for $\beta$

$\beta = -6-1 = \kern2pt \text{-}7$
Check with the answer options: $-7$ appears as option C.
4

Method 2 — Compare coefficients

Expand both sides:
LHS: $(x-8)(x+\alpha)-6 = x^2+(\alpha-8)x+(-8\alpha-6)$
RHS: $(x-9)^2+\beta = x^2-18x+(\beta+81)$
Comparing the $x$-term and the constant term:
$\begin{cases}\alpha-8=-18 & \dots(1)\\ -8\alpha-6=\beta+81 & \dots(2)\end{cases}$
Note: the $x^2$ terms already agree ($1=1$), so only two equations are needed for the two unknowns $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
5

Finish the comparison

From $(1)$: $\alpha = -18+8 = -10$.
$(1)\times 8 + (2)$: $\;-70 = \beta - 63$
$\beta = -70+63 = \text{-}7$
Consistency check: substituting $\alpha=-10$ into $(2)$ gives $-8(-10)-6 = 74 = \beta+81$, so $\beta=-7$. Both methods agree.
Answer:  $\beta = -7$
So the correct choice is C. $-7$  (with $\alpha=-10$).
