Polynomials Factorisation Grouping Difference of Squares

Question

Factorise the following expression:

$$2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}+4x+4y = \;?$$

Tap an option to test your guess (or follow the guided steps below).

🔍 Live Verification

Pick any numbers for \(x\) and \(y\). The original expression and the factorised form should always give the same value. If an option gives a different value, it must be wrong.

\(x =\) 2 drag me
\(y =\) 1 drag me

💡 The correct factorisation must match for every pair \((x,y)\), not just one.

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 — Look at the structure: group the first three terms

The expression has five terms. The first three terms \(2x^2+xy-y^2\) form a quadratic in two variables that can itself be factorised.

$$2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}+4x+4y = \underbrace{2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}}_{(1)} + \underbrace{4x+4y}_{(2)}$$
💡 Hint: For \(2x^2+xy-y^2\), split the middle term \(xy\) into \(2xy-xy\) and factorise by grouping.

Step 2 — Factorise \(2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}\)

Product of the \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) coefficients: \(2 \times (-1) = -2\). We need two numbers whose product is \(-2\) and whose sum is the middle coefficient \(1\):

$$2x^{2}+xy-y^{2} = 2x^{2}+2xy-xy-y^{2}$$ $$= 2x(x+y) - y(x+y)$$ $$= (x+y)(2x-y)$$
💡 Hint: \((x+y)\) is the common factor that appears in both groups — this is the key to the whole question.

Step 3 — Factorise the remaining two terms

The last two terms share the factor \(4\) (and also the factor \(x+y\)? Let's see):

$$4x+4y = 4(x+y)$$
✅ Notice: \((x+y)\) appears here too! That means the whole expression has a common factor \((x+y)\).

Step 4 — Take out the common factor \((x+y)\)

Put the two factorised pieces together:

$$2x^{2}+xy-y^{2}+4x+4y$$ $$= (x+y)(2x-y) + 4(x+y)$$ $$= (x+y)\big[(2x-y) + 4\big]$$
💡 Hint: Treat \((x+y)\) like a single object — say \(A\). Then the line reads \(A(2x-y) + 4A = A(2x-y+4)\).

Step 5 — Simplify and match the option

$$= (x+y)(2x-y+4)$$

Compare with the choices:

  • A. \((x+y)(2x+y-4)\) — sign of \(y\) inside is wrong
  • B. \((x+y)(2x-y+4)\) — ✅ matches
  • C. \((x-y)(2x+y-4)\) — first factor wrong
  • D. \((x-y)(2x-y+4)\) — first factor wrong
🎯 Answer: B

Step 1 of 5

🧠 Key Takeaway

  • When an expression has more than 3 terms and contains a repeated binomial, try factorisation by grouping.
  • Look for a common binomial factor — here \((x+y)\) appeared in both groups.
  • Always verify: expand your answer mentally, or substitute small numbers such as \(x=2,\;y=1\).
