Type 1

Data Types, Frequency Tables and Reading Charts · 7 questions

Numerical vs categorical, and reading frequencies off tables and charts

Core idea: data are either categorical (labels such as blood group or grade) or numerical, and numerical data are discrete (counted: goals, number of pets) or continuous (measured: height, time). Only numerical data can be averaged.

Organising data into a frequency table loses nothing and makes everything easier: the total frequency is the number of observations, a cumulative frequency counts "up to" a class boundary, and for a histogram the area of each bar is proportional to frequency, so the frequency density is the frequency divided by the class width.

\(\text{frequency density}=\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}\)\(\text{total frequency}=n\)\(\text{pie chart angle}=\frac{f}{n}\times360^{\circ}\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Classify each piece of data as categorical, discrete numerical or continuous numerical: (a) the number of pets a family owns; (b) the time a runner takes for 100 m; (c) the blood group of a student; (d) the grade (A, B, C) awarded to a project.

Answer(a) discrete numerical　(b) continuous numerical　(c) categorical　(d) categorical (ordinal)
  1. Counted whole numbers are discrete numerical data: the number of pets must be \(0,1,2,\ldots\), so (a) is discrete numerical.
  2. A measurement that can take any value in an interval is continuous: time for 100 m could be 12.83 s, so (b) is continuous numerical.
  3. Blood groups are labels with no order or arithmetic, so (c) is categorical.
  4. Grades are labels but have a natural order A, B, C, so (d) is categorical (ordinal).
Common mistake

Grades look "numerical" because they can be ranked, but you cannot add or average A + B; ranking alone does not make data numerical.

  1. (a) Boys: \(\frac{56}{70}\times100\%=80\%\). Girls: \(\frac{45}{50}\times100\%=90\%\).

Q7★★★☆5 marks

A company advertises with a bar chart of its yearly sales. The vertical axis starts at 400 (thousand dollars) rather than 0, and the bars for 2024 and 2025 reach 420 and 470. A journalist writes "sales jumped by 47% in one year". (a) Find the true percentage increase. (b) Explain why the chart exaggerates the change. (c) Suggest one improvement to the chart.

Answer(a) \(11.9\%\)　(b) the truncated axis exaggerates the visible difference　(c) start the axis at 0
  1. (a) True increase \(=470-420=50\) (thousand dollars) on a base of 420.
  2. Percentage increase \(=\frac{50}{420}\times100\%=11.9\%\) (3 s.f.), not 47%.
  3. (b) On the chart the visible bar heights are only \(420-400=20\) and \(470-400=70\) units, so the second bar looks about 3.5 times as tall although sales rose by only 11.9%. Starting the axis at 400 cuts off the common 400 units.
  4. (c) Start the vertical axis at 0 (or use a broken-axis symbol with a clear label) so that heights are proportional to the quantities.
Common mistake

The "47%" comes from \(\frac{470-420}{420-400}\), which divides by the visible part of the bar instead of by the actual 2024 sales.

Type 2

Mean, Median and Mode from Raw Data and Frequency Tables · 7 questions

Averages by formula, by position, and by frequency weighting

Core idea: the three averages answer different questions. The mean uses every value, \(\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x}{n}\) (or \(\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\) from a frequency table); the median is the middle value once the data are in order, so it depends only on position; the mode is the most frequent value, which is the only average that works for categorical data.

Two habits: sort the data before finding a median, and use \(n+1\) / average-of-two-middle positions carefully. When two data sets are combined, add the totals — never average the two means.

\(\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x}{n}\)\(\bar{x}=\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\)\(\text{combined mean}=\frac{n_{1}\bar{x}_{1}+n_{2}\bar{x}_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

For the data \(4,\ 7,\ 7,\ 9,\ 13\), write down the mean, the median and the mode.

Answermean \(8\), median \(7\), mode \(7\)
  1. Mean: \(\frac{4+7+7+9+13}{5}=\frac{40}{5}=8\).
  2. The data are already in order and \(n=5\), so the median is the 3rd value: \(7\).
  3. 7 occurs twice and every other value once, so the mode is \(7\).
Common mistake

Reporting the middle of the two end values \((4+13)/2\) as the median: the median is a data value located by position, not an average of the extremes.

Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

Five numbers have mean 14. A sixth number, 26, is added. Find the new mean.

Answer\(16\)
  1. The five numbers add to \(5\times14=70\).
  2. Adding 26 gives a total of \(70+26=96\) for 6 numbers.
  3. New mean \(=\frac{96}{6}=16\).
Common mistake

Averaging 14 and 26 to get 20 forgets that the five original numbers still count once each.

Q10★★☆☆3 marks

The table shows the number of hours 20 students spent on homework last week.

Hours12345
Frequency25832

Find (a) the mean, (b) the median, (c) the mode.

Answer(a) \(2.9\)　(b) \(3\)　(c) \(3\)
  1. (a) \(\sum fx=1(2)+2(5)+3(8)+4(3)+5(2)=2+10+24+12+10=58\), and \(\sum f=20\).
  2. Mean \(=\frac{58}{20}=2.9\) hours.
  3. (b) The cumulative frequencies are \(2,\ 7,\ 15,\ 18,\ 20\). Both the 10th and 11th values fall in the group with 3 hours, so the median is 3.
  4. (c) The largest frequency is 8, for 3 hours, so the mode is 3.

Using \(\frac{1+2+3+4+5}{5}=3\) as the mean: that ignores the frequencies, which are what make 3 hours so common.

Q11★★☆☆3 marks

The weekly earnings (in dollars) of nine part-time tutors are \(11,14,14,16,18,20,23,25,30\). Find the mean, the median and the mode.

Answermean \(19\), median \(18\), mode \(14\)
  1. Sum: \(11+14+14+16+18+20+23+25+30=171\), so the mean is \(\frac{171}{9}=19\).
  2. With \(n=9\) the median is the 5th value in order: \(18\).
  3. 14 appears twice, more often than any other value, so the mode is \(14\).
  4. Note the mean exceeds the median because the large value 30 pulls it upwards.
Common mistake

Counting \(n=9\) but taking the median as the 4th value; with 9 values the middle position is the 5th.

Q12★★☆☆4 marks

Class A has 30 students with a mean test score of 62. Class B has 20 students with a mean test score of 71. (a) Find the mean score of the 50 students combined. (b) Explain why the answer is not \(\frac{62+71}{2}=66.5\).

Answer(a) \(65.6\)　(b) the classes have different sizes
  1. Total marks of class A: \(30\times62=1860\).
  2. Total marks of class B: \(20\times71=1420\).
  3. (a) Combined mean \(=\frac{1860+1420}{50}=\frac{3280}{50}=65.6\).
  4. (b) The simple average 66.5 would give the two classes equal weight, but class A has 10 more students, so its lower mean should count more. Indeed 65.6 is closer to 62 than to 71.
Common mistake

Averaging the two means gives 66.5, which would only be right if both classes had the same number of students.

Q13★★★☆5 marks

Eight numbers have mean 15. (a) Find their total. (b) One number is removed and the mean of the remaining seven is 14. Find the number removed. (c) A new number is then added to those seven so that the mean of the eight numbers becomes 16. Find the new number.

Answer(a) 120　(b) 22　(c) 30
  1. (a) Total of the eight numbers \(=8\times15=120\).
  2. (b) The seven remaining numbers total \(7\times14=98\), so the number removed is \(120-98=22\).
  3. (c) The seven numbers still total 98. For eight numbers with mean 16 the total must be \(8\times16=128\).
  4. New number \(=128-98=30\).
  5. Check: \(98+30=128\) and \(\frac{128}{8}=16\). ✓
Common mistake

In (c) using \(8\times16-120=8\), which still includes the removed number 22 in the running total.

Q14★★★☆5 marks

The table shows the masses of 30 parcels, grouped into classes.

Mass (kg)0 – 1010 – 2020 – 3030 – 4040 – 50
Frequency361263

(a) Write down the class marks. (b) Estimate the mean mass. (c) Write down the modal class and the median class.

Answer(a) 5, 15, 25, 35, 45　(b) 25 kg　(c) both 20 – 30
  1. (a) The class mark is the midpoint: \(5,\ 15,\ 25,\ 35,\ 45\).
  2. (b) \(\sum fx=3(5)+6(15)+12(25)+6(35)+3(45)=15+90+300+210+135=750\).
  3. Estimated mean \(=\frac{750}{30}=25\) kg.
  4. (c) The largest frequency is 12 in the class 20 – 30, so that is the modal class. The cumulative frequencies are \(3,\ 9,\ 21,\ 27,\ 30\), and the 15th and 16th values both lie in 20 – 30, so the median class is also 20 – 30.

Using class limits such as 0 and 10 as the values: grouped data have lost the individual values, so the class mark is the best stand-in and the answer is an estimate.

Type 3

Range, Quartiles, Interquartile Range and Box-and-Whisker Diagrams · 7 questions

Five-number summary, IQR as a measure of spread, spotting outliers

Core idea: the range \(=\) largest \(-\) smallest is easy but is decided by two extreme values. The interquartile range \(\text{IQR}=Q_{3}-Q_{1}\) uses the middle half of the data, so a single wild value cannot distort it — which is why box-and-whisker diagrams are drawn from the five-number summary min, \(Q_{1}\), median, \(Q_{3}\), max.

Quartile positions: with \(n\) values in order, \(Q_{1}\) and \(Q_{3}\) are the medians of the lower and upper halves (the median itself is excluded when \(n\) is odd). A value is a suspected outlier if it lies beyond \(Q_{3}+1.5\,\text{IQR}\) or below \(Q_{1}-1.5\,\text{IQR}\).

\(\text{IQR}=Q_{3}-Q_{1}\)\(\text{range}=x_{\max}-x_{\min}\)\(\text{fence}=Q_{3}+1.5\,\text{IQR}\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

For the data \(3,\ 5,\ 8,\ 9,\ 12,\ 15,\ 18\), write down the range and the median.

Answerrange \(15\), median \(9\)
  1. The largest value is 18 and the smallest is 3, so the range is \(18-3=15\).
  2. There are 7 values, so the median is the 4th: \(9\).
Common mistake

Reading the range as the number of gaps (6) or as the difference between the two middle values; the range is largest minus smallest.

Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

For the data \(2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9,\ 10,\ 12,\ 15\), find the median, the lower quartile and the upper quartile.

Answermedian \(8\), \(Q_{1}=4.5\), \(Q_{3}=11\)
  1. There are \(n=8\) values, so the median is the average of the 4th and 5th: \(\frac{7+9}{2}=8\).
  2. The lower half is \(2,4,5,7\); its median is \(\frac{4+5}{2}=4.5\), so \(Q_{1}=4.5\).
  3. The upper half is \(9,10,12,15\); its median is \(\frac{10+12}{2}=11\), so \(Q_{3}=11\).
  4. Hence \(\text{IQR}=11-4.5=6.5\).
Common mistake

Taking \(Q_{1}=4\) (the 2nd value) or \(Q_{3}=12\): with an even \(n\) each quartile is the average of the two middle values of its half.

Q17★★☆☆3 marks

The waiting times (in minutes) of 11 customers are \(4,\ 7,\ 9,\ 11,\ 13,\ 15,\ 16,\ 18,\ 20,\ 22,\ 25\). Find (a) the median, (b) the interquartile range, (c) the range.

Answer(a) \(15\)　(b) \(11\)　(c) \(21\)
  1. (a) With \(n=11\) the median is the 6th value: \(15\).
  2. (b) Excluding the median, the lower half is \(4,7,9,11,13\) with median \(9=Q_{1}\); the upper half is \(16,18,20,22,25\) with median \(20=Q_{3}\).
  3. \(\text{IQR}=20-9=11\) minutes.
  4. (c) Range \(=25-4=21\) minutes.
Common mistake

Including the median 15 in both halves and then averaging 13 and 16 to get \(Q_{1}\), \(Q_{3}\) wrongly; with odd \(n\) the median is left out of both halves.

Q18★★☆☆3 marks

The five-number summary of the daily sales (in dollars) of a shop is: minimum 12, \(Q_{1}=18\), median 24, \(Q_{3}=31\), maximum 45. (a) Find the interquartile range. (b) Find the range. (c) Test whether 45 is an outlier by the \(1.5\times\text{IQR}\) rule. (d) Describe the shape of the distribution.

Answer(a) 13　(b) 33　(c) not an outlier (fence 50.5)　(d) skewed to the right
  1. (a) \(\text{IQR}=31-18=13\).
  2. (b) Range \(=45-12=33\).
  3. (c) Upper fence \(=Q_{3}+1.5\,\text{IQR}=31+1.5(13)=31+19.5=50.5\). Since \(45<50.5\), 45 is not an outlier.
  4. (d) The distances between the five values are \(6,\ 6,\ 7,\ 14\) and the right whisker is much the longest, so the distribution is skewed to the right.
Common mistake

Declaring 45 an outlier just because it is far above the rest: the \(1.5\,\text{IQR}\) rule sets a numerical fence, and 45 sits inside it.

Q19★★☆☆4 marks

Two machines produce rods. Ten rods from machine \(A\) have lengths (cm) \(5,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12,\ 15,\ 18,\ 25\) and seven from machine \(B\) have lengths \(9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13,\ 14,\ 15\). (a) Find the interquartile range of each set. (b) Which machine is more consistent? Justify your answer.

Answer(a) \(A\): 10, \(B\): 4　(b) machine \(B\)
  1. Set \(A\) has \(n=7\), median \(12\); the lower half \(5,8,10\) gives \(Q_{1}=8\) and the upper half \(15,18,25\) gives \(Q_{3}=18\).
  2. \(\text{IQR}_{A}=18-8=10\) cm.
  3. Set \(B\) has \(n=7\), median \(12\); \(Q_{1}=10\) and \(Q_{3}=14\), so \(\text{IQR}_{B}=14-10=4\) cm.
  4. (b) Machine \(B\) has the smaller IQR (4 against 10) and also the smaller range (6 against 20), so its lengths are clustered more tightly around the median. Machine \(B\) is more consistent.
  5. Both sets have the same median 12, so the comparison is purely about spread.
Common mistake

Comparing only the means or medians cannot answer "more consistent"; consistency is a question about spread, so quote the IQR or the standard deviation.

Q20★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2013 Paper 2 Q29 ↗

The hourly wages (in dollars) of 12 workers are \(3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8,\ 9,\ 10,\ 11,\ 12,\ 14,\ 16,\ 40\). (a) Find the median, \(Q_{1}\) and \(Q_{3}\). (b) Find the interquartile range and the range. (c) Using the \(1.5\times\text{IQR}\) rule, decide whether 40 is an outlier. (d) Sketch (in words) the shape of the box-and-whisker diagram.

Answer(a) \(9.5,\ 6.5,\ 13\)　(b) IQR \(6.5\), range \(37\)　(c) yes, an outlier　(d) long right whisker
  1. (a) \(n=12\), so the median is the average of the 6th and 7th values: \(\frac{9+10}{2}=9.5\).
  2. Lower half \(3,5,6,7,8,9\): \(Q_{1}=\frac{6+7}{2}=6.5\); upper half \(10,11,12,14,16,40\): \(Q_{3}=\frac{12+14}{2}=13\).
  3. (b) \(\text{IQR}=13-6.5=6.5\) and range \(=40-3=37\).
  4. (c) Upper fence \(=13+1.5(6.5)=13+9.75=22.75\). Since \(40>22.75\), the value 40 is an outlier.
  5. (d) The left part of the box is compact (3 to 9.5) while the right whisker stretches from 13 to 40, so the diagram is strongly skewed to the right.
Common mistake

A large range alone does not create an outlier; the test is the fence built from the IQR, and here the IQR is small precisely because the data are tightly packed.

Q21★★★☆5 marks

The box-and-whisker diagram of the marks of 200 candidates in a test has minimum 30, \(Q_{1}=52\), median 61, \(Q_{3}=70\) and maximum 95. (a) Find the interquartile range and the range. (b) Approximately how many candidates scored more than 70? (c) A candidate scored 90. Is the mark extreme? Use the \(1.5\times\text{IQR}\) rule. (d) Comment on the shape of the distribution.

Answer(a) IQR 18, range 65　(b) about 50　(c) not an outlier, the fence is 97　(d) mildly skewed to the right
  1. (a) \(\text{IQR}=70-52=18\) and range \(=95-30=65\).
  2. (b) About 25% of candidates score above \(Q_{3}\), so the number is roughly \(0.25\times200=50\).
  3. (c) Upper fence \(=70+1.5(18)=70+27=97\). Since \(90<97\), the mark 90 is high but still not classified as an outlier.
  4. (d) The right whisker runs from 70 to 95 (25 marks) while the left runs from 30 to 52 (22 marks), and the median sits closer to \(Q_{1}\); the distribution is only mildly skewed to the right.

Part (b): \(Q_{3}\) is not the mark of the 75th candidate in rank order; it is the value below which 75% of the data lie, so a quarter of 200 candidates lie above it.

Standard Deviation and Variance: Both Formulas and What They Measure · 7 questions

Averaged squared deviation from the mean, and its square root

Core idea: the variance is the mean of the squared deviations from the mean, \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sum (x-\bar{x})^{2}}{n}\), and the standard deviation is its square root, \(\sigma\). Both measure spread: how far a typical value sits from the mean. A small \(\sigma\) means the data hug the mean; \(\sigma=0\) means every value is identical.

In computation the equivalent form \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sum fx^{2}}{\sum f}-\bar{x}^{2}\) is usually quicker, and it is the only practical route for a frequency table. Standard deviation keeps the units of the data, so it can be compared directly with the mean.

\(\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sum (x-\bar{x})^{2}}{n}\)\(\sigma^{2}=\frac{\sum fx^{2}}{\sum f}-\bar{x}^{2}\)\(\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^{2}}\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the mean and the standard deviation of the data \(2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10\).

Answermean \(6\), \(\sigma=2.83\) (3 s.f.)
  1. Mean: \(\bar{x}=\frac{2+4+6+8+10}{5}=\frac{30}{5}=6\).
  2. Deviations: \(-4,-2,0,2,4\), squares \(16,4,0,4,16\), total \(40\).
  3. \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{40}{5}=8\), so \(\sigma=\sqrt{8}=2.828\ldots\approx2.83\) (3 s.f.).
Common mistake

Dividing by 4 (the number of deviations after the first) instead of by \(n=5\); the variance is the mean squared deviation.

Q23★☆☆☆2 marks

Find the standard deviation of the data \(3,\ 5,\ 7\), correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer\(1.63\)
  1. Mean \(=\frac{3+5+7}{3}=5\).
  2. Deviations \(-2,0,2\), squares \(4,0,4\), total \(8\).
  3. \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{8}{3}=2.667\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt{2.666\ldots}=1.632\ldots\approx1.63\).
Common mistake

Forgetting the square root and quoting the variance 2.67; the question asks for the standard deviation, which is in the same units as the data.

Q24★★☆☆3 marks

The masses of six parcels (in kg) are \(4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12,\ 14\). Find the variance and the standard deviation, giving the standard deviation correct to 3 significant figures.

Answer\(\sigma^{2}=11.7\), \(\sigma=3.42\)
  1. Mean \(=\frac{54}{6}=9\).
  2. Deviations \(-5,-3,-1,1,3,5\); squares \(25,9,1,1,9,25\), total \(70\).
  3. \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{70}{6}=\frac{35}{3}=11.67\approx11.7\).
  4. \(\sigma=\sqrt{11.666\ldots}=3.415\ldots\approx3.42\) (3 s.f.).
Common mistake

Type 5

Grouped Data: Class Marks, Estimated Mean, Cumulative Frequency and the Median from a Curve · 7 questions

Midpoints for the mean; cumulative frequency and interpolation for the median

Core idea: once data are grouped the individual values are lost, so the mean is estimated by pretending every item sits at its class mark (the midpoint): \(\bar{x}\approx\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\). The answer is an estimate and should be described as such.

The median is found from the cumulative frequency: locate the position \(\frac{n}{2}\), find the class where the cumulative frequency passes it, then interpolate inside that class — \(L+\frac{\text{position}-cf_{\text{before}}}{f}\times w\). The same linear interpolation is what reading an ogive does graphically.

\(\text{class mark}=\frac{\text{lower}+\text{upper}}{2}\)\(\bar{x}\approx\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\)\(\text{median}=L+\frac{\frac{n}{2}-cf}{f}w\)

Type 6

Comparing Two Data Sets and the Effect of Changing Every Value · 7 questions

How mean, median and spread move under adding, removing and scaling

Core idea: adding the same constant \(k\) to every value adds \(k\) to the mean and the median but leaves the range, IQR and standard deviation unchanged, because every gap is unchanged. Multiplying every value by \(c>0\) multiplies the mean, median, range, IQR and standard deviation by \(c\), and multiplies the variance by \(c^{2}\).

To compare two data sets properly quote an average and a measure of spread: the average says which is higher, the spread says which is more consistent. A standard score \(z=\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\sigma}\) allows a fair comparison between two different distributions.

\(x\to x+k:\ \bar{x}\to\bar{x}+k,\ \sigma\) unchanged\(x\to cx:\ \bar{x}\to c\bar{x},\ \sigma\to c\sigma\)\(z=\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\sigma}\)

Exam-Level Interpretation: Which Average to Quote, and Why · 8 questions

Choosing and justifying an average; writing a conclusion that earns the mark

Core idea: in an exam the number alone rarely earns full marks — the justification must name the property being used. Quote the median when the data contain an outlier or are skewed, because the mean is dragged towards the tail; quote the mode for categorical data or for the most typical size (shoe sizes, ticket numbers); quote the mean when every value should count, for example for a total or a fair share.

A comparison answer needs three parts: quote an average for the level, quote a measure of spread (IQR or standard deviation) for the consistency, then state the conclusion in the context of the question.

\(\text{outliers}\Rightarrow\) quote the median\(\text{labels}\Rightarrow\) quote the mode\(z=\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\sigma}\)

  2. (b) Median position \(\frac{30}{2}=15\); cumulative frequencies are \(3,10,22,28,30\), so the median lies in 20 – 30: \(20+\frac{15-10}{12}\times10=20+4.17=24.6\).
  3. (c) \(\sum fx^{2}=3(25)+7(225)+12(625)+6(1225)+2(2025)=75+1575+7500+7350+4050=20550\).
  4. \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{20550}{30}-24^{2}=685-576=109\), so \(\sigma=\sqrt{109}=10.44\ldots\approx10.4\).
  5. (d) A mean and median near 24 out of 50 is a modest, not strong, performance (under half marks). The standard deviation of about 10 marks is large relative to that mean, so the class is very mixed: 10 students scored below 20 while 8 scored 30 or more.
Common mistake

Describing a class of 30 as uniform from a mean near the middle of the range; the standard deviation shows the marks are spread across all five classes.

Q49★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2012 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

The weekly study hours of 20 students are \(45,\ 48,\ 50,\ 52,\ 55,\ 57,\ 58,\ 59,\ 60,\ 61,\ 61,\ 62,\ 64,\ 65,\ 66,\ 68,\ 70,\ 72,\ 75,\ 80\). (a) Find the mean and the median. (b) One more student, who studied 20 hours, joins the group. Find the new mean and state the new median. (c) Find the standard deviation of the original 20 students and of the new group of 21. (d) Which average should now be quoted for the group, and why?

Answer(a) \(61.4\), \(61\)　(b) mean \(59.4\), median still \(61\)　(c) \(8.80\) and \(12.3\)　(d) the median
  1. (a) \(\sum x=1228\), so the mean is \(\frac{1228}{20}=61.4\). With \(n=20\) the median is \(\frac{61+61}{2}=61\).
  2. (b) Adding 20 gives a total of 1248 for 21 students: new mean \(=\frac{1248}{21}=59.43\ldots\approx59.4\).
  3. The new value 20 is the smallest, so it becomes the 1st value and the old 11th value moves to the 12th position: with \(n=21\) the median is the 11th value, still 61.
  4. (c) Original: squared deviations sum to 1548.8, so \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{1548.8}{20}=77.44\) and \(\sigma=8.80\). New group: \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{22268}{147}=151.48\), so \(\sigma=12.3\) (3 s.f.).
  5. (d) The mean fell by 2 hours and the standard deviation rose by about 40%, both driven by the one very low value. The median did not move at all, so the median is the better summary of a typical student's study time now.
Common mistake

Assuming the median must change when a value is added: the median depends on position, and one new smallest value shifts positions without changing the middle value here.

Q50★★★★8 marks

Two groups sit the same test. The 13 marks of group \(A\) are \(48,\ 52,\ 56,\ 60,\ 64,\ 68,\ 70,\ 72,\ 76,\ 80,\ 84,\ 88,\ 92\). Group \(B\) has mean 70 and standard deviation 5. (a) Find the mean and the median of group \(A\). (2 marks) (b) Show that the standard deviation of group \(A\) is 13.3, correct to 3 significant figures. (3 marks) (c) Compare the two groups. (1 mark) (d) A student in group \(A\) scored 92. Find her standard score, and the mark in group \(B\) that has the same standard score. (2 marks)

Answer(a) mean 70, median 70　(b) 13.3　(c) same level, \(A\) far more spread　(d) \(1.66\) and about \(78.3\)
  1. (a) \(\sum x=910\) over 13 values, so the mean is \(\frac{910}{13}=70\). The median is the 7th value, \(70\).
  2. (b) Deviations from 70: \(-22,-18,-14,-10,-6,-2,0,2,6,10,14,18,22\).
  3. Squares sum to \(484+324+196+100+36+4+0+4+36+100+196+324+484=2288\).
  4. \(\sigma^{2}=\frac{2288}{13}=176\), so \(\sigma=\sqrt{176}=13.266\ldots\approx13.3\) (3 s.f.).
  5. (c) Both groups average 70, but \(A\)'s standard deviation (13.3) is more than twice \(B\)'s (5): \(A\) has the same level of performance with much greater variation.
  6. (d) Standard score of 92 in \(A\): \(z=\frac{92-70}{13.266}=1.658\ldots\approx1.66\).
  7. The matching mark in \(B\): \(x=70+1.658(5)=78.29\approx78.3\).
  8. So 92 in \(A\) is as outstanding as about 78 in \(B\) — consistent with \(B\) being the tighter group.
Common mistake

Part (d): comparing 92 with 70 in raw marks and calling it more impressive, without dividing by the standard deviation; the same gap in \(B\) would be (78.3 − 70) = 8.3 marks, only 1.66 standard deviations.
