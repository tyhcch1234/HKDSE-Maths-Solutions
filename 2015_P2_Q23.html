The rectangular coordinates of the point \(A\) are \((\sqrt{3},\,-1)\). If \(A\) is reflected with respect to the \(y\)-axis, then the polar coordinates of its image are
  • A\((1,\,210^\circ)\)
  • B\((1,\,240^\circ)\)
  • C\((2,\,210^\circ)\)
  • D\((2,\,240^\circ)\)
Step 1 · Apply the reflection

Reflection about the \(y\)-axis sends \((x,\,y)\mapsto(-x,\,y)\). Hence

\[ A(\sqrt{3},\,-1) \;\longmapsto\; A'(-\sqrt{3},\,-1). \]

Reflection in the y-axis \((x,\ y)\longmapsto(-x,\ y)\) Reflecting \(A(\sqrt{3},-1)\) gives \(A^{\prime}(-\sqrt{3},-1)\).
Step 2 · Find the radius \(r\)

\[ r = \sqrt{(-\sqrt{3})^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{3+1} = \sqrt{4} = 2. \]

The radius is the distance from the pole \(O\) — it is unchanged by reflection, so \(r=2\) for both \(A\) and \(A'\).
Step 3 · Find the angle \(\theta\)

\(A'\) lies in the third quadrant, since its \(x\)- and \(y\)-coordinates are both negative: \(x=-\sqrt3<0\), \(y=-1<0\).

Reference angle: \(\tan\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} \Rightarrow \alpha = 30^\circ\).

So the polar angle is

\[ \theta = 180^\circ + 30^\circ = 210^\circ. \]

Polar Coordinates of a Point \(r=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}},\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{y}{x}\) With \(x=-\sqrt{3}\) and \(y=-1\) the point is in the third quadrant, so \(\theta=180^{\circ}+30^{\circ}\).
Step 4 · Write the polar coordinates

The image is \[ (2,\,210^\circ). \]

Check: \(A(\sqrt3,-1)\) is \((2,\,330^\circ)\). Reflection about the \(y\)-axis sends \(\theta\) to \(180^\circ-\theta\): \[ 180^\circ-330^\circ = -150^\circ \equiv 210^\circ. \]

Final Answer C. \((2,\,210^\circ)\)

Question. The rectangular coordinates of the point \(A\) are \((1,\,-\sqrt{3})\). If \(A\) is reflected with respect to the \(y\)-axis, find the polar coordinates of its image.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Reflection in the \(y\)-axis: \((1,\,-\sqrt{3})\mapsto(-1,\,-\sqrt{3})\).
Step 2. \(r=\sqrt{(-1)^{2}+(-\sqrt{3})^{2}}=\sqrt{4}=2\).
Step 3. The image lies in the third quadrant; the reference angle satisfies \(\tan\alpha=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{1}\), so \(\alpha=60^{\circ}\).
Step 4. Hence \(\theta=180^{\circ}+60^{\circ}=240^{\circ}\) and the image is \((2,\,240^{\circ})\).
Answer: \((2,\,240^{\circ})\)
