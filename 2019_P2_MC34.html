Complex Numbers Powers of i Rationalisation Real Part

HKDSE Math 2019 Paper 2 Q34

If $a$ is a real number, then the real part of

$\displaystyle \frac{4+i^5}{a+i} - i^6$

is

A. $\displaystyle \frac{4a+1}{a^2+1}$
B. $\displaystyle \frac{a^2+4a+2}{a^2-1}$
C. $\displaystyle 4a+1$
D. $\displaystyle \frac{a^2+4a+2}{a^2+1}$

💡 Hint: Simplify $i^5$ and $i^6$ first, then separate real and imaginary parts.

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

1 Simplify powers of $i$ ▼

Recall the cyclic pattern: $i^1 = i$, $i^2 = -1$, $i^3 = -i$, $i^4 = 1$, and it repeats every 4.

$i^5 = i^{4+1} = i^4 \cdot i = 1 \cdot i = i$
$i^6 = i^{4+2} = i^4 \cdot i^2 = 1 \cdot (-1) = -1$
💡 Hint: Divide the exponent by 4 and use the remainder. $5 \div 4 = 1$ r $1$, so $i^5 = i$. $6 \div 4 = 1$ r $2$, so $i^6 = i^2 = -1$.
2 Substitute back into the expression ▼

Now replace $i^5$ with $i$ and $i^6$ with $-1$:

$\displaystyle \frac{4+i^5}{a+i} - i^6 = \frac{4+i}{a+i} - (-1)$
$\displaystyle = \frac{4+i}{a+i} + 1$
💡 Hint: Subtracting a negative gives a positive: $-(-1) = +1$.
3 Rationalise the denominator ▼

To separate real and imaginary parts, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator $a+i$, which is $a-i$:

$\displaystyle \frac{4+i}{a+i} = \frac{4+i}{a+i} \cdot \frac{a-i}{a-i}$

The denominator becomes a real number: $(a+i)(a-i) = a^2 - i^2 = a^2 - (-1) = a^2 + 1$.

💡 Hint: For any complex number $a+bi$, its conjugate is $a-bi$. Multiplying them gives $a^2+b^2$ (a real number).
4 Expand the numerator ▼

Expand $(4+i)(a-i)$ carefully:

$(4+i)(a-i) = 4a - 4i + ai - i^2$
$= 4a - 4i + ai + 1$

Group real terms and imaginary terms:

$= (4a + 1) + (a - 4)i$
💡 Hint: Remember $i^2 = -1$, so $-i^2 = +1$. Combine $a i - 4i = (a-4)i$.
5 Write as real + imaginary parts ▼

Put the fraction back together and add the $+1$ from Step 2:

$\displaystyle \frac{(4a+1) + (a-4)i}{a^2+1} + 1$
$\displaystyle = \frac{4a+1}{a^2+1} + \frac{a-4}{a^2+1}i + 1$

Write $1$ as $\displaystyle \frac{a^2+1}{a^2+1}$ to combine with the real part:

$\displaystyle = \frac{a^2+1 + 4a+1}{a^2+1} + \frac{a-4}{a^2+1}i$
💡 Hint: To add $1$ to a fraction, make both have the same denominator: $1 = \frac{a^2+1}{a^2+1}$.
6 Identify the real part ▼

Simplify the real part:

$\displaystyle \frac{a^2+1 + 4a+1}{a^2+1} = \frac{a^2+4a+2}{a^2+1}$

The expression is now in the form $X + Yi$ where $X$ is the real part and $Y$ is the imaginary part.

$\displaystyle \text{Real part} = \frac{a^2+4a+2}{a^2+1}$
💡 Hint: The real part is the term without $i$. Here, it's the first fraction.

✅ ANSWER

D. $\displaystyle \frac{a^2+4a+2}{a^2+1}$

📝 Key Takeaways

  • Powers of $i$: $i^2=-1$, $i^3=-i$, $i^4=1$, then cycles every 4.
  • Rationalising: Multiply numerator & denominator by the conjugate $a-bi$.
  • Real part: The term without $i$ after writing in the form $x+yi$.
  • Common mistake: Forgetting that $-i^6 = -(-1) = +1$.
