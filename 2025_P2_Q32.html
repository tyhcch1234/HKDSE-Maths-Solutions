4. Polynomials — L.C.M. of Polynomials

The L.C.M. of $\;p^{2}-4q^{2},\;p^{3}-8q^{3}\;$ and $\;(p+2q)(p^{2}-4q^{2})$ is

  1. A.  $p-2q$
  2. B.  $p^{2}-4q^{2}$
  3. C.  $(p+2q)^{2}\bigl(p^{3}-8q^{3}\bigr)$
  4. D.  $(p+2q)\bigl(p^{2}-4q^{2}\bigr)\bigl(p^{3}-8q^{3}\bigr)$

Step-by-step solution

1Factorise each expression into prime factors.

$p^{2}-4q^{2}=(p+2q)(p-2q)$  (difference of two squares)
$p^{3}-8q^{3}=(p-2q)\bigl(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\bigr)$  (difference of two cubes)
$(p+2q)(p^{2}-4q^{2})=(p+2q)(p+2q)(p-2q)=(p+2q)^{2}(p-2q)$

Hint: for \(p>2q\) we have \(p^{2}-4q^{2}=(p+2q)(p-2q)>0\) and \(p^{3}-8q^{3}>0\), so no sign change is involved — just factorise the brackets.
Difference of Two Cubes \(p^{3}-8q^{3}=(p-2q)\left(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\right)\) The cubic factorises with the quadratic factor \(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\).
Difference of Two Squares \(p^{2}-4q^{2}=(p+2q)(p-2q)\) The first expression splits into two linear factors.
2Collect every distinct factor with its highest power.

• $(p-2q)$: appears in all three, highest power $=1$
• $(p+2q)$: appears to power $1$ in the 1st, power $2$ in the 3rd $\Rightarrow$ highest power $=2$
• $(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2})$: appears only once, highest power $=1$

For an L.C.M. of polynomials, take the maximum index of each irreducible factor.
L.C.M. of Polynomials \(\mathrm{L.C.M.}=\prod f_{i}^{\,\max e_{i}}\) Take every distinct irreducible factor to its highest power over the three expressions.
3Multiply the selected prime powers together.

$\mathrm{L.C.M.}=(p-2q)^{1}(p+2q)^{2}\bigl(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\bigr)$

This is exactly the L.C.M. — nothing more, nothing less.
4Recombine into the clearest form.

$(p+2q)^{2}(p-2q)\bigl(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\bigr)$
$=(p+2q)^{2}\,(p-2q)\bigl(p^{2}+2pq+4q^{2}\bigr)$
$=(p+2q)^{2}\bigl(p^{3}-8q^{3}\bigr)$

Regrouping the last two brackets gives back the difference of cubes — this matches option C.
5Choose the answer.

$\mathrm{L.C.M.}=(p+2q)^{2}\bigl(p^{3}-8q^{3}\bigr)$  $\Rightarrow$ Option C

Check: option D uses a product of both expressions (that's the H.C.F.-style over-count); B and A are too small.
Answer: C  —  $(p+2q)^{2}\bigl(p^{3}-8q^{3}\bigr)$

Question. Find the L.C.M. of \(\;p^{2}-9q^{2},\;p^{3}+27q^{3}\;\) and \(\;(p-3q)\bigl(p^{2}-9q^{2}\bigr)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Factorise each expression: \(p^{2}-9q^{2}=(p-3q)(p+3q)\) and \(p^{3}+27q^{3}=(p+3q)\left(p^{2}-3pq+9q^{2}\right)\).
Step 2. The third expression is \((p-3q)^{2}(p+3q)\).
Step 3. Highest powers: \((p-3q)^{2}\), \((p+3q)^{1}\), \(\left(p^{2}-3pq+9q^{2}\right)^{1}\).
Step 4. \(\mathrm{L.C.M.}=(p-3q)^{2}(p+3q)\left(p^{2}-3pq+9q^{2}\right)=(p-3q)^{2}\left(p^{3}+27q^{3}\right)\).
Step 5. Check at \(p=2,\ q=1\): the three values are \(-5,\ 35,\ 5\), whose L.C.M. is \(35\), and the answer gives \((-1)^{2}(35)=35\) ✓
Answer: \((p-3q)^{2}\left(p^{3}+27q^{3}\right)\)
