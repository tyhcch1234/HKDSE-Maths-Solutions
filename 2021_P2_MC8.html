Polynomials — Remainder Theorem

Find the remainder when $p(x)$ is divided by $x^{2}-1$.

Remainder Theorem Factor Theorem Degree of Remainder Simultaneous Equations

Q Question

Let $p(x)$ be a polynomial. When $p(x)$ is divided by $x+1$, the remainder is $-2$. If $p(x)$ is divisible by $x-1$, find the remainder when $p(x)$ is divided by $x^{2}-1$.

Choose the correct answer:

A $x+1$
B $x-1$
C $-x+1$
D $-x-1$

1 Core Idea — What is being tested?

Two classic polynomial tools are combined here:

(a) Remainder Theorem: the remainder when $p(x)$ is divided by $(x-a)$ equals $p(a)$.

(b) Factor Theorem: if $p(a)=0$, then $(x-a)$ is a factor of $p(x)$ — equivalently the remainder is $0$.

$p(x)=(x-1)Q_1(x)+0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad p(1)=0$

We are given $p(-1)=-2$ and $p(1)=0$. We must reconstruct the remainder upon division by the quadratic $x^{2}-1$.

2 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 Identify the degree of the remainder ▶

When a polynomial $p(x)$ is divided by a divisor $D(x)$, the remainder has degree strictly less than $\deg D(x)$.

Here $D(x)=x^{2}-1$ has degree $2$, so the remainder must be of the form:

$ax+b$    (degree $0$ or $1$; $a,b$ constants)

We therefore write the division identity:

$p(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(ax+b)$
General rule: If $\deg(\text{divisor})=n$, then $\deg(\text{remainder}) \le n-1$.
Dividing by $x-1$ (degree 1) gives a constant remainder.
Dividing by $x^{2}-1$ (degree 2) gives a remainder of the form $ax+b$ — two unknowns $a$ and $b$, so we need two conditions.
Step 2 Translate "divisible by $x-1$" into $p(1)=0$ ▶

If $p(x)$ is divisible by $x-1$, the remainder is $0$. By the Remainder Theorem (with $a=1$):

$p(1)=0$    …… (condition 1)
Using $p(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(ax+b)$, put $x=1$:
$\;p(1)=((1)^{2}-1)q(1)+(a\cdot 1+b)=0\cdot q(1)+a+b=a+b$.
Since $p(1)=0$, we get $\;a+b=0$.
Step 3 Translate "remainder $-2$ upon division by $x+1$" into $p(-1)=-2$ ▶

By the Remainder Theorem with $a=-1$:

$p(-1)=-2$    …… (condition 2)
Put $x=-1$ in $p(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(ax+b)$:
$\;p(-1)=((-1)^{2}-1)q(-1)+(a(-1)+b)=0\cdot q(-1)-a+b=\;-a+b$.
Since $p(-1)=-2$, we get $\;-a+b=-2$.
Step 4 Solve the simultaneous equations ▶

We now have a simple linear system:

$\begin{cases} a+b=0 & \text{…… (1)}\\ -a+b=-2 & \text{…… (2)}\end{cases}$

Elimination: subtract (2) from (1):

$(a+b)-(-a+b)=0-(-2)\;\Rightarrow\;2a=2\;\Rightarrow\;a=1$

Substitute $a=1$ into (1):

$1+b=0\;\Rightarrow\;b=-1$
Watch the signs: $(a+b)-(-a+b) = a+b+a-b = 2a$ (the $b$'s cancel).
Right side: $0-(-2)=+2$. So $2a=2 \Rightarrow a=1$.
Then $b=-1$. Both are quick to verify: $1+(-1)=0$ ✓ and $-1+(-1)=-2$ ✓.
Step 5 Write the answer ▶

Substituting $a=1,\;b=-1$ back into the remainder $ax+b$:

$p(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(x-1)$

Hence the remainder when $p(x)$ is divided by $x^{2}-1$ is:

Remainder $=x-1$  →  Answer: B

Sanity check: Does $x-1$ give $p(1)=0$? Yes: $1-1=0$ ✓. Does it give $p(-1)=-2$? Yes: $-1-1=-2$ ✓.
