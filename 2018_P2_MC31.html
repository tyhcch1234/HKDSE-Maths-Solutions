The Question

Let \(f(x)\) be a quadratic function. The figure below may represent the graph of \(y=f(x)\) and

  • Athe graph of \(y=f(x)+4\).
  • Bthe graph of \(y=f(-x)+4\).
  • Cthe graph of \(y=-f(x)+4\).
  • Dthe graph of \(y=-f(x)-4\).

The figure shows two curves: the first opens downward and cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(x=2\) and \(x=5\); the second opens upward and cuts the \(x\)-axis at \(x=6\) and \(x=9\).

Part (a) — Read off the given graph of \(y=f(x)\)

The question says the figure may represent the graph of \(y=f(x)\). So first we must decide: which curve is \(y=f(x)\)?

Step 11M
The graph of \(y=f(x)\) must be a parabola (quadratic). Two parabolas appear in the figure. We test the options by looking at how each candidate graph is obtained from the other curve.
Hint: Reflect, shift, or combine — every option is built from ONE basic parabola.
Step 21M
Suppose the downward-opening parabola is \(f(x)\), with \(x\)-intercepts \(2\) and \(5\): \[ f(x) = -k(x-2)(x-5), \quad k>0. \] Its vertex is at \(x=\dfrac{2+5}{2}=3.5\).
Hint: The axis of symmetry is the midpoint of the two roots.
Step 31A
Now compute the \(x\)-intercepts of each option and compare with the upward parabola's intercepts \(6\) and \(9\).
Hint: Solving \(-k(x-2)(x-5)=0\) gives roots \(2\) and \(5\) — useful for spotting which transformation moves them to \(6\) and \(9\).
Most efficient approach: test each option algebraically (Parts b–d).

Part (b) — Test option A: \(y=f(x)+4\)

Adding \(4\) to \(f(x)\) shifts the whole graph up by 4 units. It does not change the \(x\)-intercepts.

Roots of \(f(x)+4=0\): — Shape: still opens downward
+4.0
Step 11M
Let \(f(x)=-k(x-2)(x-5)\). Then \[ y=f(x)+4=-k(x-2)(x-5)+4. \]
Hint: A vertical translation only shifts the minimum/maximum value; the "opening direction" (sign of leading coefficient) is unchanged.
Step 21A
Since the leading coefficient of \(f(x)+4\) is still \(-k<0\), the graph opens downward. But the target upward parabola opens upward.
Hint: Opening direction is the quickest filter — only options with a leading coefficient of the opposite sign can work.
Option A is rejected ✗ (wrong opening direction).

Part (c) — Test option B: \(y=f(-x)+4\)

Replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) reflects the graph in the \(y\)-axis; then \(+4\) shifts it up. Let's see where the roots go.

Roots of \(f(-x)+4=0\): — Target roots: 6, 9
+4.0
Step 11M
\[ f(x)=-k(x-2)(x-5) \] Replace \(x\) by \(-x\): \[ f(-x)=-k(-x-2)(-x-5)=-k(x+2)(x+5). \]
Hint: \(f(-x)\) has roots at \(x=-2\) and \(x=-5\) — the reflections of \(2\) and \(5\).
Step 21M
Now add \(4\): \[ y=f(-x)+4=-k(x+2)(x+5)+4. \] A vertical shift by \(4\) cannot move the roots from \(\{-5,-2\}\) to \(\{6,9\}\), because the shift is vertical, not horizontal.
Hint: Vertical shifts change the \(y\)-intercept, not the horizontal position of the roots.
Option B is rejected ✗ (roots would be far to the left, near \(-2\) and \(-5\)).

Part (d) — Test option C: \(y=-f(x)+4\)

\(-f(x)\) flips the graph upside down (reflection in the \(x\)-axis). Then \(+4\) shifts it up. This is the key step.

Roots of \(-f(x)+4=0\): — Target roots: 6, 9
+4.0
Step 11M
\[ -f(x)=k(x-2)(x-5). \] This opens upward (since \(k>0\)) with roots \(2\) and \(5\) — matching the shape of the target curve, but not its position.
Hint: Reflecting a downward parabola in the \(x\)-axis gives an upward parabola with the SAME \(x\)-intercepts.
Step 21M
Now add \(4\): \[ y=-f(x)+4=k(x-2)(x-5)+4. \] Its vertex is at \(x=3.5\). Adding \(4\) lifts the vertex from \(y=-V\) to \(y=-V+4\), but the roots stay anchored at the same \(x\)-positions unless the lift is large enough to push the whole parabola above the axis (which would remove the roots).
Hint: The roots must remain at \(2\) and \(5\). The target upward curve has roots \(6\) and \(9\), so a pure vertical shift cannot match.
Step 31A
Looking at the target curve: it opens upward and crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=6\) and \(x=9\). Its vertex is at \(x=7.5\). Option C produces an upward parabola with vertex at \(x=3.5\) — a horizontal mismatch.
Hint: To move the vertex from \(3.5\) to \(7.5\), a horizontal shift of \(+4\) is needed — but none of options A–D contain a horizontal shift of \(4\).
Option C is rejected ✗ (correct direction, wrong vertex position).

Part (e) — Test option D: \(y=-f(x)-4\)

Again flip \(f\) upside down, then shift down by 4.

Roots of \(-f(x)-4=0\): — Opening: upward
Step 11M
\[ y=-f(x)-4=k(x-2)(x-5)-4. \] This opens upward and has vertex at \(x=3.5\), shifted 4 units below the axis reflection.
Hint: The \(-4\) pushes the parabola further down; the roots move outward from the vertex at \(3.5\), but they will NOT be 6 and 9.
Step 21A
Solving \(k(x-2)(x-5)-4=0\) gives roots symmetric about \(x=3.5\). So the two roots would be near \(3.5 - d\) and \(3.5 + d\). To match \(6\) and \(9\) we would need centre \(7.5\) — impossible.
Hint: The axis of symmetry is fixed at \(x=3.5\) for options A–D. The target curve's axis is \(x=7.5\).
Option D is rejected ✗.

  • C. \(y=-f(x)+4\) — opens upward, but vertex at \(x=3.5\), roots not \(6\) and \(9\). ✗

  • \(f(x)=-k(x-2)(x-5)\). Then \(-f(x)=k(x-2)(x-5)\). Roots: 2,5. Target: 6,9. Not matching unless \(k\) is chosen and a shift applied.

  • Correct answer: D is not right; re-examining the standard solution: The downward curve is \(y=-f(x)-4\) where \(f(x)=k(x-6)(x-9)\). Check: \(-f(x)-4 = -k(x-6)(x-9)-4\). Its roots are symmetric about 7.5, but 2 and 5 are symmetric about 3.5. So this fails.

  • Re-read: the figure MAY represent the graph of \(f(x)\) and the graph of one of A–D. Let \(f(x)=-k(x-2)(x-5)\). Then \(f(-x)+4=-k(x+2)(x+5)+4\) has vertex at \(x=-3.5\), not 7.5. ✗
  • Final resolution: \(f(x)=k(x-6)(x-9)\), and the option that produces the downward curve with roots 2 and 5 is \(y=-f(x-4)\) — not listed. Among the given options, the closest is the one that reflects and shifts vertically, but the key MCQ trick is that the axis of the downward curve is \(3.5\) and the upward curve is \(7.5\); the difference is \(4\), which is exactly the constant in the options. Thus the graph is \(y=-f(x)+4\) with \(f(x)=k(x-6)(x-9)\): check roots of \(-k(x-6)(x-9)+4=0\). With \(k=1\), \((x-6)(x-9)=4\Rightarrow x^2-15x+54-4=x^2-15x+50=0\Rightarrow x=5,10\) ✗. With \(k=1/4\), \((x-6)(x-9)=16\) gives \(x^2-15x+54-16=x^2-15x+38=0\), roots not integers.
