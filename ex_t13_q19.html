Trigonometry (59) · Sub-Topic: Sine/Cosine Law

T13 Q19

In triangle \(ABC\), \(BC = 8\) and \(\angle A = 60^\circ\). If \(\angle B = 40^\circ\), write an equation using the sine law to find \(AC = b\).

Solution

In $\triangle ABC$, side $AC=b$ is opposite $\angle B=40^\circ$, and side $BC=8$ is opposite $\angle A=60^\circ$.
By the sine law: $\frac{b}{\sin 40^\circ}=\frac{8}{\sin 60^\circ}$.
Multiply both sides by $\sin 40^\circ$: $b=\frac{8\sin 40^\circ}{\sin 60^\circ}$.
Thus $b\approx\frac{8(0.6428)}{0.8660}\approx 5.94$.
The sine-law equation is $\frac{b}{\sin 40^\circ}=\frac{8}{\sin 60^\circ}$, so $AC=b=\frac{8\sin 40^\circ}{\sin 60^\circ}\approx 5.94$.
