Solve the compound inequality

The solution of \(-3x < 6 < 2x\) is
A \( x > 0 \)
B \( x > 3 \)
C \( x > 3 \) correct
D \( -2 < x < 3 \)

Step-by-Step Solution

A compound inequality \( -3x < 6 < 2x \) can be broken into two separate inequalities:

\[ -3x < 6 \quad \text{and} \quad 6 < 2x \]

Hint: Think of the inequality as two statements joined by “and”. Both must be true simultaneously.

Divide both sides by \( -3 \):

\[ -3x < 6 \quad \xrightarrow{\div (-3)} \quad x > -2 \]

Critical rule: When you multiply or divide an inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign flips. Here, \( < \) becomes \( > \).

Divide both sides by \( 2 \):

\[ 6 < 2x \quad \xrightarrow{\div 2} \quad 3 < x \quad \text{i.e. } x > 3 \]

Hint: Dividing by a positive number (2) keeps the inequality sign unchanged.

From step 2 and step 3, we have:

\[ x > -2 \quad \text{and} \quad x > 3 \]

For both conditions to be true, \( x \) must satisfy the more restrictive condition (the intersection):

\[ \therefore x > 3 \]

Check with a number line: \( x > -2 \) covers all numbers right of -2; \( x > 3 \) covers all numbers right of 3. Their intersection is \( x > 3 \).
