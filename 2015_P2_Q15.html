Question

[2015 MC #15] In the figure, $N$ is a point lying on $AC$ and $E$ is a point lying on $DN$. If $DN = 6$ cm and $EN = 5$ cm, then the area of $\Delta ABC$ is

  • A. 24 cm²
  • B. 30 cm²
  • C. 96 cm²
  • D. 192 cm²

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Mark the right angles

The figure gives $DN \perp AC$ (right-angle mark at $N$) and $BC \perp AB$ (right-angle mark at $B$). Since $E$ lies on $DN$, the segment $EN$ is also perpendicular to $AC$.

So $\Delta CDN$ and $\Delta AEN$ are both right-angled at $N$, and $\Delta ABC$ is right-angled at $B$.

STEP 2 · Find $CN$ in $\Delta CDN$

In right-angled $\Delta CDN$ (right angle at $N$), the hypotenuse is $CD = 10$ cm and $DN = 6$ cm.

$$CN=\sqrt{CD^2-DN^2}=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=\sqrt{100-36}=\sqrt{64}=8\ \text{cm}$$
STEP 3 · Find $AN$ in $\Delta AEN$

In right-angled $\Delta AEN$ (right angle at $N$), the hypotenuse is $AE = 13$ cm and $EN = 5$ cm.

$$AN=\sqrt{AE^2-EN^2}=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}=12\ \text{cm}$$
STEP 4 · Find $AB$ in $\Delta ABC$

$A$, $N$, $C$ are collinear with $N$ between $A$ and $C$, so

$$AC=AN+NC=12+8=20\ \text{cm}$$

In right-angled $\Delta ABC$ (right angle at $B$), $AC=20$ cm and $BC=16$ cm:

$$AB=\sqrt{AC^2-BC^2}=\sqrt{20^2-16^2}=\sqrt{400-256}=\sqrt{144}=12\ \text{cm}$$

(Notice $AC=20$ equals exactly $AN+CN$ — the two right triangles share the same line $AC$.)

STEP 5 · Compute the area of $\Delta ABC$

With legs $AB=12$ cm and $BC=16$ cm:

$$\text{Area}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot AB\cdot BC=\frac{1}{2}(12)(16)=96\ \text{cm}^2$$
Answer: C (96 cm²)
Key idea: use Pythagoras twice on the small right triangles ($CN=8$ and $AN=12$), add to get $AC=20$, then Pythagoras again gives $AB=12$.
