HKDSE 2019 · Paper 2 · Q34
If $a$ is a real number, then the real part of $$\frac{4+i^{5}}{a+i}-i^{6}$$ is
  • A. $\dfrac{4a+1}{a^{2}-1}$
  • B. $\dfrac{4a+1}{a^{2}+1}$
  • C. $\dfrac{a^{2}+4a+2}{a^{2}+1}$
  • D. $\dfrac{a^{2}+4a+2}{a^{2}-1}$
1Evaluate the powers of $i$
$i^2=-1,\; i^3=-i,\; i^4=1$
$i^{5}=i^{4}\cdot i=1\cdot i=i$
$i^{6}=i^{4}\cdot i^{2}=1\cdot(-1)=-1$
Powers of $i$ repeat with period 4. Reduce the exponents modulo 4 first — this makes the expression much simpler.
2Substitute back into the expression
$\dfrac{4+i^{5}}{a+i}-i^{6}=\dfrac{4+i}{a+i}-(-1)=\dfrac{4+i}{a+i}+1$
$-i^{6} = -(-1) = +1$. Treat the $+1$ as a separate real term to be combined later.
3Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate
$\dfrac{4+i}{a+i}\cdot\dfrac{a-i}{a-i}=\dfrac{(4+i)(a-i)}{(a+i)(a-i)}$
The conjugate of $a+i$ is $a-i$. Since $a$ is real, $a^2+1>0$ so the denominator is never zero.
4Expand the numerator
$(4+i)(a-i)=4a-4i+ai-i^{2}=4a-4i+ai+1=(4a+1)+(a-4)i$
Collect real parts and imaginary parts separately: real $=4a+1$, imaginary $=a-4$.
5Simplify the denominator
$(a+i)(a-i)=a^{2}-i^{2}=a^{2}+1$
This is the standard "difference of two squares" for complex conjugates.
6Split into real and imaginary parts
$\dfrac{4+i}{a+i}=\dfrac{4a+1}{a^{2}+1}+\dfrac{a-4}{a^{2}+1}i$
The real part is the term without $i$; the coefficient of $i$ is the imaginary part.
7Add the extra real term $+1$
$\text{Real part}=\dfrac{4a+1}{a^{2}+1}+1 =\dfrac{4a+1+(a^{2}+1)}{a^{2}+1} =\dfrac{a^{2}+4a+2}{a^{2}+1}$
Write $1=\dfrac{a^{2}+1}{a^{2}+1}$ over a common denominator, then combine like terms.
Answer Real part $=\dfrac{a^{2}+4a+2}{a^{2}+1}$  →  Option C
