HKDSE Math 2018 · Paper 2 · Q33

Logarithmic Graphs & Power Functions

Relating a straight line in the \((\log_4 x,\ \log_4 y)\)-plane to the power function \(y = kx^a\).

Laws of Indices Exponential & Log Functions Linear Graph Change of Base

Q Question

In the figure, the straight line \(L\) shows the relation between \(\log_4 x\) and \(\log_4 y\). It is given that \(L\) passes through the points \((1,2)\) and \((9,6)\). If \(y = kx^a\), then \(k =\)

The straight line \(L\) in the \((\log_4 x,\ \log_4 y)\)-plane.
  • A. \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)
  • B. \(\dfrac{3}{2}\)
  • C. \(2\)
  • D. \(8\)

▶ Step-by-step Guided Solution

1 Translate the graph into an equation of a straight line

In the \(X\!-\!Y\) plane where \(X=\log_4 x\) and \(Y=\log_4 y\), the line \(L\) passes through \((1,2)\) and \((9,6)\).

Slope:

\[ m=\frac{6-2}{9-1}=\frac{4}{8}=\frac{1}{2}. \]

Using point-slope form with \((1,2)\):

\[ Y-2=\frac{1}{2}(X-1) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y=\frac{1}{2}X+\frac{3}{2}. \]
Hint: The line is written as \(\log_4 y=\frac12\log_4 x+\frac32\). This is the relation between \(x\) and \(y\).
2 Use log laws to combine the right-hand side

Apply the power law of logarithms: \(r\log_4 x=\log_4 x^{\,r}\).

\[ \log_4 y=\frac12\log_4 x+\frac32 =\log_4 x^{1/2}+\frac32. \]

We still have the constant \(\dfrac32\), so bring it inside a logarithm as well: since \(\log_4 4=1\), we get \(\dfrac32=\dfrac32\log_4 4=\log_4 4^{3/2}\).

\[ \log_4 y=\log_4 x^{1/2}+\log_4 4^{3/2}. \]
Hint: \(\log_4 4 = 1\), so any constant \(c\) can be written as \(c\log_4 4\).
3 Combine into a single logarithm and remove logs

Use the product law: \(\log_4 A+\log_4 B=\log_4(AB)\).

\[ \log_4 y=\log_4\!\left(x^{1/2}\cdot 4^{3/2}\right). \]

Since \(\log_4\) is one-to-one, we can drop the logarithms:

\[ y=4^{3/2}\,x^{1/2}. \]
Hint: If \(\log_4 A=\log_4 B\), then \(A=B\).
4 Simplify the coefficient to get \(k\)

Recall \(4^{3/2}=(\sqrt4)^3=2^3=8\).

\[ y=8\,x^{1/2}. \]

Comparing with \(y=kx^a\), we identify:

\[ k=8,\qquad a=\frac12. \]
Hint: \(4^{3/2}\) means “square-root 4, then cube”: \(4^{1/2}=2\), and \(2^3=8\).
5 Conclusion and answer
k = 8 (Option D)

So the correct choice is D.

★ Key idea to remember

  • If \(y=kx^a\), then taking \(\log_4\) of both sides gives \(\log_4 y=a\log_4 x+\log_4 k\).
  • So the graph of \(\log_4 y\) against \(\log_4 x\) is a straight line with slope \(a\) and vertical intercept \(\log_4 k\).
  • Here intercept \(=\dfrac32\), so \(\log_4 k=\dfrac32\Rightarrow k=4^{3/2}=8\).
