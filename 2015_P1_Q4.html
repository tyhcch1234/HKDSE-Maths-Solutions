HKDSE 2015 · Paper 1 · Q4 Polynomials

Factorize

(a)   $x^{3}+x^{2}y-7x^{2}$

(b)   $x^{3}+x^{2}y-7x^{2}-x-y+7$   [4 marks]

1 Part (a) — Spot the common factor
Common Factor Law \(ab+ac=a(b+c)\) All three terms contain \(x^{2}\), the lowest power of \(x\) present, so take \(x^{2}\) out.

Each of the three terms in $x^{3}+x^{2}y-7x^{2}$ contains a factor $x^{2}$:

$$x^{3}=x^{2}\cdot x,\qquad x^{2}y=x^{2}\cdot y,\qquad -7x^{2}=x^{2}\cdot(-7)$$

So take out the common factor $x^{2}$  $(1\text{A})$:

$$x^{3}+x^{2}y-7x^{2}=x^{2}(x+y-7)$$

Hint: the highest common power of $x$ is $x^{2}$ — the lowest index among the terms.
2 Part (b) — Group the terms

Line up (b) next to the answer of (a):

$$x^{3}+x^{2}y-7x^{2}-x-y+7$$

The first three terms are exactly the expression in (a),
so replace them by the factorised form  $(1\text{M})$:

$$=\underbrace{x^{2}(x+y-7)}_{\text{from (a)}}-x-y+7$$

Hint:
(b) is built from (a) — reuse the result you already have instead of starting over.
3 Create a matching factor
Factorisation by Grouping \(x^{2}(x+y-7)-(x+y-7)=(x^{2}-1)(x+y-7)\) Writing \(-x-y+7=-(x+y-7)\) creates the common bracket \((x+y-7)\).

The last three terms need a minus sign taken out so that $(x+y-7)$ appears again:

$$-x-y+7=-(x+y-7)$$

Therefore

$$x^{2}(x+y-7)-(x+y-7)$$

Now factor out the common bracket $(x+y-7)$  $(1\text{M})$:

$$=(x^{2}-1)(x+y-7)$$

Hint: $-(x+y-7)$ is the “twin” of $+(x+y-7)$ — matching brackets make a common factor.
4 Finish with difference of squares
Difference of Squares \(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)\) \(x^{2}-1=x^{2}-1^{2}=(x-1)(x+1)\), which finishes the factorisation.

$x^{2}-1$ is a difference of two squares:

$$x^{2}-1=x^{2}-1^{2}=(x-1)(x+1)$$

Substituting back gives the fully factorised form  $(1\text{A})$:

$$(x-1)(x+1)(x+y-7)$$

Hint: $a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)$. Always check whether each bracket can be factorised further.
(a)  $x^{2}(x+y-7)$
(b)  $(x-1)(x+1)(x+y-7)$ Common factor first, then group and apply $a^{2}-b^{2}=(a-b)(a+b)$.

Question. Factorise \(x^{3}+x^{2}y-4x^{2}-x-y+4\). [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Take the common factor \(x^{2}\) out of the first three terms: \(x^{2}(x+y-4)-x-y+4\).
Step 2. Factor out \(-1\) from the last three terms: \(-x-y+4=-(x+y-4)\).
Step 3. So the expression is \(x^{2}(x+y-4)-(x+y-4)=(x^{2}-1)(x+y-4)\).
Step 4. Apply the difference of squares to \(x^{2}-1\): \((x-1)(x+1)(x+y-4)\).
Answer: \((x-1)(x+1)(x+y-4)\)
