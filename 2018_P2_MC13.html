Solving "Or" Compound Inequalities

Combine solution sets with union (∪)

Question

The solution of \(x \geq 2\) or \(x < -2\) is

A. \(x \geq 2\)
B. \(x < -2\)
C. \(x > -2\)
D. \(x < -2\) or \(x \geq 2\)

Step-by-Step Solution

The word “or” means the solution set is the union (∪) of the two individual solution sets.

We need all \(x\) that satisfy \(x \geq 2\) OR \(x < -2\) (or both).

Inequality A: \(x \geq 2\) → all numbers from 2 upwards (including 2).

Inequality B: \(x < -2\) → all numbers strictly less than -2.

Hint: \(x \geq 2\) includes the endpoint 2 (closed circle). \(x < -2\) does not include -2 (open circle).

The combined solution is the set of all \(x\) that belong to either inequality.

That is: \(x < -2\) or \(x \geq 2\).

On the number line, this is the left ray ending at -2 (open circle) and the right ray starting at 2 (closed circle).

A. \(x \geq 2\) — misses \(x < -2\).

B. \(x < -2\) — misses \(x \geq 2\).

C. \(x > -2\) — wrong direction and misses part of the solution.

D. \(x < -2\) or \(x \geq 2\) — exactly what we found. ✅

Key takeaway: “or” → union (∪) of solution sets. Always draw the number line and mark open/closed circles correctly.
