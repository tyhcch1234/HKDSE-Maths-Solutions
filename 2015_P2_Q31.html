Question

Simplify $$\frac{1}{x^{2}-2x+1}-\frac{1}{x^{2}+x-2}=$$

A. $\dfrac{1}{(x-1)(x+2)}$
B. $\dfrac{1}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$
C. $\dfrac{3}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$
D. $\dfrac{2x+1}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 · Factorise each denominator

Factorise the two quadratic denominators completely.

$$x^{2}-2x+1=(x-1)^{2}$$

$$x^{2}+x-2=(x+2)(x-1)$$

Perfect square for the first; cross-method for the second: $2+(-1)=1$ and $2\times(-1)=-2$.
Cross Method \(x^{2}+(p+q)x+pq=(x+p)(x+q)\) For \(x^{2}+x-2\), \(2+(-1)=1\) and \(2\times(-1)=-2\), so it is \((x+2)(x-1)\).
Step 2 · Rewrite with factorised denominators

$$\frac{1}{x^{2}-2x+1}-\frac{1}{x^{2}+x-2}=\frac{1}{(x-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x+2)(x-1)}$$

Now the structure of each fraction is visible — this tells us the LCM of the denominators.
Step 3 · Find the common denominator (LCM)

The LCM of $(x-1)^{2}$ and $(x+2)(x-1)$ is

$$(x-1)^{2}(x+2)$$

Take the highest power of each distinct factor: $(x-1)^{2}$ and $(x+2)$.
LCM of Algebraic Expressions \(\text{LCM}\big((x-1)^{2},\ (x+2)(x-1)\big)=(x-1)^{2}(x+2)\) Take the highest power of each distinct factor: \((x-1)^{2}\) and \((x+2)\).
Step 4 · Convert both fractions

Multiply the first by $\dfrac{x+2}{x+2}$ and the second by $\dfrac{x-1}{x-1}$:

$$=\frac{(x+2)}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}-\frac{(x-1)}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$$

Each fraction is multiplied by a "clever one" so the denominators match.
Step 5 · Combine the numerators

$$=\frac{(x+2)-(x-1)}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$$

Write as a single fraction; keep the brackets — the minus sign acts on the whole numerator.
Subtracting Algebraic Fractions \(\dfrac{a}{c}-\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{a-b}{c}\) Once the denominators match, the numerators \((x+2)-(x-1)\) are combined over the common denominator.
Step 6 · Expand and simplify the numerator

$$(x+2)-(x-1)=x+2-x+1=3$$

$$\therefore\ \frac{3}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$$

The $x$-terms cancel; only the constant remains. The denominator is already fully factorised.
Answer: C  —  $\dfrac{3}{(x-1)^{2}(x+2)}$

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{1}{x^{2}-4x+4}-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}-4}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Factorise the denominators: \(x^{2}-4x+4=(x-2)^{2}\) and \(x^{2}-4=(x-2)(x+2)\).
Step 2. The LCM of the denominators is \((x-2)^{2}(x+2)\).
Step 3. \(\dfrac{1}{(x-2)^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\dfrac{(x+2)-(x-2)}{(x-2)^{2}(x+2)}\).
Step 4. \((x+2)-(x-2)=4\), so the expression is \(\dfrac{4}{(x-2)^{2}(x+2)}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{4}{(x-2)^{2}(x+2)}\)
