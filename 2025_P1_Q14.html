Mensuration of a cone & frustum — volume, surface area and a cube comparison
Cone Volume Frustum Similar Solids Total Surface Area Estimation

QThe Question

The height and the base radius of a solid metal right circular cone are \(45\text{ cm}\) and \(24\text{ cm}\) respectively. The circular cone is divided into a circular cone and a frustum \(X\) by a plane parallel to its base. It is given that the height of \(X\) is \(30\text{ cm}\).
  1. Express the volume of \(X\) in terms of \(\pi\). (3 marks)
  2. Express the total surface area of \(X\) in terms of \(\pi\). (3 marks)
  3. \(X\) is melted and recast into a solid cube. Does the total surface area of the cube exceed the total surface area of \(X\)? Explain your answer. (3 marks)

aPart (a) — Volume of the frustum \(X\)

Slide the plane to move the cut parallel to the base, and watch how the frustum’s volume changes. The question uses the cut at height \(30\text{ cm}\) (small cone height \(15\text{ cm}\)).

Step 11MRecognise the two cones are similar

The whole cone has height \(45\text{ cm}\) and base radius \(24\text{ cm}\). The small cone above the cut has height \(45-30=15\text{ cm}\). For similar solids, radii are proportional to heights:

\[ \frac{r_{\text{small}}}{24}=\frac{15}{45}=\frac13 \quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{\text{small}}=8\text{ cm}. \]
Hint: The height ratio \(15:45=1:3\) is the linear scale factor. Every length shrinks by the same factor \(k=\tfrac13\).
Step 21MUse \(V_{\text{frustum}}=V_{\text{big}}-V_{\text{small}}\)

Volumes scale by the cube of the linear factor, so \[ \frac{V_{\text{small}}}{V_{\text{big}}}=k^{3}=\left(\frac13\right)^{3}=\frac1{27}. \]

Hint: Rather than computing two separate volumes, subtract in one expression: \(V_X=\tfrac13\pi(24)^2(45)\left[1-\left(\tfrac{15}{45}\right)^{3}\right].\)
Step 31AEvaluate
\[ V_X=\frac13\pi(24)^2(45)\left[1-\left(\frac13\right)^{3}\right] =8640\pi\left(1-\frac1{27}\right) =8640\pi\cdot\frac{26}{27}=8320\pi. \]
Volume of \(X\) \(= 8320\pi\ \text{cm}^3\)

bPart (b) — Total surface area of \(X\)

A frustum has three surfaces: the big base, the small top circle, and the curved (lateral) surface. Use the slider to see each contribution at the given cut height \(15\text{ cm}\).

Step 11MSlant height of the whole cone

For a cone of height \(45\) and radius \(24\),

\[ l=\sqrt{24^{2}+45^{2}}=\sqrt{576+2025}=\sqrt{2601}=51\text{ cm}. \]
Hint: The slant height is the hypotenuse of the right triangle with legs \(r=24\) and \(h=45\). Note \(24,45,51\) is \(3\times(8,15,17)\).
Step 21MLateral area of the frustum

The slant height of the small cone is \(51\times\tfrac13=17\text{ cm}\), so the slant height of the frustum is \(51-17=34\text{ cm}\).

\[ A_{\text{lat}}=\pi(r_1+r_2)\,l_{\text{fr}}=\pi\big(24+8\big)(34)=1088\pi. \]
Hint: Formula for a frustum: curved area \(=\pi(r_1+r_2)\times(\text{slant height of the frustum})\). Check \(r_1+r_2=32\) and \(l=34\).
Step 31MAdd the two circular faces
\[ A_{\text{base}}=\pi(24)^2=576\pi,\qquad A_{\text{top}}=\pi(8)^2=64\pi. \] \[ \text{Total}=1088\pi+576\pi+64\pi=1728\pi. \]
Hint: Do not forget the flat top circle of radius \(8\text{ cm}\) — it is part of the frustum’s surface.
Total surface area of \(X\) \(=1728\pi\ \text{cm}^2\)

cPart (c) — Recasting into a cube

The volume is unchanged by melting, but the surface area changes! Drag the slider to see how the cube’s surface area compares with \(1728\pi\text{ cm}^2\).

Step 11MEquate volume of cube to volume of \(X\)

Let \(x\text{ cm}\) be the side of the cube. Melting preserves volume:

\[ x^{3}=8320\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\sqrt[3]{8320\pi}\approx 29.67730087. \]
Hint: Keep full calculator accuracy — rounding \(x\) too early will change the final comparison near the boundary.
Step 21MSurface area of the cube
\[ A_{\text{cube}}=6x^{2}\approx 6(29.67730087)^{2}\approx 5284.453123\ \text{cm}^2. \]
Hint: A cube has 6 identical square faces, so \(A=6x^2\). One face is about \(880.7\text{ cm}^2\).
Step 31ACompare and conclude

The frustum’s surface area in numerals is

\[ 1728\pi\approx 5428.67\ \text{cm}^2. \] \[ A_{\text{cube}}\approx 5284.45 < 5428.67 = 1728\pi. \]
No. The total surface area of the cube (\( \approx 5284.45\text{ cm}^2\)) does not exceed the total surface area of \(X\) (\(=1728\pi\approx 5428.67\text{ cm}^2\)).
Hint: A sphere minimises surface area for a given volume. The cube is “closer” to a sphere than the flat frustum, so its surface area is smaller.

ΣFinal Answers at a Glance

  • Part (a): Volume of \(X\) \(=\; \dfrac13\pi(24)^2(45)\left[1-\left(\dfrac{15}{45}\right)^{3}\right]=\;\) \(8320\pi\ \text{cm}^3\)
  • Small cone radius \(=\dfrac{15}{45}\times 24=8\text{ cm}\); slant height \(=\sqrt{24^2+45^2}=51\text{ cm}\).
  • Part (b): Total surface area of \(X\) \(=1088\pi+576\pi+64\pi=\;\) \(1728\pi\ \text{cm}^2\)
  • Part (c): Cube side \(x=\sqrt[3]{8320\pi}\approx 29.6773\text{ cm}\), so cube surface area \(\approx 5284.45\text{ cm}^2\).
  • No — \(5284.45 < 1728\pi\ (\approx 5428.67)\), so the cube’s total surface area does not exceed that of \(X\).
