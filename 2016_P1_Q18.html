Step 0 — the given parabola $y=f(x)$ with vertex $V(18,-13)$.

\(y=f(x)\) \(y=g(x)\) reflection of \(f\) in the \(y\)-axis vertex / key point

HKDSE 2016 · Paper 1 · Q18

Topic 7 · Functions and Graphs  |  Marks: 2 + 2 + 2

Question

Let $f(x)=\dfrac{-1}{3}x^{2}+12x-121$.

(a) Using the method of completing the square, find the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of $y=f(x)$. [2 marks]

(b) The graph of $y=g(x)$ is obtained by translating the graph of $y=f(x)$ vertically. If the graph of $y=g(x)$ touches the $x$-axis, find $g(x)$. [2 marks]

(c) Under a transformation, $f(x)$ is changed to $\dfrac{-1}{3}x^{2}-12x-121$. Describe the geometric meaning of the transformation. [2 marks]

1
(a) Factor out the coefficient of $x^2$

Start from $f(x)=\dfrac{-1}{3}x^{2}+12x-121$ and take $-\dfrac13$ out of the first two terms:

$f(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}\left(x^{2}-36x\right)-121$
Hint: dividing $12x$ by $-\tfrac13$ gives $-36x$; keep the constant $-121$ outside for now.
2
(a) Complete the square in the bracket

Half of $-36$ is $-18$, and $(-18)^2=324$. Add and subtract $324$ inside the bracket:

$f(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}\left(x^{2}-36x+18^{2}-18^{2}\right)-121$
$=-\dfrac{1}{3}\left(x^{2}-36x+18^{2}\right)+\dfrac{324}{3}-121$
$=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}+108-121=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13$
Hint (1M): the mark is for the correct completing-the-square step; always expand back to check.
3
(a) Read off the vertex  1A

The vertex form $y=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13$ shows the vertex (turning point) is at

$\boxed{(18,\,-13)}$
Hint: for $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ the vertex is $(h,k)$. Here $a=-\tfrac13<0$, so the parabola opens downwards and $(18,-13)$ is a maximum point.
4
(b) Set up the vertical translation  1M

A vertical translation by $k$ units gives $g(x)=f(x)+k$. From (a),

$f(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13 \;\Longrightarrow\; g(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13+k$
Hint: the vertex moves vertically to $(18,\,-13+k)$; the shape and the axis $x=18$ are unchanged.
5
(b) Apply the tangency condition

“Touches the $x$-axis” means the curve is tangent to $y=0$, so the vertex must lie on the $x$-axis:

$-13+k=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; k=13$
$g(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13+13=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}$
Hint: tangency to the $x$-axis $\Leftrightarrow$ discriminant $=0$ $\Leftrightarrow$ vertex $y$-coordinate $=0$.
6
(b) Answer  1A
$\boxed{g(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}}$

The graph of $g$ is obtained by shifting the graph of $f$ upwards by 13 units; its only $x$-intercept is the vertex $(18,0)$.

7
(c) Compare $f(-x)$ with the new expression  1A

Replace $x$ by $-x$ in $f(x)$:

$f(-x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(-x)^{2}+12(-x)-121=-\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2}-12x-121$

This is exactly the given new expression (only the sign of the $x$-term changed).

8
(c) Describe the geometric meaning  1A

Since $-\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2}-12x-121=f(-x)$, the transformation is

$\boxed{\text{a reflection with respect to the } y\text{-axis}}$
Hint: every point $(x,y)$ on $y=f(x)$ maps to $(-x,y)$. The new vertex is $(-18,-13)$ — the mirror image of $(18,-13)$ about the $y$-axis.
Answer:
(a) Vertex $=(18,\,-13)$  (maximum point); vertex form $f(x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}-13$.
(b) $k=13$, so $g(x)=f(x)+13=-\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}$ (translate upwards by 13 units).
(c) The transformation is the reflection with respect to the $y$-axis, because $f(-x)=-\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2}-12x-121$.
