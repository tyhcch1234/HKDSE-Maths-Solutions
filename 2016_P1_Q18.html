Quadratic Functions — Completing the Square, Vertical Translation & Reflection
Completing the Square Vertex of a Parabola Vertical Translation Tangent to x-axis Reflection in y-axis

Question

Let \( f(x) = -\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2} + 12x - 121 \).

(a) Using the method of completing the square, find the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of \( y = f(x) \). 2 marks

(b) The graph of \( y = g(x) \) is obtained by translating the graph of \( y = f(x) \) vertically. If the graph of \( y = g(x) \) touches the \( x \)-axis, find \( g(x) \). 2 marks

(c) Under a transformation, \( f(x) \) is changed to \( -\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2} - 12x - 121 \). Describe the geometric meaning of the transformation. 2 marks

Part (a) — Vertex by Completing the Square 2 marks

Complete the square to write \( f(x) \) in the form \( a(x-h)^2 + k \), so the vertex is \( (h,\,k) \).

Standard form: \( a=-\tfrac13,\ b=12 \) Vertex: (18, −13) Axis: x = 18 \( y \)-intercept: (0, −121)
11M

Factor out the leading coefficient from the \( x^2 \) and \( x \) terms:

\[ f(x) = -\frac{1}{3}\left(x^{2} - 36x\right) - 121 \]
Hint: \(-12x\) divided by \(-\frac13\) gives \(+\ 36x\). Always divide, never subtract.
21M

Add and subtract \(\left(\dfrac{36}{2}\right)^2 = 18^2\) inside the bracket:

\[ f(x) = -\frac{1}{3}\left(x^{2} - 36x + 18^{2} - 18^{2}\right) - 121 \]
Hint: Half of \(36\) is \(18\); square it to get \(324\). The \(+\,18^2\) and \(-\,18^2\) cancel, so the expression is unchanged.
31A

The bracket is a perfect square, and the leftover constant simplifies:

\[ f(x) = -\frac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2} + \frac{324}{3} - 121 = -\frac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2} - 13 \]
Hint: \(-\frac13 \times (-324) = +108\), and \(108-121 = -13\).
Answer: The vertex of the graph is \(\;(18,\;-13)\).
0 / 3

Part (b) — Vertical Translation Touching the \(x\)-axis 2 marks

A vertical translation adds a constant \(k\) to the whole function: \( g(x) = f(x) + k \). The graph touches the \(x\)-axis when its vertex lies exactly on the axis.

g(x) = f(x) + k Vertex y: −13 Discriminant Δ: 169 Status: 2 distinct roots
11M

Since the translation is vertical, \( g(x) = f(x) + k = -\frac13(x-18)^2 - 13 + k \).

For the graph to touch the \(x\)-axis, the vertex must satisfy \( y = 0 \):

\[ -13 + k = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = 13 \]
Hint: "Touches the \(x\)-axis" ⇔ the quadratic equation \(g(x)=0\) has exactly one repeated root ⇔ discriminant \(\Delta = 0\).
21A

Substitute \(k = 13\) back:

\[ g(x) = f(x) + 13 = -\frac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2} - 13 + 13 = -\frac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2} \]
Hint: Check with \(k=13\): the vertex moves from \((18,-13)\) to \((18,0)\), which sits exactly on the \(x\)-axis, giving a double root at \(x=18\).
Answer: \(\;g(x) = -\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}\;\)
0 / 2

Part (c) — Geometric Meaning of the Transformation 2 marks

Compare \(f(x)\) with the new expression \(-\frac13 x^2 - 12x - 121\).

f(19) = −16.33 f(−19) = −364.33 Vertex f: (18, −13) Vertex of f(−x): (−18, −13)
11A

Replace \(x\) by \(-x\) in \(f\):

\[ f(-x) = -\frac{1}{3}(-x)^{2} + 12(-x) - 121 = -\frac{1}{3}x^{2} - 12x - 121 \]
Hint: \((-x)^2 = x^2\) so the \(x^2\) term is unchanged, while the \(x\) term changes sign. This is the signature of a horizontal flip.
21A

The new expression equals \(f(-x)\). Every point \((x, y)\) on \(y=f(x)\) maps to \((-x, y)\) on the new curve.

Hint: The \(y\)-coordinate is preserved, but the \(x\)-coordinate is negated — the graph is folded over the \(y\)-axis. The vertex \((18,-13)\) becomes \((-18,-13)\).
Answer: The transformation is a reflection with respect to the \(y\)-axis.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • (a) \( f(x) = -\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2} - 13 \), so the vertex is \(\;(18,\,-13)\).
  • (b) Vertical shift \(k = 13\), hence \(\;g(x) = -\dfrac{1}{3}(x-18)^{2}\).
  • (c) \(-\dfrac{1}{3}x^{2} - 12x - 121 = f(-x)\), so the transformation is a reflection with respect to the \(y\)-axis (the \(y\)-coordinates are unchanged, the \(x\)-coordinates change sign).
