Question

If $$\begin{cases}4\alpha+\beta=5\quad\ldots(1)\\[4pt] 7\alpha+3\beta=5\quad\ldots(2)\end{cases}$$ then $\beta=$
A. \(-3\)    B. \(-2\)    C. \(2\)    D. \(3\)

Step-by-step solution

1Write down the two equations
$$4\alpha+\beta=5 \quad\ldots(1)$$ $$7\alpha+3\beta=5 \quad\ldots(2)$$
We must eliminate one unknown. It is simplest to eliminate $\alpha$, since we only need $\beta$.
2Make the $\alpha$-coefficients match
Method of Elimination \(\text{multiply each equation so that one coefficient matches}\) Multiplying \\((2)\\) by \\(4\\) and \\((1)\\) by \\(7\\) makes both \\(\\alpha\\)-coefficients equal to \\(28\\), so \\(\\alpha\\) can be removed.
Multiply $(2)$ by \(4\) and \((1)\) by \(7\) so that both $\alpha$-coefficients become \(28\): $$(2)\times 4:\quad 28\alpha+12\beta=20 \quad\ldots(3)$$ $$(1)\times 7:\quad 28\alpha+\phantom{1}7\beta=35 \quad\ldots(4)$$
$4$ divides $(2)$ exactly; $7$ divides $(1)$ exactly — cross-multiplying by the opposite $\alpha$-coefficients is a standard trick.
3Subtract to eliminate $\alpha$
$(3)-(4):$ $$(28\alpha-28\alpha)+(12\beta-7\beta)=20-35$$ $$5\beta=-15$$
$\alpha$ cancels completely — that is the whole point of Step 2. Take care with the signs when subtracting the whole of $(4)$.
4Solve for $\beta$
Solving a Linear Equation in One Unknown \(a\beta=b\ \Longrightarrow\ \beta=\dfrac{b}{a}\qquad(a\neq0)\) From \\(5\\beta=-15\\) dividing by the coefficient \\(5\\) gives \\(\\beta=-3\\).
$$5\beta=-15 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \beta=\frac{-15}{5}=\mathbf{-3}$$
Divide both sides by the coefficient of $\beta$, which is $5$.
5Check (optional but recommended)
Substitute $\beta=-3$ into $(1)$: $$4\alpha-3=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 4\alpha=8 \;\Rightarrow\; \alpha=2.$$ Now test in $(2)$: $\;7(2)+3(-3)=14-9=5$ ✓
A quick check with both original equations guards against sign slips.
Answer: $\beta=-3$ Matching the options given: A.
Note: since $\alpha=2$, the correct option is the one equal to $-3$ (option A in the original paper).
