QQuestion

\[ \bigl(\cos(90^\circ+\theta)+1\bigr)\bigl(\sin(360^\circ-\theta)-1\bigr) \;=\; \]
  • A.   \(-\cos^2\theta\)
  • B.   \(-\sin^2\theta\)
  • C.   \(\cos^2\theta\)  ✓
  • D.   \(\sin^2\theta\)
Key ideas: reduce each compound angle to a simple single-angle expression, then use \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).

1Step 1 — Reduce \(\cos(90^\circ+\theta)\) and \(\sin(360^\circ-\theta)\)

Use the compound-angle identities (or the CAST / ASTC quadrant rules) to simplify each bracket. Drag the slider below to watch the angles rotate on the unit circle.

cos(90°+θ) = –0.643
–sin θ = –0.643
sin(360°−θ) = –0.643
–sin θ = –0.643
0 / 2
Step 1
Reduce each compound angle: \[ \cos(90^\circ+\theta) = -\sin\theta, \qquad \sin(360^\circ-\theta) = -\sin\theta \]
In Quadrant II, \(\cos(90^\circ+\theta)\) is the “co-function” of \(\sin\theta\) and is negative — hence \(-\sin\theta\). In Quadrant IV, \(\sin(360^\circ-\theta)\) equals \(-\sin\theta\).
Step 2
Substitute into the original product: \[ \bigl(-\sin\theta+1\bigr)\bigl(-\sin\theta-1\bigr) = -\bigl(1-\sin\theta\bigr)\bigl(1+\sin\theta\bigr) = -\bigl(1-\sin^2\theta\bigr) \] \[ = -1+\sin^2\theta = -\cos^2\theta \quad \text{(since } \cos^2\theta = 1-\sin^2\theta\text{)} \]
Answer: \(-\cos^2\theta\) → Option A

2Step 2 — Verify with the Pythagorean Identity

The expression also equals \(-\bigl(1-\sin^2\theta\bigr)\). Explore how \(\sin\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\) trade off against each other as \(\theta\) changes, and watch the expression’s value.

sin²θ = 0.413
cos²θ = 0.587
sin²θ+cos²θ = 1.000
expr = −cos²θ = –0.587
0 / 3
Step 1
From Part (a) the expression simplifies to: \[ E(\theta) = -\bigl(1-\sin^2\theta\bigr) \]
Keep the bracket \(1-\sin^2\theta\) intact for a moment — it is the key to the Pythagorean identity.
Step 2
Apply the fundamental Pythagorean identity: \[ \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1 - \sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta \]
The slider above shows the red dot moving along \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\) — the two squares always sum to exactly \(1\).
Step 3
Therefore: \[ E(\theta) = -\cos^2\theta \]
Final answer: \(-\cos^2\theta\) — Option A

✓Quick Numerical Check

Substitute \(\theta = 40^\circ\) into the original expression and into each option. Only Option A agrees.

★Summary of Answers

  • \(\cos(90^\circ+\theta) = -\sin\theta\) and \(\sin(360^\circ-\theta) = -\sin\theta\).
  • The product becomes \((-\sin\theta+1)(-\sin\theta-1) = -\bigl(1-\sin^2\theta\bigr)\).
  • Using \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\):  \(1-\sin^2\theta = \cos^2\theta\).
  • Hence the expression equals \(-\cos^2\theta\).
  • Correct option: A. \(-\cos^2\theta\)
