Sum of the First n Terms of a Sequence

The sum of the first n terms of a sequence is $\;n(2n+3)\;$. Which of the following are true?

  1. 14 is a term of the sequence.
  2. The nth term of the sequence is $\;4n+1$.
  3. The sequence is an arithmetic sequence.
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Sequences Sum $S(n)$ General Term $T(n)$ Arithmetic Sequence Common Difference $d$

★Core Idea: From Sum to Term

We are given the sum of the first $n$ terms, $S(n)=n(2n+3)$. To find the $n$th term $T(n)$, remember that the sum up to $n$ terms is the sum up to $(n-1)$ terms plus the $n$th term:

$$S(n)=S(n-1)+T(n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$$
💡 Hint / Why this matters This "subtract the previous total" trick works for any sequence when the sum formula is given. It is the key to finding the general term $T(n)$.

1Step-by-Step Guided Solution

1Recall the relationship $T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$ ▼

Let $T(n)$ be the $n$th term of the sequence, and $S(n)$ be the sum of the first $n$ terms.

$$S(n)=T(1)+T(2)+\cdots+T(n-1)+T(n)$$ $$S(n-1)=T(1)+T(2)+\cdots+T(n-1)$$ $$\therefore\; T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$$
🔑 Key identity $T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$ — this is how we turn a "sum formula" into a "term formula".
2Substitute $S(n)=n(2n+3)$ and $S(n-1)$ ▼

Replace $n$ by $(n-1)$ in the given formula to get $S(n-1)$:

$$S(n-1)=(n-1)\big[\,2(n-1)+3\,\big]$$ $$=(n-1)(2n-2+3)=(n-1)(2n+1)$$
💡 Hint Careful with the bracket: $2(n-1)+3 = 2n-2+3 = 2n+1$. A common mistake is forgetting to add $3$ after distributing the $2$.
3Compute $T(n)=S(n)-S(n-1)$ ▼
$$T(n)=n(2n+3)-(n-1)(2n+1)$$ $$=2n^{2}+3n-\big(2n^{2}-2n+2n-1\big)$$ $$=2n^{2}+3n-\big(2n^{2}-1\big)$$ $$=2n^{2}+3n-2n^{2}+1$$ $$=4n+1$$
✅ Conclusion for II $T(n)=4n+1$, so statement II is TRUE. This also matches the answer key.
4Check the common difference: $T(n)-T(n-1)$ ▼

A sequence is arithmetic if the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

$$T(n)-T(n-1)=\big(4n+1\big)-\big[\,4(n-1)+1\,\big]$$ $$=(4n+1)-(4n-4+1)$$ $$=(4n+1)-(4n-3)$$ $$=4 \quad\text{(a constant)}$$
✅ Conclusion for III Since the difference is a constant $4$, the sequence is an arithmetic sequence, so statement III is TRUE.
5Test whether $14$ is a term of the sequence ▼

Set $T(n)=14$ and solve for $n$:

$$4n+1=14$$ $$4n=13$$ $$n=\frac{13}{4}=3.25$$

Since $n$ must be a positive integer, $n=3.25$ is not valid.

❌ Conclusion for I $14$ is NOT a term of the sequence, so statement I is FALSE.

Only II and III are true ⟹ the answer is Option C.
