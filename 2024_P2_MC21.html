Rhombus, rectangle & congruent triangles — which statements must be true?

Q21 The Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a rhombus. Denote the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(BD\) by \(E\). Let \(F\) be a point such that \(AEFH\) and \(BCFG\) is a rectangle, where \(F\) and \(H\) are points lying on \(DC\) produced and \(AB\) produced respectively. Denote the point of intersection of \(AC\) and \(FH\) by \(I\). Which of the following must be true?

I. \(\; CI = FI\)
II. \(\; \angle ABE = \angle GCH\)
III. \(\; \triangle ADE \cong \triangle HCF\)

A. I and II only     B. I and III only     C. II and III only     D. I, II and III

1 Setup Label the key regions

In the rhombus \(ABCD\):

  • All four sides are equal, so \(AB = BC = CD = DA\).
  • Diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) bisect each other perpendicularly, so \(AC \perp BD\) and \(AE = EC\), \(BE = ED\).
  • Point \(F\) lies on \(DC\) produced, and \(BCFG\) is a rectangle.
  • Point \(H\) lies on \(AB\) produced, and \(AEFH\) is a rectangle.
  • \(I = AC \cap FH\) on the line \(AC\).
Hint: Use the slider to change the angles. Notice how \(AEFH\) growing a right angle at \(E\) forces \(FH \perp AC\), and \(BCFG\) forces \(FC \perp BC\).
Key fact: Since \(BCFG\) is a rectangle, \(\angle BCF = 90^\circ\). Since \(AEFH\) is a rectangle, \(\angle EFH = 90^\circ\).

Part B(i) Is \(CI = FI\)? (Assertion I)

1 Set up the two angles \(x\) and \(y\)

Since \(BCFG\) is a rectangle, the corner angle at \(C\) is

\[\angle FCG = 90^\circ.\]

Split this by the line \(CH\):

\[\text{Let } \angle FCH = x \quad \text{and} \quad \angle GCH = y, \quad \text{so } x + y = 90^\circ.\]
Hint: \(CH\) here is the diagonal of the rectangle \(BCFG\) — the whole angle \(FCG\) is a right angle, so the two parts add to \(90^\circ\).
2 Find \(\angle IFC\) using alternate angles

Because \(AEFH\) is a rectangle, \(EH /\!/ AF\) — and in fact \(BH /\!/ EF\). The transversal \(FH\) gives

\[\angle IFC = \angle FCH = x \quad (\text{alt. } \angle\text{s},\, BH /\!/ EF).\]
Hint: Look at the parallel lines \(EF /\!/ BH\). The line \(FH\) cuts them; the angles \(\angle IFC\) and \(\angle FCH\) are alternate interior angles.
So \(\angle IFC = x\).
3 Express \(\angle FCI\) in terms of \(y\)

Vertically opposite angles at \(C\):

\[\angle BCE = \angle GCH = y \quad (\text{vert. opp. } \angle\text{s}).\]

Because \(AC\) is the diagonal of rhombus \(ABCD\), it bisects \(\angle BCD\):

\[\angle DCE = \angle BCE = y \quad (\text{properties of rhombus}).\]
Hint: Watch the canvas: as you drag the slider, ∠BCE and ∠DCE stay equal — that's the rhombus diagonal bisecting the vertex angle.
So \(\angle FCI = \angle FCD = y\). And since \(\angle FCD + \angle DCE = 90^\circ\) (corner of rectangle \(BCFG\) measured through \(C\)), we get \(x + y = 90^\circ\) again.
4 Conclude \(CI = FI\)

We have

\[\angle FCD = 90^\circ - y = x = \angle IFC.\]

So in triangle \(CIF\), the base angles at \(F\) and \(C\) are equal:

\[\angle IFC = \angle FCI = x.\]

Therefore

\[CI = FI \quad (\text{sides opp. equal } \angle\text{s}).\]
Hint: Equal base angles ⇒ isosceles triangle ⇒ equal opposite sides. This is exactly why the slider keeps CI and FI the same length.
I is true. \(\boxed{CI = FI}\)

Part B(ii) Is \(\angle ABE = \angle GCH\)? (Assertion II)

This is the trap. Use the slider to compare \(\angle ABE\) and \(\angle GCH\) at different rhombus shapes. Notice that one is \(x\) and the other is \(y\) — they are only equal when \(x = y = 45^\circ\).

1 Use the right angle at \(E\)

Diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular:

\[AC \perp DB \quad (\text{properties of rhombus}), \quad\text{so}\quad \angle CEB = 90^\circ.\]
Hint: In the canvas, the diagonals \(AC\) and \(BD\) always cross at a clean \(90^\circ\) no matter how you drag the slider — that is the special rhombus property.
2 Find \(\angle CBE\) via angle sum of \(\triangle CEB\)

In \(\triangle CEB\):

\[\angle CBE + \angle CEB + \angle BCE = 180^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle)\] \[\angle CBE + 90^\circ + y = 180^\circ\] \[\angle CBE = 90^\circ - y = x.\]

Since \(AC\) bisects \(\angle DAB\) in a rhombus, \(\angle ABE = \angle CBE = x\).

Hint: The diagonal \(BD\) bisects \(\angle ABC\). So \(\angle ABE = \angle CBE = x\).
\(\angle ABE = x = 90^\circ - y\).
3 Compare with \(\angle GCH\)

But from the setup,

\[\angle GCH = y.\]

In general \(x \ne y\) (they are equal only when \(x = y = 45^\circ\), i.e. \(\angle DAB = 90^\circ\)).

Hint: Drag the slider to 90° to confirm the unique case where the two angles coincide. Everywhere else, \(\angle ABE \ne \angle GCH\).
II may NOT be true. \(\boxed{\angle ABE \ne \angle GCH \text{ in general}}\)

Part B(iii) Is \(\triangle ADE \cong \triangle HCF\)? (Assertion III)

1 Four congruent triangles in the rhombus

Because \(ABCD\) is a rhombus with diagonals bisecting each other perpendicularly, the four triangles formed by the diagonals are congruent:

\[\triangle ADE \cong \triangle ABE \cong \triangle CDE \cong \triangle CBE\]

Each is a right triangle with legs \(AE = CE\) and \(BE = ED\) and hypotenuse the rhombus side.

Hint: \(AE = CE\) and \(BE = ED\) (diagonals bisect each other), together with \(AB = BC = CD = DA\). ASA or SSS gives the four congruences.
\(\triangle ADE \cong \triangle ABE \cong \triangle CDE \cong \triangle CBE\)
2 \(\triangle CBE \cong \triangle EFC\) (ASA)

Look at \(\triangle CBE\) and \(\triangle EFC\):

  • \(\angle CEB = 90^\circ = \angle EFC\) (rectangle \(AEFH\) has a right angle at \(F\)).
  • \(CE = EF\) (rectangle \(AEFH\) gives equal opposite sides... use \(AEFH\) is a rectangle, so \(EF /\!/ AH\) and the corner \(E\) is right).
  • \(\angle BCE = y = \angle ECF\).

By ASA (or RHS), \(\triangle CBE \cong \triangle EFC\).

Hint: Both triangles have a right angle, a shared piece of the diagonal, and an angle \(y\) at \(C\). This is the standard ASA / RHS congruence.
\(\triangle CBE \cong \triangle EFC \quad (\text{ASA})\)
3 \(\triangle EFC \cong \triangle HCF\) (ASA)

Now in \(\triangle EFC\) and \(\triangle HCF\):

  • \(\angle EFC = 90^\circ = \angle HCF\) (rectangle \(BCFG\) has right angle at \(C\); also \(EH \perp AC\)).
  • \(FC = CF\) (common side).
  • \(\angle ECF = y = \angle HFC\) (alternate angles, \(EF /\!/ BH\)).

By ASA, \(\triangle EFC \cong \triangle HCF\).

Hint: The right angle at \(C\) from rectangle \(BCFG\) matches the right angle at \(F\) from the other rectangle. The shared side \(CF\) and the alternate angle pair complete ASA.
\(\triangle EFC \cong \triangle HCF \quad (\text{ASA})\)
4 Chain the congruences together

Combining Step 1, Step 2 and Step 3:

\[\triangle ADE \cong \triangle CBE \cong \triangle EFC \cong \triangle HCF\]

Therefore:

\[\triangle ADE \cong \triangle HCF.\]
Hint: Congruence is transitive. If A ≅ B and B ≅ C, then A ≅ C. The canvas shows you the equal areas in real time.
III is true. \(\boxed{\triangle ADE \cong \triangle HCF}\)

📘 Summary & Final Answer

Let \(\angle FCH = x\) and \(\angle GCH = y\), so \(x + y = 90^\circ\).

  • I. \(\angle IFC = x\) and \(\angle FCI = x\), so \(\triangle CIF\) is isosceles, hence \(CI = FI\). TRUE.
  • II. \(\angle ABE = x\) but \(\angle GCH = y\), and \(x \ne y\) in general. NOT always true.
  • III. \(\triangle ADE \cong \triangle ABE \cong \triangle CDE \cong \triangle CBE\), \(\triangle CBE \cong \triangle EFC\), \(\triangle EFC \cong \triangle HCF\), so \(\triangle ADE \cong \triangle HCF\). TRUE.
Answer: B. I and III only
