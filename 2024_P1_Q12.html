12. Coordinate Geometry — squares, slopes and straight lines

Coordinate Geometry Slope of a Line Square Geometry Intersection of Lines

QQuestion

Denote the origin by \(O\).

(a) \(A\) and \(B\) are points lying on the positive \(x\)-axis such that the \(x\)-coordinate of \(A\) is greater than the \(x\)-coordinate of \(B\). A vertical line which passes through \(B\) cuts the straight line \(y=mx\) at the point \(C\) such that \(AB=BC\), where \(m\) is a positive constant. Let \(D\) be a point such that \(ABCD\) is a square. Express the slope of \(OD\) in terms of \(m\). (3 marks)

(b) The coordinates of the points \(M\) and \(N\) are \((6,5)\) and \((10,0)\) respectively. Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be points lying on \(OM\) and \(MN\) respectively while \(R\) and \(S\) be points lying on the \(x\)-axis. If the quadrilateral \(PQRS\) is a square, find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\). (4 marks)

aPart (a) — Express the slope of \(OD\) in terms of \(m\)

Step 11M

Let the coordinates of \(B\) be \((b,0)\). Since \(A\) lies on the positive \(x\)-axis to the right of \(B\), the square \(ABCD\) has side \(AB=BC\).

The vertical line through \(B\) meets \(y=mx\) at \(C\), so

\[ C=(b,\,mb), \qquad B=(b,\,0). \]

Thus \(BC=mb\), and since \(AB=BC\), we get \(A=(b+mb,\,0)=\bigl(mb+b,\,0\bigr)\).

Hint: "Vertical line through \(B\)" means \(x=b\). Substituting into \(y=mx\) gives the \(y\)-coordinate of \(C\).
Coordinates: \(A=(mb+b,0)\), \(B=(b,0)\), \(C=(b,mb)\).
Step 21M

Since \(ABCD\) is a square with vertices in order, \(\overrightarrow{AD}=\overrightarrow{BC}\). Therefore

\[ D=A+\overrightarrow{BC}=\bigl(mb+b,\,0\bigr)+\bigl(0,\,mb\bigr)=\bigl(mb+b,\,mb\bigr). \]
Hint: Moving from \(A\) to \(D\) is the same vector as moving from \(B\) to \(C\) (both are side vectors of the square, perpendicular to \(AB\)).
Point \(D\): \(\bigl(mb+b,\,mb\bigr)\).
Step 31A

The slope of \(OD\) is

\[ \text{slope}=\frac{mb-0}{mb+b-0}=\frac{mb}{b(m+1)}=\frac{m}{m+1}. \]
Hint: The slope is \(\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\) between \(O=(0,0)\) and \(D\). Factor out \(b\) and cancel (note \(b\neq 0\)).
Slope of \(OD\) \(=\dfrac{m}{m+1}\).

bPart (b) — Find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\)

Step 11M

Find the slope of \(OM\):

\[ \text{slope}_{OM}=\frac{5-0}{6-0}=\frac{5}{6}. \]

Using the result from part (a) with \(m=\dfrac{5}{6}\):

\[ \text{slope}_{OQ}=\frac{m}{m+1}=\frac{\frac{5}{6}}{\frac{5}{6}+1}=\frac{\frac{5}{6}}{\frac{11}{6}}=\frac{5}{11}. \]
Hint: Here \(Q\) plays the role of \(D\), the far corner of the square built with diagonal along the line through \(O\) and \(M\).
Slope of \(OQ\) \(=\dfrac{5}{11}\).
Step 21M

Equation of the line through \(O\) and \(Q\):

\[ y=\frac{5}{11}x. \]

Equation of the line through \(M(6,5)\) and \(N(10,0)\):

\[ y-0=\frac{5-0}{6-10}(x-10) \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac{5}{4}x+\frac{25}{2}. \]
Hint: Use the two-point form \(y-y_1=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}(x-x_1)\) for line \(MN\).
Lines: \(OQ:\;y=\dfrac{5}{11}x\), \(MN:\;y=-\dfrac{5}{4}x+\dfrac{25}{2}\).
Step 31M

Solve the two equations simultaneously:

\[ \frac{5}{11}x=-\frac{5}{4}x+\frac{25}{2}. \] \[ \frac{5}{11}x+\frac{5}{4}x=\frac{25}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x\!\left(\frac{20+55}{44}\right)=\frac{25}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x\cdot\frac{75}{44}=\frac{25}{2}. \] \[ x=\frac{25}{2}\cdot\frac{44}{75}=\frac{22}{3}. \]

Then \(y=\dfrac{5}{11}\cdot\dfrac{22}{3}=\dfrac{10}{3}\). So \(Q=\left(\dfrac{22}{3},\dfrac{10}{3}\right)\).

Hint: The common denominator \(44\) makes the algebra clean; check that \(\frac{75}{44}\) is correct: \(5/11+5/4 = 20/44+55/44\).
Point \(Q=\left(\dfrac{22}{3},\dfrac{10}{3}\right)\).
Step 41A

Because \(PQRS\) is a square and \(R,S\) lie on the \(x\)-axis, the vertical distance from \(Q\) to the \(x\)-axis equals the side length \(RS=PQ\). Therefore the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\) is the \(x\)-coordinate of \(Q\) minus its \(y\)-coordinate:

\[ x_P=\frac{22}{3}-\frac{10}{3}=\frac{12}{3}=4. \]
Hint: \(P\) is the corner of the square adjacent to \(Q\); since \(Q\) is at height \(\frac{10}{3}\), \(P\) is shifted left by that same distance along the line \(OQ\).
The \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\) is \(4\).

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): Slope of \(OD = \dfrac{m}{m+1}\).
  • Part (b): Slope of \(OQ=\dfrac{5}{11}\), \(Q=\left(\dfrac{22}{3},\dfrac{10}{3}\right)\), and the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\) is \(\mathbf{4}\).
