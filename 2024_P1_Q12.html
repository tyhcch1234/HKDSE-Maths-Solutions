[2024 #12]

Denote the origin by $O$.

(a) $A$ and $B$ are points lying on the positive $x$-axis such that the $x$-coordinate of $A$ is greater than the $x$-coordinate of $B$. A vertical line which passes through $B$ cuts the straight line $y=mx$ at the point $C$ such that $AB=BC$, where $m$ is a positive constant. Let $D$ be a point such that $ABCD$ is a square. Express the slope of $OD$ in terms of $m$. (3 marks)

(b) The coordinates of the points $M$ and $N$ are $(6,5)$ and $(10,0)$ respectively. Let $P$ and $Q$ be points lying on $OM$ and $MN$ respectively while $R$ and $S$ be points lying on the $x$-axis. If the quadrilateral $PQRS$ is a square, find the $x$-coordinate of $P$. (4 marks)

STEP 1 · Set coordinates for part (a)1M

Let $B=(b,0)$. Since $AB=BC$ and $ABC\;D$ is a square, $AB=BC=m b$ (because $C$ lies on $y=mx$ at $x=b$). Thus $A=(mb+b,\,0)=(mb+b,0)$.

Also $C=(b,mb)$ and $D=(mb+b,\,mb)$.

STEP 2 · Slope of OD1M 1A

Slope of $OD$ (where $O$ is the origin):

\[ \text{slope}=\frac{mb-0}{mb+b-0}=\frac{mb}{b(m+1)}=\frac{m}{m+1}. \]
Slope of $OD=\dfrac{m}{m+1}$
STEP 3 · Part (b): slope of OM1M

Given $M=(6,5)$ and $N=(10,0)$:

\[ \text{slope of } OM=\frac{5-0}{6-0}=\frac{5}{6}. \]

By (a) with $m=\dfrac{5}{6}$, the slope of $OQ$ is

\[ \frac{5/6}{5/6+1}=\frac{5/6}{11/6}=\frac{5}{11}. \]

So the equation of line $OQ$ is $y=\dfrac{5x}{11}$.

STEP 4 · Equation of MN and find Q1M

Line through $M$ and $N$:

\[ y-0=\frac{5-0}{6-10}(x-10)\;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac54 x+\frac{25}{2}. \]

Solving $y=\dfrac{5x}{11}$ and $y=-\dfrac54 x+\dfrac{25}{2}$:

\[ \frac{5x}{11}+\frac54 x=\frac{25}{2}\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{20x+55x}{44}=\frac{25}{2}\;\Rightarrow\;x=\frac{22}{3}. \]

So $Q=\left(\dfrac{22}{3},\dfrac{10}{3}\right)$.

STEP 5 · x-coordinate of P1M 1A

Since $PQRS$ is a square lying with $R,S$ on the $x$-axis, $PQ$ is vertical, so the $x$-coordinate of $P$ equals that of $Q$ shifted by the side length...

Using the given result, the $x$-coordinate of $P$ is

\[ \frac{22}{3}-\frac{10}{3}=4. \]
The $x$-coordinate of $P$ is $4$.

Check: $P$ lies on $OM$, so $P=\left(4,\dfrac{10}{3}\right)$ which satisfies $y=\dfrac56 x$.
