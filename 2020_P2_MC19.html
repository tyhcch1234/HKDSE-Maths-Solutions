Angles in a Transversal Figure — Deciding Which Statements Must Be True

Angles & Parallel Lines Interior Angles of a Triangle "Must be true" Reasoning Reflex Angle

QQuestion

According to the figure (two parallel lines \(ABF\) and \(ED\), with a broken line \(B\to C\to D\), forming angles \(u\) at \(B\), reflex angle \(v\) at \(C\) and angle \(w\) at \(D\)), which of the following must be true?

  1. \(u-v+w=0^\circ\)
  2. \(u+v-w=180^\circ\)
  3. \(u+v+w=450^\circ\)

A. I only     B. II only     C. I and III only     D. II and III only

Key idea: "must be true" means the equation has to hold for every possible shape of the figure. So we test each statement by distorting the figure and seeing whether the equation still survives. Only statement II survives all distortions.

Step 11M Set up the parallel lines

Draw a line through \(C\) parallel to \(AB\) (and \(DE\)), and call the direction it points on the right \(F\). So \(AB /\!/ CF /\!/ DE\), with \(B\hat{A}F\) … actually the horizontal direction from \(B\) to the right is parallel to the direction from \(D\) to \(E\).

Then the angle \(u\) at \(B\) is the angle between \(BA\) and the parallel direction, and \(w\) at \(D\) is the angle between the parallel direction and \(DC\)'s extension.

Hint: Once the two parallel directions are fixed, \(u\) and \(w\) are each controlled by one ray only — so they cannot be forced into a fixed linear relation by themselves.
Step 21M Distort \(AB\) only

Fix the polyline \(B\to C\to D\to E\). Now move \(B\) along the line so that the ray \(BA\) swings up. Then:

  • \(u\) decreases (the ray tilts closer to the transversal),
  • \(w\) is unchanged (it depends only on \(DE\) and \(CD\)),
  • so the sum \(u-v+w\) changes even though the right-hand side \(0^\circ\) is fixed.
Hint: The slider above shows exactly this — sweep it and watch the \(u-v+w\) readout leave \(0^\circ\).
Step 31A Conclusion for I

Since a value of \(u\) can be found for which \(u-v+w\neq 0^\circ\), statement I is not always true. In the slider's starting position, \(u-v+w\) is clearly non-zero, so it fails immediately.

ResultStatement I is FALSE — it does not have to be true.

Step 11M Draw the parallel through \(C\) (Method 1)

Through \(C\) draw \(F\) so that \(AB/\!/ CF/\!/ DE\), with \(F\) on the same side as the interior of the figure. Then use interior angles on the same side of a transversal:

\[ \angle BCF = 180^\circ - \angle ABC = 180^\circ - u \quad (\text{int. } \angle s,\; CF/\!/ AB) \]

Hint: \(BCF\) and \(ABC\) are interior angles between the parallels \(AB\) and \(CF\), so they add to \(180^\circ\).
Step 21M Alternate angles give \(\angle DCF\)

\[ \angle DCF = \angle CDE = w \quad (\text{alt. } \angle s,\; CF/\!/ DE) \]

Hence the reflex angle at \(C\) is \[ v = \operatorname{reflex}\angle BCD = \angle BCF + \angle DCF = (180^\circ - u) + w. \]

Hint: The reflex angle at \(C\) opens to the outside of the polyline, exactly the sum of the two angles we just found.
Step 31M Rearrange

From \(v = (180^\circ - u) + w\), rearrange: \[ v = 180^\circ - u + w \;\Longrightarrow\; u + v - w = 180^\circ. \]

Hint: This is an identity forced by the parallel lines — it holds for every shape, so it must be true.
Step 41A Alternative check by triangle (Method 2)

Produce \(BA\) to a point \(G\) so that \(GCD\) is a straight line. Then \(GB/\!/ DE\), and interior angles give \(\angle BGD = 180^\circ - w\).

Also \(\angle BCG = \operatorname{reflex}\angle BCD - 180^\circ = v - 180^\circ\). In \(\triangle GBC\):

\[ (180^\circ - w) + u + (v - 180^\circ) = 180^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; u + v - w = 180^\circ. \]

ResultStatement II is TRUE — it must always hold.

Step 11M Increase \(DE\) by moving \(D\) only

Keep the direction of \(DE\) fixed and move \(D\) further along so that the segment \(DE\) lengthens. This changes the angle \(w\) only — \(u\) stays fixed, and the reflex angle \(v\) at \(C\) also changes (since \(\angle BCD\) changes as the ray \(DC\) swings).

Hint: Use the slider above: as you move \(D\), watch \(w\) change while \(u\) is untouched.
Step 21M Compare the two sides

Since \(u\) is fixed, \(w\) changes and \(v\) changes, the sum \(u+v+w\) is not pinned to a constant. For example, from the starting configuration one can check that \(u+v+w \neq 450^\circ\) in general.

In fact, using the identity from II, \(u+v-w=180^\circ\), we get \(u+v+w = 180^\circ + 2w\), which depends on \(w\) — not a constant.

Hint: \(180^\circ+2w=450^\circ\) would force \(w=135^\circ\), which is not required by the figure.
Step 31A Conclusion for III

Since \(w\) can take values other than \(135^\circ\), statement III is not always true.

ResultStatement III is FALSE — it does not have to be true.

✓Answers at a Glance

  • Statement I: \(u-v+w=0^\circ\) — NOT always true (moving \(B\) keeps \(w\) fixed but changes \(u\) and \(v\)).
  • Statement II: \(u+v-w=180^\circ\) — TRUE. Shown by \(CF/\!/ AB\): \(\angle BCF=180^\circ-u\), \(\angle DCF=w\), so \(v=(180^\circ-u)+w\).
  • Statement III: \(u+v+w=450^\circ\) — NOT always true (varies with \(w\); equivalently \(u+v+w=180^\circ+2w\)).
  • Only statement II must be true, so the correct option is B (II only).
