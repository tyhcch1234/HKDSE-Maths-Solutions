Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Tap each step to reveal the working. Try to predict the next line before you open it.

The region \(PQRS\) is a trapezium. Two sides are horizontal:

  • Top side \(SR\) lies on the horizontal line \(y=12\).
  • Bottom side \(PQ\) lies on the horizontal line \(y=6\).
  • The two slanted sides \(SP\) and \(RQ\) both pass through \((0,24)\).
Hint — Key idea of linear programming:
When a linear expression \(ax+by+c\) is maximised (or minimised) over a polygonal feasible region, the extreme value is always attained at a corner point (vertex) of the region. So we only need to test the four corners: \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\), \(S\).

Line \(SP\) passes through \(S(12,12)\) and the point \((0,24)\).

Using the two-point form (or slope-intercept form):

\[ y-24=\frac{0-24}{12-0}(x-0) \] \[ y-24=-2x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{y=-2x+24} \]
Hint — slope: \(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{0-24}{12-0}=-2\). The \(y\)-intercept is \(24\).

Line \(RQ\) passes through \(R(12,12)\) and the point \((0,24)\).

\[ y-24=\frac{0-24}{24-0}(x-0) \] \[ y-24=-x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{y=-x+24} \]
Hint — slope: \(m=\dfrac{0-24}{24-0}=-1\). Note the point \((0,24)\) can be thought of as the point \((0,24)\) on the \(y\)-axis; \(R=(12,12)\) lies on \(y=12\) and the line \(RQ\).

Point \(S\) — intersection of \(y=-2x+24\) with \(y=12\):

\[12=-2x+24 \Rightarrow 2x=12 \Rightarrow x=6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad S(6,12)\]

Point \(P\) — intersection of \(y=-2x+24\) with \(y=6\):

\[6=-2x+24 \Rightarrow 2x=18 \Rightarrow x=9 \quad\Rightarrow\quad P(9,6)\]

Point \(R\) — intersection of \(y=-x+24\) with \(y=12\):

\[12=-x+24 \Rightarrow x=12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad R(12,12)\]

Point \(Q\) — intersection of \(y=-x+24\) with \(y=6\):

\[6=-x+24 \Rightarrow x=18 \quad\Rightarrow\quad Q(18,6)\]
Corners: \(P(9,6)\), \(Q(18,6)\), \(R(12,12)\), \(S(6,12)\).

Because the expression is linear, the greatest value must occur at a corner point. Substitute each:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{At }P(9,6):\quad & 7(6)-5(9)+3 = 42-45+3 = 0\\[3pt] \text{At }Q(18,6):\quad & 7(6)-5(18)+3 = 42-90+3 = -45\\[3pt] \text{At }R(12,12):\quad & 7(12)-5(12)+3 = 84-60+3 = 27\\[3pt] \text{At }S(6,12):\quad & 7(12)-5(6)+3 = 84-30+3 = 57 \end{aligned} \]
Hint — why only corners? If \(7y-5x+3=k\), then \(y=\frac{5}{7}x+\frac{k-3}{7}\). Changing \(k\) just shifts this straight line up/down. The largest \(k\) whose line still touches the region is the one that passes through the highest relevant corner — here \(S\).
Corner point\((x,y)\)\(7y-5x+3\)
P\((9,6)\)0
Q\((18,6)\)−45
R\((12,12)\)27
S\((6,12)\)57 ← largest

The largest value is \(57\), attained at \(S(6,12)\).

\(\therefore\; 7y-5x+3\) attains its greatest value at \(S\).   Answer: D
✅ Correct option: D (S) — the greatest value of \(7y-5x+3\) is \(57\), attained at \(S(6,12)\).
