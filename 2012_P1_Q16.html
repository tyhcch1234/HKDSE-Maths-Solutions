2012 Paper 1  Question 16

Probability — selecting 4 members from 8 departments

Probability Combinations Cnr Complementary Events Ordered Selection

Q The Question

There are 8 departments in a company. To form a task group of 16 members, 2 representatives are nominated by each department. From the task group, 4 members are randomly selected.

  1. Find the probability that the 4 selected members are nominated by 4 different departments. [2 marks]
  2. Find the probability that the 4 selected members are nominated by at most 3 different departments. [2 marks]
Key structure: 8 departments × 2 representatives = 16 members in total. Only 4 of them are drawn at random, without replacement.

2 Step-by-Step Solution

1 Understand the sample space ▼

There are 8 departments, each supplying 2 representatives, giving a task group of size

$$8 \times 2 = 16$$

We randomly select 4 members from these 16, without replacement. So the total number of equally likely selections is the number of combinations of 4 chosen from 16:

$$C^{16}_{4} = \frac{16!}{4!\,12!} = 1820$$

Hint — Combination formula $$C^{n}_{r} \;=\; \frac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$$ Here $n = 16$ is the number of members available, and $r = 4$ is the number we take.

Every one of these 1820 selections is equally likely, so for any event $E$,

$$P(E) = \frac{\text{number of favourable selections}}{1820}.$$

2 (a) Count selections from 4 different departments ▼

We want the 4 chosen members to come from 4 distinct departments.

Choosing the departments: pick any 4 of the 8 departments:

$$C^{8}_{4}$$

Choosing the representatives: from each chosen department, there are 2 representatives, so 2 choices per department. Across 4 departments:

$$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^{4} = C^{2}_{1}^{4}$$

Hint — Multiplication principle If a task is done in ordered stages, the total number of ways is the product of the number of choices at each stage. Here the stages are: department 1, department 2, department 3, department 4.

So the number of favourable selections is $C^{8}_{4} \times 2^{4} = 70 \times 16 = 1120$.

3 (a) Compute the probability ▼

Putting Step 2 over Step 1:

$$P = \frac{C^{8}_{4}C^{2}_{1}^{4}}{C^{16}_{4}} = \frac{70 \times 16}{1820} = \frac{1120}{1820} = \frac{8}{13}$$

Both numerator and denominator reduce by 140: $1120 \div 140 = 8$ and $1820 \div 140 = 13$.
(a) The required probability $= \dfrac{8}{13}$  (1M for the count, 1A for the answer)
4 (a) Alternative method — multiply the probabilities step by step ▼

Instead of counting, we can pick the 4 members one at a time and multiply the probabilities:

$$\frac{16}{16} \times \frac{14}{15} \times \frac{12}{14} \times \frac{10}{13}$$

Hint — Why these fractions? The first pick is free (16 out of 16). Once a member is gone, that member's partner — from the same department — must be avoided, so only $16-2=14$ of the remaining 15 members work. Then $12$ of $14$, then $10$ of $13$.

Simplifying:

$$1 \times \frac{14}{15} \times \frac{6}{7} \times \frac{10}{13} = \frac{8}{13}$$

This gives exactly the same value $\dfrac{8}{13}$, confirming the answer.
5 (b) Read carefully — "at most 3 different departments" ▼

With only 4 members chosen, the phrase "at most 3 different departments" means the 4 members do not all come from different departments. So event (b) is exactly the complement of event (a):

$$P(\text{at most 3 departments}) = 1 - P(\text{4 different departments})$$

Hint — Complementary event If $A'$ is the complement of $A$, then $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$. Using the complement is usually much faster than listing every case (3 departments, 2 departments, …).
6 (b) Finish the calculation ▼

Using the answer from (a):

$$P = 1 - \frac{8}{13} = \frac{13-8}{13} = \frac{5}{13}$$

(b) The required probability $= \dfrac{5}{13}$  (1M for the complement, 1A for the answer)

3 Summary & Exam Tips

What we used

  • $C^n_r$ to count equally likely selections
  • Multiplication principle for staged choices
  • Complement: $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$

Final answers

(a) $\dfrac{8}{13} \approx 0.615$

(b) $\dfrac{5}{13} \approx 0.385$

Note $\frac{8}{13} + \frac{5}{13} = 1$, as they are complements.
