Question 7 (4 marks)
In Figure 1, PR is a diameter of the circle PQRS. Denote the point of intersection of PR and QS by T.
If ∠PSQ = 41° and ∠PTQ = 68°, find ∠RQS and ∠PQS.
Hints: ∠PTQ is an exterior angle of triangle PTS, so ∠RPS = ∠PTQ − ∠PSQ. Then use the angles in the same segment (∠RQS = ∠RPS) and the angle in a semicircle (∠PQR = 90°).
STEP 1 · Exterior angle of △PTS
Exterior Angle of a Cyclic Quadrilateral \(\text{exterior angle}=\text{interior opposite angle}\) The angle outside the cyclic quadrilateral equals the angle at the far vertex, giving \(27^{\circ}\).
∠PTQ is the exterior angle of △PTS at T, so it equals the sum of the two remote interior angles ∠TPS and ∠TSP (i.e. ∠RPS and ∠PSQ).
$$ \angle RPS = \angle PTQ - \angle PSQ $$ $$ = 68^\circ - 41^\circ = 27^\circ $$
STEP 2 · Angles in the same segment
Angles in the Same Segment \(\angle AXB=\angle AYB\ \text{for }A,B,X,Y\text{ on a circle}\) Both angles stand on the same chord, so the second angle is also \(27^{\circ}\).
∠RQS and ∠RPS subtend the same arc RS (both stand on the chord RS),
so they are equal.
$$ \angle RQS = \angle RPS = 27^\circ $$
STEP 3 · Angle in a semicircle
Angle in a Semicircle \(\angle APB=90^{\circ}\ \text{if }AB\text{ is a diameter}\) The angle at the circumference on the diameter is a right angle, so \(90^{\circ}\) enters the split.
Since PR is a diameter of the circle, the angle in the semicircle ∠PQR is a right angle.
$$ \angle PQR = 90^\circ $$
STEP 4 · Split ∠PQR at QS QS divides ∠PQR into ∠PQS and ∠RQS, so ∠PQS = ∠PQR − ∠RQS.
$$ \angle PQS = \angle PQR - \angle RQS $$ $$ = 90^\circ - 27^\circ = 63^\circ $$
∠RQS = 27°   ·   ∠PQS = 63°

Step 2. In \(\triangle ABC\), the angle sum is \(180^{\circ}\), so \(\angle BAC=180^{\circ}-80^{\circ}-48^{\circ}\).
