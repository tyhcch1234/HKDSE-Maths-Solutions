Q7 The Question 4 marks

In Figure 1, \(PR\) is a diameter of the circle \(PQRS\). Denote the point of intersection of \(PR\) and \(QS\) by \(T\).

If \(\angle PSQ = 41^\circ\) and \(\angle PTQ = 68^\circ\), find \(\angle RQS\) and \(\angle PQS\).

(4 marks)

\(\angle PSQ\) is an inscribed angle at \(S\); \(\angle PTQ\) is the angle between the two chords \(PR\) and \(QS\) measured at the intersection point \(T\) inside the circle.

Part (a) Find \(\angle RQS\) 1M + 1A

Key idea: \(\angle PTQ\) is the exterior angle of \(\triangle PST\) at vertex \(T\), so it equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles \(\angle PST\) and \(\angle PST\)… Drag the slider to watch the two interior angles always add up to the exterior angle.

STEP 11M

Consider \(\triangle PST\). \(T\) lies on the line \(PR\), so \(\angle PTS = 180^\circ\) (straight line). Hence \(\angle PTQ\) is the exterior angle of \(\triangle PST\) at \(T\):

\[ \angle PTQ = \angle PST + \angle TPS = \angle PSQ + \angle RPS \]
Hint: \(\angle PST = \angle PSQ\) because \(T\) lies on the line \(QS\); and \(\angle TPS = \angle RPS\) because \(T\) lies on the diameter \(PR\).
STEP 2—

Substitute the given values and solve for \(\angle RPS\):

\[ \angle RPS = \angle PTQ - \angle PSQ = 68^\circ - 41^\circ = 27^\circ \]
Hint: This is a direct subtraction — one line, no extra construction needed.
STEP 31A

Both \(\angle RQS\) and \(\angle RPS\) subtend the same chord \(RS\) (they are angles in the same segment):

\[ \angle RQS = \angle RPS = 27^\circ \]
Hint: Angles in the same segment subtended by the same chord are equal — the classic "butterfly" pair here is \(\angle RPS\) and \(\angle RQS\), both standing on chord \(RS\).
\(\angle RQS = 27^\circ\)

Part (b) Find \(\angle PQS\) 1M + 1A

Since \(PR\) is a diameter, the angle in a semi-circle at \(Q\) is a right angle. Toggle the layers and drag \(\angle RQS\) to see how \(\angle PQS\) changes.

STEP 11M

\(PR\) is a diameter and \(Q\) lies on the circle. By the angle in a semi-circle (converse of the angle in a semi-circle theorem):

\[ \angle PQR = 90^\circ \]
Hint: An angle in a semi-circle is a right angle — this is the single most used circle theorem in this question. Look at \(\triangle PQR\): the side \(PR\) passes through the centre \(O\).
STEP 2—

The chord \(QS\) splits the right angle \(\angle PQR\) into two parts: \(\angle PQS\) and \(\angle RQS\):

\[ \angle PQS = \angle PQR - \angle RQS \]
Hint: The segment \(QS\) lies inside the right angle \(\angle PQR\), so the two smaller angles are complementary.
STEP 31A

Substitute \(\angle PQR = 90^\circ\) and the result of Part (a):

\[ \angle PQS = 90^\circ - 27^\circ = 63^\circ \]
\(\angle PQS = 63^\circ\)

Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle RPS = \angle PTQ - \angle PSQ = 68^\circ - 41^\circ = 27^\circ\)  (1M)
  • \(\angle RQS = \angle RPS = \mathbf{27^\circ}\)  (1A)
  • \(\angle PQR = 90^\circ\) (angle in semi-circle)  (1M)
  • \(\angle PQS = \angle PQR - \angle RQS = 90^\circ - 27^\circ = \mathbf{63^\circ}\)  (1A)
