H.C.F. and L.C.M. of Expressions

Question

The H.C.F. and the L.C.M. of three expressions are \(xy^2z^3\) and \(x^2y^4z^6\) respectively. If the first expression and the second expression are \(xy^2z^6\) and \(xy^2z^4\) respectively, then the third expression is

  • A. \(xy^2z^3\)
  • B. \(xy^4z^4\)
  • C. \(x^2y^2z^4\)
  • D. \(x^2y^4z^3\)

Solution

Set up Let the third expression be \(x^a y^b z^c\).
Product Law of Indices \(x^{m}\cdot x^{n}\cdot x^{p}=x^{m+n+p}\) All three expressions have the same bases \(x,y,z\), so the indices of each base simply add — the index of \(y\) in the product is \(2+2+b\).
For y \(2=\min(2,2,b)\) and \(4=\max(2,2,b)\Rightarrow b=4\).
Matching Indices: Largest and Smallest \(\min(2,2,b)=2\ \text{and}\ \max(2,2,b)=4\ \Rightarrow\ b=4\) Two of the three indices are already \(2\), so the sum is pinned down exactly when the total \(4\) exceeds the smallest index — hence \(b=4\).
For z \(3=\min(6,4,c)\Rightarrow c=3\); check \(6=\max(6,4,3)\).
Checking a Candidate Index \(\min(6,4,c)=3\ \text{and}\ \max(6,4,c)=6\ \Rightarrow\ c=3\) For \(z\) the total is \(3\), which is below both given indices \(6\) and \(4\); only \(c=3\) makes the sum come out right, and \(6\) remains the largest index.
For x \(1=\min(1,1,a)\) and \(2=\max(1,1,a)\Rightarrow a=2\).
Result The third expression is \(x^2y^4z^3\).
Answer: D — x2y4z3

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Adding the indices of \(x\): \(2+2+a=4\), so \(a=4\). Check \(\min(2,2,4)=2\) and \(\max(2,2,4)=4\).
Step 2. Adding the indices of \(y\): \(3+2+b=7\), so \(b=7\). Check \(\min(3,2,7)=2\) and \(\max(3,2,7)=7\).
Step 3. Adding the indices of \(z\): \(4+2+c=9\), so \(c=9\). Check \(\min(4,2,9)=2\) and \(\max(4,2,9)=9\).
Step 4. The third expression is \(x^{4}y^{7}z^{9}\).
Answer: \(a=4,\ b=7,\ c=9\), i.e. \(x^{4}y^{7}z^{9}\)
