[2017 MC #39]

In the figure, \(AD\) is a vertical pole standing on the horizontal ground \(BCD\). If \(AB = 25\text{ m}\), \(AD = 15\text{ m}\), \(BC = 29\text{ m}\) and \(CD = 21\text{ m}\), find the angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\) correct to the nearest degree.

A. 53°
B. 54°
C. 69°
D. 70°
Step 1 · Set up an auxiliary point
Angle between a Line and a Plane \(\theta=\angle(\text{line},\ \text{its projection on the plane})\) The angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\) is measured between \(AB\) and its projection on that plane.

The angle between a line and a plane is measured between the line and its projection on the plane.

Drop a perpendicular from \(B\) onto the plane \(ACD\);
call the foot \(D\). We first justify why the foot is \(D\) in Step 3 — for now we need the length \(BD\).

Hint: look for a right-angled triangle using the vertical pole \(AD\).

Step 2 · Find BD from \(\triangle ABD\)

Since \(AD\) is vertical, \(AD \perp BD\) (the ground is horizontal),
so \(\triangle ABD\) is right-angled at \(D\).

\[BD=\sqrt{AB^{2}-AD^{2}}=\sqrt{25^{2}-15^{2}}=\sqrt{625-225}=\sqrt{400}=20\text{ m}\]

Step 3 · Show \(B\) projects onto \(D\)
Converse of Pythagoras Theorem \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{the angle opposite }c\text{ is }90^{\circ}\) \(20^{2}+21^{2}=841=29^{2}\), so \(\angle BDC=90^{\circ}\) and \(BD\perp CD\), making \(BD\perp\) plane \(ACD\).

Check the triangle \(BCD\) with \(BD=20,\;CD=21,\;BC=29\):

\[BD^{2}+CD^{2}=20^{2}+21^{2}=400+441=841=29^{2}=BC^{2}\]

By the converse of the Pythagoras Theorem, \(\angle BDC = 90^{\circ}\),
i.e. \(BD \perp CD\).

Also \(BD \perp AD\) (Step 2). Since \(AD\) and \(CD\) lie in the plane \(ACD\) and intersect at \(D\), \(BD\) is perpendicular to the whole plane \(ACD\). Hence \(D\) is the projection of \(B\) on the plane \(ACD\).

Step 4 · Identify the required angle

The projection of \(AB\) onto the plane \(ACD\) is the segment \(AD\). Therefore the angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\) is

\[\angle BAD\]

Hint: in the right-angled \(\triangle ABD\), \(\angle BAD\) is at the top vertex \(A\).

Step 5 · Compute \(\angle BAD\)
Cosine Ratio in a Right Triangle \(\cos\angle BAD=\dfrac{AD}{AB}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle ABD\), \(\cos\angle BAD=\dfrac{15}{25}=0.6\), so the angle is about \(53^{\circ}\).

In right-angled \(\triangle ABD\) (right angle at \(D\)):

\[\cos\angle BAD=\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac{15}{25}=\frac{3}{5}=0.6\]

\[\angle BAD=\cos^{-1}(0.6)\approx 53.1301^{\circ}\]

Correct to the nearest degree: \(\angle BAD \approx 53^{\circ}\).

Final Answer

The angle between \(AB\) and the plane \(ACD\) is approximately \(53^{\circ}\),
so the answer is A.

Answer: A (53°)

Show the worked solution
