QUESTION

The bar chart below shows the distribution of the ages of the children in a group, where $a>11$ and $4

(a) Find $a$ and $b$.  [3 marks]

(b) Four more children now join the group. It is found that the ages of these four children are all different and the range of the ages of the children in the group remains unchanged. Find

(i) the greatest possible median of the ages of the children in the group,

(ii) the least possible mean of the ages of the children in the group.  [4 marks]

1Set up the median condition on the total number of children.
Median of a Data Set (even n) \(\text{median}=\dfrac{x_{\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}+x_{\left(\frac{N}{2}+1\right)}}{2}\) A median of \\(7.5\\) must lie midway between an age \\(7\\) and an age \\(8\\), so \\(N\\) has to be even.

Let $N$ be the total number of children. Then

$$N=11+a+11+b+4=a+b+26$$

With median $7.5$, the two middle values (the $\tfrac{N}{2}$-th and $\left(\tfrac{N}{2}+1\right)$-th) must be $7$ and $8$, so $N$ must be even. Hence

$$a+b+26 \text{ is even} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; a+b \text{ is even.}$$

Hint: A median of 7.5 lies midway between ages 7 and 8, so equal numbers of children lie on each side.
2Count children with age $\le 7$ and with age $\ge 8$.
Balanced Counts about the Median \(\#\{x\le 7\}=\#\{x\ge 8\}\) The children of age \\(7\\) or below number \\(11+a\\) and those of age \\(8\\) or above number \\(b+15\\), so \\(a=b+4\\).

Children with age $\le 7$: $\;11+a$. Children with age $\ge 8$: $\;11+b+4=b+15$.

The lower half of the ordered list must end at age $7$, and the upper half must start at age $8$. Since $a>11$ and $b<10$,

$$\underbrace{a+11}_{\text{age}\le 7}=\underbrace{b+15}_{\text{age}\ge 8}$$

$$\Rightarrow\; a = b+4$$

Hint: For the median to be exactly $7.5$, the count below the middle equals the count above it — this balances the two sides of the chart.
3Apply the given ranges $a>11$ and $4

Since $a=b+4>11$, we get $b>7$; together with $b<10$ and $b$ an integer,

$$b\in\{8,\,9\}$$

Case 1: $b=8 \Rightarrow a=12$.   Case 2: $b=9 \Rightarrow a=13$.

Both keep $a>11$ and $4

$$\boxed{a=12,\;b=8}\qquad\text{or}\qquad \boxed{a=13,\;b=9}$$

Hint: Everything is determined — the answer to (a) is not unique; it is one of two cases.
4(b)(i) — Make the median as large as possible.

Before joining, the group has $N=a+b+26$ children. After the four children join there are $N+4$ children.

The four ages are all different, so at most one of them can be $7$. To push the median upward, choose the smallest possible ages but distinct:

  • Ages $6,7,8,9$ are the smallest four distinct ages that keep the range unchanged at $6$–$10$.

Then the ordered list of all $N+4$ children ends its lower half at age $9$, giving median

$$\text{greatest possible median}=9$$

(Check: with $6,7,8,9$ added, the middle pair straddles age 9; any larger choice would raise the central values and cannot keep the "greatest median" achieved here.)

Hint: The median is the "middle" age — add the SMALLEST distinct ages to drag the middle of the ordered list upward as far as possible.
5(b)(ii) — Make the mean as small as possible.
Mean of a Frequency Table \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\) Total ages divided by total children: \\(\dfrac{374}{50}=7.48\\) for the case \\(a=12\\), \\(b=8\\).

Now add the four smallest distinct ages that keep the range $6$–$10$ unchanged: $\;6,\,7,\,8,\,9$.

Total before joining: $\;11(6)+a(7)+11(8)+b(9)+4(10)$.

Case 1: $a=12,\;b=8$

$$\bar{x}=\frac{12\cdot 6+13\cdot 7+12\cdot 8+9\cdot 9+4\cdot 10}{12+13+12+9+4}=\frac{66+91+96+81+40}{50}=\frac{374}{50}=7.48$$

Case 2: $a=13,\;b=9$

$$\bar{x}=\frac{12\cdot 6+14\cdot 7+12\cdot 8+10\cdot 9+4\cdot 10}{12+14+12+10+4}=\frac{72+98+96+90+40}{52}=\frac{396}{52}\approx 7.6154$$

The smaller value is $7.48$, so the least possible mean is

$$\boxed{7.48}$$

Hint: Try both (a)-cases with the smallest distinct ages; the smaller total divided by the smaller count is the answer.
Answer: (a) $(a,b)=(12,8)$ or $(13,9)$  ·  (b)(i) $9$  ·  (b)(ii) $7.48$
