Statistics — Bar Chart, Median & Mean of Grouped Ages

Bar Chart Median Mean Range Case Analysis

Question

The bar chart below shows the distribution of the ages of the children in a group, where \(a > 11\) and \(4 < b < 10\). The median of the ages of the children in the group is \(7.5\).

(Bar chart: ages 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 with frequencies \(11,\;a,\;11,\;b,\;4\).)

(a) Find \(a\) and \(b\). 3 marks

(b) Four more children now join the group. It is found that the ages of these four children are all different and the range of the ages of the children in the group remains unchanged. Find

(i) the greatest possible median of the ages of the children in the group, 2 marks

(ii) the least possible mean of the ages of the children in the group. 2 marks

Part (a) — Finding \(a\) and \(b\)

Step 11M

Equate the total number of children counted from the two equal-frequency bars:

\[ 11 + a = 11 + b + 4 \]
Hint: The total number of children is the same whether you add the bars left-to-right or compare the given totals. This gives a relation between \(a\) and \(b\).
Step 21M

Simplify the equation:

\[ a = b + 4 \]
Hint: This means \(a\) is always 4 more than \(b\).
Step 31A+1A

Use the conditions \(a > 11\) and \(4 < b < 10\), so \(12 \le a \le 13\) and \(5 \le b \le 9\).

Combined with \(a=b+4\):

\[ \begin{cases} a=12,\\ b=8 \end{cases} \quad \text{or} \quad \begin{cases} a=13,\\ b=9 \end{cases} \]
Answer: \(a=12,\;b=8\)  or  \(a=13,\;b=9\)
Hint: Both cases satisfy all conditions. We must keep both for part (b)!

Part (b)(i) — Greatest Possible Median

Step 11M

To make the median as large as possible, the four new ages should be as large as possible: \(7,\,8,\,9,\,10\).

Hint: The range must remain unchanged. Original smallest age is \(6\), so the new children's ages must be \(\ge 6\) and the largest stays \(10\).
Step 21A

Total children: \(12+13+12+9+4 = 50\). With 4 more, \(n=54\).

The median is the average of the 27th and 28th ordered values. Placing the new high ages at the top pushes the middle pair up to \(8\) and \(8\).

So the greatest possible median is:

Greatest possible median \(= 8\)
Hint: Try sliding the new ages down — the median can only decrease, confirming \(8\) is the maximum.

Part (b)(ii) — Least Possible Mean

Step 11M

To minimise the mean, choose the four new ages as small as possible while keeping the range: \(6,\,7,\,8,\,9\).

Hint: Range unchanged means smallest \(=6\), largest \(=10\). So the new ages are \(6,7,8,9\) (all distinct).
Step 21M

Compute the mean for each case (from part (a)):

Case 1: \(a=12,\;b=8\):

\[ \frac{12(6)+13(7)+12(8)+9(9)+4(10)}{12+13+12+9+4} = \frac{380}{50} = 7.6 \]

Case 2: \(a=13,\;b=9\):

\[ \frac{12(6)+14(7)+12(8)+10(9)+4(10)}{12+14+12+10+4} = \frac{396}{52} \approx 7.61538 \]
Hint: Compare \(7.6\) and \(7.61538\). The smaller value wins.
Step 31A

Since \(7.6 < 7.61538\), the least possible mean is \(7.6\).

Least possible mean \(= 7.6\)
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(a=12,\;b=8\) or \(a=13,\;b=9\)
  • (b)(i) Greatest possible median \(= 8\)
  • (b)(ii) Least possible mean \(= 7.6\)
