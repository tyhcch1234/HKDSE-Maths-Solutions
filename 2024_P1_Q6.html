Question 6 · Percentages

Cost, Marked Price & Discount

Percentages Profit & Discount 4 Marks

The marked price of a calculator is 40% higher than its cost. The calculator is sold at a discount of 25% on its marked price and the profit is $13. Find the marked price of the calculator.

📊 Cost → Marked Price → Selling Price

Adjust the slider to see how the cost \(c\) changes the marked price and selling price. The profit (selling price − cost) should always be $13.

Cost $100.00
Marked Price (1.4 × cost) $140.00
Selling Price (75% of marked price) $105.00
Profit (Selling − Cost) $5.00
$50 $400

Hint: Move the slider. Notice that only when cost = $260 does the profit become exactly $13.

✓ Step-by-Step Solution

Let \(c\) be the cost of the calculator (in dollars).

💡 Hint: Always define a variable for the unknown base value. Here the cost is the base for all percentage changes.

The marked price is 40% higher than the cost:

\[ \text{Marked price} = (1 + 40\%)c = 1.4c \]

💡 Hint: "40% higher than cost" means cost + 40% of cost = \(1.4c\).

A 25% discount on the marked price is applied:

\[ \text{Selling price} = 75\% \times 1.4c = 1.05c \]

💡 Hint: 25% discount means the customer pays 75% of the marked price. The selling price is always more than the cost when there is a profit.

Profit = Selling price − Cost = $13

\[ 1.05c - c = 13 \]

\[ 0.05c = 13 \]

💡 Hint: This is the key equation. The difference between selling price and cost is exactly the profit.

\[ 0.05c = 13 \quad \Rightarrow \quad c = \frac{13}{0.05} = 260 \]

The cost of the calculator is $260.

The question asks for the marked price:

\[ \text{Marked price} = 1.4c = 1.4 \times 260 = 364 \]

✅ Marked price = $364

💡 Hint: Always re-read the question. It asks for the marked price, not the cost. A common mistake is to stop at \(c = 260\) and forget to multiply by 1.4.

Final Answer

$364

📝 Key Formulas

  • • Marked price = Cost × (1 + markup%)
  • • Selling price = Marked price × (1 − discount%)
  • • Profit = Selling price − Cost
