Geometry of a Parallelogram — Angles at Vertices
Parallelogram Angle Sum Adjacent Angles Angles at a Point

Q The Question

The figure shows a parallelogram \(ABCD\). Angles \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), \(d\) are the interior angles at vertices \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\) respectively. Which of the following must be true?

\(\text{I.}\quad a+b=180^\circ\)
\(\text{II.}\quad b+c=360^\circ\)
\(\text{III.}\quad c+d=540^\circ\)

A. I and II only    B. I and III only    C. II and III only    D. I, II and III

Notes: the interior angle at a vertex, together with the exterior angle shown, add up to \(360^\circ\) (angles at a point).

I Is \(a+b=180^\circ\) always true?1A

In a parallelogram the opposite sides are parallel. Here \(AD \parallel BC\). Treating \(AB\) as a transversal cutting the parallel lines \(AD\) and \(BC\), the angles \(a\) and \(b\) are interior angles on the same side of the transversal.

Vertex angle \(b\) 70°
Step 1 1M

Drop a perpendicular? No — simpler: use the parallel-line angle property.

Hint: \(AD \parallel BC\), so the interior angles on the same side of the transversal \(AB\) are supplementary.
Step 2 1A

\[ \angle BAD + \angle ABC = 180^\circ \] \[ \boxed{a+b=180^\circ} \]

✓ I is true. In a parallelogram, adjacent (consecutive) angles are supplementary.

II Is \(b+c=360^\circ\) always true?1M

Moving around the parallelogram \(A \to B \to C\), the angles \(b\) (interior at \(B\)) and \(c\) (exterior at \(C\)) are not directly linked by a simple sum — one is an interior angle and the other is an exterior angle.

Vertex angle \(b\) 70°
Step 1 1M

At vertex \(C\), the interior angle \(c\) and the exterior angle add up to \(360^\circ\) (angles at a point).

Hint: The interior angle at \(C\) is \(180^\circ - a'\) where \(a'\) is the exterior... but easier: use the exterior angle formula. \(\angle BCD_{\text{ext}} = 360^\circ - c\).
Step 2 1A

Since \(AB \parallel DC\), the interior angles on the same side of transversal \(BC\) are supplementary: \[ \angle ABC + \angle BCD_{\text{int}} = 180^\circ \] Now \(\angle BCD_{\text{int}} = 360^\circ - c\), so \[ b + (360^\circ - c) = 180^\circ \] \[ b - c = -180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad b + c = 360^\circ - 180^\circ + \ldots \] Actually simplifying: \(b + 360^\circ - c = 180^\circ \Rightarrow c - b = 180^\circ\). There is no universal equation \(b+c=360^\circ\).

Hint: \(b+c\) varies with the shape of the parallelogram — it is not fixed. Try different slider positions to see this.
✗ II is not necessarily true. It happens only for special shapes (e.g. when \(c=270^\circ\)).

III Is \(c+d=540^\circ\) always true?1A

Use the parallel sides \(BC \parallel AD\). The interior angles on the same side of transversal \(CD\) at vertices \(C\) and \(D\) are supplementary.

Vertex angle \(b\) 70°
Step 1 1M

At vertices \(C\) and \(D\), the interior angles are \(360^\circ - c\) and \(360^\circ - d\) (angles at a point).

Hint: Since \(BC \parallel AD\), consecutive interior angles sum to \(180^\circ\): \((360^\circ-c) + (360^\circ-d) = 180^\circ\).
Step 2 1A

\[ (360^\circ - c) + (360^\circ - d) = 180^\circ \] \[ 720^\circ - (c+d) = 180^\circ \] \[ \boxed{c+d = 540^\circ} \]

✓ III is true. The sum of the two reflex exterior angles \(c\) and \(d\) is always \(540^\circ\).
0 / 2

Final Answer & Summary

  • I. \(a+b=180^\circ\) — TRUE (interior angles on the same side of transversal \(AB\), with \(AD \parallel BC\)).
  • II. \(b+c=360^\circ\) — NOT always true. It is not a property of a general parallelogram.
  • III. \(c+d=540^\circ\) — TRUE (using \(BC \parallel AD\) and the \(360^\circ\) angle sum at each vertex).
Only I and III must be true  →  Answer: B
