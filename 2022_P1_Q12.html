[2022#12] The equation of the circle \(C\) is

\(x^{2}+y^{2}-154x-128y+224=0\).

Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). The coordinates of the point \(H\) are \((65,48)\).

(a) Find the distance between \(G\) and \(H\). [3 marks]

(b) Let \(P\) be a moving point on \(C\). When the area of \(\Delta GHP\) is the greatest,

(i) describe the geometric relationship between \(GH\) and \(GP\);

(ii) find the perimeter of \(\Delta GHP\). [4 marks]

STEP 1 · Find the centre G (completing the square)
\[ x^{2}+y^{2}-154x-128y+224=0 \] \[ (x^{2}-154x)+(y^{2}-128y)=-224 \] \[ (x-77)^{2}-77^{2}+(y-64)^{2}-64^{2}=-224 \] \[ (x-77)^{2}+(y-64)^{2}=77^{2}+64^{2}-224 \] \[ (x-77)^{2}+(y-64)^{2}=99^{2} \]
Centre \(G=(77,\,64)\)
The centre is \(\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)=\left(\tfrac{154}{2},\tfrac{128}{2}\right)=(77,64)\).
Completing the Square for a Circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{centre}\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)\) Here \(D=-154\) and \(E=-128\), so the centre is \((77,\,64)\) and the radius is \(\sqrt{77^{2}+64^{2}-224}=99\).
STEP 2 (a) · Distance between G and H
\[ GH=\sqrt{(77-65)^{2}+(64-48)^{2}} \] \[ =\sqrt{12^{2}+16^{2}}=\sqrt{144+256}=\sqrt{400} \]
\(GH=20\)
Use the distance formula \(\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\). \(12,16\) is a \(3\text{-}4\text{-}5\) triple scaled by 4.
Distance Formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) \(GH=\sqrt{(77-65)^{2}+(64-48)^{2}}=\sqrt{12^{2}+16^{2}}=20\).
STEP 3 (b)(i) · Greatest area ⟹ GH ⟂ GP
\[ [\Delta GHP]=\tfrac{1}{2}\,(GH)(GP)\sin\angle HGP \]

Here \(GH\) is fixed and \(GP=r=99\) is fixed (radius). To maximise the area, \(\sin\angle HGP\) must equal \(1\).

\[ \therefore \angle HGP=90^{\circ} \]
\(GH\) is perpendicular (\(\perp\)) to \(GP\)
Geometric view: the height of the triangle from \(P\) to line \(GH\) is greatest when \(P\) is farthest from line \(GH\) — this happens when \(GP\perp GH\).
STEP 4 (b)(ii) · Find the radius of C
\[ \text{radius } r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{154}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{128}{2}\right)^{2}-224} \] \[ =\sqrt{77^{2}+64^{2}-224}=\sqrt{5929+4096-224}=\sqrt{9801} \]
\(GP=r=99\)
Formula: \(r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}\).
STEP 5 · Find \(HP\) by Pythagoras

Since \(GH\perp GP\), triangle \(GHP\) is right-angled at \(G\).

\[ HP^{2}=GH^{2}+GP^{2}=20^{2}+99^{2} \] \[ HP^{2}=400+9801=10201 \] \[ HP=\sqrt{10201}=101 \]
\(20,99,101\) is a Pythagorean triple: \(20^{2}+99^{2}=101^{2}\).
Pythagoras Theorem \(HP^{2}=GH^{2}+GP^{2}\) Since \(GH\perp GP\), \(\triangle GHP\) is right-angled at \(G\), so \(HP=\sqrt{20^{2}+99^{2}}=101\).
STEP 6 · Perimeter of \(\Delta GHP\)
\[ \text{Perimeter}=GH+GP+HP \] \[ =20+99+101 \]
Answers:
(a) \(GH=20\)
(b)(i) \(GH\perp GP\)
(b)(ii) Perimeter of \(\Delta GHP = 220\) units
Maximum area \(=\tfrac{1}{2}(20)(99)=990\) square units.

Question. The centre of the circle \(C:x^{2}+y^{2}-40x-30y+225=0\) is \(G\). The point \(H(32,\,24)\) lies outside \(C\). A point \(P\) moves on \(C\). (a) Find the coordinates of \(G\) and the radius of \(C\). (b) Find \(GH\). (c) Find the greatest possible area of \(\triangle GHP\). (d) Find the perimeter of \(\triangle GHP\) when its area is greatest.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Complete the square: \(x^{2}-40x=(x-20)^{2}-400\) and \(y^{2}-30y=(y-15)^{2}-225\), so \((x-20)^{2}+(y-15)^{2}=400\). Hence \(G=(20,15)\) and the radius is \(20\).
Step 2. \(GH=\sqrt{(32-20)^{2}+(24-15)^{2}}=\sqrt{144+81}=\sqrt{225}=15\).
Step 3. \(\text{area of }\triangle GHP=\tfrac{1}{2}(GH)(GP)\sin\angle HGP\), and this is greatest when \(\sin\angle HGP=1\), i.e. \(GP\perp GH\).
Step 4. The greatest area is \(\tfrac{1}{2}(15)(20)=150\) square units, and then \(\triangle GHP\) is right-angled at \(G\), so \(HP=\sqrt{15^{2}+20^{2}}=\sqrt{625}=25\).
Step 5. The perimeter is \(GH+GP+HP=15+20+25=60\) units.
Answer: (a) \(G(20,15)\), \(r=20\); (b) \(GH=15\); (c) \(150\) square units; (d) \(60\) units
