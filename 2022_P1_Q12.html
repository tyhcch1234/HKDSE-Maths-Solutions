Coordinate Geometry — Circle, centre, distance & greatest area of a triangle

Circle Equation Centre & Radius Distance Formula Maximum Area Perpendicular Radius Pythagoras’ Theorem

Q The Question

The equation of the circle \(C\) is \[x^{2}+y^{2}-154x-128y+224=0.\] Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). The coordinates of the point \(H\) are \((65,48)\).

(a) Find the distance between \(G\) and \(H\). 3 marks

(b) Let \(P\) be a moving point on \(C\). When the area of \(\triangle GHP\) is the greatest,

(i) describe the geometric relationship between \(GH\) and \(GP\); 1 mark
(ii) find the perimeter of \(\triangle GHP\). 3 marks

[Total: 7 marks]

a Finding the Centre \(G\) and the Distance \(GH\)

Centre \(G\) = (77, 64)
Radius \(r\) = 99
\(GH\) = 20
STEP 11M

To find the centre of a circle from its general equation, complete the square in \(x\) and \(y\).

\[ x^{2}+y^{2}-154x-128y+224=0 \] \[ (x^{2}-154x)+(y^{2}-128y)+224=0 \] \[ \left(x^{2}-154x+\left(\tfrac{154}{2}\right)^{2}\right)+\left(y^{2}-128y+\left(\tfrac{128}{2}\right)^{2}\right)+224-\left(\tfrac{154}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\tfrac{128}{2}\right)^{2}=0 \] \[ (x-77)^{2}+(y-64)^{2}=77^{2}+64^{2}-224 \]
Hint: For \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\), the centre is \(\left(-\tfrac{D}{2},-\tfrac{E}{2}\right)\). Here \(D=-154,\;E=-128\).
Centre \(G=\left(\dfrac{154}{2},\dfrac{128}{2}\right)=(77,\,64)\)
STEP 21M

Apply the distance formula between \(G(77,64)\) and \(H(65,48)\).

\[ GH=\sqrt{(77-65)^{2}+(64-48)^{2}} \] \[ GH=\sqrt{12^{2}+16^{2}}=\sqrt{144+256}=\sqrt{400} \]
Hint: The vector \(\overrightarrow{GH}=(-12,-16)\) is a \(3\text{-}4\text{-}5\) right triangle scaled by \(4\): \(12=3\cdot4,\;16=4\cdot4\).
\(GH=\sqrt{400}=20\)
STEP 31A

State the final answer clearly with the correct unit-free value.

\[ GH = 20 \]
Hint: No unit is given in the question, so the answer is simply the number \(20\). Always check if the question asks for an exact value or a rounded one — here it is exact.
The distance between \(G\) and \(H\) is \(20\).

i Part (b)(i) — Relationship between \(GH\) and \(GP\)

\(\angle GPH\) (deg) = 90.0
\(GP\) = 99
Area \(\triangle GHP\) = 990
STEP 11M

The area of \(\triangle GHP\) is greatest when the perpendicular height from \(P\) to the fixed base \(GH\) is greatest.

\[ \text{Area}=\tfrac{1}{2}\times GH \times d(P,GH) \]
Hint: \(GH\) is fixed (length \(20\)), so only the distance from \(P\) to the line \(GH\) can vary. Maximise that distance.
Maximise the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to line \(GH\).
STEP 21A

The distance from \(P\) to the line \(GH\) is greatest when \(P\) is the point on the circle farthest from the line \(GH\). Since the centre \(G\) is itself on the circle, the farthest point from line \(GH\) lies on the line through \(G\) perpendicular to \(GH\).

At that point, \(GP\) (a radius) is perpendicular to the line \(GH\).

\[ GP \perp GH \]
Hint: For a circle, the maximum distance from an external line to a point on the circle is achieved along the normal from the centre — so the radius at that point is perpendicular to the line.
When the area of \(\triangle GHP\) is greatest, \(GH\) is perpendicular to \(GP\).

ii Part (b)(ii) — Perimeter of \(\triangle GHP\)

\(GH\) = 20
\(GP\) = 99
\(HP\) = 101
Perimeter = 220
STEP 11M

First find the radius of circle \(C\), i.e. the length \(GP\) (since \(P\) is on the circle and \(G\) is the centre).

\[ r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{154}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{128}{2}\right)^{2}-224} \] \[ r=\sqrt{77^{2}+64^{2}-224}=\sqrt{5929+4096-224}=\sqrt{9801} \]
Hint: For \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\), radius \(r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}\).
\(r=GP=99\)
STEP 21M

Since \(GH\perp GP\), triangle \(GHP\) is right-angled at \(G\). Use Pythagoras' theorem to find \(HP\).

\[ HP^{2}=GH^{2}+GP^{2} \] \[ HP^{2}=20^{2}+99^{2}=400+9801=10201 \] \[ HP=101 \]
Hint: \(20\text{-}99\text{-}101\) is a Pythagorean triple. Check: \(20^{2}+99^{2}=400+9801=10201=101^{2}\). ✓
\(HP=101\)
STEP 31A

Now sum the three sides of \(\triangle GHP\).

\[ \text{Perimeter}=GH+GP+HP=20+99+101 \] \[ \text{Perimeter}=220 \]
Hint: Always add all three sides: the two perpendicular legs \(GH\) and \(GP\), plus the hypotenuse \(HP\).
The perimeter of \(\triangle GHP\) is \(220\).

✓ Summary of Answers

  • Centre \(G=(77,\,64)\)
  • Radius \(r=GP=99\)
  • (a) \(GH=20\)
  • (b)(i) \(GH\perp GP\) (perpendicular)
  • (b)(ii) \(HP=101\), so perimeter of \(\triangle GHP\) \(=20+99+101=220\)
