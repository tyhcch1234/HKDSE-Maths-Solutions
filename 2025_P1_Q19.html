Showing: Overview
4 + 8 marks Question 19. The equation of the straight line $L$ is $$4x-3y+83=0.$$ The equation of the circle $C$ is $$x^{2}+y^{2}+ax-2y+b=0,$$ where $a$ and $b$ are positive constants. It is given that $C$ passes through the point $(-10,9)$ and $L$ is a tangent to $C$.

(a) Prove that the equation $$25x^{2}+(9a+640)x+(90a+4924)=0$$ has equal roots. Hence, find $a$ and $b$.

(b) The coordinates of the point $P$ are $(-20,1)$. Let $Q$ be a point lying below $L$ such that $PQ$ is tangent to $C$ and $PQ=25$. Denote the centre of $C$ by $I$.
  (i) Find $\cos\angle IPQ$.
  (ii) Find, in surd form, the distance between $I$ and $Q$.
  (iii) Let $R$ be a point lying on $L$ such that $C$ is the inscribed circle of $\triangle PQR$. Denote the radius of the circumcircle of $\triangle PQR$ by $r$. Someone claims that $r>PQ$. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer.

Worked Solution

Step 1 · Use the point $(-10,9)$ on $C$

Since $(-10,9)$ lies on $C$, substitute $x=-10,\ y=9$ into $x^{2}+y^{2}+ax-2y+b=0$:

$$10^{2}+9^{2}-10a-2(9)+b=0$$ $$100+81-10a-18+b=0$$ $$163-10a+b=0.$$

So $b=10a-163$. This gives one relation between $a$ and $b$.

Step 2 · Substitute the line into the circle

From $L:\ 4x-3y+83=0$ we get

$$y=\frac{4x+83}{3}.$$

Substitute into $x^{2}+y^{2}+ax-2y+b=0$ and multiply by $9$:

$$9x^{2}+(4x+83)^{2}+9ax-18(4x+83)+9b=0$$ $$25x^{2}+(9a+640)x+(90a+4924)=0.$$

(using $b=10a-163$).

Step 3 · Tangency ⇒ equal roots ⇒ find $a$, $b$

Since $L$ is tangent to $C$, the line meets the circle at exactly one point, so the above quadratic in $x$ has equal roots:

$$\Delta=(9a+640)^{2}-4(25)(90a+4924)=0$$ $$81a^{2}+11520a+409600-9000a-492400=0$$ $$9a^{2}+280a-9200=0.$$

Factorising: $9a^{2}+280a-9200=9a^{2}+460a-180a-9200$ $=a(9a+460)-20(9a+460)=(a-20)(9a+460)=0$.

Since $a>0$: $a=20$, and $b=10(20)-163=37$.

$a=20,\qquad b=37$
Step 4 · (b)(i) Find $\cos\angle IPQ$

With $a=20,\ b=37$, the circle is $x^{2}+y^{2}+20x-2y+37=0$, i.e. $$(x+10)^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=64,$$ so the centre is $I(-10,1)$ and the radius is $8$.

Since $P(-20,1)$ and $I(-10,1)$ share the same $y$-coordinate, $IP$ is horizontal, with $IP=10$. And $P$ lies on $L$, while $Q$ lies below $L$.

The slope of $L$ is $\frac{4}{3}$, so the tangent of the angle it makes with the negative $x$-direction (along $PI$) satisfies $\tan\angle IPQ=\frac{4}{3}$ $\Rightarrow \cos\angle IPQ=\frac{3}{5}$.

$\cos\angle IPQ=\dfrac{3}{5}$
Step 5 · (b)(ii) Distance $IQ$ (cosine rule)

In $\triangle IPQ$: $IP=10$, $PQ=25$, $\cos\angle IPQ=\frac{3}{5}$. By the cosine formula,

$$IQ^{2}=IP^{2}+PQ^{2}-2(IP)(PQ)\cos\angle IPQ$$ $$IQ^{2}=10^{2}+25^{2}-2(10)(25)\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)$$ $$IQ^{2}=100+625-300=425.$$ $$IQ=\sqrt{425}=5\sqrt{17}.$$
$IQ=5\sqrt{17}$
Step 6 · (b)(iii) Locate $R$, the apex of $\triangle PQR$

$PQ$ and $PR$ are the two tangents from $P$, so the incircle of $\triangle PQR$ is exactly $C$, centre $I$, radius $8$. The base $QR$ lies on the tangent $L$.

By the sine formula in $\triangle IPQ$:

$$\frac{\sin\angle IQP}{IP}=\frac{\sin\angle IPQ}{IQ} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\sin\angle IQP}{10}=\frac{4/5}{5\sqrt{17}}$$ $$\angle IQP\approx 22.8337^\circ \quad(\text{or }157.1663^\circ\text{ rejected}).$$

By symmetry about the line $PI$, $\angle GQR=360^\circ-2(2\angle IPQ) \approx 147.4796^\circ$ where $G$ is the circumcentre. Then

$$\angle GQP=\frac{180^\circ-\angle GQR}{2}+2\angle IQP \approx 61.9275^\circ .$$
Step 7 · Compare $r$ with $PQ$ (claim check)

In the right-angled setup at $G$, $\cos\angle GQP=\dfrac{PQ}{2r}$, so

$$r=\frac{PQ}{2\cos\angle GQP}.$$

Now $60^\circ<\angle GQP<90^\circ$, hence $0<2\cos\angle GQP<1$, giving

$$r=\frac{PQ}{2\cos\angle GQP}>\frac{PQ}{1}=PQ.$$
The claim is correct: $r>PQ$. (With $PQ=25$, $r\approx 26.85>25$.)
Exam Tips
  • Tangency = equal roots. Always write $\Delta=0$ after substituting the line into the circle.
  • Pick the positive root — the question states $a,b>0$.
  • Complete the square to read off the centre and radius: $x^{2}+y^{2}+20x-2y+37=0\Rightarrow (x+10)^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=64$.
  • In part (iii), use the inscribed-circle fact to identify the tangents, then relate $r$ to $\cos\angle GQP$ — a small angle gives a large $r$.
