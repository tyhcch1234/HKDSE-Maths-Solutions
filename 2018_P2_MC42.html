HKDSE Math 2018 Paper 2 Q42
Probability Combinations Counting

Question

In a class, there are 14 boys and 15 girls. If 3 students of the same gender are selected from the class to form a team, how many different teams can be formed?

Step-by-Step Solution

1 Understand the requirement: “same gender” ▶

The team must consist of 3 boys OR 3 girls. These two cases cannot happen together.

So we count the number of ways for each case and add them.

💡 Key idea: mutually exclusive cases → addition principle
2 Count the number of ways to choose 3 boys from 14 ▶
🔑 Formula: \( C_r^n = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \)

We are choosing 3 boys out of 14, order does not matter.

\[ C_3^{14} = \frac{14!}{3! \times 11!} = \frac{14 \times 13 \times 12}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 364 \]

So there are 364 ways to form a team of 3 boys.

3 Count the number of ways to choose 3 girls from 15 ▶
🔑 Same combination formula

Now choose 3 girls out of 15.

\[ C_3^{15} = \frac{15!}{3! \times 12!} = \frac{15 \times 14 \times 13}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 455 \]

So there are 455 ways to form a team of 3 girls.

4 Add the two possibilities together ▶

Since the team can be all boys OR all girls, we add the two counts.

\[ \text{Total} = C_3^{14} + C_3^{15} = 364 + 455 = 819 \]
✅ The answer is 819 (Option A).
