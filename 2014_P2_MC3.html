💡 Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1
\[ px(x-1) + x^2 \equiv qx(x-2) + 4x \]

The identity holds for all values of \(x\). Choose a value that makes the terms simple.

💡 Try \(x=2\). Then \(x-2 = 0\), which eliminates the \(q\) term — perfect for isolating \(p\).
Step 2
\[ p(2)(2-1) + (2)^2 = q(2)(2-2) + 4(2) \]

Substitute \(x=2\) into both sides.

Step 3
\[ 2p(1) + 4 = q(2)(0) + 8 \]
\[ 2p + 4 = 8 \]

Simplify. The term containing \(q\) becomes \(0\).

Step 4
\[ 2p = 4 \]
\[ p = 2 \]

Solve for \(p\). The answer is B.

Final Answer
\(p = 2\)  (Option B)
Step 1
\[ px(x-1) + x^2 \equiv qx(x-2) + 4x \]

Expand both sides and group like powers of \(x\).

Step 2
\[ px^2 - px + x^2 \equiv qx^2 - 2qx + 4x \]
\[ (p+1)x^2 - px \equiv qx^2 + (-2q+4)x \]
💡 For two polynomials to be identical, the coefficients of each corresponding power of \(x\) must match.
Step 3
\[ \begin{cases} p+1 = q & \cdots (1) \\ -p = -2q+4 & \cdots (2) \end{cases} \]

Compare coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(x\) respectively.

Step 4
\[ (1) \times 2 + (2): \; p + 2 = 4 \]
\[ p = 2 \]

Eliminate \(q\) and solve for \(p\). The answer is B.

Final Answer
\(p = 2\)  (Option B)
