Trapezium edges AE (mid-segment) BE (right angle) CE (right angle) Vertices Right angles
In the figure, $ABCD$ is a trapezium with $AD // BC$. Let $E$ be the mid-point of $AD$. It is given that $\angle ABE = \angle BCE = 90^\circ$.

Find $\dfrac{CE}{DE}$.
A. $\dfrac{1}{2}$ B. $1$ C. $\tan\alpha$ D. $\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$
💡 Hint: Since $AD // BC$ and $\angle BCE = 90^\circ$, the transversal $EC$ gives you a $90^\circ$ angle at $E$ too — then chase angles in $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle BCE$.
Step 1 · Angle chase at E
Interior Angles of Parallel Lines \(\angle AEC+\angle BCE=180^\circ\) \(AD // BC\), so co-interior angles on the same side of a transversal are supplementary.

Since $AD // BC$ and $\angle BCE = 90^\circ$, the co-interior angle gives

$$\angle AEC = 180^\circ - \angle BCE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ.$$

(int. $\angle$s, $AD // BC$)

Step 2 · $\angle BEC = \alpha$

In $\triangle ABE$, the angle sum gives $\angle AEB = 180^\circ - \angle ABE - \angle BAE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \alpha = 90^\circ - \alpha.$

Since $A$, $E$, $D$ are collinear, $\angle AEC = 180^\circ$, so

$$\angle BEC = 180^\circ - \angle AEB - \angle BEC? \quad\Rightarrow\quad \angle BEC = 90^\circ - (90^\circ - \alpha) = \alpha.$$

Step 3 · Ratio in $\triangle ABE$
Sine Ratio \(\sin\angle BAE=\dfrac{BE}{AE}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle ABE\), \(BE\) is opposite \(\angle BAE\) and \(AE\) is the hypotenuse.

In the right triangle $\triangle ABE$ (right angle at $B$), with $\angle BAE = \alpha$,

$$\frac{BE}{AE} = \sin\angle BAE = \sin\alpha.$$

Step 4 · Ratio in $\triangle BCE$
Cosine Ratio \(\cos\angle BEC=\dfrac{CE}{BE}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle BCE\), \(CE\) is adjacent to \(\angle BEC\) and \(BE\) is the hypotenuse.

In the right triangle $\triangle BCE$ (right angle at $C$), with $\angle BEC = \alpha$,

$$\frac{CE}{BE} = \cos\angle BEC = \cos\alpha.$$

Step 5 · Combine the ratios

Since $E$ is the mid-point of $AD$, $DE = AE$. Thus

$$\frac{CE}{DE} = \frac{CE}{AE} = \frac{BE}{AE}\cdot\frac{CE}{BE} = \sin\alpha\cdot\cos\alpha.$$

Step 6 · Answer
Answer $$\frac{CE}{DE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\text{D}}$$

Question. In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a trapezium with \(AD // BC\), and \(E\) is the mid-point of \(AD\). It is given that \(\angle ABE=\angle BCE=90^\circ\) and \(\angle BAE=35^\circ\). If \(AE=10\), find \(CE\) correct to 3 significant figures.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(\angle AEC=180^\circ-\angle BCE=90^\circ\) (int. \(\angle\)s, \(AD // BC\)).
Step 2. In \(\triangle ABE\): \(\angle AEB=90^\circ-35^\circ=55^\circ\), so on the straight line \(AED\), \(\angle BEC=90^\circ-55^\circ=35^\circ\).
Step 3. \(BE=AE\sin 35^\circ=10\sin 35^\circ\approx 5.736\).
Step 4. \(CE=BE\cos 35^\circ\approx 5.736\times 0.81915\approx 4.70\).
Step 5. Check: \(CE=AE\sin 35^\circ\cos 35^\circ=5\sin 70^\circ\approx 4.70\) ✓
Answer: \(CE\approx 4.70\)
