2020 Paper 2 · Question 4

Topic 4 · Polynomials

Factorization Common Factor Algebraic Manipulation

Simplify the following expression:

\[ (3a+2b)(4a-5b)-a(6a+4b)=\ ? \]

Choose the correct answer:

A. \((3a+2b)(2a-5b)\)
B. \((3a+2b)(6a-5b)\)
C. \((3a-2b)(2a+5b)\)
D. \((3a-2b)(6a+5b)\)

✓ Step-by-Step Solution

0 / 4 steps

Look carefully at the second term: \(a(6a+4b)\).

Notice that we can factor out \(2a\):

\[ a(6a+4b) = 2a(3a+2b) \]

💡 Hint: \(6a+4b = 2(3a+2b)\). By pulling out \(2\), the expression \((3a+2b)\) magically appears!

Substitute the factored form back into the original expression:

\[ (3a+2b)(4a-5b) - 2a(3a+2b) \]

Now both terms share the common factor \((3a+2b)\).

Apply the distributive law in reverse (taking out the common factor):

\[ (3a+2b)\big[(4a-5b) - 2a\big] \]

💡 Hint: Think of it as \(X \cdot Y - X \cdot Z = X(Y - Z)\), where \(X = (3a+2b)\), \(Y = (4a-5b)\), and \(Z = 2a\).

Simplify the terms inside the square bracket:

\[ (4a - 5b) - 2a = 4a - 5b - 2a = 2a - 5b \]

Therefore, the fully simplified expression is:

\[ (3a+2b)(2a-5b) \]

✅ This matches option A.

🔑 Key Takeaway

When you see two terms that look different, always ask: "Can I rewrite one of them so a common factor appears?" Spotting \(6a+4b = 2(3a+2b)\) is the key manoeuvre. This technique is called factoring by grouping / extracting a common factor, and it appears very often in HKDSE Paper 2.
