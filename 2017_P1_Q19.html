QQuestion 13 marks

\(ABC\) is a thin triangular metal sheet, where \(BC = 24\text{ cm}\), \(\angle BAC = 30^\circ\) and \(\angle ACB = 42^\circ\).

(a) Find the length of \(AC\). [2 marks]
(b) In Figure 2, the thin metal sheet \(ABC\) is held such that only the vertex \(B\) lies on the horizontal ground. \(D\) and \(E\) are points lying on the horizontal ground vertically below the vertices \(A\) and \(C\) respectively. \(AC\) produced meets the horizontal ground at the point \(F\). A craftsman finds that \(AD = 10\text{ cm}\) and \(CE = 2\text{ cm}\).
(i) Find the distance between \(C\) and \(F\).
(ii) Find the area of \(\Delta ABF\).
(iii) Find the inclination of the thin metal sheet \(ABC\) to the horizontal ground.
(iv) The craftsman claims that the area of \(\Delta BDF\) is greater than \(460\text{ cm}^2\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. [11 marks]

Step 11M

First find the third angle of the triangle using the angle sum of a triangle:

\[ \angle ABC = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 42^\circ = 108^\circ \]
Hint: The angles of a triangle always add up to \(180^\circ\). Knowing all three angles lets us apply the sine formula immediately.
Step 21A

Apply the sine formula \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}\):

\[ \frac{AC}{\sin \angle ABC} = \frac{BC}{\sin \angle BAC} \]
\[ \frac{AC}{\sin 108^\circ} = \frac{24}{\sin 30^\circ} \]
\[ AC = \frac{24 \sin 108^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ} \approx 45.6507 \text{ cm} \]
Answer: \(AC \approx 45.7\text{ cm}\) (correct to 3 significant figures).
Hint: Keep the full value \(AC \approx 45.6507\) on your calculator — you will need it again in parts (b)(ii) and (b)(iii).

Step 11M

Since \(AD\) and \(CE\) are both vertical and \(F\), \(C\), \(A\) are collinear, the two right-angled triangles are similar:

\[ \Delta ADF \sim \Delta CEF \quad (\text{AA}) \]

Therefore the ratios of corresponding sides are equal:

\[ \frac{AF}{CF} = \frac{AD}{CE} = \frac{10}{2} = 5 \]

But \(AF = AC + CF\), so:

\[ \frac{AC + CF}{CF} = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; AC + CF = 5\,CF \;\Rightarrow\; CF = \frac{1}{4}AC \]
Hint: Make sure you interpret \(AF = AC + CF\) correctly — \(C\) lies between \(A\) and \(F\) because \(AC\) is produced to meet the ground at \(F\).
Step 21A

Substitute the value of \(AC\) found in part (a):

\[ CF = \frac{1}{4}(45.65071278) \approx 11.412678 \text{ cm} \]
Answer: \(CF \approx 11.4\text{ cm}\) (correct to 3 significant figures).
Hint: The similar-triangle ratio is a powerful shortcut — no need to find \(AF\) or the height first.

Step 11M

We use the formula \(\text{Area} = \tfrac12 \cdot (\text{side}_1) \cdot (\text{side}_2) \cdot \sin(\text{included angle})\).

Take the base \(AF\) and the side \(BC\) with the included angle \(\angle ACB = 42^\circ\):

\[ \text{Area of } \Delta ABF = \frac{1}{2}(AF)(BC)\sin \angle ACB = \frac{1}{2}(AC + CF)(BC)\sin \angle ACB \]
Hint: \(B\) is a common vertex for both \(\Delta ABC\) and \(\Delta ABF\), so the height from \(B\) to line \(AF\) is the same in both triangles. This is why we can use \(AF\) and \(\angle ACB\).
Step 21A

Substitute the values \(AC \approx 45.65071278\), \(CF \approx 11.412678\), \(BC = 24\):

\[ \text{Area} \approx \frac{1}{2}(45.65071278 + 11.412678)(24)\sin 42^\circ \]
\[ \approx \frac{1}{2}(57.063391)(24)(0.6691306) \approx 458.1943 \text{ cm}^2 \]
Answer: The area of \(\Delta ABF\) is approximately \(458\text{ cm}^2\) (correct to 3 significant figures).
Hint: Store the intermediate value in your calculator's memory to avoid rounding errors. The exact answer is about \(458.19 \text{ cm}^2\).

Step 11M

Since \(A\), \(C\), \(F\) are collinear in the plane of the sheet, the angle between \(BC\) and \(CF\) is the same as \(\angle BCA\) of the triangle:

\[ \angle BCF = 180^\circ - \angle BCA = 180^\circ - 42^\circ = 138^\circ \]
Hint: \(B\), \(C\), \(F\) lie in the slanted plane. \(\angle BCF\) is on the plane itself, not on the ground.
Step 21M

Apply the cosine formula in \(\Delta BCF\) on the slanted plane:

\[ BF^2 = BC^2 + CF^2 - 2(BC)(CF)\cos \angle BCF \]
\[ BF^2 \approx 24^2 + (11.412678)^2 - 2(24)(11.412678)\cos 138^\circ \approx 1113.35 \]
\[ BF \approx 33.3669 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(\cos 138^\circ < 0\), so the term \(-2(BC)(CF)\cos 138^\circ\) becomes positive, making \(BF\) larger than both \(BC\) and \(CF\).
Step 31M

Let \(P\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(FB\) produced. Then the area of \(\Delta ABF\) (computed in (b)(ii)) equals \(\tfrac12 (AP)(BF)\). Hence:

\[ AP = \frac{2 \times \text{Area of } \Delta ABF}{BF} \approx \frac{2(458.1943)}{33.3669} \approx 27.4640 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: We use two different expressions for the same area of \(\Delta ABF\): one from (b)(ii) with base \(AF\), and one here with base \(BF\).
Step 41M

Since \(AD\) is the vertical height of \(A\) above the ground, the angle of inclination of the plane \(ABC\) to the horizontal is \(\angle APD\). In the right-angled triangle \(ADP\):

\[ \sin \angle APD = \frac{AD}{AP} = \frac{10}{27.4640} \approx 0.36411 \]
Hint: \(AP\) lies on the horizontal ground (since \(P\) is on \(FB\) which is on the ground). \(AD\) is vertical. So \(\angle APD\) measures the tilt of the plane.
Step 51A

Finally, take the inverse sine:

\[ \angle APD = \sin^{-1}(0.36411) \approx 21.355^\circ \]
Answer: The inclination is approximately \(21.4^\circ\) (correct to 3 significant figures).
Hint: The inclination is always between \(0^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\), so the principal value of \(\sin^{-1}\) is the correct one.

Step 11M

\(\Delta BDF\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\Delta ABF\) onto the horizontal ground, so:

\[ \text{Area of } \Delta BDF = (\text{Area of } \Delta ABF)\cos \angle APD \]
\[ \approx 458.1943 \times \cos 21.355^\circ \approx 458.1943 \times 0.9311 \approx 426.6 \text{ cm}^2 \]
Hint: Projecting a plane figure onto a horizontal plane always multiplies the area by \(\cos(\text{inclination})\), which is less than 1.
Step 21A

Since the projection factor \(\cos \angle APD \le 1\):

\[ \text{Area of } \Delta BDF = (\text{Area of } \Delta ABF)\cos\angle APD \le \text{Area of } \Delta ABF \approx 458 \text{ cm}^2 \]
\[ 458 \text{ cm}^2 < 460 \text{ cm}^2 \]
Answer: The claim is disagreed. The area of \(\Delta BDF\) is less than \(460\text{ cm}^2\).
Hint: There is no need to compute the area of \(\Delta BDF\) precisely — the projection argument gives the inequality directly, which is exactly what the question asks for.
