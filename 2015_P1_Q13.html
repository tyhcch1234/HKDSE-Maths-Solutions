Geometry — Congruent Triangles, Right Angles & Lengths in a Square

Congruent Triangles RHS Condition Perpendicular Lines Pythagoras' Theorem

QQuestion

In Figure 2, \(ABCD\) is a square. \(E\) and \(F\) are points lying on \(BC\) and \(CD\) respectively such that \(AE = BF\). \(AE\) and \(BF\) intersect at \(G\).

(a) Prove that \(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF\). 2 marks

(b) Is \(\triangle BGE\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer. 3 marks

(c) If \(CF = 15\text{ cm}\) and \(EG = 9\text{ cm}\), find \(BG\). 2 marks

Figure 2 — drag the slider to move \(E\) along \(BC\); \(F\) follows automatically so that \(AE = BF\).

aPart (a) — Prove \(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF\)

\(BE = \) 15.0 cm \(CF = \) 15.0 cm \(AE = \) 25.0 cm \(BF = \) 25.0 cm \(AB = BC = 20.0\) cm

At every position, \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle BCF\) remain congruent.

Step 11M

Identify the equal sides from the square and the given condition:

\[ AB = BC \quad (\text{sides of square}) \] \[ AE = BF \quad (\text{given}) \]
Hint: Always start with what the square gives you — all four sides equal, all four angles \(90^\circ\).
Step 21A

Identify the included right angle:

\[ \angle ABE = 90^\circ = \angle BCF \quad (\text{angles of square}) \]

Therefore:

\[ \triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF \quad (\text{RHS}) \]
Hint: RHS = Right angle, Hypotenuse, Side. Here the hypotenuses are \(AE\) and \(BF\); the sides are \(AB\) and \(BC\).
\(\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF\) (RHS)

bPart (b) — Is \(\triangle BGE\) right-angled?

\(\angle BAE = \) 36.9° \(\angle CBF = \) 36.9° \(\angle AEB = \) 53.1° \(\angle BGE = \) 90.0°

No matter how \(E\) slides, \(\angle BGE\) stays exactly \(90^\circ\) — \(\triangle BGE\) is always right-angled at \(G\).

Step 11M

From part (a), corresponding angles of the congruent triangles are equal:

\[ \angle BAE = \angle CBF \]
Hint: Congruence is powerful — it gives you three pairs of equal angles, not just equal sides.
Step 21M

In \(\triangle ABE\), since \(\angle ABE = 90^\circ\):

\[ \angle AEB = 180^\circ - \angle ABE - \angle BAE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \angle BAE = 90^\circ - \angle BAE \]
Hint: Angle sum of a triangle is \(180^\circ\). Using the right angle at \(B\) saves work.
Step 31A

In \(\triangle BGE\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \angle BGE &= 180^\circ - \angle CBF - \angle AEB \\ &= 180^\circ - \angle BAE - (90^\circ - \angle BAE) \\ &= 90^\circ \end{aligned} \]

Hence \(\triangle BGE\) is a right-angled triangle (right angle at \(G\)).

Hint: The \(\angle BAE\) terms cancel — that's why the result is independent of where \(E\) is placed.
Yes. \(\angle BGE = 90^\circ\), so \(\triangle BGE\) is right-angled at \(G\).
0 / 3

cPart (c) — Given \(CF = 15\text{ cm}\) and \(EG = 9\text{ cm}\), find \(BG\)

\(BE = CF = \) 15.0 cm \(EG = \) 9.0 cm \(BG = \sqrt{BE^2 - EG^2} = \) 12.0 cm

Since \(\angle BGE = 90^\circ\), Pythagoras' theorem applies directly to \(\triangle BGE\).

Step 11M

From the congruence in part (a), corresponding sides are equal:

\[ BE = CF = 15\text{ cm} \]

From part (b), \(\angle BGE = 90^\circ\), so \(\triangle BGE\) is right-angled at \(G\).

Hint: The hypotenuse of \(\triangle BGE\) is \(BE\), because the right angle is at \(G\).
Step 21A

By Pythagoras' theorem in \(\triangle BGE\):

\[ \begin{aligned} BG &= \sqrt{BE^2 - EG^2} \\ &= \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} \\ &= \sqrt{225 - 81} \\ &= \sqrt{144} \\ &= 12\text{ cm} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: \(9\text{–}12\text{–}15\) is a \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) Pythagorean triple scaled by 3.
\(BG = 12\text{ cm}\)
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(AB = BC\) (square), \(AE = BF\) (given), \(\angle ABE = 90^\circ = \angle BCF\) (square) \(\Rightarrow \triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF\) (RHS).
  • (b) Yes. \(\angle BAE = \angle CBF\) and \(\angle AEB = 90^\circ - \angle BAE\), so \(\angle BGE = 180^\circ - \angle CBF - \angle AEB = 90^\circ\). Hence \(\triangle BGE\) is right-angled at \(G\).
  • (c) \(BE = CF = 15\text{ cm}\), so \(BG = \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = 12\text{ cm}\).
