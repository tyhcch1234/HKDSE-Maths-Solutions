Question

[2015 #13] In Figure 2, $ABCD$ is a square. $E$ and $F$ are points lying on $BC$ and $CD$ respectively such that $AE = BF$. $AE$ and $BF$ intersect at $G$.

(a) Prove that $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF$.  [2 marks]

(b) Is $\triangle BGE$ a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer.  [3 marks]

(c) If $CF = 15\text{ cm}$ and $EG = 9\text{ cm}$, find $BG$.  [2 marks]

💡 Key idea: A square gives you equal sides and right angles everywhere — that is all you need for an RHS congruence. After that, chase angles through the congruent triangles.
STEP 1 · (a) Congruence data

In square $ABCD$, every side is equal and every interior angle is $90^\circ$.

$AB = BC \quad$ (sides of square)
$AE = BF \quad$ (given)
$\angle ABE = 90^\circ = \angle BCF \quad$ (property of square)

So the two right-angled triangles $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle BCF$ have equal hypotenuse and one equal leg.

STEP 2 · (a) Conclusion
RHS Congruence Test \(AB=BC,\quad AE=BF,\quad \angle ABE=\angle BCF=90^{\circ}\ \Longrightarrow\ \triangle ABE\cong\triangle BCF\) The two right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuses \(AE=BF\) and one equal leg \(AB=BC\).

Therefore by the RHS test:

$\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF$   (RHS)

Consequently the corresponding angles are equal:

$\angle BAE = \angle CBF \qquad \angle AEB = \angle BFC$
STEP 3 · (b) Angle chase, part 1
Angle Sum of a Triangle \(\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^{\circ}\) In the right-angled \(\triangle ABE\) this gives \(\angle AEB=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}-\angle BAE=90^{\circ}-\angle BAE\).

In right-angled $\triangle ABE$, the two acute angles are complementary:

$\angle AEB = 180^\circ - \angle ABE - \angle BAE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \angle BAE$
$\phantom{\angle AEB} = 90^\circ - \angle BAE$

Replacing $\angle BAE$ by $\angle CBF$ (from Step 2), we get

$\angle AEB = 90^\circ - \angle CBF$
STEP 4 · (b) Angle chase, part 2

Now look at $\triangle BGE$. Angles on a straight line at $E$ give $\angle BGE$ directly through the angle sum of the triangle:

$\angle BGE = 180^\circ - \angle CBF - \angle AEB$
$\phantom{\angle BGE} = 180^\circ - \angle BAE - (90^\circ - \angle BAE)$
$\phantom{\angle BGE} = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$

Hence $BG \perp AE$, so $\triangle BGE$ is a right-angled triangle (right angle at $G$).

STEP 5 · (c) Set up the lengths

Since $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF$, corresponding sides are equal:

$BE = CF = 15\text{ cm}$

From (b), $\angle BGE = 90^\circ$, so $\triangle BGE$ is right-angled at $G$ with hypotenuse $BE$ and legs $BG,\,EG$.

STEP 6 · (c) Pythagoras
Pythagoras Theorem \(c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}\) \(\triangle BGE\) is right-angled at \(G\) with hypotenuse \(BE=15\) and leg \(EG=9\), so \(BG^{2}=15^{2}-9^{2}\).
$BG^2 + EG^2 = BE^2$
$BG = \sqrt{BE^2 - EG^2} = \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2}$
$\phantom{BG} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144} = 12$
Final Answer (a) $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCF$ (RHS)  ·  (b) Yes, $\angle BGE = 90^\circ$  ·  (c) $BG = 12\text{ cm}$

Question. In square \(ABCD\), \(E\) is a point on \(BC\) and \(F\) is a point on \(CD\) such that \(AE=BF\). The line segments \(AE\) and \(BF\) meet at \(G\). It is given that \(BE=CF=17\text{ cm}\) and \(EG=8\text{ cm}\).
(a) Prove that \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle BCF\).
(b) Prove that \(\angle BGE=90^{\circ}\).
(c) Find \(BG\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. (a) In square \(ABCD\), \(AB=BC\) (sides of a square) and \(\angle ABE=\angle BCF=90^{\circ}\) (interior angles of a square). Together with the given \(AE=BF\), the two right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuse and one equal leg,
so \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle BCF\) (RHS).
Step 2. (b) From the congruence, \(\angle BAE=\angle CBF\). In the right-angled \(\triangle ABE\), \(\angle AEB=180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}-\angle BAE=90^{\circ}-\angle CBF\).
Step 3. In \(\triangle BGE\): \(\angle BGE=180^{\circ}-\angle CBF-\angle AEB=180^{\circ}-\angle CBF-(90^{\circ}-\angle CBF)=90^{\circ}\). Hence \(BG\perp AE\).
Step 4. (c) \(BE=CF=17\text{ cm}\) by the congruence, and \(\triangle BGE\) is right-angled at \(G\) with hypotenuse \(BE\).
Step 5. \(BG=\sqrt{BE^{2}-EG^{2}}=\sqrt{17^{2}-8^{2}}=\sqrt{289-64}=\sqrt{225}=15\text{ cm}\).
Answer: (a) \(\triangle ABE\cong\triangle BCF\) (RHS) (b) \(\angle BGE=90^{\circ}\) (c) \(BG=15\text{ cm}\)
