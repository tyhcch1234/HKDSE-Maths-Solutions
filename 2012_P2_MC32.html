HKDSE Math 2012 Paper 2 · Q32

Linear relation between \(x\) and \(\log_3 y\)

Topic 8 — Laws of Indices, Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Logarithmic Functions Laws of Logarithms Straight-line Graph Slope / Gradient Base 3

Question

The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between \(x\) and \(\log_3 y\). If \(y = m n^{x}\), then \(n =\)
A \(\dfrac{1}{81}\)
B \(\dfrac{1}{9}\)
C \(9\)
D \(81\)
Hint: Try the guided steps below first — then reveal the answer.

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

The straight line cuts the axes at two easy–to–read points:

  • \(x\)-intercept: \((-2,\,0)\)
  • \(y\)-intercept: \((0,\,4)\) — here the vertical coordinate is \(\log_3 y\).
Hint: A straight line only needs two points to determine its slope.

Because the vertical axis is \(\log_3 y\), take \(\log_3\) on both sides of \(y=mn^{x}\):

\[\log_3 y=\log_3\!\left(mn^{x}\right)\]
Hint — Product Law: \(\log_3(AB)=\log_3 A+\log_3 B\), so \(\log_3(mn^x)=\log_3 m+\log_3 n^{x}\).

Bring the exponent \(x\) down:

\[\log_3 n^{x}=x\log_3 n\]

So \(\log_3 y=\log_3 m+x\log_3 n\), which is a linear function of \(x\) with

\[\text{slope}=\log_3 n,\qquad y\text{-intercept}=\log_3 m.\]
Hint: This is exactly the "linear relation" the question mentions — the slope of the \((\log_3 y)\)–\(x\) graph equals \(\log_3 n\).

Slope \(=\dfrac{\text{change in }(\log_3 y)}{\text{change in }x}\):

\[\log_3 n=\frac{4-0}{0-(-2)}=\frac{4}{2}=2\]
Hint: Substitute the two points \((-2,0)\) and \((0,4)\) carefully — mind the double negative in the denominator.

By the definition of a logarithm:

\[\log_3 n=2 \;\Longrightarrow\; n=3^{2}=9\]
Answer: \(n = 9\)  →  Option C
Hint: \(\log_b a = c \iff a=b^{c}\). Don't confuse this with \(n=2^{3}\)! The base of the log is \(3\), not \(2\).

Key Takeaways

  • Taking \(\log_3\) turns an exponential relation \(y=mn^{x}\) into a linear relation \(\log_3 y=\log_3 m+x\log_3 n\).
  • The slope of the \((\log_3 y)\)–\(x\) graph is \(\log_3 n\) (not \(n\) itself!).
  • Convert the slope back with \(n=3^{\text{slope}}\). Here \(n=3^{2}=9\).
  • Common trap: choosing \(n=3^{2}\) vs. \(n=2^{3}\). Always identify the base of the log first.
