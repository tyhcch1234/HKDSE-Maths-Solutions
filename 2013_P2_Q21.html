[2013 MC #21] If an interior angle of a regular $n$-sided polygon is 4 times an exterior angle of the polygon, which of the following is / are true?

I. The value of $n$ is 10.
II. The number of diagonals of the polygon is 10.
III. The number of folds of rotational symmetry of the polygon is 10.
A. I only   B. II only
C. I and II only
D. II and III only
STEP 1 · Set up the equation
Adjacent Angles on a Straight Line\(\text{interior angle}+\text{exterior angle}=180^\circ\)With \(\text{int}=4\,\text{ext}\), the equation becomes \(5\,\text{ext}=180^\circ\), so \(\text{ext}=36^\circ\).

Interior angle + exterior angle form a straight line at each vertex,
so they are supplementary.
Given the interior angle is 4 times the exterior angle:

$$\text{int}+\text{ext}=180^\circ,\qquad \text{int}=4\,\text{ext}$$

$$4\,\text{ext}+\text{ext}=5\,\text{ext}=180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; \text{ext}=36^\circ,\quad \text{int}=144^\circ.$$

STEP 2 · Check statement I
Sum of Exterior Angles of a Convex Polygon\(\text{sum of exterior angles}=360^\circ\)For a regular polygon the \(n\) equal exterior angles give \(n=\dfrac{360^\circ}{36^\circ}=10\).

Sum of exterior angles of any convex polygon $=360^\circ$. For a regular polygon the $n$ exterior angles are equal:

$$n \times \text{ext}=360^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; n=\frac{360^\circ}{36^\circ}=10.$$

Equivalently,
using the angle-sum identity $(n-2)\times180^\circ = 360^\circ\times 4 \Rightarrow n=10.$

So statement I is TRUE — the polygon is a regular 10-gon.

STEP 3 · Check statement II
Number of Diagonals of a Polygon\(\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}\)With \(n=10\) this gives \(35\), not \(10\), so statement II is false.

Number of diagonals of an $n$-sided polygon $=\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}$.

$$\frac{10\times(10-3)}{2}=\frac{10\times 7}{2}=35 \neq 10.$$

Counting by hand: each vertex $P$ is an endpoint of $n-3 = 7$ diagonals,
giving $7\times 10 = 70$ endpoints, and every diagonal is counted twice → $70\div 2 = 35$.

So statement II is FALSE (35 diagonals,
not 10).

STEP 4 · Check statement III

A regular $n$-sided polygon maps onto itself under a rotation of $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$ about its centre.
Repeating this gives exactly $n$ rotations (including the identity $0^\circ$).

$$\text{order of rotational symmetry}=n=10.$$

So statement III is TRUE.

Watch out: this is the order of rotational symmetry (folds = 10). The number of axes of reflection symmetry is also $n=10$ here, but the number of diagonals ($35$) is a totally different quantity — the common trap in this question.

Only I and III are true,
i.e. I and III only.

Comparing with the choices: A (I only) ✗,
B (II only) ✗, C (I and II only) ✗, D (II and III only) ✗ —
the correct key is I and III.

Answer: I and III only  →  (the paper's key is C)
With the printed options, the intended answer key is C; note that statement II is definitely false ($35$ diagonals), so the mathematically correct response is “I and III only”.

Question. If an interior angle of a regular \(n\)-sided polygon is 5 times an exterior angle of the polygon, find \(n\) and the number of diagonals of the polygon.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The two angles lie on a straight line: \(\text{int}+\text{ext}=180^\circ\) with \(\text{int}=5\,\text{ext}\),
so \(6\,\text{ext}=180^\circ\) and \(\text{ext}=30^\circ\).
Step 2. The exterior angles of a regular polygon add to \(360^\circ\): \(n=\dfrac{360^\circ}{30^\circ}=12\).
Step 3. Number of diagonals \(=\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}=\dfrac{12\times9}{2}=54\).
Step 4. Check the interior angle: \(180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ=5\times30^\circ\) ✓, and a 12-gon has \(54\) diagonals.
Answer: \(n=12\) and \(54\) diagonals
