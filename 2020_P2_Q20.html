Question

In the figure, ABC is an equilateral triangle and CDE is an isosceles triangle with CD = CE. If $\angle DCE = 78^\circ$ and $\angle ADC = \angle CAD = 40^\circ$, then $\angle CBE =$
A. $14^\circ$
B. $19^\circ$
C. $24^\circ$
D. $29^\circ$

Solution

STEP 1 · Triangle ACD
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle \(\angle B=\angle C\iff AC=AB\) \(\angle ADC=\angle CAD=40^\circ\) forces \(AC=CD\), and \(\angle ACD=180^\circ-40^\circ-40^\circ=100^\circ\).

In $\triangle ACD$: $\angle ADC = \angle CAD = 40^\circ$ (given).

∴ $CD = AC$ (sides opposite equal angles).

$\angle ACD = 180^\circ - 40^\circ - 40^\circ = \mathbf{100^\circ}$ (∠ sum of $\triangle$).

Hint: equal base angles ⇒ equal opposite sides. So $AC = CD$.
STEP 2 · Collect equal lengths

$ABC$ is equilateral ⇒ $AC = BC$ and $\angle ABC = 60^\circ$.

From Step 1, $AC = CD$. Also given $CD = CE$.

∴ $\;AC = BC = CD = CE$ — all four points $A, B, D, E$ lie on one circle centred at $C$.

STEP 3 · Angles at the centre

At centre $C$: $\angle ACD = 100^\circ$ and $\angle DCE = 78^\circ$.

Going round $C$, the remaining reflex is $\angle ACE = 100^\circ + 78^\circ = \mathbf{178^\circ}$ (the reflex $360^\circ-178^\circ=182^\circ$ is the minor arc measure we compare).

Use the arc $AE$ (the reflex angle at the centre) to relate $A$ and $E$.
STEP 4 · ∠ at centre twice ∠ at circumference
Angle at the Centre Twice the Angle at the Circumference \(\angle\text{ at centre}=2\times\angle\text{ at circumference}\) \(A,B,E\) lie on the circle centred at \(C\), so \(2\angle ABE=178^\circ\), the reflex \(\angle ACE\).

Points $A, B, E$ are on the circle (centre $C$). The angle subtended by arc $AE$ at the centre is the reflex $\angle ACE = 100^\circ + 78^\circ = 178^\circ$.

At the circumference $B$ on the minor arc, the inscribed angle is half the major arc: $\angle ABE = \tfrac{1}{2}(360^\circ-178^\circ)$ — but $B$ lies on the minor arc, so we instead use the inscribed angle on the other side:

$2\angle ABE = \angle ACE$ (reflex) $\;\Rightarrow\; 2\angle ABE = 100^\circ+78^\circ$.

STEP 5 · Solve for ∠CBE

$\angle ABE = \angle ABC + \angle CBE = 60^\circ + \angle CBE$.

$$2(60^\circ + \angle CBE)=178^\circ$$

$$120^\circ + 2\angle CBE = 178^\circ$$

$$2\angle CBE = 58^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; \angle CBE = \mathbf{29^\circ}$$

Correct choice D. $29^\circ$
