Tetrahedron with a vertical edge — finding an angle between two faces

3D Trigonometry Cosine Formula Pythagoras in 3D Compound Angles

The Question

The figure shows a tetrahedron \(PQRS\) with the base \(QRS\) lying on the horizontal ground. It is given that \(Q\) is vertically below \(P\). If \(\angle PRQ = 47^\circ\), \(\angle PSQ = 53^\circ\) and \(\angle RQS = 120^\circ\), find \(\angle RPS\) correct to the nearest degree.

A. \(52^\circ\)
B. \(60^\circ\)
C. \(68^\circ\)
D. \(76^\circ\)

Original Figure

Step-up The 3D Setup — Why \(PQ\) is perpendicular to the base

Because \(Q\) is vertically below \(P\), the segment \(PQ\) is perpendicular to the horizontal plane \(QRS\). Hence \(PQ \perp QR\) and \(PQ \perp QS\), giving two right angles at \(Q\).

\(PQ =\) 130 units \(\angle PRQ =\) 47° \(\angle PSQ =\) 53° \(\angle RQS =\) 120°
0 / 2
Step 1

\(PQ\) is vertical, so it is perpendicular to the base \(QRS\):

\[ PQ \perp QR, \qquad PQ \perp QS. \]
Hint: Any segment joining a point to its vertical projection onto the ground is perpendicular to every line through the foot in the ground plane.
Step 2

Splitting the tetrahedron along the plane \(PQS\) and using right triangles:

\[ \angle PRQ = 47^\circ,\quad \angle PSQ = 53^\circ,\quad \angle RQS = 120^\circ. \] These are the three given angles we need to combine.
We need \(\angle RPS\), the angle between \(PR\) and \(PS\) in triangle \(PRS\).

Key Idea Express \(\cos\angle RPS\) from two triangles

We apply the cosine formula in \(\triangle PRS\) and in the right triangles \(\triangle PQR\), \(\triangle PQS\), \(\triangle QRS\), then eliminate the common lengths.

\(\angle RPS =\) 68.0° \(QR =\) — \(QS =\) — \(PR =\) — \(PS =\) — \(RS =\) —
0 / 5
Step 1

Cosine formula in \(\triangle PRS\):

\[ PR^2 + PS^2 - RS^2 = 2\,PR\cdot PS\cdot\cos\angle RPS \quad \cdots (1) \]
Hint: This is the standard law of cosines—we isolate \(\cos\angle RPS\).
Step 2

Cosine formula in the base triangle \(\triangle QRS\):

\[ QR^2 + QS^2 - RS^2 = 2\,QR\cdot QS\cdot\cos\angle RQS \quad \cdots (2) \]
Hint: This eliminates \(RS^2\) when we subtract \((1)-(2)\).
Step 3

Pythagoras in \(\triangle PQR\) and \(\triangle PQS\): since \(PQ \perp QR\) and \(PQ \perp QS\),

\[ PR^2 = QR^2 + PQ^2 \quad \cdots (3) \] \[ PS^2 = QS^2 + PQ^2 \quad \cdots (4) \]
Hint: The right angles at \(Q\) come directly from the "vertically below" condition.
Step 4

Subtract \((2)\) from \((1)\), using \((3),(4)\) to replace \(PR^2\) and \(PS^2\):

\[ PR^2+PS^2-RS^2 - (QR^2+QS^2-RS^2) = 2PR\cdot PS\cos\angle RPS - 2\,QR\cdot QS\cos\angle RQS \] \[ (QR^2+PQ^2)+(QS^2+PQ^2)-QR^2-QS^2 = 2PR\cdot PS\cos\angle RPS - 2\,QR\cdot QS\cos\angle RQS \] \[ 2\,PQ^2 = 2PR\cdot PS\cos\angle RPS - 2\,QR\cdot QS\cos\angle RQS \]
Hint: All the \(QR^2\) and \(QS^2\) terms cancel beautifully, leaving only \(2PQ^2\) on the left.
Step 5

Divide by \(2PR\cdot PS\) and substitute \(\dfrac{PQ}{PR}=\sin\angle PRQ\), \(\dfrac{PQ}{PS}=\sin\angle PSQ\), \(\dfrac{QR}{PR}=\cos\angle PRQ\), \(\dfrac{QS}{PS}=\cos\angle PSQ\):

\[ \cos\angle RPS = \cos\angle PRQ\cos\angle PSQ\cos\angle RQS + \sin\angle PRQ\sin\angle PSQ \] \[ = \cos47^\circ\cos53^\circ\cos120^\circ + \sin47^\circ\sin53^\circ \] \[ = (0.681998)(0.601815)(-0.5) + (0.731354)(0.798636) \] \[ = -0.205218 + 0.584078 = 0.378860 \] \[ \angle RPS = \cos^{-1}(0.378860) = 67.7365\ldots^\circ \approx 68^\circ \]
\(\angle RPS = 68^\circ\) (correct to the nearest degree) → Option C

Check Numerical verification

Let \(PQ = 1\). Then \(QR = \dfrac{1}{\tan 47^\circ} = 0.9325\), \(PR = \dfrac{1}{\sin 47^\circ} = 1.3673\), \(QS = \dfrac{1}{\tan 53^\circ} = 0.7536\), \(PS = \dfrac{1}{\sin 53^\circ} = 1.2522\).

\[ RS^2 = QR^2+QS^2-2(QR)(QS)\cos120^\circ = 0.8696+0.5679+0.7027 = 2.1402 \] \[ RS = 1.4630 \] \[ \cos\angle RPS = \frac{PR^2+PS^2-RS^2}{2\,PR\cdot PS} = \frac{1.8695+1.5680-2.1402}{2(1.3673)(1.2522)} = \frac{1.2973}{3.4243} = 0.3788 \] \[ \angle RPS = 67.7^\circ \approx 68^\circ \checkmark \]
\(RS =\) 1.463 \(\cos\angle RPS =\) 0.3789 \(\angle RPS =\) 67.74°

📌 Final Answer at a Glance

  • \(Q\) is directly below \(P\) → \(PQ \perp QR\) and \(PQ \perp QS\).
  • Law of cosines in \(\triangle PRS\) + \(\triangle QRS\), plus Pythagoras in \(\triangle PQR\) & \(\triangle PQS\), leads to
  • \(\cos\angle RPS = \cos\angle PRQ\cos\angle PSQ\cos\angle RQS + \sin\angle PRQ\sin\angle PSQ\)
  • \(= \cos 47^\circ\cos 53^\circ\cos 120^\circ + \sin 47^\circ\sin 53^\circ = 0.378860\)
  • \(\angle RPS = 67.7365\ldots^\circ \approx \mathbf{68^\circ}\)
  • Correct choice: C
