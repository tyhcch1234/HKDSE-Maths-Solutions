Probability (Combinations, Inclusion-Exclusion)

Question

A bag contains \(3\) red balls, \(3\) white balls and \(5\) black balls. If \(4\) balls are randomly drawn from the bag at the same time, find

(a)  the probability that exactly \(2\) red balls are drawn; (2 marks)

(b)  the probability that exactly \(2\) red balls or exactly \(2\) white balls are drawn. (2 marks)

Solution

STEP 1 — total outcomes
Combinations \({}^{n}C_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\) Drawing \(4\) balls from \(11\) gives \({}^{11}C_{4}=330\) equally likely draws.
There are \(3+3+5=11\) balls. The number of ways to draw 4 balls: \[ {}^{11}C_{4}=330 \]
PART (a) — exactly 2 red
Multiplication Principle \(n(A\times B)=n(A)\,n(B)\) Choosing \(2\) of the \(3\) red balls and \(2\) of the other \(8\) gives \(3\times28=84\) draws.
Choose 2 of the 3 red, and the other 2 from the remaining 8 (white or black): \[ {}^{3}C_{2}{}^{8}C_{2}=3\times28=84 \] \[ P(\text{exactly 2 red})=\frac{84}{330}=\frac{14}{55} \]
PART (b) — use inclusion–exclusion
Let \(A\) = exactly 2 red, \(B\) = exactly 2 white. By symmetry \(n(B)={}^{3}C_{2}{}^{8}C_{2}=84\).
Inclusion-Exclusion Principle \(n(A\cup B)=n(A)+n(B)-n(A\cap B)\) Both events count the \(9\) draws with \(2\) red and \(2\) white, so \(84+84-9=159\).
\(A\cap B\): 2 red, 2 white and 0 black: \[ {}^{3}C_{2}{}^{3}C_{2}{}^{5}C_{0}=9 \] \[ n(A\cup B)=84+84-9=159 \] \[ P(A\cup B)=\frac{159}{330}=\frac{53}{110} \]
(a) \(P=\dfrac{14}{55}\)    (b) \(P=\dfrac{53}{110}\)

Question. A bag contains \(4\) red balls, \(2\) white balls and \(6\) black balls. Four balls are drawn at random at the same time.
(a) Find the probability that exactly \(2\) of them are red.
(b) Find the probability that exactly \(2\) of them are red or exactly \(2\) of them are white.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total draws \({}^{12}C_{4}=495\).
Step 2. Exactly \(2\) red: \({}^{4}C_{2}\times{}^{8}C_{2}=6\times28=168\), so \(P=\dfrac{168}{495}=\dfrac{56}{165}\).
Step 3. Exactly \(2\) white: \({}^{2}C_{2}\times{}^{10}C_{2}=1\times45=45\).
Step 4. Both at once: \({}^{4}C_{2}\times{}^{2}C_{2}\times{}^{6}C_{0}=6\).
Step 5. \(168+45-6=207\), so \(P=\dfrac{207}{495}=\dfrac{23}{55}\).
Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{56}{165}\) (b) \(\dfrac{23}{55}\)
