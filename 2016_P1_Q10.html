Coordinate Geometry — Locus, Circle & Circumference
Locus of a Point Perpendicular Bisector Circle through 3 Points Diameter & Thales Circumference Estimate

📋 The Question 5 marks total

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((5, 7)\) and \((13, 1)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).

(a) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). 2 marks

(b) \(\Gamma\) intersects the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis at \(H\) and \(K\) respectively. Denote the origin by \(O\). Let \(C\) be the circle which passes through \(O\), \(H\) and \(K\). Someone claims that the circumference of \(C\) exceeds \(30\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. 3 marks

Step 11M

Let the coordinates of \(P\) be \((x, y)\). Since \(P\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\):

\[ PA = PB \] \[ \sqrt{(x-5)^2 + (y-7)^2} = \sqrt{(x-13)^2 + (y-1)^2} \]
Hint: The condition "equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\)" means \(PA = PB\). Square both sides immediately to get rid of the radicals.
Step 21A

Squaring both sides and expanding:

\[ (x-5)^2 + (y-7)^2 = (x-13)^2 + (y-1)^2 \] \[ x^2 -10x +25 + y^2 -14y +49 = x^2 -26x +169 + y^2 -2y +1 \] \[ -10x -14y +74 = -26x -2y +170 \] \[ 16x -12y -96 = 0 \] \[ 4x - 3y - 24 = 0 \]
Hint: After cancelling \(x^2\) and \(y^2\), divide every term by the common factor \(4\) to get the simplest form — this is the required 1A mark for a correct equation.
Answer (a): \(\Gamma:\; 4x - 3y - 24 = 0\)

Step 11M

Find where \(\Gamma\) meets the axes. To find \(H\), put \(y = 0\) into \(4x - 3y - 24 = 0\):

\[ 4x - 24 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 6 \]

So \(H = (6, 0)\). To find \(K\), put \(x = 0\):

\[ -3y - 24 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -8 \]

So \(K = (0, -8)\).

Hint: Intersections with the \(x\)-axis always have \(y = 0\); intersections with the \(y\)-axis always have \(x = 0\).
Step 21M

Since \(\angle OHK = 90^\circ\) (the axes are perpendicular), \(HK\) is a diameter of circle \(C\) (converse of the angle in a semicircle).

\[ \text{Diameter of } C = HK = \sqrt{(6-0)^2 + (0-(-8))^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \]
Hint: The right angle at \(O\) is the key observation — it tells you the hypotenuse \(HK\) is the diameter of the circumcircle of \(\triangle OHK\).
Step 31A

The circumference of \(C\) is:

\[ \text{Circumference} = \pi \times \text{diameter} = 10\pi \approx 31.41592654 \] \[ 10\pi > 30 \]

Therefore, the claim is correct.

Hint: Use \(\pi \approx 3.14159\) to show \(10\pi \approx 31.42\), which clearly exceeds \(30\). The explicit comparison is required for the 1A mark.
Answer (b): Yes, the claim is correct — \(HK = 10\), so the circumference is \(10\pi \approx 31.42 > 30\).

🎯 Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) The locus \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\):
    \[4x - 3y - 24 = 0\]
  • (b) Intersections with axes: \(H = (6, 0)\) and \(K = (0, -8)\).
  • (b) Since \(\angle OHK = 90^\circ\), \(HK\) is the diameter of \(C\): \(HK = 10\).
  • (b) Circumference \(= 10\pi \approx 31.4159 > 30\).
    ∴ The claim is correct.
