The coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$ are $(5,\,7)$ and $(13,\,1)$ respectively. Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that $P$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$.

(a) Find the equation of $\Gamma$. [2 marks]

(b) $\Gamma$ intersects the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis at $H$ and $K$ respectively. Denote the origin by $O$. Let $C$ be the circle which passes through $O$, $H$ and $K$. Someone claims that the circumference of $C$ exceeds $30$. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [3 marks]
STEP 1 · Set up the locus condition
Distance Formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) Writing \\(PA=PB\\) as \\((x-5)^{2}+(y-7)^{2}=(x-13)^{2}+(y-1)^{2}\\) squares the two distances from \\(P\\) to \\(A\\) and \\(B\\).
Let $(x,y)$ be the coordinates of $P$. Since $P$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$, $$PA = PB$$ Square both sides (distances are non-negative): $$(x-5)^2+(y-7)^2=(x-13)^2+(y-1)^2$$
💡 Squaring is safe here because both sides are distances (always ≥ 0), so no extra solutions appear.
STEP 2 · Expand and simplify to get Γ
Expanding both sides: $$x^2-10x+25+y^2-14y+49=x^2-26x+169+y^2-2y+1$$ Cancel $x^2$ and $y^2$: $$-10x-14y+74=-26x-2y+170$$ Rearranging: $$16x-12y-96=0$$ Divide throughout by $4$: $$4x-3y-24=0$$
⚠️ This is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ — the classic locus of points equidistant from two fixed points.
STEP 3 · Find the intercepts $H$ and $K$
For $H$ (x-intercept): put $y=0$ in $4x-3y-24=0$: $$4x-24=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad H(6,\,0)$$ For $K$ (y-intercept): put $x=0$ in $4x-3y-24=0$: $$-3y-24=0 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad K(0,\,-8)$$
STEP 4 · Analyse circle $C$ through $O$, $H$, $K$
Since $O(0,0)$, $H(6,0)$ and $K(0,-8)$ are the three points on $C$,
note that $\angle HOK = 90^\circ$ because $OH$ lies on the $x$-axis and $OK$ lies on the $y$-axis.

By the converse of the angle in a semicircle, $HK$ must be a diameter
Converse of the Angle in a Semicircle \(\angle AXB=90^\circ\ \Longrightarrow\ AB\ \text{is a diameter}\) \\(\angle HOK=90^\circ\\) because \\(OH\\) and \\(OK\\) lie on the axes, so \\(HK\\) must be a diameter of circle \\(C\\).
of circle $C$.
💡 A right angle at $O$ subtending chord $HK$ forces $HK$ to be a diameter, so the circumcircle has centre $(3,-4)$ and radius $\tfrac{HK}{2}$.
STEP 5 · Test the claim
Circumference of a Circle \(C=2\pi r=\pi d\) \\(HK=10\\) is the diameter, so \\(C=10\pi\approx31.42\\), which is greater than \\(30\\).
Length of the diameter $HK$: $$HK=\sqrt{(6-0)^2+(0-(-8))^2}=\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10$$ Circumference of $C$: $$\text{Circumference}=10\pi \approx 31.41592654$$ Comparing with $30$: $$10\pi \approx 31.42 > 30$$
Conclusion: Since the circumference of $C$ is $10\pi \approx 31.42$, which exceeds $30$, the claim is correct.
