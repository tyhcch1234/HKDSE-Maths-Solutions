Question. In the figure, $AC$ is a diameter of the circle $ABCD$. $PB$ and $PD$ are tangents to the circle. $AD$ produced and $BC$ produced meet at $Q$. If $\angle BPD = 68^\circ$, then $\angle AQB =$
A. $22^\circ$
B. $28^\circ$
C. $32^\circ$
D. $34^\circ$
STEP 1 · Tangents from an external point

Tangents drawn from the same external point $P$ are equal in length:

$$PB = PD$$

So $\triangle BPD$ is isosceles, with base angles at $B$ and $D$ equal:

$$\angle PBD = \angle PDB \quad (\text{base }\angle\text{s, isos. }\triangle)$$
STEP 2 · Angle sum of △BPD

Using the angle sum of $\triangle BPD$:

$$\angle PBD + \angle PDB = 180^\circ - \angle BPD = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$$ $$\therefore\ \angle PBD = 112^\circ \div 2 = 56^\circ$$

Note: $PD$ is a tangent at $D$ and $AD$ is a chord, so $\angle BAD$ and $\angle PBD$ are angles in the alternate segment.

STEP 3 · Angle in alternate segment

Since $PB$ is a tangent at $B$ and $AB$ is a chord:

$$\angle BAD = \angle PBD = 56^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in alt. segment})$$

But $A, D, Q$ are collinear (with $Q$ on $AD$ produced), so $\angle BAQ = \angle BAD = 56^\circ$.

STEP 4 · Angle in a semi-circle

Because $AC$ is a diameter of the circle, the angle subtended by $AC$ at $B$ is a right angle:

$$\angle ABC = 90^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in semi-circle})$$

As $B, C, Q$ are collinear, we get $\angle ABC = 90^\circ \Rightarrow$ in $\triangle ABQ$, $\angle ABQ = 90^\circ$.

STEP 5 · Angle sum of △ABQ

In $\triangle ABQ$, using the angle sum:

$$\angle AQB = 180^\circ - \angle ABQ - \angle BAQ$$ $$= 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 56^\circ = 34^\circ$$
Answer: $\angle AQB = 34^\circ$  →  Option D Tangents from external point → isosceles triangle → alternate segment → angle in semi-circle.
