15. Statistics — Mean, inter-quartile range, standard deviation & effect of removing a data value

Mean Inter-quartile Range Standard Deviation \(\sum x\) & \(\sum x^2\) Effect of Removing a Data Value

QQuestion

The bar chart below shows the distribution of the numbers of family members of the employees of company \(D\).

Distribution of the numbers of family members of the employees of company \(D\)

No. of family members \(x\)1234567
No. of employees \(f\)4162016844

(a) Find the mean, the inter-quartile range and the standard deviation of the above distribution. 4 marks

(b) An employee leaves company \(D\). The number of family members of this employee is \(7\). Find the change in the standard deviation of the numbers of family members of the employees of company \(D\) due to the leaving of this employee. 5 marks

aPart (a) — Mean, IQR and Standard Deviation

\(N=\sum f = 72\) \(L_1\) (median) = position 36.5 → 3 \(L_3\) (upper quartile) = position 54.5 → 4 IQR \(=L_3-L_1=1\) \(\sigma=1.5\)
STEP 11M

First identify the total number of employees \(N\) and the sum \(\sum f\,x\).

\[ N=\sum f = 4+16+20+16+8+4+4 = 72 \] \[ \sum f x = 1(4)+2(16)+3(20)+4(16)+5(8)+6(4)+7(4) = 252 \]
Hint: Use the bar heights as the frequencies \(f\). The values \(x\) are the numbers on the horizontal axis. Weighted totals are needed for every statistic in this question.
So: \(N=72\) and \(\sum fx = 252\).
STEP 21A

Mean:

\[ \bar{x}=\frac{\sum fx}{N}=\frac{252}{72}=\frac{7}{2}=3.5 \]
Hint: \(\frac{7}{2}\) is expected as the answer; \(3.5\) is the same number.
Answer (a) mean: \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{7}{2}\) (i.e. \(3.5\))
STEP 31A

Inter-quartile range. With \(N=72\), the positional indices are:

\[ \text{median}: \frac{N}{2}=36,\ \frac{N}{2}+1=37 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{average of 36th and 37th} \] Cumulative frequencies: \(4,\ 20,\ 40,\ 56,\ 64,\ 68,\ 72\). Both the 36th and 37th values lie in the group \(x=3\). \[ L_1=3 \] For the quartiles, the upper quartile sits at \(\frac{3N}{4}=54\); the 54th value also lies in the group \(x=3\), while the 55th lies in \(x=4\). Hence \(L_3=4\). \[ \text{IQR}=L_3-L_1=4-3=1 \]
Hint: Reading off cumulative frequencies is the fastest route. Most candidates lose marks here by using 4 as the upper quartile position incorrectly — check the cumulative counts carefully.
Answer (a) IQR: \(1\)
STEP 41A

Standard deviation. Compute \(\sum f x^2\):

\[ \sum f x^2 = 1^2(4)+2^2(16)+3^2(20)+4^2(16)+5^2(8)+6^2(4)+7^2(4) \] \[ =4+64+180+256+200+144+196=1044 \] \[ \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum f x^2}{N}-\bar{x}^{2}} =\sqrt{\frac{1044}{72}-\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^{2}} =\sqrt{14.5-12.25}=\sqrt{2.25}=1.5 \]
Hint: Use the shortcut formula \(\sigma=\sqrt{\overline{x^{2}}-\bar{x}^{2}}\) to avoid subtracting \(72\) individual deviations. Always keep the exact value \(\frac{7}{2}\) for \(\bar{x}\).
Answer (a) standard deviation: \(\sigma=1.5\)

bPart (b) — Change in Standard Deviation After an Employee Leaves

Illustrative removed value \(x_r\): 7
STEP 11M

After the employee leaves, the new totals are obtained by removing one value \(x=7\) from the original data set:

\[ N'=72-1=71,\qquad \sum f x' = 252-7=245,\qquad \sum f x'^{2}=1044-7^{2}=995 \]
Hint: Removing a single observation simply subtracts that observation from \(\sum fx\) and subtracts its square from \(\sum fx^{2}\). Do not recompute the whole table.
So: \(N'=71,\ \sum fx'=245,\ \sum fx'^{2}=995\).
STEP 21M

Apply the same shortcut formula to the reduced data set:

\[ \sigma'=\sqrt{\frac{\sum f x'^{2}}{N'}-\left(\frac{\sum f x'}{N'}\right)^{2}} =\sqrt{\frac{995}{71}-\left(\frac{245}{71}\right)^{2}} \]
Hint: Keep at least 6 decimal places in intermediate steps — a premature rounding to \(3\) s.f. changes the final 4th significant figure.
STEP 31A

Evaluate numerically:

\[ \frac{995}{71}\approx 14.01408451,\qquad \left(\frac{245}{71}\right)^{2}\approx 11.91033485 \] \[ \sigma'=\sqrt{14.01408451-11.91033485}=\sqrt{2.10374966}\approx 1.451456116 \]
Hint: An exact fraction form is \(\sigma'=\sqrt{\dfrac{10606}{5041}}\); the decimal \(1.451456116\ldots\) continues, so quote enough figures.
New standard deviation: \(\sigma'\approx 1.451456116\)
STEP 41A

The change in the standard deviation is the difference between the two values:

\[ \text{change} = 1.5-1.451456116 = 0.048543884 \]
Hint: The question asks for "the change", so the sign matters. Removing the largest value \(7\) (which is far from the mean \(3.5\)) pulls the data closer together, so the standard deviation decreases.
Decrease in standard deviation: \(0.048543884\)
STEP 51A

Round to a sensible accuracy (4 significant figures, matching the official marking scheme):

\[ 0.048543884 \approx 0.0485 \]
Hint: The integer part is \(0\), so the first significant figure is the \(4\) in the hundredths place: \(0.0485\) (4 s.f.).
Answer (b): the standard deviation decreases by \(\approx 0.0485\) (i.e. \(\sigma'\approx 1.451456116\)).

★Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Mean: \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{7}{2}\) (i.e. \(3.5\))
  • (a) Inter-quartile range: \(4-3=1\)
  • (a) Standard deviation: \(\sigma=1.5\)
  • (b) New standard deviation after one \(x=7\) leaves: \(\approx 1.451456116\)
  • (b) Change in standard deviation: \(\approx 0.0485\) (a decrease)

Key technique: from \(\sum f\), \(\sum fx\) and \(\sum fx^{2}\) you can obtain every measure of dispersion used in this question.
