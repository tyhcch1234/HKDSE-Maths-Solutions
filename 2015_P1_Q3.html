Probability — Drawing from Two Bags

Question. Bag $A$ contains four cards numbered $1$, $3$, $5$ and $7$ respectively while bag $B$ contains five cards numbered $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$ respectively. If one card is randomly drawn from each bag, find the probability that the sum of the two numbers drawn is less than $9$. [3 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

Multiplication Principle for Counting \(\text{total outcomes}=4\times 5\) Each of the \(4\) cards from bag \(A\) pairs with each of the \(5\) cards from bag \(B\).
1 Count the total number of outcomes

Each draw is a pair $(a,b)$ with $a$ from Bag $A$ and $b$ from Bag $B$. Since the two bags are independent,

$$\text{Total outcomes} = 4 \times 5 = 20.$$

Hint: Both draws are equally likely, and the order of the pair is fixed (A first, B second).
Systematic Listing of Favourable Outcomes \(a+b<9 \iff b<9-a\) Fixing \(a\) and counting the \(b\) in \(\{2,4,6,8,10\}\) below \(9-a\) gives \(3+2+1+0=6\).
2 List the favourable outcomes (sum $< 9$)

Check each pair $a + b < 9$, i.e. $b < 9 - a$:

Counting the ✓ entries: $a=1$ gives $3$ favourable, $a=3$ gives $2$, $a=5$ gives $1$, $a=7$ gives $0$.

Number of favourable outcomes $= 3 + 2 + 1 + 0 = 6.$

Hint: A quick way is to fix $a$ and count how many $b\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$ satisfy $b < 9-a$.
Probability of an Event \(P(E)=\dfrac{n(E)}{n(S)}\) There are \(6\) favourable pairs out of \(20\) equally likely pairs, so \(P=\dfrac{6}{20}\).
3 Compute the required probability

$$\text{Probability} = \frac{\text{favourable}}{\text{total}} = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}.$$

Hint: Always reduce the fraction to lowest terms.
Answer The required probability is $\; \dfrac{3}{10}\; (=\,0.3)$.

Question. Bag \(A\) contains four cards numbered \(2\), \(4\), \(6\) and \(8\) while bag \(B\) contains five cards numbered \(1\), \(3\), \(5\), \(7\) and \(9\). One card is drawn at random from each bag. Find the probability that the sum of the two numbers drawn is at least \(10\). [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total number of outcomes \(=4\times 5=20\), all equally likely.
Step 2. Count the pairs with \(a+b\ge 10\): \(a=2\) gives \(1\) pair \((2,9)\); \(a=4\) gives \(2\) pairs \((4,7),(4,9)\); \(a=6\) gives \(3\) pairs; \(a=8\) gives \(4\) pairs.
Step 3. Number of favourable outcomes \(=1+2+3+4=10\).
Step 4. Probability \(=\dfrac{10}{20}=\dfrac{1}{2}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)
