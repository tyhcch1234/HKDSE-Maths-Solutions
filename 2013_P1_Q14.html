Coordinate Geometry — Centre of a Circle, Distance to a Line & Area Ratio

Circle Equation Perpendicular Distance Collinearity Area Ratio

QUESTION Full Question

Total: 9 marks  ·  Part (a): 1 mark  ·  Part (b): 8 marks

The equation of the circle \(C\) is

\[ x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-34y+225=0 . \]

Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(R\).

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(R\).  [1 mark]

(b) The equation of the straight line \(L\) is

\[ 4x+3y+50=0 . \]

It is found that \(C\) and \(L\) do not intersect. Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(L\) such that \(P\) is nearest to \(R\).

(i) Find the distance between \(P\) and \(R\).

(ii) Let \(Q\) be a moving point on \(C\). When \(Q\) is nearest to \(P\),

(1)  describe the geometric relationship between \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\);

(2)  find the ratio of the area of \(\Delta OPQ\) to the area of \(\Delta OQR\), where \(O\) is the origin.

[8 marks]

PART (a) Finding the Centre R

1 mark · 1A

r = 10, R = (6, 17)
STEP 1 1A

Complete the square (or use the standard result). For a circle in the form \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\), the centre is \(\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},\,-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)\).

Here \(D=-12\) and \(E=-34\), so:

\[ R=\left(-\frac{-12}{2},\,-\frac{-34}{2}\right)=(6,\,17). \]
Hint: Remember the centre is \(\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)\), not \(\left(\frac{D}{2},\frac{E}{2}\right)\) — the sign flips completely.
Answer (a): \(R=(6,\,17)\)

PART (b)(i) Distance between P and R

4 marks (1M + 1M + 1M + 1A)

PR = 25
STEP 1 1M

Let \(P=(h,k)\). Since \(P\) lies on \(L\): \(4h+3k+50=0\).

For \(P\) to be the point on \(L\) nearest to \(R\), the segment \(RP\) must be perpendicular to \(L\).

Hint: The nearest point on a line to an external point is always the foot of the perpendicular.
STEP 2 1M

Slope of \(L\): \(4x+3y+50=0 \Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x-\tfrac{50}{3}\), so \(m_L=-\tfrac{4}{3}\).

Hence slope of the perpendicular \(RP\) is \(m_{RP}=\dfrac{3}{4}\). Also \(m_{RP}=\dfrac{k-17}{h-6}\). Therefore:

\[ \frac{k-17}{h-6}=\frac{3}{4}. \]
Hint: The perpendicular slope is the negative reciprocal: \(m_\perp=-\frac{1}{m_L}=\frac{3}{4}\).
STEP 3 1M

From \(4h+3k+50=0\) we get \(3k=-4h-50\). From the slope condition \(4(k-17)=3(h-6)\), i.e. \(4k-68=3h-18\), so \(4k=3h+50\).

Solve simultaneously: substitute \(k=\frac{3h+50}{4}\) into \(4h+3k+50=0\):

\[ 4h+3\left(\frac{3h+50}{4}\right)+50=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 16h+9h+150+200=0 \;\Rightarrow\; 25h=-350 \;\Rightarrow\; h=-14. \]

Then \(k=\dfrac{3(-14)+50}{4}=2\). So \(P=(-14,\,2)\).

Hint: Always substitute one equation into the other — do not guess. Check: \(4(-14)+3(2)+50=-56+6+50=0\) ✓
STEP 4 1A

Now find the distance \(PR\) using the distance formula:

\[ PR=\sqrt{(-14-6)^{2}+(2-17)^{2}}=\sqrt{(-20)^{2}+(-15)^{2}}=\sqrt{400+225}=\sqrt{625}=25. \]
Hint: Alternatively, use the perpendicular distance formula \(d=\dfrac{|4(6)+3(17)+50|}{\sqrt{4^2+3^2}}=\dfrac{125}{5}=25\).
Answer (b)(i): \(PR=25\)

PART (b)(ii) Collinearity & Area Ratio

4 marks (1M + 1M + 1M + 1A)

PQ : QR = 15 : 10 = 3 : 2
STEP 1 1M

(1) The geometric relationship: When \(Q\) is the point on \(C\) nearest to \(P\), the points \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are collinear.

Reason: The nearest point on a circle from an external point \(P\) lies on the line joining \(P\) to the centre \(R\). Here \(Q\) is on the segment \(PR\), so \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) are collinear (with \(Q\) between \(P\) and \(R\)).

Hint: Because \(P\) is outside the circle (distance \(25\) from the centre \(R\)), the nearest point \(Q\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\). This is exactly the key idea of the "nearest point on a circle".
Answer (b)(ii)(1): \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are collinear.
STEP 2 1M

Find the radius of \(C\). Completing the square:

\[ x^2-12x+y^2-34y+225=0 \Rightarrow (x-6)^2+(y-17)^2=36+289-225=100. \]

So the radius is \(r=\sqrt{100}=10\). Hence the distance \(QR=10\) (since \(Q\) lies on the circle and \(R\) is the centre).

Hint: Radius \(r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^2-F} =\sqrt{6^2+17^2-225}=\sqrt{100}=10\).
Radius of C: \(10\), so \(QR=10\).
STEP 3 1M

Since \(Q\) lies between \(P\) and \(R\) on the straight line, \(PR=PQ+QR\). Therefore:

\[ PQ=PR-QR=25-10=15. \]

Thus \(PQ:QR=15:10=3:2\).

Hint: Because \(P,Q,R\) are collinear with \(Q\) between \(P\) and \(R\), the segments add: \(PR=PQ+QR\).
STEP 4 1A

(2) Area ratio: The triangles \(\Delta OPQ\) and \(\Delta OQR\) have a common vertex \(O\) and their bases \(PQ\) and \(QR\) lie on the same straight line \(PR\).

Hence they share the same perpendicular height from \(O\) to the line \(PR\), so their areas are in the ratio of their bases:

\[ \frac{[OPQ]}{[OQR]}=\frac{PQ}{QR}=\frac{15}{10}=\frac{3}{2}=3:2. \]
Hint: Triangles with a common vertex and bases on the same straight line have areas proportional to their bases.
Answer (b)(ii)(2): Area of \(\Delta OPQ :\) Area of \(\Delta OQR = 3:2\)
0 / 4

📋 Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(R=(6,\,17)\)
  • Part (b)(i): \(P=(-14,\,2)\) and \(PR=\sqrt{(-20)^2+(-15)^2}=25\)
  • Part (b)(ii)(1): \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\) are collinear (with \(Q\) between \(P\) and \(R\))
  • Part (b)(ii)(2): \(PQ:QR=15:10=3:2\), so \(\dfrac{[OPQ]}{[OQR]}=\dfrac{3}{2}\)
