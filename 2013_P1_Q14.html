[2013 #14] The equation of the circle \(C\) is \[x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-34y+225=0.\] Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(R\).

(a) Write down the coordinates of \(R\). [1 mark]

(b) The equation of the straight line \(L\) is \(4x+3y+50=0\). It is found that \(C\) and \(L\) do not intersect. Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(L\) such that \(P\) is nearest to \(R\).

(i) Find the distance between \(P\) and \(R\).

(ii) Let \(Q\) be a moving point on \(C\). When \(Q\) is nearest to \(P\), (1) describe the geometric relationship between \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\); (2) find the ratio of the area of \(\triangle OPQ\) to the area of \(\triangle OQR\), where \(O\) is the origin.

Step 1 · Centre of C by completing the square \[x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-34y+225=0\] \[(x-6)^{2}+(y-17)^{2}=6^{2}+17^{2}-225=100\] So \(C\) has centre \(R(6,17)\) and radius \(r=10\).
(a) \(R=(6,\;17)\)
Equation of a Circle \((x-h)^{2}+(y-k)^{2}=r^{2}\) Completing the square on \(x^{2}+y^{2}-12x-34y+225=0\) gives centre \((6,17)\) and radius \(10\).
Step 2 · P lies on L and RP ⊥ L Let \(P=(h,k)\). Since \(P\in L\): \[4h+3k+50=0\] Slope of \(L\) is \(-\tfrac43\), and \(RP\perp L\): \[\frac{k-17}{h-6}\cdot\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)=-1\] So \(3h-4k+50=0\). Solving with \(4h+3k+50=0\): \[h=-14,\qquad k=2\]
\(P=(-14,\;2)\)
Cross-check: \(4(-14)+3(2)+50=-56+6+50=0\) ✓
Perpendicular Lines \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) The slope of \(L\) is \(- frac{4}{3}\), so \(RP\) has slope \( frac{3}{4}\) and meets \(L\) at \(P\).
Step 3 · Distance PR \[PR=\sqrt{(-14-6)^{2}+(2-17)^{2}}=\sqrt{400+225}=\sqrt{625}\]
(b)(i) \(PR=25\)
Since \(PR=25>r=10\), \(P\) lies outside the circle — consistent with \(C\) and \(L\) not intersecting.
Distance Formula \(d=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) Between \(P(-14,2)\) and \(R(6,17)\) this gives \(PR=\sqrt{400+225}=25\).
Step 4 · Position of the nearest Q The nearest point of \(C\) to \(P\) lies on the segment \(RP\), so \(R,\,Q,\,P\) are collinear with \(Q\) between \(P\) and \(R\). Then \[RQ = r = 10,\qquad PQ = PR - RQ = 25-10 = 15\]
(b)(ii)(1) \(P,\;Q,\;R\) are collinear.
Step 5 · Ratio PQ : QR \[PQ:QR = 15:10 = 3:2\]
Triangles \(OPQ\) and \(OQR\) share the base line \(PR\), so their areas compare as the bases \(PQ:QR\).
Step 6 · Area ratio Since \(P,Q,R\) are collinear, \(\triangle OPQ\) and \(\triangle OQR\) have the same altitude from \(O\) to line \(PR\): \[\frac{\text{area of }OPQ}{\text{area of }OQR}=\frac{PQ}{QR}=\frac{15}{10}=\frac{3}{2}\]
(b)(ii)(2) \(\text{area of }OPQ:\text{area of }OQR=3:2\)

Question. \(C:x^{2}+y^{2}-10x-4y+13=0\), and \(L:3x+4y+77=0\). \(P\) is the point on \(L\) such that \(RP\perp L\), where \(R\) is the centre of \(C\). (a) Find \(R\) and the radius of \(C\). (b) Find \(P\). (c) Find \(PR\) and \(PQ\), where \(Q\) is the point of \(C\) nearest to \(P\). (d) Find \(\text{area of }OPQ:\text{area of }OQR\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(x^{2}-10x+y^{2}-4y+13=0\) gives \((x-5)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=25+4-13=16\), so \(R=(5,2)\) and the radius is \(4\).
Step 2. The slope of \(L\) is \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\), so \(RP\) has slope \(\dfrac{4}{3}\), i.e. \(4x-3y-14=0\). Solving with \(3x+4y+77=0\) gives \(P=(-7,-14)\).
Step 3. \(PR=\sqrt{(-7-5)^{2}+(-14-2)^{2}}=\sqrt{144+256}=20\).
Step 4. \(Q\) lies on \(RP\) with \(RQ=4\), so \(PQ=20-4=16\).
Step 5. \(\triangle OPQ\) and \(\triangle OQR\) share the altitude from \(O\) to \(PR\), so \(\text{area of }OPQ:\text{area of }OQR=PQ:QR=16:4=4:1\).
Answer: (a) \(R=(5,2)\), \(r=4\) (b) \(P=(-7,-14)\) (c) \(PR=20\), \(PQ=16\) (d) \(4:1\)
