HKDSE 2015 · Paper 2 · Q12 Rate, Ratio and Variations

Joint Variation

It is given that \(z\) varies as \(x^3\) and \(y^2\). When \(x = 2\) and \(y = 1\), \(z = 14\).

When \(x = 3\) and \(y = -2\), \(z =\)

A. \(-189\) B. \(-126\) C. \(126\) D. \(189\)

📘 Step-by-step solution

Click on each step to reveal the explanation and working.

1 Set up the variation equation ▼

“\(z\) varies as \(x^3\) and \(y^2\)” means \(z\) is jointly proportional to \(x^3\) and \(y^2\).

\[ z = k \cdot x^3 \cdot y^2 \]
Hint: \(k\) is the variation constant (non-zero). We must find \(k\) using the given pair \((x, y) = (2, 1)\) and \(z = 14\).
2 Substitute the first data point to find \(k\) ▼

When \(x = 2\), \(y = 1\), \(z = 14\):

\[ 14 = k \cdot (2)^3 \cdot (1)^2 \] \[ 14 = k \cdot 8 \cdot 1 \] \[ k = \frac{14}{8} = \frac{7}{4} \]
Hint: \(2^3 = 8\) and \(1^2 = 1\). Then divide \(14\) by \(8\) and simplify.
3 Write the particular variation formula ▼

Now substitute \(k = \frac{7}{4}\) back into the general equation:

\[ z = \frac{7}{4} \, x^3 y^2 \]

This formula works for any valid pair of \(x\) and \(y\).

4 Substitute the new values and compute ▼

When \(x = 3\) and \(y = -2\):

\[ z = \frac{7}{4} \cdot (3)^3 \cdot (-2)^2 \]

Compute step by step:

\[ (3)^3 = 27, \quad (-2)^2 = 4 \] \[ z = \frac{7}{4} \cdot 27 \cdot 4 = \frac{7}{4} \cdot 108 = 7 \cdot 27 = 189 \]
Hint: \((-2)^2 = 4\) (positive), so the product is positive. The constant \(\frac{7}{4}\) cancels nicely with \(4\).
✅ Therefore, \(z = 189\). The correct answer is D.

Key takeaway

In joint variation, always form the equation with a constant \(k\).
\(z \propto x^3 y^2 \implies z = k x^3 y^2\).
Pay attention to signs: even powers like \(y^2\) remove the negative sign, so \((-2)^2 = 4\).
