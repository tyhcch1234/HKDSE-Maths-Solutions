Question Multiple Choice

The figure shows the graph of \[y=(px+5)^2+q,\] where \(p\) and \(q\) are constants.

Which of the following is true?

  • A. \(p>0\) and \(q>0\)
  • B. \(p<0\) and \(q>0\)
  • C. \(p>0\) and \(q<0\)
  • D. \(p<0\) and \(q<0\)

From the figure: the parabola opens upwards and its vertex lies in Quadrant III (i.e. \(x_v<0\) and \(y_v<0\)).

Step 1

Factor \(p\) out of the bracket to expose the vertex form:

\[y=(px+5)^2+q=p^2\left(x+\frac{5}{p}\right)^2+q.\]
Step 2

So the vertex is

\[\left(-\frac{5}{p},\; q\right).\]
Hint: the coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(p^2>0\) (a perfect square), so the parabola always opens upwards — this matches the figure and tells us nothing about the sign of \(p\) yet.
Step 3

From the figure the vertex lies in Quadrant III, so both coordinates are negative:

\[-\frac{5}{p}<0 \quad\text{and}\quad q<0.\]
From this step\(q<0\).
Step 4

From \(-\dfrac{5}{p}<0\), and since \(-5<0\), we need a negative denominator:

\[p<0.\]
Hint: dividing/multiplying by a negative flips the inequality — \(\frac{-5}{p}<0 \iff p<0\) because a positive denominator would give a positive fraction.
Answer\(p<0\) and \(q<0\) → Option D.

Summary — Final Answers

  • Vertex form: \(y=(px+5)^2+q=p^2\left(x+\dfrac{5}{p}\right)^2+q\).
  • Vertex of the parabola: \(\left(-\dfrac{5}{p},\ q\right)\).
  • Vertex lies in Quadrant III \(\Rightarrow -\dfrac{5}{p}<0\) and \(q<0\).
  • From \(-\dfrac{5}{p}<0\) with \(-5<0\): \(p<0\).
  • Therefore \(p<0\) and \(q<0\).
Answer: D  —  \(p<0\) and \(q<0\).
