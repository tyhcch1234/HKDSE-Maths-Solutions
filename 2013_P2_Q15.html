Question

Hint: A symmetry axis must map the shaded region onto the shaded region — colours matter, not just the outline.

[2013 MC #15] In the figure, the regular octagon is divided into eight identical isosceles triangles and four of them are shaded. The number of axes of reflectional symmetry of the octagon is

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Read the figure

Join the centre $O$ to each vertex. The regular octagon is cut into 8 congruent isosceles triangles with apex angle

$$\frac{360^\circ}{8}=45^\circ$$

Because all 8 triangles are congruent, the outline of the octagon has 8 axes of symmetry. But the shaded pattern may destroy some of them — we must check each axis separately.

STEP 2 · A symmetry must preserve the shading

Reflection in an axis is a symmetry of the given figure only if it maps shaded triangles onto shaded triangles (and unshaded onto unshaded).

Label the 8 triangles $T_0,T_1,\dots,T_7$ going anticlockwise from the top, with $T_0,T_2,T_4,T_6$ shaded. Each reflection permutes the $T_i$.

STEP 3 · Test the vertex-to-vertex axes

There are 4 axes through opposite vertices (vertical, horizontal and the two diagonals through $O$).

  • These axes lie along the sides of the triangles (the radii $OT_i$), so no triangle is cut.
  • The reflection swaps each shaded triangle with the shaded triangle next to it: $T_0\leftrightarrow T_2$, $T_4\leftrightarrow T_6$, etc.
  • Shaded ↔ shaded and unshaded ↔ unshaded, so all 4 of these axes work.

For example, the vertical axis maps the top shaded triangle $T_0$ onto $T_2$ (also shaded).

STEP 4 · Test the side-to-side axes

The other 4 axes of the octagon pass through midpoints of opposite sides. Such an axis runs through the interior of two opposite triangles, cutting them in half.

  • One half of a shaded triangle would reflect onto half of an unshaded triangle.
  • So shaded does not map onto shaded — these 4 axes fail.

Hence only half of the octagon's 8 axes survive.

STEP 5 · Count the valid axes

Valid axes = the 4 axes through opposite vertices:

$$\text{Number of axes of reflectional symmetry}=4$$

(By the orbit–stabiliser idea: there are 8 rotations, but reflections preserving the shading are exactly the 4 vertex-axes.)

STEP 6 · Conclusion

The figure has exactly 4 axes of reflectional symmetry.

Answer: B  (4 axes)
