Question 19. In the figure, $ABC$ is an equilateral triangle. Let $D$ and $E$ be points lying on $AC$ and $BC$ respectively such that $AD = CE$. If $\angle CBD = 38^\circ$, then $\angle AEB =$

A. 73°
B. 75°
C. 78°
D. 82°
Hint: $AD = CE$ lets you convert the segment condition into $BE = CD$ since $ABC$ is equilateral. Then look for a pair of triangles with two sides and the included angle equal — that is a SAS congruence.
Step 1 · Set up the equilateral triangle

Since $ABC$ is equilateral, all three sides are equal and every interior angle is $60^\circ$:

$$AB = BC = CA,\qquad \angle ABC = \angle BCA = \angle CAB = 60^\circ.$$

We are told $\angle CBD = 38^\circ$. Since $BC$ and $BE$ lie along the same ray $BE$ on $BC$, $\angle CBE = 38^\circ$.

Step 2 · Convert $AD = CE$ into $BE = CD$

On side $BC$, the point $E$ lies between $B$ and $C$, so

$$BE = BC - CE = AC - AD = CD,$$

using $BC = AC$ (equilateral) and $CE = AD$ (given). This is the crucial bridge: $BE = CD$.

Step 3 · Prove $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD$ (SAS)

In $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle BCD$:

$$AB = BC\ \text{(equilateral)},\qquad \angle ABE = \angle BCD = 60^\circ\ \text{(equilateral)},\qquad BE = CD\ \text{(proved)}.$$

So $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle BCD$ by SAS.

Step 4 · Use the congruence and angle sum of $\triangle ABE$

Corresponding angles of congruent triangles are equal:

$$\angle BAE = \angle CBD = 38^\circ.$$

Also $\angle ABE = 60^\circ$ (interior angle of the equilateral triangle). By the angle sum of $\triangle ABE$:

$$\angle AEB = 180^\circ - \angle ABE - \angle BAE = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 38^\circ = 82^\circ.$$

ANSWER (option D)
$\angle AEB = 82^\circ$
