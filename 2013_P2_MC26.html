2013 Paper 2 — Question 26

Probability Counting Outcomes Combinations Parity (Odd / Even)

QQuestion

Two numbers are randomly drawn at the same time from seven cards numbered \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\) and \(7\) respectively. Find the probability that the product of the numbers drawn is an odd number.
  • A. \(\dfrac{2}{7}\)
  • B. \(\dfrac{4}{7}\)
  • C. \(\dfrac{12}{49}\)
  • D. \(\dfrac{16}{49}\)

1Guided Solution — Step by Step

Choosing two cards at the same time means the order does not matter. So \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{2,1\}\) count as the same outcome.
Key idea: "At the same time" ⇒ unordered selection ⇒ we count sets of two numbers, not ordered pairs.
We need to choose \(2\) cards out of \(7\), with order irrelevant:
\[ C^{7}_{2} = \frac{7 \times 6}{2 \times 1} = 21 \]
Formula used: \(C^n_r = \dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\)
For \(C^{7}_{2}\): divide by \(2!\) to remove the double counting of ordered pairs.
A product of two integers is odd only when both factors are odd. If either factor is even, the product is even.
odd × odd = odd   |   odd × even = even   |   even × even = even
Why? An even number contains a factor of \(2\), which can never disappear by multiplying — so the product stays even.
Among \(1,2,3,4,5,6,7\), the odd numbers are \(\{1, 3, 5, 7\}\) — there are \(4\) of them. We must pick both cards from these four:
\[ C^{4}_{2} = \frac{4 \times 3}{2 \times 1} = 6 \]
They are \(\{1,3\},\{1,5\},\{1,7\},\{3,5\},\{3,7\},\{5,7\}\).
\[ P = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} = \frac{6}{21} = \frac{2}{7} \]
✓ Answer: A — \(\dfrac{2}{7}\)
Check the alternatives: \(\frac{12}{49}\) and \(\frac{16}{49}\) would arise if you (wrongly) treated the two draws as ordered with replacement. Since the cards are distinct and drawn together, \(21\) is the correct denominator.

3Alternative Method — Using Conditional Logic

Draw the two cards one after another without replacement (equivalent to drawing together):

\[ P(\text{1st odd}) \times P(\text{2nd odd} \mid \text{1st odd}) = \frac{4}{7} \times \frac{3}{6} \]
\[ = \frac{4}{7} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{7} \]
Meaning of each number: \(\frac{4}{7}\) → 4 odd cards among 7. After taking one odd card, only 3 odd cards remain among the 6 left, giving \(\frac{3}{6}\).

Instead of counting favourable pairs, count the pairs that fail (product even):

Total pairs \(= 21\)
Pairs containing at least one even card \(= 21 - C^{4}_{2} = 21 - 6 = 15\)
\[ P(\text{odd}) = 1 - \frac{15}{21} = 1 - \frac{5}{7} = \frac{2}{7} \]
Why this works: "Product even" and "product odd" are complementary events. Subtracting from 1 is often faster when the "bad" cases are easier to count.

4Exam Tips & Common Traps

  • "At the same time" ⇒ unordered: use \(C^{n}_{r}\), denominator \(= 21\), not \(49\) or \(42\).
  • Odd product ⟺ both factors odd. This single fact collapses the whole question.
  • Odd numbers in \(1\ldots7\): \(1,3,5,7\) — that's \(4\) cards, so \(C^{4}_{2}=6\) pairs.
  • Options \(C\) and \(D\) have denominator \(49\) — a trap for students who assume replacement / ordered pairs.
  • Always reduce the fraction: \(\dfrac{6}{21} = \dfrac{2}{7}\) (option A).
