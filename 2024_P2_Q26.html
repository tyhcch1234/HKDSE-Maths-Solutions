Topic 12 · Straight Lines & Slopes

Step 1 — The two given lines.

Question

Find the constant \(k\) such that the straight lines \[6x-8y=7k \quad\text{and}\quad kx+12y=5\] do not intersect with each other.

A. −16
B. −9
C. 9
D. 16

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 · Slope-intercept form
Slope of a Line in General Form \(ax+by=c\ \Rightarrow\ m=-\dfrac{a}{b}\) \(6x-8y=7k\) has slope \(\dfrac34\); \(kx+12y=5\) has slope \(-\dfrac{k}{12}\).

Rewrite both lines as \(y=mx+c\) so we can compare slopes and intercepts.

\[6x-8y=7k \;\Rightarrow\; -8y = -6x+7k \;\Rightarrow\; y=\frac{3}{4}x-\frac{7k}{8}\]

\[kx+12y=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 12y=-kx+5 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac{k}{12}x+\frac{5}{12}\]

A line written as \(y=mx+c\) shows its slope \(m\) and its \(y\)-intercept \(c\) directly.
Step 2 · Condition for no intersection
Condition for Parallel Lines \(m_1=m_2\ \text{ and }\ c_1\neq c_2\) Parallel and distinct means no intersection: \(-\dfrac{k}{12}=\dfrac34\) with different intercepts.

Two straight lines do not intersect ⇔ they are parallel or coincident (same slope). For them not to overlap,
their \(y\)-intercepts must be different.

Equal slopes: \[-\frac{k}{12}=\frac{3}{4}\]

Different \(y\)-intercepts: \[-\frac{7k}{8}\neq\frac{5}{12}\]

“Do not intersect” = parallel and distinct. If the intercepts were also equal, the lines would coincide and overlap everywhere (infinitely many common points).
Step 3 · Solve for \(k\)

\[-\frac{k}{12}=\frac{3}{4} \;\Rightarrow\; -k = 12\cdot\frac{3}{4}=9 \;\Rightarrow\; k=-9\]

This immediately picks option B.
Step 4 · Verify the lines are distinct

Put \(k=-9\) into the intercepts:

Line 1: \(-\frac{7(-9)}{8}=\frac{63}{8}\)

Line 2: \(\frac{5}{12}\)

Since \(\dfrac{63}{8}\neq\dfrac{5}{12}\),
the intercepts differ → the lines are parallel but distinct,
so they never meet.

If instead the intercepts were equal, the lines would coincide — then they would “intersect” at every point, not “not intersect”.
Answer: \(\;k=-9\;\)  →  Option B

Question. The straight lines \(4x-6y=3k\) and \(kx+9y=7\) do not intersect. Find \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Slope of \(4x-6y=3k\): \(y=\dfrac23x-\dfrac{k}{2}\), so \(m_1=\dfrac23\).
Step 2. Slope of \(kx+9y=7\): \(m_2=-\dfrac{k}{9}\).
Step 3. No intersection requires \(m_1=m_2\): \(-\dfrac{k}{9}=\dfrac23\Rightarrow k=-6\).
Step 4. Check the intercepts: \(\dfrac{3(-6)}{-6}=3\) and \(\dfrac79\) differ, so the lines are parallel and distinct.
Answer: \(k=-6\)
