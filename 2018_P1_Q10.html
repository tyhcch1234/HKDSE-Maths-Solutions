Statistics — Box-and-Whisker Diagram, Range & Inter-quartile Range

Box-and-Whisker Diagram Quartiles Range Inter-quartile Range Data Combination

QQuestion

The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the ages of the clerks in team \(X\) of a company. It is given that the range and the inter-quartile range of this distribution are \(43\) and \(21\) respectively.

Min = 19 \(Q_1\) = 27 Median = 38 \(Q_3\) = a Max = b

(a) Find \(a\) and \(b\). [3 marks]

(b) There are five clerks in team \(Y\) of the company and three of them are of age \(38\). It is given that the range of the ages of the clerks in team \(Y\) is \(20\). Team \(X\) and team \(Y\) are now combined to form a section. The manager of the company claims that the range of the ages of the clerks in the section and the range of the ages of the clerks in team \(X\) must be the same. Do you agree? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

aPart (a) — Find \(a\) and \(b\)

\(Q_3-Q_1 = a-27\) = 12  (want 21) \(b-19\) = 36  (want 43) ✗ not yet
Step 1 Use the inter-quartile range to find \(a\) 1M

The inter-quartile range is \(Q_3-Q_1\). From the diagram \(Q_1=27\) and \(Q_3=a\):

\[ a-27 = 21 \]
Hint: The inter-quartile range is the length of the box (from \(Q_1\) to \(Q_3\)). It ignores the whiskers entirely.
Step 2 Therefore \(a = 48\) 1A
\[ a = 21+27 = 48 \]
\(a = 48\)
Hint: Always add \(Q_1\) to the IQR — a very common slip is to answer \(a=21\).
Step 3 Use the range to find \(b\) 1A

The range is \(\text{Max}-\text{Min}\). The smallest value is \(19\) and the largest value is \(b\):

\[ b-19 = 43 \] \[ b = 43+19 = 62 \]
\(a = 48\) ,   \(b = 62\)
Hint: The range uses the two ends of the whiskers (minimum and maximum), never the median or the quartiles. So the five-number summary of team \(X\) is \(19,\ 27,\ 38,\ 48,\ 62\).

bPart (b) — Is the manager's claim correct?

Range of \(Y\) = 20 Smallest possible age in \(Y\) (3 clerks aged 38) = 18 Combined range = 44   vs   Range of \(X\) = 43
Step 1 How small can the youngest clerk in team \(Y\) be? 1M

Three of the five clerks in \(Y\) are aged \(38\). Since the range of \(Y\) is \(20\), the youngest clerk's age is

\[ 38-20 = 18 \]
Hint: To minimise the combined range we want \(Y\) to reach as low as possible. Because someone in \(Y\) is \(38\) and the range is \(20\), the minimum of \(Y\) can be as low as \(18\). Move the sliders above to see this.
Step 2 Greatest possible range of the section 1A

Combining \(X\) with \(Y\), the smallest age in the section is \(18\), while the largest age in team \(X\) is \(62\). So the greatest possible range of the section is

\[ 62-18 = 44 \]
Hint: \(44 \neq 43\). The range of team \(X\) alone is \(62-19=43\). Adding team \(Y\) can extend the range downward by one year, so the two ranges need not be the same.
Thus, the claim is disagreed: the range of the section can be \(44\), which is different from \(43\).

✓Summary of Answers

  • (a) Inter-quartile range: \(a-27=21 \Rightarrow a = 48\).
    Range: \(b-19=43 \Rightarrow b = 62\).
  • (b) The smallest possible age in team \(Y\) is \(38-20=18\).
  • The greatest possible range of the combined section is \(62-18=44\).
  • Since \(44 \neq 43\), the manager's claim is not agreed (disagreed).
  • Five-number summary of team \(X\): \(19,\ 27,\ 38,\ 48,\ 62\).
