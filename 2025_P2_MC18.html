QQuestion

The figure shows the triangle \(XYZ\). Let \(W\) be a point lying on \(XY\) such that \(ZW \perp XY\). If \(XW = 65\text{ cm}\), \(WZ = 60\text{ cm}\) and \(WZ = 25\text{ cm}\), then \(XY =\)

A. \(144\text{ cm}\)    B. \(145\text{ cm}\)    C. \(156\text{ cm}\)    D. \(169\text{ cm}\) Multiple Choice · 1 mark
Note: the given data are \(XW = 65\text{ cm}\), \(WZ = 60\text{ cm}\), \(ZW = 25\text{ cm}\) — i.e. \(WZ = 25\text{ cm}\) and \(XZ = 60\text{ cm}\) in the standard labelling of the figure.

aPart (a) — Set up the right angle at \(Z\)

Use the given side lengths to recognise that \(\triangle XZW\) is right-angled at \(Z\).

Scale: 1.0×
Use the slider below to compare \(XZ^2 + ZW^2\) with \(XW^2\). Keep it at 1.0 to see the exact equality \(60^2+25^2=65^2\).
Step 11MCompute the squares of the three sides of \(\triangle XZW\).

\[ XZ^2 + WZ^2 = 60^2 + 25^2 = 3600 + 625 = 4225 \]

\[ XW^2 = 65^2 = 4225 \]

Hint: Compare the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides with the square of the longest side.
Step 21AApply the converse of Pythagoras' theorem.

Since \(XZ^2 + WZ^2 = XW^2\), the converse of Pythagoras' theorem gives:

\[ \angle XZW = 90^\circ \]

Hint: If \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) for the three sides of a triangle, then the angle opposite the longest side \(c\) is a right angle.
\(\angle XZW\) is a right angle.

bPart (b) — Find the unknown length \(XY\)

Now use the right angle at \(Z\) to apply Pythagoras' theorem in the larger triangle \(XZY\).

Drag the slider to vary \(XY\). The readout shows the computed \(YZ = \sqrt{XY^2 - 60^2}\). At \(XY=156\), \(YZ = 144\) and \(XY\) matches \(65+25+144 = 234\)? Wait — check the split: \(XW=65\), \(WZ=25\) lies on the base, and \(YZ = 144\).
Step 11MUse the right angle in \(\triangle XZW\) to find \(XZ\).

Actually \(XZ\) is already known: \(XZ = 60\text{ cm}\).
We also know \(WZ = 25\text{ cm}\) and \(XW=65\text{ cm}\).

Hint: The right angle at \(Z\) lets us apply Pythagoras' theorem to the bigger triangle \(XZY\) as well.
Step 21MSet up Pythagoras' theorem in \(\triangle XZY\).

Since \(\angle XZY = 90^\circ\),

\[ XZ^2 + ZY^2 = XY^2 \]

\[ 60^2 + ZY^2 = XY^2 \]

Hint: Note that \(XY = XW + WZ + ZY\) if \(W\) lies between \(X\) and \(Y\), but here \(W\) is on \(XY\) with \(ZW \perp XY\), so \(XY\) is the hypotenuse of \(\triangle XZY\).
Step 31AFind \(ZY\) using the small right triangle \(XZW\).

Wait — \(W\) lies on \(XY\), and \(ZW \perp XY\). So \(XW\) and \(WZ\) are legs of \(\triangle XZW\). But \(XZ = 60\) and \(XW = 65\) are not the perpendicular pair. Let us re-read: \(ZW = 60\), \(WZ = 25\), \(XW = 65\).

Correct data: \(XZ = 60\), \(WZ = 25\), \(XW = 65\).

Since \(\triangle XZW\) is right-angled at \(Z\): \[ XZ^2 + ZW^2 = XW^2 \Rightarrow 60^2 + 25^2 = 65^2 \]

Hint: In the figure, \(XW\) is the hypotenuse of \(\triangle XZW\), so \(XW=65\), \(XZ=60\), \(ZW=25\).
Step 41ACompute \(YZ\) and hence \(XY\).

In \(\triangle XZY\), \(\angle XZY = 90^\circ\):

\[ XZ^2 + ZY^2 = XY^2 \]

But we still need \(YZ\). Notice \(YZ = YW + WZ\)? Actually \(W\) lies on \(XY\), not on \(YZ\).

Use similarity: \(\triangle WYX \sim \triangle XYZ\) (see the official solution).

Hint: The official solution uses similar triangles: \(\dfrac{WY}{XY} = \dfrac{XY}{YZ}\) and \(\angle XYW = \angle ZYX\).
Step 51AUse similar triangles \(\triangle WYX \sim \triangle XYZ\).

\[ \frac{WY}{XY} = \frac{XY}{YZ} \quad\text{and}\quad \angle XYW = \angle ZYX \]

\[ \therefore \triangle WYX \sim \triangle XYZ \]

\[ \frac{XY}{YZ} = \frac{WX}{XZ} = \frac{65}{60} = \frac{13}{12} \]

\[ YZ = \frac{12}{13}\,XY \]

Hint: The ratio of corresponding sides from the similarity gives the key relation \(YZ = \frac{12}{13}XY\).
Step 61AApply Pythagoras in \(\triangle XZY\).

\[ XZ^2 + YZ^2 = XY^2 \]

\[ 60^2 + \left(\frac{12}{13}XY\right)^2 = XY^2 \]

\[ 3600 + \frac{144}{169}XY^2 = XY^2 \]

\[ 3600 = XY^2\left(1 - \frac{144}{169}\right) = XY^2 \cdot \frac{25}{169} \]

\[ XY^2 = 3600 \cdot \frac{169}{25} = 144 \cdot 169 \]

\[ XY = 12 \cdot 13 = 156\text{ cm} \]

\(XY = 156\text{ cm}\) → Answer C.

Summary of Answers

  • \(\triangle XZW\) is right-angled at \(Z\) because \(60^2 + 25^2 = 65^2\).
  • By the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, \(\angle XZW = 90^\circ\).
  • \(\triangle WYX \sim \triangle XYZ\) by the ratio of two sides and the included angle.
  • \(\dfrac{XY}{YZ} = \dfrac{65}{60} = \dfrac{13}{12}\), so \(YZ = \dfrac{12}{13}XY\).
  • Pythagoras in \(\triangle XZY\): \(60^2 + \left(\frac{12}{13}XY\right)^2 = XY^2\).
  • \(XY = 156\text{ cm}\) — Answer C.
