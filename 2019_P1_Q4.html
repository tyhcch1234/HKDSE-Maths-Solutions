HKDSE 2019 Paper 1 · Q4 Polynomials Factorization Difference of Squares

Factorization Challenge

Factorize each of the following expressions.

(a)
\(4m^2 - 9\)
(b)
\(2m^2n + 7mn - 15n\)
(c)
\(4m^2 - 9 - 2m^2n - 7mn + 15n\)

[4 marks]

Visual Demo — Difference of Squares

Part (a) uses the identity \(a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)\). Adjust \(m\) to see how the area of a square minus a smaller square rearranges into a rectangle.

\(4m^2 - 9 = (2m)^2 - 3^2\)

Step-by-Step Solution

Click on each step to reveal the working. Try to solve it yourself first!

Part (a) — Factorize \(4m^2 - 9\)
💡 Hint: This is a difference of two squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)\).
Identify \(a\) and \(b\): \(4m^2 = (2m)^2\) and \(9 = 3^2\).
\(4m^2 - 9 = (2m)^2 - 3^2 = (2m + 3)(2m - 3)\)
Answer: \((2m + 3)(2m - 3)\)   [1A]
Part (b) — Start factorizing \(2m^2n + 7mn - 15n\)
💡 Hint: First take out the common factor \(n\) (since every term contains \(n\)).
\(2m^2n + 7mn - 15n = n(2m^2 + 7m - 15)\)

Now we need to factorize the quadratic \(2m^2 + 7m - 15\).

Part (b) — Factorize \(2m^2 + 7m - 15\)
💡 Hint: Look for two numbers that multiply to \(2 \times (-15) = -30\) and add to \(7\).
Those numbers are \(10\) and \(-3\).
\(2m^2 + 10m - 3m - 15\)
\(= 2m(m + 5) - 3(m + 5)\)
\(= (2m - 3)(m + 5)\)

So \(2m^2n + 7mn - 15n = n(2m - 3)(m + 5)\).

Answer: \(n(2m - 3)(m + 5)\)   [1A]
Part (c) — Recognize the structure
💡 Hint: Notice that part (c) is \(4m^2 - 9\) minus \(2m^2n + 7mn - 15n\).
Use the results from (a) and (b) to factorize!
\(4m^2 - 9 - 2m^2n - 7mn + 15n\)
\(= 4m^2 - 9 - (2m^2n + 7mn - 15n)\)

Substitute the factorized forms from (a) and (b).

Part (c) — Final factorization
\(= (2m + 3)(2m - 3) - n(2m - 3)(m + 5)\)
Common factor: \((2m - 3)\)
\(= (2m - 3)\big[(2m + 3) - n(m + 5)\big]\)
\(= (2m - 3)(2m + 3 - mn - 5n)\)
\(= (2m - 3)(2m - mn - 5n + 3)\)
Answer: \((2m - 3)(2m - mn - 5n + 3)\)   [1M, 1A]
🎉 Excellent! You have gone through all the steps. Review the key ideas below.

Key Takeaways

  • Difference of squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Common factor: Always look for a common factor across all terms first.
  • Grouping: For four terms, group them into pairs and factorize each pair, then take out the common binomial factor.
  • Link parts: In DSE, part (c) often builds on (a) and (b). Use previous answers to save time!
