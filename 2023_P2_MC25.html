Q25 The Question MC

Find the constant \(a\) such that the straight lines \[ 2x+(a+3)y-5=0 \quad\text{and}\quad ax-4y+1=0 \] are perpendicular to each other.

A. \(-6\)
B. \(-5\)
C. \(-2\)
D. \(4\)

Part (a) Find the slopes of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\)

a = 0.0
m₁ = — m₂ = —

Drag the slider to change \(a\) and watch how the slopes \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) of the two lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) change. Notice that \(m_1\) depends only on \(a+3\), while \(m_2\) depends only on \(a\).

1Write \(L_1\) in slope-intercept form

Rearrange \(2x+(a+3)y-5=0\):

\[ (a+3)y = -2x+5 \]
Hint: To get the slope, we must make \(y\) the subject. This is valid provided \(a+3\ne 0\), i.e. \(a\ne -3\).
2Obtain the slope of \(L_1\)
\[ y = -\frac{2}{a+3}x + \frac{5}{a+3} \]

So the slope of \(L_1\) is

\[ m_1 = -\frac{2}{a+3} \]
Hint: For an equation \(Ax+By+C=0\), the slope is \(m = -\dfrac{A}{B}\). Here \(m_1 = -\dfrac{2}{a+3}\).
3Obtain the slope of \(L_2\)

For \(ax-4y+1=0\), rearrange to \(4y = ax+1\), giving \(y=\dfrac{a}{4}x+\dfrac{1}{4}\). Hence

\[ m_2 = \frac{a}{4} \]
Hint: Again use \(m=-\dfrac{A}{B}\): here \(A=a\) and \(B=-4\), so \(m_2=-\dfrac{a}{-4}=\dfrac{a}{4}\).
Answer (a): \(\;m_1=-\dfrac{2}{a+3}\;\) and \(\;m_2=\dfrac{a}{4}.\)

Part (b) Apply the perpendicular condition

m₁ · m₂ = — product ≠ −1

Using the same \(a\)-slider, observe the product \(m_1 \cdot m_2\). When the product equals \(-1\), the two lines meet at a right angle (the marker ◻ at the intersection turns green).

1Check the case \(a=-3\)

If \(a=-3\), then \(L_1: 2x-5=0\) is a vertical line, while \(L_2: -3x-4y+1=0\) is not horizontal. A vertical line is perpendicular only to a horizontal line, so in this case \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are not perpendicular.

Hint: Always check whether the coefficient of \(y\) could be zero before dividing by it — this avoids losing the special case \(a=-3\).
2Set up the perpendicular condition

For \(a\ne -3\), two non-vertical lines are perpendicular exactly when the product of their slopes is \(-1\):

\[ m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1 \] \[ \left(-\frac{2}{a+3}\right)\times\frac{a}{4} = -1 \]
Hint: The condition \(m_1 m_2 = -1\) works only after both slopes exist. Here \(m_2=\tfrac a4\) always exists, and \(m_1\) exists once \(a\ne -3\).
3Solve for \(a\)
\[ -\frac{2a}{4(a+3)} = -1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{2a}{4(a+3)} = 1 \] \[ 2a = 4(a+3) \] \[ 2a = 4a+12 \] \[ -2a = 12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a = -6 \]
Hint: Cross-multiplying is valid since \(a\ne -3\). The solution \(a=-6\) also satisfies the discarded case \(a\ne -3\), so it is the genuine answer.
4Verify with slopes

With \(a=-6\):

\[ m_1 = -\frac{2}{-6+3} = -\frac{2}{-3} = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad m_2=\frac{-6}{4}=-\frac{3}{2} \] \[ m_1 m_2 = \frac{2}{3}\times\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right) = -1 \;\checkmark \]
Answer (b): \(\;a=-6\;\) → Option A.

✔ Summary of Answers

  • Slope of \(L_1\): \(\;m_1 = -\dfrac{2}{a+3}\quad (a\ne -3)\)
  • Slope of \(L_2\): \(\;m_2 = \dfrac{a}{4}\)
  • Perpendicular condition: \(\;m_1 m_2 = -1 \Rightarrow 2a = 4(a+3)\)
  • Value of \(a\): \(\;a = -6\) → Option A
