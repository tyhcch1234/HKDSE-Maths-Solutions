HKDSE 2015 Paper 2 — Q35

Let $z=(a+5)i^{6}+(a-3)i^{7}$, where $a$ is a real number. If $z$ is a real number, then $a=$

A. $-5$
B. $-3$
C. $3$
D. $5$
1 Recall the powers of $i$
Powers of the Imaginary Unit\(i^{4n+r}=i^{r}\)The powers repeat with period 4, so \(i^{6}=i^{2}=-1\) and \(i^{7}=i^{3}=-i\).

The powers cycle with period 4:

$i^{1}=i,\quad i^{2}=-1,\quad i^{3}=-i,\quad i^{4}=1,\quad i^{5}=i,\ \dots$

So reduce the exponents modulo 4:   $6 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and $7 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$.

$i^{6}=i^{2}=-1,\qquad i^{7}=i^{3}=-i$

Hint: Divide the exponent by 4 and take the remainder — that remainder gives the power of $i$.
2 Substitute into the expression for $z$

$$z=(a+5)i^{6}+(a-3)i^{7}$$ $$=(a+5)(-1)+(a-3)(-i)$$

Hint: Keep the terms separate: one contributes to the real part, the other to the imaginary part.
3 Separate the real and imaginary parts
Standard Form of a Complex Number\(z=x+yi\quad \text{where}\ x=\operatorname{Re}(z),\ y=\operatorname{Im}(z)\)Collecting terms gives real part \(-a-5\) and imaginary part \(-a+3\).

$$z=\underbrace{(-a-5)}_{\text{real part}}+\underbrace{(-a+3)}_{\text{imaginary part}}\,i$$

Write it in the standard form $z=x+yi$:

Re$(z)$
$x=-a-5$
Im$(z)$
$y=-a+3$
Hint: A number is real exactly when its imaginary part is zero, i.e. $y=0$.
4 Impose the condition "$z$ is real"
Condition for a Real Number\(z\ \text{is real}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \operatorname{Im}(z)=0\)So \(-a+3=0\) gives \(a=3\); the real part \(-a-5\) never affects the test.

Since $z$ is real, its imaginary part must be zero:

$-a+3=0$

$a=3$

Hint: The real part $-a-5=-8$ is then just a real constant — it never affects the "is $z$ real?" test.
Answer: C  ( $a=3$ )

Question. Let \(z=(b-2)i^{9}+(b+7)i^{10}\), where \(b\) is a real number. If \(z\) is a real number, find \(b\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Reduce the indices modulo 4: \(9\equiv1\) and \(10\equiv2\), so \(i^{9}=i\) and \(i^{10}=-1\).
Step 2. Substitute: \(z=(b-2)i+(b+7)(-1)=-(b+7)+(b-2)i\).
Step 3. Now \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=-(b+7)\) and \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=b-2\).
Step 4. \(z\) is real when \(b-2=0\), i.e. \(b=2\). Then \(z=-9\), which is real ✓.
Answer: \(b=2\)
