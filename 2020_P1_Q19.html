Q19 Question

PQRS is a quadrilateral paper card, where \(PQ = 60\) cm, \(PS = 40\) cm, \(\angle PQR = 30^\circ\), \(\angle PRQ = 55^\circ\) and \(\angle QPS = 120^\circ\). The paper card is held with \(QR\) lying on the horizontal ground as shown in Figure 3.

(a) Find the length of \(RS\). [3 marks]

(b) Find the area of the paper card. [2 marks]

(c) It is given that the angle between the paper card and the horizontal ground is \(32^\circ\).

(i) Find the shortest distance from \(P\) to the horizontal ground. [3 marks]

(ii) A student claims that the angle between \(RS\) and the horizontal ground is at most \(20^\circ\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [4 marks]

(a) Find the length of \(RS\)

\(\triangle PQR\) (fully solvable) \(\triangle PRS\) \(RS\) — required
Step 11M
In \(\triangle PQR\), two angles and one side are known, so apply the sine rule to find \(PR\): \[ \frac{PR}{\sin\angle PQR} = \frac{PQ}{\sin\angle PRQ} \] \[ \frac{PR}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{60}{\sin 55^\circ} \] \[ PR = \frac{60\sin 30^\circ}{\sin 55^\circ} \approx 36.62323766 \ \text{cm} \]
Hint: Always pair a side with the angle opposite it. \(PQ=60\) is opposite \(\angle PRQ = 55^\circ\); \(PR\) is opposite \(\angle PQR=30^\circ\).
Step 21M
Find \(\angle QPR\) using the angle sum of a triangle: \[ \angle QPR = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 55^\circ = 95^\circ \] Since \(\angle QPS = 120^\circ\), \[ \angle RPS = \angle QPS - \angle QPR = 120^\circ - 95^\circ = 25^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle RPS\) is the leftover angle after removing \(\angle QPR\) from \(\angle QPS\). Sketching the vertex \(P\) with the three rays \(PQ, PR, PS\) makes this obvious.
Step 31A
Now apply the cosine rule in \(\triangle PRS\), where \(PS = 40\) and the included angle is \(\angle RPS = 25^\circ\): \[ RS^2 = PS^2 + PR^2 - 2(PS)(PR)\cos\angle RPS \] \[ RS^2 \approx 40^2 + 36.62323766^2 - 2(40)(36.62323766)\cos 25^\circ \] \[ RS^2 \approx 285.8711059 \]
\( RS \approx 16.90879944 \approx \boxed{16.9 \text{ cm}}\)
Hint: The two sides used in the cosine rule must enclose the known angle. Here \(PS=40\), \(PR\approx36.62\) enclose \(\angle RPS=25^\circ\).

(b) Find the area of the paper card

\(\triangle PQR\) area \(\triangle PRS\) area Diagonal \(PR\) splits the card
Area ≈ 1404.07 cm²
Step 11M
The diagonal \(PR\) splits the card into \(\triangle PQR\) and \(\triangle PRS\). Sum the two areas using \(\tfrac12 ab\sin C\): \[ \text{Area} = \tfrac12(PQ)(PR)\sin\angle QPR + \tfrac12(PR)(PS)\sin\angle RPS \] \[ \approx \tfrac12(60)(36.62323766)\sin 95^\circ + \tfrac12(36.62323766)(40)\sin 25^\circ \] \[ \approx 1094.953633 + 309.1156035 \]
Hint: Reuse \(PR \approx 36.62323766\) from part (a) — do not round it early, or the final answer may drop a mark.
Step 21A
\[ \text{Area} \approx 1404.069236 \]
Area \(\approx \boxed{1400 \text{ cm}^2}\) (to 3 significant figures)
Hint: The question asks for 3 significant figures, so \(1404.07\) rounds to \(1400\). Keep the full value in your calculator until the last step.

(c)(i) Shortest distance from \(P\) to the ground

Paper card (plane) Horizontal ground \(PH \perp QR\) — steepest line \(PG\) — vertical height
PG = 30 sin 32° ≈ 15.9 cm
Step 11M
Let \(H\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(P\) to \(QR\). Since \(QR\) lies on the horizontal ground, \(PH\) is the steepest line on the card — the line of greatest slope. It therefore makes the given maximum angle \(32^\circ\) with the ground. \[ PH = PQ\sin\angle PQH \] \[ PH = 60\sin 30^\circ = 30\ \text{cm} \]
Hint: In the right triangle \(PQH\), \(PQ = 60\) is the hypotenuse and \(PH\) is opposite the \(30^\circ\) angle at \(Q\).
Step 21M
Let \(G\) be the projection of \(P\) on the horizontal ground. The angle between the card and the ground is measured in the plane perpendicular to \(QR\) through \(PH\), so \[ \angle PHG = 32^\circ \] In the right triangle \(PGH\): \[ PG = PH\sin\angle PHG = 30\sin 32^\circ \] \[ PG \approx 15.89757793 \ \text{cm} \]
Hint: \(PG\) is the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to the ground plane — that is exactly the "shortest distance" required.
Step 31A
Shortest distance \(= PG \approx \boxed{15.9 \text{ cm}}\)
Hint: Move the Tilt angle slider — when it is \(90^\circ\), \(PG = PH = 30\) cm (the card stands upright); when it is \(0^\circ\), \(PG = 0\) cm (the card lies flat).

(c)(ii) Is the angle between \(RS\) and the ground at most \(20^\circ\)?

\(QR\) (on the ground) Extended \(PS\) & \(QR\) meet at \(T\) Similar triangles \(\triangle PKT \sim \triangle PGT\) Foot of \(\perp\) from \(K\) to the ground
∠SRK ≈ 18.26° ≤ 20° ✓
Step 11M
Let \(K\) be the projection of \(S\) on the ground, and let \(T\) be the point where \(PS\) produced meets \(QR\) produced.
Because \(PG \perp\) ground and \(SK \perp\) ground, \(PG /\!/ SK\), so \[ \triangle SKT \sim \triangle PGT \] Also from part (a), \(PT = PQ = 60\) cm (isosceles \(\triangle PQT\) with base angles \(30^\circ\)), and \(PS = 40\) cm, so \(ST = 60 - 40 = 20\) cm. \[ \frac{SK}{PG} = \frac{ST}{PT} = \frac{60-40}{60} = \frac{1}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad SK = \frac13 PG \]
Hint: Use the whole length \(PT = PQ = 60\), not \(PS = 40\), in the ratio — \(T\) is beyond \(S\), not between \(P\) and \(S\).
Step 21M
From (c)(i), \(PG \approx 15.89757793\) cm, hence \[ SK \approx \frac13 (15.89757793) \approx 5.299192643 \ \text{cm} \] The angle between the line \(RS\) and the horizontal ground is \(\angle SRK\), where \(K\) is the projection of \(S\) on the ground: \[ \sin\angle SRK = \frac{SK}{RS} \]
Hint: \(RS\) lies in the card, and \(RK\) is its projection on the ground. The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection — that is \(\angle SRK\).
Step 31M
Using \(RS \approx 16.90879944\) cm from part (a): \[ \sin\angle SRK \approx \frac{5.299192643}{16.90879944} \approx 0.3133978 \] \[ \angle SRK \approx 18.26416068^\circ \]
Hint: Keep \(RS\) in full precision here; rounding to \(16.9\) would give \(\angle SRK \approx 18.28^\circ\) — the conclusion is unchanged but the working loses accuracy.
Step 41A
Since \(\angle SRK \approx 18.26^\circ \le 20^\circ\),
Yes — the claim is correct. The angle between \(RS\) and the horizontal ground is \(\approx 18.26^\circ\), which is at most \(20^\circ\).
Hint: A "claim" question always needs a conclusion with reason. Quote the numeric angle and compare it with \(20^\circ\) explicitly.

Answers at a glance

  • (a) \(PR = \dfrac{60\sin 30^\circ}{\sin 55^\circ} \approx 36.62\) cm, \(\angle RPS = 120^\circ - 95^\circ = 25^\circ\),   \(RS \approx\) 16.9 cm.
  • (b) Area \(= \tfrac12(60)(PR)\sin 95^\circ + \tfrac12(PR)(40)\sin 25^\circ \approx\) 1400 cm² (3 s.f.).
  • (c)(i) \(PH = 60\sin 30^\circ = 30\) cm,   \(PG = 30\sin 32^\circ \approx\) 15.9 cm.
  • (c)(ii) \(\triangle SKT \sim \triangle PGT \Rightarrow SK = \tfrac13 PG \approx 5.30\) cm;   \(\sin\angle SRK = \dfrac{SK}{RS} \approx 0.3134\), so \(\angle SRK \approx\) 18.26° < 20° — the claim is correct.
