Question. In the figure, $O$ is the centre of the circle $ABCDEF$. $\triangle PQR$ intersects the circle at $A,B,C,D,E$ and $F$. If $\angle QPR = 38^\circ$ and $AB = CD = EF$, then $\angle QOR =$
A. $100^\circ$ B. $123^\circ$ C. $112^\circ$ D. $109^\circ$
STEP 1 · Drop perpendiculars from O
Equal Chords Equidistant from the Centre \(AB=CD=EF\;\Longrightarrow\;OX=OY=OZ\) The three equal chords \(AB\), \(CD\), \(EF\) lie on the sides of \(\triangle PQR\), so \(O\) is equidistant from them.

Let $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ be the feet of the perpendicular from $O$ to $QR$, $RP$ and $PQ$ respectively.

Since $AB = CD = EF$ (equal chords),
the centre $O$ is equidistant from the chords: $OX = OY = OZ$. So $O$ is the incentre of $\triangle PQR$ and $OP, OQ, OR$ are the angle bisectors.

STEP 2 · Angle sum in quadrilateral PYOZ
Angle Sum of a Quadrilateral \(\angle A+\angle B+\angle C+\angle D=360^\circ\) In \(PYOZ\) two angles are \(90^\circ\) and \(\angle YPZ=38^\circ\), so the fourth angle is \(142^\circ\).

In quadrilateral $PYOZ$: $\angle PYO = \angle PZO = 90^\circ$ (construction) and $\angle YPZ = \angle QPR = 38^\circ$.

$$\angle YOZ = 360^\circ - 38^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = 142^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ sum of polygon})$$

STEP 3 · Angle sum at point O

Angles at a point: $\angle XOY + \angle ZOX = 360^\circ - \angle YOZ = 360^\circ - 142^\circ = 218^\circ$.

STEP 4 · Congruent right triangles
RHS Congruence \(\angle OXQ=\angle OZQ=90^\circ,\; OQ \text{ common},\; OX=OZ \;\Longrightarrow\; \triangle QOX\cong\triangle QOZ\) Both triangles are right-angled, share \(OQ\) and have equal legs \(OX=OZ\) coming from the equal chords.

In $\triangle QOX$ and $\triangle QOZ$:

$\angle OXQ = \angle OZQ = 90^\circ$ (construction), $OQ = OQ$ (common side), $OX = OZ$ (equal chords,
equidistant from centre).

$\therefore \triangle QOX \cong \triangle QOZ$ (RHS) and so $\angle QOX = \angle QOZ$, giving $$\angle QOX = \tfrac{1}{2}\angle ZOX .$$

STEP 5 · Similarly for R

Similarly $\triangle ROX \cong \triangle ROY$,
hence $$\angle ROX = \tfrac{1}{2}\angle XOY .$$

STEP 6 · Add up to get ∠QOR

$$\angle QOR = \angle QOX + \angle ROX = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\angle ZOX + \angle XOY\right) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 218^\circ = 109^\circ .$$

$\angle QOR = 109^\circ$ Correct option: D

Question. In the figure, \(AB=CD=EF\) are chords of a circle with centre \(O\), lying on \(QR\), \(RP\), \(PQ\) respectively. If \(\angle QPR=46^\circ\), then \(\angle QOR=\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Equal chords are equidistant from \(O\), so \(OX=OY=OZ\);
hence \(O\) is the incentre of \(\triangle PQR\) and \(OP\), \(OQ\), \(OR\) bisect the angles at \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\).
Step 2. In quadrilateral \(PYOZ\), \(\angle PYO=\angle PZO=90^\circ\), so \(\angle YOZ=360^\circ-46^\circ-90^\circ-90^\circ=134^\circ\).
Step 3. Angles at \(O\): \(\angle XOY+\angle ZOX=360^\circ-134^\circ=226^\circ\).
Step 4. \(OX=OZ\), \(OQ\) is common and both triangles are right-angled,
so \(\triangle QOX\cong\triangle QOZ\) (RHS) and \(\angle QOX=\tfrac{1}{2}\angle ZOX\);
similarly \(\angle ROX=\tfrac{1}{2}\angle XOY\).
Step 5. \(\angle QOR=\angle QOX+\angle ROX=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\angle ZOX+\angle XOY\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\times226^\circ=113^\circ\).
Answer: \(\angle QOR=113^\circ\)
Key insight: equal chords $AB=CD=EF$ $\Rightarrow$ $O$ is equidistant from all three sides of $\triangle PQR$ $\Rightarrow$ $O$ is the incentre. Then each of $OQ$ and $OR$ bisects its triangle angle, and $\angle QOR = 180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\big(\angle Q + \angle R\big) = 180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}(180^\circ - 38^\circ) = 109^\circ$ immediately.
