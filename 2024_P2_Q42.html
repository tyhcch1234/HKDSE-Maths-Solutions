Question

A queue is formed by 2 managers and 7 officers. If no managers are next to each other, how many different queues can be formed?

🧩 Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to think ahead before opening!

The 7 officers can be arranged in a queue in:

\[ {}_7P_7 = 7! = 5040 \text{ ways} \]
💡 Hint: Since all 7 officers are distinct, we arrange all of them: \(7!\).

When the 7 officers stand in a row, they create gaps around and between them:

↓ O ↓ O ↓ O ↓ O ↓ O ↓ O ↓ O ↓

Number of gaps = \( 7 + 1 = 8 \).

💡 Hint: With \(n\) people in a row, there are \(n+1\) gaps (including the ends).

We must choose 2 different gaps from the 8 available gaps, and then arrange the 2 distinct managers in those gaps.

Number of ways:

\[ {}_8P_2 = 8 \times 7 = 56 \]

Each manager is distinct, so order matters — we use permutations \({}_8P_2\), not combinations.

💡 Hint: Choosing 2 gaps from 8 and arranging 2 managers: \({}_8P_2 = \frac{8!}{(8-2)!} = 8 \times 7\).

The total number of different queues is the product of the two stages:

\[ \text{Total} = {}_8P_2 \times 7! \]
💡 Hint: Multiplication principle — if one task can be done in \(m\) ways and another in \(n\) ways, together they can be done in \(m \times n\) ways.

Compute:

\[ {}_8P_2 \times 7! = 56 \times 5040 = 282\,240 \]

Therefore, the answer is C. 282 240.

📝 Key Formulas to Remember

Permutations

\[ {}_nP_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!} \]

Arranging \(r\) items out of \(n\) distinct items (order matters).

Gap Method

\[ \text{For } n \text{ in a row} \Rightarrow n+1 \text{ gaps} \]

Useful when no two specified items can be adjacent.
