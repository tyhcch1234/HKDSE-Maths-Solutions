Question

Susan sells two cars for $80 080 each. She gains 30% on one and loses 30% on the other. After the two transactions, Susan

  • A loses $15 840.
  • B gains $5 544.
  • C gains $10 296.
  • D has no gain and no loss.
💡

Key Concept — The Golden Formula

\[ \text{Selling Price} = \text{Cost Price} \times (1 \pm \text{Profit/Loss}\%) \]

To find the Cost Price from selling price, we divide by the multiplier.

  • Gain 30% → multiplier \(= 1 + 30\% = 1.3\)
  • Lose 30% → multiplier \(= 1 - 30\% = 0.7\)
  • Total profit = Total Selling Price − Total Cost Price

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

Susan sells two different cars, each for $80,080.

  • On Car A, she makes a 30% gain (profit).
  • On Car B, she makes a 30% loss.

We need to find the overall profit or loss after both transactions.

⚠️ Hint: A 30% gain and a 30% loss do not cancel out when the selling prices are the same. The costs are different.

Let the cost price of Car A be \(C_A\).

Since she gains 30%:

\[ C_A \times (1 + 30\%) = 80\,080 \] \[ C_A \times 1.3 = 80\,080 \] \[ C_A = \frac{80\,080}{1.3} = \$61\,600 \]
✅ Check: \(61\,600 \times 1.3 = 80\,080\) ✓

Let the cost price of Car B be \(C_B\).

Since she loses 30%:

\[ C_B \times (1 - 30\%) = 80\,080 \] \[ C_B \times 0.7 = 80\,080 \] \[ C_B = \frac{80\,080}{0.7} = \$114\,400 \]
✅ Check: \(114\,400 \times 0.7 = 80\,080\) ✓

Total Selling Price

2 × $80,080

$160,160

Total Cost Price

$61,600 + $114,400

$176,000

Profit / Loss

$160,160 − $176,000

−$15,840

\[ \text{Profit} = 80\,080 \times 2 - (61\,600 + 114\,400) \] \[ = 160\,160 - 176\,000 = -15\,840 \]

Since the result is negative, Susan suffers a loss of $15,840.

✓

Final Answer

Susan loses $15,840.  →  Option A

The key insight: because the selling prices are equal, the 30% loss on the more expensive car outweighs the 30% gain on the cheaper car.
