Functions and Graphs — Factor Theorem & Optimisation of a Rectangle Area

Factor Theorem Cubic Equations Area of Rectangle Repeated Roots Number of Real Solutions

📘 Question

(a) Find the value of \(k\) such that \(x-2\) is a factor of \(kx^{3}-21x^{2}+24x-4\). 2 marks

(b) Figure 4 shows the graph of \(y=15x^{2}-63x+72\). \(Q\) is a variable point on the graph in the first quadrant. \(P\) and \(R\) are the feet of the perpendiculars from \(Q\) to the \(x\)-axis and the \(y\)-axis respectively.

(i) Let \((m,0)\) be the coordinates of \(P\). Express the area of the rectangle \(OPQR\) in terms of \(m\).

(ii) Are there three different positions of \(Q\) such that the area of the rectangle \(OPQR\) is \(12\)? Explain your answer. 5 marks

PART (a) Finding the value of \(k\)

Figure: graph of \(f(x)=kx^{3}-21x^{2}+24x-4\) — move the slider to see when the curve passes through \(x=2\)
1
1M
By the Factor Theorem, \(x-2\) is a factor of \(f(x)=kx^{3}-21x^{2}+24x-4\) if and only if \(f(2)=0\). Substitute \(x=2\): \[k(2)^{3}-21(2)^{2}+24(2)-4=0\] \[8k-84+48-4=0\] \[8k-40=0\]
Hint: The Factor Theorem says: \(x-a\) is a factor of a polynomial \(\iff\) the polynomial evaluated at \(a\) equals zero. Always substitute the root, then solve for the unknown.
2
1A
\[8k=40 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=5\]
Answer (a): \(\;k=5\)

PART (b)(i) Area of rectangle \(OPQR\) in terms of \(m\)

Figure 4: \(Q\) on \(y=15x^{2}-63x+72\). Slide the point \(Q\) along the curve; the dashed rectangle \(OPQR\) updates live.
1
1M
Since \(P=(m,0)\) lies below \(Q\) and \(Q\) is on the curve, the \(y\)-coordinate of \(Q\) is \[y_Q=15m^{2}-63m+72.\] So \(Q=\bigl(m,\;15m^{2}-63m+72\bigr)\).

Because \(OPQR\) is a rectangle with \(O=(0,0)\): \[\text{width }OP=m,\qquad \text{height }PQ=15m^{2}-63m+72.\]
Hint: \(P\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(Q\) to the \(x\)-axis, so \(Q\) and \(P\) share the same \(x\)-coordinate \(m\). The \(y\)-coordinate of \(Q\) is simply the value of the function at \(x=m\).
2
1A
\[\text{Area of }OPQR = OP\times PQ = m\,(15m^{2}-63m+72)\] \[=15m^{3}-63m^{2}+72m\]
Answer (b)(i): \(\;\text{Area}=15m^{3}-63m^{2}+72m \;\text{square units}\)

PART (b)(ii) Are there three positions of \(Q\) with area \(=12\)?

Graph of \(A(m)=15m^{3}-63m^{2}+72m\). The horizontal line \(A=12\) intersects the curve at exactly two distinct \(m\)-values in the first quadrant.
1
1M
Set the area found in (b)(i) equal to \(12\): \[15m^{3}-63m^{2}+72m=12\] Divide every term by \(3\): \[5m^{3}-21m^{2}+24m-4=0\]
Hint: Dividing a cubic equation by a common constant does not change its roots — this makes the numbers smaller and easier to factorise.
2
1M
Try \(m=2\) (this is hinted by (a), since \(k=5\) makes \(x-2\) a factor of the related cubic): \[5(2)^{3}-21(2)^{2}+24(2)-4=40-84+48-4=0\;\checkmark\] So \((m-2)\) is a factor. By long division / grouping: \[5m^{3}-21m^{2}+24m-4=(m-2)\,(5m^{2}-11m+2)\]
Hint: Look back at part (a)! The polynomial \(5m^{3}-21m^{2}+24m-4\) is exactly \(km^{3}-21m^{2}+24m-4\) with \(k=5\). Since \(x-2\) is a factor there, \(m-2\) is a factor here — a classic exam linkage.
3
1M+1A
Factorise the quadratic: \[5m^{2}-11m+2=(5m-1)(m-2)\] Hence \[5m^{3}-21m^{2}+24m-4=(m-2)^{2}(5m-1)=0\] So the solutions are \[m=2\ \text{(repeated)}\quad\text{or}\quad m=\tfrac{1}{5}.\]
Hint: \((m-2)^2\) means \(m=2\) is a double root — it is only ONE position of \(Q\), not two. Graphically, the curve touches \(A=12\) there but does not cross.
4
1A
Since \(Q\) must lie in the first quadrant, we need \(m>0\) and \(y_Q>0\). Both \(m=2\) and \(m=\tfrac{1}{5}\) are positive, and \[y_Q(2)=15(4)-63(2)+72=60-126+72=6>0,\] \[y_Q\!\left(\tfrac15\right)=15\!\left(\tfrac1{25}\right)-63\!\left(\tfrac15\right)+72=\tfrac35-\tfrac{63}{5}+72=\tfrac{300}{5}=60>0.\] So both positions of \(Q\) are valid, giving exactly two distinct positions: \[Q=\left(2,\,6\right)\quad\text{and}\quad Q=\left(\tfrac15,\,60\right).\]
Answer (b)(ii): There are only two different positions of \(Q\) (namely \(m=2\) and \(m=\tfrac15\)). Hence there are NOT three different positions of \(Q\) such that the area of \(OPQR\) is \(12\).

✅ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(k=5\)
  • (b)(i) Area of rectangle \(OPQR = 15m^{3}-63m^{2}+72m\)
  • (b)(ii) No — there are only two positions of \(Q\) with area \(12\): \(m=2\) (double root) and \(m=\tfrac15\).
