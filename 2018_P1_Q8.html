Angles in a circle — parallel chords & intersecting chords
Parallel Chords \(AB \parallel ED\) Angle in Alternate Segment Interior Angles of a Triangle Vertically Opposite Angles

Q8 The Question

5 marks

In Figure 1, \(ABCDE\) is a circle. It is given that \(AB \parallel ED\). \(AD\) and \(BE\) intersect at the point \(F\).

Express \(x\) and \(y\) in terms of \(\theta\).

Here \(x = \angle BAD\), \(\;y = \angle AFB\), and \(\theta = \angle BCD\).

a Express \(x\) in terms of \(\theta\)

2 marks  ·  1A + 1M
72°
\(\theta =\) 72°
\(x = \angle BAD = 180°-\theta =\) 108°
STEP 1 1A

Since \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\) lie on the same circle, the quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral. Therefore its opposite angles are supplementary:

\[ \angle BAD + \angle BCD = 180^\circ \] Substituting \(x = \angle BAD\) and \(\theta = \angle BCD\): \[ x + \theta = 180^\circ \]
Hint: look at the quadrilateral formed by the four points \(A,B,C,D\) on the circle — the two angles at opposite corners \(A\) and \(C\) must add up to \(180^\circ\).
STEP 2 1M

Rearranging the equation from Step 1 to make \(x\) the subject:

\[ x = 180^\circ - \theta \]
\(\boxed{x = 180^\circ - \theta}\)
Hint: drag the slider above. As \(\theta\) increases, \(x = 180^\circ-\theta\) decreases — the two angles always sum to exactly \(180^\circ\).
0 / 2

b Express \(y\) in terms of \(\theta\)

3 marks  ·  1M + 1M + 1A
72°
\(\angle ADE =\) 108°
\(\angle BED =\) 108°
\(y = \angle AFB = 2\theta - 180° =\) 36°
STEP 1 1M

Because \(AB \parallel ED\), alternate angles (Z-angles) with the transversal \(AD\) are equal:

\[ \angle ADE = \angle BAD = x = 180^\circ - \theta \]
Hint: extend the parallel lines \(AB\) and \(ED\) in your mind — the line \(AD\) cuts across them, so \(\angle BAD\) and \(\angle ADE\) are alternate angles.
STEP 2 1M

Similarly, with the transversal \(BE\) and the parallel lines \(AB \parallel ED\):

\[ \angle BED = \angle ABE \] But \(\angle ABE\) subtends the same chord \(AE\) as \(\angle ADE\) (both are angles in the same segment on chord \(AE\)), so \[ \angle BED = \angle ABE = \angle ADE = 180^\circ - \theta \]
Hint: the equal-length "Z" configuration appears again — flip the diagram and the two angles on the perpendicular-looking sides are still equal in the same segment.
STEP 3 1A

Now apply the angle sum of the triangle \(\triangle FED\) (the triangle containing the vertically opposite angle \(y\)):

\[ 180^\circ = \angle FED + \angle FDE + \angle DFE \] \[ 180^\circ = (180^\circ - \theta) + (180^\circ - \theta) + \angle DFE \] \[ \angle DFE = 180^\circ - (180^\circ-\theta) - (180^\circ-\theta) = 2\theta - 180^\circ \] Since \(y = \angle AFB\) and \(\angle AFB\) is vertically opposite to \(\angle DFE\): \[ y = 2\theta - 180^\circ \]
\(\boxed{y = 2\theta - 180^\circ}\)
Hint: use the slider to check. When \(\theta = 100^\circ\), we get \(y = 2(100)-180 = 20^\circ\), and indeed \(x = 80^\circ\) still satisfies \(x = 180^\circ-\theta\).
0 / 3

✦ Final Answers at a Glance

  • Part (a):   \(\displaystyle x = 180^\circ - \theta\)
  • Part (b):   \(\displaystyle y = 2\theta - 180^\circ\)
  • Key fact 1: cyclic quad \(ABCD\) \(\Rightarrow \angle BAD + \angle BCD = 180^\circ\)
  • Key fact 2: \(AB \parallel ED\) \(\Rightarrow \angle ADE = \angle BAD\) and \(\angle BED = \angle ABE\)
  • Key fact 3: angles in \(\triangle FED\) \(\Rightarrow \angle DFE = 180^\circ - \angle ADE - \angle BED\)
  • Key fact 4: \(\angle AFB = \angle DFE\) (vertically opposite angles)
