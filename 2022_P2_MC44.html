Statistics — Effects of a linear transformation on the median, mean, standard deviation and standard score
Median Standard Score (z-score) Mean & Standard Deviation Linear Transformation

QQuestion

In a test, the median of the test scores of a class of students is \(30\) marks. All the students fail in the test, so the test score of each student is adjusted such that each score is increased by \(50\%\) and then extra \(8\) marks are added. Let \(x\) marks be the median of the test scores of the class of students after the score adjustment. In the test, the standard score of a student before the score adjustment is \(-2\). Denote the standard score of this student after the score adjustment by \(z\). Find \(x\) and \(z\).

A. \(x=45\) and \(z=-2\)
B. \(x=45\) and \(z=-1\)
C. \(x=53\) and \(z=-2\)
D. \(x=53\) and \(z=-1\)

(Multiple-choice question · all working must be reasoned, no marks for guessing)

1Part (a) — Finding the new median \(x\)

The transformation applied to every score is \[ \text{new score} = 1.5\times(\text{old score}) + 8 . \] Move the slider below to see how each data value (orange) maps to its new value (green) under this transformation, and watch how the median moves.

1Effect on the median

Adding a constant (or multiplying by a positive constant) to every score shifts the whole distribution; the middle order statistic moves with it. Hence the median undergoes exactly the same transformation:

\[ \text{new median} = 1.5\times(\text{old median}) + 8 . \]
Hint: The median depends only on the position of the middle value, not on the size of the other values. Since every value is transformed by the same rule, the middle value is transformed by the same rule too.
2Compute \(x\)

With old median \(=30\):

\[ x = 1.5\times 30 + 8 = 45 + 8 = 53 . \]
\(x = 53\) marks

2Part (b) — Finding the new standard score \(z\)

The standard score (z-score) of a value \(v\) in a distribution with mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\) is \[ z = \frac{v-\mu}{\sigma}. \] The standard score changes only if the relative position of the value within the distribution changes. Use the slider to change the student's raw score and observe.

1New mean and new standard deviation

Let the original mean and standard deviation be \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\). For a linear transformation new \(= m\cdot\text{old}+k\) with \(m>0\):

\[ \mu_{\text{new}} = m\mu + k, \qquad \sigma_{\text{new}} = m\sigma . \] Here \(m=1.5\) and \(k=8\), so \[ \mu_{\text{new}} = 1.5\mu + 8, \qquad \sigma_{\text{new}} = 1.5\sigma . \]
Hint: Adding a constant does not change the spread, so the standard deviation is unaffected by the \(+8\). Multiplying does scale the spread, so \(\sigma\) is multiplied by \(1.5\).
2Transform the student's score

Given the original standard score is \(-2\): \[ \frac{v_0-\mu}{\sigma} = -2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_0 = \mu - 2\sigma . \] After adjustment this student's score becomes \[ v_1 = 1.5v_0+8 = 1.5(\mu-2\sigma)+8 = 1.5\mu - 3\sigma + 8 . \]

Hint: Substitute \(v_0=\mu-2\sigma\) before expanding — this keeps everything in terms of \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\), which is exactly what appears in the new z-score formula.
3Compute the new standard score

\[ z = \frac{v_1-\mu_{\text{new}}}{\sigma_{\text{new}}} = \frac{(1.5\mu-3\sigma+8)-(1.5\mu+8)}{1.5\sigma} = \frac{-3\sigma}{1.5\sigma} = -2 . \]

\(z = -2\)
Key insight: A linear transformation \(v\mapsto 1.5v+8\) preserves standard scores exactly: \(z_{\text{new}}=\dfrac{1.5(v-\mu)}{1.5\sigma}=\dfrac{v-\mu}{\sigma}=z_{\text{old}}\). So a student sitting at \(2\) standard deviations below the mean is still \(2\) standard deviations below the mean afterwards.

★ Answers at a Glance

  • \(x\) (new median): the median is transformed the same way as each score → \(x=30\times1.5+8=\mathbf{53}\)
  • New mean: \(\mu_{\text{new}}=1.5\mu+8\)  ·  New standard deviation: \(\sigma_{\text{new}}=1.5\sigma\)
  • \(z\) (new standard score): \(\;z=\dfrac{1.5(\mu-2\sigma)+8-(1.5\mu+8)}{1.5\sigma}=\dfrac{-3\sigma}{1.5\sigma}=\mathbf{-2}\)
  • Correct option: \(\boxed{\text{C. } x=53 \text{ and } z=-2}\)
