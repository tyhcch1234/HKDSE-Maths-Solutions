Simplify \[ \frac{2}{4h-7} - \frac{3}{6h-5} \]

\[ \frac{2}{4(2)-7} - \frac{3}{6(2)-5} \]
1 Find a common denominator ▶

To subtract two fractions, they must have the same denominator. The denominators are \(4h-7\) and \(6h-5\). The simplest common denominator is their product:

\[ (4h-7)(6h-5) \]
💡 Hint: Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by the other denominator. This does not change the value.
2 Rewrite with the common denominator ▶

Multiply the first fraction by \(\dfrac{6h-5}{6h-5}\) and the second by \(\dfrac{4h-7}{4h-7}\):

\[ \frac{2(6h-5)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} - \frac{3(4h-7)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]
3 Combine the numerators ▶

Now subtract the numerators over the single denominator:

\[ \frac{2(6h-5) - 3(4h-7)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]

Be careful with the minus sign — distribute it to both terms inside the parentheses.

4 Expand the numerator ▶

Expand each bracket:

\[ 2(6h-5) = 12h - 10 \] \[ 3(4h-7) = 12h - 21 \]

Then subtract:

\[ (12h - 10) - (12h - 21) = 12h - 10 - 12h + 21 \]
5 Simplify the numerator ▶

Combine like terms:

\[ 12h - 10 - 12h + 21 = 11 \]

The \(h\) terms cancel out, leaving a constant.

6 Write the final answer ▶

The simplified expression is:

\[ \frac{11}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]

You can leave the denominator in factored form.

Final Answer
\[ \frac{11}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]
