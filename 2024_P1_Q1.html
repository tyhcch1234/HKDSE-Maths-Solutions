1. Simplify $\ \dfrac{2}{4h-7}-\dfrac{3}{6h-5}$.

1 Set up a common denominator
The two denominators are $4h-7$ and $6h-5$. They have no common factor, so the common denominator is their product: \[ (4h-7)(6h-5) \]
Hint: Multiply each fraction top-and-bottom by the other denominator — this changes the form but not the value.
2 Rewrite each fraction
\[ \frac{2}{4h-7}\cdot\frac{6h-5}{6h-5} \;-\; \frac{3}{6h-5}\cdot\frac{4h-7}{4h-7} \] \[ =\;\frac{2(6h-5)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \;-\; \frac{3(4h-7)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]
Hint: Keep the denominator identical so the numerators can be combined in one bracket. 1M
3 Expand the numerators
\[ =\;\frac{12h-10}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \;-\; \frac{12h-21}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]
Hint: Distribute carefully: $2(6h-5)=12h-10$ and $3(4h-7)=12h-21$. Watch the minus sign in front of the second fraction. 1M
4 Subtract and simplify
\[ =\;\frac{(12h-10)-(12h-21)}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \;=\;\frac{12h-10-12h+21}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \;=\;\frac{11}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \] The $12h$ terms cancel — that is exactly why the answer is a pure number over the product of the two linear factors. 1A
Hint: Only like terms can be combined. $-10+21=+11$; the $12h$'s cancel.
Answer \[ \frac{2}{4h-7}-\frac{3}{6h-5}=\frac{11}{(4h-7)(6h-5)} \]
