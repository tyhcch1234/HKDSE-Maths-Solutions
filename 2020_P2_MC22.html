Question MC

In the figure, \(ABCDE\) is a circle. If \(AB = 10\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 5\text{ cm}\), \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\) and \(\angle CED = 40^\circ\), find \(CD\) correct to the nearest cm.

A. \(5\text{ cm}\)    B. \(6\text{ cm}\)    C. \(7\text{ cm}\)    D. \(8\text{ cm}\)

Original Figure

Part (a) — Find the chord \(AC\) Step-by-step

Drag the slider to rotate the circle and confirm \(AC\) is always the same length.
\(AB =\) 10.0 cm
\(BC =\) 5.0 cm
\(AC =\) 11.18 cm
\(\angle ABC =\) 90°
1
Recognise the right angle at \(B\)
Given

The figure gives \(AB=10\), \(BC=5\) and \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\). Since \(\angle ABC\) is the angle subtended by chord \(AC\) at a point \(B\) on the circle, \(\triangle ABC\) is a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse \(AC\).

Hint: Look for a right angle. Here \(\angle ABC=90^\circ\) means we can use Pythagoras' Theorem directly.
2
Apply Pythagoras' Theorem
1M

In \(\triangle ABC\): \[ AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 = 10^2 + 5^2 = 100 + 25 = 125 \] \[ AC = \sqrt{125} = 5\sqrt{5} \approx 11.18\text{ cm} \]

Hint: \(\sqrt{125}\) can be simplified as \(5\sqrt{5}\), but keeping it exact helps in the next step.
\(AC = \sqrt{125}\text{ cm}\)
0 / 2

Part (b) — Use the cyclic quadrilateral Step-by-step

Toggle the checkbox to explore the cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\).
\(\angle ABC =\) 90°
\(\angle ADC =\) 90°
Sum \(= \) 180°
1
Identify the cyclic quadrilateral
1M

Since \(A\), \(B\), \(C\), \(D\) all lie on the same circle, \(ABCD\) is a cyclic quadrilateral. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

Hint: The four vertices lie on the circle — a cyclic quadrilateral.
2
Find \(\angle ADC\)
1A

\[ \angle ADC = 180^\circ - \angle ABC \quad (\text{opp. } \angle\text{s, cyclic quad.}) \] \[ = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ \]

Hint: When opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary (\(\angle ADC+\angle ABC=180^\circ\)).
\(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\)
0 / 2

Part (c) — Find \(CD\) using trigonometry Step-by-step

Drag the slider to change \(\angle CED\) and watch \(CD\) update live.
\(40^\circ\)
\(AC =\) 11.180 cm
\(\angle CAD =\) 40°
\(CD =\) 7.19 cm
Nearest cm: 7 cm
1
Use angles in the same segment
1M

\(\angle CAD\) and \(\angle CED\) are angles subtended by the same chord \(CD\) at the circumference (both in the same segment).

\[ \angle CAD = \angle CED = 40^\circ \quad (\angle\text{s in the same segment}) \]

Hint: Same chord \(CD\) ⇒ equal angles at the circumference, provided the points are in the same segment.
\(\angle CAD = 40^\circ\)
2
Solve in \(\triangle ACD\)
1M

In \(\triangle ACD\), we now know:

  • \(AC = \sqrt{125}\text{ cm}\) (from part a)
  • \(\angle ADC = 90^\circ\) (from part b)
  • \(\angle CAD = 40^\circ\)

Using the sine ratio in the right-angled triangle: \[ \sin\angle CAD = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{CD}{AC} \] \[ CD = AC \cdot \sin\angle CAD \]

Hint: Right angle at \(D\) ⇒ use \(\sin\), \(\cos\) or \(\tan\) in \(\triangle ACD\).
3
Substitute and round
1A

\[ CD = \sqrt{125}\,\sin 40^\circ \] \[ \approx 11.1803 \times 0.642788 \] \[ \approx 7.18658 \] \[ \approx 7\text{ cm} \quad (\text{correct to the nearest cm}) \]

Hint: Round only at the very end. The answer is C (7 cm).
\(CD \approx 7\text{ cm}\)  → Answer: C
0 / 3

✅ Final Answer at a Glance

  • In \(\triangle ABC\): \(AC = \sqrt{10^2+5^2} = \sqrt{125}\text{ cm}\)
  • Cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\): \(\angle ADC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ\)
  • Angles in the same segment: \(\angle CAD = \angle CED = 40^\circ\)
  • In right-angled \(\triangle ACD\): \(CD = \sqrt{125}\sin 40^\circ \approx 7.19\text{ cm}\)
  • Correct to the nearest cm: \(CD = 7\text{ cm}\) → Option C
