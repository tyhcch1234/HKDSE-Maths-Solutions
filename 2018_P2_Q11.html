HKDSE 2018 · Paper 2 · Q11  |  Rate, Ratio and Variations

Variation — which expression must be constant?

If $w$ varies directly as the square root of $u$ and inversely as the square of $v$, which of the following must be constant?
A.  $u^{4}vw^{2}$
B.  $uv^{4}w^{2}$
C.  $\dfrac{vw^{2}}{u^{4}}$
D.  $\dfrac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}$

↓ Scroll through the steps; the figure on the left updates.

1 Translate the words into an equation
“$w$ varies directly as the square root of $u$”  ⟹  $w \propto \sqrt{u}$.
“and inversely as the square of $v$”  ⟹  multiply by $\dfrac{1}{v^{2}}$.
Direct ⟹ on top; inverse ⟹ at the bottom.
2 Introduce the variation constant $k$
$$w = k\,\frac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}, \qquad k \text{ is a non-zero constant.}$$ $k$ is fixed for the whole question — that is the quantity that “must be constant”.
Write $w$ alone on the left; everything else on the right.
Joint Variation\(w=\dfrac{k\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}\)Direct variation with \(\sqrt{u}\) puts \(\sqrt{u}\) on top; inverse variation with \(v^{2}\) puts \(v^{2}\) at the bottom.
3 Make $k$ the subject of the formula
Multiply both sides by $\dfrac{v^{2}}{\sqrt{u}}$: $$k = \frac{w\,v^{2}}{\sqrt{u}}$$
Anything equal to $k$ must be constant — so we need an option that equals $k^{2}$ (or $k$).
Making the Constant the Subject\(k=\dfrac{w\,v^{2}}{\sqrt{u}}\)Multiplying both sides by \(\dfrac{v^{2}}{\sqrt{u}}\) isolates the constant \(k\).
4 Test option A: $u^{4}vw^{2}$
Substitute $w=k\dfrac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}$: $$u^{4}vw^{2}=u^{4}v\left(k\frac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}\right)^{2}=u^{4}v\cdot\frac{k^{2}u}{v^{4}}=\frac{k^{2}u^{5}}{v^{3}}$$ Still contains $u$ and $v$  ⟹  NOT constant.
Eliminate any option that keeps $u$ or $v$ in it.
5 Test option B: $uv^{4}w^{2}$
$$uv^{4}w^{2}=uv^{4}\left(k\frac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}\right)^{2}=uv^{4}\cdot\frac{k^{2}u}{v^{4}}=k^{2}u^{2}$$ Contains $u$  ⟹  NOT constant.
$v^{4}$ cancels nicely, but $u^{2}$ remains.
6 Test option C: $\dfrac{vw^{2}}{u^{4}}$
$$\frac{vw^{2}}{u^{4}}=\frac{v\left(k\sqrt{u}/v^{2}\right)^{2}}{u^{4}}=\frac{k^{2}}{u^{3}v^{3}}$$ Contains $u$ and $v$  ⟹  NOT constant.
The powers of $u$ and $v$ do not cancel.
7 Test option D: $\dfrac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}$
$$\frac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}=\frac{v^{4}\left(k\sqrt{u}/v^{2}\right)^{2}}{u}=\frac{v^{4}\cdot k^{2}u/v^{4}}{u}=\frac{k^{2}u}{u}=k^{2}$$ Only $k$ remains  ⟹  MUST be constant.
This is the required balance: $u$ appears once below, and $v^{2}$ is squared to $v^{4}$ on top.
Power of a Quotient\(\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{u}{v^{4}}\)Squaring \(w=k\dfrac{\sqrt{u}}{v^{2}}\) turns \(v^{4}w^{2}\) into \(k^{2}u\), so \(\dfrac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}=k^{2}\).
8 Conclusion
Since $k$ is a constant, $\dfrac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}=k^{2}$ is also a constant.
Answer: D.
Answer:  D   $\left(\dfrac{v^{4}w^{2}}{u}=k^{2}\right)$

Question. It is given that \(y\) varies directly as \(x^{2}\) and inversely as \(\sqrt{z}\). Which of the following must be constant? A. \(\dfrac{x^{4}}{y^{2}z}\) \quad B. \(\dfrac{y^{2}z}{x^{4}}\) \quad C. \(\dfrac{y^{2}x^{4}}{z}\) \quad D. \(\dfrac{z}{y^{2}x^{4}}\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Translate the words: \(y=\dfrac{kx^{2}}{\sqrt{z}}\), where \(k\) is a non-zero constant.
Step 2. Make \(k\) the subject: \(k=\dfrac{y\sqrt{z}}{x^{2}}\).
Step 3. Square both sides so that the root disappears: \(k^{2}=\dfrac{y^{2}z}{x^{4}}\).
Step 4. Since \(k^{2}\) is constant, \(\dfrac{y^{2}z}{x^{4}}\) must be constant — option B.
Answer: B — \(\dfrac{y^{2}z}{x^{4}}\)
