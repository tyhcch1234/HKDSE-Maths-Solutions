QUESTION

In the figure, \(O\) is the centre of the circle \(PQRS\). \(PR\) and \(OQ\) intersect at the point \(T\). If \(\angle POT=82^\circ\) and \(\angle PTQ=104^\circ\), then \(\angle QSR=\)

A. \(22^\circ\)
B. \(27^\circ\)
C. \(30^\circ\)
D. \(49^\circ\)
💡 Hint: \(O,T,Q\) lie on the same straight line, so \(\angle POQ = \angle POT\). Then use the isosceles triangle \(OPR\) (two radii) and the angle at the centre.
    STEP 1 · Same Line collinear O, T, Q Since \(O,\,T,\,Q\) are collinear, \[ \angle POQ=\angle POT=82^\circ. \]
    STEP 2 · Triangle \(POT\) 💡 \(T\) lies on \(OQ\), so \(PTH\) is not straight — instead use angles on a straight line at \(T\): \[ \angle PTO=180^\circ-104^\circ=76^\circ. \] Angle sum of \(\triangle POT\): \[ \angle OPT=180^\circ-82^\circ-76^\circ=22^\circ. \]
    STEP 3 · Isosceles \(\triangle OPR\) \(OP=OR\) (radii of the same circle), so \[ \angle ORP=\angle OPR=22^\circ \] \[ \angle POR=180^\circ-2(22^\circ)=136^\circ. \]
    STEP 4 · Angle at the centre Since \(OQ\) lies inside \(\angle POR\), \[ \angle QOR=\angle POR-\angle POQ=136^\circ-82^\circ=54^\circ. \]
    STEP 5 · Angle at the circumference The angle subtended by arc \(QR\) at the circumference is half the central angle: \[ \angle QSR=\tfrac12\,\angle QOR=\tfrac12(54^\circ)=27^\circ. \]
B  \(\angle QSR = 27^\circ\)
