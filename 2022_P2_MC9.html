Topic 4 · Polynomials — Remainder Theorem & Divisibility

Remainder Theorem Factor Theorem Quadratic Polynomial Even / Odd terms

Q The Question

Let \(g(x)=x^{2}+ax+b\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants. If \(g(x)\) is divisible by \(x+2a\), find the remainder when \(g(x)\) is divided by \(x-2a\).

A. \(-2a^{2}\)
B. \(0\)
C. \(2a^{2}\)
D. \(4a^{2}\)
Tap an option to check your answer.

1 Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict the next line before you open it!

1 Translate "divisible by \(x+2a\)" into an equation ▶

If a polynomial \(g(x)\) is divisible by \((x+2a)\), then the divisor is a factor, so the remainder is \(0\).

By the Factor Theorem: \(g(-2a)=0\).
Hint — Factor Theorem: \((x-c)\) is a factor of \(g(x)\) \(\iff\) \(g(c)=0\).
Here \(x+2a = x-(-2a)\), so \(c=-2a\).
2 Write down what we are looking for ▶

Let \(r\) be the required remainder when \(g(x)\) is divided by \(x-2a\).

By the Remainder Theorem: \(g(2a)=r\).
Hint — Remainder Theorem: The remainder on dividing \(g(x)\) by \((x-c)\) equals \(g(c)\).
Here \(x-2a = x-(2a)\), so \(c=2a\). We must find \(g(2a)\).
3 Use \(g(-2a)=0\) to find the relationship between \(a\) and \(b\) ▶

Substitute \(x=-2a\) into \(g(x)=x^{2}+ax+b\):

\[ g(-2a)=(-2a)^{2}+a(-2a)+b = 4a^{2}-2a^{2}+b = 2a^{2}+b \]

Since \(g(-2a)=0\):

\[ 2a^{2}+b=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=-2a^{2} \]
Hint: \((-2a)^2 = 4a^2\) (squaring removes the minus sign), while \(a(-2a) = -2a^2\). Careful with signs!
4 Evaluate \(g(2a)\) using \(b=-2a^{2}\) ▶

Substitute \(x=2a\) into \(g(x)=x^{2}+ax+b\):

\[ g(2a)=(2a)^{2}+a(2a)+b = 4a^{2}+2a^{2}+b = 6a^{2}+b \]

Now replace \(b\) with \(-2a^{2}\):

\[ g(2a)=6a^{2}+\left(-2a^{2}\right)=4a^{2} \]
5 Alternative neat trick (the official method) ▶

Notice the \(x^2\) and the constant \(b\) are the same for \(g(x)\) and \(g(-x)\). Compute the difference:

\[ g(x)-g(-x)=\bigl(x^{2}+ax+b\bigr)-\bigl(x^{2}-ax+b\bigr)=2ax \]

Put \(x=2a\):

\[ g(2a)-g(-2a)=2a(2a)=4a^{2} \]

Since \(g(-2a)=0\):

\[ r-0=4a^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=4a^{2} \]
Why this works: The "even" parts \(x^2\) and \(b\) cancel, leaving only the "odd" part \(ax\). This avoids needing to find \(b\) at all.
6 Conclusion ▶
The remainder is \(\;4a^{2}\) — that is Option D.

Why is the remainder the same for every value of \(b\)?

The condition "divisible by \(x+2a\)" forces \(b=-2a^{2}\) — there is no freedom left. So \(a\) is the only free parameter, and the remainder \(g(2a)\) is a fixed multiple of \(a^{2}\), namely \(4a^{2}\). This is why the answer is expressed in terms of \(a\) only.
