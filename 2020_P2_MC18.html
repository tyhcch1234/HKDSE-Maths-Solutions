Mensuration — Area ratios of similar triangles inside a parallelogram
Similar Triangles (AAA) Area Ratio Parallelogram Multiple Choice

Question

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. Let \(E\) be a point lying on \(AD\) such that \(AE:ED=2:5\). \(CB\) is produced to the point \(F\) such that \(BF=DE\). Denote the point of intersection of \(AB\) and \(EF\) by \(G\). It is given that \(BD\) and \(CG\) intersect at the point \(H\).

If the area of \(\triangle AEG\) is \(48\text{ cm}^2\), then the area of \(\triangle CDH\) is

  1. \(98\text{ cm}^2\)
  2. \(343\text{ cm}^2\)
  3. \(420\text{ cm}^2\)
  4. \(588\text{ cm}^2\)

Original Figure

Part 1 — Set up ratios from similar triangles 1M

We transform the given length ratios into side ratios of similar triangles. Move the slider to see how the shape deforms while all ratios stay fixed.

\(AE:ED=2:5\) \(AB:CD=1:1\) \(AE:BF=2:5\) \(AG:GB=2:5\) \(CD:GB=7:5\)
0 / 3
Step 11MEstablish \((\text{AAA})\) similarity: \(\triangle AEG \sim \triangle BFG\)
\[ \angle AGE=\angle BGF \ (\text{vert. opp. }\angle\text{s}) \] \[ \angle AEG=\angle BFG \ (\text{alt. }\angle\text{s},\ AE/\!/ FB) \] \[ \therefore\ \triangle AEG \sim \triangle BFG \quad (\text{AAA}) \]
Hint: In a parallelogram \(AD/\!/ BC\), so \(AE/\!/ BF\). The two triangles are “rotated copies” with a common vertical angle at \(G\).
Similarity: \(\triangle AEG \sim \triangle BFG\)
Step 21ACorresponding sides give the key ratio \(2:5\)
\[ AE:BF = AE:DE = 2:5 \] \[ \therefore\ AG:GB = AE:BF = 2:5 \] (corresponding sides of similar triangles)
Hint: \(BF=DE\) is given, so the ratio \(AE:DE\) instantly becomes \(AE:BF\).
Result: \(AG:GB = 2:5\)
Step 31AApply the parallelogram property \(AB=CD\)
\[ AB=CD \quad (\text{opp. sides of } /\!/\text{gram}) \] \[ CD:AG:GB = AB:AG:GB = (2+5):2:5 = 7:2:5 \]
Hint: \(AB\) is split by \(G\) into \(AG=2\) parts and \(GB=5\) parts, so \(AB=7\) parts \(=CD\).
Result: \(CD:AG:GB = 7:2:5\)

Part 2 — Second pair of similar triangles 1A

The diagonals and the cevian meet at \(H\). Compare \(\triangle CDH\) with \(\triangle GBH\) in the interactive diagram.

\(CD:GB=7:5\) \(DH:BH=7:5\) \(DH:DB = 7:12\)
0 / 2
Step 11MProve \(\triangle CDH \sim \triangle GBH\) (AAA)
\[ \angle CHD=\angle GHB \ (\text{vert. opp. }\angle\text{s}) \] \[ \angle CDH=\angle GBH \ (\text{alt. }\angle\text{s},\ CD/\!/ GB) \] \[ \therefore\ \triangle CDH \sim \triangle GBH \quad (\text{AAA}) \]
Hint: Because \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, \(CD/\!/ AB\), hence \(CD/\!/ GB\). The “Z” angle at \(H\) gives equality.
Similarity: \(\triangle CDH \sim \triangle GBH\)
Step 21AFind \(DH:BH\)
\[ DH:BH = CD:GB = 7:5 \] \[ \therefore\ DB=DH+HB = 7+5 = 12 \text{ parts} \]
Hint: \(H\) lies between \(D\) and \(B\), so the whole diagonal \(DB\) has \(7+5=12\) equal parts.
Result: \(DH:BH:DB = 7:5:12\)

Part 3 — The area chain \(48 \to \triangle CDH\) 1A

Areas are linked step by step. Drag the slider to grow/shrink the whole figure — the ratios of the coloured areas never change.

\([\triangle AEG]=48\) \([\triangle ADG]=48\times\tfrac{7}{2}=168\) \([\triangle CBD]=168\times\tfrac{7}{2}=588\) \([\triangle CDH]=588\times\tfrac{7}{12}=343\)
0 / 3
Step 11MFrom \(\triangle AEG\) to \(\triangle ADG\) (common height)
\(\triangle ADG\) and \(\triangle AEG\) share the same altitude from \(D\) (or \(G\)) to the line \(AD\). The ratio of their areas equals the ratio of their bases on that line: \[ \frac{[\triangle ADG]}{[\triangle AEG]} = \frac{AD}{AE} = \frac{2+5}{2} = \frac{7}{2} \] \[ [\triangle ADG]=48\times\frac{7}{2}=168\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint: A common height means “area ratio = base ratio”. \(AD = AE+ED = 2+5 = 7\) parts.
Result: \([\triangle ADG]=168\text{ cm}^2\)
Step 21AFrom \(\triangle ADG\) to \(\triangle CBD\) (equal heights)
\(\triangle CBD\) and \(\triangle ADG\) have equal heights: the distance between the parallel lines \(AD\) and \(BC\). \[ \frac{[\triangle CBD]}{[\triangle ADG]} = \frac{CD}{AG} = \frac{7}{2} \] \[ [\triangle CBD]=168\times\frac{7}{2}=588\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint: Bases \(CD\) and \(AG\) lie on parallel lines \(AD/\!/ BC\), so the perpendicular distance between them is the same.
Result: \([\triangle CBD]=588\text{ cm}^2\)
Step 31AFrom \(\triangle CBD\) to \(\triangle CDH\) (common height)
\(\triangle CDH\) and \(\triangle CBD\) share the same altitude from \(C\) to line \(DB\), so \[ \frac{[\triangle CDH]}{[\triangle CBD]} = \frac{DH}{DB} = \frac{7}{12} \] \[ [\triangle CDH]=588\times\frac{7}{12}=49\times 7 = 343\ \text{cm}^2 \]
Hint: \(588/12 = 49\), then \(49\times 7=343\). This matches option B.
Final answer: \([\triangle CDH]=343\text{ cm}^2\) → Option B

Summary of answers

  • Similarity 1: \(\triangle AEG \sim \triangle BFG \Rightarrow AG:GB = AE:DE = 2:5\)
  • Parallelogram: \(AB=CD \Rightarrow CD:AG:GB = 7:2:5\)
  • Similarity 2: \(\triangle CDH \sim \triangle GBH \Rightarrow DH:BH = 7:5 \Rightarrow DH:DB = 7:12\)
  • Area chain: \([\triangle ADG]=48\times\frac72=168\)
  • \([\triangle CBD]=168\times\frac72=588\)
  • \([\triangle CDH]=588\times\frac{7}{12}=\mathbf{343\ \text{cm}^2}\)
  • Correct choice: B — \(343\text{ cm}^2\)
