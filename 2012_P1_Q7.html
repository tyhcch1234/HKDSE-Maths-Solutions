Statistics — Box-and-Whisker Diagram, Quartiles & Range

Box-and-Whisker Diagram Inter-quartile Range Range Quartiles & Percentiles

Question 4 marks

The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the times taken by a large group of students of an athletic club to finish a \(100\text{ m}\) race:

├────┤ │ 12.1 │ 13.2 │ ├────┤ 18.1

(lower whisker end = \(a\), box edges at \(12.1\) and \(13.2\), median inside, upper whisker end = \(18.1\), time in s)

The inter-quartile range and the range of the distribution are \(3.2\text{ s}\) and \(6.8\text{ s}\) respectively.

(a) Find \(a\) and \(b\). 2 marks

(b) The students join a training program. It is found that the longest time taken by the students to finish a \(100\text{ m}\) race after the training is \(2.9\text{ s}\) less than that before the training. The trainer claims that at least \(25\%\) of the students show improvement in the time taken to finish a \(100\text{ m}\) race after the training. Do you agree? Explain your answer. 2 marks

Part (a) — Finding \(a\) and \(b\) 2 marks

In a box-and-whisker diagram:

  • the box spans from \(Q_1\) to \(Q_3\),
  • the whiskers run from the minimum to \(Q_1\), and from \(Q_3\) to the maximum.

From the diagram, the upper whisker ends at the maximum, i.e. \(18.1\). So \(b\) is the upper quartile \(Q_3\) and \(a\) is the lower whisker end / minimum.

IQR = 3.2 s

The diagram shows the box-and-whisker plot with \(a\), \(Q_1=12.1\), \(Q_3=b\), and the maximum \(=18.1\). The IQR \(=Q_3-Q_1=3.2\) and the range \(=\text{max}-\text{min}=6.8\).

11MUse the range to find \(a\).

Range \(= \text{maximum} - \text{minimum}\)

\[ 6.8 = 18.1 - a \] \[ a = 18.1 - 6.8 = 11.3 \]
Hint: The largest time is the right end of the upper whisker, \(18.1\). Subtract the range to get the smallest time \(a\).
\(a = 11.3\)
21AUse the inter-quartile range to find \(b\).

Inter-quartile range \(= Q_3 - Q_1\)

\[ 3.2 = b - 12.1 \] \[ b = 12.1 + 3.2 = 15.3 \]
Hint: \(b\) is the upper quartile \(Q_3\), the right edge of the box. The lower quartile \(Q_1 = 12.1\) is the left edge.
\(b = 15.3\)

Part (b) — The trainer's claim 2 marks

Before training the maximum time is \(18.1\text{ s}\). After training the longest time is \(2.9\text{ s}\) less, so the new maximum is

\[ 18.1 - 2.9 = 15.2\text{ s}. \]

We must decide whether at least \(25\%\) of students improved, i.e. whether at least the top \(25\%\) of the class beat their previous time.

New max = 15.2 s < Q₃ = 15.3 s

Drag the slider to change the new longest time. The key comparison is between the new maximum and the old upper quartile \(Q_3 = 15.3\text{ s}\).

11MCompare the new longest time with the old \(Q_3\).

New longest time after training \(= 18.1 - 2.9 = 15.2\text{ s}\).

The old upper quartile is \(Q_3 = b = 15.3\text{ s}\). Since

\[ 15.2 < 15.3, \]

the new longest time is less than the upper quartile of the original distribution.

Hint: The top \(25\%\) of the original distribution had times greater than \(Q_3 = 15.3\text{ s}\). A student who previously took more than \(15.3\text{ s}\) must have improved, because the new maximum is only \(15.2\text{ s}\).
Since \(15.2 < 15.3\), at least \(25\%\) of the students improved.
21AState the conclusion with the reason.

At least \(25\%\) of the students originally took longer than \(15.3\text{ s}\) to finish the race. In the new result, all students finished within \(15.2\text{ s}\). Hence every one of those slowest \(25\%\) must have improved.

Hint: "At least \(25\%\)" comes from the fact that the top quarter of the original distribution lies beyond \(Q_3\), and the new maximum is below \(Q_3\).
Yes, the claim is agreed.

Summary of Answers

  • (a)   \(a = 18.1 - 6.8 = 11.3\)
  • (a)   \(b = 12.1 + 3.2 = 15.3\)
  • (b)   New longest time \(= 18.1 - 2.9 = 15.2\text{ s}\), which is less than \(Q_3 = 15.3\text{ s}\).
  • (b)   Therefore at least \(25\%\) of the students improved — Yes, the claim is agreed.
