Mensuration — Area of a pentagon formed from a square and a right-angled isosceles triangle

Mensuration Area of Polygon Right-angled Isosceles Triangle 45°–45°–90° Triangle Subtracting Areas

Question Full Question Text Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(AB = AE\) and \(\angle BAE = \angle BCD = \angle CDE = 90^\circ\). If \(BC = CD = DE = 16\text{ cm}\), then the area of the pentagon \(ABCDE\) is

A. \(71\text{ cm}^2\)
B. \(128\text{ cm}^2\)
C. \(192\text{ cm}^2\)
D. \(224\text{ cm}^2\)

Part 1 Recognising the Shapes: Square \(BCDE\) & \(\triangle ABE\)

Drag the sliders to explore how the figure changes with the side length and the apex height. Toggle layers to isolate the square \(BCDE\) and the triangle \(ABE\).

\(BC=CD=DE\) = 16 cm \(\angle BAE\) = 90° Area \(BCDE\) = 256.00 cm² Area \(\triangle ABE\) = 128.00 cm² Pentagon \(ABCDE\) = 128.00 cm²
0 / 3
1Identify the quadrilateral \(BCDE\)

Given \(\angle BCD = \angle CDE = 90^\circ\) and \(BC = CD = DE\). A quadrilateral with three equal consecutive sides and two right angles at \(C\) and \(D\) must be a square.

\[ \angle DEB? \quad \text{Since } BC /\!/ DE \text{ (both }\perp CD\text{), and } BC=DE, \]

\(BCDE\) is a square with side \(16\text{ cm}\), so \[ BE = 16\text{ cm}, \qquad \angle CBE = \angle BED = 90^\circ. \]

Two right angles plus three equal consecutive sides force the fourth side to be equal and parallel — that is exactly the definition of a square.
2Classify \(\triangle ABE\)

We are told \(AB = AE\) and \(\angle BAE = 90^\circ\). So \(\triangle ABE\) is a right-angled isosceles triangle with the right angle at \(A\).

Hence the two base angles are equal: \[ \angle ABE = \angle AEB = \frac{180^\circ - 90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ. \]

"Isosceles + right angle at the apex" always gives two \(45^\circ\) base angles — the classic \(45^\circ\!-\!45^\circ\!-\!90^\circ\) triangle.
\(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled isosceles with \(\angle ABE=\angle AEB=45^\circ\).
3Relate the pentagon to square minus triangle

The pentagon \(ABCDE\) is exactly the square \(BCDE\) with the triangle \(ABE\) "indented" from the top side \(BE\): \[ \text{Area}(ABCDE) = \text{Area}(BCDE) - \text{Area}(\triangle ABE). \]

Using \(BC=CD=DE=16\): \[ \text{Area}(BCDE) = 16^2 = 256\text{ cm}^2. \]

The apex \(A\) hangs below the segment \(BE\), so subtract the triangle's area from the square's area.
\(\text{Area}(ABCDE) = 256 - \text{Area}(\triangle ABE)\).

Part 2 Finding \(AB\), \(AE\) then the Area

Use the slider to change the square's side length. The readouts update the computed \(AB=AE\) and the final pentagon area live, so you can verify the formula.

\(BE\) = 16 cm \(AB=AE\) = \(BE\cos45^\circ\) = 11.31 cm Area \(\triangle ABE\) = 128.00 cm² Area \(ABCDE\) = \(s^2-\tfrac12 s^2\) = 128.00 cm²
0 / 4
1Length \(BE\) from the square

Since \(BCDE\) is a square of side \(16\text{ cm}\), its side \(BE\) equals the other sides:

\[ BE = BC = 16\text{ cm}. \]
\(BE\) is a side of the square, not a diagonal — no Pythagoras needed here.
\(BE = 16\text{ cm}\).
2Use right-triangle trigonometry in \(\triangle ABE\)

In the right-angled triangle \(ABE\) with the right angle at \(A\), taking the base angle at \(B\):

\[ \cos \angle ABE = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{BE}. \]

Therefore

\[ AB = BE \cos 45^\circ = 16 \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{16}{\sqrt{2}} = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}. \]

By symmetry (\(AB=AE\)), \(AE = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\) as well.

Alternatively, \(AB:AE:BE = 1:1:\sqrt{2}\) for a \(45^\circ\!-\!45^\circ\!-\!90^\circ\) triangle, so \(AB = BE/\sqrt{2}\).
\(AB = AE = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\).
3Area of square \(BCDE\) and triangle \(ABE\)

Square:

\[ \text{Area}(BCDE) = 16^2 = 256\text{ cm}^2. \]

Triangle (using the two equal legs \(AB\) and \(AE\) perpendicular at \(A\)):

\[ \text{Area}(\triangle ABE) = \frac{1}{2}(AB)(AE) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(8\sqrt{2}\bigr)\bigl(8\sqrt{2}\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}(128) = 64 \times 2? \;\; \text{check} = 64? \]

Actually, \((8\sqrt{2})^2 = 64\times 2 = 128\), so \[ \text{Area}(\triangle ABE) = \frac{1}{2}(128) = 64\text{ cm}^2. \]

Formula: \(\text{Area of } \triangle = \tfrac12 \times \text{leg}_1 \times \text{leg}_2\) when the two legs are perpendicular.
\(\text{Area}(BCDE)=256\text{ cm}^2\), \(\text{Area}(\triangle ABE)=64\text{ cm}^2\).
4Subtract to get the pentagon area

\[ \text{Area}(ABCDE) = \text{Area}(BCDE) - \text{Area}(\triangle ABE) = 256 - 64 = 192\text{ cm}^2. \]

Common trap: forgetting to subtract the triangle, giving \(256\) — but \(256\) is not even among the options, which is a useful check!
\(\text{Area}(ABCDE) = 192\text{ cm}^2\) → the answer is C.

Summary — Answers at a Glance

  • Shape 1: \(BCDE\) is a square of side \(16\text{ cm}\).
  • Shape 2: \(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled isosceles with \(\angle ABE=\angle AEB=45^\circ\).
  • Base: \(BE = 16\text{ cm}\).
  • Equal sides: \(AB = AE = 8\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\).
  • Areas: \(\text{Area}(BCDE)=256\text{ cm}^2\), \(\text{Area}(\triangle ABE)=64\text{ cm}^2\).
  • Pentagon area: \(256-64 = \boxed{192\text{ cm}^2}\).
  • Correct option: C.
