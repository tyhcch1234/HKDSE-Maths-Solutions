2016 P2 Q34 Complex Numbers

HKDSE Math
Complex Numbers Rational & Irrational Real & Imaginary Parts

Let u = 7/(a + i) and v = 7/(a - i), where a is a real number. Which of the following must be true?

  • I. \(uv\) is a rational number.
  • II. The real part of \(u\) is equal to the real part of \(v\).
  • III. The imaginary part of \(\frac{1}{u}\) is equal to the imaginary part of \(\frac{1}{v}\).
A. I only
B. II only
C. I and III only
D. II and III only

Step-by-step Solution

Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:

\(u = \frac{7}{a+i} \cdot \frac{a-i}{a-i} = \frac{7(a-i)}{a^2+1} = \frac{7a}{a^2+1} - \frac{7}{a^2+1}i\)

\(v = \frac{7}{a-i} \cdot \frac{a+i}{a+i} = \frac{7(a+i)}{a^2+1} = \frac{7a}{a^2+1} + \frac{7}{a^2+1}i\)

Hint: Recall \((a+bi)(a-bi) = a^2 + b^2\).

Multiply \(u\) and \(v\):

\(uv = \frac{7}{a+i} \cdot \frac{7}{a-i} = \frac{49}{a^2+1}\)

This expression is a rational number if \(a^2+1\) is a rational number. But \(a\) is a real number, so \(a^2+1\) could be irrational (e.g. \(a = \sqrt[4]{7} - 1\) gives \(a^2+1 = \sqrt{7}\)).

Counterexample: Let \(a = \sqrt{\sqrt{7}-1}\). Then \(a^2+1 = \sqrt{7}\), so \(uv = 49/\sqrt{7} = 7\sqrt{7}\), which is not rational.

Therefore Statement I is false.

From Step 1:

\(\operatorname{Re}(u) = \frac{7a}{a^2+1}\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(v) = \frac{7a}{a^2+1}\)

They are identical for any real \(a\).

Therefore Statement II is true.

First find the reciprocals:

\(\frac{1}{u} = \frac{a+i}{7} = \frac{a}{7} + \frac{1}{7}i\)

\(\frac{1}{v} = \frac{a-i}{7} = \frac{a}{7} - \frac{1}{7}i\)

Imaginary parts are \(\frac{1}{7}\) and \(-\frac{1}{7}\) respectively. They are not equal (unless we consider absolute values, but the statement says "equal").

Therefore Statement III is false.

Conclusion

Only Statement II is always true. So the correct answer is B. II only.
