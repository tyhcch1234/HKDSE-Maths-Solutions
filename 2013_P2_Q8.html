HKDSE 2013 Paper 2 · Q8 · Polynomials

If $a$, $b$ and $c$ are non-zero constants such that $$x(x+3a)+a \equiv x^2+2(bx+c),$$ then $a:b:c=$

A. $2:3:1$
B. $2:3:4$
C. $3:2:6$
D. $6:4:3$
Step 1 — Expand the left-hand side
Distributive Law\(a(b+c)=ab+ac\)Expanding \(x(x+3a)\) gives \(x^{2}+3ax\), which matches the \(x^{2}\) term on the right-hand side.
Expand $x(x+3a)$ and then add $a$: $$x(x+3a)+a = x^2+3ax+a$$ Write the LHS as a polynomial in descending powers of $x$ so it can be compared term-by-term with the RHS.
Step 2 — Expand the right-hand side Expand $2(bx+c)$: $$x^2+2(bx+c) = x^2+2bx+2c$$ Now both sides are written as $x^2 + (\text{coefficient of }x)\,x + (\text{constant})$.
Step 3 — Equate the coefficients of $x$
Equating Coefficients of an Identity\(Ax^{2}+Bx+C\equiv ax^{2}+bx+c\ \Rightarrow\ A=a,\ B=b,\ C=c\)The identity holds for every \(x\), so the coefficient of \(x\) on the left, \(3a\), equals that on the right, \(2b\).
Comparing the $x$-terms of $$x^2+\underline{3a}\,x+\underline{a} \;\equiv\; x^2+\underline{2b}\,x+\underline{2c}$$ gives $$3a = 2b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a}{b} = \frac{2}{3}$$ An identity holds for all $x$,
so corresponding coefficients must be equal.
Step 4 — Equate the constant terms Comparing the constants gives $$a = 2c \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a}{c} = \frac{2}{1}$$ The constant on the left is $a$;
the constant on the right is $2c$.
Step 5 — Form the ratio $a:b:c$
Simplifying a Ratio\(2k:3k:k=2:3:1\)Dividing all three parts by the common factor \(k\neq 0\) gives the ratio in its simplest form.
Let $a = 2k$ ($k \neq 0$). Then $$c = \frac{a}{2} = k, \qquad b = \frac{3a}{2} = 3k.$$ So $$a:b:c = 2k : 3k : k = 2:3:1$$ Divide every quantity by the common factor $k$ to get the simplified ratio.
Answer: A  ($2:3:1$)

Question. It is given that \(x(x+4p)+2p\equiv x^{2}+3(qx+r)\) for all real values of \(x\). Find \(p:q:r\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand the left-hand side: \(x^{2}+4px+2p\).
Step 2. Compare the coefficients of \(x\): \(4p=3q\), so \(q=\dfrac{4p}{3}\).
Step 3. Compare the constant terms: \(2p=3r\), so \(r=\dfrac{2p}{3}\).
Step 4. Let \(p=3k\) (\(k\neq 0\)). Then \(q=4k\) and \(r=2k\), so \(p:q:r=3:4:2\). Check: \(4(3k)=12k=3(4k)\) ✓ and \(2(3k)=6k=3(2k)\) ✓.
Answer: \(p:q:r=3:4:2\)
