Without Replacement Conditional Probability Exactly 3 Draws

Question

A box contains six balls numbered 7, 8, 8, 9, 9 and 9 respectively. John repeats drawing one ball at a time randomly from the box without replacement until the number drawn is 9. Find the probability that he needs exactly three draws.

A. 1/2
B. 1/6
C. 1/8
D. 3/20 Answer

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

Click each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict before you click!

For John to need exactly three draws, the sequence must be:

  • Draw 1: not a 9 (i.e. 7 or 8)
  • Draw 2: not a 9 (i.e. 7 or 8)
  • Draw 3: a 9

💡 Hint: The process stops as soon as a 9 is drawn. So the first two draws must not be 9, and the third must be 9.

Total balls = 6: one 7, two 8's, and three 9's.

7 × 1 8 × 2 9 × 3

🔢 Key idea: There are 3 non-9 balls (1 seven + 2 eights) and 3 nine-balls, so the total is 6.

Initially, there are 6 balls: 3 are not 9, and 3 are 9.

The first draw must be not 9:

\( P(\text{not 9}) = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2} \)

📐 Formula: \( P(\text{event}) = \frac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}} \)

After the first draw, one non-9 ball is removed. So the box now has:

  • Total balls = 5
  • Non-9 balls = 2
  • 9-balls = 3

The second draw must be not 9 again:

\( P(\text{not 9} \mid \text{1st not 9}) = \dfrac{2}{5} \)

⚠️ Important: Because the balls are drawn without replacement, both the numerator and denominator decrease by 1.

After the second draw, one more non-9 ball is removed. So the box now has:

  • Total balls = 4
  • Non-9 balls = 1
  • 9-balls = 3

The third draw must be 9:

\( P(\text{9} \mid \text{1st not 9, 2nd not 9}) = \dfrac{3}{4} \)

✅ Notice that the number of 9-balls stays at 3 throughout, because no 9 has been drawn yet.

Use the multiplication rule for dependent events:

\( P = \dfrac{3}{6} \times \dfrac{2}{5} \times \dfrac{3}{4} \)

Simplify step by step:

  • \( = \dfrac{3 \times 2 \times 3}{6 \times 5 \times 4} \)
  • \( = \dfrac{18}{120} \)
  • \( = \dfrac{3}{20} \)

🎯 Final Answer: The probability is \( \boxed{\dfrac{3}{20}} \), which is option D.

🔑 Key Takeaway

When drawing without replacement, the total number of balls decreases after each draw, and so do the counts of the drawn category. The probability of a sequence is the product of the conditional probabilities at each step.

Formula: \( P(A \cap B \cap C) = P(A) \times P(B \mid A) \times P(C \mid A \cap B) \)
