2021 MC #4 Topic: Polynomials / Rational expressions

Simplify the following expression.

$$ \frac{6}{\,n-6\,} \;-\; \frac{7}{\,n-7\,} \;=\; ? $$
  • A. $\dfrac{n}{(n-6)(n-7)}$
  • B. $\dfrac{n}{(n-6)(7-n)}$
  • C. $\dfrac{n+84}{(n-6)(n-7)}$
  • D. $\dfrac{n+84}{(n-6)(7-n)}$
Least Common Denominator \(\text{LCD of }\dfrac{1}{n-a},\ \dfrac{1}{n-b}\ \text{is}\ (n-a)(n-b)\) The denominators \(n-6\) and \(n-7\) are distinct linear factors, so both must be kept.
1 Identify the two denominators &
their LCD

The two denominators are $(n-6)$ and $(n-7)$. They are distinct linear factors,
so the Least Common Denominator is their product:

$$\text{LCD} = (n-6)(n-7)$$

The LCD is the product of two different linear factors — keep both.

Equivalent Fractions \(\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{a\,k}{b\,k}\quad (k\ne0)\) Multiply \(\dfrac{6}{n-6}\) by \(\dfrac{n-7}{n-7}\) and \(\dfrac{7}{n-7}\) by \(\dfrac{n-6}{n-6}\).
2 Make each fraction share the LCD

Multiply numerator and denominator of each fraction by the missing factor:

$$ \frac{6}{n-6}=\frac{6\,(n-7)}{(n-6)(n-7)} \qquad\qquad \frac{7}{n-7}=\frac{7\,(n-6)}{(n-6)(n-7)} $$

Multiplying top and bottom by the same thing does not change the value of a fraction.

3 Subtract over the single common denominator
$$ \frac{6}{n-6}-\frac{7}{n-7} = \frac{6(n-7)-7(n-6)}{(n-6)(n-7)} $$

Once the denominators match,
combine the numerators into one fractional line.

Removing a Bracket after a Minus Sign \(-(a-b)=-a+b\) \(-7(n-6)=-7n+42\), which is why the \(42\) cancels in \(6n-42-7n+42\).
4 Expand the numerator carefully (watch the minus sign)
$$ =\frac{(6n-42)-(7n-42)}{(n-6)(n-7)} = \frac{6n-42-7n+42}{(n-6)(n-7)} = \frac{-n}{(n-6)(n-7)} $$

A minus sign in front of a bracket flips every sign inside: $-7(n-6)=-7n+42$.

5 Put the minus sign into the denominator

Since $-n = n\times(-1)$ and $(n-7)=-(7-n)$:

$$ \frac{-n}{(n-6)(n-7)} = \frac{-n}{-(n-6)(7-n)} = \frac{n}{(n-6)(7-n)} $$

Moving one minus sign from the numerator to a bracket in the denominator gives the form in option B.

Answer: B  —  $\displaystyle \frac{6}{n-6}-\frac{7}{n-7}=\frac{n}{(n-6)(7-n)}$
(with $n\neq 6$ and $n\neq 7$)

Question. \(\dfrac{5}{n-3}-\dfrac{4}{n-2}=\)
A. \(\dfrac{n+2}{(n-3)(n-2)}\)    B. \(\dfrac{n-2}{(n-3)(n-2)}\)    C. \(\dfrac{1}{(n-3)(n-2)}\)    D. \(\dfrac{n+2}{(n-3)(2-n)}\)  (1 mark)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The LCD is \((n-3)(n-2)\). Rewrite: \(\dfrac{5(n-2)}{(n-3)(n-2)}-\dfrac{4(n-3)}{(n-3)(n-2)}\).
Step 2. Combine the numerators: \(5n-10-4n+12=n+2\).
Step 3. So the expression equals \(\dfrac{n+2}{(n-3)(n-2)}\) (with \(n\ne2,3\)), which is option A.
Answer: A. \(\dfrac{n+2}{(n-3)(n-2)}\)
