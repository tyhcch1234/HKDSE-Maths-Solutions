Mensuration of an Inverted Conical Vessel
Volume of Cone Similar Solids · Volume Ratio Curved Surface Area Slant Height Justifying a Claim

Question

An inverted right circular conical vessel contains some milk. The vessel is held vertically. The depth of milk in the vessel is \(12\text{ cm}\). Peter then pours \(444\pi\text{ cm}^3\) of milk into the vessel without overflowing. He now finds that the depth of milk in the vessel is \(16\text{ cm}\).

(a) Express the final volume of milk in the vessel in terms of \(\pi\). 3 marks

(b) Peter claims that the final area of the wet curved surface of the vessel is at least \(800\text{ cm}^2\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. 3 marks

Part (a) — Finding the final volume

Initial depth \(h_0 = 12\) cm Initial volume \(V_0 = 432\pi\text{ cm}^3\) Final volume \(V = 768\pi\) cm³
Step 11M + 1A

Let \(V\text{ cm}^3\) be the final volume of milk in the vessel. The milk at depth \(12\text{ cm}\) and at depth \(16\text{ cm}\) form two similar cones (same apex, parallel circular surfaces).

For similar solids, volumes are in the ratio of the cubes of corresponding lengths:

\[ \frac{V-444\pi}{V}=\left(\frac{12}{16}\right)^{3}=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{3}=\frac{27}{64} \]
Hint: “\(V-444\pi\)” is the volume of milk before pouring (the shallow cone, depth 12). The ratio of the depths is \(\dfrac{12}{16}=\dfrac34\), so the volume ratio is \(\left(\dfrac34\right)^3\).
Step 21A

Solve for \(V\):

\[ 64(V-444\pi)=27V \] \[ 64V-27V=28416\pi \] \[ 37V=28416\pi \] \[ V=768\pi \]
Hint: Expand, collect like terms, then divide. Cross-check: \(768\pi-444\pi=324\pi\), and \(\left(\frac34\right)^3(768\pi)=324\pi\) ✓
∴ The final volume of milk in the vessel is \(\;768\pi\text{ cm}^3\).

Part (b) — Testing Peter's claim

Radius \(r = 12\) cm Slant height \(l=\sqrt{r^2+h^2} = 20\) cm Wet area \(=\pi r l = 240\pi \approx 753.98\text{ cm}^2\)
Step 11M

Find the final radius \(r\) of the milk surface using the volume formula for a cone (depth \(h=16\)).

\[ \frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}(16)=768\pi \] \[ \frac{16 r^{2}}{3}=768 \;\Rightarrow\; r^{2}=144 \] \[ r=12\ \text{(reject } r=-12\text{)} \]
Hint: \(V=\frac13\pi r^2 h\) with \(h=16\) and \(V=768\pi\). Notice \(16\cdot 48 = 768\), so \(r^2/3 = 48\).
Step 21M

The wet curved surface is the lateral surface of the cone of radius \(12\) and height \(16\). Its slant height is \(l=\sqrt{12^2+16^2}=20\text{ cm}\).

\[ \text{Wet area}=\pi r l=\pi(12)\sqrt{12^{2}+16^{2}}=\pi(12)(20)=240\pi \] \[ 240\pi \approx 753.9822369\ \text{cm}^{2} \]
Hint: The “wet curved surface” is only the side of the cone (like a lampshade) — not a full sphere or a flat disc. Its area is \(\pi r l\) with \(l\) the slant height, not \(h\).
Step 31A

Compare with the claimed value \(800\text{ cm}^2\):

\[ 240\pi \approx 753.98 < 800 \]

Since the final wet curved surface area is strictly less than \(800\text{ cm}^2\), Peter's claim is not correct.

Hint: A claim of “at least \(800\)” requires \(\text{area}\ge 800\). Since \(240\pi-800<-40<0\) (as \(\pi<3.34\)), the claim fails. For a safe check, \(240\pi<240(3.15)=756<800\).
∴ No, I do not agree. The final wet curved surface area is \(240\pi \approx 753.98\text{ cm}^2 < 800\text{ cm}^2\), so it is not at least \(800\text{ cm}^2\).

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Final volume of milk: \(\;V=768\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
  • (b) Final radius of milk surface: \(\;r=12\text{ cm}\) (with \(h=16\))
  • Slant height: \(\;l=\sqrt{12^2+16^2}=20\text{ cm}\)
  • Wet curved surface area: \(\;240\pi \approx 753.98\text{ cm}^2\)
  • Since \(753.98 < 800\), Peter's claim (at least \(800\text{ cm}^2\)) is disagreed.
