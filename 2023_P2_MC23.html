Trigonometry — ratios in right-angled triangles inside a trapezium
Trapezium Right-angled Triangles Sine & Cosine Ratios Angle Sum of Triangle Mid-point

QUESTION Full statement & options

In the figure, \(ABCD\) is a trapezium with \(AD /\!/ BC\). Let \(E\) be the mid-point of \(AD\). It is given that \(\angle ABE = \angle BCE = 90^\circ\).

Find \(\dfrac{CE}{DE}\). (1 mark)

A.  \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)     B.  \(1\)     C.  \(\tan\alpha\)     D.  \(\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\)

Here \(\alpha = \angle BAE\) (angle at \(A\) in the figure).

Drag the slider to change \(\alpha\) and watch the whole configuration adjust. The trapezium's shape is completely determined by \(\alpha\) because of the right angles.

Part (a) Find \(\angle AEC\) using \(AD /\!/ BC\)

Step 11M

Since \(BC \perp CE\) (given \(\angle BCE = 90^\circ\)) and \(AD /\!/ BC\), the transversal \(CE\) makes the same angle with \(AD\) as with \(BC\).

Hint: Corresponding angles / interior angles on the same side of a transversal between parallel lines.
Step 21A

\(\angle AEC = 180^\circ - \angle BCE = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ\).

Hint: So \(CE\) is perpendicular to \(AD\) as well — it is an altitude of the trapezium.
\(\angle AEC = 90^\circ\)
0 / 2

Part (b)(i) Angle sum in \(\triangle ABE\) — find \(\angle BEC\)

Step 11M

In \(\triangle ABE\): \(\angle ABE = 90^\circ\) (given), \(\angle BAE = \alpha\). So \[\angle AEB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - \alpha = 90^\circ - \alpha.\]

Hint: Sum of angles of a triangle \(= 180^\circ\).
Step 21M

Since \(A, E, D\) are collinear and \(\angle AEC = 90^\circ\): \[\angle BEC = \angle AEC - \angle AEB = 90^\circ - (90^\circ - \alpha).\]

Hint: \(EB\) and \(EC\) lie on the same side of \(AE\).
Step 31A

Simplifying: \(\angle BEC = \alpha\).

Hint: A neat coincidence: \(\angle BEC = \angle BAE = \alpha\), so triangles \(ABE\) and \(BCE\) share the same acute angle at \(B\) and \(E\) respectively.
\(\angle BEC = \alpha\)
0 / 3

Part (b)(ii) Use trigonometry in the two right triangles

Step 11M

In \(\triangle ABE\) (right-angled at \(B\)): \[\sin\angle BAE = \frac{BE}{AE} \quad\Rightarrow\quad BE = AE\sin\alpha.\]

Hint: \(\angle ABE = 90^\circ\), so \(AE\) is the hypotenuse of triangle \(ABE\).
Step 21M

In \(\triangle BCE\) (right-angled at \(C\)): \[\cos\angle BEC = \frac{CE}{BE} \quad\Rightarrow\quad CE = BE\cos\alpha.\]

Hint: \(\angle BCE = 90^\circ\) makes \(BE\) the hypotenuse of triangle \(BCE\); and \(\angle BEC = \alpha\) from part (b)(i).
Step 31A

Combining: \(CE = AE\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha\). Since \(E\) is the mid-point of \(AD\), \(DE = AE\).

Hint: \(DE = AE\) is the crucial use of the mid-point condition.
\(CE = AE\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\) and \(DE = AE\)
0 / 3

Part (b)(iii) Find the ratio \(\dfrac{CE}{DE}\)

\(\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\) = –  |  \(\tfrac{CE}{DE}\) = –
Step 11M

Substitute the results from (b)(ii): \[\frac{CE}{DE} = \frac{AE\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{AE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha.\]

Hint: The common factor \(AE\) cancels — the answer is independent of the length of \(AD\).
Step 21A

Therefore \(\dfrac{CE}{DE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha\), which matches option D.

Hint: You can also think of it as \(\frac{CE}{BE}\cdot\frac{BE}{DE} = \cos\alpha\cdot\sin\alpha\) — a chain of ratios.
\(\dfrac{CE}{DE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha\)  →  Answer: D
0 / 2

Summary of answers

  • \(\angle AEC = 90^\circ\)  (since \(AD /\!/ BC\) and \(\angle BCE = 90^\circ\)).
  • \(\angle BEC = \alpha\)  (angle sum in \(\triangle ABE\), then subtract from \(90^\circ\)).
  • \(BE = AE\sin\alpha\)  and  \(CE = BE\cos\alpha\).
  • \(DE = AE\)  (\(E\) is the mid-point of \(AD\)).
  • \[\frac{CE}{DE} = \frac{AE\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{AE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha.\]
Correct option: D  ·  \(\dfrac{CE}{DE} = \sin\alpha\cos\alpha\)
