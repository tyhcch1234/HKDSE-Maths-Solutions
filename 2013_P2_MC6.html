Algebra Quadratic Equations Difference of Squares Square Root Property

Question

Let \(k\) be a constant. Solve the equation \((x - k)^2 = 4k^2\).

A. \(x = 5k\)

B. \(x = 3k\)

C. \(x = -3k\) or \(x = k\)

D. \(x = -k\) or \(x = 3k\)

💡 Core Concept

The equation \((x - k)^2 = 4k^2\) is a quadratic equation in disguise. The key insight is to take the square root of both sides. This gives two separate linear equations because \(\sqrt{4k^2} = 2|k|\), which leads to \(x - k = 2k\) or \(x - k = -2k\). Solving each yields the two roots.

Important: Never forget the negative square root! Ignoring it is the most common mistake.

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

We are given:

\((x - k)^2 = 4k^2\)

This is already in a perfect square form, which is very convenient.

Applying the square root property:

\(x - k = \pm \sqrt{4k^2}\)

Since \(\sqrt{4k^2} = 2|k|\), we get two cases. But because \(k\) is an arbitrary constant, we can write it as \(\pm 2k\) in algebraic form.

Hint: Remember \(\sqrt{4k^2} = 2k\) only if \(k \geq 0\). In general, use \(\pm 2k\) to cover both signs.

Case 1: \(x - k = 2k\)

\(x = 2k + k = 3k\)

This gives one of the roots.

Case 2: \(x - k = -2k\)

\(x = -2k + k = -k\)

This gives the second root.

The two solutions are:

\(x = 3k\) or \(x = -k\)

This matches option D.

Final Answer

C. \(x = -k\) or \(x = 3k\)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ✗ Forgetting the negative square root: only writing \(x = 3k\) and missing \(x = -k\).
  • ✗ Incorrect square root: writing \(\sqrt{4k^2} = 2k\) instead of \(\pm 2k\).
  • ✗ Expanding \((x-k)^2\) and applying the quadratic formula without simplifying — more room for error.

🎯 Exam Tip

When you see a perfect square equal to another square, immediately take the square root. This turns a quadratic into two simple linear equations. This technique saves time and reduces errors in the DSE exam.
