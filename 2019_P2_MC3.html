Question

If \(6x - 7y = 40\) and \(2x + 11y = 40\), then \(y =\)

A. −4
B. 2
C. 4
D. 9
Elimination method Linear equations Coefficient matching

Step-by-step guided solution

Click each step to expand. Follow the hints to build understanding.

\[ \begin{cases} 6x - 7y = 40 \quad \text{......(1)} \\ 2x + 11y = 40 \quad \text{......(2)} \end{cases} \]

Hint

To solve for \(y\), we need to eliminate \(x\). Look at the coefficients of \(x\): 6 and 2. Their LCM is 6, so multiplying (2) by 3 will make the \(x\)-term match the \(x\)-term in (1).

\[ (2) \times 3: \quad 6x + 33y = 120 \quad \text{......(3)} \]

Hint

\(2x \times 3 = 6x\) and \(11y \times 3 = 33y\), while the right‑hand side \(40 \times 3 = 120\).

\[ (3) - (1): \quad (6x + 33y) - (6x - 7y) = 120 - 40 \] \[ 40y = 80 \]

Hint

When subtracting, remember to subtract the entire left‑hand side of (1), so \(-7y\) becomes \(+7y\). The \(x\) terms cancel out.

\[ 40y = 80 \quad \Rightarrow \quad y = \frac{80}{40} = 2 \]

Conclusion

Therefore \(y = 2\), which corresponds to option B.

Answer: B (2)
