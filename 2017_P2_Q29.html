The box-and-whisker diagram below shows the distribution of the numbers of online hours spent by a class of students in a certain week. Find the lower quartile of the distribution.

  • A. 5
  • B. 15
  • C. 25
  • D. 40
STEP 1
Read the key features of the box-and-whisker diagram from the scale:
Minimum $= 5$, Lower quartile $Q_1 = ?$, Median $= 20$, Upper quartile $Q_3 = 36$, Maximum $= 52$.
The left edge of the box marks the lower quartile $Q_1$.
Box-and-Whisker Diagram \(\text{min}\ \big|\ Q_{1}\ \boxed{\ \text{median}\ }\ Q_{3}\ \big|\ \text{max}\) The left edge of the box is the lower quartile, the line inside it is the median \(20\) and the right edge is \(Q_{3}=36\).
STEP 2
The box starts at the first tick after the label "10" on the axis. Each small subdivision on the axis represents $5$ units.
Reading the left edge of the box: it lies exactly at the line marked $15$.
Count 5 units per subdivision from 0: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, …
STEP 3
Thus the lower quartile (first quartile) is $Q_1 = 15$, which matches option B.
The lower quartile is the value below which 25% of the data lie — visually the left side of the box.
Lower Quartile \(Q_{1}\ \text{is the value below which }25\%\ \text{of the data lie}\) Reading the left edge of the box against the scale gives \(Q_{1}=15\).
Answer: B. 15

Question. The box-and-whisker diagram shows the marks of a class in a test, where the minimum is \(12\), the median is \(46\), the upper quartile is \(58\), the maximum is \(72\) and the inter-quartile range is \(28\). Find the lower quartile.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. In a box-and-whisker diagram the width of the box is the inter-quartile range, and its left edge is the lower quartile \(Q_{1}\).
Step 2. \(\text{IQR}=Q_{3}-Q_{1}\), so \(28=58-Q_{1}\).
Step 3. Hence \(Q_{1}=58-28=30\).
Step 4. Check: \(25\%\) of the marks lie below \(30\) and \(25\%\) lie above \(58\), a spread of \(28\) marks ✓.
Answer: \(Q_{1}=30\)
