Step 1 — read the two given inequalities.

HKDSE 2024 · Paper 1 · Q4 · 4 marks

(a) Find the range of values of $x$ which satisfy both $\dfrac{5x+7}{4}-1 < 2x$  and  $3x+9 \ge 0$.

(b) Write down the least integer satisfying both inequalities in (a).

1Write down the two given inequalities

$$\frac{5x+7}{4}-1 < 2x \qquad\text{and}\qquad 3x+9 \ge 0$$

We must solve each inequality separately, then keep only the $x$-values satisfying both (the intersection).
2Clear the denominator of the first inequality
Clearing the Denominator of an Inequality \(\dfrac{5x+7}{4}-1<2x\Rightarrow 5x+3<8x\) Multiplying every term by \(4\), a positive number, leaves the direction of the inequality unchanged.

Multiply every term by $4$:

$$5x+7-4 < 8x$$

Multiplying by a positive number ($4$) does not change the direction of the inequality sign.
3Solve the first inequality (collect like terms)
Solving a Linear Inequality \(3<3x\Rightarrow x>1\) Dividing by the positive number \(3\) keeps the sign, so the first inequality gives \(x>1\).

$$5x+3 < 8x$$

$$3 < 3x$$

$$x > 1$$

Dividing by $3$ (positive) keeps the sign. Note: dividing by a negative number would reverse the sign.
4Solve the second inequality

$$3x+9 \ge 0$$

$$3x \ge -9$$

$$x \ge -3$$

Subtract $9$ from both sides, then divide by $3$.
5Intersect the two solution sets
Intersection of Two Solution Sets \(\{x>1\}\cap\{x\ge-3\}=\{x>1\}\) Every \(x>1\) already satisfies \(x\ge-3\), so the stricter condition \(x>1\) wins.

$$x > 1 \quad\textbf{and}\quad x \ge -3$$

The overlap of the two sets is simply the stricter condition:

$$x > 1$$

On the number line, $x>1$ lies entirely inside $x \ge -3$, so the intersection is $x>1$.
6Part (b): least integer

The integers greater than $1$ are $2,\,3,\,4,\dots$

The least of these is $2$.

Since the inequality is strict ($x > 1$), $x = 1$ is not included — so the smallest integer is $2$.
(a) $\;x > 1$
(b) $\;\text{least integer} = $ 2

Question.
(a) Find the range of values of \(x\) which satisfy both \(\dfrac{4x+9}{3}-2<3x\) and \(5x+10\ge0\).
(b) Write down the least integer satisfying both inequalities in (a).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Multiply the first inequality by \(3\): \(4x+9-6<9x\), i.e. \(4x+3<9x\).
Step 2. Then \(3<5x\), so \(x>0.6\).
Step 3. Second inequality: \(5x\ge-10\), so \(x\ge-2\).
Step 4. The intersection is \(x>0.6\).
Step 5. (b) The integers greater than \(0.6\) start at \(1\), so the least integer is \(1\).
Answer: (a) \(x>0.6\); (b) \(1\)
