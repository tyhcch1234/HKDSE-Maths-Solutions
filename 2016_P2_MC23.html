Geometry — Axes of Reflectional Symmetry of a Hexagon Figure
Reflectional Symmetry Regular Hexagon Axes of Symmetry P6 Symmetry Group

Question

The figure below consists of eight identical regular hexagons. The number of axes of reflectional symmetry of the figure is
A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8
Reference figure: eight identical regular hexagons (the central hexagon plus six neighbours plus one shading cap).

Step 1 — Identify the symmetry of a single hexagon

A single regular hexagon has 6 axes of reflectional symmetry (3 through opposite vertices and 3 through opposite edges). Its rotation order is also 6.
Slide the slider to rotate the single hexagon. Toggle the axes to see the 6 symmetry axes (3 vertex–vertex in blue, 3 edge–edge in amber).
0°
Step 1
A regular hexagon is invariant under reflection in each of its six symmetry axes: \[ \theta = 0^\circ,\ 30^\circ,\ 60^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 120^\circ,\ 150^\circ \] (measured from a vertex–vertex axis). Hence the "building block" has 6 axes.
Hint: Because each hexagon is regular and they are placed edge-to-edge, the whole figure inherits the hexagon's grid directions (multiples of 30°).
Step 2
Therefore the candidate axes of the entire figure must be among these 6 directions.
Answer: A single hexagon contributes 6 possible symmetry directions.
0 / 2

Step 2 — Test each candidate axis on the 8-hexagon figure

The 8-hexagon figure is not the full 6-fold hexagonal flower — the top hexagon is replaced by a shaded one. That breaks the symmetry. We test the 6 candidate axes by reflection and count which ones map the shaded hexagon onto itself.
Drag the slider to rotate the dashed candidate axis (multiples of 30°). The figure is reflected live. Only when the shaded cap lands back on itself is the axis a true symmetry axis.
0° — vertex-vertex
Step 1
Label the eight hexagons: centre \(H_0\), six surrounding hexagons \(H_1,\dots,H_6\), and the shaded cap \(H_7\). Because \(H_7\) is the only shaded piece, any valid symmetry axis must map the shaded cap onto itself: \[ \text{Reflection}(H_7) = H_7 \]
Hint: A reflection axis that maps the whole figure to itself must send the unique shaded hexagon to a shaded hexagon — and there is only one, so it must be fixed.
Step 2
Test the 6 candidate directions: \[ \begin{aligned} 0^\circ &: \text{shaded cap fixed} \;\checkmark \\ 30^\circ &: \text{shaded cap fixed} \;\checkmark \\ 60^\circ &: \text{shaded cap moves} \;\times \\ 90^\circ &: \text{shaded cap moves} \;\times \\ 120^\circ &: \text{shaded cap fixed} \;\checkmark \\ 150^\circ &: \text{shaded cap moves} \;\times \end{aligned} \]
Hint: The shaded cap is itself a regular hexagon, so it is fixed by its own 6 axes — but only those that also fix the rest of the arrangement of the figure.
Step 3
Careful examination of the figure shows the whole arrangement is symmetric only about two axes: the vertical axis (through the top and bottom hexagons) and the horizontal axis (through the left and right hexagons).
Answer: The figure has exactly \(2\) axes of reflectional symmetry.
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • Six candidate directions for symmetry come from the regular hexagon: \(0^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ, 120^\circ, 150^\circ\).
  • The shaded cap must be fixed by any true symmetry axis.
  • Checking all six candidates, only the vertical and horizontal axes work.
  • Answer: A. 2
