HKDSE Math 2015 Paper 2

Question 32: Laws of Indices & Logarithmic Functions

Logarithmic Graphs Laws of Indices Line Equation

Question Overview

The graph in the figure shows the linear relation between log3x and log3y. Which of the following must be true?

3x log3y 2 3

Graph: Linear relation between log3x and log3y

A. $$x^2 y^3 = 729$$
B. $$x^3 y^2 = 729$$
C. $$x^2 + y^3 = 729$$
D. $$x^3 + y^2 = 729$$
Correct Answer: A

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

From the graph, we can identify two key points on the straight line:

  • \(y\)-intercept: \((0, 2)\)
  • \(x\)-intercept: \((3, 0)\)
Hint: The axes are in terms of \(\log_3 x\) and \(\log_3 y\). So the points are \((\log_3 x, \log_3 y) = (0, 2)\) and \((3, 0)\).

Use the two-point form of a straight line equation:

$$\frac{\log_3 y - y_1}{x_1 - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

Or equivalently, using slope-intercept form \(y = mx + c\)

Let's find the slope \(m\):

$$m = \frac{0 - 2}{3 - 0} = -\frac{2}{3}$$

The \(y\)-intercept is \(c = 2\). So the equation is:

$$\log_3 y = -\frac{2}{3} \log_3 x + 2$$
Hint: Here, the "\(y\)" variable is \(\log_3 y\) and the "\(x\)" variable is \(\log_3 x\).

Multiply the entire equation by 3 to eliminate the fraction:

$$3 \log_3 y = -2 \log_3 x + 6$$

Bring all terms to one side:

$$2 \log_3 x + 3 \log_3 y = 6$$
Hint: This is a linear combination of logarithms. We will use the Laws of Logarithms to combine them.

Recall the Power Law of Logarithms: \(n \log_b a = \log_b a^n\)

Apply it to both terms:

$$\log_3 x^2 + \log_3 y^3 = 6$$

Recall the Product Law: \(\log_b a + \log_b c = \log_b(ac)\)

Combine the left side:

$$\log_3 (x^2 y^3) = 6$$
Hint: We are very close! We just need to convert from logarithmic form to exponential form.

The definition of logarithm states: if \(\log_b a = c\), then \(a = b^c\).

Here, \(b = 3\), \(a = x^2 y^3\), and \(c = 6\). Thus:

$$x^2 y^3 = 3^6$$

Calculate \(3^6\):

$$3^6 = 729$$
$$x^2 y^3 = 729$$
Hint: This matches Option A exactly! Notice how the powers \(2\) and \(3\) from the original log equation become the exponents in the final algebraic expression.
