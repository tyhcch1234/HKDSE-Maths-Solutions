Geometry (106) · Sub-Topic: Congruence/Similarity

T11 Q36

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$, $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$, and $BC = EF$. Which congruence condition can be used to prove that the two triangles are congruent?

Solution

Given $\angle A=\angle D$ and $\angle B=\angle E$.
By the angle sum of a triangle, $\angle C=180^\circ-\angle A-\angle B=180^\circ-\angle D-\angle E=\angle F$.
The given side $BC$ is opposite $\angle A$, and $EF$ is opposite $\angle D$. Since $\angle A=\angle D$, $BC$ and $EF$ are corresponding sides, and $BC=EF$.
Thus the triangles have two equal corresponding angles and an equal corresponding non-included side, so the congruence condition is AAS. Equivalently, after proving $\angle C=\angle F$, ASA can also be used with $BC=EF$ as the included side between $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.
The congruence condition is AAS (or equivalently ASA after proving $\angle C=\angle F$).
