Topic 4 — Polynomials

Polynomials Expansion Distributive Law Like Terms Matching Coefficients

Q Question

\[ (x+1)(x^{2}+x+1) = \;? \]

Simplify / expand the product of the two polynomials.

A Choose the correct answer

Tap an option below — we'll reveal whether you are right.

S Step-by-Step Solution

Tap each step to expand. Try to work it out yourself before you peek! 👀

We are asked to expand the product of two polynomials:

\[ (x+1)\,(x^{2}+x+1) \]

This means we must multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second bracket, then collect like terms.

Multiply the first term \(x\) by the whole second bracket:

\[ x\,(x^{2}+x+1) = x^{3}+x^{2}+x \]

💡 Hint — Power Rule: \(x^{a}\cdot x^{b} = x^{a+b}\).
So \(x\cdot x^{2} = x^{3}\), \(\;x\cdot x = x^{2}\), \(\;x\cdot 1 = x\).

Now multiply the second term \(+1\) by the whole second bracket:

\[ 1\,(x^{2}+x+1) = x^{2}+x+1 \]

Multiplying by \(1\) leaves every term unchanged in value.

Combining the two partial products:

\[ (x^{3}+x^{2}+x) + (x^{2}+x+1) \]

We can drop the brackets since all signs between them are \(+\).

Group terms with the same power of \(x\):

\(x^{3}\) \(x^{2}\) \(x^{2}\) \(x\) \(x\) \(1\)

Combine the pairs of like terms:

  • \(x^{2}\) terms: \(\;x^{2}+x^{2}=2x^{2}\)
  • \(x\) terms: \(\;x+x=2x\)

There is only one \(x^{3}\) term and one constant term, so they stay as they are.

\[ (x+1)(x^{2}+x+1) = x^{3}+2x^{2}+2x+1 \]

This matches option D. ✅

Why the other options fail:

Option Expression Why it's wrong
A \(x^{3}+1\) Missing \(2x^{2}+2x\); comes from \((x+1)(x^{2}-x+1)\), not here.
B \((x+1)^{3}\) \((x+1)^{3}=(x+1)(x^{2}+2x+1)\), a different second factor.
C \(x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1\) Coefficients of \(x^{2}\) and \(x\) are not added — like terms forgotten.
D \(x^{3}+2x^{2}+2x+1\) Correct — like terms properly collected.

🔑 Key Takeaway

  • Multiply each term of the first bracket by each term of the second bracket (distributive law).
  • Apply the power rule \(x^{a}\cdot x^{b}=x^{a+b}\).
  • Always collect like terms at the end — this is where students lose marks.
  • Quick check: substitute a simple value of \(x\) (e.g. \(x=2\)) into both forms to verify they match.
