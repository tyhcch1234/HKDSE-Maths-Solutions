Trigonometric Identities — Compound Angle Reduction & Simplification
Compound Angles Reduction Formulae Simplification Pythagorean Identity

QQuestion

\[ \frac{\cos 180^\circ}{1+\sin(90^\circ+\theta)}+\frac{\cos 360^\circ}{1+\sin(270^\circ+\theta)} = \;? \]
A. \(\;0\)
B. \(\;\dfrac{2}{\cos\theta}\)
C. \(\;\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\)
D. \(\;\dfrac{2\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta}\)

1Reduce the constant angles

Step-by-step · Interactive unit circle
60°
\(\cos 180^\circ\)−1
\(\cos 360^\circ\)1
\(\sin(90^\circ+\theta)\)—
\(\sin(270^\circ+\theta)\)—
Step 1

Evaluate the constant angles first. On the unit circle, \(180^\circ\) lies on the negative \(x\)-axis, so

\[ \cos 180^\circ = -1 \]

and \(360^\circ\) coincides with \(0^\circ\) on the positive \(x\)-axis, so

\[ \cos 360^\circ = 1. \]
Hint: Think of the unit circle: the \(x\)-coordinate of the point at angle \(\phi\) is \(\cos\phi\), and the \(y\)-coordinate is \(\sin\phi\).
Step 2

Apply the reduction formulae for the two sine terms:

\[ \sin(90^\circ+\theta) = \cos\theta \] \[ \sin(270^\circ+\theta) = -\cos\theta \]
Hint: \(\sin(90^\circ+\theta)\) rotates the point \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise — a rotation \((x,y)\to(-y,x)\), turning sine into cosine. For \(270^\circ+\theta\), the rotation gives \((y,-x)\), turning sine into \(-\cos\theta\).
Step 3

Substitute everything into the original expression:

\[ \frac{-1}{1+\cos\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta} \]
Reduced expression \[ \frac{-1}{1+\cos\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta} \]
Hint: The denominators are now in terms of \(\cos\theta\) only — combine them over a common denominator next.
0 / 3

2Combine into a single fraction

Common denominator & Pythagorean identity
60°
Original value—
\(\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\)—
\(1-\cos^2\theta\)—
Match?—
Step 1

Take the common denominator \((1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)\):

\[ \frac{-1}{1+\cos\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta} =\frac{-(1-\cos\theta)+(1+\cos\theta)}{(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)} \]
Hint: Multiply the first fraction by \(\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\) and the second by \(\dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta}\).
Step 2

Simplify the numerator by expanding:

\[ -(1-\cos\theta)+(1+\cos\theta) = -1+\cos\theta+1+\cos\theta = 2\cos\theta \]

So the expression becomes:

\[ \frac{2\cos\theta}{(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)} \]
Hint: The constant terms \(-1\) and \(+1\) cancel, leaving \(2\cos\theta\) on top.
Step 3

Simplify the denominator using the difference of squares and the Pythagorean identity:

\[ (1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta) = 1-\cos^2\theta = \sin^2\theta \]
Hint: \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 1-\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta\).
Step 4

Therefore the whole expression simplifies to:

\[ \frac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} \]
Final answer — Option C \[ \frac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} \]
Hint: Check by substituting a value of \(\theta\) (use the slider above) — both the original expression and \(\dfrac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\) must give the same number.
0 / 4

✓Summary of Answers

  • \(\cos 180^\circ = -1\),   \(\cos 360^\circ = 1\)
  • \(\sin(90^\circ+\theta)=\cos\theta\),   \(\sin(270^\circ+\theta)=-\cos\theta\)
  • Reduced form: \(\displaystyle \frac{-1}{1+\cos\theta}+\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}\)
  • Combining and using \(1-\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta\):
  • \(\displaystyle \frac{\cos180^\circ}{1+\sin(90^\circ+\theta)}+\frac{\cos360^\circ}{1+\sin(270^\circ+\theta)}=\frac{2\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\)
  • Answer: Option C
