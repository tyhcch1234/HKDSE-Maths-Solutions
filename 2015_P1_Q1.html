numerator / base power of a power quotient law final answer
HKDSE 2015 · Paper 1 · Q1

Simplify $\dfrac{m^{9}}{\left(m^{3}n^{-7}\right)^{5}}$ and express your answer with positive indices. [3 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

1Power of a product & power of a power

Expand the denominator by multiplying each inner power by the outer index $5$:

$$\left(m^{3}n^{-7}\right)^{5}=m^{3\times 5}\,n^{-7\times 5}=m^{15}n^{-35}$$

So the expression becomes

$$\frac{m^{9}}{m^{15}n^{-35}}$$

Hint: $(a^{p}b^{q})^{k}=a^{pk}b^{qk}$ — multiply the indices.
2Quotient law for the $n$ term

Bring $n^{-35}$ to the numerator:

$$\frac{m^{9}}{m^{15}n^{-35}}=\frac{m^{9}\,n^{35}}{m^{15}}=\frac{n^{35}}{m^{15-9}}=\frac{n^{35}}{m^{6}}$$

Hint: $\dfrac{1}{a^{-k}}=a^{k}$ and $\dfrac{a^{p}}{a^{q}}=a^{p-q}$.
3Check positive indices

Both remaining powers, $n^{35}$ and $m^{6}$, already have positive indices, so no further change is needed.

Hint: the question demands positive indices — the final form must not contain any negative power.
4Final answer

$$\boxed{\dfrac{n^{35}}{m^{6}}}$$

Hint: state the fraction with both powers written as positive indices.
ANSWER
$\dfrac{m^{9}}{\left(m^{3}n^{-7}\right)^{5}}=\dfrac{n^{35}}{m^{6}}$
