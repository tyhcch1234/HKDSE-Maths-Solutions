Topic 4 · Polynomials — Solving quadratic equations with a parameter

Let \(m\) be a constant. Solve the equation \[ x^2 - 3x = (m-1)^2 - 3(m-1). \]

A. \(x = m-1\) or \(x = m-4\)
B. \(x = m-1\) or \(x = 4-m\)
C. \(x = 1-m\) or \(x = m-4\)
D. \(x = 1-m\) or \(x = 4-m\)
Quadratic equation Factorisation by grouping Difference of squares

🔍 Visual Insight: Grouping Method

The key is to recognise that the equation is already in the form \(x^2 - 3x = \text{(something)}\), and the right side looks exactly like the left side but with \(x\) replaced by \(m-1\). We can rearrange it as a quadratic in \(x\) and factorise by grouping. Use the slider to see how the constant term changes with \(m\), and watch the factorisation structure.

-20246

Equation to solve

\(x^2 - 3x = 4 - 3(2)\)

i.e. \(x^2 - 3x = -2\)

Rearranged / Factorised

\([x + (m-1)][x - (m-1)] - 3[x - (m-1)] = 0\)

Then grouping leads to $[x - (m-1)][x + (m-1) - 3] = 0$

For \(m=3\): \(x = 2\) or \(x = 1\)

🧩 Step-by-Step Solution

Subtract the RHS from both sides to form a quadratic equation in standard form.

\[ x^2 - 3x - \big[(m-1)^2 - 3(m-1)\big] = 0 \]

💡 Hint: Group the \(x\) terms and the \(m\) terms separately later.

Write the equation as a difference of two squares, then factorise \(x^2 - (m-1)^2\).

\[ x^2 - (m-1)^2 - 3x + 3(m-1) = 0 \] \[ [x + (m-1)][x - (m-1)] - 3[x - (m-1)] = 0 \]

💡 Hint: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)\). Here \(a = x\), \(b = m-1\).

Both terms contain the factor \([x - (m-1)]\). Factor it out.

\[ [x - (m-1)]\big[\,[x + (m-1)] - 3\,\big] = 0 \] \[ [x - (m-1)]\,[x + (m-1) - 3] = 0 \] \[ [x - (m-1)]\,[x + m - 4] = 0 \]

💡 Hint: Simplify \((m-1) - 3 = m - 4\).

Set each factor to zero and solve for \(x\).

\[ x - (m-1) = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x + m - 4 = 0 \] \[ x = m-1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4 - m \]

💡 Hint: This matches option B.

✅ Answer: B

\(x = m-1\) or \(x = 4-m\)
