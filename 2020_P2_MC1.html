Topic 8 · Laws of Indices, Exponential & Logarithmic Functions

Laws of Indices Negative Exponents

Q The Question

\[ \frac{6x}{\left(3x^{-5}\right)^{-2}} = \;? \]
  • A\(54x^{8}\)
  • B\(\dfrac{2x^{8}}{3}\)
  • C\(\dfrac{54}{x^{9}}\)
  • D\(\dfrac{2}{3x^{9}}\)

1 Step-by-Step Solution

0 / 5 steps

The denominator is \(\left(3x^{-5}\right)^{-2}\). The exponent \(-2\) sits outside the bracket, so it applies to everything inside: both the number \(3\) and the power \(x^{-5}\).

\[ \frac{6x}{\left(3x^{-5}\right)^{-2}} = \frac{6x}{3^{-2}\cdot\left(x^{-5}\right)^{-2}} \]
Rule: \((ab)^{n} = a^{n}b^{n}\).
Here \(a=3\), \(b=x^{-5}\) and \(n=-2\), so both factors get raised to the power \(-2\). Do not leave the \(3\) untouched.

For a power of a power we multiply the indices:

\[ \left(x^{-5}\right)^{-2} = x^{(-5)\times(-2)} = x^{10} \]

And for the number: \(3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{3^{2}} = \dfrac{1}{9}\).

So the denominator becomes:

\[ \left(3x^{-5}\right)^{-2} = 3^{-2}\cdot x^{10} = \frac{1}{9}x^{10} \]
Rules used here:
  • \(\left(x^{m}\right)^{n} = x^{mn}\) — multiply the indices.
  • \(a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^{n}}\) — a negative index means “take the reciprocal”.
Be careful: \((-5)\times(-2) = +10\), not \(-10\).

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal:

\[ \frac{6x}{\frac{1}{9}x^{10}} = 6x \times \frac{9}{x^{10}} = \frac{54x}{x^{10}} \]

Now we only have one variable \(x\) to handle — a simple index law.

Idea: \(\dfrac{A}{\frac{B}{C}} = A\times\dfrac{C}{B}\).
Multiplying by \(9\) on top: \(6 \times 9 = 54\). Keep the numbers and the powers of \(x\) separate.

Use \(\dfrac{x^{m}}{x^{n}} = x^{m-n}\). Remember \(x\) alone means \(x^{1}\):

\[ \frac{54x}{x^{10}} = 54x^{1-10} = 54x^{-9} = \frac{54}{x^{9}} \]
Rule: \(\dfrac{x^{m}}{x^{n}} = x^{m-n}\) — subtract the index of the denominator from the index of the numerator.
Here \(1 - 10 = -9\). A negative index in the final answer is usually rewritten as a fraction, matching the MCQ options.
\[ \frac{6x}{\left(3x^{-5}\right)^{-2}} = \frac{6x}{3^{-2}x^{10}} = \frac{6x}{\frac{1}{9}x^{10}} = \frac{54x}{x^{10}} = \frac{54}{x^{9}} \]

Therefore the answer is C.

\((ab)^n = a^n b^n\) \((x^m)^n = x^{mn}\) \(\dfrac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}\) \(a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}\)

3 Why the Other Options Are Wrong

Option A — \(54x^{8}\): the exponents were added instead of subtracted (\(10-2=8\)), and the \(x\) was misplaced in the numerator.

Option B — \(\dfrac{2x^{8}}{3}\): only the \(3\) inside the bracket was raised to the power \(-2\), giving \(\frac{1}{9}\), but the \(x^{-5}\) index was treated as \(+10\) then incorrectly combined (a sign slip).

Option D — \(\dfrac{2}{3x^{9}}\): the numbers were simplified incorrectly — \(6 \times 3 = 18\) should have been \(6\div 9 = \frac23\), but here \(3^2\) was multiplied instead of using the reciprocal \(3^{-2}=\frac19\).

Correct: \(\;6 \times \frac{9}{1} = 54\;\) (multiply by the reciprocal of \(\frac19\)), not \(6 \div 9 = \frac23\).
