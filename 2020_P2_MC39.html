Two circles with a common tangent — untangle the cyclic angle relationships

Geometry Angles in Alt. Segment Adjacent ∠s on St. Line ∠ Sum of Triangle Common Tangent

Question Multiple Choice

Read the figure carefully: which angles are given, and which equalities does the tangent create?

In the figure, \(ABC\) and \(CDE\) are circles such that \(ADC\) is a straight line. \(PQ\) is the common tangent to the two circles at \(C\).

\(AB\) is the tangent to the circle \(CDE\) at \(E\).

If \(\angle ADE = 100^\circ\) and \(\angle BCQ = 35^\circ\), then \(\angle ABC =\)

A. 55°
B. 65°
C. 70°
D. 55° ✓ (A)
Plan of attack: Convert \(\angle ADE\) into \(\angle CDE\) first, then use the angle in alternate segment twice — once for \(\angle BEC\) and once for \(\angle ECQ\).

Original Figure

Part (a) Use the straight line \(ADC\)

Drag the slider to change \(\angle ADE\) and watch \(\angle CDE\) respond on the straight line.

∠ADE = 100° ∠CDE = 180° − ∠ADE = 80°
Step 1
Since \(ADC\) is a straight line, \(\angle ADE\) and \(\angle CDE\) are adjacent angles on a straight line: \[ \angle CDE = 180^\circ - \angle ADE = 180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ \]
Hint: "Adjacent angles on a straight line" always sum to \(180^\circ\). The two angles sit side by side along \(D\)–\(C\).
\(\angle CDE = \mathbf{80^\circ}\)
0 / 1

Part (b) Angles in the alternate segment

Toggle it: the tangent \(PQ\) at \(C\) forces \(\angle ECQ\) to equal the angle in the alternate segment.

∠CDE = 80° ∠BEC = 80° ∠ECQ = 80°
Step 1
\(BE\) is a chord of circle \(CDE\) and \(AB\) is the tangent at \(E\). In the alternate segment: \[ \angle BEC = \angle CDE = 80^\circ \]
Hint: The tangent at \(E\) is the line \(AB\), not \(BE\). The angle between tangent \(AB\) and chord \(BE\) equals the angle in the alternate segment, which is \(\angle CDE\).
\(\angle BEC = \mathbf{80^\circ}\)
Step 2
\(PQ\) is the common tangent at \(C\) and \(CE\) is a chord. In the alternate segment: \[ \angle ECQ = \angle CDE = 80^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle ECQ\) is measured from the tangent ray \(CQ\) to the chord \(CE\). This equals the angle in the opposite arc, which is \(\angle CDE\).
\(\angle ECQ = \mathbf{80^\circ}\)
Step 3
Now split \(\angle ECQ\) using the given \(\angle BCQ = 35^\circ\): \[ \angle BCE = \angle ECQ - \angle BCQ = 80^\circ - 35^\circ = 45^\circ \]
Hint: The ray \(CB\) lies inside \(\angle ECQ\), so \(\angle BCE\) is the difference between the two angles.
\(\angle BCE = \mathbf{45^\circ}\)
0 / 3

Part (c) Angle sum of \(\triangle BCE\)

Adjust \(\angle BCQ\) and watch triangle \(BCE\) close up: the three angles always sum to \(180^\circ\).

∠BCE = 45° ∠BEC = 80° ∠CBE = 180° − 45° − 80° = 55°
Step 1
In \(\triangle BCE\), the interior angles add to \(180^\circ\): \[ \angle CBE = 180^\circ - \angle BCE - \angle BEC \] \[ = 180^\circ - 45^\circ - 80^\circ = 55^\circ \]
Hint: \(\angle CBE\) is the same angle as \(\angle ABC\) because \(A\), \(B\), \(E\) are collinear (both \(AB\) and \(BE\) lie on the tangent line through \(B\) and \(E\)).
\(\angle CBE = \angle ABC = \mathbf{55^\circ}\)
Step 2
Since \(AB\) is the tangent at \(E\), the points \(A\), \(B\), \(E\) lie on the same line, so \(\angle ABC = \angle CBE\). Therefore: \[ \boxed{\angle ABC = 55^\circ} \] This matches option A.
Hint: Always double-check by re-drawing: the answer \(55^\circ\) is smaller than \(65^\circ\), which fits a slender triangle with a \(80^\circ\) angle already taken.
Answer: Option A · 55°

📘 Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle CDE = 180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ\)  (adjacent angles on straight line \(ADC\))
  • \(\angle BEC = \angle CDE = 80^\circ\)  (angle in alternate segment, tangent \(AB\) / chord \(BE\))
  • \(\angle ECQ = \angle CDE = 80^\circ\)  (angle in alternate segment, tangent \(PQ\) / chord \(CE\))
  • \(\angle BCE = 80^\circ - 35^\circ = 45^\circ\)
  • In \(\triangle BCE\): \(\angle CBE = 180^\circ - 45^\circ - 80^\circ = 55^\circ\)
  • Since \(A, B, E\) are collinear, \(\angle ABC = \angle CBE = 55^\circ\)
Answer = Option A · 55°
