QQuestion Multiple Choice

If the straight lines \[hx+ky+15=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 4x+3y-5=0\] are perpendicular to each other and intersect at a point on the \(x\)-axis, then \(k=\)

A. \(-12\)
B. \(-4\)
C. \(3\)
D. \(16\)
Read carefully. Two conditions must hold at the same time: the lines are perpendicular, and they meet on the \(x\)-axis (i.e. their common point has \(y=0\)).

aPart (a) — Common point on the \(x\)-axis

Common point: —
Step 1
Write the second line in the form \(y=mx+c\). For \[4x+3y-5=0:\qquad 3y=-4x+5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x+\tfrac{5}{3}.\] Its \(x\)-intercept is found by putting \(y=0\): \[0=-\tfrac{4}{3}x+\tfrac{5}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ \tfrac{4}{3}x=\tfrac{5}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\tfrac{5}{4}.\]
Hint: To find an \(x\)-intercept, always set \(y=0\) — not \(x=0\). The point on the \(x\)-axis is \(\left(\tfrac54,\,0\right)\).
\(L_2\) meets the \(x\)-axis at \(\left(\dfrac{5}{4},\,0\right)\).
Step 2
The two lines intersect at a point on the \(x\)-axis, so their common point has \(y=0\). Putting \(y=0\) into \(L_1:\ hx+ky+15=0\): \[hx+15=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=-\frac{15}{h}.\] This \(x\)-intercept must equal the one we found for \(L_2\): \[-\frac{15}{h}=\frac{5}{4}.\]
Hint: The slider on the left controls \(h\) and \(k\). Watch the red \(x\)-intercept marker — it turns green only when both lines cross the \(x\)-axis at the same point.
Same \(x\)-intercept condition: \(-\dfrac{15}{h}=\dfrac{5}{4}\).
Step 3
Solve for \(h\): \[-\frac{15}{h}=\frac{5}{4}\ \Longrightarrow\ 5h=-60\ \Longrightarrow\ h=-12.\] So \(L_1\) becomes \(-12x+ky+15=0\), and both lines pass through \(\left(\dfrac{5}{4},\,0\right)\).
Hint: A negative \(h\) is expected — the line must cross the \(x\)-axis on the positive side, at \(x=\tfrac54>0\).
\(h=-12\)

Slope of \(L_2\): —
Product \(m_1 m_2\): —
Step 1
Find the slope of each line by rearranging into the form \(y=mx+c\). \[L_2:\ 4x+3y-5=0 \ \Longrightarrow\ 3y=-4x+5 \ \Longrightarrow\ y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x+\tfrac{5}{3},\quad m_2=-\tfrac{4}{3}.\] \[L_1:\ -12x+ky+15=0 \ \Longrightarrow\ ky=12x-15 \ \Longrightarrow\ y=\tfrac{12}{k}x-\tfrac{15}{k},\quad m_1=\tfrac{12}{k}.\]
Hint: When you divide by \(k\), remember \(k\neq 0\) (if \(k=0\), \(L_1\) would be vertical — not perpendicular to \(L_2\)). The slider shows how the slope of \(L_1\) changes as \(k\) changes.
\(m_1=\dfrac{12}{k},\qquad m_2=-\dfrac{4}{3}\).
Step 2
For perpendicular lines, the product of their slopes is \(-1\): \[m_1\cdot m_2=-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{12}{k}\times\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)=-1.\]
Hint: This is different from the "parallel" condition, where slopes would be equal. Perpendicular means one slope is the negative reciprocal of the other.
\(\dfrac{12}{k}\times\left(-\dfrac{4}{3}\right)=-1\)
Step 3
Simplify the left-hand side: \[\frac{12}{k}\times\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)=-\frac{48}{3k}=-\frac{16}{k}.\] So \[-\frac{16}{k}=-1\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{16}{k}=1\ \Longrightarrow\ k=16.\]
Hint: Check with the canvas readout: at \(k=16\), \(m_1=\tfrac{12}{16}=\tfrac34\) and \(m_2=-\tfrac43\), so \(m_1 m_2=\tfrac34\times\left(-\tfrac43\right)=-1\).
\(k=16\)  →  Answer: D

★Summary of Answers

  • \(x\)-intercept of \(L_2:\ 4x+3y-5=0\) is \(\left(\dfrac{5}{4},\,0\right)\).
  • Common-point condition gives \(-\dfrac{15}{h}=\dfrac{5}{4}\), hence \(h=-12\).
  • Slopes: \(m_1=\dfrac{12}{k}\) and \(m_2=-\dfrac{4}{3}\).
  • Perpendicular condition \(m_1m_2=-1\) gives \(-\dfrac{16}{k}=-1\), hence \(k=16\).
  • Final answer: D — \(k=16\)
Trap: using \(-\frac{15}{h}=-\frac{5}{4}\) or forgetting \(h\) before solving for \(k\)
