Ratio problem

Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be non-zero numbers.

If $a:c=5:3$ and $b:c=3:2$, then $(a+c):(b+c)=$

  • A. $7:5$
  • B. $8:5$
  • C. $16:15$
  • D. $19:15$
Method 1 — Unify $c$
Step 1

Write down the two given ratios

$$a:c=5:3 \qquad b:c=3:2$$

The common quantity between both ratios is $c$,
but the numbers are different ($3$ and $2$).

Hint: to compare $a$, $b$ and $c$ together,
the $c$-value must be the same in both ratios.

Step 2

Make the $c$-parts equal

Equivalent Ratios \(a:c=5:3=\dfrac{10}{6}\qquad b:c=3:2=\dfrac{9}{6}\) Multiplying the first ratio by \(2\) and the second by \(3\) makes the \(c\)-part \(6\) in both.

LCM of $3$ and $2$ is $6$. Scale each ratio:

$$a:c=5:3 \;=\; 10:6 \qquad b:c=3:2 \;=\; 9:6$$

Multiply the first ratio by $2$ and the second by $3$ — both now show $c$ as $6$ parts.

Step 3

Combine into one three-part ratio

Three-part Ratio with a Common Term \(a:b:c=10:9:6\) With \(c=6\) in both ratios, the parts of \(a\) and \(b\) can be written against the same unit.

Since $c=6$ in both, we get:

$$a:b:c=10:9:6$$

Now $a$, $b$ and $c$ are all measured against the same unit.

Step 4

Form the required ratio $(a+c):(b+c)$

Ratio of Sums \((a+c):(b+c)=(10+6):(9+6)=16:15\) Adding corresponding parts of a ratio is valid because all parts share the same unit.

$$a+c = 10+6 = 16$$

$$b+c = 9+6 = 15$$

$$\therefore\;(a+c):(b+c) = 16:15$$

Add the corresponding parts directly — no need to find the actual values of $a$, $b$, $c$.

Answer: C. $\;16:15$

Question. If \(a:c=4:5\) and \(b:c=2:3\), then \((a+c):(b+c)=\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The common quantity is \(c\), but its parts are \(5\) and \(3\);
the LCM of \(5\) and \(3\) is \(15\).
Step 2. Scale both ratios to \(c=15\): \(a:c=4:5=12:15\) and \(b:c=2:3=10:15\).
Step 3. Hence \(a:b:c=12:10:15\).
Step 4. \(a+c=12+15=27\) and \(b+c=10+15=25\), so \((a+c):(b+c)=27:25\).
Answer: \(27:25\)
