QUESTION 38
Which of the following may represent the graph of $y=f(x)$ and the graph of $y=f(x-2)+1$ on the same rectangular coordinate system?
A. $f(x)$ on the right, $f(x-2)+1$ shifted left & up
B. $f(x)$ on the left, $f(x-2)+1$ shifted right & up
C. $f(x)$ on the left, $f(x-2)+1$ shifted right & up (shifted version to the right and above)
D. $f(x)$ on the right, $f(x-2)+1$ shifted left & up
1Decode the transformation
Comparing $y=f(x)$ with $$y=f(x-2)+1,$$ the inner change $x \mapsto x-2$ is a horizontal change and the outer $+1$ is a vertical change. Take them one at a time.
Hint: inside the bracket → horizontal (x-direction); outside → vertical (y-direction).
2Horizontal: the $x-2$ (shift RIGHT 2)
Replacing $x$ by $x-2$ shifts the graph 2 units to the RIGHT, because a point $(a,\,f(a))$ moves to $(a+2,\,f(a))$ — the feature now occurs 2 units later. $$\boxed{\;\text{right }2\;}$$
Remember: inside the bracket it acts “opposite” to intuition — $x-2$ means right, not left.
3Vertical: the $+1$ (shift UP 1)
Adding $1$ outside the function raises every $y$-value by $1$: 1 unit UP. $$\boxed{\;\text{up }1\;}$$
4Combine into one translation vector
The two movements combine into a single translation: $${}^{\,2\,}C_{\,1\,}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{2 right, 1 up}.$$ So the second curve is the first curve pushed up-and-right.
Both curves have exactly the same shape — only the position changes.
5Eliminate the wrong options
In options A and D, the curve $y=f(x-2)+1$ is placed to the left of $y=f(x)$ — that would correspond to the form $f(x+2)+1$ (a shift left). ✗

Option B has it to the right, but the two curves do not show the 1-unit vertical lift consistently (the “+1” curve sits at the wrong height relative to the base curve). ✗
Test each picture by asking: “from the red curve to the blue curve, do I travel right AND up?”
6Confirm option C
In C, the curve labelled $y=f(x)$ lies to the left, and $y=f(x-2)+1$ lies to the right and is raised — exactly a translation by ${}^{2}C_{1}$. Every point $(a,f(a))$ lands on $(a+2,\,f(a)+1)$. ✓
Answer: C
Key rule: $y=f(x-h)+k$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ translated by ${}^{h}C_{k}$ — i.e. $h$ units right, $k$ units up. Here $h=2$, $k=1$.
