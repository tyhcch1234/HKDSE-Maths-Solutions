HKDSE Math 2020 Paper 1 Q10

Partial Variation — a quantity that is “partly constant and partly varies as \(h^3\)”

📄 The Question

The price of a brand X souvenir of height \(h\text{ cm}\) is \(\$P\). \(P\) is partly constant and partly varies as \(h^3\). When \(h=3\), \(P=59\) and when \(h=7\), \(P=691\).

(a)  Find the price of a brand X souvenir of height \(4\text{ cm}\). [4 marks]

(b)  Someone claims that the price of a brand X souvenir of height \(5\text{ cm}\) is higher than the total price of two brand X souvenirs of height \(4\text{ cm}\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

Key concepts

Partial Variation \(P = a + b h^3\) Simultaneous Equations Substitution Comparison / Justification

Variation language translator:
“\(P\) is partly constant” → add a constant term \(a\).
“partly varies as \(h^3\)” → add \(b h^3\). So the general form is always \[ P = a + b\,h^{3} \]

🧩 Part (b) — Step-by-step

A “claim” question. You must compare two numbers and give a conclusion.

⚖️ Compare: one \(5\text{ cm}\) vs two \(4\text{ cm}\) souvenirs

One \(5\text{ cm}\) souvenir

🎁

P = 5 + 2(5)³

$255

Two \(4\text{ cm}\) souvenirs

🎁🎁

2 × (5 + 2(4)³) = 2 × 133

$266

Since \[ 255 < 266 \] the price of one \(5\text{ cm}\) souvenir is not higher than the total price of two \(4\text{ cm}\) souvenirs.

∴ The claim is not correct.

Common trap: students sometimes write “correct” because 255 “looks big”. Always compute BOTH sides first, then use the inequality sign to decide.

💡 Exam Tips & Marking Scheme

How are the marks awarded?

Part (a) [4 marks]:

1A — correct setup \(a + 27b = 59\) (or equivalent)
1M — setting up \(a + 343b = 691\) and solving the simultaneous equations
1A — correct \(a=5\), \(b=2\)
1A — correct price for \(4\text{ cm}\) (= $133)

Part (b) [2 marks]:

1M — computing \(P\) when \(h=5\) = $255
1A — comparing with \(2(133)=266\) and concluding the claim is not correct

Why must I write “let \(P = a + bh^3\)” first?

The phrase “partly constant and partly varies as \(h^3\)” translates directly into the relation \(P = a + bh^3\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are non-zero constants. Writing this relation earns the setup mark, and it shows the marker you understand the variation type.

Common mistakes to avoid
  • Writing \(P = a(h)^3\) instead of \(P = a + bh^3\) (forgetting the constant term).
  • Confusing \(h\) and \(P\) when setting up equations.
  • In part (b), forgetting to double the \(4\text{ cm}\) price (the question says two souvenirs).
  • Not writing a conclusion sentence — the last mark in (b) requires a clear statement that the claim is not correct.
