HKDSE 2017 Paper 2 Q42 Probability Combination

Committee Team Selection

There are 13 students and 6 teachers in a committee. If 5 students and 4 teachers are selected from the committee to form a team, how many different teams can be formed?

A. 4 290
B. 19 305
C. 92 378
D. 55 598 400

📘 Step-by-Step Solution

1 Understand the task
▾

We need to form a team by choosing 5 students out of 13 and 4 teachers out of 6.

The order of selection does not matter — we only care about which individuals are chosen.

This is a classic combination problem. The total number of teams is the product of the two independent selections.

2 Choose the students
▾

Number of ways to choose 5 students from 13:

\[ C^{13}_{5} = C^{13}_{5} = \frac{13!}{5! \cdot 8!} \]

Hint: \(n! = n \times (n-1) \times \dots \times 1\)

3 Choose the teachers
▾

Number of ways to choose 4 teachers from 6:

\[ C^{6}_{4} = C^{6}_{4} = \frac{6!}{4! \cdot 2!} \]

4 Multiply the results
▾

Since the choice of students and teachers are independent, we multiply the two counts:

\[ \text{Total teams} = C^{13}_{5} \cdot C^{6}_{4} \]

Now compute each part:

  • \(C^{13}_{5} = \frac{13 \times 12 \times 11 \times 10 \times 9}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{154440}{120} = 1287\)
  • \(C^{6}_{4} = \frac{6 \times 5}{2 \times 1} = 15\)

Thus total \(= 1287 \times 15 = 19305\).

5 Answer
▾

\(C^{13}_{5} \cdot C^{6}_{4} = 19305\)

Therefore, the correct option is B. 19 305.

Common mistake: using permutations (ordered selection) instead of combinations.
