Circle Geometry: Tangent & Angle Bisector

In the figure, O is the centre of the circle ABC. DE is the tangent to the circle at A. If AB is the angle bisector of $\angle CAE$, and $\angle AOC = 124^\circ$, find $\angle ACO$.
  • A. $28^\circ$
  • B. $26^\circ$
  • C. $31^\circ$
  • D. $34^\circ$
STEP 1 · Use the isosceles triangle AOC

Since $OA$ and $OC$ are radii of the circle, $\triangle AOC$ is isosceles with $OA = OC$.

Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, so

$$\angle OAC = \angle ACO = x.$$

Angle sum in $\triangle AOC$:

$$x + x + 124^\circ = 180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; 2x = 56^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; x = 28^\circ.$$

Hint: first find the base angles of the isosceles triangle $AOC$ using the given central angle $124^\circ$.
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle \(CA=CB\ \Longrightarrow\ \angle A=\angle B\) \\(OA\\) and \\(OC\\) are radii, so \\(OA=OC\\) and the two base angles of \\(\\triangle AOC\\) are equal, giving \\(x=28^\\circ\\).
STEP 2 · Use the tangent–radius property

The tangent $DE$ meets the radius $OA$ at right angles (tangent $\perp$ radius at point of contact). Hence

$$\angle OAE = 90^\circ, \qquad \angle OAD = 90^\circ.$$

At $A$, the angles on the straight line $DE$ give

$$\angle OAC + \angle CAB + \angle BAE = 90^\circ.$$

Hint: a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.
Tangent–Radius Property \(\text{tangent}\perp\text{radius at the point of contact}\) \\(DE\\) touches the circle at \\(A\\), so \\(\\angle OAE=90^\\circ\\) and the angles of \\(\\angle OAE\\) sum to \\(90^\\circ\\).
STEP 3 · Apply the angle bisector condition

Let $\angle BAE = y$. Since $AB$ bisects $\angle CAE$, we have

$$\angle CAB = y.$$

Substituting into Step 2:

$$28^\circ + y + y = 90^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; 2y = 62^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; y = 31^\circ.$$

So $\angle CAB = \angle BAE = 31^\circ$.

Hint: an angle bisector splits the angle into two equal parts.
STEP 4 · Use the inscribed angle theorem

The inscribed angle $\angle CAB$ subtends arc $CB$ at the circumference, while the central angle $\angle COB$ subtends the same arc at the centre. Hence

$$\angle COB = 2\angle CAB = 2 \times 31^\circ = 62^\circ.$$

Hint: angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference subtending the same arc.
Inscribed Angle Theorem \(\angle\text{ at centre}=2\times\angle\text{ at circumference (same arc)}\) Both \\(\\angle COB\\) and \\(\\angle CAB\\) stand on arc \\(CB\\), so \\(\\angle COB=2\\times31^\\circ=62^\\circ\\).
STEP 5 · Find the required angle

In $\triangle AOC$, we already found $\angle ACO = x = 28^\circ$.

Check with the full figure: $\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 124^\circ - 62^\circ = 174^\circ$, consistent with the geometry.

Therefore $\angle ACO = \boxed{28^\circ}$ and the correct option is A.
