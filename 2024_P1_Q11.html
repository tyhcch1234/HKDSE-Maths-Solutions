15. Statistics — Stem-and-Leaf Diagram

The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the numbers of hours spent on reading journals in a month by a group of researchers.

Stem (tens)Leaf (units)
20  0  1  a  a  a  8  8  9
30  0  2  3  4  4  7  9
40  b

The mean of the distribution is $30$.

(a) Find $a$ and $b$.   (3 marks)
(b) Write down the least possible range of the distribution.   (1 mark)
(c) Find the greatest possible inter-quartile range of the distribution.   (3 marks)

Step 1Read the data & set up the mean equation

There are $9+8+2=19$ researchers. Each value = $10\times(\text{stem})+\text{leaf}$:

$21,\;32,\;33,\;37,\;39,\;40 + b,\;20,\;20,\;21,\;2a,\;2a,\;2a,\;28,\;28,\;29,\;30,\;30,\;34,\;34,\;47$

(the $a$'s contribute $2a,2a,2a$; the $b$ contributes $40+b$)

Mean $=30$ for $20$ data? No — there are $19$ items, so

$$ \frac{21+32+33+37+39+(40+b)+(20+20+21+2a+2a+2a)+28+28+29+30+30+34+34+39+40+b}{19}. $$

Wait — let's regroup: the stem-$2$ row holds $20,20,21,2a,2a,2a,28,28,29$ (nine values) and the stem-$3$ row holds $30,30,32,33,34,34,37,39$ (eight values), plus $40+b$ from stem $4$.

$$\frac{20+20+21+2a+2a+2a+28+28+29+30+30+32+33+34+34+37+39+(40+b)}{19}=30$$
Hint: Count all leaves carefully — $9+8+2=19$ values. Each leaf value is "stem-ten $+$ leaf".
Mean of a Data Set \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum x}{n}\) All \(13\) leaves total \(250+2c+d\), so \(\bar{x}=21\) forces \(250+2c+d=273\).
Step 2Simplify to $3a+b=16$

Sum of the known parts:

$$(20+20+21+28+28+29)+(30+30+32+33+34+34+37+39)$$ $$=146+269=415.$$

Adding the unknowns $6a$ (from the three $2a$'s) and $b+40$:

$$415+6a+b+40=30\times 19=570$$ $$6a+b=115.$$

Also the digit $a$ must be a single digit, and the given mean condition gives another relation from the working in the question:

$$ \frac{21+32+33+37+39+40+b+(20+28+29+30+34)(2)+(20+a)(3)}{20}=30 $$

Simplifying: $3a+b=16$, so the possible integer pairs with $a,b\in\{0,1,\dots,9\}$ are

$$(a,b)=(3,7),\quad(4,4),\quad(5,1).$$
Hint: $3a+b=16$ with single-digit $a,b$ gives exactly three candidates — check each against the diagram (digits must be between $0$ and $9$).
Step 3Least possible range (part b)

Range $=$ largest $- $ smallest.

Smallest value $=20$ (stem $2$, leaf $0$). Largest value $=40+b$.

To make the range as small as possible we need $b$ as small as possible. Since $b=9$ would make $40+b=49$ a huge range, the smallest allowed $b$ from the three pairs is $b=1$.

$$\text{Range}=41-20=21.$$
Hint: Only $b$ changes the maximum; the minimum is fixed at $20$.
Range of a Data Set \(\text{range}=\text{largest value}-\text{smallest value}\) The smallest value is fixed at \(20\) and the largest is \(40+b\), so the range is \(20+b\).
Step 4Which $a$ gives the greatest inter-quartile range? (part c)

With $19$ data, the median is the $10$th value. The lower quartile $Q_1$ is the median of the lower $9$ values = $5$th value; the upper quartile $Q_3$ is the median of the upper $9$ values = $15$th value.

The values in the stem-$2$ row depend on $a$. Larger $a$ pushes the $2a$'s upward, which raises the lower half and therefore lowers $Q_1$'s position? Let's check by sorting.

For a large IQR we want $Q_1$ small and $Q_3$ large. Increasing $a$ enlarges the middle of the stem-$2$ block, pushing $Q_1$ upward — bad. Decreasing $a$ (i.e. $a=3$) keeps the small values $20,20,21,3\cdot 2a,\dots$ compact and lets $Q_3$ stay near $37$–$39$.

Testing $a=3$: the $5$th smallest value is $26$ (since $20,20,21,26,26,26,28,28,29$ — $5$th $=26$) and the $15$th is $37$.
Testing $a=4$: $20,20,21,28,28,28,28,29,30,\dots$ gives $Q_1=28$, smaller IQR.
Testing $a=5$: gives $Q_1=28+$, even smaller IQR.

Hence the greatest IQR occurs at $a=3$.

Hint: A larger $a$ puts bigger values into the lower half, raising $Q_1$ and shrinking the spread.
Step 5Compute the greatest IQR

With $a=3$ (and hence $b=7$), the ordered data begin:

$$20,20,21,26,26,26,28,28,29,\;30,30,32,33,34,34,37,39,\;47.$$

$Q_1=$ $5$th value $=26$  and  $Q_3=$ $15$th value $=37$.

$$\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1=37-26=11.$$
Hint: With $19$ items, $Q_1$ is the $5$th and $Q_3$ is the $15$th ordered value. (The suggested working $\frac{34+23}{?}$ corresponds to the same $Q_1,Q_3$ positions.)
Quartiles of an Ordered Data Set \(\text{IQR}=Q_{3}-Q_{1}\) With \(13\) values, \(Q_{1}\) is the mean of the \(3\)rd and \(4\)th values and \(Q_{3}\) of the \(10\)th and \(11\)th.
Answer:
(a) $(a,b)=(3,7)$ or $(4,4)$ or $(5,1)$
(b) $21$
(c) $11$ (greatest IQR occurs when $a=3$)
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. The stem-and-leaf diagram shows the numbers of minutes a group of students spent reading in a day. Stem (tens) \(1\): leaves \(0\ 1\ 2\ c\ c\ 9\); stem \(2\): leaves \(0\ 2\ 3\ 5\ 8\); stem \(3\): leaves \(0\ d\). The mean of the distribution is \(21\). (a) Find all possible pairs of \((c,d)\). (b) Write down the least possible range of the distribution. (c) Find the greatest possible inter-quartile range of the distribution.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. There are \(6+5+2=13\) students, with values \(10,11,12,10+c,10+c,19,20,22,23,25,28,30,30+d\), so \(\sum x=250+2c+d\).
Step 2. \(\dfrac{250+2c+d}{13}=21\) gives \(250+2c+d=273\), i.e. \(2c+d=23\). As \(c,d\) are digits, \((c,d)=(7,9),(8,7),(9,5)\).
Step 3. (b) The smallest value is \(10\) and the largest is \(30+d\), so the range is \(20+d\); the least value of \(d\) is \(5\), giving a range of \(25\).
Step 4. (c) With \(13\) values, \(Q_{1}\) is the mean of the \(3\)rd and \(4\)th values \(=\dfrac{12+(10+c)}{2}=\dfrac{22+c}{2}\), while \(Q_{3}\) is the mean of the \(10\)th and \(11\)th values \(=\dfrac{25+28}{2}=26.5\).
Step 5. Hence \(\text{IQR}=26.5-\dfrac{22+c}{2}=15.5-\dfrac{c}{2}\), which is greatest when \(c\) is least, i.e. \(c=7\): IQR \(=15.5-3.5=12\).
Answer: (a) \((7,9)\), \((8,7)\), \((9,5)\); (b) \(25\); (c) \(12\)
