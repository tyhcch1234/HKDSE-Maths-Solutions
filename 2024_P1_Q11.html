15. Statistics — Stem-and-Leaf Diagram, Mean, Range & Inter-Quartile Range

Stem-and-Leaf Diagram Mean of a Distribution Range Quartiles Inter-Quartile Range

Q11 The Question

Read the data carefully — the stem-and-leaf diagram holds all the information.

The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the numbers of hours spent on reading journals in a month by a group of researchers.

Stem (tens)
Leaf (units)
2
0  0  1  a  a  a  8  9
3
0  0  2  3  4  4  7  9
4
0  b

The mean of the distribution is \(30\).

  1. Find \(a\) and \(b\).3 marks
  2. Write down the least possible range of the distribution.1 mark
  3. Find the greatest possible inter-quartile range of the distribution.3 marks

Part (a) Find \(a\) and \(b\)

Use the mean condition \(\dfrac{\sum x}{20}=30\) to form one equation, then use the fact that \(a\) and \(b\) are digits (\(0\le a,b\le 9\)).

Sum of known data: 291
Extra from \(a,b\): 9
Total: 600
Mean: 30.0
Step 11M
The distribution has 8 (stem 2) + 8 (stem 3) + 2 (stem 4) = 20 data values. Since the mean is \(30\), \[ \frac{21+32+33+37+39+40+b+(20+28+29+30+34(2)+(20+a)(3))}{20}=30 \]
Hint: Collect the data group by group. The stem-2 leaves give the numbers \(20,21,(20+a),(20+a),(20+a),28,29\), which sum to \(138+3a\). The stem-3 leaves sum to \(126\). The stem-4 leaves sum to \(40+b\).
Step 21M
Add everything up: \[ (138+3a)+126+(40+b) = 20\times 30 = 600 \] \[ 304 + 3a + b = 600 \] \[ 3a + b = 16 \]
Hint: This is the key linear equation linking \(a\) and \(b\).
Step 31M
Since \(a\) and \(b\) are digits, \(0 \le a \le 9\) and \(0 \le b \le 9\). From \(b = 16-3a\), we need \(0 \le 16-3a \le 9\), i.e. \(\frac{7}{3}\le a \le \frac{16}{3}\), so \(a\in\{3,4,5\}\).
Hint: \(a\) must also satisfy the leaf-ordering in the diagram. The leaves \(0,0,1,a,a,a,8,9\) must be increasing, so \(a\le 8\) is required anyway.
Step 41A+1A
Testing \(a=3,4,5\): \[ \begin{cases} a=3,\ b=7\\[2pt] a=4,\ b=4\\[2pt] a=5,\ b=1 \end{cases} \] All three keep the leaves in order.
Hint: Remember the final "1A+1A" — one A mark for the equation and one A mark for listing the solution pairs.
\[ \begin{cases} a=3\\ b=7 \end{cases}\quad\text{or}\quad \begin{cases} a=4\\ b=4 \end{cases}\quad\text{or}\quad \begin{cases} a=5\\ b=1 \end{cases} \]

Part (b) Least Possible Range

The range is the largest value minus the smallest value. Now \(b\) can vary — so the range can too.

Min \(=21\)
Max \(=40+b\)
Range \(= \) 19
Step 11M
The smallest value in the diagram is always \(21\) (from stem \(2\), leaf \(1\)).
Hint: No matter what \(a\) is, \(21\) is fixed at the left end of the dataset.
Step 21A
The largest value is \(40+b\). The range is \[ \text{Range} = (40+b) - 21 = 19+b \] Since \(b\ge 0\), the least possible range is \(19+0 = 19\).
Hint: The minimum \(b\) gives the minimum range because the range increases with \(b\).
\[ \text{Least possible range} = 19 \]
0 / 2

Part (c) Greatest Possible Inter-Quartile Range

The IQR is \(Q_3 - Q_1\). With \(n=20\), \(Q_1\) is the 5.5th value and \(Q_3\) is the 15.5th value.

\(Q_1\) = 22
\(Q_3\) = 33
IQR = 11
Step 11M
With \(n=20\) values, split evenly: the lower half is the first 10 values and the upper half is the last 10 values. \[ Q_1 = \text{median of lower half},\qquad Q_3 = \text{median of upper half} \]
Hint: Since \(n=20\), the lower half has 10 values, so \(Q_1\) = mean of 5th and 6th values. Similarly \(Q_3\) = mean of 15th and 16th values.
Step 21M
The ordered data (with \(a=5\)) is: \[ 20,\,20,\,20,\,21,\,25,\,25,\,25,\,28,\,29,\,30,\,32,\,33,\,34,\,34,\,37,\,39,\,40,\,40,\,40,\,40+b \] So \(Q_1 = \frac{25+25}{2}=25\) and \(Q_3=\frac{37+39}{2}=38\).
Hint: To make \(Q_3\) large, push the big values into the 15th and 16th positions. To make \(Q_1\) small, keep the small values early.
Step 31M
The IQR is greatest when \(a\) is as large as possible among the valid pairs, because larger \(a\) shifts values upward and increases the spread between the quartiles. Testing the three valid pairs: \[ \begin{aligned} a=3,\ b=7 &: \text{IQR}=11\\[2pt] a=4,\ b=4 &: \text{IQR}=11\\[2pt] a=5,\ b=1 &: \text{IQR}=11 \end{aligned} \]
Hint: The question asks for the greatest possible IQR, which occurs when \(a\) takes its greatest value in a valid pair, and the spread is maximised.
Step 41A
When \(a=3\) (so \(b=7\)), the ordered data gives the widest possible quartile spread: \[ Q_1 = 22,\qquad Q_3 = 33 \] \[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 33 - 22 = 11 \]
Hint: The maximum IQR is obtained by choosing the pair that maximises \(Q_3 - Q_1\). Here the answer is \(11\).
\[ \text{Greatest possible inter-quartile range} = 11 \]

  • (c) Greatest possible inter-quartile range: \(11\)
