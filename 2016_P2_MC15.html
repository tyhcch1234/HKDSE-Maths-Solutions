Angles formed by a transversal cutting two parallel lines — which statements must be true?

Parallel Lines Transversal Interior Angles Exterior Angle of △ “Must be true”

Q The Question Multiple Choice

According to the figure, which of the following must be true?

  1. \(a + c = 180^\circ\)
  2. \(a + b - c = 180^\circ\)
  3. \(b + c = 360^\circ\)
  • A. I only
  • B. II only
  • C. I and III only
  • D. II and III only

In the given figure, the two arrowed rays are parallel; a broken (zig-zag) line joins them, creating angles \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\).

Step 1

The two arrowed rays are parallel. Slide a straight line through the middle vertex so that it is also parallel to them.

Label the two parts of angle \(b\) cut off by this helper line as \(x\) and \(y\), so \(x + y = b\) (angles at a point on the same side of a straight line).

Hint: Parallel lines + a transversal always give equal corresponding / alternate angles — that is the whole tool for this question.
Step 2

Because the helper line and the upper arrowed ray are parallel, the angle between the upper ray and the first segment of the zig-zag equals the alternate angle at the middle vertex:

\[ x = c \qquad (\text{so } x = 120^\circ \text{ in the current picture}) \]

Likewise, the lower arrowed ray and the helper line are parallel, so the angle at the bottom vertex equals the other part:

\[ y = 180^\circ - a \]
Hint: Rotate your head — the angle \(a\) and the angle \(y\) are co-interior (same-side interior) angles between the two parallel lines.
Step 3

Add the two pieces: \(b = x + y = c + (180^\circ - a)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} b &= c + 180^\circ - a \\[4pt] a + b - c &= 180^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Statement II is proven: \(a + b - c = 180^\circ\). Rearranging also gives \(a + c = b - 180^\circ + 2c\), so statements I and III are not forced by parallelism.

Step 1

From part (a), \(b\) is completely determined by \(a\) and \(c\):

\[ b = 180^\circ + c - a \]

So you may choose \(a\) and \(c\) freely, and \(b\) follows automatically.

Hint: Do not treat \(a, b, c\) as independent. The two parallel arrowed rays remove one degree of freedom from the zig-zag.
Step 2

Substitute the forced value of \(b\) into each statement and simplify:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{I. } & a + c \;\overset{?}{=}\; 180^\circ \\[3pt] \text{II. } & a + (180^\circ + c - a) - c = 180^\circ \quad \checkmark \\[3pt] \text{III. } & (180^\circ + c - a) + c \;\overset{?}{=}\; 360^\circ \end{aligned} \]

Statement II collapses to the identity \(180^\circ = 180^\circ\), true for every legal choice of \(a\) and \(c\).

Hint: Statements I and III still depend on \(a\) and \(c\), so a counter-example kills them — e.g. the default \(a=60^\circ,\ c=120^\circ\) gives I true but \(a=60^\circ,\ c=80^\circ\) gives I false.
Step 3

Drag the sliders: chip II never moves off \(180^\circ\), while chips I and III move around. Hence only II must be true.

Answer: B — II only.

Summary at a glance

  • Draw a helper line through the middle vertex parallel to both arrowed rays.
  • Split angle \(b\) into \(x\) and \(y\): \(x=c\) (alternate ∠s, ∥ lines) and \(y=180^\circ-a\) (co-interior ∠s, ∥ lines).
  • Therefore \(b=c+180^\circ-a\), i.e. \[\boxed{a+b-c=180^\circ}\]
  • Statement I \(a+c=180^\circ\) — not forced. ✗
  • Statement II \(a+b-c=180^\circ\) — always true. ✓
  • Statement III \(b+c=360^\circ\) — not forced. ✗
  • Correct option: B. II only
