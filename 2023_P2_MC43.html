Question

When a boy throws a dart, the probability that he hits the target is $0.6$. If this boy throws the dart $4$ times, find the probability that he hits the target at least 2 times.

  • A. 0.5248
  • B. 0.7056
  • C. 0.8208
  • D. 0.8464

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Tap each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict the next line before opening it!

1 Set up the probability model ▼

Each throw is an independent trial with only two outcomes: hit (H) or miss (M).

$p = P(\text{hit}) = 0.6 \qquad q = P(\text{miss}) = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$

The number of hits $X$ in $4$ throws follows a binomial distribution $X \sim B(4,\ 0.6)$.

Hint: Since the throws are independent, the probability of one specific sequence (e.g. H M H M) is found by multiplying the probabilities of each throw.
2 Recognise "at least 2" is many cases ▼

"At least 2 times" means $X \ge 2$, i.e. the possible cases are $X = 2,\ 3,\ 4$.

Hint: Adding three separate cases is long and error-prone. Look for a shortcut — the complement!
3 Use the complement rule ▼

Every outcome is either "at least 2 hits" or its complement "fewer than 2 hits". Fewer than 2 means $X = 0$ or $X = 1$.

$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - P(X = 0) - P(X = 1)$
Hint: For any event $E$, $P(E) = 1 - P(E')$. Choosing the complement here reduces the work to only two cases.
4 Compute $P(X = 0)$ — all misses ▼

All 4 throws must be misses:

$P(X = 0) = (0.4)^4 = 0.0256$
Hint: There is only one arrangement (M M M M), so no multiplication by a binomial coefficient is needed.
5 Compute $P(X = 1)$ — exactly one hit ▼

The single hit can occur on the 1st, 2nd, 3rd or 4th throw, giving 4 equally likely arrangements, each with probability $(0.6)(0.4)^3$:

$P(X = 1) = 4 \times (0.6)(0.4)^3 = 4 \times 0.0384 = 0.1536$
Hint (Binomial formula): $P(X = k) = C^{n}_{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$ with $n = 4$, $p = 0.6$. Here $k = 1$, so $C^{4}_{1} = 4$.
6 Combine and obtain the answer ▼
$P(X \ge 2) = 1 - 0.0256 - 0.1536 = 1 - 0.1792 = \mathbf{0.8208}$

The answer is C. 0.8208.

Hint: Always check your final value lies between $0$ and $1$, and that it is larger than the probability of a single case (here $0.6$ for one hit) — a good sanity check.

The required probability $= P(\text{hits at least 2 times})$
$= 1 - P(\text{hits 0 times}) - P(\text{hits 1 time})$
$= 1 - P(\text{no darts hit}) - \big[\,P(\text{only 1st dart hits}) + P(\text{only 2nd dart hits})$
$\quad + P(\text{only 3rd dart hits}) + P(\text{only 4th dart hits})\,\big]$
$= 1 - (1-0.6)^4 - \big[(0.6)(1-0.6)^3 + (1-0.6)(0.6)(1-0.6)^2$
$\quad + (1-0.6)^2(0.6)(1-0.6) + (1-0.6)^3(0.6)\big]$
$= 0.8208$
