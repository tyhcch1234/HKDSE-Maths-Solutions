QUESTION

17. The coordinates of the points $Q$ and $R$ are $(10,-1)$ and $(-4,-9)$ respectively.

(a) Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that $PQ = PR$. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$.

  1. Describe the geometric relationship between $\Gamma$ and $QR$.
  2. Find the equation of $\Gamma$.

(3 marks)

(b) Let $C$ be the circle which passes through $Q$, $R$ and the point $(4,3)$.

  1. Find the equation of $C$.
  2. The coordinates of the point $U$ are $(10,4)$. It is found that $U$ lies outside $C$. $UV$ and $UW$ are the tangents to $C$ at the points $V$ and $W$ respectively. Is the area of the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$ greater than $100$? Explain your answer.

(5 marks)

STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

Step 1 · (a)(i) The locus Γ

Since $P$ moves so that $PQ = PR$, $P$ is always equidistant from $Q$ and $R$.

Therefore $\Gamma$ is the perpendicular bisector of $QR$.

Γ is the perpendicular bisector of QR.

Step 2 · (a)(ii) Equation of Γ

Perpendicular Bisector as a Locus \(PX=QY\ \text{type condition}\ \Longrightarrow\ P\ \text{lies on the perpendicular bisector}\) Since \(PQ=PR\) for every position of \(P\), the locus \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(QR\), passing through the mid-point \((3,-5)\) with slope \(-\dfrac{7}{4}\).

Mid-point of $QR$:

$$M=\left(\frac{10+(-4)}{2},\ \frac{-1+(-9)}{2}\right)=(3,-5)$$

Slope of $QR$:

$$m_{QR}=\frac{-9-(-1)}{-4-10}=\frac{-8}{-14}=\frac{4}{7}$$

Slope of $\Gamma$ (perpendicular):

$$m_\Gamma=-\frac{7}{4}$$

Equation of $\Gamma$ through $(3,-5)$:

$$y-(-5)=-\frac{7}{4}(x-3)$$ $$7x+4y-1=0$$

Γ : 7x + 4y − 1 = 0

Step 3 · (b)(i) Perpendicular bisector of RS

Let $S$ denote $(4,3)$. The centre of $C$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $QR$ and $RS$.

Mid-point of $RS$:

$$\left(\frac{4+(-4)}{2},\ \frac{3+(-9)}{2}\right)=(0,-3)$$

Slope of $RS$:

$$m_{RS}=\frac{3-(-9)}{4-(-4)}=\frac{12}{8}=\frac{3}{2}$$

Slope of the perpendicular bisector:

$$-\frac{2}{3}$$

Its equation through $(0,-3)$:

$$y-(-3)=-\frac{2}{3}(x-0)\ \Longrightarrow\ 2x+3y+9=0$$

Step 4 · (b)(i) Centre and radius → equation of C

Solving the two perpendicular bisectors:

$$\begin{cases} 7x+4y-1=0\\ 2x+3y+9=0\end{cases}$$

gives the centre $G=(3,-5)$.

Radius $r = GQ$:

$$r=\sqrt{(10-3)^2+(-1+5)^2}=\sqrt{49+16}=\sqrt{65}$$

Equation of $C$:

$$(x-3)^2+(y+5)^2=65$$

C : (x − 3)² + (y + 5)² = 65

Step 5 · (b)(ii) Observing the circle of △UVW

Angle in a Semicircle \(\angle GVU=90^{\circ}\ \Longrightarrow\ G\ \text{lies on the circle with diameter }GU\) The two tangent-radius right angles put \(G\), \(V\) and \(W\) on the circle with diameter \(GU\), which is the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\).

Let $G$ be the centre of $C$. Because $UV$ and $UW$ are tangents,

$$\angle GVU=\angle GWU=90^\circ$$

Hence $G$ lies on the circle with diameter $GU$ (angles in a semicircle), and that circle is the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$.

So $GU$ is a diameter of the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$.

Key idea: the tangent–radius right angle puts G, V, W on a circle with diameter GU.

Step 6 · (b)(ii) Compare the area with 100

Area of a Circle \(A=\pi r^{2},\qquad r=\dfrac{\text{diameter}}{2}\) With diameter \(GU=\sqrt{130}\) the area is \(\pi\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{130}}{2}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{65\pi}{2}\approx102.1\).

Diameter of the circumcircle:

$$GU=\sqrt{(10-3)^2+(4+5)^2}=\sqrt{49+81}=\sqrt{130}$$

Area of the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$:

$$\pi\left(\frac{\sqrt{130}}{2}\right)^2=\pi\cdot\frac{130}{4}=\frac{65\pi}{2}\approx 102.10$$

Since $\dfrac{65\pi}{2} \approx 102.10 > 100$:

Yes — the area is greater than 100. ( ≈ 102.1 > 100 )

Full conclusion: $G$ lies on the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$ and $GU$ is a diameter, so $\text{Area}=\pi\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{130}}{2}\right)^2=\frac{65\pi}{2}\approx102.1>100$. Therefore the area of the circumcircle of $\triangle UVW$ is greater than 100.

Show the worked solution
