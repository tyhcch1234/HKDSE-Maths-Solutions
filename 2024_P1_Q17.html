Coordinate Geometry — perpendicular bisector, circle equation & tangents

Perpendicular Bisector Locus Circle Equation Tangency Circumcircle Angle in Semicircle

Question Full text

The coordinates of the points \(Q\) and \(R\) are \((10,-1)\) and \((-4,-9)\) respectively.

(a) Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(PQ=PR\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). 3 marks

  1. Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(QR\). 1M
  2. Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). 1M+1A

(b) Let \(C\) be the circle which passes through \(Q\), \(R\) and the point \((4,3)\). 5 marks

  1. Find the equation of \(C\). 1M+1M+1A
  2. The coordinates of the point \(U\) are \((10,4)\). It is found that \(U\) lies outside \(C\). \(UV\) and \(UW\) are the tangents to \(C\) at the points \(V\) and \(W\) respectively. Is the area of the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\) greater than \(100\)? Explain your answer. 1M+1A

Part (a)(i) Geometric relationship 1M

Midpoint \(M\) = (3, −5)
\(MQ\) = \(MR\) = 8.06
dist(\(P,\Gamma\)) = 0.00
1
Interpret the condition \(PQ=PR\)
1M

Since \(PQ=PR\), the moving point \(P\) is always equidistant from the two fixed points \(Q\) and \(R\). The set of such points is the perpendicular bisector of the segment \(QR\).

Hint: Any point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is equidistant from the two endpoints — and conversely.
\(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(QR\).

Part (a)(ii) Equation of \(\Gamma\) 1M+1A

\(P\) = (6, −10.25)
\(PQ\) = 8.42
\(PR\) = 8.42
\(PQ-PR\) = 0.00
1
Midpoint and slope of \(QR\)
1M

Midpoint of \(QR\):

\[ M=\left(\frac{10+(-4)}{2},\ \frac{-1+(-9)}{2}\right)=(3,-5) \]

Slope of \(QR\):

\[ m_{QR}=\frac{-9-(-1)}{-4-10}=\frac{-8}{-14}=\frac{4}{7} \]
Hint: The perpendicular bisector passes through \(M\) and its slope is the negative reciprocal of \(m_{QR}\).
2
Slope of \(\Gamma\) and point–slope form
1M

Since \(\Gamma\perp QR\),

\[ m_\Gamma=-\frac{1}{m_{QR}}=-\frac{7}{4} \]

Using \(M(3,-5)\):

\[ y-(-5)=-\frac{7}{4}(x-3) \]
Hint: Perpendicular slopes multiply to \(-1\): \(\frac{4}{7}\times\left(-\frac{7}{4}\right)=-1\).
3
Simplify to general form
1A
\[ 4(y+5)=-7(x-3) \] \[ 4y+20=-7x+21 \] \[ 7x+4y-1=0 \]
Equation of \(\Gamma\): \(\;7x+4y-1=0\)

Part (b)(i) Equation of circle \(C\) 1M+1M+1A

Centre \(G\) = (3, −5)
Radius \(r\) = 8.062
\(r^{2}\) = 65
1
Perpendicular bisector of \(RS\)
1M

Let \(S(4,3)\). Midpoint of \(RS\):

\[ \left(\frac{4+(-4)}{2},\ \frac{3+(-9)}{2}\right)=(0,-3) \]

Slope of \(RS\):

\[ m_{RS}=\frac{3-(-9)}{4-(-4)}=\frac{12}{8}=\frac{3}{2} \]

So the perpendicular bisector of \(RS\) has slope \(-\dfrac{2}{3}\):

\[ y-(-3)=-\frac{2}{3}(x-0)\ \Longrightarrow\ 2x+3y+9=0 \]
Hint: The centre of a circle through three points is the intersection of any two perpendicular bisectors.
2
Solve for the centre \(G\)
1M

Solve the two perpendicular bisector equations together:

\[ \begin{cases} 7x+4y-1=0 & \text{(from (a)(ii))}\\[2pt] 2x+3y+9=0 & \text{(perp. bisector of } RS) \end{cases} \]

From the second: \(y=\dfrac{-2x-9}{3}\). Substitute into the first:

\[ 7x+4\cdot\frac{-2x-9}{3}-1=0 \ \Longrightarrow\ 21x-8x-36-3=0 \ \Longrightarrow\ 13x=39 \] \[ x=3,\qquad y=\frac{-2(3)-9}{3}=-5 \]
Centre of \(C\): \(G(3,-5)\)
3
Radius and equation of \(C\)
1M+1A

Use the distance from \(G\) to \(S(4,3)\):

\[ r=GS=\sqrt{(4-3)^2+(3+5)^2}=\sqrt{1+64}=\sqrt{65} \] \[ r^2=65 \]

Therefore:

\[ (x-3)^2+(y+5)^2=65 \]
Hint: Using \(GQ\) or \(GR\) gives the same \(r^2\): \(GQ^2=(10-3)^2+(-1+5)^2=49+16=65\).
Equation of \(C\): \(\;(x-3)^2+(y+5)^2=65\)

Part (b)(ii) Circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\) 1M+1A

\(GU\) = 11.402
\(UV=UW\) = 7.071
Area = \(\pi\left(\frac{GU}{2}\right)^{2}\) = 102.10
> 100 ? Yes
1
Key geometry: where is \(G\)?
1M

Since \(UV\) and \(UW\) are tangents to \(C\) at \(V\) and \(W\), the radius is perpendicular to the tangent:

\[ GV \perp UV,\qquad GW \perp UW \]

So \(\angle G V U=\angle G W U=90^\circ\). Hence \(V\) and \(W\) both lie on the circle with diameter \(GU\) — and that circle is exactly the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\).

Hint: Angle in semicircle (converse of the angle in a semicircle theorem): if \(\angle GVU=90^\circ\), then \(GU\) is a diameter of the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\).
\(G\) lies on the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\), and \(GU\) is a diameter of it.
2
Compute \(GU\) and the area
1M

With \(G(3,-5)\) and \(U(10,4)\):

\[ GU=\sqrt{(10-3)^2+(4-(-5))^2}=\sqrt{49+81}=\sqrt{130} \]

The circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\) has radius \(\dfrac{GU}{2}\):

\[ \text{Area}=\pi\left(\frac{GU}{2}\right)^{2}=\pi\cdot\frac{130}{4}=\frac{65\pi}{2} \] \[ \frac{65\pi}{2}\approx 102.1017612 \]
Hint: \(\frac{65\pi}{2}>100 \iff 65\pi>200 \iff \pi>\frac{40}{13}\approx 3.077\), which is true.
Area \(\approx 102.1>100\). Yes, the area of the circumcircle of \(\triangle UVW\) is greater than \(100\).
