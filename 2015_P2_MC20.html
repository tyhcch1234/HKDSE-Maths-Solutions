Question Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(AD\) is a diameter of the circle \(ABCDE\). If \(\angle BAD = 58^\circ\) and \(BC = CD\), then \(\angle AEC =\)

A. \(32^\circ\)
B. \(58^\circ\)
C. \(61^\circ\)
D. \(64^\circ\)

(Tap an option to check — the full reasoning is revealed below.)

Original Figure

Part (a) — Using \(AD\) is a diameter

\(AD\) is a diameter, so any angle subtended by \(AD\) at the circumference is a right angle. In particular, in triangle \(ACD\): \[\angle ACD = 90^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ in semicircle})\]
\(\angle ACD = \) 90.0° arc \(AD\) subtends \(90^\circ\)
0 / 1
11M Angle in a semicircle
Since \(AD\) is a diameter, the angle subtended by \(AD\) at any point on the circumference is \(90^\circ\): \[\angle ACD = 90^\circ\]
Hint: Whenever you see "\(AD\) is a diameter", immediately mark a right angle at every other point on the circle. Here it gives \(\angle ABD = \angle AED = \angle ACD = 90^\circ\).
\(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\)

Part (b) — Use \(BC = CD\) to split \(\angle BAD\)

Equal chords subtend equal angles at the circumference, so \[\angle BAC = \angle CAD\] Since \(\angle BAC + \angle CAD = \angle BAD = 58^\circ\), \[\angle CAD = 58^\circ \div 2 = 29^\circ\]
\(\angle BAD = \) 58° \(\angle BAC = \) 29° \(\angle CAD = \) 29°
0 / 2
11M Equal chords ⇒ equal angles
Since \(BC = CD\), the chords are equal, hence the angles they subtend at the circumference are equal: \[\angle BAC = \angle CAD\]
Hint: "Equal chords, equal angles" — the two arcs cut by equal chords at \(A\) give equal inscribed angles.
21A Halve \(\angle BAD\)
Since \(\angle BAC + \angle CAD = \angle BAD = 58^\circ\) and the two parts are equal: \[\angle CAD = \frac{58^\circ}{2} = 29^\circ\]
Hint: Drag the slider in the diagram: the two angles stay equal no matter what \(\angle BAD\) is.
\(\angle CAD = 29^\circ\)

Part (c) — Find \(\angle ADC\) in \(\triangle ACD\)

In triangle \(ACD\): \[\angle ADC = 180^\circ - \angle ACD - \angle CAD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 29^\circ = 61^\circ\]
\(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\) \(\angle CAD = 29^\circ\) \(\angle ADC = \) 61.0°
0 / 1
11A Angle sum of a triangle
In \(\triangle ACD\), \[\angle ADC = 180^\circ - \angle ACD - \angle CAD = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 29^\circ = 61^\circ \quad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle)\]
Hint: Once you have two angles in a triangle, the third is just \(180^\circ\) minus their sum. No trigonometry needed!
\(\angle ADC = 61^\circ\)

Part (d) — Transfer to \(\angle AEC\)

Points \(A, E, D, C\) all lie on the same circle. \(\angle ADC\) and \(\angle AEC\) stand on the same chord \(AC\): \[\angle AEC = \angle ADC = 61^\circ \quad (\angle \text{s in the same segment})\] Therefore the answer is C.
\(\angle ADC = \) 61.0° \(\angle AEC = \) 61.0°
0 / 1
11A Angles in the same segment
\(\angle ADC\) and \(\angle AEC\) are both angles subtended by the chord \(AC\), with vertices \(D\) and \(E\) on the same arc. Hence \[\angle AEC = \angle ADC = 61^\circ \quad (\angle \text{s in the same segment})\]
Hint: The slider proves it: as \(E\) slides along the arc, \(\angle AEC\) stays fixed at \(61^\circ\). That's the magic of the constant-angle theorem.
\(\angle AEC = 61^\circ\)  →  answer C

Summary of Answers

  • \(\angle ACD = 90^\circ\)  — angle in a semicircle
  • \(\angle BAC = \angle CAD = 29^\circ\)  — equal chords \(BC = CD\)
  • \(\angle ADC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 29^\circ = 61^\circ\)  — angle sum of \(\triangle ACD\)
  • \(\angle AEC = \angle ADC = 61^\circ\)  — angles in the same segment
  • Final answer: C. \(61^\circ\)
