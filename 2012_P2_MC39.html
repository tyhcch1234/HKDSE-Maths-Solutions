Trigonometry — Graphs of trigonometric functions

QQuestion

The figure shows the graph of a trigonometric function \(y=f(x)\), defined for \(0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ\), with maximum value \(7\) (at \(x=0^\circ\) and \(x=180^\circ\)) and minimum value \(1\) (at \(x=90^\circ\)).

Which of the following could be \(f(x)\)?

  • A. the graph of \(y=1+3\cos\dfrac{x^\circ}{2}\)
  • B. the graph of \(y=1+3\cos 2x^\circ\)
  • C. the graph of \(y=4+3\cos\dfrac{x^\circ}{2}\)
  • D. the graph of \(y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ\)  Answer

Multiple-choice · 1 mark

1Part (a): Read off the amplitude and the centre line

Max \(= d+a\) = 7.0 Min \(= d-a\) = 1.0 Amplitude \(a = \dfrac{\text{max}-\text{min}}{2}\) = 3.0 Centre \(d = \dfrac{\text{max}+\text{min}}{2}\) = 4.0
Step 1 1M

From the figure, the curve attains its maximum value \(7\) at \(x=0^\circ\) and \(x=180^\circ\), and its minimum value \(1\) at \(x=90^\circ\).

Hint: A cosine curve \(y=d+a\cos(bx^\circ)\) oscillates halfway between its maximum \(d+a\) and its minimum \(d-a\). First find where the "middle line" sits.
Maximum \(=7\), Minimum \(=1\)
Step 2 1A

The vertical shift \(d\) (the centre line) is the average:

\[ d=\frac{7+1}{2}=4 \]

The amplitude \(a\) is half the total height:

\[ a=\frac{7-1}{2}=3 \]
Hint: Slide the two sliders in the diagram — only \(a=3,\;d=4\) makes the dashed max/min lines touch the curve exactly at \(7\) and \(1\).
\(a=3,\qquad d=4\)
0 / 2

2Part (b): Find the period, hence \(b\)

Period \(=\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}\) = 180.0° Min at \(x=\dfrac{180^\circ}{b}\) = 90.0° Half-period reached at \(x=90^\circ\) ✓ only when \(b=2\)
Step 1 1M

A standard cosine curve \(y=\cos x^\circ\) has a maximum at \(0^\circ\), a minimum at \(180^\circ\) and a maximum again at \(360^\circ\) — i.e. a full period of \(360^\circ\).

Hint: Compare the given figure with \(\cos\). Here the curve goes maximum → minimum within just \(90^\circ\), not \(180^\circ\). That means the wave is "squashed horizontally" by a factor.
The curve completes half a period from \(x=0^\circ\) to \(x=90^\circ\).
Step 2 1A

So half a period \(=90^\circ\), giving a full period of \(180^\circ\). For \(y=d+a\cos(bx^\circ)\), the period is \(\dfrac{360^\circ}{b}\):

\[ \frac{360^\circ}{b}=180^\circ \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=2 \]
Hint: Drag the \(b\) slider. Watch the readout — the minimum only lands exactly at \(x=90^\circ\) when \(b=2\).
\(b=2\)
0 / 2

3Part (c): Assemble the function and test the options

A: at \(x=0^\circ,\ y=\) 1 B: at \(x=0^\circ,\ y=\) 4 C: at \(x=0^\circ,\ y=\) 7 D: at \(x=0^\circ,\ y=\) 7
Step 1 1M

Putting \(a=3\), \(d=4\), \(b=2\) together, the curve is

\[ y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ \]
Hint: Start from \(y=\cos 2x^\circ\) (period \(180^\circ\), range \([-1,1]\)) → enlarge vertically ×3: \(y=3\cos 2x^\circ\), range \([-3,3]\) → translate up 4 units: range \([1,7]\). This is exactly the figure on the right of the question paper.
Model: \(y=4+3\cos 2x^\circ\)
Step 2 1A

Check each option at \(x=0^\circ\), where \(\cos 0^\circ=1\):

  • A: \(y=1+3(1)=4\ne 7\) ✗
  • B: \(y=1+3(1)=4\ne 7\) ✗
  • C: \(y=4+3(1)=7\) ✓ but at \(x=90^\circ\): \(y=4+3\cos 45^\circ\approx 6.12\ne 1\) ✗
  • D: \(y=4+3(1)=7\) ✓ and at \(x=90^\circ\): \(y=4+3\cos 180^\circ=4-3=1\) ✓
Hint: In the diagram tick Option C and compare it with the grey target points. C matches at \(x=0^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\) but fails in the middle (wrong period), while D matches everywhere.
Only D passes both tests, so the answer is D.
0 / 2

★Summary of Answers

  • Maximum \(=7\) at \(x=0^\circ,\,180^\circ\); Minimum \(=1\) at \(x=90^\circ\).
  • Centre line: \(d=\dfrac{7+1}{2}=4\).
  • Amplitude: \(a=\dfrac{7-1}{2}=3\).
  • Half-period \(=90^\circ\) ⟹ period \(=180^\circ\) ⟹ \(b=\dfrac{360^\circ}{180^\circ}=2\).
  • ∴ \(f(x)=4+3\cos 2x^\circ\).
  • ∴ The answer is D.
