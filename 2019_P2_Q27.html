[2019 MC #27]
Denote the circle  $2x^{2}+2y^{2}+4x-12y+15=0$  by $C$.
Which of the following is/are true?
I. The area of $C$ is $25\pi$.
II. The point $(-3,\,3)$ lies outside $C$.
III. The centre of $C$ lies in the fourth quadrant.
A. I only
B. II only
C. I and III only
D. II and III only
Step 1 · Normalise the equation

Divide the whole equation by $2$ so that the $x^2$ and $y^2$ coefficients become $1$:

$$2x^{2}+2y^{2}+4x-12y+15=0$$

$$x^{2}+y^{2}+2x-6y+\frac{15}{2}=0$$

Now the circle is in the form $x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0$ with $D=2,\;E=-6,\;F=\frac{15}{2}$.

Step 2 · Find the centre

For $x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0$, the centre is $\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},\,-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)$:

$$\text{Centre}=\left(-\frac{2}{2},\,-\frac{-6}{2}\right)=(-1,\,3)$$

The centre $(-1,3)$ has $x<0$ and $y>0$ → it lies in the second quadrant, NOT the fourth. So statement III is false.
Centre of a Circle from the General Form \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{centre}\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)\) With \(D=2\) and \(E=-6\) the centre is \((-1,\,3)\), which lies in the second quadrant, not the fourth.
Step 3 · Find the radius and area

Radius $r=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\dfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}$:

$$r=\sqrt{(1)^{2}+(-3)^{2}-\frac{15}{2}}=\sqrt{1+9-\frac{15}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{20-15}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$$

$$\text{Area}=\pi r^{2}=\pi\cdot\frac{5}{2}=\frac{5}{2}\pi$$

I is FALSE — the area is $\frac{5}{2}\pi$, not $25\pi$.
Radius and Area of a Circle \(r^{2}=\left(\dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\dfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F,\qquad A=\pi r^{2}\) Here \(r^{2}=1+9-\dfrac{15}{2}=\dfrac{5}{2}\), so the area is \(\dfrac{5}{2}\pi\), not \(25\pi\).
Step 4 · Test the point $(-3,\,3)$

Distance from the centre $(-1,3)$ to $P(-3,3)$:

$$d=\sqrt{(-1-(-3))^{2}+(3-3)^{2}}=\sqrt{4}=2$$

Compare with the radius:   $2 > \sqrt{\tfrac{5}{2}}\approx 1.58$.

So $d>r$ ⟹ the point lies outside $C$.

II is TRUE — $(-3,3)$ lies outside $C$.
Step 5 · Evaluate all three statements

I. Area $=\frac{5}{2}\pi$  →  FALSE

II. $(-3,3)$ lies outside $C$  →  TRUE

III. Centre $(-1,3)$ is in the second quadrant  →  FALSE

Only statement II is true.
ANSWER B. II only

Question. Denote the circle \(3x^{2}+3y^{2}-6x+12y+5=0\) by \(C\). Which of the following are true? I. The area of \(C\) is \(\dfrac{10}{3}\pi\). II. The centre of \(C\) lies in the fourth quadrant. III. The point \((-2,\,1)\) lies outside \(C\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Divide by \(3\): \(x^{2}+y^{2}-2x+4y+\dfrac{5}{3}=0\), so \(D=-2\), \(E=4\) and \(F=\dfrac{5}{3}\).
Step 2. Centre \(=\left(-\dfrac{D}{2},-\dfrac{E}{2}\right)=(1,\,-2)\), which lies in the fourth quadrant: II is true.
Step 3. \(r^{2}=\left(\dfrac{-2}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\dfrac{4}{2}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{5}{3}=1+4-\dfrac{5}{3}=\dfrac{10}{3}\), so the area is \(\pi r^{2}=\dfrac{10}{3}\pi\): I is true.
Step 4. Distance from the centre \((1,-2)\) to \((-2,1)\): \(d=\sqrt{9+9}=\sqrt{18}\approx4.24\), while \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{10}{3}}\approx1.83\). Since \(d>r\), the point lies outside \(C\): III is true.
Step 5. All three statements are true.
Answer: I, II and III
