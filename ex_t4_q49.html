Polynomials (89) · Sub-Topic: Identities

T4 Q49

Given that $(x+a)^2 = x^2+bx+16$, find the possible values of $a$ and $b$.

Solution

Expand the left-hand side: $(x+a)^2=x^2+2ax+a^2$.
Compare coefficients with $x^2+bx+16$: the coefficient of $x$ gives $b=2a$, and the constant term gives $a^2=16$.
Solve $a^2=16$: $a=4$ or $a=-4$.
If $a=4$, then $b=2(4)=8$. If $a=-4$, then $b=2(-4)=-8$.
The possible values are $(a,b)=(4,8)$ or $(a,b)=(-4,-8)$.
