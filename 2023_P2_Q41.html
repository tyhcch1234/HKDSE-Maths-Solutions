Coordinate Geometry — Straight Lines, Angles & the Incentre
Equations of Straight Lines Angle Bisector Incentre Locus Slope & Tangents Multiple Choice

QUESTION Full Question

Read carefully — only one option (A–D) is correct.

The equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \[3x - 4y + k = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad 4x + 3y - k = 0\] respectively, where \(k\) is a positive constant. It is given that \(L_1\) cuts the \(x\)-axis at the point \(P\). Denote the point of intersection of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) by \(Q\). Denote the origin by \(O\).

If \(R\) is a point lying on \(L_2\) such that the in-centre of \(\triangle PQR\) lies on the \(x\)-axis, then the \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\) is

  • A. \(-7k\)
  • B. \(-k\)
  • C. \(k\)
  • D. \(7k\)
    • >

PART (a) Find \(P\) and the slope of \(L_1\)

Locate where \(L_1\) meets the \(x\)-axis, and read off its slope.

\(x\)-intercept \(= -k/3\)

STEP 1

\(P\) lies on \(L_1\) and on the \(x\)-axis. Put \(y=0\) into the equation of \(L_1\):

\[3x - 4(0) + k = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x = -k \;\Rightarrow\; x = -\frac{k}{3}\]
Hint: An \(x\)-intercept always has \(y=0\). Just substitute and solve.
STEP 2

So the coordinates of \(P\) are:

\[P\left(-\frac{k}{3},\;0\right)\]
Answer: \(P\left(-\dfrac{k}{3},\,0\right)\), which lies on the negative \(x\)-axis since \(k>0\).
STEP 3

Rewrite \(L_1\) in slope-intercept form \(y = mx + c\):

\[3x - 4y + k = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 4y = 3x + k \;\Rightarrow\; y = \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{k}{4}\]

Hence the slope of \(L_1\) is

\[m_{L_1} = \frac{3}{4}\]
Hint: Check perpendicularity later: if \(L_2\) has slope \(-4/3\), then \(m_{L_1}\cdot m_{L_2}=-1\) — the two lines are perpendicular!
Answer: Slope of \(L_1 = \dfrac{3}{4}\).

PART (b) Why the \(x\)-axis bisects \(\angle PQR\)

Use the in-centre condition to impose a condition on the slope of \(PR\).
slope \(L_1 =\) 3/4
slope \(L_2 =\) -4/3
\(\angle QPR\) bisected by \(x\)-axis

0 / 3
STEP 1

An in-centre is the point where the internal angle bisectors of a triangle meet. If the in-centre lies on the \(x\)-axis, then the \(x\)-axis is one of these bisectors.

Since \(P\) lies on the \(x\)-axis, the \(x\)-axis is the bisector of the angle at \(P\), i.e. it bisects \(\angle QPR\).

Hint: A bisector through the vertex \(P\) separates the two sides \(PQ\) and \(PR\) into equal angles.
STEP 2

Let \(\alpha\) be the angle that \(PQ\) makes with the positive \(x\)-direction and \(\beta\) the angle for \(PR\). Reflection across the \(x\)-axis swaps the sign of the angle, so:

\[\beta = -\alpha\]

Taking tangent of both sides (tangent is an odd function):

\[\tan\beta = -\tan\alpha\]

But \(\tan\alpha\) is just the slope of \(L_1\) (with sign), and \(\tan\beta\) is the slope of \(PR\). Hence

\[m_{PR} = -m_{L_1} = -\frac{3}{4}\]
Hint: Reflection in the \(x\)-axis sends a line of slope \(m\) to a line of slope \(-m\). Since the \(x\)-axis is the bisector, the slopes of \(PQ\) and \(PR\) are opposites.
STEP 3

Therefore the slope of the line \(PR\) is

\[m_{PR} = -\frac{3}{4}\]
Answer: The slope of \(PR\) is \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\).

PART (c) Find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\)

Intersect the new line \(PR\) with \(L_2\) to obtain \(R\).
\(R = \) ( , )
\(x\)-coordinate of \(R =\) k
Correct option: C (\(x_R = k\))

STEP 1

We have the slope of \(PR\) and a point on it, namely \(P\left(-\frac{k}{3},0\right)\). By point–slope form:

\[y - 0 = -\frac{3}{4}\left[x - \left(-\frac{k}{3}\right)\right]\]

Multiplying through by 4:

\[4y = -3\left(x + \frac{k}{3}\right) = -3x - k\]
\[3x + 4y + k = 0\]
Hint: Notice that the line \(PR\) has the same coefficients as \(L_1\) but with \(3\) and \(4\) swapped — a hallmark of a reflected line.
STEP 2

\(R\) lies on \(L_2\), so its coordinates satisfy \(4x + 3y - k = 0\). Solve the two linear equations simultaneously:

\[\begin{cases}4x + 3y = k & (L_2)\\[4pt] 3x + 4y = -k & (PR)\end{cases}\]

Subtracting the second from the first gives \(x - y = 2k\). Adding the two gives \(7x + 7y = 0\), i.e. \(x + y = 0\).

\[x + y = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -x\]

Substitute \(y=-x\) into \(4x + 3y = k\):

\[4x - 3x = k \;\Rightarrow\; x = k\]
Hint: The two equations are symmetric in \(x\) and \(y\), forcing \(x + y = 0\) — a quick way to eliminate.
STEP 3

Thus \(R\) has coordinates

\[R\,(k,\;-k)\]

The \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\) is therefore \(k\). This matches option D.

Answer: \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\) is \(\boxed{k}\) — option D.

🏆 Summary of Answers

  • \(P\) ( \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\) ): \(P\left(-\dfrac{k}{3},\,0\right)\)
  • Slope of \(L_1\): \(m_{L_1} = \dfrac{3}{4} \quad\) (and slope of \(L_2 = -\dfrac{4}{3}\))
  • Angle bisector insight: In-centre on the \(x\)-axis \(\Rightarrow\) \(x\)-axis bisects \(\angle QPR\) \(\Rightarrow m_{PR} = -\dfrac{3}{4}\)
  • Line \(PR\): \(3x + 4y + k = 0\)
  • Intersection with \(L_2\) (\(L_2: 4x + 3y - k = 0\)): \(R\,(k,\,-k)\)
  • Final answer: the \(x\)-coordinate of \(R\) is \(k\) — Option D
Correct Choice: C  ·  \(x_R = k\)
