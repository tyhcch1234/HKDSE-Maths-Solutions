Question

It is given that \(ABCDEF\) is a regular hexagon. If \(AC\) and \(BF\) intersect at point \(G\), which of the following are true?
I. \(BG // CE\)
II. \(\triangle ABG \sim \triangle BDC\)
III. \(\triangle AGF \cong \triangle BGC\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
I ✓ II ✓ III ✓
Step 1 · Statement I — BG // CE
Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon \(\dfrac{(n-2)\times 180^{\circ}}{n}=120^{\circ}\) Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is \(120^{\circ}\), so \(AB=AF\) gives \(\angle ABF=30^{\circ}\).

In \(\triangle ABF\), \(AB=AF\) and \(\angle BAF=120^\circ\) (interior angle of a regular hexagon),
so \(\angle ABF=30^\circ\). Hence

\[ \angle FBC=\angle ABC-\angle ABF=120^\circ-30^\circ=90^\circ . \]

Similarly, in \(\triangle CDE\), \(CD=DE\) and \(\angle CDE=120^\circ\),
so \(\angle DCE=30^\circ\) and \[ \angle BCE=120^\circ-30^\circ=90^\circ . \] Both \(BF\) (hence \(BG\)) and \(CE\) are perpendicular to \(BC\),
so \(BG // CE\).

Statement I is true.

Step 2 · Statement II — △ABG ∼ △BDC
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle \(AB=BC\Rightarrow\angle BAC=\angle BCA=30^{\circ}\) With \(\angle ABC=120^{\circ}\) the base angles are \(30^{\circ}\), so \(\triangle ABG\) has angles \(30^{\circ},30^{\circ},120^{\circ}\).

\(\angle ABG=\angle ABF=30^\circ\). In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB=BC\) and \(\angle ABC=120^\circ\), so \(\angle BAC=30^\circ\);
since \(G\) lies on \(AC\), \(\angle BAG=30^\circ\). Thus \(\triangle ABG\) has angles \(30^\circ,30^\circ,120^\circ\).

In \(\triangle BDC\), \(BC=CD\) and \(\angle BCD=120^\circ\),
so it also has angles \(30^\circ,30^\circ,120^\circ\).

Both triangles are isosceles with the same set of angles,
hence they are similar.

Statement II is true.

Step 3 · Statement III — △AGF ≅ △BGC

Let the side length be \(s\). Then \(AC=BF=\sqrt{3}\,s\) (short diagonals),
and from \(\triangle ABG\)

\[ AG=BG=\frac{s}{\sqrt{3}} . \]

So

\[ GF=BF-BG=\sqrt{3}\,s-\frac{s}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{2s}{\sqrt{3}}, \qquad GC=AC-AG=\sqrt{3}\,s-\frac{s}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{2s}{\sqrt{3}} . \]
Step 4 · Conclusion (SSS)
S.S.S. Congruence \(AG=BG,\qquad GF=GC,\qquad AF=BC\) Both triangles have sides \(\left\{\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}},\dfrac{2s}{\sqrt{3}},s\right\}\), so they are congruent.

Sides of \(\triangle AGF\): \(\left\{AG=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}},\; GF=\dfrac{2s}{\sqrt{3}},\; AF=s\right\}\).

Sides of \(\triangle BGC\): \(\left\{BG=\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}},\; GC=\dfrac{2s}{\sqrt{3}},\; BC=s\right\}\).

All three pairs of sides are equal,
so the triangles are congruent (SSS).

Statement III is true.

Since I, II and III are all true → Final Answer: D
Tip: in a regular hexagon the interior angle is \(120^\circ\) and the short diagonal (skipping one vertex) has length \(\sqrt{3}\,s\). Scroll the steps to change the highlighted parts of the diagram.

Question. It is given that \(ABCDEF\) is a regular hexagon. Which of the following are true? I. \(AC=\sqrt{3}\,AB\) II. \(\angle ABC=120^{\circ}\) III. \(AD\perp BE\)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is \(\dfrac{(6-2)\times 180^{\circ}}{6}=120^{\circ}\), so statement II is true.
Step 2. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB=BC=s\) and \(\angle ABC=120^{\circ}\), so by the cosine formula \(AC^{2}=s^{2}+s^{2}-2s^{2}\cos 120^{\circ}=3s^{2}\) and \(AC=\sqrt{3}\,s\): statement I is true.
Step 3. Take \(s=1\) on a unit circle: \(A=(1,0)\), \(B=\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\), \(D=(-1,0)\), \(E=\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\).
Step 4. \(AD\) is horizontal (slope \(0\)) while \(BE\) has slope \(\sqrt{3}\),
so \(AD\) is not perpendicular to \(BE\): statement III is false.
Step 5. Hence I and II only are true.
Answer: I and II only
