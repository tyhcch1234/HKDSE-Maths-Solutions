HKDSE Math 2017 Paper 2 Q1

Factorization by Grouping

Topic 4 · Polynomials
Factorization Grouping Terms Common Factor Quadratic in \(m, n\)

QQuestion

Factorize: \[ 3m^2 - 5mn + 2n^2 + m - n \]
A \((m-n)(3m-2n+1)\)
B \((m-n)(3m+2n+1)\)
C \((m+n)(3m-2n-1)\)
D \((m+n)(3m+2n-1)\)

💡Step-by-Step Solution

Tap each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to predict the next line before opening it!

1 Spot the structure — group the terms ▾

The expression has 5 terms. Notice the "quadratic part" \(3m^2 - 5mn + 2n^2\) and the "linear part" \(+m - n\).

💡 Hint: When a polynomial has more than 3 terms, try grouping terms that share a common factor.

Let's group the first two and the last two terms to find common factors:

\[ \underbrace{3m^2 - 5mn}_{\text{group 1}} + \underbrace{2n^2 + m - n}_{\text{group 2}} \]
2 Factor the quadratic part ▾

Consider \(3m^2 - 5mn + 2n^2\). Treat \(n\) as a constant and factor the quadratic in \(m\).

💡 Hint: Find two numbers that multiply to \(3 \times 2 = 6\) and add to \(-5\). Those are \(-2\) and \(-3\).
\[ 3m^2 - 5mn + 2n^2 = 3m^2 - 3mn - 2mn + 2n^2 \] \[ = 3m(m-n) - 2n(m-n) \] \[ = (m-n)(3m-2n) \]
3 Rewrite the whole expression ▾

Now substitute the factorized quadratic back:

\[ 3m^2 - 5mn + 2n^2 + m - n = (m-n)(3m-2n) + (m-n) \]

Notice that \((m-n)\) is now a common factor in both terms!

4 Factor out the common factor \((m-n)\) ▾

Factor \((m-n)\) out of both terms:

\[ (m-n)(3m-2n) + (m-n) \] \[ = (m-n)\big[(3m-2n) + 1\big] \]
💡 Hint: The second term is \((m-n) \times 1\), so the "1" must remain inside the bracket.
5 Simplify the final answer ▾

Simplify inside the bracket:

\[ (m-n)(3m - 2n + 1) \]

This matches Option A. ✅
