Question 3 — Rounding & Approximation

Round up, round off, round down — three different instructions, three different answers.

Estimation & Approximation Significant Figures Rounding 3 marks

QThe Question

  1. (a)Round up \(123.45\) to 1 significant figure.
  2. (b)Round off \(123.45\) to the nearest integer.
  3. (c)Round down \(123.45\) to 1 decimal place.
Total: 3 marks (1 mark each)

★Three Instructions, Three Rules

Round UP ↑
Take the next larger value that fits the required accuracy. It never goes down.
Round OFF ≈
Look at the next digit: \(0\text{–}4\) down, \(5\text{–}9\) up. The usual rounding.
Round DOWN ↓
Take the next smaller value — cut off (truncate) the extra digits.

🔑 Key skill: first decide which digit the answer should stop at, then apply the correct direction rule.

➜Step-by-Step Solution

0 / 6 steps revealed

Write the number and label each digit position. Starting from the left, the first non-zero digit is the first significant figure.

\[ \underbrace{1}_{\substack{\text{1st s.f.}}}\;\underbrace{2}_{\text{2nd s.f.}}\;\underbrace{3}_{\text{3rd s.f.}}\;.\;\underbrace{4}_{\text{1 d.p.}}\;\underbrace{5}_{\text{2 d.p.}} \]
Hint — place values: the digit right after the decimal point is the tenths place (1 d.p.); the next one is the hundredths place (2 d.p.).

We keep only the first significant figure, which is the digit \(1\) in the hundreds place. Everything to the right of it will be replaced by zeros.

\[ 1\,\underline{23.45} \;\longrightarrow\; 1\,\text{_}\,\text{_}\,\text{_}\,\text{_}\,\text{_} \]
Hint — what does “1 s.f.” mean? Only one digit (the leading digit) carries information. The place value of the answer is the hundreds, so the answer must be a multiple of \(100\).

“Round up” means: go to the next larger multiple of \(100\).

  • Lower multiple of \(100\): \(\;1\underline{00}\)
  • Upper multiple of \(100\): \(\;2\underline{00}\)
  • Since \(123.45\) lies between them, rounding up gives the upper one.
\[ 123.45 \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{round up to }1\text{ s.f.}\;}\; 200 \]

Answer: (a) 2001A

Note: \(100\) would be rounding down — that would be wrong here.

The integer part is \(123\). The number lies between the two neighbouring integers \(123\) and \(124\).

Round off means the standard rule — look at the first digit after the decimal point:

\[ 123.\underline{4}5 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{first discarded digit is }4 \]
Hint — rounding rule: if the next digit is \(0,1,2,3,4\) → keep the integer part unchanged. If it is \(5,6,7,8,9\) → add \(1\) (round up).

Since \(4 < 5\), we keep \(123\).

Answer: (b) 1231A

1 decimal place means we keep one digit after the decimal point:

\[ 123.\underbrace{4}_{\text{kept}}\,\underline{5}\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad 123.4 \]

“Round down” means we simply discard the remaining digit \(5\) — we do not look at it to decide.

Hint — why is this not \(123.5\)? Because the instruction is “round down” (truncate), not “round off”. Under the normal rule, \(123.45\) would round off to \(123.5\) — but here the instruction changes the answer.

Answer: (c) 123.41A

\[ \text{(a) } 200 \qquad \text{(b) } 123 \qquad \text{(c) } 123.4 \]
  • ⚠️ Writing \(100\) in (a) — that is rounding down.
  • ⚠️ Writing \(124\) in (b) — that is rounding to the wrong side; the next digit \(4\) is less than \(5\).
  • ⚠️ Writing \(123.5\) in (c) — that is rounding off, but the question says round down.

Each part is worth 1A — the accuracy mark for the correct rounded value.
