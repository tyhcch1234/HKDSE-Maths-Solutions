Question

[2014 #3]

(a) Round up $123.45$ to $1$ significant figure.

(b) Round off $123.45$ to the nearest integer.

(c) Round down $123.45$ to $1$ decimal place.

[3 marks]

STEP 1 · Locate the number

Place $123.45$ on the number line. The digits are:

  • Hundreds: 1   ·   Tens: 2   ·   Units: 3
  • Tenths: 4   ·   Hundredths: 5

So $123.45$ sits between $120$ and $130$, and also just under $123.5$.

The key idea: “round up / round off / round down” mean three different things — check the wording carefully.
STEP 2 (a) · Round up to 1 significant figure

“Round up” means: always take the next higher value — never look at the digit after.

For $1$ significant figure we keep only the leading digit, which is $1$ (in the hundreds place).

Rounding up forces the hundreds digit from $1$ to $2$, giving $2 \times 10^2$:

$$123.45 \;\longrightarrow\; 200$$

If we had used normal “round off”, $123.45$ would give $100$; but “round up” gives $200$. Do not confuse the two!
STEP 3 (b) · Round off to the nearest integer

Now we round off (normal rounding): look at the digit immediately after the units place, i.e. the tenths digit.

  • Units digit: $3$ (the digit we keep)
  • Next digit (tenths): $4$

Since $4 < 5$, we round down — the units digit stays $3$ and the decimal part is dropped:

$$123.45 \;\longrightarrow\; 123$$

Rule of thumb: next digit $0$–$4$ → keep; next digit $5$–$9$ → increase by $1$.
STEP 4 (c) · Round down to 1 decimal place

“Round down” means: always take the next lower value — truncate, never increase.

To $1$ decimal place we keep the tenths digit, which is $4$, and discard the hundredths digit $5$:

$$123.45 \;\longrightarrow\; 123.4$$

Normal rounding of $123.45$ to 1 d.p. would also give $123.4$ here, but “round down” guarantees we never round up — even if the discarded digit were $9$.
Answer:
(a) $200$  ·  (b) $123$  ·  (c) $123.4$
