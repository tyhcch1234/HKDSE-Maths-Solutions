Coordinate Geometry — Conditions for two straight lines not to intersect

Coordinate Geometry Slope-Intercept Form Parallel Lines Simultaneous Equations

Question Find the constant \(k\)

Find the constant \(k\) such that the straight lines \[6x-8y=7k \quad\text{and}\quad kx+12y=5\] do not intersect with each other. Multiple Choice

A.\(-16\)
B.\(-9\)
C.\(9\)
D.\(16\)

Part (a) Rewrite both lines in slope-intercept form

To compare two straight lines, we first make \(y\) the subject of each equation.

Diagram 1 · The family of lines \(y=\dfrac{3}{4}x-\dfrac{7k}{8}\) as \(k\) varies
4.0
slope of L₁ = 0.75 y-intercept of L₁ = -3.50 Lines intersect (not parallel)
1 Make \(y\) the subject of \(6x-8y=7k\)

Rearrange the first equation:

\[6x-8y=7k \;\Rightarrow\; 8y=6x-7k \;\Rightarrow\; y=\frac{3}{4}x-\frac{7k}{8}\]
Hint: Divide every term by \(8\). The coefficient of \(x\) is the slope, and the constant term is the \(y\)-intercept.
\(y=\dfrac{3}{4}x-\dfrac{7k}{8}\) — slope \(m_1=\dfrac{3}{4}\), \(y\)-intercept \(c_1=-\dfrac{7k}{8}\).
2 Make \(y\) the subject of \(kx+12y=5\)

Rearrange the second equation:

\[kx+12y=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 12y=-kx+5 \;\Rightarrow\; y=-\frac{k}{12}x+\frac{5}{12}\]
Hint: Divide every term by \(12\). Keep the \(k\) in the coefficient — it is the unknown we are solving for.
\(y=-\dfrac{k}{12}x+\dfrac{5}{12}\) — slope \(m_2=-\dfrac{k}{12}\), \(y\)-intercept \(c_2=\dfrac{5}{12}\).

Part (b) Solve for \(k\) and check the \(y\)-intercepts

1 Condition for not intersecting: equal slopes

If two straight lines are parallel and the \(y\)-intercepts are not equal, then they do not intersect with each other. Equate the slopes:

\[-\frac{k}{12}=\frac{3}{4}\]
Hint: Parallel lines have the same slope. Solve this small linear equation for \(k\).
\(-\dfrac{k}{12}=\dfrac{3}{4}\) — this is the key equation.
2 Solve for \(k\)

Multiply both sides by \(12\):

\[-k = 12 \times \frac{3}{4} = 9 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{k=-9}\]
Hint: Be careful with the negative sign — multiplying by 12 gives \(-k=9\), so \(k=-9\). This matches option B.
\(k = -9\)
3 Check the \(y\)-intercepts are not equal

When \(k=-9\):

\[c_1=-\frac{7(-9)}{8}=\frac{63}{8},\qquad c_2=\frac{5}{12}\] \[\frac{63}{8}\ne \frac{5}{12} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{the two parallel lines are distinct, so they do not intersect.}\]
Hint: If the \(y\)-intercepts had been equal, the lines would coincide (infinitely many intersections), not "not intersect". The check confirms \(-9\) is valid.
\(\dfrac{63}{8} \ne \dfrac{5}{12}\) ✓ — the lines are parallel and distinct.

Summary of Answers

  • Line 1 in slope-intercept form: \(y=\dfrac{3}{4}x-\dfrac{7k}{8}\)  (slope \(\tfrac{3}{4}\))
  • Line 2 in slope-intercept form: \(y=-\dfrac{k}{12}x+\dfrac{5}{12}\)  (slope \(-\tfrac{k}{12}\))
  • No intersection requires equal slopes: \(-\dfrac{k}{12}=\dfrac{3}{4}\)
  • \(k=-9\)  — the correct choice is B
  • Check: \(y\)-intercepts \(\dfrac{63}{8}\) and \(\dfrac{5}{12}\) are unequal, so the lines are parallel and distinct ✓
