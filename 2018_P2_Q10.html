QUESTION

Let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be non-zero numbers. If $3a=4b$ and $a:c=2:5$, then $\dfrac{a+3b}{b+3c}=$

  • A. $\dfrac{5}{3}$  .
  • B. $\dfrac{13}{33}$  .
  • C. $\dfrac{30}{53}$  .
  • D. $\dfrac{75}{38}$  .
1Translate the first condition into a ratio
Converting an Equation to a Ratio\(ka=lb\ \Rightarrow\ a:b=l:k\)From \(3a=4b\) the coefficients cross over, so \(a:b=4:3\).

From $3a=4b$, divide both sides by $12b$  ($b\neq0$):

$$\frac{3a}{12b}=\frac{4b}{12b}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{a}{b}=\frac{4}{3}\quad\text{so}\quad a:b=4:3.$$

Hint: an equation $3a=4b$ becomes a ratio $a:b=4:3$ — cross the coefficients.
2Write down the second ratio

Given directly in the question:

$$a:c=2:5.$$

Hint: $a$ appears in both ratios — it is the "bridge" linking $b$ and $c$.
3Combine the two ratios into $a:b:c$
Combining Two Ratios\(a:b=4:3,\quad a:c=2:5=4:10\)Scaling \(2:5\) by \(2\) makes the \(a\)-share \(4\) in both ratios, so \(a:b:c=4:3:10\).

The value of $a$ must be the same in both.  LCM of $4$ and $2$ is $4$:

$$a:b=4:3 \qquad a:c=2:5=4:10$$

So combining (the parts of $a$ now match):

$$a:b:c=4:3:10.$$

Hint: scale the second ratio by $\times2$ so that both ratios show the same $a$-share ($=4$).
4Substitute the values into the expression
Ratio as a Common Multiplier\(a=4k,\ b=3k,\quad c=10k\)Every part carries the same factor \(k\), which cancels in \(\dfrac{a+3b}{b+3c}\).

Take $a=4k,\ b=3k,\ c=10k$ for some $k\neq0$ (the $k$ cancels):

$$\frac{a+3b}{b+3c}=\frac{4k+3(3k)}{3k+3(10k)}$$

Hint: use a common multiplier $k$ so that every variable has the same "unit".
5Simplify the fraction

Numerator: $\;4k+9k=13k$    Denominator: $\;3k+30k=33k$

$$\frac{a+3b}{b+3c}=\frac{13k}{33k}=\frac{13}{33}.$$

Hint: the factor $k$ cancels completely — the answer depends only on the ratio.
Answer:  $\dfrac{13}{33}$   —   Option B Check: $a:b:c=4:3:10$, so $\frac{a+3b}{b+3c}=\frac{4+9}{3+30}=\frac{13}{33}$.

Question. If \(2p=5q\) and \(p:r=3:4\), find \(\dfrac{p+2q}{q+2r}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. From \(2p=5q\): \(p:q=5:2\).
Step 2. Take \(p=15k\);
then \(q=\dfrac{2}{5}p=6k\) and \(r=\dfrac{4}{3}p=20k\).
Step 3. Substitute: \(\dfrac{15k+2(6k)}{6k+2(20k)}=\dfrac{15k+12k}{6k+40k}=\dfrac{27k}{46k}\).
Step 4. The factor \(k\) cancels, giving \(\dfrac{27}{46}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{27}{46}\)
