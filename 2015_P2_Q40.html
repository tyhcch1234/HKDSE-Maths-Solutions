Question

2015 MC #40
In the figure, \(AB\) and \(AC\) are tangents to the circle at \(B\) and \(C\) respectively. \(BD\) is a diameter of the circle. \(AC\) produced and \(BD\) produced meet at \(E\). If \(AB = 6\text{ cm}\) and \(AE = 10\text{ cm}\), then \(BD =\)
A. \(3\text{ cm}\) B. \(5\text{ cm}\)
C. \(6\text{ cm}\) D. \(8\text{ cm}\)

Step-by-step solution

STEP 1 · Equal tangents and the extended tangent
Equal Tangents from an External Point \(AB=AC\) Both tangents are drawn from \(A\), so \(AC=AB=6\) and hence \(CE=AE-AC=10-6=4\).

Tangents from the same external point are equal, so \(AC=AB=6\text{ cm}\). Since \(A,\,C,\,E\) are collinear and \(AE=10\text{ cm}\):

\[ CE=AE-AC=10-6=4\text{ cm}. \]

Also \(OC\perp AC\),
because \(OC\) is a radius and \(AC\) is the tangent at \(C\).

STEP 2 · A right angle at \(B\)
Tangent-Radius Property \(OB\perp AB,\qquad OC\perp AC\) Each radius is perpendicular to its tangent, so \(\angle ABE=90^\circ\) and \(BE=\sqrt{100-36}=8\).

\(BD\) is a diameter, so the radius \(OB\) is perpendicular to the tangent \(AB\) at \(B\). Since \(E\) lies on \(BD\) produced, \(B,\,O,\,D,\,E\) are collinear,
so \(BE\perp AB\) and

\[ \angle ABE=90^\circ . \]

In right triangle \(ABE\):

\[ BE=\sqrt{AE^{2}-AB^{2}}=\sqrt{10^{2}-6^{2}}=\sqrt{64}=8\text{ cm}. \]

STEP 3 · Put the centre on \(BE\)

Let the radius be \(r\),
so \(OB=OD=r\) and \(BD=2r\). The points lie in the order \(B,\,O,\,D,\,E\), hence

\[ OE=BE-OB=8-r. \]

Triangle \(OCE\) is right-angled at \(C\) (radius \(OC\perp\) tangent \(AC\),
and \(A,\,C,\,E\) are collinear), with \(OC=r\) and \(CE=4\).

STEP 4 · Solve for \(r\)
Square of a Difference \((a-b)^{2}=a^{2}-2ab+b^{2}\) \((8-r)^{2}=64-16r+r^{2}\), so the \(r^{2}\) terms cancel and \(16r=48\).

Pythagoras in triangle \(OCE\):

\[ OC^{2}+CE^{2}=OE^{2} \]

\[ r^{2}+4^{2}=(8-r)^{2} \]

\[ r^{2}+16=64-16r+r^{2} \]

\[ 16r=48\ \Rightarrow\ r=3. \]

STEP 5 · The diameter
Tangent-Secant Theorem \(EC^{2}=ED\cdot EB\) The tangent \(EC=4\) and the secant \(ED\cdot EB=2\times8=16\) agree, confirming \(r=3\).

\[ BD=2r=2(3)=6\text{ cm}. \]

Check with the power of \(E\): \(ED=OE-OD=5-3=2\) and \(EB=8\),
so \(ED\cdot EB=16=EC^{2}=4^{2}\).

Final Answer
C  ·  \(BD = 6\) cm

Key facts used: equal tangents from \(A\) (\(AC=AB=6\)), radius \(\perp\) tangent at the point of contact, and Pythagoras in the right triangles \(ABE\) and \(OCE\).

Question. \(AB\) and \(AC\) are tangents to a circle of radius \(12\text{ cm}\) at \(B\) and \(C\) respectively. If \(AB=5\text{ cm}\), find (a) \(OA\),
(b) the length of the chord \(BC\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(OB\perp AB\) (tangent-radius), so \(\triangle OAB\) is right-angled at \(B\): \(OA=\sqrt{5^{2}+12^{2}}=13\text{ cm}\).
Step 2. Equal tangents from \(A\) give \(AC=AB=5\text{ cm}\), and \(OA\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(BC\).
Step 3. Let \(M\) be the mid-point of \(BC\). Area of \(\triangle OAB\): \(\tfrac12(5)(12)=\tfrac12(13)(BM)\).
Step 4. \(BM=\dfrac{60}{13}\), so \(BC=2\,BM=\dfrac{120}{13}\approx9.23\text{ cm}\).
Answer: (a) \(OA=13\text{ cm}\) (b) \(BC=\dfrac{120}{13}\text{ cm}\approx9.23\text{ cm}\)
