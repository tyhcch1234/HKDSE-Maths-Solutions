Geometry · Tangent–Chord, Angles in the Alternate Segment & Angles in the Same Segment

Tangent at a Point ∠ in Alternate Segment ∠s in Same Segment ∠ Sum of △ Cyclic Quadrilateral

QQuestion Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(TA\) is the tangent to the circle \(ABCDE\) at the point \(A\). If \(\angle BAD = 64^\circ\), \(\angle EAT = 38^\circ\) and \(\angle DCE = 22^\circ\), then \(\angle ADB =\)

A. \(52^\circ\)     B. \(56^\circ\)     C. \(60^\circ\)     D. \(68^\circ\)

∠EAT = 38° ∠DCE = 22° ∠BAD = 64° ∠ABE = 38° ∠DBE = 22° ∠ADB = 56°

The diagram above is drawn to scale. Use the checkboxes to reveal the key angles that build up the solution.

▶Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1

Use the tangent–chord angle (angle in the alternate segment).

The angle between the tangent \(TA\) and the chord \(AB\) equals the angle in the alternate segment subtended by \(AB\), i.e. \(\angle ABE\).

\[ \angle ABE = \angle EAT = 38^\circ \]
Hint: “Angle in the alternate segment” — the tangent at \(A\) is \(AT\), and the chord is \(AB\). The angle between the tangent and the chord equals the angle in the opposite arc.
\(\angle ABE = 38^\circ\)
Step 2

Use angles in the same segment.

\(\angle DCE\) and \(\angle DBE\) both stand on the same chord \(DE\), so they are equal.

\[ \angle DBE = \angle DCE = 22^\circ \]
Hint: Angles at the circumference subtending the same arc (here arc \(DE\)) are equal. So \(\angle DBE = \angle DCE\).
\(\angle DBE = 22^\circ\)
Step 3

Find \(\angle ABD\) by adding the two parts at \(B\).

From the figure, the ray \(BE\) lies between \(BA\) and \(BD\), so

\[ \angle ABD = \angle ABE + \angle DBE = 38^\circ + 22^\circ = 60^\circ \]
Hint: At vertex \(B\), the angle \(\angle ABD\) is split by the chord \(BE\) into \(\angle ABE\) and \(\angle DBE\).
\(\angle ABD = 60^\circ\)
Step 4

Apply the angle sum of a triangle in \(\triangle ABD\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \angle ADB &= 180^\circ - \angle BAD - \angle ABD \\ &= 180^\circ - 64^\circ - 60^\circ \\ &= 56^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Hint: The three interior angles of \(\triangle ABD\) add up to \(180^\circ\). Substitute the known values and solve for \(\angle ADB\).
\(\angle ADB = 56^\circ\) — so the answer is B.

  • \(\angle ADB = 180^\circ - 64^\circ - 60^\circ = 56^\circ\)   (∠ sum of △)
  • Answer: B · 56°
