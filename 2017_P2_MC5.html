Solving Compound Inequalities

Topic 9 · Inequalities & Linear Programming

Inequalities Linear Inequalities Number Line Union of sets

Question

The solution of   6 - x < 2x - 3   or   7 - 3x > 1   is

A \(x > 3\)
B \(x < 2\)
C \(2 < x < 3\)
D \(x < 2\) or \(x > 3\)

Step-by-step solution

1

Solve the first inequality \(6 - x < 2x - 3\)

Bring all \(x\) terms to one side and constant terms to the other.

\(6 - x < 2x - 3\)

\(6 + 3 < 2x + x\)

\(9 < 3x\)

\(3 < x \quad \Rightarrow \quad x > 3\)

💡 Hint: We move \(-x\) to the right and \(-3\) to the left. Remember: when you move a term across the inequality, its sign changes. No multiplication/division by a negative number yet, so the inequality direction is unchanged.
2

Solve the second inequality \(7 - 3x > 1\)

Isolate the \(x\) term, then divide. Watch the inequality sign!

\(7 - 3x > 1\)

\(-3x > 1 - 7\)

\(-3x > -6\)

Divide by \(-3\) (flip the inequality):

\(x < 2\)

💡 Hint: Dividing or multiplying both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. \(-6 \div -3 = 2\) and \(>\) becomes \(<\).
3

Combine using “or”

The word “or” means the solution is the union of the two individual solution sets.

First inequality: \(x > 3\)

Second inequality: \(x < 2\)

Union: \(x < 2\)  or  \(x > 3\)

📌 Key idea: “or” keeps both regions. If it were “and”, we would take the overlap (intersection), which would be empty here.
