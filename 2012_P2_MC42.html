QUESTION 2012 Paper 2 · Q42 Multiple Choice

Find the range of values of \(k\) such that the circle \[x^2+y^2+2x-4y-13=0\] and the straight line \[x-y+k=0\] intersect at two distinct points.

A. \(-9<k<3\)
B. \(-3<k<9\)
C. \(k<-9\) or \(k>3\)
D. \(k<-3\) or \(k>9\)

STEP A Rewrite the Line as \(y=x+k\)

Line parameter \(k\) = 0.0
Shade valid region \(-3<k<9\)
Show tangent cases
Step 1Setting up
Make \(y\) the subject of the line: \[x-y+k=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = x + k\]
Hint: Substituting one variable into the circle turns the geometry problem into a pure algebra problem in one variable — this is the key trick for circle–line intersection questions.
Step 2Geometry idea
The line \(y=x+k\) has slope \(1\) for every value of \(k\). Changing \(k\) slides the line up/down the \(y\)-axis. A vertical shift means the distance from the circle's centre to the line changes, so the number of intersection points changes.
Hint: A family of parallel lines. Only its vertical position matters, so one single parameter \(k\) controls everything.
0 / 2

STEP B Substitute and Form the Quadratic (*)

Intersection parameter \(x\)
\(k\) = 0.0
Show \(f(x)=\) LHS of (*)
Step 1Substitution
Put \(y=x+k\) into \(x^2+y^2+2x-4y-13=0\): \[x^2+(x+k)^2+2x-4(x+k)-13=0\]
Hint: Expand \((x+k)^2=x^2+2kx+k^2\) carefully — the \(k^2\) term carries through to the discriminant later.
Step 2Collect terms
\[x^2+(x^2+2kx+k^2)+2x-4x-4k-13=0\] \[2x^2+2kx+k^2-2x-4k-13=0\] \[2x^2+2(k-1)x+\left(k^2-4k-13\right)=0 \quad\cdots(*)\]
Hint: Every point of intersection \((x,y)\) corresponds to a real root \(x\) of \((*)\). So “number of intersections” = “number of real roots”.
Quadratic obtained: \(2x^2+2(k-1)x+(k^2-4k-13)=0\)
0 / 2

STEP C Two Distinct Points \(\Longleftrightarrow \Delta>0\)

\(k\) = 0.0
Show roots of \((*)\)
Step 1Condition
For \((*)\) to have two distinct real roots: \[\Delta>0\] where \(a=2,\; b=2(k-1),\; c=k^2-4k-13\).
Hint: “Two distinct points” ⇒ two distinct real roots ⇒ strictly \(\Delta>0\) (not \(\ge 0\)). If \(\Delta=0\) the line is tangent and there is only one point.
Step 2Compute Δ
\[\Delta=\left[2(k-1)\right]^2-4(2)\left(k^2-4k-13\right)\] \[=4(k^2-2k+1)-8(k^2-4k-13)\] \[=4k^2-8k+4-8k^2+32k+104\] \[=-4k^2+24k+108\]
Hint: Distribute the \(-8\) to all three terms inside the bracket — a very common slip.
\(\Delta=-4k^2+24k+108\)
Step 3Solve Δ > 0
\[-4k^2+24k+108>0\] Divide by \(-4\) (reverse the inequality sign!): \[k^2-6k-27<0\] Factorise: \[(k+3)(k-9)<0\]
Hint: Dividing an inequality by a negative number flips the direction of \(>\) to \(<\). The parabola \(y=k^2-6k-27\) opens upward, so it lies below the \(k\)-axis between its two roots.
\(-3<k<9\)  ⟹  Answer: B

SUMMARY Answers at a Glance

  • Line rewritten: \(x-y+k=0 \Rightarrow y=x+k\)
  • Substituting into the circle gives \(2x^2+2(k-1)x+(k^2-4k-13)=0\;(*)\)
  • Two distinct points \(\Rightarrow\) \(\Delta>0\): \(-4k^2+24k+108>0\)
  • Simplify: \(k^2-6k-27<0 \Rightarrow (k+3)(k-9)<0\)
  • Final: \(-3<k<9\)
  • Correct option: B
