HKDSE Math 2017 Paper 1 Q14

Polynomials — Remainder, Quotient & "All Roots Integer?" Claim

Topic 4 · Polynomials  |  (a) 3 marks  ·  (b) 5 marks

Polynomial Identity Remainder Theorem Factorisation Roots of Equations Disproving a Claim

Question

Let \( f(x)=6x^{3}-13x^{2}-46x+34 \). When \( f(x) \) is divided by \( 2x^{2}+ax+4 \), the quotient and the remainder are \( 3x+7 \) and \( bx+c \) respectively, where \( a \), \( b \) and \( c \) are constants.

(a) Find \( a \).  [3 marks]

(b) Let \( g(x) \) be a quadratic polynomial such that when \( g(x) \) is divided by \( 2x^{2}+ax+4 \), the remainder is \( bx+c \).

(i) Prove that \( f(x)-g(x) \) is divisible by \( 2x^{2}+ax+4 \).  [2 marks]

(ii) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \( f(x)-g(x)=0 \) are integers. Do you agree? Explain your answer.  [3 marks]

Step-by-step Guided Solution

1 Write the Division Identity for \( f(x) \) ▾

By the division algorithm, for any polynomial \( P(x) \) divided by a divisor \( D(x) \) with quotient \( Q(x) \) and remainder \( R(x) \):

\[ P(x)=D(x)\,Q(x)+R(x) \]

Here \( P(x)=f(x) \), \( D(x)=2x^{2}+ax+4 \), \( Q(x)=3x+7 \), \( R(x)=bx+c \). So:

\[ f(x)=(2x^{2}+ax+4)(3x+7)+bx+c \]
Hint: This identity holds for all values of \( x \). We will expand the RHS and compare coefficients with \( f(x)=6x^{3}-13x^{2}-46x+34 \).
2 (a) Expand and Compare Coefficients of \( x^{3} \) and \( x^{2} \) ▾

Expand the RHS:

\[ (2x^{2}+ax+4)(3x+7)+bx+c = 6x^{3}+14x^{2}+3ax^{2}+7ax+12x+28+bx+c \]

So \( f(x) \) must equal:

\[ f(x)=6x^{3}+(14+3a)x^{2}+(7a+12+b)x+(28+c) \]

Compare with \( f(x)=6x^{3}-13x^{2}-46x+34 \):

Coefficient of \( x^{3} \): \( 6=6 \) ✓ (already matches)
Coefficient of \( x^{2} \): \( 14+3a=-13 \)
\[ 3a=-27 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{a=-9} \]
Hint: We only need the \( x^{2} \)-coefficient to find \( a \). The \( x \)-coefficient gives \( 7(-9)+12+b=-46 \Rightarrow b=3 \), and the constant gives \( c=6 \).
3 (b)(i) Use the Division Identity for \( g(x) \) ▾

Since \( g(x) \) is a quadratic polynomial whose remainder upon division by \( 2x^{2}+ax+4 \) is \( bx+c \), its quotient must be a constant \( k \). So:

\[ g(x)=k(2x^{2}+ax+4)+bx+c \]

Also, from Step 1, \( f(x)=(2x^{2}+ax+4)(3x+7)+bx+c \).

Subtract the two identities:

\[ f(x)-g(x)=(2x^{2}+ax+4)(3x+7)-k(2x^{2}+ax+4) \] \[ f(x)-g(x)=(2x^{2}+ax+4)\big[(3x+7)-k\big] \]
Since \( (2x^{2}+ax+4) \) is a factor of \( f(x)-g(x) \), \( f(x)-g(x) \) is divisible by \( 2x^{2}+ax+4 \). ∎
Hint: The key idea is that two polynomials with the same remainder mod \( D(x) \) differ by a multiple of \( D(x) \).
4 (b)(ii) Set Up the Equation \( f(x)-g(x)=0 \) ▾

From Step 3, with \( a=-9 \):

\[ f(x)-g(x)=(2x^{2}-9x+4)\big[(3x+7)-k\big]=0 \]

Since \( g(x) \) is not uniquely determined (any constant \( k \) works), we may choose a simple value of \( k \).

Smart choice: Take \( k=7 \). Then \( f(x)-g(x)=(2x^{2}-9x+4)(3x+7-7)=(2x^{2}-9x+4)(3x) \).

Factorise the quadratic:

\[ 2x^{2}-9x+4=(2x-1)(x-4) \] \[ \therefore f(x)-g(x)=3x(2x-1)(x-4)=0 \]
Hint: The question only asks whether the claim is true for some \( g(x) \). One counter-example is enough to disagree.
5 Find the Roots and Reach the Conclusion ▾

Set each factor to zero:

\[ 3x=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=0 \] \[ 2x-1=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=\tfrac{1}{2} \] \[ x-4=0 \;\Rightarrow\; x=4 \]

The roots are \( x=0,\ \tfrac{1}{2},\ 4 \).

Note: \( \tfrac{1}{2} \) is not an integer.
Not all roots are integers. Therefore the claim is disagreed (false). ∎

Key Takeaways

  • Division identity: \( P(x)=D(x)Q(x)+R(x) \) — expand and compare coefficients to find unknowns.
  • Same remainder ⇒ difference is divisible: if \( f \) and \( g \) leave the same remainder mod \( D \), then \( D \mid (f-g) \).
  • To disprove a universal claim, one counter-example suffices. Choosing a convenient \( k \) (e.g. \( k=7 \)) simplifies the equation.
  • Always state explicitly which root is not an integer to justify "disagree".
