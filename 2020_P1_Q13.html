HKDSE 2020 · Paper 1 · Q13 · 4. Polynomials

The cubic polynomial $f(x)$ is divisible by $x-1$. When $f(x)$ is divided by $x^{2}-1$, the remainder is $kx+8$, where $k$ is a constant.

(a) Find $k$. [3 marks]

(b) It is given that $x+3$ is a factor of $f(x)$. When $f(x)$ is divided by $x$, the remainder is $24$. Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $f(x)=0$ are integers. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [5 marks]

Part (a) — Find $k$

1Use the division identity
Factor Theorem\((x-a)\ \text{is a factor}\ \Leftrightarrow\ f(a)=0\)\(x-1\) is a factor, so \(f(1)=k+8=0\) and \(k=-8\).
Division Algorithm for Polynomials\(f(x)=d(x)q(x)+r(x),\quad \deg r<\deg d\)Dividing the cubic \(f(x)\) by the quadratic \(x^{2}-1\) leaves a remainder of degree at most \(1\), written \(kx+8\).

Since $f(x)$ is a cubic and the divisor $x^{2}-1$ is quadratic, write

$$f(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(kx+8),$$

where $q(x)$ is a polynomial (here it is linear).

Why: Remainder theorem — the remainder after dividing by a quadratic is at most linear.
2Apply $f(1)=0$
Remainder Theorem\(\text{remainder of }f(x)\div(x-a)\ \text{is}\ f(a)\)Division by \(x\) means \(a=0\), so the remainder \(24\) is \(f(0)=-3b\), giving \(b=-8\).

$f(x)$ is divisible by $x-1$, so $f(1)=0$.

$$f(1)=(1^{2}-1)q(1)+(k(1)+8)=0$$ $$0+ k+8=0 \;\Rightarrow\;\boxed{k=-8}$$
Key idea: the term $(x^{2}-1)q(x)$ vanishes at $x=1$.

Part (b) — Are all roots integers?

3Fix the shape of $f(x)$

Given $x+3$ is a factor, write

$$f(x)=(x-1)(x+3)(ax+b).$$

This uses that $x-1$ and $x+3$ are both factors of the cubic.

4Use remainder $24$ on division by $x$

Remainder on division by $x$ is $f(0)=24$:

$$f(0)=(-1)(3)(b)=24 \;\Rightarrow\; -3b=24 \;\Rightarrow\; b=-8.$$
5Determine $a$ from Part (a)

From (a), $f(x)=(x^{2}-1)q(x)+(-8x+8)$, so $f(-1)=-8(-1)+8=16$.

$$f(-1)=(-1-1)(-1+3)(-a+b)=16$$ $$(-2)(2)(-a-8)=16 \;\Rightarrow\; -4(-a-8)=16$$ $$a+8=4 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{a=-4}$$
6Read off the roots

So $f(x)=(x-1)(x+3)(-4x-8)=-4(x-1)(x+3)(x+2)$.

Setting $f(x)=0$ gives the roots

$$x=1,\quad x=-3,\quad x=-2 .$$

All three are integers, so the claim is correct.

Conclusion: every root of $f(x)=0$ is an integer — the claim holds.
(a) $k=-8$  ·  (b) $f(x)=-4(x-1)(x+3)(x+2)$, roots $1,-3,-2$ → all integers, claim correct.
Answer: k = −8 ; the claim is correct (roots: 1, −3, −2).

Question. Let \(f(x)\) be a cubic polynomial. When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x^{2}-4\), the remainder is \(kx+c\). It is given that \(x-2\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) and that the remainder when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+2\) is \(-4\). (a) Find the remainder. (b) It is given that \(x+1\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) and \(f(0)=-6\). Find \(f(x)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Since \(x^{2}-4\) is quadratic, the remainder is linear, so write \(r(x)=kx+c\).
Step 2. \(x-2\) is a factor, so \(f(2)=0\) and hence \(r(2)=0\): \(2k+c=0\).
Step 3. Remainder on division by \(x+2\) is \(f(-2)=-4\), so \(r(-2)=-4\): \(-2k+c=-4\). Solving gives \(c=-2\) and \(k=1\), so the remainder is \(x-2\).
Step 4. With factors \(x-2\) and \(x+1\), write \(f(x)=(x-2)(x+1)(ax+b)\). \(f(0)=(-2)(1)(b)=-6\) gives \(b=3\).
Step 5. \(f(-2)=(-4)(-1)(-2a+3)=-4\) gives \(-8a+12=-4\), so \(a=2\). Hence \(f(x)=2(x-2)(x+1)(x+\tfrac{3}{2})=2x^{3}+x^{2}-7x-6\).
Answer: (a) \(x-2\) (b) \(f(x)=2x^{3}+x^{2}-7x-6\)
