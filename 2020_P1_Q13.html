Topic 4: Polynomials

Factor Theorem Remainder Theorem Cubic Polynomial Roots of Equations

Q Question

The cubic polynomial \(f(x)\) is divisible by \(x-1\). When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x^2-1\), the remainder is \(kx+8\), where \(k\) is a constant.

(a) Find \(k\). [3 marks]

(b) It is given that \(x+3\) is a factor of \(f(x)\). When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x\), the remainder is \(24\). Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \(f(x)=0\) are integers. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [5 marks]

💡 Core Concepts Tested

  • Remainder Theorem: When \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x-a)\), the remainder is \(f(a)\).
  • Factor Theorem: \((x-a)\) is a factor of \(f(x)\) \(\iff f(a)=0\).
  • Division Algorithm: \(f(x) = (x^2-1)q(x) + (kx+8)\) since \(x^2-1\) has degree 2, the remainder has degree ≤ 1.
  • Solving for unknown constants using given conditions, then finding roots of a cubic equation.

a Find \(k\)

Since \(f(x)\) is cubic and dividing by \(x^2-1\) (degree 2), the quotient \(q(x)\) is linear and the remainder is \(kx+8\).

\[ f(x) = (x^2-1)q(x) + (kx+8) \]
Hint: This is the Division Algorithm: Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder.

Since \(f(x)\) is divisible by \(x-1\), by the Factor Theorem, \(f(1)=0\).

Substitute \(x=1\) into the division equation:

\[ f(1) = (1^2-1)q(1) + (k(1)+8) = 0 \]

Since \(1^2-1=0\), the \(q(1)\) term vanishes:

\[ k + 8 = 0 \]
Hint: The Factor Theorem says if \((x-a)\) is a factor, then \(f(a)=0\). Here \(a=1\).
\[ \boxed{k = -8} \]

b Is the claim correct?

We know \((x-1)\) and \((x+3)\) are factors. Since \(f(x)\) is cubic, we can write:

\[ f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(ax+b) \]

where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants to be determined.

Hint: A cubic with two known linear factors must have one remaining linear factor \((ax+b)\).

When divided by \(x\), the remainder is 24. By the Remainder Theorem:

\[ f(0) = 24 \]

Substitute \(x=0\) into \(f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(ax+b)\):

\[ (-1)(3)(b) = 24 \] \[ -3b = 24 \implies b = -8 \]

From Part (a), the remainder when divided by \(x^2-1\) is \(-8x+8\). So:

\[ f(x) = (x^2-1)q(x) + (-8x+8) \]

Substitute \(x=-1\) (which makes \(x^2-1=0\)):

\[ f(-1) = (-8)(-1) + 8 = 16 \]

Now substitute \(x=-1\) into \(f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(ax+b)\) with \(b=-8\):

\[ (-1-1)(-1+3)(a(-1)-8) = 16 \] \[ (-2)(2)(-a-8) = 16 \] \[ -4(-a-8) = 16 \] \[ a + 8 = 4 \implies a = -4 \]
Hint: These steps should be written carefully in the exam: state \(f(x)=(x^2-1)q(x)+(-8x+8)\), find \(f(-1)=16\), then use the factorised form with \(x=-1\).

With \(a=-4\) and \(b=-8\):

\[ f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(-4x-8) \] \[ f(x) = -4(x-1)(x+3)(x+2) \]

Set \(f(x)=0\), the roots are:

\[ x = 1,\quad x = -3,\quad x = -2 \]

All roots are integers, so the claim is correct.

✅ Full Explicit Form of \(f(x)\)

\[ f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(-4x-8) = -4(x-1)(x+3)(x+2) \]

Roots: \(1,\; -3,\; -2\) — all integers.

📝 Final Answer Summary

  • (a) \(k = -8\)
  • (b) \(f(x) = (x-1)(x+3)(-4x-8)\). Roots are \(1, -3, -2\). All are integers → the claim is correct.
