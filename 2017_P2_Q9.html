Question

The figure shows the graph of $$y=(px+5)^2+q,$$ where $p$ and $q$ are constants. Its vertex (turning point) lies in Quadrant III. Which of the following is true?
  • A.  $p>0$ and $q>0$
  • B.  $p>0$ and $q<0$
  • C.  $p<0$ and $q>0$
  • D.  $p<0$ and $q<0$

Step-by-step Solution

1Read the given information
The graph opens upwards. The curve is a parabola written in vertex form $$y=(px+5)^2+q .$$ From the picture, the lowest point (vertex) sits in the third quadrant, i.e. it has negative $x$ and negative $y$ coordinates.
Opening upwards $\Rightarrow$ the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive.
2Rewrite in standard vertex form
Factor $p$ out of the bracket: $$y=(px+5)^2+q = p^2\left(x+\frac{5}{p}\right)^2+q .$$ Compare with the standard form $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ : $$a=p^2,\qquad h=-\frac{5}{p},\qquad k=q .$$
Note $a=p^2\ge 0$ always, and $a\neq0$ (a genuine quadratic), so $a>0$ automatically.
Vertex Form of a Quadratic Function \(y=a(x-h)^{2}+k \Rightarrow \text{vertex } (h,k)\) Writing \((px+5)^{2}+q=p^{2}\left(x+\tfrac{5}{p}\right)^{2}+q\) shows the vertex is \(\left(-\tfrac{5}{p},q\right)\).
3Use the direction of opening to find the sign of $p$
Since the parabola opens upwards, $a>0$, i.e. $$p^2>0,$$ which holds for any $p\neq 0$. So this alone does not yet fix $p$ — use the vertex position.
The opening direction only confirms $p\neq 0$.
4Vertex in Quadrant III $\Rightarrow$ $x$-coordinate is negative
The vertex is $\left(-\dfrac{5}{p},\; q\right)$. Its $x$-coordinate is negative, so $$-\frac{5}{p}<0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{5}{p}>0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{p>0}.$$
Dividing by a negative number flips the inequality — here $5>0$ forces $\frac1p>0$.
Dividing an Inequality by a Negative Number \(\dfrac{5}{p}<0 \Rightarrow p<0\) The vertex has negative \(x\)-coordinate, and here \(-\tfrac{5}{p}<0\) forces \(p>0\).
5Vertex in Quadrant III $\Rightarrow$ $y$-coordinate is negative
The vertex $y$-coordinate equals $q$, and it lies below the $x$-axis, so $$\boxed{q<0}.$$
The whole curve (including the $y$-intercept) stays below/below-left of $O$ for the vertex.
Signs of Coordinates by Quadrant \(\text{Quadrant III}: x<0 \text{ and } y<0\) The vertex y-coordinate equals \(q\), so lying in Quadrant III gives \(q<0\).
6Combine the two results
$$p>0 \quad\text{and}\quad q<0 .$$ This matches option B. (In fact, taking $p=1,\;q=-2$ reproduces the given sketch perfectly: $y=(x+5)^2-2$ with vertex at $(-5,-2)\in$ Quadrant III.)
Always double-check by sketching a quick example.
Answer:  B.  $p>0$ and $q<0$

Question. The graph of \(y=(px-4)^{2}+q\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are constants, has its vertex in Quadrant II. Which of the following must be true?

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write the function in vertex form: \(y=(px-4)^{2}+q=p^{2}\left(x-\dfrac{4}{p}\right)^{2}+q\).
Step 2. So the vertex is \(\left(\dfrac{4}{p},\,q\right)\), and \(p^{2}>0\) means the parabola opens upwards.
Step 3. In Quadrant II the \(x\)-coordinate is negative: \(\dfrac{4}{p}<0\), so \(p<0\).
Step 4. In Quadrant II the \(y\)-coordinate is positive, and it equals \(q\), so \(q>0\).
Answer: \(p<0\) and \(q>0\)
