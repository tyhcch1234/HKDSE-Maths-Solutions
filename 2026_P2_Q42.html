Probability: Committee

Question

In a class, there are \(3\) boys and \(7\) girls. If \(5\) students are randomly selected from the class to form a committee, find the probability that at most \(2\) boys are selected.

  • A. \(\tfrac56\)
  • B. \(\tfrac{11}{12}\)
  • C. \(\tfrac{119}{120}\)
  • D. \(\tfrac{251}{252}\)

Solution

Total
Combinations \(C_{r}^{n}=\binom{n}{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\) The total number of ways of choosing \(5\) students from \(10\) is \(C_{5}^{10}=252\), the denominator of every probability here.
\(C(10,5)=252\).
Complement
Complement Rule in Probability \(P(A)=1-P(A^{\prime})\) With only \(3\) boys available, at most \(2\) boys is the complement of exactly \(3\) boys — one easy case instead of three.
\(\text{P(at most 2 boys)}=1-\text{P(3 boys)}\).
3 boys \(C(3,3)C(7,2)=1\times21=21\).
Result
Multiplication Principle of Counting \(C_{3}^{3}\times C_{2}^{7}=1\times 21=21\) Choosing all \(3\) boys and \(2\) of the \(7\) girls gives \(21\) favourable selections, so the probability is \(\dfrac{21}{252}=\dfrac{1}{12}\).
\(1-\dfrac{21}{252}=1-\dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{11}{12}\).
Answer: B — 11/12

Question. A class has \(4\) boys and \(8\) girls. \(5\) students are chosen at random from the class. Find the probability that at most \(3\) of them are boys. (3 marks)

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total number of selections: \(C_{5}^{12}=792\).
Step 2. At most \(3\) boys is the complement of choosing \(4\) boys (all of them),
since only \(4\) boys exist.
Step 3. Choosing \(4\) boys and \(1\) girl: \(C_{4}^{4}\times C_{1}^{8}=1\times 8=8\) ways.
Step 4. \(P=1-\dfrac{8}{792}=1-\dfrac{1}{99}=\dfrac{98}{99}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{98}{99}\)
