Statistics — Mean, Median, Range & Solving from Changes in Averages

Stem-and-Leaf Diagram Mean Median Range Case Analysis

Q The Question

The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the weights (in kg) of the students in a football club.

Stem (tens)
Leaf (units)
4
0   2   3   3   3   3   9
5
1   1   2   2   3   7   9
6
3   5   8   9
7
8   9

(a) Find the mean, the median and the range of the above distribution.  3 marks

(b) Two more students now join the club. It is found that both the mean and the range of the distribution of the weights are increased by \(1 \text{ kg}\). Find the weight of each of these two students.  4 marks

a Part (a) — Mean, Median, Range

Read the 20 values off the stem-and-leaf diagram, then compute the three statistics.

Step 11M
List the data and count them. Each stem \(s\) with leaf \(\ell\) gives the weight \(10s+\ell\). \[ \text{Data: } 40,42,43,43,43,43,49,\;51,51,52,52,53,57,59,\;63,65,68,69,\;78,79 \] Total number of students \(n = 20\).
Hint: Read each row carefully — the stem is the tens digit, the leaf is the units digit. So stem 4 leaf 0 means \(40\text{ kg}\).
Step 21A
Sum of all weights: \[ \sum x = 40+42+43+43+43+43+49+51+51+52+52+53+57+59+63+65+68+69+78+79 = 1100 \] \[ \text{Mean} = \frac{1100}{20} = 55 \text{ kg} \]
Mean = 55 kg
Step 31A
With \(n = 20\) (even), the median is the average of the \(10^{\text{th}}\) and \(11^{\text{th}}\) values. \[ \text{10}^{\text{th}} = 52, \quad \text{11}^{\text{th}} = 52 \] \[ \text{Median} = \frac{52+52}{2} = 52 \text{ kg} \]
Median = 52 kg
Step 41A
Largest value \(=79\), smallest value \(=40\). \[ \text{Range} = 79 - 40 = 39 \text{ kg} \]
Hint: The median is a positional measure — it does not depend on the actual leaf values, only on their order.
Range = 39 kg   (Mean \(=55\) kg, Median \(=52\) kg)

b Part (b) — The Two New Students

Both the mean and the range increase by \(1\text{ kg}\). Let the new weights be \(a\) and \(b\), with \(a \le b\).

Drag \(a\) and watch \(b\) adjust so that the new mean is \(56\text{ kg}\). The new range must also become \(40\text{ kg}\) — only one value of \(a\) works!

Step 11M
The original total sum is \(1100\) and there were \(20\) students. After adding \(a\) and \(b\), there are \(22\) students and the new mean is \[ \frac{1100 + a + b}{22} = 55 + 1 = 56 \] \[ \Rightarrow \; 1100 + a + b = 56 \times 22 = 1232 \] \[ \therefore \; a + b = 132 \]
Hint: "Mean increased by \(1\text{ kg}\)" means the new mean is \(55+1=56\text{ kg}\). Use: new total \(=\) new mean \(\times\) new count.
Step 21M
The original range is \(39\text{ kg}\). Since the range increases by \(1\text{ kg}\), \[ \text{New range} = 40 \text{ kg} \] The smallest original weight is \(40\) and the largest is \(79\), so the new maximum must be \[ \max\{79,\,a,\,b\} = 40 + 40 = 80 \] Hence \(b = 80\), and from Step 1, \(a = 132 - 80 = 52\).
Hint: There are two cases to consider: (i) the new smallest value is \(a < 40\), or (ii) the new smallest value stays \(40\). Check which case is consistent.
Step 31A
Case 1: \(a = 39\) (i.e. the new minimum is below the old minimum \(40\)).
For the new range to be \(40\), we would need the new maximum to be \(39 + 40 = 79\).
But then \(b = 79\), and \[ a + b = 39 + 79 = 118 \neq 132 \] So this contradicts Step 1 — impossible.
Hint: If \(a < 40\), the new range is \(b - a\). Setting \(b-a = 40\) with \(a+b=132\) gives \(b = 86,\ a = 46\) — but then the maximum would be \(86\), not \(79\), so the minimum really is \(a\)... test carefully which case satisfies both maximum and minimum conditions.
Step 41A
Case 2: \(40 \le a \le 80\). The minimum stays \(40\), so the new range is determined by the new maximum, which must be \(80\). \[ \therefore b = 80 \] Then from \(a + b = 132\): \[ a = 132 - 80 = 52 \] Check: \(40 \le 52 \le 80\) ✓. The new range is \(80 - 40 = 40\text{ kg}\) ✓, and the new mean is \(1232/22 = 56\text{ kg}\) ✓.
The two weights are 52 kg and 80 kg.

✓ Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) Mean\(= \dfrac{1100}{20} = 55 \text{ kg}\)
  • (a) Median\(= \dfrac{52+52}{2} = 52 \text{ kg}\)
  • (a) Range\(= 79 - 40 = 39 \text{ kg}\)
  • (b) Weights\(\boxed{52 \text{ kg and } 80 \text{ kg}}\)
  • Key idea (b)\(a+b = 1232-1100 = 132\); reject the case \(a=39\), then \(b = 40+40 = 80\).
