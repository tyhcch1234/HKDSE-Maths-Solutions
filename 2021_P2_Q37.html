HKDSE 2021 P2 · Q37 Topic 9 · Inequalities
Let $k$ be a constant. Find the range of values of $k$ such that $$x^{2}+kx+k+8 \ge 0$$ for any real number $x$.
  • A. $-8 \le k \le 4$
  • B. $-4 \le k \le 8$
  • C. $k \le -8$ or $k \ge 4$
  • D. $k \le -4$ or $k \ge 8$

Step-by-step Solution

1
Identify the shape of the graph
Consider the graph of $y=x^{2}+kx+k+8$. The coefficient of $x^{2}$ is $1>0$, so the parabola opens upwards.
A “U-shaped” curve — the minimum point is the turning point.
2
Translate the condition into a discriminant condition
For $x^{2}+kx+k+8 \ge 0$ for all real $x$, the upward parabola must never dip below the $x$-axis. Hence the equation $x^{2}+kx+k+8 = 0$ has at most one real root:
$\Delta \le 0$
“Touching” the axis ($\Delta = 0$) is allowed because of the “$\ge$”.
Discriminant Condition for a Non-negative Quadratic \(ax^{2}+bx+c\ge0\ \text{for all } x\iff a>0\ \text{and}\ \Delta\le0\) The upward parabola must never cross below the \(x\)-axis, so \(x^{2}+kx+k+8=0\) has at most one root.
3
Set up the discriminant
$$\Delta = (k)^{2}-4(1)(k+8)$$ $$\Delta = k^{2}-4k-32$$
Use $\Delta=b^{2}-4ac$ with $a=1,\;b=k,\;c=k+8$.
Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) With \(a=1\), \(b=k\) and \(c=k+8\), \(\Delta=k^{2}-4k-32\).
4
Solve the quadratic inequality
$$k^{2}-4k-32 \le 0$$ $$(k+4)(k-8) \le 0$$ So the parabola $y=(k+4)(k-8)$ (opening upwards in $k$) is $\le 0$ between its roots: $$-4 \le k \le 8$$
Factorise first, then read the interval between the two roots.
Solving a Quadratic Inequality \((k+4)(k-8)\le0\iff -4\le k\le 8\) The upward parabola in \(k\) is below zero between its roots \(-4\) and \(8\).
5
Conclusion
The range of values of $k$ is $$-4 \le k \le 8$$ which corresponds to option B.
Both endpoints are included since $\Delta \le 0$ allows equality.
Answer: B  —  $-4 \le k \le 8$
