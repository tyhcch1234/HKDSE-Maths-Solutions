Question 11 — Rate, Ratio & Variations

Partial variation + similar solids: build the cost model $C = r + sA$, then use the volume ratio to find the new surface area.

Partial Variation Cost Model Similar Figures Volume ↔ Area Ratio Linear Equations

Question

Let $C$ be the cost of painting a can of surface area $A \text{ m}^2$. It is given that $C$ is the sum of two parts, one part is a constant and the other part varies as $A$. When $A=2$, $C=62$; when $A=6$, $C=74$.

(a) Find the cost of painting a can of surface area $13\text{ m}^2$.  [4 marks]

(b) There is a larger can which is similar to the can described in (a). If the volume of the larger can is $8$ times that of the can described in (a), find the cost of painting the larger can.  [2 marks]

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

1
Translate the words into an equation
▶

"$C$ is the sum of two parts": one part is a constant (fixed cost, call it $r$) and the other part varies as $A$ (call the constant of variation $s$).

$$C = r + sA$$

where $r$ and $s$ are non-zero constants. This is a partial variation (linear model) — exactly the form the marker scheme writes.

Hint: "varies as $A$" means directly proportional to $A$, i.e. the term looks like $s \times A$. The fixed part does not depend on the area at all.
2
Form two equations from the given data
▶

Substitute each data point into $C = r + sA$:

  • When $A=2$, $C=62$:   $r + 2s = 62$  … (1)
  • When $A=6$, $C=74$:   $r + 6s = 74$  … (2)
Hint: Two unknowns ($r$ and $s$) need two independent data points — that is exactly why the question gives two pairs of values.
3
Solve for $r$ and $s$
▶

Subtract (1) from (2):

$$(r+6s) - (r+2s) = 74 - 62 \;\Rightarrow\; 4s = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; s = 3$$

Put $s=3$ into (1):

$$r + 2(3) = 62 \;\Rightarrow\; r = 62 - 6 = 56$$
So the model is $C = 56 + 3A$.

Meaning of the numbers: $r = 56$ is the fixed cost (paid regardless of area), and $s = 3$ means each extra $1\text{ m}^2$ of surface adds \$3 to the cost.

4
Part (a): use $A = 13$
▶

Now that the model is known, simply substitute $A = 13$:

$$C = 56 + 3(13) = 56 + 39$$
The required cost is $\${95}$.
5
Part (b): link volume ratio to area ratio
▶

For similar solids, if the linear scale factor is $k$, then:

$$\text{volume ratio} = k^3, \qquad \text{surface-area ratio} = k^2$$

Here the volume of the larger can is $8$ times that of the can in (a):

$$k^3 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; k = 2$$

Hence the surface-area ratio is:

$$k^2 = 2^2 = 4$$
Hint: Do not multiply the cost by 8. Cost depends on surface area, and area only scales by $k^2 = 4$, not $k^3$.
6
Find the larger can's surface area
▶

The can in (a) has surface area $13\text{ m}^2$. The larger can's area is $4$ times that:

$$\text{Surface area of larger can} = (13)(4) = 52\text{ m}^2$$
7
Compute the cost of the larger can
▶

Use the same model $C = 56 + 3A$ with $A = 52$:

$$C = 56 + 3(52) = 56 + 156$$
The required cost is $\${212}$.

Notice the fixed cost \$56 does not scale — only the variable part $3A$ grows with area.
