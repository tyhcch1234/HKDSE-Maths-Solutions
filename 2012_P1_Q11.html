HKDSE 2012 P1 Q11 Rate, Ratio & Variations

Let $C$ be the cost of painting a can of surface area $A\ \text{m}^2$. It is given that $C$ is the sum of two parts, one part is a constant and the other part varies as $A$. When $A=2$, $C=62$; when $A=6$, $C=74$.

(a) Find the cost of painting a can of surface area $13\ \text{m}^2$. [4 marks]

(b) There is a larger can which is similar to the can described in (a). If the volume of the larger can is 8 times that of the can described in (a), find the cost of painting the larger can. [2 marks]

1Set up the variation model
Partial Variation \(C=r+sA\) A constant part \(r\) plus a part that varies directly as \(A\); \(r,s\) are non-zero constants.

“One part is a constant and the other part varies as $A$” means the cost is the sum of a fixed charge and a charge proportional to the surface area:

$$C = r + sA$$

where $r$ and $s$ are non-zero constants.

Identify the two parts: constant term $r$ + direct variation term $sA$.
2Substitute the given data

Using $A=2,\ C=62$ and $A=6,\ C=74$:

$$r + 2s = 62 \qquad \text{…(1)}$$

$$r + 6s = 74 \qquad \text{…(2)}$$

Two data pairs give two linear equations in $r$ and $s$.
3Solve for the constants

$(2)-(1)$: $\;4s = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; s = 3$.

Substitute into (1): $\;r + 2(3) = 62 \;\Rightarrow\; r = 56$.

So the cost function is $\;C = 56 + 3A$.

Elimination: subtract to remove $r$, then back-substitute.
4(a) Cost for $A = 13$

$$C = 56 + 3(13) = 56 + 39 = 95$$

The required cost is $\$95$.

Substitute the surface area straight into $C = 56 + 3A$.
5(b) Similar solids — volume ratio
Similar Solids — Volume Ratio \(\dfrac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=k^{3}\) If the lengths are in the ratio \(k:1\), the volumes are in the ratio \(k^{3}:1\).

For similar solids, volume ratio $= k^3$ where $k$ is the length ratio.

$$\frac{V_{\text{large}}}{V_{\text{small}}} = 8 = 2^3 \;\Rightarrow\; k = 2$$

Similar figures: lengths scale by $k$, areas by $k^2$, volumes by $k^3$.
6(b) Surface area of the larger can
Similar Solids — Area Ratio \(\dfrac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}=k^{2}\) Surface areas scale as the square of the length ratio.

Area ratio $= k^2 = 4$, so

$$A_{\text{large}} = 4 \times 13 = 52\ \text{m}^2$$

Area scales with the square of the length ratio.
7(b) Cost of the larger can

$$C = 56 + 3(52) = 56 + 156 = 212$$

The required cost is $\$212$.

Use the same cost model with the new surface area.
Answer: (a) $\$95$    (b) $\$212$

Question. The cost \(\$C\) of a metal sheet consists of a fixed charge and a charge varying directly as its surface area \(A\text{ m}^{2}\). When \(A=3\), \(C=68\); when \(A=7\), \(C=84\). (a) Find the cost when \(A=15\). (b) Two similar sheets have volumes in the ratio \(27:1\). If the smaller sheet has area \(15\text{ m}^{2}\), find the cost of the larger sheet.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write \(C=r+sA\). Substituting: \(r+3s=68\) …(1) and \(r+7s=84\) …(2).
Step 2. \((2)-(1)\): \(4s=16\), so \(s=4\); then \(r=68-12=56\). Hence \(C=56+4A\).
Step 3. (a) \(C=56+4(15)=56+60=\$116\).
Step 4. (b) Volume ratio \(27=3^{3}\), so the length ratio is \(k=3\) and the area ratio is \(k^{2}=9\).
Step 5. Area of larger sheet \(=9\times15=135\text{ m}^{2}\), so \(C=56+4(135)=56+540=\$596\).
Answer: (a) \(\$116\). (b) \(\$596\).
