Step 1 : Start from the given formula $k=\dfrac{5}{2m}+n$.

HKDSE 2024 Paper 2 · Q3 · Formulas & Sequences

Question. If $\;k=\dfrac{5}{2m}+n$, then $m=$

  • A.  $\dfrac{5}{2(k-n)}$
  • B.  $\dfrac{5}{2(n-k)}$
  • C.  $\dfrac{2(k-n)}{5}$
  • D.  $\dfrac{2(n-k)}{5}$
Step 1 Write down the given formula.
$$k=\frac{5}{2m}+n$$
Goal: rearrange so that $m$ stands alone on one side.
Step 2 Subtract $n$ from both sides to keep the term containing $m$ alone.
$$k-n=\frac{5}{2m}$$
Move everything without $m$ to the left-hand side.
Step 3 Take reciprocals of both sides.
$$\frac{1}{k-n}=\frac{2m}{5}$$
Since $k-n=\dfrac{5}{2m}$, flipping both fractions gives the reciprocal relation.
Step 4 Multiply both sides by $\dfrac{5}{2}$ to isolate $m$.
$$m=\frac{5}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{k-n}=\frac{5}{2(k-n)}$$
$\dfrac{2m}{5}\cdot\dfrac{5}{2}=m$, so multiply the other side by $\dfrac{5}{2}$.
Step 5 Compare with the options.

$m=\dfrac{5}{2(k-n)}$ matches option A exactly. Option B has $n-k$ in the denominator (wrong sign), and options C and D are the reciprocal form (wrong).

Check the sign inside the bracket carefully — the answer requires $(k-n)$.
Answer: A  —  $m=\dfrac{5}{2(k-n)}$
