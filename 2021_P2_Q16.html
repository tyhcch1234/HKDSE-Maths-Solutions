The sum of the total surface areas of two solid hemispheres is $351\pi\text{ cm}^2$. If the ratio of the radius of the smaller hemisphere to the radius of the larger hemisphere is $2:3$, then the difference of the volumes of the two hemispheres is

  • A. $342\pi\text{ cm}^3$
  • B. $630\pi\text{ cm}^3$
  • C. $684\pi\text{ cm}^3$
  • D. $1260\pi\text{ cm}^3$

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Set up the radii

Let the radius of the smaller hemisphere be $2a$ cm and that of the larger hemisphere be $3a$ cm, so the ratio $2a:3a = 2:3$.

$$r_{\text{small}} = 2a,\qquad r_{\text{large}} = 3a$$
Hint: The total surface area of a solid hemisphere = curved surface $+$ flat base $= 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2$.
STEP 2 · Write total surface area of each

Total surface area of the smaller hemisphere: $3\pi(2a)^2$. Total surface area of the larger hemisphere: $3\pi(3a)^2$.

$$3\pi(2a)^2 = 12\pi a^2,\qquad 3\pi(3a)^2 = 27\pi a^2$$
STEP 3 · Apply the sum of areas

The sum of the total surface areas is $351\pi$:

$$12\pi a^2 + 27\pi a^2 = 351\pi$$
$$39\pi a^2 = 351\pi$$
STEP 4 · Solve for $a$

Divide both sides by $39\pi$:

$$a^2 = 9 \;\Rightarrow\; a = 3 \ \text{ or }\ a = -3$$

Since a radius must be positive, $a = 3$ (reject $a=-3$). Hence

$$r_{\text{small}} = 6\text{ cm},\qquad r_{\text{large}} = 9\text{ cm}$$
STEP 5 · Volume of a hemisphere

Volume of a solid hemisphere $= \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3$.

$$V_{\text{large}} = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(9)^3 = 486\pi,\qquad V_{\text{small}} = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(6)^3 = 144\pi$$
STEP 6 · Find the difference in volumes
$$\Delta V = 486\pi - 144\pi = 342\pi\text{ cm}^3$$
FINAL ANSWER A.   $342\pi\text{ cm}^3$
