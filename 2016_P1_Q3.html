HKDSE 2016 P1 Q3Polynomials — Simplify a Rational Expression

Question

Simplify $\dfrac{2}{4x-5}+\dfrac{3}{1-6x}$.  [3 marks]

STEP 1 · Write the L.C.M. denominator
Adding Algebraic Fractions\(\dfrac{a}{b}+\dfrac{c}{d}=\dfrac{ad+bc}{bd}\)\(4x-5\) and \(1-6x\) share no factor, so the common denominator is their product.

The two denominators are $4x-5$ and $1-6x$. They share no common factor,
so the least common multiple is the product:

$$\text{L.C.M.}=(4x-5)(1-6x)$$

Hint: factorise each denominator first — if any factor repeats,
use it only once.
Here nothing repeats.

STEP 2 · Convert to a common denominator  (1M)

Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by the missing factor:

$$\frac{2}{4x-5}+\frac{3}{1-6x}=\frac{2\,\color{#1d63b8}{(1-6x)}+\color{#c0392b}{3\,(4x-5)}}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}$$

Hint: the whole numerator $2(1-6x)+3(4x-5)$ must be written over the single common denominator.

STEP 3 · Expand the numerator  (1M)
Distributive Law\(a(b+c)=ab+ac\)Expanding \(2(1-6x)\) and \(3(4x-5)\) gives \(2-12x\) and \(12x-15\).

Expand each product carefully:

$$2(1-6x)=2-12x,\qquad 3(4x-5)=12x-15$$

$$\Rightarrow\ \frac{2-12x+12x-15}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}$$

Hint: watch the brackets — expand the whole bracket,
don't drop the sign of $-15$.

STEP 4 · Collect like terms  (1A)

The $-12x$ and $+12x$ cancel, and $2-15=-13$:

$$\frac{-13}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}$$

Hint: the $x$ terms vanish — the answer is a constant over the product of the denominators.

STEP 5 · Tidy the sign (optional)
Opposite Binomials\(1-6x=-(6x-1)\)Absorbing the minus sign turns \(\dfrac{-13}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}\) into \(\dfrac{13}{(4x-5)(6x-1)}\).

Since $1-6x=-(6x-1)$, absorb the minus sign into the numerator:

$$\frac{-13}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}=\frac{13}{(4x-5)(6x-1)}$$

Hint: in HKDSE,
leaving $-13$ in the numerator is acceptable;
tidying the denominator is a nice finishing touch.

Answer: $\dfrac{-13}{(4x-5)(1-6x)}\;=\;\dfrac{13}{(4x-5)(6x-1)}$

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{3}{2x+1}+\dfrac{5}{1-4x}\). [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The denominators \(2x+1\) and \(1-4x\) share no factor, so use \(\text{L.C.M.}=(2x+1)(1-4x)\).
Step 2. Put over the common denominator: \(\dfrac{3(1-4x)+5(2x+1)}{(2x+1)(1-4x)}\).
Step 3. Expand: \(3-12x+10x+5\).
Step 4. Collect like terms: \(8-2x\), so the fraction is \(\dfrac{8-2x}{(2x+1)(1-4x)}\).
Answer: \(\dfrac{8-2x}{(2x+1)(1-4x)}\)
