HKDSE 2018 · Paper 2 · Q38 Trigonometry — Number of Roots

$y=\cos x$ $y=1$ (level) $y=-\tfrac56$ (level) Roots of equation $0^\circ\!\le x<360^\circ$
[2018MC#38]
For $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$, how many roots does the equation $$6\cos^2 x = \cos x + 5$$ have?
A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 5
STEP 1 · Rearrange into a quadratic in cos x
Quadratic Equation in a Trigonometric Function \(a\cos^{2}x+b\cos x+c=0\) With \\(c=\\cos x\\) the equation becomes \\(6c^{2}-c-5=0\\); dividing by \\(\\cos x\\) would lose the root \\(\\cos x=0\\).
Bring every term to one side: $$6\cos^2 x - \cos x - 5 = 0$$ This is a quadratic equation in the unknown $c=\cos x$: $$6c^2 - c - 5 = 0$$
Do not divide by $\cos x$ — $\cos x$ can be $0$ and you would lose solutions. Keep it as a quadratic.
STEP 2 · Factorise
Factorisation of a Quadratic \(6c^{2}-c-5=(c-1)(6c+5)\) The factorised form shows the two cases \\(\\cos x=1\\) and \\(\\cos x=-\\dfrac{5}{6}\\) separately.
$$6c^2 - c - 5 = (c-1)(6c+5)$$ Check: $(c-1)(6c+5) = 6c^2 + 5c - 6c - 5 = 6c^2 - c - 5$ ✓ So either $$\cos x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad \cos x = -\tfrac{5}{6}.$$
STEP 3 · Solve cos x = 1 on the interval For $0^\circ \le x < 360^\circ$, $$\cos x = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; x = 0^\circ .$$ This is the blue intersection of the cosine curve with the orange line $y=1$: only one root (at $x=360^\circ$ the value equals $1$ again,
but $x=360^\circ$ is excluded).
STEP 4 · Solve cos x = −5/6 on the interval The angle is NOT a special angle.
A cosine of $-\tfrac56 \approx -0.8333$ means $x$ lies in quadrant II or III, and the reference angle is $$\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac56\right) \approx 33.5573^\circ .$$ Hence $$x = 180^\circ - 33.5573^\circ \approx 146.4427^\circ$$ $$x = 180^\circ + 33.5573^\circ \approx 213.5573^\circ$$ Both lie in $[0^\circ, 360^\circ)$ — that is two more
Solving cos x = -k in Two Quadrants \(x=180^\circ\pm\theta\ \ (\theta\ \text{the reference angle})\) With reference angle \\(33.56^\circ\\) the roots are \\(180^\circ-33.56^\circ\\) and \\(180^\circ+33.56^\circ\\).
roots (the purple intersections).
STEP 5 · Count the roots $$\underbrace{x=0^\circ}_{\text{from }\cos x=1} \quad+\quad \underbrace{x\approx146.44^\circ,\;213.56^\circ}_{\text{from }\cos x=-5/6}$$ Total number of distinct roots $= 1 + 2 = 3$.
Quick check: a cosine curve over one full period crosses any horizontal line $y=k$ with $-1twice, and the tangent level $y=1$ exactly once (at the peak). Here $-\tfrac56$ gives $2$ and $1$ gives $1$.
Answer: B  (3 roots) x ≈ 0°, 146.44°, 213.56°

Question. Solve the equation \\(2\cos^{2}x-3\cos x+1=0\\), where \\(0^\circ\leq x<360^\circ\\). How many distinct roots are there?

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \\(c=\\cos x\\): the equation becomes \\(2c^{2}-3c+1=0\\).
Step 2. Factorise: \\((2c-1)(c-1)=0\\), so \\(c=\\dfrac{1}{2}\\) or \\(c=1\\).
Step 3. \\(\\cos x=1\\) gives \\(x=0^\circ\\) on \\(0^\circ\leq x<360^\circ\\).
Step 4. \\(\\cos x=\\dfrac{1}{2}\\) gives \\(x=60^\circ\\) or \\(x=360^\circ-60^\circ=300^\circ\\).
Step 5. The distinct roots are \\(0^\circ,\\ 60^\circ,\\ 300^\circ\\) \u2014 three roots in all.
Answer: \(x=0^\circ,\ 60^\circ,\ 300^\circ\) (3 roots)
