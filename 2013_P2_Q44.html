[2013MC#44]
If the first three digits and the last five digits of an eight-digit phone number are formed by a permutation of $5,6,9$ and a permutation of $2,3,4,7,8$ respectively, how many different eight-digit phone numbers can be formed?
  • A. 15
  • B. 126
  • C. 720
  • D. 40 320
Step 1Split the number into two blocks.
The 8-digit number is split into the first 3 digits (from $\{5,6,9\}$) and the last 5 digits (from $\{2,3,4,7,8\}$). Hint: the two blocks use completely different digit sets, so they are independent.
Step 2Arrange the first 3 digits.
A permutation of the 3 digits $5,6,9$: \[ 3! = 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6 \] Hint: order matters for phone digits — use permutations, not combinations.
Step 3Arrange the last 5 digits.
A permutation of the 5 digits $2,3,4,7,8$: \[ 5! = 5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 120 \] Hint: all 5 digits are used exactly once.
Step 4Apply the multiplication rule.
Since the two blocks are independent, multiply the numbers of arrangements: \[ 3! \times 5! = 6 \times 120 = 720 \] Hint: independent choices ⇒ multiply.
Answer: C  ·  $3! \times 5! = 720$ different eight-digit phone numbers.
