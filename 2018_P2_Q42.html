Step 1 — A class of 14 boys and 15 girls; a team of 3 must all be the same gender.

HKDSE 2018 · Paper 2 · Q42

In a class, there are $14$ boys and $15$ girls. If $3$ students of the same gender are selected from the class to form a team, how many different teams can be formed?

  • A. 819
  • B. 3 654
  • C. 4 914
  • D. 165 620

Step-by-step solution

1 Interpret the condition

The team has 3 students and they must be of the same gender. So the team is either 3 boys or 3 girls — never a mix. Gender is the only restriction; the order in which members are listed does not matter, so we use combinations.

Hint: “select … to form a team” ⇒ unordered ⇒ use $C_r^n$.
2 Count all-boy teams

Choose $3$ boys out of the $14$ boys: \[ C^{14}_{3}={}^{14}C_{3}=\frac{14\times13\times12}{3\times2\times1} =\frac{2184}{6}=364 . \]

Divide by $3!$ because the three boys are not ordered.
3 Count all-girl teams

Choose $3$ girls out of the $15$ girls: \[ C^{15}_{3}={}^{15}C_{3}=\frac{15\times14\times13}{3\times2\times1} =\frac{2730}{6}=455 . \]

The two cases (boys / girls) cannot happen together, so the counts add.
4 Add the two cases

Since the two cases are mutually exclusive, \[ C^{14}_{3}+C^{15}_{3}=364+455=819 . \]

Addition principle: total = (all-boy teams) + (all-girl teams).
Answer: A  (819) 819 = $C^{14}_{3}+C^{15}_{3}=364+455$
