Question

If \((3c+1)(d-4)=2d(5c-1)\), then \(c=\)

A. \(\dfrac{3d-4}{7d+12}\)
B. \(\dfrac{3d+4}{7d-12}\)
C. \(\dfrac{7d-12}{3d+4}\)
D. \(\dfrac{7d+12}{3d-4}\)

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Expand both sides. \[ (3c+1)(d-4)=2d(5c-1) \] \[ 3cd-12c+d-4=10cd-2d \]
Multiply out the brackets carefully — watch the signs.
Step 2Collect like terms. Bring every term to one side or group \(cd\), \(c\) and \(d\) terms: \[ 3cd+ d - 12c - 4 = 10cd - 2d \] \[ 7cd + 12c = 3d - 4 \]
Move the \(c\)-terms to one side and the \(d\)-only terms to the other.
Collecting Like Terms \(ac+bc=(a+b)c\) Bringing the \(c\)-terms together gives \(15c-cd\) on one side and \(5d+6\) on the other.
Step 3Factorise \(c\) on the left. \[ c(7d+12)=3d-4 \]
Both terms on the left contain \(c\) — take it out as a common factor.
Factorising by a Common Factor \(ac+bc=(a+b)c\) Both terms \(15c\) and \(-cd\) contain \(c\), so \(c(15-d)\) is taken out.
Step 4Divide to make \(c\) the subject. \[ c=\frac{3d-4}{7d+12} \]
Divide both sides by the bracket \((7d+12)\). This matches option A.
Making a Variable the Subject \(cx=d\ \to\ x=\dfrac{d}{c}\quad(c\neq 0)\) Dividing both sides by \((15-d)\) leaves \(c\) alone.
Answer \(\displaystyle c=\frac{3d-4}{7d+12}\)   —   Option A

Question. If \((5c-2)(d+3)=3d(2c+1)\), express \(c\) in terms of \(d\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Expand both sides: \(5cd+15c-2d-6=6cd+3d\).
Step 2. Collect the \(c\)-terms on one side: \(15c-cd=5d+6\).
Step 3. Factorise: \(c(15-d)=5d+6\).
Step 4. Divide by \((15-d)\): \(c=\dfrac{5d+6}{15-d}\).
Answer: \(c=\dfrac{5d+6}{15-d}\)

Check: substituting \(c=\frac{3d-4}{7d+12}\) back into the original equation satisfies both sides identically for \(d\neq -\frac{12}{7}\).
