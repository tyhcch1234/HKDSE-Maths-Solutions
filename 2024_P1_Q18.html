QUESTION Paper 1 · Q18

(a) \(PQRS\) is a thin quadrilateral metal sheet, where \(PQ=12\text{ cm}\), \(PS=10\text{ cm}\), \(QR=13\text{ cm}\), \(\angle QPS=82^\circ\) and \(\angle QRS=65^\circ\). Find

(i) the length of \(QS\),
(ii) \(\angle RQS\). 4 marks

(b) The metal sheet \(PQRS\) described in (a) is now folded along \(QS\) (see Figure 2). It is given that the angle between the plane \(PQS\) and the plane \(QRS\) is \(80^\circ\).

(i) Find the shortest distance from \(R\) to the plane \(PQS\).

(ii) Let \(X\) be any point lying on the plane \(QRS\). Someone claims that the distance between \(P\) and \(X\) exceeds \(8\text{ cm}\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. 4 marks

Step 11M
Apply the cosine rule in \(\triangle PQS\) with the two given sides \(PQ\), \(PS\) and the included angle \(\angle QPS = 82^\circ\): \[QS^2 = PQ^2 + PS^2 - 2(PQ)(PS)\cos\angle QPS\]
Hint: The cosine rule works whenever you know two sides and the included angle (SAS).
Step 21A
Substitute the numbers: \[QS^2 = 12^2 + 10^2 - 2(12)(10)\cos 82^\circ\] \[QS^2 = 144 + 100 - 240\cos 82^\circ \approx 210.6067\] \[QS \approx 14.51201074\]
Hint: Keep the full calculator value (or at least 4–5 d.p.) before rounding at the end.
\(QS \approx 14.5\text{ cm}\)  (to 3 significant figures)

Step 11M
In \(\triangle QRS\) we know \(QR = 13\), \(QS \approx 14.51201074\) and \(\angle QRS = 65^\circ\). Apply the sine rule: \[\frac{\sin\angle QSR}{QR} = \frac{\sin\angle QRS}{QS}\] \[\sin\angle QSR = \frac{13\sin 65^\circ}{14.51201074} \approx 0.811619\]
Hint: The sine rule relates each side to the sine of its opposite angle.
Step 21M
Solve for the angle: \[\angle QSR \approx 54.27995332^\circ \quad\text{or}\quad 125.7200468^\circ\] Since \(QS < QR\), \(\angle QRS < \angle QSR\). But the case \(125.72^\circ\) would force \(\angle RQS < 0^\circ\) (as \(65^\circ+125.72^\circ=190.72^\circ>180^\circ\)), so it is rejected. \[\angle QSR \approx 54.27995332^\circ\]
Hint: \(\arcsin\) gives two solutions in \((0^\circ,180^\circ)\). Use the angle sum \(=180^\circ\) to test which one is valid.
Step 31A
Angle sum of \(\triangle QRS\): \[\angle RQS \approx 180^\circ - 65^\circ - 54.27995332^\circ\] \[\angle RQS \approx 60.72004668^\circ\]
Hint: Always round to 3 significant figures at the very end.
\(\angle RQS \approx 60.7^\circ\)

(b)(i) Shortest distance from \(R\) to plane \(PQS\)

80°

The perpendicular distance from \(R\) to \(QS\) is \(RT = 13\sin\angle RQS\). The height of \(R\) above the plane \(PQS\) is \(h = RT\sin 80^\circ\).

Step 11M
Let \(T\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(R\) to \(QS\). Then \[RT = 13\sin\angle RQS\] Let \(h\) cm be the shortest distance from \(R\) to the plane \(PQS\). Since \(RT \perp QS\) and the dihedral angle between the planes is \(80^\circ\), we get \[h = RT\sin 80^\circ\]
Hint: The perpendicular distance from a point to a plane is measured along the line perpendicular to the plane.
Step 21A
Substitute \(RT = 13\sin 60.72004668^\circ\): \[h = 13\sin 60.72004668^\circ \times \sin 80^\circ\] \[h \approx 11.16685898\]
Hint: Use the full-precision value of \(\angle RQS\) from part (a)(ii) to avoid rounding errors.
\(h \approx 11.2\text{ cm}\)  (shortest distance from \(R\) to plane \(PQS\))

(b)(ii) Does the distance from \(P\) to \(X\) exceed \(8\text{ cm}\)?

8.06

The true shortest distance from \(P\) to the plane \(QRS\) is \(d^* \approx 8.064\text{ cm}\). The slider lets you see what happens when we (wrongly) choose a slightly too-small \(d\).

Step 11M
Let \(d\) cm be the shortest distance from \(P\) to the plane \(QRS\). The tetrahedron can be described with base \(\triangle QRS\) and vertex \(P\), or with base \(\triangle PQS\) and vertex \(R\). Equate the volumes: \[\tfrac13(\text{area of }\triangle PQS)\,h = \tfrac13(\text{area of }\triangle QRS)\,d\]
Hint: Two different choices of base and apex give the same volume — use this to compare the two perpendicular distances.
Step 21M
Write the areas in terms of sides and included angles: \[\frac{d}{h} = \frac{\tfrac12(PQ)(PS)\sin\angle QPS}{\tfrac12(QR)(QS)\sin\angle RQS}\] \[\frac{d}{11.16685898} = \frac{(12)(10)\sin 82^\circ}{(13)(14.51201074)\sin 60.72004668^\circ}\]
Hint: The \(\tfrac12\) factors cancel immediately — this keeps the arithmetic simple.
Step 31A
Compute the ratio: \[d \approx \frac{(12)(10)\sin 82^\circ}{(13)(14.51201074)\sin 60.72004668^\circ} \times 11.16685898\] \[d \approx 8.064136851\]
Hint: The value \(d\approx 8.064\) is the minimum possible distance from \(P\) to the plane \(QRS\).
Shortest distance from \(P\) to plane \(QRS\): \(d \approx 8.064\text{ cm}\)
Step 41A
For any point \(X\) on the plane \(QRS\), \(PX \geq d\) (the perpendicular distance is the minimum). Since \(d \approx 8.064\text{ cm} > 8\text{ cm}\), we get \[PX \geq d > 8\text{ cm}\]
Hint: The shortest distance from a point to a plane equals the length of the perpendicular segment; every other segment is longer.
The distance between \(P\) and \(X\) exceeds \(8\text{ cm}\). Since \(PX \geq d \approx 8.064 > 8\), the claim is correct.
