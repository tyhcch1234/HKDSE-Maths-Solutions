Question

[2014 MC #41] In the figure, PQS is a circle. PQ is produced to R such that RS is the tangent to the circle at S. I is the in-centre of $\triangle QRS$. If $\angle IRQ = 12^\circ$ and $\angle PSQ = 70^\circ$, then $\angle QPS =$
A. $24^\circ$
B. $37^\circ$
C. $43^\circ$
D. $62^\circ$
Key facts: the tangent–chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment, and a triangle's in-centre lies on each internal angle bisector.
Step 1 · Name the unknown angle
Let $\angle QPS = x$.
Because $R$ lies on the line $PQ$ produced, the chord $QS$ of the circle subtends $\angle PSQ$ at $P$ and $\angle QSR$ at $S$ (a point on the tangent).
Tangent–chord (alternate segment) theorem: \[ \angle QSR = \angle QPS = x \]
Tangent-Chord Theorem (Alternate Segment Theorem) \(\angle QSR=\angle QPS\) The chord \(QS\) and the tangent at \(S\) give \(ngle QSR=x\), the angle in the alternate segment.
Step 2 · Use the in-centre property
$I$ is the in-centre of $\triangle QRS$, so $IR$ bisects $\angle QRS$: \[ \angle QRS = 2\angle IRQ = 2(12^\circ) = 24^\circ \]
In-centre Property \(\angle QRS=2\angle IRQ\) The in-centre \(I\) lies on the bisector of \(ngle QRS\), so \(2 imes 12^{\circ}=24^{\circ}\).
Step 3 · Angle sum of $\triangle PRS$
In $\triangle PRS$ (with $R$ on the extension of $PQ$): \[ \angle RPS + \angle PSR + \angle PRS = 180^\circ \] where \[ \angle RPS = x, \quad \angle PSR = \angle PSQ + \angle QSR = 70^\circ + x, \quad \angle PRS = 24^\circ \]
Angle Sum of a Triangle \(\angle RPS+\angle PSR+\angle PRS=180^{\circ}\) In \( riangle PRS\) the three angles are \(x\), \(70^{\circ}+x\) and \(24^{\circ}\).
Step 4 · Solve for $x$
\[ x + (70^\circ + x) + 24^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ 2x + 94^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ 2x = 86^\circ \] \[ x = 43^\circ \]
Step 5 · Conclusion
Therefore \[ \angle QPS = x = 43^\circ \] Matching the choices, the answer is C.
Answer: C. $43^\circ$

Question. In the figure, \(PQS\) is a circle. \(PQ\) is produced to \(R\) such that \(RS\) is the tangent to the circle at \(S\). \(I\) is the in-centre of \(\triangle QRS\). If \(\angle IRQ=14^{\circ}\) and \(\angle PSQ=68^{\circ}\), find \(\angle QPS\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. By the tangent-chord theorem, \(\angle QSR=\angle QPS\). Let \(\angle QPS=x\), so \(\angle QSR=x\).
Step 2. \(I\) is the in-centre, so \(IR\) bisects \(\angle QRS\): \(\angle QRS=2\angle IRQ=2(14^{\circ})=28^{\circ}\).
Step 3. In \(\triangle PRS\): \(\angle RPS=x\), \(\angle PSR=68^{\circ}+x\) and \(\angle PRS=28^{\circ}\).
Step 4. Angle sum: \(x+(68^{\circ}+x)+28^{\circ}=180^{\circ}\), so \(2x=84^{\circ}\) and \(x=42^{\circ}\).
Answer: \(\angle QPS=42^{\circ}\)
