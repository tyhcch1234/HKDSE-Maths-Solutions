HKDSE 2018 Paper 2 Q14 Estimation & Error

Error & Measurement Range

In the figure, \(ABCDEFGH\) is an octagon. All measurements are correct to the nearest cm. Let \(x \text{ cm}^2\) be the actual area of the octagon. Find the range of values of \(x\).

Measurements (to nearest cm):

• \(BC = 6\) cm (bottom)

• \(AB = CD = 4\) cm (sides)

• Notch depth: \(2\) cm (from \(H\) down to \(G\))

• Notch width: \(FG = 2\) cm

⚡ Each measurement has an error of up to \(0.5\) cm.

Step-by-step breakdown

1 Understand the error bounds for each measurement ▼

All measurements are correct to the nearest cm. So the maximum absolute error is \(0.5 \text{ cm}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{For a stated value } m \text{ cm:}\\ m - 0.5 \le \text{actual length} < m + 0.5 \end{aligned} \]
Hint: Why upper bound uses \(<\) (not \(\le\))? Because a length of exactly \(m+0.5\) would round up to \(m+1\) cm, so it is excluded.
2 Apply bounds to the rectangle dimensions ▼

Consider the full rectangle \(ABCDEH\) (before cutting out the notch). Its sides are affected by the actual lengths of \(BC\), \(AB\) and \(CD\).

\[ \begin{aligned} BC &: \quad 5.5 \le BC < 6.5 \\[4pt] AB &: \quad 3.5 \le AB < 4.5 \\[4pt] CD &: \quad 3.5 \le CD < 4.5 \end{aligned} \]

The area of the outer rectangle \(ABCDEH\) (before removing the notch) is \(BC \times \text{(average of AB and CD)}\). Because both \(AB\) and \(CD\) are both given as 4 cm, but their actual values can differ within the error range.

3 Find the minimum and maximum area of the big rectangle ▼

Let \(S(AB, CD)\) denote the shorter of \(AB\) and \(CD\), and \(L(AB, CD)\) the longer.

\[ BC \times S(AB, CD) \le \text{Area}(ABCDEH) < BC \times L(AB, CD) \] \[ \begin{aligned} \text{Min possible } BC &= 5.5,\quad \text{Min possible } S = 3.5 \\ \text{Max possible } BC &< 6.5,\quad \text{Max possible } L < 4.5 \end{aligned} \] \[ \therefore \quad 5.5 \times 3.5 \le \text{Area}(ABCDEH) < 6.5 \times 4.5 \] \[ 19.25 \le \text{Area}(ABCDEH) < 29.25 \]
Why use shorter × shorter for lower bound? To minimise area, we need the smallest possible width and smallest possible height. Similarly, for the upper bound, we pair the largest possible width and largest possible height.
4 Bounds for the notch (rectangle EFGH) ▼

The cut-out rectangle \(EFGH\) has width \(FG = 2\) cm and depth \(2\) cm (from \(H\) to \(G\) or \(E\) to \(F\)). Both are correct to the nearest cm.

\[ \begin{aligned} FG &: \quad 1.5 \le FG < 2.5 \\[4pt] \text{Depth (HG or EF)} &: \quad 1.5 \le \text{depth} < 2.5 \end{aligned} \] \[ \therefore \quad 1.5 \times 1.5 \le \text{Area}(EFGH) < 2.5 \times 2.5 \] \[ 2.25 \le \text{Area}(EFGH) < 6.25 \]
Hint: Even though the depth is \(2\) cm, both horizontal and vertical sides of the notch have their own errors.
5 Combine to find the actual area of the octagon ▼

The octagon area is the big rectangle area minus the notch area.

\[ \text{Area}(ABCDEFGH) = \text{Area}(ABCDEH) - \text{Area}(EFGH) \]

Lower bound for \(x\): To minimise the octagon area, we want the smallest big rectangle and the largest possible notch.

\[ x_{\min} = 19.25 - 6.25 = 13 \]

Upper bound for \(x\): To maximise the octagon area, we want the largest big rectangle and the smallest possible notch.

\[ x_{\max} = 29.25 - 2.25 = 27 \]
6 Write the final range and choose the answer ▼

Combining the inequalities:

\[ 13 < x < 27 \]

Look at the options:

  • A. \(17 < x < 23\)
  • B. \(17 < x < 27\)
  • C. \(13 < x < 27\)
  • D. \(13 < x < 23\)

The correct option is C.

Answer: C
