HKDSE Math 2024 Paper 1 Q3 — Factorize

Topic 4 · Polynomials — Factorization by grouping & cross method

QQuestion

3. Factorize

(a) \(\;6r^2-13rs-28s^2,\)

(b) \(\;4r-14s+6r^2-13rs-28s^2.\)

(3 marks)

✎Guided Solution

1 Part (a) — Set up the factoring brackets ▼

We factorize \(6r^2-13rs-28s^2\) into two brackets of the form \((ar+bs)(cr+ds)\).

Comparing coefficients:

\[ ac = 6,\qquad bd = -28,\qquad ad+bc = -13 \]

Guess the "\(r\)" bracket pair from the factor pairs of \(6\): \((1,6)\), \((2,3)\), \((3,2)\), \((6,1)\).

Guess the "\(s\)" bracket pair from the factor pairs of \(-28\): \((\pm1,\mp28)\), \((\pm2,\mp14)\), \((\pm4,\mp7)\), …

Formula (in symbolic form): \[ (ar+bs)(cr+ds) = ac\,r^2 + (ad+bc)\,rs + bd\,s^2 \] Meaning of each number: \(ac=6\) comes from the \(r^2\) term, \(bd=-28\) from the \(s^2\) term, \(ad+bc=-13\) from the \(rs\) term. Only the last one is negative, so one of \(b,d\) must be negative and the other positive. Because \(ac>0\), \(a\) and \(c\) have the same sign.
2 Part (a) — Cross-method trial table ▼

Try \((2r\;\pm\;7s)(3r\;\mp\;4s)\) and \((2r\;\pm\;4s)(3r\;\mp\;7s)\) etc. The correct one gives the middle term \(-13rs\).

Try it yourself — pick the correct factorization:

Check: expand \((2r-7s)(3r+4s)\)

\[ = 2r\cdot 3r + 2r\cdot 4s - 7s\cdot 3r - 7s\cdot 4s \] \[ = 6r^2 + 8rs - 21rs - 28s^2 \] \[ = 6r^2 - 13rs - 28s^2 \quad\color{#059669}{\checkmark} \]
Trial when \(ac=6\) and \(bd=-28\): with brackets \(2r\times 3r\) and \(7s\times 4s\) (one minus), the cross products are \(2r\cdot 4s=8rs\) and \(-7s\cdot 3r=-21rs\). Sum \(=8rs-21rs=-13rs\).
3 Part (a) — Answer ▼
ANSWER (a)
\[ 6r^2-13rs-28s^2 = (2r-7s)(3r+4s) \]
4 Part (b) — Look for a common factor in the first two terms ▼

Part (b) is \(4r-14s+6r^2-13rs-28s^2\).

Look at the first two terms \(4r-14s\). Their common factor is \(2\):

\[ 4r-14s = 2(2r-7s) \]

And by part (a):

\[ 6r^2-13rs-28s^2 = (2r-7s)(3r+4s) \]

So the whole expression becomes:

\[ \underbrace{2(2r-7s)}_{\text{first two terms}} + \underbrace{(2r-7s)(3r+4s)}_{\text{last three terms}} \]
Take out \(2\) from the first two terms so that the factor \((2r-7s)\) appears in both groups. That common factor is the key to the next step.
5 Part (b) — Take out the common bracket \((2r-7s)\) ▼

Both groups now share the bracket \((2r-7s)\):

\[ 2(2r-7s) + (2r-7s)(3r+4s) \]

Take out the common factor \((2r-7s)\):

\[ = (2r-7s)\bigl[\,2 + (3r+4s)\,\bigr] \]

Simplify inside the square bracket:

\[ = (2r-7s)(2+3r+4s) \]
Key idea: "factorising by grouping" — split the expression into two groups, factor each group, then pull out the shared bracket.
6 Part (b) — Final answer & check ▼
ANSWER (b)
\[ 4r-14s+6r^2-13rs-28s^2 = (2r-7s)(2+3r+4s) \]

Verify:

\[ (2r-7s)(2+3r+4s) = 4r-14s + (6r^2-13rs-28s^2) \]

which matches the original expression. ✓
