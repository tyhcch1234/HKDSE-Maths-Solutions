Q21 The Question

In the figure, \(PQRS\) is a rectangle. Let \(T\) be a point lying on \(QR\) such that \(\angle PTS = 90^\circ\). \(PQ\) produced and \(ST\) produced meet at the point \(U\). \(PT\) is produced to the point \(V\) such that \(RT = RV\). Which of the following must be true?
A \(RV \,/\kern-0.6em/\, ST\)
B \(\angle PTQ=\angle RTS\)
C \(\triangle PST \sim \triangle UTQ\) ✓ correct
D \(\triangle PQT \equiv \triangle TRS\)

A Choice A — Is \(RV \,/\kern-0.6em/\, ST\)?

∠RVT = – ∠STV = – ∠RVT < 90°? –
0 / 4
Step 1Given
\[\text{In } \triangle RTV,\quad RT=RV \quad (\text{given})\]
Hint: Two equal sides meeting at \(R\) ⇒ \(\triangle RTV\) is isosceles with vertex \(R\).
Step 2
\[\therefore \angle RTV=\angle RVT \quad (\text{base }\angle\text{s, isos. }\triangle)\]
Hint: Equal sides ⇒ equal base angles (the angles opposite the equal sides).
Step 3
\[\angle RTV+\angle RVT=180^\circ-\angle TRV < 180^\circ\] \[\therefore \angle RVT<180^\circ\div 2=90^\circ\]
Hint: Angle sum of \(\triangle RTV\) is \(180^\circ\). Since \(\angle TRV>0^\circ\), the two base angles each must be strictly less than \(90^\circ\).
Step 4
\[\angle STV=90^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; \angle STV+\angle RVT<180^\circ\] \[\therefore RV \text{ and } ST \text{ are not parallel}\]
Hint: If \(RV\,/\kern-0.6em/\,ST\), the co-interior (allied) angles \(\angle STV\) and \(\angle RVT\) would sum to \(180^\circ\). They sum to less than that.
Conclusion: Choice A is NOT necessarily true.

B Choice B — Is \(\angle PTQ=\angle RTS\)?

∠PTQ = – ∠RTS = – QR : PQ = –
0 / 4
Step 1Assume
\[\text{Consider the case when } \angle PTQ=\angle RTS.\]
Hint: To test choice B, suppose equality holds and see what it forces on the rectangle.
Step 2
\[\angle PTQ+\angle RTS=180^\circ-\angle PTS=180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ\] \[\therefore \angle PTQ=\angle RTS=90^\circ\div 2=45^\circ\]
Hint: \(P,T,S\) … actually \(P,T,Q\) and \(R,T,S\) are angles on the straight line \(QR\) around \(T\)? No — \(\angle PTS=90^\circ\) and \(\angle PTQ+\angle QTS\)… Use: angles at \(T\) on the straight line are supplementary.
Step 3
\[\text{In } \triangle PQT:\; QT=\frac{PQ}{\tan 45^\circ}=PQ\] \[\text{In } \triangle RTS:\; RT=\frac{RS}{\tan 45^\circ}=RS\]
Hint: Right triangle trigonometry: \(\tan\angle=\dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\). With a \(45^\circ\) angle, opposite = adjacent.
Step 4
\[QR=QT+TR=PQ+RS=2PQ\] \[\therefore \angle PTQ=\angle RTS \text{ only when } QR=2PQ\]
Hint: \(RS=PQ\) (opposite sides of rectangle). Equality of those angles happens only for one special rectangle shape — not for every rectangle.
Conclusion: Choice B is NOT always true — it fails unless \(QR=2PQ\).

C Choice C — Prove \(\triangle PST \sim \triangle UTQ\)

∠PST = – ∠UTQ = – ∠SPT = – ∠TUQ = –
0 / 3
Step 1
\[\angle PST=\angle UTQ \quad (\text{corr. }\angle\text{s},\; PS\,/\kern-0.6em/\,QR)\]
Hint: \(U\) lies on \(ST\) produced and \(T\) lies on \(QR\) (which is parallel to \(PS\)). Look for the F-shaped configuration: corresponding angles.
Step 2
\[\angle PTS=\angle UQT=90^\circ\] \[(\text{since }\angle PTS=90^\circ\text{ given, and }\angle UQT=90^\circ\text{ rect.})\]
Hint: In a rectangle all four corners are right angles, so \(\angle PQR=\angle UQT=90^\circ\).
Step 3
\[\angle SPT=\angle TUQ \quad (\angle\text{ sum of }\triangle)\] \[\therefore \triangle PST \sim \triangle UTQ \quad (\text{AAA})\]
Hint: Once two pairs of angles are equal, the third pair is forced equal (angle sum of a triangle is \(180^\circ\)). Three pairs of equal angles ⇒ similar triangles.
Conclusion: Choice C MUST be true for every rectangle and every valid position of \(T\). ✓

\[\text{Consider the case when } \triangle PQT\equiv\triangle TRS.\]
Hint: Test choice D by assuming congruence and tracing its consequences.
Step 2
\[PQ=TR \quad\text{and}\quad QT=RS \quad (\text{corr. sides},\equiv\triangle\text{s})\]
Hint: Congruent triangles have all corresponding sides equal — matching vertex order \(P\!\leftrightarrow\!T\), \(Q\!\leftrightarrow\!R\), \(T\!\leftrightarrow\!S\).
Step 3
\[QR=TR+QT=PQ+RS=2PQ\] \[\therefore \triangle PQT\equiv\triangle TRS \text{ only when } QR=2PQ\]
Hint: Again a special-shape restriction — just like choice B. Only the "half-square" rectangle works; not all rectangles.
Conclusion: Choice D is NOT always true.

Summary & Answer

  • Correct answer: C  —  \(\triangle PST\sim\triangle UTQ\) by AAA.
  • Why C works: \(\angle PST=\angle UTQ\) (corr. ∠s, \(PS\,/\kern-0.6em/\,QR\)); \(\angle PTS=\angle UQT=90^\circ\); hence \(\angle SPT=\angle TUQ\). All three angle pairs equal ⇒ similar.
  • A ✗: \(\triangle RTV\) is isosceles with \(RT=RV\), so \(\angle RVT<90^\circ\). Then \(\angle STV+\angle RVT<180^\circ\), so \(RV\) is not parallel to \(ST\).
  • B ✗: \(\angle PTQ=\angle RTS\) forces \(45^\circ\) each, giving \(QR=2PQ\). Only true for a special rectangle shape.
  • D ✗: Congruence would force \(QT=RS\), hence again \(QR=2PQ\). Not generally true.
  • Key technique: use parallel-line angle relations in a rectangle + AAA similarity, and reject choices that only hold for a special shape.
