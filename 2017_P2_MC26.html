Coordinate Geometry — Equations, Centres and Radii of Circles
Circle Equation General Form Centre & Radius Collinearity Equidistance Multiple Choice

QQuestion

The equations of the circles \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are \[x^2+y^2+8x-4y-5=0\] and \[2x^2+2y^2+8x-4y-5=0\] respectively. Let \(G_1\) and \(G_2\) be the centres of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) respectively. Denote the origin by \(O\). Which of the following is/are true?

I. \(G_1\), \(G_2\) and \(O\) are collinear.

II. The radii of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are equal.

III. \(O\) is equidistant from \(G_1\) and \(G_2\).

A. I only    B. II only    C. I and III only    D. II and III only

0Getting Ready — Standard Form of a Circle

Any circle can be written in the general form

\[x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\]

Then, by completing the square,

\[\left(x+\tfrac{D}{2}\right)^2+\left(y+\tfrac{E}{2}\right)^2=\frac{D^2+E^2}{4}-F\] \[\Rightarrow\ \text{centre}=\left(-\tfrac{D}{2},-\tfrac{E}{2}\right),\qquad \text{radius}=\sqrt{\frac{D^2+E^2}{4}-F}\]

Important: this formula requires the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) to be \(1\). So for \(C_2\) we must first divide the whole equation by \(2\).

Hint: Watch the coefficient of \(x^2\)! A common trap in this question is forgetting to divide \(2x^2+2y^2+8x-4y-5=0\) by \(2\).

IStatement I — Are \(O\), \(G_1\), \(G_2\) collinear?

Step 1 Find the centre \(G_1\) of \(C_1\)

For \(C_1:\ x^2+y^2+8x-4y-5=0\), we have \(D=8,\ E=-4,\ F=-5\).

\[G_1=\left(-\frac{8}{2},\,-\frac{-4}{2}\right)=(-4,\ 2)\]
Hint: The centre is \(\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)\) — remember the negative sign.
Step 2 Find the centre \(G_2\) of \(C_2\)

First divide \(C_2\) by \(2\):

\[2x^2+2y^2+8x-4y-5=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2+y^2+4x-2y-\frac{5}{2}=0\]

Now \(D=4,\ E=-2,\ F=-\frac{5}{2}\):

\[G_2=\left(-\frac{4}{2},\,-\frac{-2}{2}\right)=(-2,\ 1)\]
Hint: Dividing by \(2\) does not change the circle — it only makes the leading coefficients equal to \(1\).
Step 3 Compare the slopes of \(OG_1\) and \(OG_2\)

Since \(O=(0,0)\):

\[\text{slope of }OG_1=\frac{2-0}{-4-0}=-\frac{1}{2}\] \[\text{slope of }OG_2=\frac{1-0}{-2-0}=-\frac{1}{2}\]

Both segments pass through \(O\) and have the same slope, so they lie on the same straight line.

Hint: Two lines through the same point with equal slopes must be the same line.
∴ \(G_1\), \(G_2\) and \(O\) are collinear. Statement I is TRUE.

IIStatement II — Are the radii equal?

Step 1 Radius of \(C_1\)

Using \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{D^2+E^2}{4}-F}\) with \(D=8,\ E=-4,\ F=-5\):

\[r_1=\sqrt{\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{-4}{2}\right)^2-(-5)} =\sqrt{16+4+5}=\sqrt{25}=5\]
Hint: \(-\,F=-\,(-5)=+5\). A sign slip here changes the answer.
Step 2 Radius of \(C_2\)

With the divided form \(x^2+y^2+4x-2y-\frac{5}{2}=0\):

\[r_2=\sqrt{\left(\frac{4}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{-2}{2}\right)^2-\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)} =\sqrt{4+1+\frac{5}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{15}{2}}\]
Hint: \(\sqrt{\frac{15}{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{30}}{2}\approx 2.74\) — clearly not equal to \(5\).
Step 3 Compare
\[r_1=5,\qquad r_2=\sqrt{\frac{15}{2}}\approx 2.739\] \[r_1\neq r_2\]
The radii are NOT equal. Statement II is FALSE.
0 / 3

IIIStatement III — Is \(O\) equidistant from \(G_1\) and \(G_2\)?

Step 1 Distance \(OG_1\)

Since \(G_1=(-4,2)\) and \(O=(0,0)\), by the distance formula:

\[OG_1=\sqrt{(-4-0)^2+(2-0)^2}=\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}\approx 4.472\]
Step 2 Distance \(OG_2\)

Since \(G_2=(-2,1)\) and \(O=(0,0)\):

\[OG_2=\sqrt{(-2-0)^2+(1-0)^2}=\sqrt{4+1}=\sqrt{5}\approx 2.236\]
Step 3 Compare
\[OG_1=2\sqrt{5}=2\,\bigl(\sqrt{5}\bigr)=2\cdot OG_2\]

so \(OG_1\neq OG_2\).

Hint: Because the three points are collinear, \(OG_1=OG_2+OG_2=2OG_2\) — a quick way to see they cannot be equidistant from \(O\).
\(O\) is NOT equidistant from \(G_1\) and \(G_2\). Statement III is FALSE.
0 / 3

✓Conclusion

I. \(G_1,G_2,O\) collinear — TRUE
II. radii equal — FALSE
III. \(O\) equidistant from \(G_1,G_2\) — FALSE

Only statement I is true.

The answer is A. I only.

ΣSummary of Final Answers

  • \(G_1=(-4,\ 2)\)
  • \(G_2=(-2,\ 1)\)  (after dividing \(C_2\) by \(2\))
  • \(r_1=5\),   \(r_2=\sqrt{\dfrac{15}{2}}\approx 2.74\)
  • \(OG_1=2\sqrt{5}\),   \(OG_2=\sqrt{5}\)
  • Slopes: \(OG_1=-\tfrac12\), \(OG_2=-\tfrac12\) ⟹ collinear
  • Statement I is TRUE; Statements II & III are false.
  • Correct option: A. I only
