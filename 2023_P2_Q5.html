Question

If $c$ and $d$ are constants such that \[(x+2)(x+c)+12 \equiv x(x+d)+6c(x+1),\] then $d=$

A. $-13$
B. $-3$
C. $3$
D. $17$

Step-by-step Solution

1Expand the left-hand side
\[(x+2)(x+c)+12 = x^2+cx+2x+2c+12\] \[=x^2+(c+2)x+(2c+12)\]
Multiply out the two brackets, then collect like terms.
2Expand the right-hand side
\[x(x+d)+6c(x+1) = x^2+dx+6cx+6c\] \[=x^2+(6c+d)x+6c\]
Distribute $x$ and $6c$ separately, then group the $x$-terms.
3Compare coefficients of $x$ and the constant
Since it is an identity in $x$, corresponding coefficients must be equal: \[\begin{cases} c+2=6c+d & \cdots(1)\\[2pt] 2c+12=6c & \cdots(2)\end{cases}\]
Match the $x^2$ (both $1$ ✓), the $x$ terms, and the constants.
4Solve equation (2) for $c$
\[2c+12=6c \;\Rightarrow\; 12=4c \;\Rightarrow\; c=3\]
Collect $c$ on one side and divide.
5Substitute into (1) to find $d$
\[c+2=6c+d \;\Rightarrow\; 3+2=18+d\] \[d=5-18=-13\]
Substitute $c=3$ and rearrange for $d$.
6Alternative — substitute a value of $x$
Put $x=-\tfrac45$ (this makes $x+1=\tfrac15$, simplifying the RHS): \[\left(-\tfrac45+2\right)\left(-\tfrac45+c\right)+12 = -\tfrac45\left(-\tfrac45+d\right)+6c\left(\tfrac15\right)\] \[\tfrac{6}{5}\cdot\tfrac{5c-4}{5}+12=\tfrac{16}{25}-\tfrac{4d}{5}+\tfrac{6c}{5}\] With $c=3$: $\tfrac{6}{5}\cdot\tfrac{11}{5}+12 = \tfrac{66}{25}+\tfrac{300}{25}=\tfrac{366}{25}$ and $\tfrac{16}{25}-\tfrac{4d}{5}+\tfrac{18}{5}=\tfrac{16-20d+90}{25}$, giving $d=-13$.
Choose an $x$ that keeps the arithmetic clean.
7Check both sides
With $c=3,\;d=-13$: \[\text{LHS}=x^2+5x+18,\qquad \text{RHS}=x^2+(18-13)x+18=x^2+5x+18\ \checkmark\]
Verifying confirms the identity holds for all $x$.
Answer: A  —  $d=-13$
