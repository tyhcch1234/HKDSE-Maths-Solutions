HKDSE 2025 MC #44

The standard scores of a boy and a girl in a Mathematics test are $-2$ and $z$ respectively. The standard deviation of the scores of the Mathematics test is $2$ marks. If the difference of the test score of the boy and the test score of the girl is $6$ marks, find $z$.

  • A. $-5$ or $1$
  • B. $-5$ or $3$
  • C. $-3$ or $1$
  • D. $-3$ or $3$
STEP 1
Standard Score \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\) With \(\sigma=2\): \(\dfrac{b-\mu}{2}=-2\) and \(\dfrac{g-\mu}{2}=z\).
Let $b$ and $g$ be the test scores of the boy and the girl,
and let $\mu$ be the mean of the test scores.
The standard deviation is $\sigma = 2$.
Standard score $=\dfrac{\text{score}-\mu}{\sigma}$. Set up two equations using $\sigma=2$.
STEP 2
Boy: $\dfrac{b-\mu}{2}=-2 \;\Rightarrow\; b-\mu=-4 \;\Rightarrow\; b=\mu-4$
STEP 3
Girl: $\dfrac{g-\mu}{2}=z \;\Rightarrow\; g-\mu=2z \;\Rightarrow\; g=\mu+2z$
STEP 4
Difference of Two Scores \(b\) and \(g\) differ by \(6\): \(b-g=6\) or \(g-b=6\) Taking \(b-g=6\) gives \(z=-5\); taking \(g-b=6\) gives \(z=1\).
The two scores differ by $6$: $b-g=6$ or $g-b=6$
$b-g=6:\quad (\mu-4)-(\mu+2z)=6 \;\Rightarrow\; -4-2z=6 \;\Rightarrow\; z=-5$
$g-b=6:\quad (\mu+2z)-(\mu-4)=6 \;\Rightarrow\; 4+2z=6 \;\Rightarrow\; z=1$
“Difference” is not directional — take both orders.
STEP 5
Hence $z=-5$ or $z=1$, which matches option A.
Check: $z=-5$ means the girl scored $5$ s.d. below the mean; $z=1$ means $1$ s.d. above.
Answer: A  ($z = -5$ or $1$)

Question. The standard scores of a boy and a girl in a test are \(-1\) and \(z\). The standard deviation of the scores is \(3\) marks and the difference between their scores is \(9\) marks. Find \(z\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Boy: \(\dfrac{b-\mu}{3}=-1\Rightarrow b=\mu-3\).
Step 2. Girl: \(\dfrac{g-\mu}{3}=z\Rightarrow g=\mu+3z\).
Step 3. The two scores differ by \(9\), and \(b-g=(\mu-3)-(\mu+3z)=-3-3z\), so \(-3-3z=9\) or \(-3-3z=-9\).
Step 4. \(-3-3z=9\Rightarrow z=-4\), or \(-3-3z=-9\Rightarrow 3+3z=9\Rightarrow z=2\).
Answer: \(z=2\) or \(z=-4\)
