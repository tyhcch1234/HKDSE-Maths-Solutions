HKDSE Math 2012 Paper 2 Q7

Topic 9 · Inequalities and Linear Programming

Inequalities "or" logic

Question

The solution of 15 + 4x < 3 or 9 - 2x > 1 is
  1. $x < -3$
  2. $x < 4$
  3. $x > -3$
  4. $x > 4$

Note: The correct answer is B ($x < 4$).

Step-by-step solution

1

Separate the two inequalities

We have a compound inequality joined by "or". First, write down the two parts separately:

(1)   15 + 4x < 3
(2)   9 - 2x > 1

Hint: The word "or" means we solve each inequality independently, then combine the final solution sets.

2

Solve first inequality: 15 + 4x < 3

Subtract 15 from both sides:

4x < 3 - 15
4x < -12

Divide both sides by 4 (positive, so inequality sign stays the same):

x < -3

Key rule: Dividing/multiplying by a positive number keeps the direction of the inequality.

3

Solve second inequality: 9 - 2x > 1

Subtract 9 from both sides:

-2x > 1 - 9
-2x > -8

Divide both sides by -2. Remember: dividing by a negative flips the inequality sign!

x < 4

Common trap: Forgetting to flip the sign ⇒ would wrongly get x > 4.

4

Combine using "OR"

From steps 2 and 3:

x < -3     or     x < 4

The word "or" means any number that satisfies at least one of the inequalities.

On the number line, $x < -3$ is completely contained in $x < 4$. So the union is simply:

x < 4

✅ Key takeaway

For an "or" compound inequality, solve each part separately. Then take the union of the solution sets. Here, $x < -3$ is inside $x < 4$, so the final answer is $x < 4$ (option B).
