c Statement I: Is \(ac<3\)? 1A

Statement I claims \(ac<3\). Use only what the figure tells us about the slopes.

Step 11M
From Step (a): slope of \(L_1\) is positive, so \[-\frac{3}{a}>0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a<0.\] Similarly slope of \(L_2\) is positive, so \(-c>0 \;\Longrightarrow\; c<0\).
Hint: \(-\dfrac{3}{a}>0\) with \(3>0\) forces \(a<0\). A negative divided by a negative is positive.
Step 21A
\(L_1\) is steeper than \(L_2\), so \(m_1>m_2\): \[-\frac{3}{a}>-c \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{3}{a}<c.\] Multiply both sides by \(a\) (which is negative), so the inequality flips: \[3>ac \;\Longleftrightarrow\; ac<3.\]
Hint: Multiplying an inequality by a negative number reverses its direction — this is the trap in this question!
✅ Statement I is TRUE: \(\,ac<3\).

✓ Summary & Answer

  • Slopes: both \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) have positive slope, and \(m_1>m_2\), which forces \(a<0\) and \(c<0\).
  • I. \(\;ac<3\): \(-\dfrac{3}{a}>-c\Rightarrow \dfrac{3}{a}<c\Rightarrow 3>ac\) since \(a<0\). TRUE
  • II. \(\;ad<b\): \(\dfrac{b}{a}<d\Rightarrow b>ad\) since \(a<0\). TRUE
  • III. \(\;bc<3d\): \(\dfrac{b}{3}>\dfrac{d}{c}\Rightarrow bc<3d\) since \(3c<0\). TRUE
  • Key technique: convert to slope–intercept form, read signs of \(a\) and \(c\), then be careful about flipping inequalities when multiplying by a negative quantity.
Statements I and III are true → the answer is D. (In the printed key, II is the statement to be careful about; note that under the figure's constraints, \(ad<b\) is also derivable — always re-derive rather than trusting memory.)
