Coordinate Geometry — reading slopes & intercepts of two straight lines from a graph

Question2012MC #25

In the figure, the equations of the straight lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \[ax+y=b \qquad\text{and}\qquad cx+y=d\] respectively. Which of the following must be true?

I.  \(a < 0\)
II.  \(a < c\)
III.  \(b > d\)
IV.  \(ad > bc\)

A. I, II and III only
B. I, II and IV only
C. I, III and IV only
D. II, III and IV only

(2 marks · Multiple-choice — one correct option)

Part (a)Signs of \(a\) and \(c\) — Statements I & II

Rewrite each line in slope–intercept form to read off its slope and intercept.

a = −(−a) = −0.60 c = −1.30 a < c ✓ (since slopes: L₁ less steep than L₂ & both positive ⇒ a, c < 0)

Drag the sliders to keep both lines rising to the right. Both slopes stay positive, so \(a=-(\text{slope})<0\) and \(c<0\). Line \(L_1\) is the less steep one ⇒ \(a<c\).

1Reasoning Read the slope of each line

From the figure, both lines rise from left to right, so both slopes are positive.

\[ ax+y=b \;\Rightarrow\; y=-ax+b \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{slope of }L_1=-a \] \[ cx+y=d \;\Rightarrow\; y=-cx+d \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{slope of }L_2=-c \]

Hint: A line \(Ax+By=C\) has slope \(-A/B\). With \(B=1\), the slope is simply \(-\,(\)coefficient of \(x)\).
21A Deduce the signs of a and c

Slope of \(L_1\) is positive ⇒ \(-a>0\) ⇒ \(\boxed{a<0}\).   Statement I is true.

Slope of \(L_2\) is positive ⇒ \(-c>0\) ⇒ \(c<0\).

Hint: Statement I is confirmed directly — the figure shows an upward-sloping \(L_1\).
31A Compare a and c through the slopes

In the figure, \(L_2\) is steeper than \(L_1\): slope of \(L_2\) > slope of \(L_1\).

\[ -c > -a \;\Rightarrow\; c < a \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{a > c} \]

Therefore Statement II, \(a<c\), is false.

From Part (a): \(a<0\) is TRUE (I)  ·  \(a>c\) so \(a<c\) is FALSE (II).

Part (b)Intercepts \(b\) and \(d\) — Statement III

The \(y\)-intercepts of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \(b\) and \(d\) respectively.

b > d ✓ — the figure shows L₁ cutting the y-axis above L₂

Move the sliders. The diagram follows the question’s figure only when \(b>d\); that inequality is the one that must hold here.

1Reasoning Locate where each line meets the y-axis

Put \(x=0\):   \(L_1: y=b\),   \(L_2: y=d\).

In the figure, the \(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\) lies above the \(y\)-intercept of \(L_2\).

Hint: The constant term of \(y=mx+k\) is exactly the \(y\)-intercept.
21A Conclude the inequality

Hence \(\boxed{b>d}\).   Statement III is true.

From Part (b): \(b>d\) is TRUE (III).

Part (c)\(x\)-intercepts — Statement IV (\(ad>bc\))

The \(x\)-intercepts of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are \(\dfrac{b}{a}\) and \(\dfrac{d}{c}\) respectively.

x-int L₁ = b/a = −5.33 x-int L₂ = d/c = −1.08 b/a < d/c ⇒ ad > bc ✓

The figure shows \(L_1\) crossing the \(x\)-axis to the left of \(L_2\), i.e. \(b/a < d/c\). With \(a,c<0\) this flips into \(ad > bc\).

0 / 4
1Reasoning Find the x-intercepts

Set \(y=0\):   \(L_1: ax=b \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{b}{a}\);   \(L_2: cx=d \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{d}{c}\).

Hint: Both \(a\) and \(c\) are negative here, so the \(x\)-intercepts can be to the left of the \(y\)-axis even though \(b,d>0\).
21A Compare the x-intercepts from the figure

In the figure, the \(x\)-intercept of \(L_1\) is smaller than the \(x\)-intercept of \(L_2\):

\[ \frac{b}{a} < \frac{d}{c} \]

Hint: “Smaller” means further to the left on the \(x\)-axis.
31M Multiply by \(ac\) (careful: ac > 0)

Since \(a<0\) and \(c<0\), we have \(ac>0\). Multiplying an inequality by a positive number keeps its direction:

\[ \frac{b}{a}\cdot ac < \frac{d}{c}\cdot ac \quad\Longrightarrow\quad bc < ad \]

Hint: Never multiply by a negative quantity without reversing the inequality sign — checking \(ac>0\) first is the safe move.
41A State the conclusion

\[ bc < ad \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \boxed{ad>bc} \]

Statement IV is true.

From Part (c): \(ad>bc\) is TRUE (IV).

Summary & Answer

  • I. \(a<0\)  — TRUE (slope of \(L_1=-a>0\))
  • II. \(a<c\)  — FALSE (\(L_2\) steeper ⇒ \(-c>-a\) ⇒ \(a>c\))
  • III. \(b>d\)  — TRUE (\(y\)-intercept of \(L_1\) is higher)
  • IV. \(ad>bc\)  — TRUE (\(b/a<d/c\) and \(ac>0\))
  • True statements: I, III and IV  ⇒  Correct option: C
