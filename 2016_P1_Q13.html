13. In Figure 1, \(ABC\) is a triangle. \(D\), \(E\) and \(M\) are points lying on \(BC\) such that \(BD = CE\), \(\angle ADC = \angle AEB\) and \(DM = EM\).

(a) Prove that \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\). 2 marks

(b) Suppose that \(AD = 15\text{ cm}\), \(BD = 7\text{ cm}\) and \(DE = 18\text{ cm}\).

(i) Find \(AM\). 2 marks

(ii) Is \(\triangle ABE\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer. 3 marks

Reading the figure: The order along \(BC\) is \(B,\;D,\;M,\;E,\;C\). The condition \(DM=EM\) means \(M\) is the midpoint of \(DE\) — this is the key that unlocks part (b).

Part (a) Prove \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\) 2 marks

\(BD = CE =\) 7.0
\(DM = EM =\) 9.0
\(CD = BE =\) 16.0
\(AD = AE =\) 13.5
Explore: Drag the slider. \(BD=CE\) forces \(CD=BE\) automatically, and \(AD=AE\) always holds. So the two triangles share two sides and the included angle — the SAS condition never breaks.

1Establish the two given angle/side facts1M

From the question, in \(\triangle ACD\) and \(\triangle ABE\):

\[ \angle ADC = \angle AEB \quad (\text{given}) \] \[ BD = CE \quad (\text{given}) \]
Hint: Always write the reason next to each statement — HKDSE examiners award marks for correct reasons, not just statements.
2Show \(AD = AE\)1M

By part (a)'s conclusion we will get congruent triangles, but first notice that \(\angle ADC = \angle AEB\) means the exterior angles \(\angle ADB = \angle AEC\).

In \(\triangle ADE\), since \(\angle ADE = \angle AED\) (same angles as above, on the straight line \(BC\)),

\[ AD = AE \quad (\text{sides opposite equal } \angle s) \]
Hint: The phrase "sides opp. equal \(\angle\)s" (base angles equal → isosceles) is exactly what the official marking scheme uses here.
3Show \(CD = BE\)1M

Since \(BD = CE\), add the common length \(DE\) to both sides:

\[ CE + DE = BD + DE \] \[ CD = BE \]
Hint: This is the classic "add a common segment" trick. \(CD = CE + DE\) and \(BE = BD + DE\) because \(D, M, E\) lie on \(BC\).
4Conclude by SAS1A

We now have:

\[ AD = AE,\quad \angle ADC = \angle AEB,\quad CD = BE \]

These are two sides and the included angle of \(\triangle ACD\) and \(\triangle ABE\) respectively, therefore

\[ \boxed{\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE \quad (\text{SAS})} \]
Answer (a): \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\) by SAS.
Hint: Check the correspondence is \(A \leftrightarrow A\), \(C \leftrightarrow B\), \(D \leftrightarrow E\) — writing them in the correct order (or stating SAS) secures the answer mark.

Part (b)(i) Find \(AM\) 2 marks

\(DM = EM =\) 9.0
\(AD =\) 15.0 (fixed)
\(AM = \sqrt{AD^2 - DM^2} =\) 12.0
\(\angle AMD =\) 90°
Explore: Slide \(DM\). Because \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\), we get \(AD = AE\); combined with \(DM = EM\), the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BC\) must pass through \(M\). So \(\angle AMD = 90^\circ\) and Pythagoras applies.

1Locate \(M\) and get the half-length1M

Given \(DE = 18\text{ cm}\) and \(DM = EM\):

\[ DM = EM = \tfrac{1}{2}\times 18 = 9\text{ cm} \]
Hint: \(M\) is the midpoint of \(DE\) — that is precisely what \(DM = EM\) tells you.
2Show \(\angle AMD = 90^\circ\)1M

From part (a), \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\), hence \(AD = AE\).

Also \(DM = EM\). In \(\triangle AMD\) and \(\triangle AME\):

\[ AD = AE,\quad DM = EM,\quad AM = AM \text{ (common)} \] \[ \therefore \triangle AMD \cong \triangle AME \ (\text{SSS}) \]

Hence \(\angle AMD = \angle AME\). Since \(D, M, E\) are collinear, \(\angle AMD + \angle AME = 180^\circ\), so

\[ \angle AMD = \angle AME = 90^\circ \]
Hint: Two congruent triangles on a straight line force each angle to be \(90^\circ\). This is the standard "perpendicular from apex of isosceles triangle bisects the base" result.
3Apply Pythagoras in \(\triangle AMD\)1A
\[ AM = \sqrt{AD^2 - DM^2} = \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144} \] \[ \boxed{AM = 12\text{ cm}} \]
Answer (b)(i): \(AM = 12\text{ cm}\)
Hint: \(9\text{–}12\text{–}15\) is a \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) Pythagorean triple times \(3\) — memorising these saves exam time.

Part (b)(ii) Is \(\triangle ABE\) right-angled? 3 marks

\(BM = BD + DM = 7 + 9 =\) 16 cm
\(AB^2 = AM^2 + BM^2 =\) 400
\(AE = AD =\) 15 cm ⟹ \(AE^2 =\) 225
\(BE = BD + DE = 7 + 18 =\) 25 cm ⟹ \(BE^2 =\) 625
\(AB^2+AE^2 =\) 625 vs \(BE^2 =\) 625
Explore: Slide to compare the two quantities. They match exactly (\(625 = 625\)), so \(\angle BAE = 90^\circ\) by the converse of Pythagoras.

1Compute \(AB^2\) using the right angle at \(M\)1M

Since \(\angle AMB = 90^\circ\) (from (b)(i)) and \(BM = BD + DM = 7 + 9 = 16\text{ cm}\):

\[ AB^2 = AM^2 + BM^2 = 12^2 + 16^2 = 144 + 256 = 400 \]
Hint: \(12^2 + 16^2 = 20^2\), so \(AB = 20\text{ cm}\) — another \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) triple scaled by \(4\).
2Get \(AE\) from the congruence in (a)1M

From part (a), \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\), so corresponding sides are equal:

\[ AE = AD = 15\text{ cm} \]
Hint: The correspondence is \(D \leftrightarrow E\), so \(AD \leftrightarrow AE\). Don't confuse \(AE\) with \(AD\) — they happen to be equal here.
3Test the converse of Pythagoras1M

Find \(BE\) and compare:

\[ BE = BD + DE = 7 + 18 = 25\text{ cm} \Rightarrow BE^2 = 625 \] \[ AB^2 + AE^2 = 400 + 15^2 = 400 + 225 = 625 \] \[ \therefore AB^2 + AE^2 = BE^2 \]
Hint: Always square the longest side for comparison. Here \(BE = 25\) is the longest, so it plays the role of the hypotenuse.
4Conclude1A

Since \(AB^2 + AE^2 = BE^2\), by the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, the angle opposite the longest side \(BE\) is a right angle:

\[ \angle BAE = 90^\circ \] \[ \boxed{\text{Yes, } \triangle ABE \text{ is a right-angled triangle.}} \]
Answer (b)(ii): Yes. \(AB^2 + AE^2 = 400 + 225 = 625 = BE^2\), so \(\angle BAE = 90^\circ\).
Hint: The 1A mark is given only when you explicitly state which angle is the right angle (\(\angle BAE\)) and cite the converse of Pythagoras.

📘 Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\triangle ACD \cong \triangle ABE\)  —  SAS
    (using \(\angle ADC = \angle AEB\), \(AD = AE\), \(CD = BE\))
  • (b)(i) \(AM = \sqrt{15^2 - 9^2} = \sqrt{144} = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}\)
  • (b)(ii) Yes, \(\triangle ABE\) is right-angled at \(A\):
    \(AB^2 = 12^2 + 16^2 = 400\),   \(AE^2 = 15^2 = 225\),   \(BE^2 = 25^2 = 625\)
    \(AB^2 + AE^2 = 625 = BE^2 \Rightarrow \angle BAE = 90^\circ\) (converse of Pythagoras)
