Coordinate Geometry — Locus of points equidistant from two fixed points

Perpendicular Bisector Distance Formula Simultaneous Equations Locus

Question

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-3,\,1)\) and \((-7,\,-5)\) respectively. If \(P\) is a point lying on the straight line \(x-y+13=0\) such that \(AP=PB\), then the \(y\)-coordinate of \(P\) is (2024 MC Q25)
  • A.  \(-11\)
  • B.  \(-2\)
  • C.  \(2\)
  • D.  \(11\)

a Set up the equation for the line and the equidistant condition

Step 11M

Let \(P=(x,\,y)\). Since \(P\) lies on the straight line,

\[ x-y+13=0 \quad \cdots (1) \]
Hint: This line has slope \(1\) and \(y\)-intercept \(13\). Every point on it satisfies \(y=x+13\).
Step 21M

Given \(AP=PB\), apply the distance formula:

\[ [x-(-3)]^2+(y-1)^2=[x-(-7)]^2+[y-(-5)]^2 \] \[ (x+3)^2+(y-1)^2=(x+7)^2+(y+5)^2 \]
Hint: The locus of points equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\). Its equation is linear once the squares cancel.
Equation of the perpendicular bisector: \(\;2x+3y+16=0 \quad \cdots (2)\)
0 / 2

b Solve \((1)\) and \((2)\) for the coordinates of \(P\)

Step 11M

From \((1)\), \(\;y=x+13\). Substitute into \((2)\):

\[ 2x+3(x+13)+16=0 \] \[ 5x+55=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=-11 \]
Hint: Substitution is the fastest route because \((1)\) is already solved for \(y\).
Step 21A

Substitute \(x=-11\) back into \(y=x+13\):

\[ y=-11+13=2 \]
\(P=(-11,\,2)\), so the \(y\)-coordinate of \(P\) is \(\;2\).
Hint: Always verify by checking \(AP=PB\); the answer choice is C.
0 / 2

Summary of Answers

  • Line containing \(P\): \(\;x-y+13=0\)  \(\Rightarrow\)  \(y=x+13\)
  • Perpendicular bisector of \(AB\): \(\;2x+3y+16=0\)
  • Solving the two equations gives \(x=-11,\;y=2\)
  • Coordinates of \(P\): \(P=(-11,\;2)\)
  • Required \(y\)-coordinate: \(2\)  →  Answer C
