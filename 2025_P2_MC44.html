Statistics — Standard Scores, Mean & Standard Deviation

Standard Score (z-score) Mean μ Standard Deviation σ Difference of Scores MC Answer A

QThe Question

Read carefully — the standard deviation tells you everything about the scale.

The standard scores of a boy and a girl in a Mathematics test are \(-2\) and \(z\) respectively. The standard deviation of the scores of the Mathematics test is \(2\) marks. If the difference of the test score of the boy and the test score of the girl is \(6\) marks, find \(z\).
A. \(-5\) or \(1\)
B. \(-5\) or \(3\)
C. \(-3\) or \(1\)
D. \(-3\) or \(3\)

1Part (a) — Convert Standard Scores to Raw Scores

Let \(b\), \(g\) be the boy's and girl's test scores, and \(\mu\) the mean. Use the standard score formula.

Standard score formula: \[ z\text{-score} = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \qquad \text{where } x = \text{raw score},\ \mu = \text{mean},\ \sigma = \text{s.d.} \] Given: boy's z-score \(=-2\), girl's z-score \(=z\), \(\sigma = 2\).
Step 1 Write the standard score formula for the boy

The boy's raw score is \(b\), his standard score is \(-2\), and \(\sigma = 2\):

\[ \frac{b-\mu}{2} = -2 \]
Hint: A negative standard score means the boy scored below the mean — his score is \(\mu - 4\), which is less than \(\mu\). ✓
Step 2 Solve for \(b\) in terms of \(\mu\)
\[ \frac{b-\mu}{2} = -2 \;\Rightarrow\; b - \mu = -4 \;\Rightarrow\; b = \mu - 4 \]
Hint: Multiply both sides by 2 first, then isolate \(b\). Everything stays in terms of the unknown mean \(\mu\).
\[ b = \mu - 4 \]
Step 3 Write the standard score equation for the girl

The girl's raw score is \(g\), her standard score is \(z\), and \(\sigma = 2\):

\[ \frac{g-\mu}{2} = z \]
Hint: This time \(z\) is unknown, so the equation expresses \(g\) in terms of both \(\mu\) and \(z\).
Step 4 Solve for \(g\) in terms of \(\mu\) and \(z\)
\[ \frac{g-\mu}{2} = z \;\Rightarrow\; g - \mu = 2z \;\Rightarrow\; g = \mu + 2z \]
Hint: Now we have both scores expressed using the same mean \(\mu\). Next we use the difference condition.
\[ g = \mu + 2z \]

2Part (b) — Use the 6-Mark Difference and Solve for \(z\)

The scores of the boy and girl differ by 6, so \(|g-b|=6\). This gives two linear equations in \(z\).

Step 1 Set up the absolute-value condition

From Part (a): \(b = \mu - 4\) and \(g = \mu + 2z\). The difference is 6, so:

\[ |g - b| = 6 \] \[ (\mu + 2z) - (\mu - 4) = \pm 6 \]
Hint: The mean \(\mu\) cancels out immediately — that's why the answer doesn't depend on \(\mu\). Repeated terms cancel, leaving only \(z\).
Step 2 Simplify to \(2z+4 = \pm 6\)
\[ 2z + 4 = \pm 6 \]
Hint: Two cases: \(2z+4 = 6\) (girl scored higher) or \(2z+4 = -6\) (boy scored higher).
\[ 2z + 4 = 6 \quad \text{or} \quad 2z + 4 = -6 \]
Step 3 Solve both cases for \(z\)

Case 1: \[ 2z+4 = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; 2z = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; z = 1 \]

Case 2: \[ 2z+4 = -6 \;\Rightarrow\; 2z = -10 \;\Rightarrow\; z = -5 \]

Hint: Both values satisfy \(|2z+4|=6\) — don't throw either away! The question allows two answers, hence "−5 or 1".
\[ z = -5 \quad \text{or} \quad z = 1 \]
Step 4 Answer Match with the options

The pair \((z=-5,\; z=1)\) is exactly Option A.

📋 Final Answers at a Glance

  • Standard score formula: \(\displaystyle z = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\)
  • Boy's score: \(\displaystyle \frac{b-\mu}{2} = -2 \;\Rightarrow\; b = \mu - 4\)
  • Girl's score: \(\displaystyle \frac{g-\mu}{2} = z \;\Rightarrow\; g = \mu + 2z\)
  • Difference: \(\displaystyle |g-b| = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; |2z+4| = 6\)
  • Solve: \(2z+4 = 6\) or \(2z+4 = -6\)
  • \(z = 1\) or \(z = -5\)
  • Correct choice: A
