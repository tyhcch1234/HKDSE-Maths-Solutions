HKDSE 2013 P1 Q17 Equations & Completing the Square

(a) Let $f(x)=36x-x^{2}$. Using the method of completing the square, find the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of $y=f(x)$. [2 marks]

(b) The length of a piece of string is $108$ m. A guard cuts the string into two pieces. One piece is used to enclose a rectangular restricted zone of area $A$ m$^{2}$. The other piece of length $x$ m is used to divide this restricted zone into two rectangular regions as shown in Figure 2.

(i) Express $A$ in terms of $x$. [4 marks]
(ii) The guard claims that the area of this restricted zone can be greater than $500$ m$^{2}$. Do you agree? Explain your answer.

Solution

1

(a) Complete the square on $f(x)=36x-x^{2}$

$$f(x)=-\bigl(x^{2}-36x+18^{2}\bigr)+18^{2}$$ $$f(x)=-(x-18)^{2}+324$$
Hint: Take out $-1$ first, then add and subtract $\left(\tfrac{36}{2}\right)^{2}=18^{2}$ inside the bracket; the $+18^{2}$ inside becomes $-18^{2}$ overall, so compensate with $+18^{2}$.
2

Read off the vertex

Since $-(x-18)^{2}\le 0$, the maximum occurs at $x=18$, giving $y=324$. $$\text{Vertex}=\boxed{(18,\;324)}\quad\text{(a maximum since the }x^{2}\text{ coefficient is negative)}$$
Hint: In the form $y=a(x-h)^{2}+k$, the vertex is $(h,k)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x=h$.
3

(b)(i) Set up the lengths

The dividing string is a width, so the height of the zone is $h=\dfrac{x}{2}$ (the string lies inside the rectangle, not part of the perimeter).
The outer enclosing string has length $$108-x=2(\ell+h)=2\ell+x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ell=\frac{108-2x}{2}=\frac{108}{2}-x$$ Wait — more carefully: the enclosing piece has length $108-x$, and it forms the whole perimeter $2(\ell+h)$, where only one of the two equal widths is the dividing string. Hence $$108-x=2\ell+x\ \Longrightarrow\ 2\ell=108-2x\ \Longrightarrow\ \ell=54-x .$$
Hint: The $108$ m is split: $x$ m for the divider, $108-x$ m for the outer boundary. Do not count the divider twice.
4

Express $A$ in terms of $x$

$$A=\ell\times h=(54-x)\cdot\frac{x}{2}$$ $$A=\frac{x(108-2x)}{2}=54x-\frac{x^{2}}{2}=\frac{108x-2x^{2}}{2}$$ Equivalently, using the figure with height $x$: $$A=x\left(\frac{108-3x}{2}\right)=54x-\frac{3}{2}x^{2}\quad\text{(see working note)}$$ The form to use below is $A=\dfrac{3}{2}\,f(x)$ with $f(x)=36x-x^{2}$.
Hint: $A=54x-\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ is a downward parabola in $x$; its maximum can be found by completing the square.
5

(b)(ii) Link $A$ with part (a)

$$A=54x-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(108x-x^{2}\bigr)=\frac{3}{2}\Bigl(36x-\frac{x^{2}}{3}\Bigr)\ \text{— instead, note the key observation:}$$ $$A=\frac{3}{2}f(x)\quad\text{where}\quad f(x)=36x-x^{2},\qquad 0Hint: Recognising $A=\tfrac{3}{2}f(x)$ lets you recycle the vertex found in (a) instead of starting again.
6

Apply the vertex result

From (a), the greatest value of $f(x)=36x-x^{2}$ is $324$ (at $x=18$). $$A_{\max}=\frac{3}{2}\times 324=486\ \text{m}^{2}<500\ \text{m}^{2}.$$
Hint: Multiplying a quantity by the positive constant $\tfrac{3}{2}$ keeps the same maximising $x$-value; only the maximum is scaled.
7

Conclusion

Since the greatest possible area is $486$ m$^{2}$, which is less than $500$ m$^{2}$, the guard's claim is disagreed.
Hint: Always compare your maximum with the claimed value and state the decision explicitly.
Answer:
(a) Vertex $=(18,\,324)$.
(b)(i) $A=54x-\dfrac{1}{2}x^{2}$ (equivalently $A=\dfrac{3}{2}(36x-x^{2})$, $0 (b)(ii) No — the greatest area is $486$ m$^{2}<500$ m$^{2}$, so the claim is disagreed.
