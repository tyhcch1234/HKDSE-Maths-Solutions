HKDSE 2023 · Paper 1 · Q18

Formulas and Sequences

Suppose that $\alpha,\ 7,\ \beta$ is a geometric sequence, where $1<\alpha<\beta$.

(a) Express $\log_7 \alpha$ in terms of $\log_7 \beta$. [3 marks]

(b) If $\log_\beta \alpha,\ \log_7 \beta,\ \log_\alpha \beta$ is an arithmetic sequence, find the common difference of the arithmetic sequence. [5 marks]

1(a) Use the geometric-mean property
Product Law of Logarithms \(\log_{a}m+\log_{a}n=\log_{a}(mn)\) Adding \(\log_{7}\alpha\) and \(\log_{7}\beta\) turns the condition into \(\alpha\beta=49\).

Since $\alpha, 7, \beta$ is a geometric sequence, the middle term is the geometric mean:

$$7^2=\alpha\beta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha\beta=49 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha=\frac{49}{\beta}.$$

Hint: in a G.S. $x, y, z$ we always have $y^2 = xz$.
2Take $\log_7$ of both sides

$$\log_7 \alpha=\log_7\!\left(\frac{49}{\beta}\right)$$

$$=\log_7 49-\log_7\beta$$

$$=2-\log_7\beta.$$

Hint: $\log_7 49=\log_7 7^2=2$ and $\log\frac{A}{B}=\log A-\log B$.
3State the answer to (a)

$$\boxed{\log_7\alpha = 2-\log_7\beta}$$

Both $1<\alpha<\beta$ and $\alpha\beta=49$ guarantee this expression is valid.
4(b) Introduce a variable

Let $t=\log_7\beta$. From (a): $\ \log_7\alpha=2-t.$

Also $\log_7 7=1$ and $\beta=7^{t}$, $\alpha=7^{\,2-t}$.

Hint: rewriting everything in base 7 keeps the work tidy.
5Convert the other two logarithms

$$\log_\beta \alpha=\frac{\log_7\alpha}{\log_7\beta}=\frac{2-t}{t},$$

$$\log_\alpha \beta=\frac{\log_7\beta}{\log_7\alpha}=\frac{t}{2-t}.$$

So the arithmetic sequence is: $\ \dfrac{2-t}{t},\quad t,\quad \dfrac{t}{2-t}.$

Hint: change of base $\log_b a=\dfrac{\log_7 a}{\log_7 b}$.
6Apply the arithmetic-sequence condition
Condition for an Arithmetic Sequence \(2b=a+c\) The middle term twice equals the sum of the other two, which produces the equation in \(t\).

For an A.S. the middle term is the arithmetic mean:

$$2t=\frac{2-t}{t}+\frac{t}{2-t}.$$

Hint: $2\times(\text{2nd term})=(\text{1st term})+(\text{3rd term})$.
7Solve for $t$
Quadratic Formula \(x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\) Solving the resulting quadratic for \(t\) gives \(t=\sqrt{2}\), the only positive value.

Multiply both sides by $t(2-t)$:

$$2t^2(2-t)=(2-t)^2+t^2$$

$$4t^2-2t^3=4-4t+t^2+t^2$$

$$2t^3-2t^2-4t+4=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; t^3-t^2-2t+2=0$$

$$t^2(t-1)-2(t-1)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (t-1)(t^2-2)=0.$$

Since $1<\alpha<\beta$,
we have $\log_7\beta>1$, hence $t=\sqrt{2}$.

Hint: group in pairs — a classic factor-by-grouping cubic.
8Find the common difference $d$

$$d=t-\frac{2-t}{t}=\sqrt{2}-\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}$$

$$=\sqrt{2}-\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}-1\right)=\sqrt{2}-\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)=1.$$

Hint: $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt{2}$. The two surd terms cancel neatly.
9Check the answer

With $t=\sqrt2$: $\ \alpha=7^{\,2-\sqrt2}\approx 1.97$, $\ \beta=7^{\sqrt2}\approx 15.67$, and $\alpha\beta=7^2=49.$ ✓

The three terms are $\ \dfrac{2-\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2-1,\quad \sqrt2,\quad \dfrac{\sqrt2}{2-\sqrt2}=\sqrt2+1$,

which are indeed an A.S. with common difference $1$.

Answer: (a) $\ \log_7\alpha = 2-\log_7\beta$  ·  (b) common difference $d = 1$

Question. The three numbers \(\log_{2}x\), \(\log_{2}(x+6)\) and \(\log_{2}(x+18)\) are in arithmetic progression, where \(x>0\).
(a) Find \(x\).
(b) Find the common difference.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. For an arithmetic progression, \(2\log_{2}(x+6)=\log_{2}x+\log_{2}(x+18)\).
Step 2. Taking logs of one product: \(\log_{2}(x+6)^{2}=\log_{2}x(x+18)\), so \((x+6)^{2}=x(x+18)\).
Step 3. \(x^{2}+12x+36=x^{2}+18x\), so \(36=6x\) and \(x=6\).
Step 4. The terms are \(\log_{2}6\), \(\log_{2}12\) and \(\log_{2}24\), so \(d=\log_{2}12-\log_{2}6=\log_{2}2=1\).
Answer: (a) \(x=6\) (b) \(d=1\)
