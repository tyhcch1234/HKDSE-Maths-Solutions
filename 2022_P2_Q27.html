[2022 MC #27]

The coordinates of the points $P$ and $Q$ are $(10,\,-24)$ and $(17,\,-7)$ respectively. Let $C$ be the circle which passes through the origin, $P$ and $Q$. Which of the following is true?

  • A. $PQ$ is a diameter of $C$.
  • B. The area of $C$ is $196\pi$.
  • C. The point $(16,\,-9)$ lies inside $C$.
  • D. The centre of $C$ lies on the straight line $5x+12y=0$.
STEP 1 · FIND THE CENTRE OF $C$ — USE THE RIGHT ANGLE AT $O$

Let $O$ denote the origin. Compute the slopes:

$$m_{OQ}=\frac{-7-0}{17-0}=-\frac{7}{17},\qquad m_{PQ}=\frac{-7-(-24)}{17-10}=\frac{17}{7}$$

The product of the slopes is

$$m_{OQ}\cdot m_{PQ}=-\frac{7}{17}\times\frac{17}{7}=-1$$

Hence $OQ \perp PQ$, so $\angle OQP = 90^\circ$.

By the converse of the angle in a semi-circle, the side opposite the right angle, $OP$, must be a diameter of $C$.

✗ A: $PQ$ is not a diameter — $OP$ is. (The diameter is the side facing the right angle.)

STEP 2 · RADIUS & AREA OF $C$

Since $OP$ is a diameter:

$$OP=\sqrt{(10-0)^2+(-24-0)^2}=\sqrt{100+576}=\sqrt{676}=26$$

So the radius is

$$r=\frac{OP}{2}=\frac{26}{2}=13$$

and the area is

$$\text{Area of }C=\pi r^2=\pi(13)^2=169\pi$$

✗ B: area is $169\pi$, not $196\pi$.

STEP 3 · CENTRE OF $C$ = MID-POINT OF DIAMETER $OP$

The centre of a circle is the mid-point of any diameter. Taking diameter $OP$:

$$\text{Centre}=\left(\frac{0+10}{2},\;\frac{0+(-24)}{2}\right)=(5,\,-12)$$

So $C$ has centre $(5,-12)$ and radius $13$.

✗ D: put $(x,y)=(5,-12)$ into $5x+12y=0$:

$$\text{L.H.S.}=5(5)+12(-12)=25-144=-119\neq 0$$

So the centre does not lie on $5x+12y=0$.

STEP 4 · TEST THE POINT $(16,-9)$ AGAINST $C$

Distance from the centre $(5,-12)$ to the point $(16,-9)$:

$$d=\sqrt{(16-5)^2+(-9-(-12))^2}=\sqrt{11^2+3^2}=\sqrt{121+9}=\sqrt{130}$$

Compare with the radius $r=13=\sqrt{169}$:

$$\sqrt{130}<\sqrt{169}=13 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad dSince the distance from the centre is smaller than the radius, the point $(16,-9)$ lies inside $C$.

✓ C is TRUE.

Quick check: $OP$ is a diameter of length $26$, so any point inside has centre-distance $< 13$. Indeed $\sqrt{130}\approx 11.4 < 13$.
ANSWER: C  —  The point $(16,-9)$ lies inside $C$.
