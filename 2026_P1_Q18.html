Geometric Sequence & Logarithmic Sum

Question

Let \(G(n)\) be the \(n\)-th term of a geometric sequence. It is given that \(G(2)=12\) and \(G(5)=768\).

(a)   Find \(G(1)\). (2 marks)

(b)   Define \(T(n)=\log_2 G(n)\) for all positive integers \(n\). Find

(i)  \(T(n+1)-T(n)\),

(ii)  the greatest value of \(k\) such that \(T(1)+T(2)+T(3)+\cdots+T(k)<10^6\). (5 marks)

Solution

PART (a) — common ratio and first term
Common Ratio of a Geometric Sequence \(r^{3}=\dfrac{G(5)}{G(2)}\) \(\dfrac{768}{12}=64\), so \(r=4\) and \(G(1)=\dfrac{12}{4}=3\).
If the common ratio is \(r\): \[ r^3=\frac{G(5)}{G(2)}=\frac{768}{12}=64 \implies r=4 \] \[ G(1)=\frac{G(2)}{r}=\frac{12}{4}=3 \]
PART (b)(i) — difference of consecutive T terms
Logarithm of a Power \(\log_{2}4^{\,n-1}=2(n-1)\) \(T(n)=2n-2+\log_{2}3\), so \(T(n+1)-T(n)=2\) for every \(n\).
\[ G(n)=3\cdot 4^{\,n-1}=3\cdot 2^{\,2n-2} \implies T(n)=\log_2 G(n)=2n-2+\log_2 3 \] \[ T(n+1)-T(n)=\big[2(n+1)-2+\log_2 3\big]-\big[2n-2+\log_2 3\big]=2 \]
PART (b)(ii) — sum the arithmetic sequence
Sum of the First k Terms of an Arithmetic Sequence \(S(k)=\dfrac{k}{2}\left[2a+(k-1)d\right]\) With \(a=\log_{2}3\) and \(d=2\), \(S(k)=k^{2}+k(\log_{2}3-1)\), which stays below \(10^{6}\) at \(k=999\).
\(T(n)\) is arithmetic (first term \(T(1)=\log_2 3\), common difference 2): \[ S(k)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}T(n)=\frac{k}{2}\Big[2\log_2 3+2(k-1)\cdot... \Big] \] More directly,
since \(T(n)=2n+(\log_2 3-2)\): \[ S(k)=2\cdot\frac{k(k+1)}{2}+k(\log_2 3-2)=k^2-k+k\log_2 3=k^2+k(\log_2 3-1) \]
PART (b)(ii) — solve S(k) < 10⁶
With \(\log_2 3\approx 1.58496\): \[ k^2+0.58496\,k < 10^6 \] Since the left side is increasing in \(k\), test near \(k\approx 1000\): \[ k=1000:\; 10^6+584.96=1000584.96>10^6\ \ (\text{fails}) \] \[ k=999:\; 999^2+0.58496\times999=998001+584.38=998585.38<10^6\ \ (\text{OK}) \] \[ \therefore\ k_{\max}=999 \]
(a) \(G(1)=3\)    (b)(i) \(2\)    (b)(ii) \(k=999\)

Question. \(G(n)\) is a geometric sequence with \(G(2)=6\) and \(G(5)=162\). Let \(T(n)=\log_{3}G(n)\).
(a) Find \(G(1)\).
(b) Show that \(T(n)\) is an arithmetic sequence and state its common difference.
(c) Find the least value of \(k\) such that \(\sum_{n=1}^{k}T(n)>100\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(r^{3}=\dfrac{162}{6}=27\), so \(r=3\).
Step 2. \(G(1)=\dfrac{6}{3}=2\), so \(G(n)=2\cdot3^{\,n-1}\).
Step 3. \(T(n)=\log_{3}2+(n-1)\), so \(T(n+1)-T(n)=1\): the sequence is arithmetic with difference \(1\).
Step 4. \(S(k)=k\log_{3}2+\dfrac{k(k-1)}{2}\);
at \(k=14\), \(S\approx99.8<100\), and at \(k=15\), \(S\approx114.5>100\).
Step 5. Hence the least value is \(k=15\).
Answer: (a) \(G(1)=2\) (b) \(d=1\) (c) \(k=15\)
