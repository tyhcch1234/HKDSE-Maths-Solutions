Question

(a) Let $a$ and $p$ be the area and the perimeter of $\Delta CDE$ respectively. Denote the radius of the inscribed circle of $\Delta CDE$ by $r$. Prove that $pr=2a$.

[2 marks]

(b) The coordinates of the points $H$ and $K$ are $(9,12)$ and $(14,0)$ respectively. Let $P$ be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $OH$ is equal to the perpendicular distance from $P$ to $HK$, where $O$ is the origin. Denote the locus of $P$ by $\Gamma$.

(i) Describe the geometric relationship between $\Gamma$ and $\angle OHK$.

(ii) Using (a), find the equation of $\Gamma$.

[5 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 · Part (a) — Area by triangles from the in-centre
Area of a Triangle via the In-radius \(a=\dfrac{1}{2}rp,\quad\text{i.e. } pr=2a\) Joining the in-centre to \(C,D,E\) splits \(\triangle CDE\) into three triangles of height \(r\).

Let $I$ be the in-centre of $\Delta CDE$ and $r$ its in-radius.
Join $I$ to the three vertices $C,D,E$. The triangle splits into three triangles each of height $r$:

$$ a = \tfrac{1}{2}r\!\cdot\! CD + \tfrac{1}{2}r\!\cdot\! DE + \tfrac{1}{2}r\!\cdot\! CE $$ $$ a=\tfrac{r}{2}\,(CD+DE+CE) $$

Since $p=CD+DE+CE$ is the perimeter,
we get $2a=pr$, i.e. $pr=2a$. $\blacksquare$

Key idea: the perpendicular distance from the in-centre to every side equals the in-radius $r$.
STEP 2 · Part (b) — Set up with $O,H,K$

Place the points: $O(0,0)$, $H(9,12)$, $K(14,0)$. Since a point is equidistant from the two lines $OH$ and $HK$ (and interior to the angle),
the locus is the angle bisector of $\angle OHK$.

Also:

$$ OH=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=\sqrt{225}=15, \qquad HK=\sqrt{(14-9)^2+12^2}=\sqrt{25+144}=13 $$
STEP 3 · Area &
in-radius of $\Delta OHK$

Area of $\Delta OHK$ (base $OK=14$, height $=12$):

$$ \text{Area} = \frac{14\times 12}{2} = 84 $$

Perimeter: $p = OH+HK+KO = 15+13+14 = 42$. By part (a) with $a=84$:

$$ 42r = 2(84) \;\Rightarrow\; 42r=168 \;\Rightarrow\; r=4 $$
STEP 4 · Locate the in-centre $I$
Distance from a Point to a Line \(d=\dfrac{Ax_{1}+By_{1}+C}{\sqrt{A^{2}+B^{2}}}\) The in-centre \((h,4)\) is \(4\) units from \(12x+5y-168=0\). If the numerator is negative, take its opposite, since a distance is never negative: \(12h-148=52\) or \(12h-148=-52\).

The in-centre is at distance $r=4$ from the line $OK$ (the $x$-axis),
and it lies inside the triangle, so its $y$-coordinate is $4$. Let $I=(h,4)$.

Its distance to the side $HK$ must also be $4$. Line $HK$ through $(9,12)$ and $(14,0)$: slope $=-\tfrac{12}{5}$,
i.e. $12x+5y-168=0$.

$$ \frac{12h+5(4)-168}{13}=4 \quad\text{or}\quad \frac{12h+5(4)-168}{13}=-4 $$ $$ \;\Rightarrow\; 12h-148=52 \quad\text{or}\quad 12h-148=-52 $$

Inside the triangle we need $12h-148=-52$,
giving $12h=96$, so $h=8$. Hence $I=(8,4)$.

The other root $h=\tfrac{50}{3}$ is the ex-centre — outside the triangle.
STEP 5 · Equation of $\Gamma$
Point-Slope Form \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\) The bisector through \(H(9,12)\) and \(I(8,4)\) has slope \(\dfrac{12-4}{9-8}=8\).

$\Gamma$ is the line through $H(9,12)$ and the in-centre $I(8,4)$:

$$ \text{slope} = \frac{12-4}{9-8}=8 $$

Using point–slope form with $I(8,4)$:

$$ y-4 = 8(x-8) $$ $$ 8x-y-60=0 $$

So the locus is $\;\Gamma:\; 8x-y-60=0$.

Answers

(a) $pr=2a$ (proved).   (b)(i) $\Gamma$ is the angle bisector of $\angle OHK$.   (b)(ii) $\Gamma:\; 8x-y-60=0$.

(b) Using \(pr=2a\), find the equation of \(\Gamma\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(OH=\sqrt{5^{2}+12^{2}}=13\), \(HK=\sqrt{(14-5)^{2}+12^{2}}=15\) and \(KO=14\).
Step 2. (a) A point equidistant from \(OH\) and \(HK\) lies on the angle bisector of \(\angle OHK\),
so \(\Gamma\) is that angle bisector.
Step 3. (b) \(\text{area of }\triangle OHK=\dfrac{14\times 12}{2}=84\) and \(p=13+15+14=42\), so \(42r=2(84)\) and \(r=4\).
Step 4. The in-centre has \(y\)-coordinate \(4\);
line \(HK\) is \(4x+3y-56=0\), so \(\dfrac{4h-44}{5}=4\) or \(\dfrac{4h-44}{5}=-4\), giving \(h=16\) or \(h=6\);
only \(h=6\) is inside the triangle.
Thus \(I=(6,4)\).
Step 5. Slope of \(HI=\dfrac{4-12}{6-5}=-8\), so \(y-4=-8(x-6)\), i.e. \(8x+y-52=0\).
Answer: (a) \(\Gamma\) is the angle bisector of \(\angle OHK\); (b) \(8x+y-52=0\)
