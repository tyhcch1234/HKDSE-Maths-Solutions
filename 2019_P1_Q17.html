Coordinate geometry — the locus of a point equidistant from two intersecting lines is the angle bisector.

Angle Bisector Incentre & Inradius Area = ½ · perimeter · r Locus Equation Distance Formula

The Question

(a) Let \(a\) and \(p\) be the area and the perimeter of \(\Delta CDE\) respectively. Denote the radius of the inscribed circle of \(\Delta CDE\) by \(r\). Prove that \(pr = 2a\). [2 marks]

(b) The coordinates of the points \(H\) and \(K\) are \((9, 12)\) and \((14, 0)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(OH\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(HK\), where \(O\) is the origin. Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).

(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(\angle OHK\). [1 mark]

(ii) Using (a), find the equation of \(\Gamma\). [4 marks]

Step 11M

Join the incentre \(I\) of \(\Delta CDE\) to the three vertices, splitting the triangle into three smaller triangles, each with height equal to the inradius \(r\) (since \(r\) is perpendicular to each side).

Hint: Area of each sub-triangle \(=\frac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac12 \times \text{side} \times r\).
Step 21M

Summing the three sub-triangle areas gives the whole area \(a\): \[ a = \tfrac12 CD\cdot r + \tfrac12 DE\cdot r + \tfrac12 CE\cdot r = \tfrac{r}{2}\,(CD+DE+CE). \]

Hint: Factor out \(\frac12 r\).
Step 31A

Since \(p = CD + DE + CE\), we get \(a = \frac12 pr\), i.e. \[\boxed{\,pr = 2a\,}\]

Proved: \(pr = 2a\). Therefore the inradius is \(r = \dfrac{2a}{p}\).

\[ OH = \sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = 15, \qquad OK = 14, \] \[ HK = \sqrt{(14-9)^2 + (0-12)^2} = \sqrt{25+144} = 13. \]

Step 21M

Perimeter and area of \(\Delta OHK\):

\[ p = 15 + 13 + 14 = 42, \qquad a = \tfrac12 \times 14 \times 12 = 84. \]
Hint: \(OK\) lies on the \(x\)-axis, so height from \(H\) is \(12\).
Step 31M

By part (a), \(pr = 2a\): \[ 42r = 2(84) = 168 \;\Rightarrow\; r = 4. \] Let \(I = (h, 4)\) be the incentre. Since \(I\) lies on the angle bisector from \(K\) to \(OH\) (distance \(4\) from \(OK\), the \(x\)-axis), and the distance from \(I\) to \(OH\) is also \(4\): \[ \text{distance from } (h,4) \text{ to } OH: \frac{|4h - 3(4)|}{5} = 4. \] Using the inner bisector: \(4h - 12 = 20 \Rightarrow h = 8\). So \(I = (8,4)\).
