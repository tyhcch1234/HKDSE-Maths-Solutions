The sum of the 2nd term and the 5th term of a geometric sequence is 9, while the sum of the 7th term and the 10th term of the sequence is 288. Find the 20th term of the sequence.

A. 65 536
B. 131 072
C. 262 144
D. 524 288

Step-by-step Solution

1Set up the general term
Let $a$ be the first term and $r$ be the common ratio of the geometric sequence. Then the $n$-th term is $$T(n)=ar^{\,n-1}.$$
Hint: always name the two unknowns — here $a$ and $r$ — before translating the given sentences into equations.
2Translate the two conditions
Sum of 2nd and 5th terms is 9: $$ar+ar^{4}=9 \quad \cdots (1)$$ Sum of 7th and 10th terms is 288: $$ar^{6}+ar^{9}=288 \quad \cdots (2)$$
Hint: watch the exponents — the $n$-th term has power $n-1$, so the 2nd term is $ar^1$ and the 5th term is $ar^4$.
3Divide (2) by (1)
Both sides of (2) are divisible by $r^5$: $$(2)\div(1):\quad \frac{ar^{6}+ar^{9}}{ar+ar^{4}} =\frac{r^{5}(ar+ar^{4})}{ar+ar^{4}}=r^{5}.$$ Hence $$r^{5}=\frac{288}{9}=32 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=2.$$
Hint: the key trick — terms 7 and 10 are exactly terms 2 and 5 multiplied by $r^{5}$, so the ratio of the two sums gives $r^5$.
4Find the first term $a$
Substitute $r=2$ into (1): $$a(2)+a(2)^{4}=9$$ $$2a+16a=9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 18a=9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=\tfrac{1}{2}.$$
Hint: substitute back into the simpler equation to avoid arithmetic slips.
5Compute the 20th term
$$T(20)=ar^{19}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{19}=2^{18}=262\,144.$$
Hint: $\tfrac12 \cdot 2^{19}=2^{-1}\cdot 2^{19}=2^{18}$. Alternatively $T(20)=r^{15}\cdot T(5)$.
Answer C. 262 144

Check: with $a=\tfrac12$, $r=2$, the sequence begins $\tfrac12,\,1,\,2,\,4,\,8,\dots$; terms 2 and 5 give $1+8=9$ ✓, terms 7 and 10 give $32+256=288$ ✓.
