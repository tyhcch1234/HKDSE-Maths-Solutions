[2023 MC #32]
The L.C.M. of $a^4b^2c$, $a^3b^4c$ and $a^2b^5c^2$ is
  • A. $a^2b^2c$.
  • B. $a^2b^2c^2$.
  • C. $a^4b^5c$.
  • D. $a^4b^5c^2$.
1Write every expression in full prime-factor-style form

Each term is a monomial, so expose the (possibly hidden) power 1 on each factor:

$$a^4b^2c = a^4b^2c^1$$ $$a^3b^4c = a^3b^4c^1$$ $$a^2b^5c^2 = a^2b^5c^2$$
Hint: writing $c$ as $c^1$ makes it easy to compare all three side by side.
Index Notation \(c=c^{1}\) Writing every factor with an explicit index lets the three monomials be compared term by term.
2Compare the power of each factor one at a time
factor1st term2nd term3rd term
$a$432
$b$245
$c$112
Hint: for a common multiple, every term must divide it — so each factor needs at least the biggest power appearing.
3Pick the largest power of each factor

For each factor, the power in the L.C.M. is the largest power among the three expressions:

$a:\ \max(4,3,2) = 4$

$b:\ \max(2,4,5) = 5$

$c:\ \max(1,1,2) = 2$

Hint: the L.C.M. must be divisible by all three; the largest powers guarantee that.
L.C.M. of Monomials \(\mathrm{L.C.M.}\left(a^{p},\,a^{q}\right)=a^{\max(p,q)}\) A common multiple must contain the highest power of each factor that appears.
4Combine the chosen powers

Multiply the largest powers together to form the L.C.M.:

$$\therefore\ \text{L.C.M.} = a^4b^5c^2$$
Hint: an L.C.M. of monomials = product of the highest powers of all factors present.
Answer: D  —  $a^4b^5c^2$

Question. Find the L.C.M. of \(a^{3}b^{2}c^{4}\), \(a^{5}b^{2}c\) and \(a^{2}b^{6}c^{3}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write each term with explicit indices: \(a^{3}b^{2}c^{4}\), \(a^{5}b^{2}c^{1}\), \(a^{2}b^{6}c^{3}\).
Step 2. Compare the powers factor by factor: \(a:\ 3,5,2\); \(b:\ 2,2,6\); \(c:\ 4,1,3\).
Step 3. Take the largest power of each factor: \(a^{5}\), \(b^{6}\), \(c^{4}\).
Step 4. L.C.M. \(=a^{5}b^{6}c^{4}\).
Answer: \(a^{5}b^{6}c^{4}\)
