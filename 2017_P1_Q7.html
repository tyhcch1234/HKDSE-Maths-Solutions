Statistics — Pie Chart, Percentage & Probability of a Sector
Pie Chart Angles of a Sector Probability Proportion

Q The Question

The pie chart below shows the distribution of the seasons of birth of the students in a school.

Pie chart: Autumn (upper-left quadrant), Summer (top-right, labelled \(158^\circ\)), Spring (bottom-right, labelled \(x\)), Winter (bottom-left). A right-angle mark (\(90^\circ\)) is shown at the centre between Autumn and Winter.

If a student is randomly selected from the school, then the probability that the selected student was born in Spring is \(\dfrac{1}{9}\).

  1. Find \(x\).
  2. In the school, there are \(180\) students born in winter. Find the number of students in the school.
[4 marks]

a Part (a) — Finding \(x\)

80°
Autumn–Winter angle = 90° Summer = 158° Spring \(x\) = 80° Winter = 32° Sum = 360°
Step 11M
The probability that a randomly chosen student was born in Spring equals the fraction of the circle occupied by the Spring sector: \[ P(\text{Spring})=\frac{x}{360}. \]
Hint: In a pie chart the angle of a sector is directly proportional to the frequency it represents — so the sector fraction \(\frac{\text{angle}}{360^\circ}\) is the probability.
Step 21M
Given \(P(\text{Spring})=\dfrac{1}{9}\), set up the equation: \[ \frac{x}{360}=\frac{1}{9}. \]
Hint: Always state the probability as a fraction of \(360^\circ\) first; then the algebra is a simple one-step equation.
Step 31A
Solve for \(x\): \[ x=\frac{360}{9}=40. \]
∴ \(x=40^\circ\)
Hint: Check: \(40^\circ\) is \(\frac{1}{9}\) of \(360^\circ\). ✓ The remaining Winter sector is \(360-90-158-40=72^\circ\), which is exactly the angle we use in part (b).
0 / 3

b Part (b) — Total number of students

180
Winter angle = 72° Fraction of Winter = 72/360 = 1/5 Total students \(n\) = 900
Step 11M
Find the Winter sector angle using the \(x=40^\circ\) from part (a): \[ \text{Winter}=360-90-158-40=72^\circ. \]
Hint: All four sector angles must add up to \(360^\circ\). Subtract the three known angles from \(360^\circ\).
Step 21M
Let \(n\) be the number of students in the school. The Winter sector is a fraction \(\frac{72}{360}\) of the whole, so: \[ \frac{n}{360}=\frac{180}{72}. \]  (Equivalently, \(\dfrac{n}{180}=\dfrac{360}{72}\).)
Hint: The ratio of "students : students" must equal the ratio of "angles : angles": \(\dfrac{\text{total students}}{180}=\dfrac{360^\circ}{72^\circ}\).
Step 31A
Simplify the fraction \(\dfrac{180}{72}=\dfrac{5}{2}\) and solve: \[ \frac{n}{360}=\frac{5}{2}\;\Longrightarrow\; n=\frac{5}{2}\times 360 = 180\times 5 = 900. \] Alternatively, \(\dfrac{n}{180}=\dfrac{360}{72}=5\), so \(n=180\times 5=900\).
∴ The number of students in the school is \(900\).
Hint: A common mistake is to write \(\frac{72}{360}=\frac{180}{n}\) upside-down. Check: \(72^\circ\) is a small sector, so \(180\) students must be a small fraction of \(n\) — and \(180/900=1/5=72/360\). ✓
0 / 3

Σ Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(x = 40^\circ\)  (the Spring sector angle)
  • (b) The number of students in the school is \(900\).
  • Key relation used: \(\dfrac{\text{sector angle}}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}}\)  (equivalently the sector's probability).
