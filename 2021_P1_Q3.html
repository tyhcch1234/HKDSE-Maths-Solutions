2021 Paper 1 · Question 3 — Factorization of Polynomials

[3 marks] · Topic 4: Polynomials
Factorization Grouping Terms Common Factor Difference of Structure (a) Cross Method

QQuestion

Factorize

(a) \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\)
(b) \(8x-4y-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}\)

APart (a) — \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\)

This is a quadratic in two variables. Treat it as a quadratic in \(x\), and factorize the coefficient pattern.

Cross method visual

We want two numbers multiplying to \(6\) and two more multiplying to \(-2\) such that the cross terms give \(1\).

\(2\)×\(3\) =6
\(-1\)×\(2\) =-2
cross: \(2\times 2 + 3\times(-1) = 4-3 = \mathbf{1}\) ✔

Check by expansion

$$(2x-y)(3x+2y)$$ $$=2x(3x+2y)-y(3x+2y)$$ $$=6x^{2}+4xy-3xy-2y^{2}$$ $$=6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\;\checkmark$$

The expression \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\) is a homogeneous quadratic in \(x\) and \(y\). Every term has total degree \(2\): \((x^{2}, xy, y^{2})\).

Hint — Cross Method: For \(ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}\), try \((mx+ny)(px+qy)\) where \(mp=a\), \(nq=c\) and \(mq+np=b\).

Split \(6=mp\) and \(-2=nq\). Testing the combination that gives the middle term \(1xy\):

$$(2x-y)(3x+2y)$$

Indeed: \(2\times 2 + 3\times(-1) = 1\), matching the \(+xy\) term.

Answer (a): \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}=(2x-y)(3x+2y)\) 1A

BPart (b) — \(8x-4y-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}\)

Compare the new expression with part (a):

$$8x-4y-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}$$

Rearrange the last three terms:

$$=\;(8x-4y)\;+\;(-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2})$$

The bracket \(-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}\) is the negative of the part (a) expression \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}\). So part (a) is reusable here!

Hint — Grouping: \(8x-4y\) shares the common factor \(4(2x-y)\), and part (a) already gave \((2x-y)\) as a factor.

Factorize \(8x-4y\):

$$8x-4y = 4(2x-y)$$

And using part (a):

$$6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}=(2x-y)(3x+2y)$$

So \(-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}=-(2x-y)(3x+2y)\).

Substitute everything back:

$$8x-4y-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}$$ $$=\;\underbrace{8x-4y}_{\text{group 1}}\;-\;\underbrace{(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2})}_{\text{from (a)}}$$ $$=\;4(2x-y)\;-\;(2x-y)(3x+2y)$$
Hint — Exact values: \(8x-4y=4(2x-y)\)  and  \(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2}=(2x-y)(3x+2y)\). Here the common binomial factor is \((2x-y)\).

Both terms now contain \((2x-y)\):

$$=\;(2x-y)\bigl[\,4-(3x+2y)\,\bigr]$$

Simplify inside the square bracket:

$$4-(3x+2y)=4-3x-2y$$

Therefore:

$$\mathbf{(2x-y)(4-3x-2y)}$$
Answer (b): \((2x-y)(4-3x-2y)\)  1A
The step \(4(2x-y)-(2x-y)(3x+2y)\) earns 1M.

Expand \((2x-y)(4-3x-2y)\) to confirm:

$$=2x(4-3x-2y)-y(4-3x-2y)$$ $$=8x-6x^{2}-4xy-4y+3xy+2y^{2}$$ $$=8x-4y-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}\;\checkmark$$
Expanding correctly is a great self-check — if you get back the original expression, your factorization is almost certainly correct.

!Common mistakes to avoid

MistakeWhy it is wrong / what to do instead
Forgetting the minus sign in part (b) \(-6x^{2}-xy+2y^{2}=-(6x^{2}+xy-2y^{2})\). It is not just \(-(2x-y)(3x+2y)\) — always keep the leading minus attached to the whole bracket.
Stopping at \(4(2x-y)-(2x-y)(3x+2y)\) You must continue and take out the common binomial \((2x-y)\). That final step is where the 1A mark usually lies.
Sign errors when removing brackets \(4-(3x+2y)=4-3x-2y\), not \(4-3x+2y\). Distribute the minus to both terms inside.
Using the wrong cross pair for (a) Always re-expand mentally: the cross terms must sum to the middle coefficient \(+1\).
