[2022MC#29]

The mean weight of 60 actors and 40 actresses is 57 kg. If the mean weight of the actors is 63 kg, then the mean weight of the actresses is

  • A. 48 kg.
  • B. 50 kg.
  • C. 53 kg.
  • D. 60 kg.

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Identify the groups & set up the unknown

Total people: $60+40=100$ acting as one group with mean $57$ kg.
Actors: $n_A=60$, mean $63$ kg. Actresses: $n_B=40$, mean $x$ kg (unknown).

Hint: Weighted-mean problems are usually easiest by comparing totals of weights.
Step 2Total weight of ALL 100 people
Total of a Data Set from its Mean \(\text{total}=\text{mean}\times n\) All \(100\) people have mean \(57\) kg, so their total weight is \(5700\) kg.

$$\text{Total}=57\times(60+40)=57\times100=5700\ \text{kg}.$$

Hint: Total weight = mean weight × number of people.
Step 3Total weight of the 60 actors

$$63\times60=3780\ \text{kg}.$$

Hint: This chunk of the total is already known.
Step 4Weight of the actresses
Weighted Mean \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{n_{1}\bar{x}_{1}+n_{2}\bar{x}_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}\) \(63(60)+40x=57(100)\), so the actresses weigh \(5700-3780=1920\) kg in total.

$$\text{Actresses' total}=5700-3780=1920\ \text{kg}.$$ Equivalently: $63\cdot60+x\cdot40=57\cdot(60+40)$.

Hint: Subtract the actors' total from the grand total.
Step 5Find the mean of the actresses
Balancing Method for a Linear Equation \(ax=b\ \Rightarrow\ x=\dfrac{b}{a}\quad(a\neq0)\) \(40x=1920\) gives \(x=48\) kg.

$$40x=1920 \;\Rightarrow\; x=\frac{1920}{40}=48\ \text{kg}.$$

Hint: Divide the actresses' total weight by 40.
Step 6Check & conclude

Combined mean check: $\dfrac{63\cdot60+48\cdot40}{100}=\dfrac{3780+1920}{100}=\dfrac{5700}{100}=57$ kg ✓
Since $x=48<57$, this is consistent (actresses pull the average down).

Hint: The answer 48 kg matches option A; the "60" trap is the number of actors.
Answer: A  —  $48$ kg
