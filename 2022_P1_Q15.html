Boys (10) Girls (12) Chosen committee (4)

Step 1 — The class: 10 boys + 12 girls = 22 students.

HKDSE 2022 Paper 1 — Question 15

There are 10 boys and 12 girls in a class. If 4 students are randomly selected from the class to form a committee,

(a) find the probability that there are 2 boys and 2 girls in the committee. [2 marks]

(b) find the probability that the number of boys and the number of girls in the committee are different. [2 marks]

Solution

Step 1
1Count the whole class.

Total students: $10+12=22$. We choose $4$ students, so the total number of equally likely committees is

$$N=\binom{22}{4}=\frac{22\cdot21\cdot20\cdot19}{4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1}=7315 .$$
Always write down the sample space first — that is the denominator of every probability here.
Step 2 (a)
2Favourable outcomes for (a): 2 boys from 10 and 2 girls from 12.

Choosing is simultaneous, so multiply the two combinations:

$$\binom{10}{2}\binom{12}{2}=\frac{10\cdot9}{2}\cdot\frac{12\cdot11}{2}=45\times66=2970 .$$

Hence

$$P(2\text{ boys},\,2\text{ girls})=\frac{\binom{10}{2}\binom{12}{2}}{\binom{22}{4}} =\frac{2970}{7315}=\frac{54}{133}\approx0.4060 .$$
"AND" between independent selections $\Rightarrow$ multiply the combinations.
Step 3 (a) — alternative
3The same answer using ordered picks (with rearrangements).

Picking 4 students in order as B, B, G, G gives $\dfrac{10}{22}\cdot\dfrac{9}{21}\cdot\dfrac{12}{20}\cdot\dfrac{11}{19}$; the boys and girls may be arranged in $\dfrac{4!}{2!\,2!}=6$ ways, so

$$6\left(\frac{10}{22}\right)\left(\frac{9}{21}\right)\left(\frac{12}{20}\right)\left(\frac{11}{19}\right) =\frac{54}{133}\approx0.4060 .$$
Both methods are accepted in HKDSE — ordered counting needs the $\dfrac{4!}{2!2!}$ factor.
Step 4 (b)
4Number of boys $\ne$ number of girls.

With 4 people, "boys = girls" happens only for $2$ boys & $2$ girls. So use the complementary event:

$$P(\text{different})=\;1-P(2\text{ boys},\,2\text{ girls})\;=\;1-\frac{54}{133}=\frac{133-54}{133}=\frac{79}{133}\approx0.5940 .$$
Complement is fastest: "different" = everything except the equal case found in (a).
Step 5 (b) — direct check
5Adding the three other cases verifies the answer.

The committee has $0,1,2,3$ boys with probabilities

$$\frac{\binom{10}{0}\binom{12}{4}}{\binom{22}{4}}+\frac{\binom{10}{1}\binom{12}{3}}{\binom{22}{4}}+\frac{\binom{10}{2}\binom{12}{2}}{\binom{22}{4}}+\frac{\binom{10}{3}\binom{12}{1}}{\binom{22}{4}}+\frac{\binom{10}{4}\binom{12}{0}}{\binom{22}{4}} .$$

Keeping only the cases with different numbers of boys and girls ($0,1,3,4$ boys):

$$\frac{495}{7315}+\frac{2640}{7315}+\frac{1980}{7315}+\frac{210}{7315}=\frac{5325}{7315}=\frac{79}{133}\approx0.5940 . \checkmark$$
Adding over all disjoint cases must total $1$ — a good self-check.
Answer: (a) $\dfrac{54}{133}$   (b) $\dfrac{79}{133}$
