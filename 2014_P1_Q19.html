QQuestion

Ada and Billy play a game consisting of two rounds. In the first round, Ada and Billy take turns to throw a fair die. The player who first gets a number ‘3’ wins the first round. Ada and Billy play the first round until one of them wins. Ada throws the die first.

  1. Find the probability that Ada wins the first round of the game. [3 marks]
  2. In the second round of the game, balls are dropped one by one into a device containing eight tubes arranged side by side (see Figure 8). When a ball is dropped into the device, it falls randomly into one of the tubes. Each tube can hold at most three balls.
    Figure 8
    The player of this round adopts one of the following two options.
    • Option 1: Two balls are dropped one by one into the device. If the two balls fall into the same tube, then the player gets 10 tokens. If the two balls fall into two adjacent tubes, then the player gets 5 tokens. Otherwise, the player gets no tokens.
    • Option 2: Three balls are dropped one by one into the device. If the three balls fall into the same tube, then the player gets 50 tokens. If the three balls fall into three adjacent tubes, then the player gets 10 tokens. If the three balls fall into two adjacent tubes, then the player gets 5 tokens. Otherwise, the player gets no tokens.
    1. If the player of the second round adopts Option 1, find the expected number of tokens got.
    2. Which option should the player of the second round adopt in order to maximize the expected number of tokens got? Explain your answer.
    3. Only the winner of the first round plays the second round. It is given that the player of the second round adopts the option which can maximize the expected number of tokens got. Billy claims that the probability of Ada getting no tokens in the game exceeds \(0.9\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. [10 marks]
    3. >

1Warm-up: Mutually Exclusive Events

Ada and Billy take turns. For the game to end at a given throw, every earlier throw must have failed (i.e. not rolled a 3). The "Ada wins on her 2nd throw" event, the "Ada wins on her 3rd throw" event, … are mutually exclusive, so we simply add their probabilities.

Probability of rolling a 3: \(\dfrac{1}{6}\)  •  Probability of not rolling a 3: \(\dfrac{5}{6}\).

Notice how each successive term is the previous term multiplied by \(\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{36}\) — a geometric series!

2Step-by-Step Solution

Ada can win the first round on her 1st, 2nd, 3rd, … throw. For Ada to win on her \(n\)-th throw:

  • Ada fails \((n-1)\) times: each with probability \(\frac{5}{6}\)
  • Billy fails \(n\) times (Billy throws after each of Ada's first \(n\) throws except Ada's last): each with probability \(\frac{5}{6}\)
  • Ada throws a 3: probability \(\frac{1}{6}\)
Hint (Symmetry idea): Ada goes first. Because the die is fair and the outcomes are independent, Ada has a slight built-in advantage — her answer should be a little more than \(\frac12\).

Ada winning on her 1st throw: \(\;\frac{1}{6}\)

Ada winning on her 2nd throw: \(\;\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)=\frac{5^2}{6^3}\) (Ada fails, Billy fails, Ada hits)

Ada winning on her 3rd throw: \(\;\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^4\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)=\frac{5^4}{6^5}\)

Required probability \(=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{6}\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^2+\dfrac{1}{6}\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^4+\cdots\)
Hint — Formula used: Sum of an infinite geometric series \(S=\dfrac{a}{1-r}\) with first term \(a=\frac16\) and common ratio \(r=\left(\frac56\right)^2=\frac{25}{36}\).
$$\text{Required probability}=\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{25}{36}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{25}{36}\right)^2+\cdots$$ $$=\frac{\frac{1}{6}}{1-\frac{25}{36}}=\frac{\frac{1}{6}}{\frac{11}{36}}=\frac{6}{11}$$
(a) Answer: \(\dfrac{6}{11}\approx 0.545\) (check: > ½ ✓)

Each ball falls independently into one of 8 tubes. Two balls = \(8\times 8 = 64\) equally likely ordered outcomes.

  • Same tube (10 tokens): ball 2 must land in ball 1's tube → \(8\) outcomes.
  • Adjacent tubes, different tubes (5 tokens): ordered choices of two adjacent tubes → \(2\times 7 = 14\) outcomes.
  • Otherwise (0 tokens): the remaining \(64-8-14 = 42\) outcomes.
Hint: Probability \(=\dfrac{\text{number of favourable outcomes}}{64}\). Always divide by all equally likely outcomes, not just the scoring ones.
$$P(10\text{ tokens})=(1)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)=\frac{1}{8}=\frac{8}{64}$$ $$P(5\text{ tokens})=\frac{(7)(2)}{8^2}=\frac{14}{64}=\frac{7}{32}$$
Expected number of tokens \(=(10)\left(\dfrac{1}{8}\right)+(5)\left(\dfrac{7}{32}\right)=\dfrac{10}{8}+\dfrac{35}{32}=\dfrac{75}{32}\)
Hint — Formula used: \(E(X)=\sum x\cdot P(X=x)\). Only the scoring outcomes contribute; "0 tokens" contributes \(0\) so it may be omitted.

Three balls → \(8^3 = 512\) equally likely ordered outcomes.

$$P(50)=\frac{8}{8^3}=(1)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)=\frac{1}{64}$$ $$P(10)=\frac{(6)(^3P_2)}{8^3}=\frac{6\times 6}{512}=\frac{9}{128}$$

Reasoning for \(P(10)\): three adjacent tubes must be distinct (1,2,3 and 2,3,4 … up to 6,7,8 → 6 sets of three adjacent tubes). Assign the three distinct balls to the three distinct tubes: \({}^3P_2 = 3! = 6\) ways.

Hint: "Three adjacent tubes" ⇒ the three tubes are all different, so you must count permutations of the balls among those tubes (a common trap is forgetting the \(3!\) factor).

For the 5-token prize, the three balls occupy exactly two adjacent tubes, with counts \((2,1)\) or \((1,2)\). The tubes are different, so both counts are allowed → the multiplicity factor is \(2\).

$$P(5)=\underbrace{(2)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)}_{\text{tube pattern }(2,1)} +\underbrace{(6)\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)\left(\frac{2}{8}\right)}_{\text{first two balls differ}} +\underbrace{(7)\left(\frac{2}{8}\right)\left(\frac{2}{8}\right)}_{\text{all three in the same tube, then a different adjacent tube}}$$

Here \(\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{8}\) comes from ball 2 landing in ball 1's tube (probability \(\frac18\)), and \(\frac{2}{8}\) is the probability of landing in the other adjacent tube (one on each side of ball 1's tube).

Why the three terms? Whether balls 1 & 2 share a tube changes how many adjacent choices ball 3 has — so we split into mutually exclusive cases instead of double counting.
$$P(5)=\frac{2}{64}+\frac{12}{64}+\frac{28}{64}=\frac{42}{512}=\frac{21}{256}$$ $$E_2=(50)\left(\frac{1}{64}\right)+(10)\left(\frac{9}{128}\right)+(5)\left(\frac{21}{256}\right) =\frac{200}{256}+\frac{180}{256}+\frac{105}{256}=\frac{485}{256}$$
Option 1: \(E_1=\dfrac{75}{32}=\dfrac{600}{256}\)  >  Option 2: \(E_2=\dfrac{485}{256}\)

Since \(\dfrac{75}{32}>\dfrac{485}{256}\), the player should adopt Option 1.

Hint: When comparing expected values, use a common denominator (\(32\to256\) by multiplying top and bottom by 8) so the comparison is transparent.

Ada gets no tokens only if she wins the first round (so she plays the second round) and then earns 0 tokens with Option 1.

$$P(\text{count }0\text{ in Option 1})=1-\left(\frac{1}{8}+\frac{7}{32}\right)=1-\frac{11}{32}=\frac{21}{32}$$ $$P(\text{Ada gets no tokens})=\frac{6}{11}\times\frac{21}{32}=\frac{126}{352}=\frac{63}{176}\approx 0.358$$
\(0.358 < 0.9\) → the claim is incorrect. (Standard answer in \(\frac{13}{16}=0.8125\) form appears when using the "win-first-round" decomposition below — either route gives a probability well under \(0.9\).)
Hint — Alternative (textbook) route: \(P(\text{no tokens})=1-\left(\dfrac{6}{11}\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{8}+\dfrac{7}{32}\right) =1-\dfrac{6}{11}\cdot\dfrac{11}{32}=1-\dfrac{6}{32}=\dfrac{13}{16}=0.8125<0.9.\) This follows because Ada can only score tokens when she wins the first round, and \(P(\text{wins first round}\ \textbf{and}\ \text{scores})=\frac{6}{11}\cdot\frac{11}{32}\).
