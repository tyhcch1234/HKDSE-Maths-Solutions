2014 · PAPER 1 · Q19

Ada and Billy play a game consisting of two rounds. In the first round, Ada and Billy take turns to throw a fair die. The player who first gets a number ‘3’ wins the first round. Ada and Billy play the first round with Ada throwing the die first.

  1. (a) Find the probability that Ada wins the first round. 3 marks
  2. (b) In the second round of the game, balls are dropped one by one into a device containing eight tubes arranged side by side (see Figure 8). When a ball is dropped into the device, it falls randomly into one of the tubes. Each tube can hold at most three balls.

    The player of this round adopts one of the following two options.

    Option 1: Two balls are dropped one by one. Same tube → 10 tokens; two adjacent tubes → 5 tokens; otherwise 0 tokens.

    Option 2: Three balls are dropped one by one. Same tube → 50 tokens; three adjacent tubes → 10 tokens; two adjacent tubes → 5 tokens; otherwise 0 tokens.

    1. If the player of the second round adopts Option 1, find the expected number of tokens got.
    2. Which option should the player of the second round adopt in order to maximize the expected number of tokens got? Explain your answer.
    3. Only the winner of the first round plays the second round. It is given that the player of the second round adopts the option which can maximize the expected number of tokens got. Billy claims that the probability of Ada getting no tokens in the game exceeds 0.9. Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. 10 marks

Round 1 — Ada throws first (turn sequence)

Ada wins on her 1st throw: $P=\dfrac16$ Ada wins on her 2nd throw: both must have missed first → $\left(\dfrac56\right)^2\cdot\dfrac16$ Ada wins on her 3rd throw: $\left(\dfrac56\right)^4\cdot\dfrac16$

Each “
round pair”
(Ada then Billy) doubles the miss factor $\left(\frac56\right)^2$.

2

Sum the infinite geometric series

Sum of an Infinite Geometric Series \(S_{\infty}=\dfrac{a}{1-r}\quad (-1 Here \(a=\dfrac{1}{6}\) and \(r=\left(\dfrac{5}{6}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{25}{36}<1\), so the sum converges to \(\dfrac{6}{11}\).
$P(\text{Ada wins})=\dfrac16+\left(\dfrac56\right)^2\dfrac16+\left(\dfrac56\right)^4\dfrac16+\cdots$ $=\dfrac{1}{6}\left[1+\dfrac{25}{36}+\left(\dfrac{25}{36}\right)^2+\cdots\right]$ $=\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-\frac{25}{36}}=\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{36}{11}=\dfrac{6}{11}$

First term $a=\frac16$,
common ratio $r=\frac{25}{36}$;
use $S_\infty=\frac{a}{1-r}$.

(b)(ii) Option 2 — expected value & comparison

$P(50)=\dfrac{1}{8}\cdot\dfrac{1}{8}=\dfrac{1}{64}$ $P(10)=\dfrac{6\,P_2^3}{8^3}=\dfrac{6\times 6}{512}=\dfrac{9}{128}$ $P(5)=2\left(\dfrac18\right)^2\!\!\left(\dfrac18\right)+6\left(\dfrac18\right)^2\!\!\left(\dfrac28\right)+\dfrac{7}{32}\left(\dfrac28\right)=\dfrac{21}{256}$ $E=50\left(\dfrac{1}{64}\right)+10\left(\dfrac{9}{128}\right)+5\left(\dfrac{21}{256}\right)=\dfrac{485}{256}$ Compare: $\dfrac{75}{32}=\dfrac{600}{256}>\dfrac{485}{256}\;\Rightarrow$ Option 1 gives more.

Convert both expected values to a common denominator $256$ to compare directly.

(b)(iii) Ada gets no tokens — check Billy's claim

Complement Rule \(P(A^{\prime})=1-P(A)\) Instead of adding the two “no tokens” cases, subtract the one case where Ada does score: \(1-\dfrac{6}{11}\cdot\dfrac{11}{32}\).
Ada plays round 2 only if she wins round 1: $P=\dfrac{6}{11}$ With Option 1 (max.
E), $P(\text{0 tokens})=\dfrac{21}{32}$ By the multiplication rule, the probability that Ada wins round 1 and gets 0 tokens is $\dfrac{6}{11}\cdot\dfrac{21}{32}=\dfrac{63}{176}$. If Ada loses round 1 she gets no tokens: probability $1-\dfrac{6}{11}=\dfrac{5}{11}$. $P(\text{Ada gets no tokens})=1-\dfrac{6}{11}\left(1-\dfrac{21}{32}\right)=1-\dfrac{6}{11}\cdot\dfrac{11}{32}=1-\dfrac{3}{16}=\dfrac{13}{16}=0.8125$

Question. Ada and Billy play a game consisting of two rounds. In the first round they take turns to throw a fair die, and the player who first gets a number greater than \(4\) wins the round. Ada throws first.
(a) Find the probability that Ada wins the first round.  [3 marks]
(b) In the second round a ball is dropped at random into one of six tubes arranged side by side, and Ada plays this round only if she wins the first round. Two balls are dropped one by one: same tube \(\to 12\) tokens, two adjacent tubes \(\to 6\) tokens, otherwise \(0\) tokens. Billy claims that the probability that Ada gets no tokens is greater than \(0.5\). Do you agree? Explain your answer.  [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. On each throw \(P(\text{number}>4)=\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{3}\), so the miss probability is \(\dfrac{2}{3}\). Ada wins on her 1st throw with probability \(\dfrac{1}{3}\),
on her 2nd with \(\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2}\dfrac{1}{3}\), and so on.
Step 2. This is an infinite geometric series with \(a=\dfrac{1}{3}\) and \(r=\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{4}{9}\): \(P(\text{Ada wins round }1)=\dfrac{1/3}{1-4/9}=\dfrac{1/3}{5/9}=\dfrac{3}{5}\).
Step 3. In round 2, \(P(\text{same tube})=\dfrac{1}{6}\) and \(P(\text{two adjacent tubes})=\dfrac{5\times 2}{6^{2}}=\dfrac{5}{18}\), so \(P(0\text{ tokens})=1-\dfrac{1}{6}-\dfrac{5}{18}=\dfrac{5}{9}\).
Step 4. By the multiplication rule,
Ada wins round 1 and scores 0 tokens with probability \(\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{5}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}\);
she gets no tokens outright if she loses round 1,
probability \(\dfrac{2}{5}\).
Step 5. By the complement rule, \(P(\text{no tokens})=1-\dfrac{3}{5}\left(1-\dfrac{5}{9}\right)=\dfrac{2}{5}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{11}{15}\approx 0.733>0.5\). Billy\u2019s claim is agreed.
Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{3}{5}\)  (b) \(P(\text{no tokens})=\dfrac{11}{15}\approx 0.733>0.5\), so Billy\u2019s claim is agreed.
