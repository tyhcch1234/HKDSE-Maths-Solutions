Question

$$\frac{1}{x^2-2x+1} - \frac{1}{x^2+x-2} = \text{?}$$
A. $\dfrac{1}{(x-1)(x+2)}$
B. $\dfrac{1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$
C. $\dfrac{3}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$
D. $\dfrac{2x+1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$

Step-by-Step Solution

Click "Next Step" to reveal each part of the working.

1

Factorize the denominators

Rewrite each denominator in factored form. Look for perfect squares and two-term factorizations.

Hint: Use the identities $$a^2 - 2a + 1 = (a-1)^2$$ and factorize by inspection: $$x^2 + x - 2 = (x+2)(x-1)$$
$$x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x-1)^2$$ $$x^2 + x - 2 = (x+2)(x-1)$$
2

Rewrite the expression

Substitute the factored forms back into the original expression.

$$\frac{1}{(x-1)^2} - \frac{1}{(x+2)(x-1)}$$
3

Find the LCM of the denominators

The lowest common multiple of $(x-1)^2$ and $(x+2)(x-1)$ is the highest power of each factor.

Hint: Take the highest power of $(x-1)$ (i.e. $(x-1)^2$) and include $(x+2)$.
$$\text{LCM} = (x-1)^2(x+2)$$
4

Convert to a common denominator

Multiply the numerator and denominator of the first fraction by $(x+2)$, and the second fraction by $(x-1)$.

$$= \frac{1 \cdot (x+2)}{(x-1)^2(x+2)} - \frac{1 \cdot (x-1)}{(x+2)(x-1) \cdot (x-1)}$$ $$= \frac{x+2}{(x-1)^2(x+2)} - \frac{x-1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$$
5

Combine the numerators

Subtract the numerators, being careful with signs. The denominator stays the same.

$$= \frac{(x+2) - (x-1)}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$$
6

Simplify the numerator

Simplify the numerator by collecting like terms. Watch the negative sign!

Hint: Distribute the negative sign: $-(x-1) = -x + 1$.
$$= \frac{x + 2 - x + 1}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$$ $$= \frac{3}{(x-1)^2(x+2)}$$

✅ Final Answer: Option C

Key Takeaway

  • • Factorize first — never rush into combining fractions. Careful factorization reveals the LCM instantly.
  • • The LCM uses the highest power of each distinct factor.
  • • When subtracting algebraic fractions, distribute the negative sign across the entire numerator.
