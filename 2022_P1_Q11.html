15. Statistics — Stem-and-Leaf Diagram, Median, Inter-quartile Range & Standard Deviation

Stem-and-Leaf Diagram Median Inter-quartile Range Mode Standard Deviation

Q11The Question

The stem-and-leaf diagram below shows the distribution of the ages of the players of a football team.

Stem (tens)Leaf (units)
17  8  9
20  a  a  8  8  9
3b  b  5  5  6  6  6  6  7  8
43

The inter-quartile range and the median of the distribution are \(14\) and \(31\) respectively.

(a) Find \(a\) and \(b\). [3 marks]

(b) A player now leaves the football team.

(i) Is there any change in the mode of the distribution due to the leaving of the player? Explain your answer.

(ii) If the range of the distribution is decreased, find the greatest possible standard deviation of the distribution. [4 marks]

(a)Finding \(a\) and \(b\)

11A Count the number of data using the median

Reading the stem-and-leaf diagram row by row:

Stem \(1\): \(3\) data · Stem \(2\): \(6\) data · Stem \(3\): \(10\) data · Stem \(4\): \(1\) data.

\[n=3+6+10+1=20\]

Since \(n=20\) is even, the median is the average of the \(\tfrac{20}{2}=10^{\text{th}}\) and the \(11^{\text{th}}\) data values.

For \(n=20\), \(\text{median}=\dfrac{x_{10}+x_{11}}{2}\). Here \(x_{10}=x_{11}=31\), which is consistent with the given median.
21M Use the inter-quartile range to find \(a\)

Lower quartile \(Q_1\) is at position \(\dfrac{n}{4}=\dfrac{20}{4}=5\), the \(5^{\text{th}}\) datum. From the diagram the \(5^{\text{th}}\) value comes after the first four values in stem \(2\):

\[Q_1=20+a\]

Upper quartile \(Q_3\) is at position \(\dfrac{3n}{4}=15\), the \(15^{\text{th}}\) datum. Counting: \(3+6=9\) values up to the end of stem \(2\), then the \(15^{\text{th}}\) value is the \(6^{\text{th}}\) value of stem \(3\):

\[Q_3=36\]

Inter-quartile range \(=Q_3-Q_1\):

\[36-(20+a)=14\]
IQR \(=Q_3-Q_1\) counts the middle \(50\%\) of the data — use the positions \(n/4\) and \(3n/4\) carefully, counting from the smallest age upwards.
\(\;a=2\;\)
31A Use the median to find \(b\)

The \(10^{\text{th}}\) and \(11^{\text{th}}\) data values are the first two leaves of stem \(3\):

\[x_{10}=30+b,\qquad x_{11}=30+b\] \[\text{Median}=\frac{(30+b)+(30+b)}{2}=30+b=31\]

Hence

\[b=1\]
The two middle values both begin with stem \(3\), so the median equals \(30+b\) — the same digits give the same number, which makes the average simple.
\(\;a=2,\quad b=1\;\)

The ordered data set is therefore

\[17,18,19,\;20,\underline{22},22,28,28,29,\;31,\underline{31},35,35,36,36,36,36,37,38,\;43\]
0 / 3

(b)(i)Is there any change in the mode?

11M Find the mode of the original distribution

From the diagram, the age \(36\) (leaf \(6\) in stem \(3\)) appears \(4\) times while every other age appears at most \(2\) times.

The original mode is \(\;36\;\)
The mode is the MOST FREQUENT value. Count the leaves: four \(6\)'s in the stem \(3\) row stand out immediately.
21A Find the mode of the new distribution

When any one player leaves, at most one copy of the age \(36\) disappears. Hence the age \(36\) still occurs at least \(3\) times, which is still strictly more than every other frequency.

\[\text{New mode}=36\]
Only one datum is removed. The count of \(36\) can drop from \(4\) to \(3\), but no other age can overtake it.
There is no change in the mode of the distribution.
0 / 2

(b)(ii)Greatest possible standard deviation

…
11M Decide which player may leave

A player leaves and the range is decreased, i.e. new range \(<\) old range.

Original range \(=43-17=26\). The range can only shrink if one of the two extreme ages, \(17\) (smallest) or \(43\) (largest), is the one removed.

If any other player left, both \(17\) and \(43\) would remain in the team, so the range would stay \(26\) — not decreased.
Two cases only: the \(17\)-year-old leaves, or the \(43\)-year-old leaves.
21M Case 1 — the \(17\)-year-old leaves (\(n=19\))

New data sum:

\[ \sum x = 17+18+19+2(22)+20+28+28+29+31+31+35+35+36\cdot4+37+38+43 \]

Removing \(17\): the new sum is \(589-17=572\), so

\[\bar{x}=\frac{572}{19}=30.10526316\] \[\sigma \approx 7.162537194\]
Use \(\displaystyle \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{n}-\bar{x}^2}\) or \(\displaystyle \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n}}\). Keep the FULL calculator value until the last rounding step.
Case 1: \(\sigma\approx 7.162537194\)
31M Case 2 — the \(43\)-year-old leaves (\(n=19\))

Removing \(43\): the new sum is \(589-43=546\), so

\[\bar{x}=\frac{546}{19}=28.73684211\] \[\sigma \approx 7.132307207\]
Removing a datum far above the mean tends to reduce spread, but here \(43\) is a single extreme value while \(17\) sits in a denser cluster — compare the two numerical results to decide which is larger.
Case 2: \(\sigma\approx 7.132307207\)
41A Choose the greatest and round off

Comparing the two cases:

\[7.162537194 > 7.132307207\]

Therefore the greatest possible standard deviation occurs in Case 1, and rounding to 3 significant figures:

The greatest possible standard deviation is \(\;7.16\;\) (cor. to 3 sig. fig.).
"Greatest possible" ⇒ you must examine EACH valid case separately, then pick the largest value. Always round at the very end only.
0 / 4

📋 Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\;a=\mathbf{2},\qquad b=\mathbf{1}\)
  • Ordered data: \(17,18,19,20,22,22,28,28,29,31,31,35,35,36,36,36,36,37,38,43\)  (\(n=20\))
  • (b)(i) Original mode \(=36\); new mode \(=36\). There is no change in the mode of the distribution.
  • (b)(ii) Range decreases only if the \(17\)- or the \(43\)-year-old leaves.
  • Case 1 (age \(17\) leaves): \(\sigma\approx 7.162537194\)  ·  Case 2 (age \(43\) leaves): \(\sigma\approx 7.132307207\)
  • Greatest possible standard deviation \(=7.16\) (cor. to 3 sig. fig.)
