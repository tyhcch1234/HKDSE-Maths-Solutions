Simplifying the Difference of Two Algebraic Fractions

Topic 4 — Polynomials (Multiple-choice)

Algebraic Fractions Common Denominator Difference of Two Squares Sign Change

The Question

\[ \frac{1}{3x+7} - \frac{1}{3x-7} = \;? \]
  • A.\(\dfrac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}\)
  • B.\(\dfrac{14}{\,9x^{2}-49\,}\)
  • C.\(\dfrac{6x}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}\)
  • D.\(\dfrac{6x}{\,9x^{2}-49\,}\)

Tap an option above to check whether it is the correct answer. Then work through the guided steps below.

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Work through the cards one at a time. Try to predict each line before you open it.

We must subtract one fraction from another. Since the denominators are different, we cannot simply subtract the numerators.

\[ \frac{1}{\,3x+7\,} \;-\; \frac{1}{\,3x-7\,} \]

Key idea: To add or subtract algebraic fractions, first rewrite them with a common denominator. The safest choice is the product of the two denominators.

The two denominators share no common factor, so the lowest common denominator is simply their product.

\[ \text{L.C.D.} = (3x+7)(3x-7) \]

Difference of two squares: \((a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2\).

Here \(a = 3x\) and \(b = 7\), so the denominator will simplify to a quadratic expression — exactly what we see in the options.

Multiply numerator and denominator of each fraction by the "missing" factor.

\[ \frac{1}{3x+7}\cdot\frac{3x-7}{3x-7} \;-\; \frac{1}{3x-7}\cdot\frac{3x+7}{3x+7} \]
\[ = \frac{3x-7}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} \;-\; \frac{3x+7}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} \]

Now both fractions have the same denominator, so we may combine them into a single fraction with one denominator and a single numerator.

Subtracting a whole expression requires a bracket, otherwise the second term's sign will be wrong.

\[ \frac{1}{3x+7} - \frac{1}{3x-7} \;=\; \frac{(3x-7) - (3x+7)}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} \]

Writing \(\dfrac{3x-7 - 3x+7}{\dots}\) is wrong. The minus sign must distribute over both terms of \((3x+7)\).

Remove the bracket by distributing the negative sign.

\[ (3x-7) - (3x+7) = 3x - 7 - 3x - 7 \]
\[ = (3x - 3x) + (-7 - 7) = -14 \]

The \(3x\) terms cancel out — that is why the answer contains no \(x\) in the numerator (this already rules out C and D).

Apply the difference of two squares with \(a = 3x\) and \(b = 7\).

\[ (3x+7)(3x-7) = (3x)^2 - 7^2 = 9x^2 - 49 \]

So far we have:

\[ \frac{-14}{\,9x^{2}-49\,} \]

The options have positive numerators, so we transfer the negative sign into the denominator.

\[ \frac{-14}{\,9x^{2}-49\,} \;=\; \frac{14}{\,-\,(9x^{2}-49)\,} \;=\; \frac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,} \]

Because \(\dfrac{-a}{b} = \dfrac{a}{-b}\) for \(b \neq 0\). Multiplying both the numerator and denominator of a fraction by \(-1\) does not change its value.

Answer: A   \(\dfrac{14}{\,49-9x^{2}\,}\)

Quick elimination check:

\[ \frac{1}{3x+7}-\frac{1}{3x-7} = \frac{(3x-7)-(3x+7)}{(3x+7)(3x-7)} = \frac{-14}{9x^{2}-49} = \frac{14}{49-9x^{2}} \]

Watch out: options B and A contain the same numbers but with opposite denominator signs — a classic distractor in this question type. Options C and D keep an \(x\) term in the numerator that should have cancelled.

Concept Summary

\[ \frac{1}{3x+7}-\frac{1}{3x-7}=\frac{(3x-7)-(3x+7)}{(3x+7)(3x-7)}=\frac{-14}{9x^{2}-49}=\frac{14}{49-9x^{2}} \]

Remember three habits: (1) build a common denominator first; (2) always put a bracket after a minus sign; (3) check whether the final form matches the option pattern — you may move a negative sign between numerator and denominator.
