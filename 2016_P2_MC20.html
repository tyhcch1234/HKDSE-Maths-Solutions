Area Ratios of Similar Triangles in a Figure of Three Squares
Mensuration Similar Triangles (AAA) Area Ratio = Side Ratio² Squares

QQuestion Multiple Choice

In the figure, \(ABCD\), \(CDEF\) and \(EFGH\) are squares. \(AG\) cuts \(CD\) and \(EF\) at \(P\) and \(Q\) respectively. Find the ratio of the area of quadrilateral \(DEQP\) to the area of quadrilateral \(ABCP\).
A. \(1:2\)
B. \(2:3\)
C. \(3:5\) ✓
D. \(4:9\)
\(AB:PC:QF =\) 3 : 2 : 1 \(\triangle ABG : \triangle PCG : \triangle QFG =\) 9 : 4 : 1 Area ratio \(DEQP : ABCP =\) 3 : 5

1Setting Up Coordinates & Spotting the Similar Triangles

\(AB = BC = CD = s\) Corresponding sides: \(AB /\!/ PC /\!/ QF\)
Step 1
Let each square have side length \(s\). Place coordinates with \(B=(0,0)\), \(C=(s,0)\), \(F=(2s,0)\), \(G=(3s,0)\) on the base and \(A=(0,3s)\).
💡 Why \(A=(0,3s)\)? \(AB\) is the first square's side, then \(BC\) and \(CF\) continue the base — so the whole base spans \(3s\) horizontally, while \(A\) is \(3s\) above \(B\) since \(AB=s\) and \(AD+DE=2s\) adds up. Actually \(A\) is at height \(s\): \(AB\) is vertical side of square \(ABCD\).
Coordinates: \(A=(0,s)\), \(B=(0,0)\), \(C=(s,0)\), \(F=(2s,0)\), \(G=(3s,0)\).
Step 2
Consider the three triangles \(\triangle ABG\), \(\triangle PCG\) and \(\triangle QFG\). \[ \angle AGB=\angle PGC=\angle QGF \quad (\text{common } \angle) \] \[ \angle ABG=\angle PCG=\angle QFG \quad (\text{corr. }\angle\text{s, } AB/\!/ PC/\!/ QF) \] \[ \therefore \triangle ABG \sim \triangle PCG \sim \triangle QFG \quad (\text{AAA}) \]
💡 Since \(AB\), \(PC\), \(QF\) are all vertical lines meeting the same transversal \(AG\), the corresponding angles are equal.
All three triangles are similar by the AAA condition.
Step 3
The horizontal bases of the three triangles are: \[ BG = 3s,\qquad CG = 2s,\qquad FG = s \] Hence the ratio of corresponding sides: \[ AB:PC:QF = BG:CG:FG = 3s:2s:s = 3:2:1 \]
💡 Corresponding sides of similar triangles share the same ratio. Here each horizontal run is \(s, 2s, 3s\) from right to left.
\(AB:PC:QF = 3:2:1\)
0 / 3

2Area Ratio of Similar Triangles = (Side Ratio)²

\(\triangle ABG\) area \(= \frac{1}{2}(3s)(3s)=\frac{9}{2}s^2\) — 9 parts \(\triangle PCG\) area \(= \frac{1}{2}(2s)(2s)=2s^2\) — 4 parts \(\triangle QFG\) area \(= \frac{1}{2}(s)(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^2\) — 1 part
Step 1
For similar figures, the ratio of areas equals the square of the ratio of corresponding sides. \[ \frac{[\triangle ABG]}{[\triangle PCG]}{:}\frac{[\triangle PCG]}{[\triangle QFG]} \;\Longrightarrow\; [\triangle ABG]:[\triangle PCG]:[\triangle QFG] = 3^2:2^2:1^2 = 9:4:1 \]
💡 Direct check: \(\frac{\frac{9}{2}s^2}{\frac{1}{2}s^2}=9\), \(\frac{2s^2}{\frac{1}{2}s^2}=4\). ✓
\([\triangle ABG]:[\triangle PCG]:[\triangle QFG]=9:4:1\)
Step 2
Split \(\triangle ABG\) using the transversal \(AG\) and the vertical lines \(PC\) and \(QF\): \[ [\triangle ABG]=[\triangle PCG]+[ABCP]+[DEQP]+[\triangle QFG] \] Actually \(\triangle PCG\) contains quadrilateral \(ABCP\) inside it? Let's identify parts carefully: quadrilateral \(ABCP\) sits between line \(AB\) (left) and line \(PC\); quadrilateral \(DEQP\) sits between \(PC\) and \(QF\).
💡 Picture \(\triangle ABG\) as the big right triangle. Traverse from left to right: \(ABCP\), then \(DEQP\), then \(\triangle QFG\). Wait — check where \(Q\) is: \(Q\) lies on \(EF\), on the segment between the 2nd and 3rd squares.
Decomposition: \([\triangle ABG] = [ABCP] + [DEQP] + [\triangle QFG]\).
Step 3
Alternatively, work with the complementary region. Note that quadrilateral \(ABCP\) has the same shape and size as \(FCPQ\) reflected, i.e. they are congruent regions. Using: \[ [ABCP] = [\triangle ABG]-[\triangle PCG] = 9-4 = 5 \text{ parts} \] \[ [FCPQ] = [\triangle PCG]-[\triangle QFG] = 4-1 = 3 \text{ parts} \]
💡 This matches the official Method I: \(ABCP\) = the region of \(\triangle ABG\) outside \(\triangle PCG\).
\([ABCP] = 5\) parts, \([FCPQ] = 3\) parts.
0 / 3

3Congruence Trick & Final Ratio

\(DEQP \cong FCPQ\) \([FCPQ]=4-1=3\) parts \([DEQP]:[ABCP]=\) 3 : 5
Step 1
Note the congruence: quadrilateral \(DEQP \cong\) quadrilateral \(FCPQ\) (the figure has reflective symmetry about the vertical line through \(E\)). Hence: \[ [DEQP]=[FCPQ] \]
💡 Look at the picture: the region \(DEQP\) mirrors \(FCPQ\) in shape and area because the squares \(CDEF\) and \(EFGH\) are congruent and the line \(AG\) hits them at matching offsets.
\([DEQP]=[FCPQ]\)
Step 2
Substituting: \[ [DEQP]=[FCPQ]=4-1=3 \text{ parts} \] \[ [ABCP]=9-4=5 \text{ parts} \] So the required ratio is: \[ \frac{[DEQP]}{[ABCP]}=\frac{3}{5} \]
💡 Check that the two answers come from the same "parts" system — both are measured in units of \(\frac{1}{2}s^2\).
The ratio of the area of quadrilateral \(DEQP\) to the area of quadrilateral \(ABCP\) is \(3:5\).
Step 3
Therefore the answer is: \[ \boxed{\text{C. }3:5} \] Quotient check: \(3/5 = 0.6\), matching option C, so we select C.
💡 Notice the ratio \(3:5\) is not simply \(3^2:5^2=9:25\), because \(DEQP\) and \(ABCP\) are not similar figures — only the original triangles were similar.
Answer: Option C — \(3:5\).

Summary · Final Answers

  • The three right triangles are similar by AAA: \(\triangle ABG\sim\triangle PCG\sim\triangle QFG\).
  • Corresponding side ratio: \(AB:PC:QF=BG:CG:FG=3:2:1\).
  • Areas of similar figures: \([\triangle ABG]:[\triangle PCG]:[\triangle QFG]=9:4:1\).
  • \([ABCP]=9-4=5\) parts and \([FCPQ]=4-1=3\) parts.
  • By congruence \(DEQP\cong FCPQ\): \([DEQP]=3\) parts, so \(\dfrac{[DEQP]}{[ABCP]}=\dfrac{3}{5}\).
  • Correct option: C. \(3:5\).
