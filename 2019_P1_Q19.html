QUESTION [2019#19]

Let $$f(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(x^{2}+(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\right),$$ where $k$ is a positive constant. Denote the point $(4,\,33)$ by $F$.

(a) Prove that the graph of $y=f(x)$ passes through $F$.  [1 mark]

(b) The graph of $y=g(x)$ is obtained by reflecting the graph of $y=f(x)$ with respect to the $y$-axis and then translating the resulting graph upwards by 4 units. Let $U$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$. Denote the origin by $O$.

(i) Using the method of completing the square, express the coordinates of $U$ in terms of $k$.

(ii) Find $k$ such that the area of the circle passing through $F$, $O$ and $U$ is the least.

(iii) For any positive constant $k$, the graph of $y=g(x)$ passes through the same point $G$.

Let $V$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$ such that the area of the circle passing through $F$, $O$ and $V$ is the least. Are $F$, $G$, $O$ and $V$ concyclic? Explain your answer.  [11 marks]

STEP 1 · (a) Test the point F(4, 33)
$$f(4)=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(4^{2}+(6k-2)(4)+(9k+25)\right)$$ $$=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(16+24k-8+9k+25\right)=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(33+33k\right)$$ $$=\frac{33(1+k)}{1+k}=33\;\checkmark$$ Since $f(4)=33$, the graph of $y=f(x)$ passes through $F(4,33)$. $\blacksquare$
STEP 2 · (b)(i) Reflect in the y-axis
Reflection in the $y$-axis replaces $x$ by $-x$: $$f(-x)+4=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\right)+4$$ So $$g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\Big(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\Big)+4.$$
💡 Remember: reflecting in the $y$-axis means $f(x)\to f(-x)$; then add 4 to shift upward.
STEP 3 · (b)(i) Complete the square to find U
Write $m=k-1$ momentarily; completing the square in $x$: $$x^{2}-(6k-2)x=x^{2}-2(3k-1)x=(x-(3k-1))^{2}-(3k-1)^{2}$$ Hence $$g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\Big[(x-(3k-1))^{2}-(3k-1)^{2}+(9k+25)\Big]+4$$ $$=\frac{1}{1+k}\Big[(x-(3k-1))^{2}+\left(9k+25-(9k^{2}-6k+1)\right)\Big]+4$$ $$=\frac{1}{1+k}(x-(3k-1))^{2}+\frac{1}{1+k}(-9k^{2}+15k+24)+4$$ $$=\frac{1}{1+k}(x-(3k-1))^{2}+(28-9k).$$ $$\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{U=\big(3k-1,\;28-9k\big)}$$
💡 The vertex of $y=a(x-h)^2+c$ is $(h,c)$; here $a=\dfrac{1}{1+k}>0$, so $U$ is a minimum.
STEP 4 · (b)(ii) Least circle area ⇒ FO is a diameter
All three points $F$, $O$, $U$ lie on some circle. Its area is least when $FO$ is a diameter of the circle, i.e. when $\angle FUO=90^{\circ}$.

Slope of $FU$: $$\frac{(28-9k)-0}{(3k-1)-0}\quad\text{and}\quad \text{slope of }FU\cdot\text{slope of }UO=-1.$$ Using $O(0,0),\;F(4,33),\;U(3k-1,28-9k)$: $$\left(\frac{33-(28-9k)}{4-(3k-1)}\right)\left(\frac{(28-9k)-0}{(3k-1)-0}\right)=-1$$ $$\left(\frac{5+9k}{5-3k}\right)\left(\frac{28-9k}{3k-1}\right)=-1$$ $$(5+9k)(28-9k)=-(5-3k)(3k-1)$$ Expand: $$140-45k+252k-81k^{2}=3k^{2}-16k+5$$ $$2k^{2}-5k-3=0$$ $$(2k+1)(k-3)=0\;\Rightarrow\;k=3 \text{ or } k=-\tfrac{1}{2}\text{ (rejected, }k>0)$$ $$\boxed{k=3}$$
⚠️ Check $k\neq\tfrac13$ (so $U\neq O$), $k\neq\tfrac53$ (so $FO$ not vertical) — $k=3$ is fine.
STEP 5 · (b)(iii) Locate the fixed point G
When $k=3$: $V=U=(3k-1,\,28-9k)=(8,\,1)$.

For general $k$, find the common point of all $y=g(x)$. Take difference between $k$ and $k'$ forms: $$g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}(x^{2}+2x+9)k+\frac{1}{1+k}(x^{2}-2x+25)+4$$ For this to be independent of $k$, the coefficient of $k$ must vanish together with consistency: $$x^{2}+2x+9=x^{2}-2x+25\;\Rightarrow\;4x=16\;\Rightarrow\;x=4.$$ Then $$g(4)=\frac{1}{1+k}(16+24k-8+9k+25)+4=\frac{33(1+k)}{1+k}+4=37.$$ $$\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{G=(-4,\,37)}$$
💡 $G$ comes from reflecting $F(4,33)$ in the $y$-axis to $(-4,33)$, then translating up by 4.
STEP 6 · (b)(iii) Are F, G, O, V concyclic?
With $k=3$: $V=(8,\,1)$, $F=(4,\,33)$, $G=(-4,\,37)$, $O=(0,0)$.

Circle through F, O, V: from Step 4, with $k=3$ it is the circle having $FO$ as diameter, so $\angle FVO=90^{\circ}$.

Does G lie on this circle? Check $\angle FGO$: $$\text{slope }FG=\frac{37-33}{-4-4}=\frac{4}{-8}=-\frac12,\qquad \text{slope }GO=\frac{37-0}{-4-0}=-\frac{37}{4}.$$ $$m_{FG}\cdot m_{GO}=\left(-\frac12\right)\left(-\frac{37}{4}\right)=\frac{37}{8}\neq -1.$$ So $\angle FGO\neq 90^{\circ}$, hence $G$ does not lie on the circle with $FO$ as diameter.

Since $F$, $O$, $V$ lie on that circle but $G$ does not, $$\boxed{F,\,G,\,O,\,V \text{ are NOT concyclic.}}$$
Answers:
(a) $f(4)=33$, so $y=f(x)$ passes through $F(4,33)$. ✔
(b)(i) $\;U=(3k-1,\;28-9k)$
(b)(ii) $\;k=3$
(b)(iii) $G=(-4,37)$; since $m_{FG}\cdot m_{GO}=\frac{37}{8}\neq-1$, $G$ is not on the circle through $F,O,V$ ⇒ not concyclic.
