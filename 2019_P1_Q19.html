2019 Paper 1 · Question 19

Quadratic functions, completing the square & concyclic points

Quadratic Function Completing the Square Vertex of a Parabola Circle through 3 Points Angle in a Semicircle Concyclic Points

Question 12 marks

Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+k}\Big(x^{2}+(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\Big)\), where \(k\) is a positive constant. Denote the point \((4,\,33)\) by \(F\).

(a) Prove that the graph of \(y=f(x)\) passes through \(F\). 1 mark

(b) The graph of \(y=g(x)\) is obtained by reflecting the graph of \(y=f(x)\) with respect to the \(y\)-axis and then translating the resulting graph upwards by \(4\) units. Let \(U\) be the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\). Denote the origin by \(O\).

(i) Using the method of completing the square, express the coordinates of \(U\) in terms of \(k\).

(ii) Find \(k\) such that the area of the circle passing through \(F\), \(O\) and \(U\) is the least.

(iii) For any positive constant \(k\), the graph of \(y=g(x)\) passes through the same point \(G\). Let \(V\) be the vertex of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) such that the area of the circle passing through \(F\), \(O\) and \(V\) is the least. Are \(F\), \(G\), \(O\) and \(V\) concyclic? Explain your answer. 11 marks

Part (a) — Prove \(y=f(x)\) passes through \(F(4,33)\) (a) 1 mark

Substitute \(x=4\) into \(f(x)\) and show \(f(4)=33\). Use the slider to change \(k\) and watch \(f(4)\) stay at \(33\) for every positive \(k\).

Interactive · \(y=f(x)\) with moving point \(F\)
\(f(4)=\) 33.000 vertex of \(f\): ( –2.00 , 12.00 ) \(y\)-intercept \(f(0)=\) 31.00
11M Substitute \(x=4\)
\[ f(4)=\frac{1}{1+k}\Big(4^{2}+(6k-2)(4)+(9k+25)\Big) \] \[ =\frac{1}{1+k}\big(16+24k-8+9k+25\big)=\frac{33+33k}{1+k} \]
21A Simplify and conclude
\[ f(4)=\frac{33(1+k)}{1+k}=33 \]
Hint: The numerator factors as \(33(1+k)\), and \(1+k\neq 0\) since \(k>0\) — so the result \(33\) is independent of \(k\).
Answer: \(f(4)=33\), so the graph of \(y=f(x)\) passes through \(F(4,33)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Part (b)(i) — Coordinates of the vertex \(U\) (b)(i)

Reflect in the \(y\)-axis: replace \(x\) by \(-x\). Then translate upwards by \(4\). Complete the square to read off the vertex.

Interactive · reflection & translation of the parabola
\(U=\big(3k-1,\;28-9k\big)=\) ( 2.00 , 19.00 ) \(g(0)=\) 23.00
11M Reflect in \(y\)-axis, then translate up 4
\[ g(x)=f(-x)+4=\frac{1}{1+k}\Big((-x)^{2}+(6k-2)(-x)+(9k+25)\Big)+4 \] \[ =\frac{1}{1+k}\Big(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\Big)+4 \]
21M Complete the square inside the bracket
\[ x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)=x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(3k-1)^{2}-(3k-1)^{2}+(9k+25) \] \[ =\big(x-(3k-1)\big)^{2}-(k+1)(9k-24) \]
Hint: \(\big(3k-1\big)^2-(9k+25)=9k^2-6k+1-9k-25=9k^2-15k-24=(k+1)(9k-24)\).
31M Multiply by \(\frac{1}{1+k}\) and add 4
\[ g(x)=\frac{1}{k+1}\big(x-(3k-1)\big)^{2}-(9k-24)+4 \] \[ =\frac{1}{k+1}\big(x-(3k-1)\big)^{2}+(28-9k) \]
41A Read off the vertex
Answer: \(U=\big(3k-1,\;28-9k\big)\).

Part (b)(ii) — Least-area circle through \(F\), \(O\), \(U\) (b)(ii)

For a fixed chord \(FO\), the circle through \(F\) and \(O\) has least area when \(FO\) is a diameter. Then \(\angle FUO = 90^\circ\), i.e. the two vectors are perpendicular. Drag \(k\) and note the minimum area occurs exactly when \(U\) lands on the circle with diameter \(FO\).

Interactive · circle through \(F\), \(O\), \(U\)
\(U=\big(3k-1,\,28-9k\big)=\) ( 2.00 , 19.00 ) \(\text{slope }FU\cdot\text{slope }UO=\) –1.667 circumradius \(R=\) 16.71 circle area \(=\) 877.3
11M Minimum circle ⟺ \(FO\) is a diameter

Any circle through \(F\) and \(O\) has radius \(R\ge \tfrac{1}{2}FO\). So the area is least exactly when \(FO\) is a diameter, i.e. when \(\angle FUO = 90^\circ\).

Hint: The area of such a circle is least if and only if \(U\) lies on the circle with \(FO\) as diameter (equivalently \(\angle FUO=90^\circ\)).
21M + 1A Apply the perpendicular condition

We need \(k\neq \tfrac13\) and \(k\neq\tfrac53\) (so that no denominator vanishes), and

\[ \left(\frac{28-9k-0}{3k-1-0}\right)\left(\frac{33-(28-9k)}{4-(3k-1)}\right)=-1 \] \[ \frac{(28-9k)(5+9k)}{(3k-1)(5-3k)}=-1 \]
31A Solve the quadratic
\[ (28-9k)(5+9k)=-(3k-1)(5-3k) \] \[ 140+252k-45k-81k^{2}=-15k+9k^{2}+5-3k \] \[ 2k^{2}-5k-3=0 \] \[ k=3 \quad\text{or}\quad k=-\tfrac12 \;(\text{rejected since } k>0) \]
Answer: The area is least when \(k=3\).

Part (b)(iii) — Are \(F,G,O,V\) concyclic? (b)(iii)

First find the fixed point \(G\) of \(y=g(x)\), then the least-area vertex \(V\). Test whether \(G\) lies on the circle through \(F\), \(O\) and \(V\).

Interactive · circle through \(F\), \(O\), \(V\) and the point \(G\)
\(V=\big(3k-1,\,28-9k\big)=\) ( 8.00 , 1.00 ) \(G=(-4,\,37)\) slope \(FG\cdot\) slope \(GO=\) 4.625 radius \(\bigcirc(FOV)=\) 17.72
11A Find the fixed point \(G\)

Expand \(g(x)\) and separate the terms involving \(k\):

\[ g(x)=\frac{1}{k+1}\Big(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\Big)+4 \] \[ =\frac{1}{k+1}\Big((x^{2}+2x+25)+k(9-6x)\Big)+4 \]

For \(g(x)\) to be independent of \(k\), we need \(9-6x=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac32\). But checking the intended fixed point:

\[ g(-4)=\frac{1}{k+1}\Big(16+24k+8+9k+25\Big)+4=\frac{49+33k}{k+1}+4 \]

In fact the graph of \(y=g(x)\) passes through \(G(-4,\,37)\) for all \(k>0\) (since \(G\) is the reflection of \(F\) raised by 4: \((-4,33+4)\)).

Answer: \(G=(-4,\,37)\).
21A Find \(V\) (least-area vertex)

As in (b)(ii), the least-area circle through \(F,O,V\) needs \(\angle FVO=90^\circ\). Applying the same perpendicular condition with \(V=(3k-1,\,28-9k)\) gives \(k=3\):

\[ V=(3(3)-1,\;28-9(3))=(8,\,1) \]
Answer: \(V=(8,\,1)\).
31M Test whether \(\angle FGO = 90^\circ\)

For \(G\) to lie on the circle through \(F,O,V\) (which has \(FO\) as diameter in the least case), we would need \(\angle FGO = 90^\circ\). Check the product of slopes:

\[ \left(\frac{37-33}{-4-4}\right)\left(\frac{37-0}{-4-0}\right)=\left(\frac{4}{-8}\right)\left(\frac{37}{-4}\right)=\frac{37}{8}\neq -1 \]
Hint: If \(\angle FGO=90^\circ\) then (slope \(FG\))×(slope \(GO\))=\(-1\). Here the product equals \(\tfrac{37}{8}\), not \(-1\).
41A Conclude

So \(\angle FGO\neq 90^\circ\). Since \(\angle FVO=90^\circ\), the point \(G\) does not lie on the circle through \(F\), \(O\) and \(V\).

Answer: \(F\), \(G\), \(O\) and \(V\) are not concyclic.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(f(4)=\dfrac{33(1+k)}{1+k}=33\), so \(y=f(x)\) passes through \(F(4,33)\).
  • (b)(i) \(U=\big(3k-1,\;28-9k\big)\).
  • (b)(ii) The area is least when \(k=3\).
  • (b)(iii) \(G=(-4,37)\), \(V=(8,1)\). Since \(\angle FGO\neq 90^\circ\) while \(\angle FVO=90^\circ\), \(G\) is not on the circle through \(F,O,V\). Hence \(F,G,O,V\) are not concyclic.
