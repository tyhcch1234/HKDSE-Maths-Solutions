Functions and Graphs — completing the square & concycicity

Let \[ f(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(x^{2}+(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\right), \] where $k$ is a positive constant. Denote the point $(4,33)$ by $F$.

(a) Prove that the graph of $y=f(x)$ passes through $F$.  [1 mark]

(b) The graph of $y=g(x)$ is obtained by reflecting the graph of $y=f(x)$ with respect to the $y$-axis and then translating the resulting graph upwards by $4$ units. Let $U$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$. Denote the origin by $O$.

(i) Using the method of completing the square, express the coordinates of $U$ in terms of $k$.
(ii) Find $k$ such that the area of the circle passing through $F$, $O$ and $U$ is the least.
(iii) For any positive constant $k$, the graph of $y=g(x)$ passes through the same point $G$.
    Let $V$ be the vertex of the graph of $y=g(x)$ such that the area of the circle passing through $F$, $O$ and $V$ is the least. Are $F$, $G$, $O$ and $V$ concyclic? Explain your answer.

[11 marks]

1 Part (a) — substitute $x=4$ into $f$
At $x=4$, \[ f(4)=\frac{1}{1+k}\left(4^{2}+(6k-2)(4)+(9k+25)\right) =\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl(16+24k-8+9k+25\bigr) =\frac{1}{1+k}(33+33k)=33. \] Hence the point $(4,33)=F$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$. $\;\blacksquare$
Hint: the whole point is that the constant part $(9k+25)$ was chosen so that $f(4)$ collapses to $33$.
2 Part (b)(i) — reflect in the $y$-axis, then shift up $4$
Reflection in the $y$-axis replaces $x$ by $-x$: \[ f(-x)+4=\frac{1}{1+k}\Bigl((-x)^{2}+(6k-2)(-x)+(9k+25)\Bigr)+4. \] So \[ g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(9k+25)\bigr)+4. \]
Hint: reflecting $y=f(x)$ in the $y$-axis gives $y=f(-x)$; “upwards by 4” then adds $4$.
3 Part (b)(i) — complete the square
\[ g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\Bigl(x^{2}-(6k-2)x+(3k-1)^{2}-(3k-1)^{2}+(9k+25)\Bigr)+4 \] \[ \phantom{g(x)}=\frac{1}{1+k}\Bigl((x-(3k-1))^{2}+\bigl[9k+25-(9k^{2}-6k+1)\bigr]\Bigr)+4 \] \[ \phantom{g(x)}=\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl((x-(3k-1))^{2}+(28-9k)\bigr)+4 =\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl(x-(3k-1)\bigr)^{2}+(28-9k)+4. \] Therefore the vertex is \[ \boxed{\,U=\bigl(3k-1,\;32-9k\bigr)\,}. \]
Hint: add and subtract $(3k-1)^2$; note $(6k-2)/2=3k-1$. Then $-9k^2+15k+24$ divided by $(1+k)$ plus $4$ simplifies to $32-9k$.
Completing the Square \(x^{2}+bx=\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{b^{2}}{4}\) Since \(\dfrac{6k-2}{2}=3k-1\), the vertex of \(y=g(x)\) has \(x\)-coordinate \(3k-1\).
4 Part (b)(ii) — least area ⇒ $FO$ is a diameter
For a fixed base $FO$, the circumcircle of $\triangle FOU$ has least area exactly when $FO$ is a diameter, which is equivalent to $\angle FUO=90^{\circ}$.
Hint: as the circle grows, the base $FO$ becomes a shorter chord; area is minimal when $FO$ is a diameter ($\angle FUO = 90^\circ$).
Angle in a Semicircle \(\angle FUO=90^{\circ}\iff FO \text{ is a diameter}\) The circle through \(F\) and \(O\) has least area exactly when \(FO\) is a diameter.
5 Part (b)(ii) — apply the perpendicular condition
Here we need $k\neq\frac13$ and $k\neq\frac53$ (so that $O,U,F$ are distinct in position). With \[ \frac{32-9k-0}{3k-1-0}\times\frac{33-(32-9k)}{4-(3k-1)}=-1, \] \[ \frac{(32-9k)(1+9k)}{(3k-1)(5-3k)}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; (32-9k)(1+9k)=-(3k-1)(5-3k). \]
Hint: slope of $OU$ times slope of $UF$ equals $-1$.
Condition for Perpendicular Lines \(m_{1}m_{2}=-1\) The slopes of \(OU\) and \(UF\) give \((32-9k)(1+9k)=-(3k-1)(5-3k)\).
6 Part (b)(ii) — solve the quadratic
Expanding: \[ 32+288k-9k-81k^{2}=-(15k-9k^{2}-5+3k) \] \[ 32+279k-81k^{2}=12k^{2}-18k+5 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2k^{2}-5k-3=0. \] \[ k=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25+24}}{4}=\frac{5\pm 7}{4} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=3 \ \text{ or }\ k=-\tfrac12\ (\text{rejected}). \]
Hint: keep $k>0$; the negative root is rejected. The answer is $k=3$.
7 Part (b)(iii) — find the fixed point $G$
Substituting $k=3$ in $g$: $U=(8,\,5)$. In general \[ g(x)=\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl(x-(3k-1)\bigr)^{2}+(32-9k). \] Trying $x=-4$: \[ g(-4)=\frac{1}{1+k}\bigl((-4)-(3k-1)\bigr)^{2}+32-9k =\frac{1}{1+k}(3+3k)^{2}+32-9k=9(1+k)+32-9k=41, \] which is independent of $k$. Hence the graph always passes through \[ \boxed{G=(-4,\,37)}. \]
Hint: look for an $x$ that makes the $(1+k)$ denominator cancel the $(1+k)^2$ factor — try $x=-4$.
8 Part (b)(iii) — is $\angle FGO=90^{\circ}$?
The product of the slope of $FG$ and the slope of $GO$ is \[ \left(\frac{37-33}{-4-4}\right)\left(\frac{37-0}{-4-0}\right) =\left(\frac{4}{-8}\right)\left(\frac{37}{-4}\right) =\left(-\tfrac12\right)\left(-\tfrac{37}{4}\right)=\frac{37}{8}. \] Since $\frac{37}{8}\neq-1$, we have $\angle FGO\neq 90^{\circ}$.
Hint: perpendicular lines require product of slopes $=-1$. Here it is $37/8$.
9 Part (b)(iii) — conclusion on concycicity
In part (b)(ii) the least circle through $F$, $O$ and $V$ has $FO$ as a diameter, so $V$ lies on the circle with diameter $FO$ and hence $\angle FVO=90^{\circ}$. But $\angle FGO\neq 90^{\circ}$, so $G$ does not lie on that circle. Therefore \[ \boxed{\,F,\;G,\;O\;\text{and}\;V\ \text{are not concyclic.}\,} \]
Hint: if $F,G,O,V$ were concyclic and $\angle FVO=90^\circ$, then $FO$ would be a diameter, forcing $\angle FGO=90^\circ$ — contradiction.
Answers:  (a) $f(4)=33$, so $F(4,33)$ lies on $y=f(x)$;   (b)(i) $U=(3k-1,\;32-9k)$;   (b)(ii) $k=3$;   (b)(iii) $G=(-4,37)$, and $F,G,O,V$ are not concyclic.

Question. Let \(g(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+k}\left(x^{2}-(8k-4)x+(16k^{2}-4k+16)\right)+2\), where \(k\) is a positive constant. Using the method of completing the square, express the coordinates of the vertex \(U\) of the graph of \(y=g(x)\) in terms of \(k\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Half of the \(x\)-coefficient is \(\dfrac{8k-4}{2}=4k-2\), and \((4k-2)^{2}=16k^{2}-16k+4\).
Step 2. Add and subtract this square: \(x^{2}-(8k-4)x+(16k^{2}-4k+16)=\bigl(x-(4k-2)\bigr)^{2}+16k^{2}-4k+16-(16k^{2}-16k+4)\).
Step 3. The remainder simplifies to \(12k+12=12(1+k)\), so \(g(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+k}\bigl(x-(4k-2)\bigr)^{2}+12+2\).
Step 4. Therefore the vertex is \(U=(4k-2,\,14)\).
Answer: \(U=(4k-2,\,14)\)
