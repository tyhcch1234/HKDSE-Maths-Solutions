Question

It is given that $y$ partly varies directly as $x^2$ and partly varies inversely as $x$. When $x=1$, $y=-4$ and when $x=2$, $y=5$. When $x=-2$, $y=$
  • A$-11$
  • B$-5$
  • C$5$
  • D$11$

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1Set up the variation model
Partial Variation \(y=k_{1}x^{2}+\dfrac{k_{2}}{x}\) The statement "partly constant, partly varies as \(x^{2}\), partly inversely as \(x\)" gives this form.

"$y$ partly varies directly as $x^2$ and partly varies inversely as $x$" means the two parts are added:

$$y = k_1x^2 + \frac{k_2}{x}, \qquad k_1,\;k_2 \neq 0$$

Hint: "partly varies directly as $A$ and partly varies inversely as $B$" gives $y=k_1A+\dfrac{k_2}{B}$.
Step 2Substitute the given data to form two equations

When $x=1,\;y=-4$:

$$k_1(1)^2+\frac{k_2}{1}=-4 \;\Rightarrow\; k_1+k_2=-4 \qquad \cdots(1)$$

When $x=2,\;y=5$:

$$k_1(2)^2+\frac{k_2}{2}=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 4k_1+\frac{k_2}{2}=5 \;\Rightarrow\; 8k_1+k_2=10 \qquad \cdots(2)$$

Hint: multiply equation (2) by 2 to clear the fraction — it makes elimination easy.
Step 3Solve the simultaneous equations
Solving for the Variation Constants \(\text{Substitute the two given pairs to obtain two equations in } k_{1},k_{2}\) Using \((1,-4)\) and \((2,5)\) gives \(k_{1}=2,\ k_{2}=-6\).

$(2)-(1)$:

$$(8k_1+k_2)-(k_1+k_2)=10-(-4)$$

$$7k_1 = 14 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{k_1=2}$$

Substitute $k_1=2$ into (1):

$$2+k_2=-4 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{k_2=-6}$$

Hint: subtracting eliminates $k_2$ because both coefficients are 1.
Step 4Write down the variation equation
Direct and Inverse Variation \(y\propto x^{2} \Rightarrow y=kx^{2};\quad y\propto\dfrac{1}{x}\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{k}{x}\) With both constants known, substitute \(x=-2\) to get \(y=11\).

$$\therefore\; y = 2x^2 - \frac{6}{x}$$

Hint: this single formula now generates $y$ for any value of $x$.
Step 5Substitute $x=-2$ to find the required value

$$y = 2(-2)^2 - \frac{6}{-2}$$

$$y = 2(4) + 3 = 8+3 = 11$$

Hint: take care with signs — dividing by $-2$ gives $+3$.
Answer: $y = 11$  →  Option D

Question. Suppose \(y=k_{1}x^{2}+\dfrac{k_{2}}{x}\). When \(x=1\), \(y=6\); when \(x=2\), \(y=10\). Find \(y\) when \(x=-1\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Substitute \((1,6)\): \(k_{1}+k_{2}=6\).
Step 2. Substitute \((2,10)\): \(4k_{1}+\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}=10\), i.e. \(8k_{1}+k_{2}=20\).
Step 3. Subtracting: \(7k_{1}=14\), so \(k_{1}=2\) and \(k_{2}=4\).
Step 4. At \(x=-1\): \(y=2(1)+\dfrac{4}{-1}=2-4=-2\).
Answer: \(y=-2\)
