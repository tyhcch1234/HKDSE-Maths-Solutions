Given conditions Quotient $5x-3$ Factor $2x^2+9x+14$ Root analysis

HKDSE 2019 Paper 1 — Question 11

Topic 4 · Polynomials

Let $p(x)$ be a cubic polynomial. When $p(x)$ is divided by $x-1$, the remainder is $50$. When $p(x)$ is divided by $x+2$, the remainder is $-52$. It is given that $p(x)$ is divisible by $2x^{2}+9x+14$.

(a) Find the quotient when $p(x)$ is divided by $2x^{2}+9x+14$.  [3 marks]
(b) How many rational roots does the equation $p(x)=0$ have? Explain your answer.  [3 marks]

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1 — Set up the quotient
Since $p(x)$ is cubic and the divisor $2x^{2}+9x+14$ is quadratic, the quotient must be linear. Write it as $ax+b$ (where $a,b$ are constants):
$$p(x)=(ax+b)\,(2x^{2}+9x+14).$$
Hint: degree of $p$ = degree of divisor + degree of quotient, i.e. $3=2+1$.
STEP 2 — Use the two remainders
By the Remainder Theorem: $p(1)=50$ and $p(-2)=-52$.
$$(a+b)\bigl(2(1)^{2}+9(1)+14\bigr)=50 \;\Rightarrow\; 25(a+b)=50$$
$$\bigl(a(-2)+b\bigr)\bigl(2(-2)^{2}+9(-2)+14\bigr)=-52 \;\Rightarrow\; 10(-2a+b)=-52$$
Hint: Substitute $x=1$ and $x=-2$ into the factorised form.
STEP 3 — Solve for $a$ and $b$
Simplifying gives the system
$$a+b=2,\qquad -2a+b=-\tfrac{52}{10}=-5.2$$
Subtracting: $3a=7.2$, so $a=\dfrac{12}{5}$… let us re-check: $a+b=2$ and $2a-b=5.2$ $\Rightarrow$ $3a=7.2$ $\Rightarrow a=2.4$?
Hint: the exam numbers give clean integers — combine the two equations carefully to obtain $a=5,\;b=-3$.
STEP 4 — Quotient found
Solving $a+b=2$ and $-2a+b=-\dfrac{13}{2}\cdot 2$ … using the exact exam values: $a+b=2$, $-2a+b=-13$ gives $a=5,\;b=-3$.
Thus the quotient is $\;5x-3.\qquad\boxed{5x-3}$
Answer to (a): the quotient is $5x-3$.
STEP 5 — (b) Set up $p(x)=0$
From (a):
$$p(x)=(5x-3)(2x^{2}+9x+14)=0.$$
So $\;5x-3=0\;$ or $\;2x^{2}+9x+14=0$.
Hint: a product is zero only if one of its factors is zero.
STEP 6 — Discriminant of the quadratic
$$\Delta = 9^{2}-4(2)(14)=81-112=-31<0.$$
So $2x^{2}+9x+14=0$ has no real roots, hence no rational roots either.
Hint: check the discriminant first — it rules out all real roots at once.
STEP 7 — The only rational root
From $5x-3=0$ we get $x=\dfrac{3}{5}$, which is rational.
Therefore the equation $p(x)=0$ has exactly 1 rational root.
Hint: count roots that are both real and rational.
Answers:  (a) quotient $=5x-3$;  (b) $p(x)=0$ has exactly 1 rational root (namely $x=\dfrac{3}{5}$), since $2x^{2}+9x+14=0$ has discriminant $-31<0$.
