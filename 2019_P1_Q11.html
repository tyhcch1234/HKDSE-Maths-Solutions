Question

Let \( P(x) \) be a cubic polynomial. When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( x - 1 \), the remainder is \( 50 \). When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( x + 2 \), the remainder is \( -52 \). It is given that \( P(x) \) is divisible by \( 2x^2 + 9x + 14 \).

  1. (a) Find the quotient when \( P(x) \) is divided by \( 2x^2 + 9x + 14 \). [3 marks]
  2. (b) How many rational roots does the equation \( P(x) = 0 \) have? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Since \( P(x) \) is cubic (degree 3) and the divisor \( 2x^2 + 9x + 14 \) is quadratic (degree 2), the quotient must be linear (degree 1).

Let the quotient be \( ax + b \), where \( a \) and \( b \) are constants. Then:

\( P(x) = (ax + b)(2x^2 + 9x + 14) \)

💡 Hint: The degree of the product equals the sum of degrees. Since \( P \) is degree 3 and the divisor is degree 2, the quotient must be degree \( 3 - 2 = 1 \).

By the Remainder Theorem:

  • When divided by \( x - 1 \), remainder is \( 50 \) \( \Rightarrow \) \( P(1) = 50 \)
  • When divided by \( x + 2 \), remainder is \( -52 \) \( \Rightarrow \) \( P(-2) = -52 \)

Substitute \( x = 1 \) into \( P(x) = (ax+b)(2x^2+9x+14) \):

\( (a(1)+b)(2(1)^2+9(1)+14) = 50 \)
\( (a+b)(2+9+14) = 50 \)
\( 25(a+b) = 50 \)
\( a + b = 2 \)   ...(1)

Substitute \( x = -2 \):

\( (a(-2)+b)(2(-2)^2+9(-2)+14) = -52 \)
\( (-2a+b)(8 - 18 + 14) = -52 \)
\( (-2a+b)(4) = -52 \)
\( -2a + b = -13 \)   ...(2)

💡 Hint: Always simplify the quadratic part first before solving the system of equations.

We have the system:

\( \begin{cases} a + b = 2 \\ -2a + b = -13 \end{cases} \)

Subtract (2) from (1):

\( (a+b) - (-2a+b) = 2 - (-13) \)
\( 3a = 15 \)
\( a = 5 \)

Substitute \( a = 5 \) into (1):

\( 5 + b = 2 \)
\( b = -3 \)

The quotient is \( ax + b = 5x - 3 \).

✅ Answer (a): The required quotient is \( 5x - 3 \).

Substituting \( a = 5 \), \( b = -3 \):

\( P(x) = (5x - 3)(2x^2 + 9x + 14) \)

To find rational roots of \( P(x) = 0 \):

\( (5x - 3)(2x^2 + 9x + 14) = 0 \)

So either \( 5x - 3 = 0 \) or \( 2x^2 + 9x + 14 = 0 \).

For \( 2x^2 + 9x + 14 = 0 \), compute the discriminant \( \Delta \):

\( \Delta = 9^2 - 4(2)(14) \)
\( \Delta = 81 - 112 \)
\( \Delta = -31 < 0 \)

Since \( \Delta < 0 \), this quadratic equation has no real roots (and hence no rational roots).

💡 Hint: Rational roots must be real roots. If the discriminant is negative, no real roots exist.

From \( 5x - 3 = 0 \):

\( 5x = 3 \)
\( x = \dfrac{3}{5} \)

This is a rational number. The quadratic factor gives no other roots (real or rational).

✅ Answer (b): The equation \( P(x) = 0 \) has 1 rational root (namely \( x = \frac{3}{5} \)).

📝 Key Takeaways

  • ● Remainder Theorem: When \( P(x) \) is divided by \( x - a \), the remainder is \( P(a) \).
  • ● Degree matching: Degree of dividend = Degree of divisor + Degree of quotient (when remainder is 0 for the relevant factor).
  • ● Discriminant \( \Delta = b^2 - 4ac \): If \( \Delta < 0 \), the quadratic has no real roots — hence no rational roots.
  • ● Rational roots must come from real roots. Check each factor separately.
