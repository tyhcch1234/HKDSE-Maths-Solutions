[2020 #15]

In a box, there are 3 blue plates, 7 green plates and 9 purple plates. If 4 plates are randomly selected from the box at the same time, find

(a) the probability that 4 plates of the same colour are selected;  [3 marks]

(b) the probability that at least 2 plates of different colours are selected.  [2 marks]

1

Count the plates & the total number of outcomes

Number of Combinations\(C^{n}_{r}=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}\)Choosing \(4\) plates out of \(19\) in any order gives \(C^{19}_{4}=3\,876\) outcomes.

Blue $=3$, Green $=7$, Purple $=9$, so the total is

$$3+7+9 = 19 \text{ plates.}$$

Selecting 4 plates at the same time (order does not matter) gives the sample space

$$n(\text{total}) = C^{19}_{4} = \frac{19\cdot 18\cdot 17\cdot 16}{4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1} = 3\,876.$$

Hint: "at the same time" ⇒ use combinations $C^n_r$, not permutations.
2

(a) Count the favourable outcomes

Combinations with r Greater than n\(r>n\ \Rightarrow\ C^{n}_{r}=0\)Only \(3\) blue plates exist, so \(C^{3}_{4}=0\) — four blue plates cannot be drawn.

A "same colour" selection is 4 blue, or 4 green, or 4 purple.

Only 3 blue plates exist,
so 4 blue cannot occur:

$$C^{3}_{4}=0,\qquad C^{7}_{4}=35,\qquad C^{9}_{4}=126.$$

Number of favourable outcomes $=0+35+126=161$.

Hint: $C^n_r = 0$ whenever $r>n$ — you cannot pick 4 from only 3 plates.
3

(a) Compute the required probability

$$P(\text{same colour}) = \frac{C^{7}_{4}+C^{9}_{4}}{C^{19}_{4}} = \frac{161}{3\,876}.$$

The fraction is already in lowest terms (since $161 = 7\cdot 23$).

Marks: 1M (using $C$ expressions) + 1M (adding the terms) + 1A (answer).
4

(b) Use the complementary event

Complement Rule\(P(A)=1-P(A^{\prime})\)“At least \(2\) colours” is the complement of “all \(4\) plates the same colour”, so one subtraction finishes part (b).

"At least 2 plates of different colours" means we exclude the case where all 4 plates are the same colour (that is the only case with just one colour used). So the complement is exactly the event in part (a).

$$P(\text{at least 2 different colours}) = 1 - P(\text{all 4 the same colour}).$$

Hint: "at least 2 colours" ⇔ NOT "exactly one colour". Use $1-P(\text{complement})$ to save work.
5

(b) Final calculation

$$= 1 - \frac{161}{3\,876} = \frac{3\,876-161}{3\,876} = \frac{3\,715}{3\,876}.$$

Since $3\,715 = 5\cdot 743$ and $3\,876 = 2^2\cdot 3\cdot 17\cdot 19$, the fraction is in lowest terms.

Marks: 1M (using $1-\frac{161}{3876}$) + 1A (answer).
Answer:  (a) $\dfrac{161}{3\,876}$   (b) $\dfrac{3\,715}{3\,876}$

Question. A box contains \(4\) red, \(6\) blue and \(5\) green cards. \(3\) cards are drawn at random from the box at the same time. Find the probability that (a) the \(3\) cards are of the same colour,
(b) at least \(2\) different colours are drawn.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total outcomes \(=C^{15}_{3}=\dfrac{15\cdot14\cdot13}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=455\).
Step 2. Same colour: \(C^{4}_{3}+C^{6}_{3}+C^{5}_{3}=4+20+10=34\).
Step 3. (a) \(P(\text{same colour})=\dfrac{34}{455}\).
Step 4. (b) By the complement rule, \(P(\text{at least 2 colours})=1-\dfrac{34}{455}=\dfrac{421}{455}\).
Answer: (a) \(\dfrac{34}{455}\) (b) \(\dfrac{421}{455}\)
