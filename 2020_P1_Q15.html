Probability Combinations $C_r^n$ Complementary Events Without Replacement

QQuestion

In a box, there are 3 blue plates, 7 green plates and 9 purple plates. If 4 plates are randomly selected from the box at the same time, find

  1. the probability that 4 plates of the same colour are selected; [3 marks]
  2. the probability that at least 2 plates of different colours are selected. [2 marks]
Blue · 3
Green · 7
Purple · 9

Total number of plates: $3+7+9 = 19$

SGuided Solution — Step by Step

Tap each step to reveal the reasoning. Try to think ahead before you open it!

The 4 plates are selected at the same time, so the order in which they come out does not matter. Therefore we use combinations $C_r^n$.

Hint: $C_r^n=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}$ gives the number of ways to choose $r$ objects from $n$ distinct objects, order irrelevant.

Key phrase: “at the same time” → order does not matter → use $C$.

There are $19$ plates in the box and we choose $4$ of them.

$$C_4^{19}=\frac{19!}{4!\,15!}=3\,876$$

So there are $3\,876$ equally likely selections — this is the total number of outcomes.

For all 4 plates to be the same colour, that colour must have at least 4 plates in the box.

  • Blue — only 3 plates → impossible to pick 4 blues. ✗
  • Green — 7 plates → choose 4 from 7. ✓
  • Purple — 9 plates → choose 4 from 9. ✓
Common trap: Including $C_4^3$ for blue. Since $C_4^3$ is undefined (you cannot choose 4 plates from only 3), blue contributes nothing.

Number of favourable selections $=C_4^{7}+C_4^{9}=35+126=161$.

$$P(\text{4 same colour})=\frac{C_4^{7}+C_4^{9}}{C_4^{19}}$$ $$=\frac{35+126}{3\,876}=\frac{161}{3\,876}$$
(a) $\dfrac{161}{3\,876}$

Marking scheme: 1M + 1M for the two combination terms, 1A for the final answer.

“At least 2 plates of different colours” means the 4 plates are not all of one colour.

The complement is exactly the event in part (a): all 4 plates are the same colour.

Hint — Complement rule: $P(E)=1-P(E')$, where $E'$ is the event “not $E$”.

This is much faster than counting every mixed-colour case one by one.
$$P(\text{at least 2 different colours})=1-\frac{161}{3\,876}$$ $$=\frac{3\,876-161}{3\,876}=\frac{3\,715}{3\,876}$$
(b) $\dfrac{3\,715}{3\,876}$

Marking scheme: 1M for using $1-\dfrac{161}{3\,876}$, 1A for the final answer.

$\dfrac{161}{3\,876}\approx 0.04154$ and $\dfrac{3\,715}{3\,876}\approx 0.9585$.

  • Both are between $0$ and $1$. ✓
  • They add up to $1$. ✓
  • Picking mixed colours is far more likely than picking all the same colour. ✓
Presentation tip: The answer must be a fraction (or a value correct to 3 significant figures). Leaving it as $\frac{C_4^7+C_4^9}{C_4^{19}}$ without simplification may not earn the A mark.

✓Key Takeaways

  • “Selected at the same time” → order does not matter → use combinations $C_r^n$.
  • Always write the total number of outcomes first: here $C_4^{19}=3\,876$.
  • For “same colour”, check that each colour actually has enough plates — blue has only 3, so it contributes 0.
  • For “at least …”, look for the complement (“none …” or “all …”). Here part (b) is simply $1-$ the answer of part (a).
  • Final answers should be simplified fractions or values correct to 3 significant figures.
