HKDSE 2014 · Paper 1 · Q13

Rate, Ratio and Variations

It is given that $f(x)$ is the sum of two parts, one part varies as $x^{2}$ and the other part is a constant. Suppose that $f(2)=59$ and $f(7)=-121$.

[4 marks] (a) Find $f(6)$.
[4 marks] (b) $A\,(6,a)$ and $B\,(-6,b)$ are points lying on the graph of $y=f(x)$. Find the area of $\triangle ABC$, where $C$ is a point lying on the $x$-axis.

Step-by-step Solution

Step 1 — Set up the model with variation language
Partial Variation \(f(x)=px^{2}+q\) One part varies as \(x^{2}\) and the other part is constant, so the model contains two constants \(p\) and \(q\).
"One part varies as $x^{2}$" and "the other part is a constant" gives \[ f(x)=px^{2}+q, \qquad p,q \text{ non-zero constants}. \] Translate the words “varies as” into a constant of variation $p$, plus a fixed constant $q$.
Step 2 — Form two equations from the given values Using $f(2)=59$: \[ p(2)^{2}+q = 59 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4p+q=59 \quad\text{… (1)} \] Using $f(7)=-121$: \[ p(7)^{2}+q = -121 \;\Longrightarrow\; 49p+q=-121 \quad\text{… (2)} \] Substitute $x=2$ and $x=7$ separately to build a $2\times 2$ system.
Step 3 — Solve for $p$ and $q$ $(2)-(1)$: \[ 45p=-180 \;\Longrightarrow\; p=-4. \] Substitute $p=-4$ into (1): \[ 4(-4)+q=59 \;\Longrightarrow\; q=75. \] Therefore, \[ f(x)=75-4x^{2}. \] Elimination is fastest here — the $q$ terms cancel when subtracting.
Step 4 — (a) Evaluate $f(6)$ \[ f(6)=75-4(6)^{2}=75-144=-69. \] Substitute $x=6$ into the completed formula — no need to re-solve.
Step 5 — (b) Find $a$ and $b$ from the graph
Even Function \(f(-x)=f(x)\) \(f\) contains only \(x^{2}\), so \(f(-6)=f(6)\) and hence \(a=b\).
Since $A\,(6,a)$ lies on $y=f(x)$: \[ a=f(6)=-69. \] Since $B\,(-6,b)$ lies on $y=f(x)$: \[ b=f(-6)=75-4(-6)^{2}=75-144=-69. \] $f$ is even ($f(-6)=f(6)$) because it contains only $x^{2}$ — so $a=b$.
Step 6 — Identify a convenient point $C$ on the $x$-axis Take $C$ as the point where the symmetry axis of the parabola meets the $x$-axis. Because $A$ and $B$ are symmetric about the $y$-axis, choose \[ C\,(0,0)\quad(\text{equivalently the $x$-intercept pair at } x=\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{75}}{2}). \] Any point on the $x$-axis works; choosing $x=0$ makes the base horizontal and the height easy.
Step 7 — Compute the base $AB$ $A$ and $B$ share the same $y$-coordinate, so $AB$ is horizontal: \[ AB = 6-(-6)=12. \] Horizontal distance = difference of $x$-coordinates.
Step 8 — Compute the height and the area
Area of a Triangle \(A=\dfrac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{height}\) \(AB\) is horizontal with length \(12\), and the perpendicular height from \(C(0,0)\) to \(y=-69\) is \(69\).
The perpendicular height from $C(0,0)$ to the line $y=-69$ is $69$. Hence \[ \text{Area of }\triangle ABC=\frac{12\times 69}{2}=414. \] Area $=\tfrac12\times$ base $\times$ vertical height (since $AB // x$-axis).
Answer:   (a) $f(6)=-69$   |   (b) Area of $\triangle ABC = 414$ square units.

Question. It is given that one part of \(f(x)\) varies as \(x^{2}\) and the other part is a constant. If \(f(3)=21\) and \(f(6)=-60\),
(a) find \(f(5)\);
(b) \(A(5,a)\) and \(B(-5,b)\) lie on the graph of \(y=f(x)\) and \(C\) is the point \((0,0)\). Find the area of \(\triangle ABC\). [4 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write \(f(x)=px^{2}+q\), where \(p,q\) are non-zero constants.
Step 2. From \(f(3)=21\): \(9p+q=21\) \u2026 (1);
from \(f(6)=-60\): \(36p+q=-60\) \u2026 (2).
Step 3. (2) \(- \) (1): \(27p=-81\), so \(p=-3\);
then \(9(-3)+q=21\) gives \(q=48\), i.e. \(f(x)=48-3x^{2}\).
Step 4. (a) \(f(5)=48-3(25)=48-75=-27\).
Step 5. (b) \(a=f(5)=-27\) and \(b=f(-5)=48-3(25)=-27\), so \(AB=5-(-5)=10\) and the height from \(C(0,0)\) is \(27\).
