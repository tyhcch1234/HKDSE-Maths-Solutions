If the roots of the equation \((\log_\pi x)^2 - 10\log_\pi x + 24 = \log_\pi x\) are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), then \(\alpha\beta =\)

A. \(\pi^{10}\)
B. \(\pi^{11}\)
C. \(\log_\pi 10\)
D. \(\log_\pi 11\)
💡 Core idea: Substitute \(u = \log_\pi x\) to turn the equation into a quadratic. Then use sum and product of roots to find \(\log_\pi(\alpha\beta)\).

🔍 Step-by-step solution

1

Substitute \(u = \log_\pi x\)

The equation becomes a quadratic in \(u\). Bring all terms to one side.

\[ (\log_\pi x)^2 - 10\log_\pi x + 24 = \log_\pi x \] \[ \Rightarrow u^2 - 10u + 24 = u \] \[ \Rightarrow u^2 - 11u + 24 = 0 \]
Hint: \(\log_\pi x\) appears three times – treating it as a single variable \(u\) simplifies the equation.
2

Identify the roots

Let \(u_1 = \log_\pi \alpha\) and \(u_2 = \log_\pi \beta\). Since \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the roots of the original equation, \(u_1\) and \(u_2\) are the roots of \(u^2 - 11u + 24 = 0\).

Hint: The transformation \(u = \log_\pi x\) is one-to-one, so each \(x\) gives one \(u\) and vice versa.
3

Sum of roots (Vieta's formula)

For the quadratic \(u^2 - 11u + 24 = 0\), the sum of roots:

\[ u_1 + u_2 = -\frac{(-11)}{1} = 11 \]

That is, \(\log_\pi \alpha + \log_\pi \beta = 11\).

4

Use log law to combine logs

\[ \log_\pi \alpha + \log_\pi \beta = \log_\pi(\alpha\beta) \]

Therefore:

\[ \log_\pi(\alpha\beta) = 11 \]
5

Convert back to exponential form

From \(\log_\pi(\alpha\beta) = 11\):

\[ \alpha\beta = \pi^{11} \]

Hence the answer is \(\pi^{11}\) (option B).
