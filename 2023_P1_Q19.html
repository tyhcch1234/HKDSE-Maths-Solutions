QUESTION 19  [2023#19]

The coordinates of the points $P$ and $Q$ are $(50,\,0)$ and $(32,\,t)$ respectively, where $t>0$. Denote the origin by $O$. Let $R$ be a point such that $OQ$ is a median of $\Delta OPR$. Suppose that $G$ and $H$ are the circumcentre and the orthocentre of $\Delta OPR$ respectively.

(a) Express the coordinates of $G$ and $H$ in terms of $t$.  [5 marks]

(b) Let $S$ be a point lying on $OP$ such that $QS$ is perpendicular to $OP$. It is given that $\angle PQS=\angle POQ$.

(i) By considering $\tan\angle PQS$, prove that $t=24$.
(ii) Are $O$, $G$ and $Q$ collinear? Explain your answer.
(iii) Denote the in-centre of $\Delta OPR$ by $I$. Find the ratio of the area of $\Delta GHR$ to the area of $\Delta IPQ$.  [7 marks]

STEP 1 · KEY SET-UP

$OQ$ is a median of $\Delta OPR$, so it meets $PR$ at its midpoint $Q$. Hence $R$ is the reflection of $P$ in $Q$:

$$R = 2Q-P = (2\cdot 32-50,\;2t-0) = (14,\,2t).$$
Hint: because $Q$ is the midpoint of $PR$, the $x$-coordinate of $R$ is $2(32)-50=14$, i.e. $R$ has $x=14$ — this is used again in part (a) and
(b)(iii).
STEP 2 · (a) CIRCUMCENTRE G
Circumcentre of a Triangle \(GA=GB=GC\) The centre is found where the perpendicular bisectors of \(GH\) and \(HI\) meet, giving \(G\).

The circumcentre lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PR$, which is the line through $Q$ perpendicular to $PR$.

Slope of $PQ$:

$$m_{PQ}=\frac{t-0}{32-50}=-\frac{t}{18}.$$

Perpendicular bisector of $PR$ (through $Q$):

$$y-t=\frac{18}{t}\,(x-32).$$

Also $G$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $OP$. Since $O=(0,0)$ and $P=(50,0)$, the $x$-coordinate of $G$ is $25$.

Put $x=25$:

$$y=t+\frac{18}{t}(25-32)=t-\frac{126}{t}=\frac{t^2-126}{t}.$$
$G=\left(25,\;\dfrac{t^{2}-126}{t}\right)$1M for $x=25$ · 1M for the perpendicular bisector · 1A answer
STEP 3 · (a) ORTHOCENTRE H
Orthocentre of a Triangle \(AH\perp BC,\qquad BH\perp AC\) Two altitudes are intersected to obtain the orthocentre \(H\).

$H$ lies on the altitude from $O$, which is perpendicular to $PR$:

$$y=\frac{18}{t}x.$$

Also $H$ lies on the altitude from $R$, i.e. the perpendicular to $OP$. Since $OP$ is horizontal, this altitude is vertical through $R=(14,2t)$.

Put $x=14$:

$$y=\frac{18}{t}(14)=\frac{252}{t}.$$
$H=\left(14,\;\dfrac{252}{t}\right)$1M for $x=14$ · 1M for the altitude through $O$ · 1A answer
STEP 4 · (b)(i) FIND $t$

$S$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $Q$ to $OP$, so $S=(32,\,0)$ and $QS=t$.

Since $\angle PQS=\angle POQ$, their tangents are equal:

$$\tan\angle PQS=\frac{PS}{QS}=\frac{50-32}{t}=\frac{18}{t},\qquad \tan\angle POQ=\frac{t}{32}.$$ $$\frac{18}{t}=\frac{t}{32}\;\Rightarrow\; t^{2}=576.$$

Since $t>0$, we have $t=24$.

$t=24$1M for setting the tangents equal · 1 for $t=24$
STEP 5 · (b)(ii) COLLINEAR?

From (a) with $t=24$:

$$G=\left(25,\frac{576-126}{24}\right)=\left(25,\frac{75}{4}\right),\qquad Q=(32,24).$$

Slope of $OG$:

$$m_{OG}=\frac{\frac{75}{4}-0}{25-0}=\frac{3}{4}.$$

Slope of $OQ$:

$$m_{OQ}=\frac{24-0}{32-0}=\frac{3}{4}.$$

The two slopes are equal and both lines pass through $O$.

Yes — $O$, $G$ and $Q$ are collinear.1M for $G=\left(25,\frac{75}{4}\right)$ · 1A for slopes equal / collinear
STEP 6 · (b)(iii) THE IN-CENTRE $I$
Coordinates of the Incentre \(I=\dfrac{aA+bB+cC}{a+b+c}\) Each vertex is weighted by the length of the opposite side, which gives \(I(20,15)\).

$OQ\perp PR$ and $\Delta OPQ\equiv\Delta ORQ$, so $OQ$ bisects $\angle POR$ and $I$ lies on $OQ$.

With $t=24$: $P=(50,0)$, $Q=(32,24)$, $R=(14,48)$ and

$$OQ=\sqrt{32^2+24^2}=40,\qquad PQ=\sqrt{18^2+24^2}=30,\qquad OR=\sqrt{14^2+48^2}=50.$$

Let $r$ be the in-radius. Drop the perpendicular $IJ\perp OP$; then $\Delta OIJ\sim\Delta OPQ$, giving

$$\frac{OQ-r}{r}=\frac{OP}{PQ}\;\Rightarrow\;\frac{40-r}{r}=\frac{50}{30}=\frac{5}{3}.$$ $$3(40-r)=5r\;\Rightarrow\;120=8r\;\Rightarrow\;r=15.$$

$IJ=r=15$, so $I$ has $y=15$; on the line $y=\frac{3}{4}x$:

$$\frac{3}{4}x=15\;\Rightarrow\;x=20.$$ $$H=\left(14,\frac{252}{24}\right)=\left(14,\frac{21}{2}\right).$$
$I=(20,\,15)$1M for $\frac{OQ-r}{r}=\frac{OP}{PQ}$ and $r=15$
STEP 7 · (b)(iii) THE RATIO

$O$, $H$, $I$, $G$, $Q$ are collinear (all on $y=\frac{3}{4}x$), and $OQ$ is a median so $PQ=QR$.

Area ratio (same base-to-height structure, height measured along $OQ$ to base $PR$):

$$\frac{\Delta GHR}{\Delta IPQ}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(GH)(QR)}{\frac{1}{2}(IQ)(PQ)}=\frac{GH}{IQ}.$$

With $G=(25,\frac{75}{4})$, $H=(14,\frac{21}{2})$, $I=(20,15)$ on the slope-$\frac34$ line, compare the $x$-coordinates:

$$GH:HQ\ \to\ GH=25-14=11,\qquad IQ=32-20=12.$$
$\Delta GHR:\Delta IPQ=11:12$1M for reducing the ratio to $GH:IQ$ · 1A final answer

Question. \(A(0,0)\), \(B(8,0)\) and \(C(0,6)\) are three points.
(a) Find the circumcentre \(G\) of \(\triangle ABC\).
(b) Find the orthocentre \(H\) of \(\triangle ABC\).
(c) Find the radius of the circumcircle.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. The perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) is \(x=4\), and of \(AC\) is \(y=3\).
Step 2. Hence the circumcentre is \(G(4,3)\).
Step 3. \(\angle BAC=90^{\circ}\) because \(AB\) lies on the \(x\)-axis and \(AC\) on the \(y\)-axis, so the orthocentre is \(H=A=(0,0)\).
Step 4. \(r=GA=\sqrt{4^{2}+3^{2}}=5\).
Answer: (a) \(G(4,3)\) (b) \(H(0,0)\) (c) \(r=5\)
