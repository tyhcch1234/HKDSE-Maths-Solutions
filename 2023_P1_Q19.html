Coordinate geometry of a triangle: circumcentre, orthocentre, in-centre, collinearity and an area ratio.

Coordinate Geometry Circumcentre Orthocentre In-centre Collinearity Area Ratio

Question Full Statement

Read the whole question first, then work through the parts below.

The coordinates of the points \(P\) and \(Q\) are \((50,\,0)\) and \((32,\,t)\) respectively, where \(t>0\). Denote the origin by \(O\). Let \(R\) be a point such that \(OQ\) is a median of \(\Delta OPR\). Suppose that \(G\) and \(H\) are the circumcentre and the orthocentre of \(\Delta OPR\) respectively.

(a) Express the coordinates of \(G\) and \(H\) in terms of \(t\). [5 marks]

(b) Let \(S\) be a point lying on \(OP\) such that \(QS\) is perpendicular to \(OP\). It is given that \(\angle PQS=\angle POQ\).

(i) By considering \(\tan\angle PQS\), prove that \(t=24\). [3 marks]

(ii) Are \(O\), \(G\) and \(Q\) collinear? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

(iii) Denote the in-centre of \(\Delta OPR\) by \(I\). Find the ratio of the area of \(\Delta GHR\) to the area of \(\Delta IPQ\). [4 marks]

Part (a) Coordinates of \(G\) and \(H\) in terms of \(t\)

Step 11M
Note that \(OQ\) is a median of \(\Delta OPR\), so \(Q\) is the midpoint of \(PR\).
Hint: A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Since \(OQ\) is the median from \(O\), its endpoint \(Q\) lies halfway between \(P\) and \(R\).
\[ R = 2Q - P = 2(32,\,t)-(50,\,0) = (64-50,\;2t-0) = (14,\,2t) \]
Answer: \(R=(14,\,2t)\).
Step 21M
The perpendicular bisector of \(PR\) bisects \(PR\) at \(Q(32,t)\), and its slope is the negative reciprocal of the slope of \(PR\).
\[ m_{PR}=\frac{2t-0}{14-50}=\frac{2t}{-36}=-\frac{t}{18} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m_{\perp}=\frac{18}{t} \]
Hint: The circumcentre \(G\) is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors. Here \(G\) also lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(OP\).
Step 31M
The perpendicular bisector of \(OP\) is the vertical line \(x=\dfrac{50}{2}=25\). Its equation through \(Q(32,t)\) with slope \(18/t\) is
\[ y-t=\frac{18}{t}(x-32) \]
Hint: Put \(x=25\) into this equation to obtain the \(y\)-coordinate of \(G\).
Step 41A
Putting \(x=25\) into \(y-t=\dfrac{18}{t}(x-32)\):
\[ y=t+\frac{18}{t}(25-32)= t-\frac{126}{t}=\frac{t^{2}-126}{t} \]
Answer: \(\;G=\left(25,\;\dfrac{t^{2}-126}{t}\right)\).
Step 51M
The orthocentre \(H\) lies on the altitude from \(R\), which is perpendicular to \(OP\) and hence vertical, so \(x_H=x_R=14\). Also \(H\) lies on the altitude from \(O\), which is perpendicular to \(PR\):
\[ m_{PR}=-\frac{t}{18}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{altitude slope}=\frac{18}{t},\qquad y=\frac{18}{t}x \]
Hint: Substitute \(x=14\) into \(y=\dfrac{18}{t}x\) to get \(y_H\).
Step 61A
\[ x=14 \;\Longrightarrow\; y=\frac{18}{t}\cdot 14=\frac{252}{t} \]
Answer: \(\;H=\left(14,\;\dfrac{252}{t}\right)\).

Part (b)(i) Proving \(t=24\) using \(\tan\angle PQS\)

Move \(t\); only when \(t=24\) do the two angles \(\angle PQS\) and \(\angle POQ\) match.

24.00
Step 11M
Since \(\angle PQS=\angle POQ\), their tangents are equal: \(\tan\angle PQS=\tan\angle POQ\).
\(QS\perp OP\), so \(QS\) is vertical and \(S=(32,0)\). Thus \(PS=50-32=18\) and \(QS=t\).
\[ \tan\angle PQS=\frac{PS}{QS}=\frac{18}{t},\qquad \tan\angle POQ=\frac{t}{32} \]
Hint: \(\angle POQ\) is the angle at the origin in the right triangle with horizontal side \(32\) and vertical side \(t\).
Step 21M
Equating the two tangents:
\[ \frac{18}{t}=\frac{t}{32} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t^{2}=18\times 32=576 \]
Hint: \(576=24^{2}\); remember the question states \(t>0\) to pick the positive root.
Step 31A
\[ t^{2}-576=0\;\Longrightarrow\; t=24 \quad (\text{since } t>0) \]
Answer: \(\;t=24\) (proved).

Part (b)(ii) Are \(O\), \(G\) and \(Q\) collinear?

With \(t=24\), compare the slopes \(m_{OG}\) and \(m_{OQ}\).

Step 11M
By (a) with \(t=24\):
\[ G=\left(25,\;\frac{24^{2}-126}{24}\right)=\left(25,\;\frac{576-126}{24}\right)=\left(25,\;\frac{450}{24}\right)=\left(25,\;\frac{75}{4}\right) \]
and \(Q=(32,\,24)\).
Hint: Two points are collinear with \(O\) exactly when the slopes \(OG\) and \(OQ\) are equal.
Step 21M
\[ m_{OG}=\frac{\frac{75}{4}-0}{25-0}=\frac{75}{100}=\frac{3}{4} \]
\[ m_{OQ}=\frac{24-0}{32-0}=\frac{24}{32}=\frac{3}{4} \]
Answer: \(m_{OG}=m_{OQ}=\dfrac{3}{4}\), so \(O\), \(G\) and \(Q\) are collinear.

Part (b)(iii) Ratio of area of \(\Delta GHR\) to area of \(\Delta IPQ\)

Reveal the in-centre \(I\), the incircle, \(G\), \(H\) and \(R\) on the same triangle.

Step 11M
With \(t=24\): \(Q=(32,24)\), \(P=(50,0)\), \(R=(14,48)\). Note \(OQ\) is perpendicular to \(PR\) and \(\Delta OPQ\cong\Delta ORQ\), so \(OQ\) bisects \(\angle POR\). Hence the in-centre \(I\) lies on \(OQ\).
\[ OQ=\sqrt{32^{2}+24^{2}}=\sqrt{1024+576}=\sqrt{1600}=40,\qquad PQ=\sqrt{18^{2}+24^{2}}=\sqrt{324+576}=30 \]
Hint: Drop the perpendicular \(IJ\perp OP\). Then \(\Delta OIJ\sim\Delta OQP\) with \(OJ=r\).
Step 21M
Let \(r\) be the in-radius. From \(\Delta OIJ\sim\Delta OQP\):
\[ \frac{OQ-r}{r}=\frac{OP}{PQ}=\frac{50}{30} \]
\[ \frac{40-r}{r}=\frac{5}{3}\;\Longrightarrow\; 3(40-r)=5r \;\Longrightarrow\; 120=8r \;\Longrightarrow\; r=15 \]
Hint: \(I\) is \(15\) units below \(Q\) along \(OQ\) since \(r=15\) and \(OQ=40\); use the \(3\!:\!4\!:\!5\) direction of \(OQ\).
Answer: \(\;r=15\;\) and \(\;I=\left(40-15\right)\cdot\dfrac{(32,24)}{40}=(20,\,15)\).
Step 31M
From (a) with \(t=24\): \(H=\left(14,\dfrac{252}{t}\right)=\left(14,\dfrac{21}{2}\right)\) and \(G=\left(25,\dfrac{75}{4}\right)\).
\(O,\,H,\,I,\,G,\,Q\) are all collinear (all slopes \(=\tfrac34\)). Since \(OQ\) is a median, \(PQ=QR\).
\[ \frac{[\Delta GHR]}{[\Delta IPQ]}=\frac{\tfrac12 (GH)(QR)}{\tfrac12 (IQ)(PQ)}=\frac{GH}{IQ} \]
Hint: The altitude from \(R\) to line \(OGQ\) is common to both triangles, and \(PQ=QR\), so the ratio collapses to a ratio of lengths on the line.
Step 41A
Using \(x\)-coordinates along the line \(OHIGQ\) (all share the same slope, so distances are proportional to differences in \(x\)):
\[ GH=25-14=11,\qquad IQ=32-20=12 \]
Answer: \(\;\dfrac{[\Delta GHR]}{[\Delta IPQ]}=\dfrac{GH}{IQ}=\dfrac{11}{12}\), i.e. \(11:12\).

Summary of Final Answers

  • (a) Circumcentre: \(\;G=\left(25,\;\dfrac{t^{2}-126}{t}\right)\)
  • (a) Orthocentre: \(\;H=\left(14,\;\dfrac{252}{t}\right)\)
  • (b)(i) \(\;\tan\angle PQS=\dfrac{18}{t}=\dfrac{t}{32}=\tan\angle POQ\;\Rightarrow\; t^{2}=576\;\Rightarrow\; t=24\) (since \(t>0\))
  • (b)(ii) \(\;m_{OG}=m_{OQ}=\dfrac{3}{4}\), hence \(O\), \(G\), \(Q\) are collinear
  • (b)(iii) In-centre: \(\;I=(20,\,15)\), in-radius \(r=15\)
  • (b)(iii) Ratio: \(\;\dfrac{[\Delta GHR]}{[\Delta IPQ]}=\dfrac{GH}{IQ}=\dfrac{11-0}{12}\;=\;\mathbf{11:12}\)
