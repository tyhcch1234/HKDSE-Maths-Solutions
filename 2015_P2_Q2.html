2015MC#2  ·  Laws of Indices

Question

\(\dfrac{(3y^{6})^{4}}{3y^{2}} =\)

  • A.  \(4y^{5}\)
  • B.  \(4y^{8}\)
  • C.  \(27y^{12}\)
  • D.  \(27y^{22}\)  
1

Read off the structure of the expression

The whole product \((3y^{6})\) is raised to the power \(4\) in the numerator, and the result is divided by \(3y^{2}\).

Two index laws are needed:

Power of a product / power of a power: \((ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}\) and \((a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}\) Quotient law: \(\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{m-n}\)
Hint: handle the numerator completely first, then divide.
2

Expand the numerator \((3y^{6})^{4}\)

Apply the power \(4\) to each factor inside the bracket — the coefficient \(3\) as well as \(y^{6}\):

\[ (3y^{6})^{4}=3^{4}\cdot (y^{6})^{4}=81\,y^{6\times 4}=81y^{24} \]

So the expression becomes

\[ \frac{(3y^{6})^{4}}{3y^{2}}=\frac{81y^{24}}{3y^{2}} \]

Hint: a very common mistake is to raise only the \(y\) to the 4th power and leave \(3\) unchanged.
Power of a Power \((a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}\) \(\left(y^{6}\right)^{4}=y^{6\times4}=y^{24}\), so the numerator is \(81y^{24}\).
Power of a Product \((ab)^{n}=a^{n}b^{n}\) Both the coefficient \(3\) and \(y^{6}\) must be raised to the power \(4\), giving \(3^{4}=81\).
3

Divide the coefficients, then the powers of \(y\)

Treat the fraction as two separate divisions:

\[ \frac{81y^{24}}{3y^{2}}=\frac{81}{3}\cdot\frac{y^{24}}{y^{2}} \]

Coefficients: \(\dfrac{81}{3}=27\).

Powers of \(y\) (quotient law — subtract the indices): \(y^{24-2}=y^{22}\).

Hint: \(y^{24}\div y^{2}\neq y^{12}\). Dividing powers means subtracting indices, not dividing them.
Quotient Law of Indices \(\dfrac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}=a^{m-n}\quad(a\neq0)\) For the powers of \(y\): \(y^{24-2}=y^{22}\), and \(\dfrac{81}{3}=27\).
4

Combine and check against the options

\[ \frac{(3y^{6})^{4}}{3y^{2}}= 27y^{22} \]

Comparing with the choices:

A. \(4y^{5}\) ✘   B. \(4y^{8}\) ✘ (wrong coefficient, wrong index)   C. \(27y^{12}\) ✘ (index 12 comes from \(24\div 2\))   D. \(27y^{22}\) ✔

Hint: a quick sanity check — the highest power of \(y\) inside is \(y^{6}\), raised to the 4th power gives \(y^{24}\), so the answer's index must be larger than 24 minus something small.

Answer:  D  \(\;27y^{22}\)

Question. Simplify \(\dfrac{(2y^{5})^{3}}{4y^{7}}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Power of a product and power of a power: \((2y^{5})^{3}=2^{3}\left(y^{5}\right)^{3}=8y^{15}\).
Step 2. So the expression is \(\dfrac{8y^{15}}{4y^{7}}=\dfrac{8}{4}\cdot\dfrac{y^{15}}{y^{7}}\).
Step 3. Quotient law: \(\dfrac{8}{4}=2\) and \(\dfrac{y^{15}}{y^{7}}=y^{15-7}=y^{8}\).
Step 4. Hence \(\dfrac{(2y^{5})^{3}}{4y^{7}}=2y^{8}\).
Answer: \(2y^{8}\)
