The table below shows the distribution of the numbers of tokens got by a group of children in a game.

Number of tokens got1234567
Number of children15925425

(a) Find the mean of the distribution. [2 marks]

(b) Are the median and the mode of the distribution equal? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

(c) If \(n\) more children play the game and each of them gets 5 tokens, write down

(i) the value of \(n\) such that the mean of the distribution is increased by 1;

(ii) the least value of \(n\) such that the median of the distribution is increased by 2;

(iii) the greatest value of \(n\) such that the mode of the distribution remains unchanged.

[3 marks]

Step 1 · (a) Mean Compute the total tokens and the total number of children.
\[ \text{Total children}=15+9+2+5+4+2+5=42 \]
\[\text{Mean}=\frac{1(15)+2(9)+3(2)+4(5)+5(4)+6(2)+7(5)}{42}=\frac{126}{42}=3\]
Hint: mean = (sum of frequency × value) ÷ total frequency.
Mean from a Frequency Table \(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\) \(\sum fx=126\) and \(\sum f=42\), so the mean is \(\dfrac{126}{42}=3\).
Step 2 · (b) Median There are 42 data values, so the median is the average of the 21st and 22nd values.
Cumulative frequencies: 15, 24, 34, 38, 40, 42.
The 21st and 22nd values both lie in the "2 tokens" group → median = 2.
Hint: with an even number of data, the median is the mean of the two middle values.
Median of an Even Number of Data \(\text{median}=\dfrac{x_{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}+x_{\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}}{2}\) With \(42\) children the median uses the 21st and 22nd values, which both lie in the “2 tokens” group.
Step 3 · (b) Mode The mode is the value with the highest frequency.
Frequency of "1 token" = 15 (the largest) → mode = 1.
Since \(2 \neq 1\), the median and the mode are not equal.
Hint: compare the two computed values directly.
Step 4 · (c)(i) New mean Each new child gets 5 tokens. Original mean = 3, increased by 1 → new mean = 4.
\[ \frac{126+5n}{42+n}=4 \]
\[ 126+5n=168+4n \]
\[ n=42 \]
Hint: keep the total tokens and total children updated.
Solving a Linear Equation \(\dfrac{126+5n}{42+n}=4\ \Rightarrow\ 126+5n=168+4n\) Clearing the denominator of the new mean gives \(n=42\).
Step 5 · (c)(ii) New median New median = 3 (increased by 2). With 5-token additions, the data \(1^{15},2^{9},3^{2}\) stay first; the median first reaches 3 when the \((42+n)/2\)-th positions fall in the "3 tokens" group.
Need cumulative up to "3" \(\ge \dfrac{42+n}{2}\): \(\;26 \ge \dfrac{42+n}{2} \Rightarrow n \le 10\).
Check \(n=11\) gives \(\frac{53}{2}=26.5\), positions 26 and 27 are "3" and "4" → median \(3.5\neq 3\).
Least \(n=11\) pushes the middle positions into "5" region actually gives median... so test \(n=11\): children \(=53\), 27th position. Cum: 15,24,26,31,35,37,42 → 27th is "5" (not desired).
Test \(n=22\): children \(=64\), 32nd,33rd positions; cum up to "3" = 26, up to "4" = 31, up to "5" = 35 → 32,33 both "5" → median 5.
We need median exactly 3, i.e. both middle positions in "3" group. That requires \(\frac{42+n}{2}\le 26\) and \(\frac{42+n}{2}+1\le 26\) → \(n\le 10\). For median = 3 (not 4), take \(n=10\): total = 52, 26th and 27th: cum 15,24,26 → 26th is "3", 27th is "4" → median \(= 3.5\).
So the median jumps from 2 (original) by +1 to 3.5 with \(n=10\) and later reaches exactly 4 (\(+1\)). To reach exactly 5 (\(+2\)), need mid positions in "5". Minimum \(n\): \(\frac{42+n}{2}\ge 32 \Rightarrow n\ge 22\). Test \(n=22\): total 64, 32nd & 33rd in "5" → median 5. ✓
Least \(n = 22\).
Hint: locate the middle position after adding \(n\) children at value 5.
Step 6 · (c)(iii) Unchanged mode Original mode = 1 with frequency 15. Adding children all at "5" can only raise the frequency of "5". The mode stays 1 while the count at 5 does not exceed 15.
Original frequency at "5" = 4. Need \(4+n \le 15 \Rightarrow n \le 11\).
At \(n=11\), the frequency at "5" becomes 15, tied with "1" — the mode remains "1".
Greatest \(n = 11\).
Hint: the mode stays "1" as long as "5" does not strictly exceed (or newly tie-break away from) 15.
Answer: (a) mean = 3;  (b) no, median = 2, mode = 1 (not equal);  (c)(i) n = 42, (ii) n = 22, (iii) n = 11.

Question. The table shows the numbers of tokens got by \(40\) children: \(1\) token (\(18\) children), \(2\) tokens (\(4\)), \(3\) tokens (\(4\)), \(4\) tokens (\(8\)), \(5\) tokens (\(6\)).  [7 marks]
(a) Find the mean. [2 marks]
(b) Are the median and the mode equal? Explain your answer. [2 marks]
(c) If \(n\) more children play and each gets \(5\) tokens, write down (i) the value of \(n\) such that the mean is increased by \(0.5\), (ii) the greatest value of \(n\) such that the mode remains unchanged. [3 marks]

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Total children \(=18+4+4+8+6=40\) and \(\sum fx=18+8+12+32+30=100\), so the mean is \(\dfrac{100}{40}=2.5\).
Step 2. Cumulative frequencies: \(18,22,26,34,40\). The 20th and 21st values both equal \(2\), so the median is \(2\).
Step 3. The highest frequency is \(18\) (at \(1\) token), so the mode is \(1\). Since \(2\ne1\), the median and the mode are not equal.
Step 4. (c)(i) New mean \(=2.5+0.5=3\): \(\dfrac{100+5n}{40+n}=3\) gives \(100+5n=120+3n\), so \(n=10\).
Step 5. (c)(ii) The frequency of \(5\) tokens is \(6\) and must not exceed \(18\), so \(6+n\le18\), i.e. \(n\le12\). The greatest value is \(n=12\).
Answer: (a) \(2.5\)  (b) No — median \(=2\), mode \(=1\)  (c)(i) \(n=10\) (ii) \(n=12\)
