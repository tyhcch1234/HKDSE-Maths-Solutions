HKDSE 2013 · Paper 1 · Q4 (4 marks)

Question. The price of 7 pears and 3 oranges is $\$47$ while the price of 5 pears and 6 oranges is $\$49$. Find the price of a pear.

1Define the unknowns

Let $x$ be the price of a pear and $y$ be the price of an orange (in dollars).

Hint: use one letter for each unknown price — this turns the word problem into algebra.
2Set up the two equations

From “7 pears and 3 oranges cost \$47”:

\[7x+3y=47 \qquad \cdots (1)\]

From “5 pears and 6 oranges cost \$49”:

\[5x+6y=49 \qquad \cdots (2)\]

Hint: the number of items multiplies the price of one item — each sentence becomes one equation.
Simultaneous Linear Equations \(a_{1}x+b_{1}y=c_{1},\qquad a_{2}x+b_{2}y=c_{2}\) Each purchase gives one equation in the two unknown prices \(x\) and \(y\).
3Eliminate $y$

Multiply equation (1) by 2 so that the $y$-terms match:

\[2(7x)+2(3y)=2(47) \;\Rightarrow\; 14x+6y=94 \qquad \cdots (1')\]

Subtract equation (2) from (1$'$):

\[(14x+6y)-(5x+6y)=94-49\]

\[9x=45\]

Hint: make the coefficients of one variable equal in magnitude, then subtract to cancel it.
4Solve for $x$

\[x=\frac{45}{9}=5\]

(Check: from (1), $7(5)+3y=47 \Rightarrow 3y=12 \Rightarrow y=4$; then $5(5)+6(4)=25+24=49$ ✓)

Hint: divide both sides by the coefficient of $x$; always substitute back to verify.
Solving a Linear Equation \(ax+b=c\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\dfrac{c-b}{a}\quad(a\neq0)\) After \(9x=45\) the price follows by dividing both sides by the coefficient \(9\).
Answer: The price of a pear is $\$5$.

Question. 3 apples and 4 oranges cost \$46, while 5 apples and 2 oranges cost \$44. Find the price of an apple and the price of an orange.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(\$x\) be the price of an apple and \(\$y\) the price of an orange.
Step 2. \(3x+4y=46\quad\cdots(1)\) and \(5x+2y=44\quad\cdots(2)\).
Step 3. Multiply (2) by 2: \(10x+4y=88\quad\cdots(2')\). Then \((2')-(1)\): \(7x=42\), so \(x=6\).
Step 4. Substitute into (1): \(18+4y=46\), so \(y=7\). Check: \(5(6)+2(7)=44\) ✓
Answer: An apple costs \$6 and an orange costs \$7.
