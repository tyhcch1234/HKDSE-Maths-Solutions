HKDSE 2013 · Paper 1 · Question 4

Prices of Pears and Oranges — Simultaneous Linear Equations

Simultaneous Equations Two Unknowns Elimination Method 4 Marks

The Question

The price of 7 pears and 3 oranges is \$47, while the price of 5 pears and 6 oranges is \$49. Find the price of a pear.

Scenario A — total \$47

🍐×7 + 🍊×3 = \$47

Scenario B — total \$49

🍐×5 + 🍊×6 = \$49

Step-by-Step Guided Solution

Tap each step to reveal the working. Try the hints first!

1 Define the unknowns ＋

Let \(x\) be the price of a pear and \(y\) be the price of an orange.

Hint: Two different items → two unknowns. Always state what each letter represents (this earns the "definition" mark).
2 Form the two equations ＋

From Scenario A and Scenario B:

$$\begin{cases} 7x + 3y = 47 \\[4pt] 5x + 6y = 49 \end{cases}$$
Hint: "7 pears at \\(x\\) each + 3 oranges at \\(y\\) each costs \$47" → \\(7x + 3y = 47\\). Do the same for the second sentence. (1A + 1A)
Labels: equation (1)  and  equation (2).
3 Spot the shortcut: eliminate \(y\) ＋

Notice the coefficients of \(y\) are 3 and 6. Multiply equation (1) by 2 so both equations have \(6y\):

$$2 \times (1):\quad 14x + 6y = 94 \tag{1'}$$
Hint: When one coefficient is a multiple of the other, scaling is the fastest way to eliminate that variable.
4 Subtract to find \(x\) ＋

Subtract equation (2) from equation (1'):

$$2(7x) - 5x = 2(47) - 49$$
$$14x - 5x = 94 - 49$$ $$9x = 45$$ $$x = 5$$
Hint: \\(2(7x)-5x = 14x - 5x = 9x\\) and \\(2(47)-49 = 94-49 = 45\\). (1M)
5 Check and conclude ＋

Substitute \(x = 5\) back into equation (1):

$$7(5) + 3y = 47 \Rightarrow 35 + 3y = 47 \Rightarrow 3y = 12 \Rightarrow y = 4$$

Check with equation (2): \(5(5) + 6(4) = 25 + 24 = 49\) ✓

Therefore, the price of a pear is \$5.

Hint: Always substitute back to verify — it protects your final answer mark. (1A)
