Question [2020MC#27]

The equations of the circles \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are

\[ 2x^2 + 2y^2 + 4x + 8y - 149 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad x^2 + y^2 - 8x - 20y - 53 = 0 \]

respectively. Which of the following are true?

  1. The centre of \(C_1\) lies on \(C_2\).
  2. The radii of \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are equal.
  3. \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) intersect at two distinct points.

A.  I only     B.  II only     C.  I and III only     D.  II and III only

Statement I Centre of \(C_1\) on \(C_2\)?

1 Reduce \(C_1\) to standard form

The equation \(2x^2+2y^2+4x+8y-149=0\) has a common factor 2. Divide throughout by 2:

\[ x^2+y^2+2x+4y-\tfrac{149}{2}=0 \]
Hint: Always divide by the coefficient of \(x^2\) so that the leading coefficients become 1 before completing the square.
2 Find the centre of \(C_1\)

Comparing with \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\), the centre is \(\left(-\tfrac{D}{2},-\tfrac{E}{2}\right)\):

\[ G_1 = \left(-\tfrac{2}{2},\,-\tfrac{4}{2}\right) = (-1,\,-2) \]
Centre of \(C_1\): \(G_1=(-1,-2)\)
3 Test whether \(G_1\) satisfies \(C_2\)

Put \((x,y)=(-1,-2)\) into \(x^2+y^2-8x-20y-53=0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{L.H.S.} &= (-1)^2+(-2)^2-8(-1)-20(-2)-53 \\ &= 1+4+8+40-53 = 0 = \text{R.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]
Hint: A point lies on a circle iff its coordinates satisfy the circle's equation. Here L.H.S. \(=\) R.H.S. \(=0\), so \(G_1\) lies on \(C_2\).
Statement I is TRUE

Statement II Equal radii?

1 Radius formula from the general equation

For a circle \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\), the centre is \(\left(-\tfrac{D}{2},-\tfrac{E}{2}\right)\) and

\[ r=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{D}{2}\right)^2+\left(\tfrac{E}{2}\right)^2-F} \]
Hint: This comes from completing the square: \(\left(x+\tfrac{D}{2}\right)^2+\left(y+\tfrac{E}{2}\right)^2=\tfrac{D^2+E^2}{4}-F\).
2 Radius of \(C_1\)

\(C_1:\; x^2+y^2+2x+4y-\tfrac{149}{2}=0\), so \(D=2,\;E=4,\;F=-\tfrac{149}{2}\):

\[ r_1=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{2}{2}\right)^2+\left(\tfrac{4}{2}\right)^2-\left(-\tfrac{149}{2}\right)} =\sqrt{1+4+\tfrac{149}{2}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{159}{2}}\approx 8.916 \]
3 Radius of \(C_2\)

\(C_2:\; x^2+y^2-8x-20y-53=0\), so \(D=-8,\;E=-20,\;F=-53\):

\[ r_2=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{-8}{2}\right)^2+\left(\tfrac{-20}{2}\right)^2-(-53)} =\sqrt{16+100+53}=\sqrt{169}=13 \]
Hint: \(r_1=\sqrt{79.5}\approx 8.916 \neq 13 = r_2\). The radii are not equal.
\(r_1=\sqrt{\tfrac{159}{2}}\approx 8.92,\quad r_2=13\) — Statement II is FALSE ✗

Statement III Two distinct intersections?

1 Centre of \(C_2\)

From \(C_2:\;x^2+y^2-8x-20y-53=0\), \(D=-8,\;E=-20\):

\[ G_2=\left(-\tfrac{-8}{2},\,-\tfrac{-20}{2}\right)=(4,\,10) \]
Centre of \(C_2\): \(G_2=(4,10)\)
2 Distance between the centres
\[ G_1G_2=\sqrt{(4-(-1))^2+(10-(-2))^2} =\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=13 \]
Hint: Notice \((5,12,13)\) is a Pythagorean triple — the distance is exactly \(13\).
3 Apply the intersection criterion

Two circles intersect at two distinct points if and only if

\[ |r_2-r_1| < G_1G_2 < r_1+r_2 \]

Compute the bounds:

\[ r_2-r_1 = 13-\sqrt{79.5}\approx 4.08,\qquad r_1+r_2 = 13+\sqrt{79.5}\approx 21.92 \]

And \(G_1G_2=13\). Since

\[ 4.08 < 13 < 21.92 \]

the circles intersect at two distinct points.

Statement III is TRUE

Summary — Answer at a Glance

  • \(C_1:\;x^2+y^2+2x+4y-\tfrac{149}{2}=0\)  ⟹  \(G_1=(-1,-2)\), \(r_1=\sqrt{\tfrac{159}{2}}\approx 8.916\)
  • \(C_2:\;x^2+y^2-8x-20y-53=0\)  ⟹  \(G_2=(4,10)\), \(r_2=13\)
  • Statement I: \(G_1\) on \(C_2\)?  TRUE  (L.H.S. \(=0\))
  • Statement II: \(r_1=r_2\)?  FALSE ✗  (\(8.916\neq 13\))
  • Statement III: Two distinct intersections?  TRUE  (\(4.08<13<21.92\))
  • Therefore the correct choice is C. I and III only
