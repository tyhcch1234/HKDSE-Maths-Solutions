Inter-quartile Range

The bar chart below shows the distribution of the numbers of books read by some teachers in a certain month. Find the inter-quartile range of the distribution.

  • A. 1
  • B. 2
  • C. 3
  • D. 4
STEP 1Read the frequencies from the bar chart
Books read $x$34567
No. of teachers $f$48622
Total number of teachers: $n = 4+8+6+2+2 = 22$.
Use the grid lines (each 1 unit) to read the heights accurately.
STEP 2Build the cumulative frequency table
\[ \begin{array}{c|ccccc} x & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7\\ \hline f & 4 & 8 & 6 & 2 & 2\\ \text{cum. } f & 4 & 12 & 18 & 20 & 22 \end{array} \]
Cumulative frequency tells us where the quartiles sit in the ordered data.
Cumulative Frequency \(\text{cum. }f_{i}=f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots+f_{i}\) Adding the frequencies running down the table shows which group holds each quartile.
STEP 3Find the lower quartile $Q_1$
With $n=22$, the lower quartile is the $\tfrac{n+1}{4}=\tfrac{23}{4}=5.75$th value — i.e. the median of the lower 11 values, which is the $6$th value.
The cumulative frequency first reaches 6 inside the group $x=4$ (cum. $f$: $4$ then $12$). \[ Q_1 = 4 \]
$4 < 6 \le 12$, so the 6th data value is 4 books.
STEP 4Find the upper quartile $Q_3$
The upper quartile is the median of the upper 11 values, i.e. the $17$th value.
Cumulative frequency reaches 17 inside the group $x=5$ (cum. $f$: $12$ then $18$). \[ Q_3 = 5 \]
$12 < 17 \le 18$, so the 17th data value is 5 books.
STEP 5Compute the inter-quartile range
\[ \text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 5 - 4 = 1 \]
The inter-quartile range measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
Interquartile Range \(\text{IQR}=Q_{3}-Q_{1}\) The interquartile range is the difference of the two quartiles just found.
Answer: A  (IQR $= Q_3 - Q_1 = 5-4 = 1$)

Question. The table shows the number of books \(x\) read by \(20\) teachers in a month. \(\;x=1,2,3,4,5\;\) with frequencies \(\;4,4,6,4,2\;\) respectively. Find the interquartile range of the data.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. There are \(n=4+4+6+4+2=20\) teachers.
Step 2. Cumulative frequencies: \(4,\ 8,\ 14,\ 18,\ 20\).
Step 3. Lower quartile: the \(5\)th and \(6\)th values both lie in the group \(x=2\) (cum. \(f\) from \(4\) to \(8\)), so \(Q_{1}=2\).
Step 4. Upper quartile: the \(15\)th and \(16\)th values both lie in the group \(x=4\) (cum. \(f\) from \(14\) to \(18\)), so \(Q_{3}=4\).
Step 5. IQR \(=Q_{3}-Q_{1}=4-2=2\).
Answer: \(\text{IQR}=2\)
