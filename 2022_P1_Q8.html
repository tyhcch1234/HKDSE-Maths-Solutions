Move the sliders — the quadrilateral always keeps \(AC \parallel ED\) and \(AD \parallel BC\), so you can see how \(\angle ACD\) responds.

Part (a) Prove \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AED\)

We prove the two triangles are congruent using AAS.

Step 11M

Use the two pairs of parallel lines to get a pair of equal angles.

\[ \angle ACB = \angle CAD \quad (\text{alt. } \angle s,\ AD \parallel BC) \]

\[ \angle CAD = \angle ADE \quad (\text{alt. } \angle s,\ AC \parallel ED) \]

Hence \[ \angle ACB = \angle ADE. \]

Hint: A transversal cutting parallel lines gives alternate angles. Think of \(AC\) as the transversal for the pair \(AD \parallel BC\), and \(AD\) as the transversal for the pair \(AC \parallel ED\).
Step 21A

List the three matching pieces of information.

\[ \angle ABC = \angle AED \quad (\text{given}) \] \[ \angle ACB = \angle ADE \quad (\text{proved in Step 1}) \] \[ AB = AE \quad (\text{given}) \]

Hint: The equal side \(AB = AE\) lies between \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle ACB\) in \(\triangle ABC\), and between \(\angle AED\) and \(\angle ADE\) in \(\triangle AED\) — so this is an AAS correspondence with corresponding sides equal.
Step 31A

Therefore, by the AAS congruence condition,

\(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AED\)   (AAS)
Hint: Because the correspondence is \(A \leftrightarrow A\), \(B \leftrightarrow E\), \(C \leftrightarrow D\), it follows that \(AC = AD\) and \(\angle BAC = \angle DAE\). These will be very useful in part (b).

Part (b) Find \(\angle ACD\)

Using \(\angle ABC = 39^\circ\) and \(\angle DAE = 87^\circ\).

Step 11M

From the congruence in part (a), corresponding angles are equal:

\[ \angle BAC = \angle DAE = 87^\circ. \]

Hint: \(\angle BAC\) and \(\angle DAE\) are corresponding angles of the congruent triangles \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AED\).
Step 21M

In \(\triangle ABC\), apply the angle sum of a triangle:

\[ \angle ACB = 180^\circ - \angle BAC - \angle ABC = 180^\circ - 87^\circ - 39^\circ = 54^\circ. \]

Hint: The three interior angles of any triangle add up to \(180^\circ\).
\(\angle ACB = 54^\circ\)
Step 31A

By alternate angles with \(AD \parallel BC\) and transversal \(AC\),

\[ \angle CAD = \angle ACB = 54^\circ. \]

Also, from the congruence in part (a), \(AC = AD\). So \(\triangle ACD\) is an isosceles triangle with vertex angle \(\angle CAD = 54^\circ\) at \(A\).

\[ \angle ACD = \angle ADC = \frac{180^\circ - \angle CAD}{2} = \frac{180^\circ - 54^\circ}{2} = \frac{126^\circ}{2} = 63^\circ. \]

Hint: In an isosceles triangle, the two base angles are equal. Since \(AC = AD\), the base angles at \(C\) and \(D\) are equal, and together they must make \(180^\circ - 54^\circ = 126^\circ\).
\(\angle ACD = 63^\circ\)

Summary Final answers

  • (a) \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AED\) — via AAS: \(\angle ACB=\angle ADE\) (alternate angles), \(\angle ABC=\angle AED\) (given), \(AB=AE\) (given).
  • (b) \(\angle BAC = \angle DAE = 87^\circ\) ⟹ \(\angle ACB = 180^\circ - 87^\circ - 39^\circ = 54^\circ\).
  • \(\angle CAD = \angle ACB = 54^\circ\); since \(AC = AD\), \(\triangle ACD\) is isosceles.
  • \(\angle ACD = \dfrac{180^\circ - 54^\circ}{2} = 63^\circ\)
