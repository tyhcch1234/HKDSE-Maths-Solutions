Trigonometry — Using the converse of Pythagoras' theorem to relate \(\cos A : \cos C\)

Converse of Pythagoras' Theorem Trigonometric Ratios Ratio of Sides Right-angled Triangle

Question

In \(\Delta ABC\), \(AB:BC:AC = 8:15:17\). Find \(\cos A : \cos C\).

A. \(8:15\)
B. \(8:17\)
C. \(15:8\)
D. \(17:8\)

Click an option to check your answer.

Part (a) — Identify the largest side and test Pythagoras

The three sides are in the ratio \(8:15:17\). The largest side is the one corresponding to \(17\), i.e. \(AC\).

Step 11M

Write the three side lengths as a multiple of a common factor:

\[ AB = 8k,\quad BC = 15k,\quad AC = 17k \]
Hint: The ratio \(8:15:17\) is a scaled copy of the famous \(8\text{–}15\text{–}17\) Pythagorean triple. Whenever you see sides in such a ratio, suspect a right angle.
Step 21M

Compute the two shorter sides squared and add them:

\[ AB^2 + BC^2 = (8k)^2 + (15k)^2 = 64k^2 + 225k^2 = 289k^2 \]
Hint: \(64 + 225 = 289\). Notice \(289 = 17^2\), suggesting \(AC^2 = 289k^2\) as well.
Step 31M

Compare with the longest side squared:

\[ AC^2 = (17k)^2 = 289k^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 \]
Hint: By the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, if \(AB^2 + BC^2 = AC^2\) then the angle opposite \(AC\) (i.e. \(\angle B\)) is a right angle.
Step 41A
\(\Delta ABC\) is right-angled at \(B\), so \(AC\) is the hypotenuse.
Hint: Since the largest side \(AC\) is opposite \(\angle B\), the right angle must be at \(B\).

Part (b) — Express \(\cos A\) and \(\cos C\) using adjacent/hypotenuse

With the right angle at \(B\), the two acute angles are \(A\) and \(C\). For each, \(\cos = \dfrac{\text{adjacent leg}}{\text{hypotenuse } AC}\).

Step 11M

For angle \(A\), the adjacent side is \(AB\) and the hypotenuse is \(AC\):

\[ \cos A = \frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{8k}{17k} = \frac{8}{17} \]
Hint: "Adjacent" means the leg that touches \(\angle A\) but is not the hypotenuse — here that is \(AB\).
Step 21M

For angle \(C\), the adjacent side is \(BC\) and the hypotenuse is still \(AC\):

\[ \cos C = \frac{BC}{AC} = \frac{15k}{17k} = \frac{15}{17} \]
Hint: The hypotenuse is shared by both angles \(A\) and \(C\) since it is \(AC\).
Step 31A

Form the required ratio of cosines:

\[ \cos A : \cos C = \frac{8}{17} : \frac{15}{17} = 8 : 15 \]
\(\cos A : \cos C = 8 : 15\) → the answer is A.
Hint: The common denominator \(17\) cancels, leaving just the two legs \(AB : BC = 8 : 15\).

Summary of Answers

  • Part (a): \(AB^2 + BC^2 = 64k^2 + 225k^2 = 289k^2 = AC^2\)
  • Conclusion: By the converse of Pythagoras' theorem, \(\Delta ABC\) is right-angled at \(B\).
  • Part (b): \(\cos A = \dfrac{8}{17}\), \(\cos C = \dfrac{15}{17}\)
  • Required ratio: \(\cos A : \cos C = 8 : 15\)
  • Correct option: A
