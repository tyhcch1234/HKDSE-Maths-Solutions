HKDSE 2019 Paper 2 · Q26 · Coordinate Geometry (Locus)

Line \(L:5x-7y-14=0\) Locus line 1 (offset \(+3\)) Locus line 2 (offset \(-3\)) Moving point \(P\) & \(\perp\) foot distance \(=3\)
2019MC #26
The equation of the straight line \(L\) is \(5x-7y-14=0\).

If \(P\) is a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\) is equal to \(3\), then the locus of \(P\) is
  • A. a sector.
  • B. a square.
  • C. a parabola.
  • D. a pair of straight lines.
STEP 1 · Recall the perpendicular-distance formula For a point \(P(x,y)\) and a line \(ax+by+c=0\), \[ d=\frac{|ax+by+c|}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}. \] Here \(a=5,\;b=-7,\;c=-14\), so \(\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}=\sqrt{25+49}=\sqrt{74}\).
STEP 2 · Set the distance equal to 3 \[ \frac{|5x-7y-14|}{\sqrt{74}}=3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |5x-7y-14|=3\sqrt{74}. \]
STEP 3 · Remove the absolute value An absolute-value equation splits into two linear equations: \[ 5x-7y-14=3\sqrt{74} \qquad\text{or}\qquad 5x-7y-14=-3\sqrt{74}. \] i.e. \[ 5x-7y-(14+3\sqrt{74})=0 \] \[ 5x-7y-(14-3\sqrt{74})=0. \]
STEP 4 · Identify the locus Both equations are of the form \(5x-7y+k=0\) — straight lines. They share the same normal direction \((5,-7)\), hence they are parallel, one on each side of \(L\), each at distance \(3\) from \(L\).
Same-\(x\) and same-\(y\) coefficients ⇒ parallel. Two distinct such lines ⇒ a pair of parallel straight lines.
Answer: D — a pair of straight lines.
