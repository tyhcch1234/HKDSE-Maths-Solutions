HKDSE Mathematics — Paper 1

HKDSE Math 2014 Paper 1 Q1

Simplify an algebraic expression and express your answer with positive indices.

Laws of Indices Positive Indices Algebraic Simplification
3 marks

Question

1. Simplify the following expression and express your answer with positive indices.

$$ \frac{(xy^{-2})^{3}}{y^{4}} $$
Concept check: you need the power of a product law, the power of a power law, the quotient law, and the negative index law.

Warm-up: The Four Laws You Need

Power of a Product \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\)
Power of a Power \((a^m)^n = a^{m\times n}\)
Quotient Law \(\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\)
Negative Index \(a^{-k} = \dfrac{1}{a^{k}}\)
\(\dfrac{x^{1}}{y^{2}}\)
Drag the sliders: the expression stays in the same family \(\dfrac{x^{a}}{y^{b}}\), and we always rewrite negative powers as positive ones.

Step-by-Step Solution

1
Apply the power of a product law to the numerator 1M
▶

The numerator is \((xy^{-2})^{3}\). Raise each factor inside the bracket to the power \(3\).

\((xy^{-2})^{3} = x^{3}\,(y^{-2})^{3}\)
Law used: \((ab)^n = a^n b^n\).
Here \(a = x\), \(b = y^{-2}\) and \(n = 3\). Each factor keeps its own power; the bracket only distributes the outer power \(3\).
2
Apply the power of a power law 1M
▶

Now simplify \((y^{-2})^{3}\) by multiplying the indices.

\((y^{-2})^{3} = y^{-2 \times 3} = y^{-6}\)
\(\therefore\ (xy^{-2})^{3} = x^{3}y^{-6} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{(xy^{-2})^{3}}{y^{4}} = \frac{x^{3}y^{-6}}{y^{4}}\)
Law used: \((a^m)^n = a^{m\times n}\).
Multiply the two exponents: \(-2 \times 3 = -6\).
A common mistake is to add \(( -2 + 3 = 1 )\) instead of multiplying.
3
Apply the quotient law to the \(y\) terms 1M
▶

Both \(y\)-powers are now in one fraction. Subtract the index in the denominator from the index in the numerator.

\(\frac{x^{3}y^{-6}}{y^{4}} = x^{3} \cdot y^{-6-4} = x^{3}y^{-10}\)
Law used: \(\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\).
Here \(m = -6\) and \(n = 4\), so the new index is \(-6 - 4 = -10\).
Remember: the \(x^{3}\) term has no partner in the denominator, so its index is left unchanged.
4
Convert to positive indices — final answer 1A
▶

The question requires positive indices. Move \(y^{-10}\) into the denominator.

\(\displaystyle x^{3}y^{-10} = \frac{x^{3}}{y^{10}}\)

The \(x^{3}\) already has a positive index, so it stays in the numerator.

Answer: \(\dfrac{x^{3}}{y^{10}}\)

Law used: \(a^{-k} = \dfrac{1}{a^{k}}\).
Here \(a = y\) and \(k = 10\). Any factor with a negative index is moved to the other side of the fraction bar and its index becomes positive.
Step 0 of 4 revealed

Model Answer (Full Working)

\(\begin{aligned} \frac{(xy^{-2})^{3}}{y^{4}} &= \frac{x^{3}\,(y^{-2})^{3}}{y^{4}} & & \text{(power of a product)}\\[6pt] &= \frac{x^{3}y^{-6}}{y^{4}} & & \text{(power of a power)}\\[6pt] &= x^{3}y^{-6-4} & & \text{(quotient law)}\\[6pt] &= x^{3}y^{-10}\\[6pt] &= \frac{x^{3}}{y^{10}} & & \text{(positive indices)} \end{aligned}\)
Marking scheme: 1M for applying the power laws to the numerator, 1M for the quotient law on the \(y\) powers, 1A for the correct final answer \(\dfrac{x^{3}}{y^{10}}\) with positive indices.
