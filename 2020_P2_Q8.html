From the graph:

  • \(L_1\) rises to the right: its slope is positive.
  • \(L_2\) falls to the right: its slope is negative.

STEP 1 · FIND THE SLOPES

Rewrite each line in slope–intercept form:

\(L_1:\ x+ay+b=0\ \Rightarrow\ ay=-x-b\ \Rightarrow\ y=-\dfrac{1}{a}x-\dfrac{b}{a}\)

\(\Rightarrow\) slope of \(L_1=-\dfrac{1}{a}\), \(y\)-intercept \(=-\dfrac{b}{a}\).

\(L_2:\ bx+y+c=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=-bx-c\)

\(\Rightarrow\) slope of \(L_2=-b\), \(y\)-intercept \(=-c\).

Key point: both graphs are straight lines, so the coefficient of \(y\) in each is non-zero — no division by zero here.
