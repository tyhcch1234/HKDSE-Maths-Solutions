STEP 0 · READ THE FIGURE

From the graph:

  • \(L_2\) cuts the \(y\)-axis above \(O\): its \(y\)-intercept is positive.
  • \(L_1\) rises to the right: its slope is positive.
  • \(L_2\) falls to the right: its slope is negative.
  • \(L_1\) is steeper than \(L_2\) (slope of \(L_1\) > slope of \(L_2\)).
STEP 1 · FIND THE SLOPES

Rewrite each line in slope–intercept form:

\(L_1:\ x+ay+b=0\ \Rightarrow\ ay=-x-b\ \Rightarrow\ y=-\dfrac{1}{a}x-\dfrac{b}{a}\)

\(\Rightarrow\) slope of \(L_1=-\dfrac{1}{a}\), \(y\)-intercept \(=-\dfrac{b}{a}\).

\(L_2:\ bx+y+c=0\ \Rightarrow\ y=-bx-c\)

\(\Rightarrow\) slope of \(L_2=-b\), \(y\)-intercept \(=-c\).

Key point: both graphs are straight lines, so the coefficient of \(y\) in each is non-zero — no division by zero here.
STEP 2 · TEST I   \(c<0\)

The \(y\)-intercept of \(L_2\) is \(-c\). From the figure this intercept is positive:

\(-c>0\ \Rightarrow\ c<0\)

✔ Statement I is TRUE.

STEP 3 · TEST II   \(ab<1\)

Slope of \(L_1\) is positive: \(-\dfrac{1}{a}>0\Rightarrow a<0\).

Slope of \(L_1\) > slope of \(L_2\):

\(-\dfrac{1}{a}>-b\ \Rightarrow\ -\dfrac{1}{a}+b>0\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{ab-1}{a}>0\)

Since \(a<0\), multiplying by the negative \(a\) flips the sign:

\(ab-1<0\ \Rightarrow\ ab<1\)

✔ Statement II is TRUE.
