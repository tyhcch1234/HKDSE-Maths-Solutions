Similar Triangles from Parallel Lines — then testing a Right-angled Triangle

Similar Triangles (A.A.A.) Corresponding Sides Converse of Pythagoras Angles: alt. ∠s / vert. opp. ∠s

Question 5 marks

In Figure 2, \(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at the point \(E\). It is given that \(AC /\!/ DB\).

(a) Prove that \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\). [2 marks]

(b) Suppose that \(AB = 20\text{ cm}\), \(AC = 10\text{ cm}\), \(BD = 15\text{ cm}\) and \(CE = 7\text{ cm}\). Is \(\Delta BDE\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

The two triangles share vertex \(E\) and look like an “hourglass”. The parallel sides \(AC\) and \(DB\) give the equal angles that make the triangles similar.

Part (a) — Prove \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\) 2 marks

∠CAE = ∠BDE ∠AEC = ∠BED ∠ACE = ∠DBE
Step 11M

Since \(AC /\!/ DB\) and \(AB\) is a transversal, the alternate angles are equal:

\[ \angle CAE = \angle BDE \qquad (\text{alt. } \angle\text{s},\ AC /\!/ DB) \]
Hint: Look at the “Z-shape” formed by the parallel lines \(AC\), \(DB\) and the transversal \(AB\).
Step 21M

\(AB\) and \(CD\) intersect at \(E\), so the vertically opposite angles are equal:

\[ \angle AEC = \angle BED \qquad (\text{vert. opp. } \angle\text{s}) \]
Hint: The vertical angles at the “crossing point” \(E\) are always equal — no parallel lines needed for this one.
Step 31A

In \(\Delta ACE\), the angle sum is \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle ACE = 180^\circ - \angle CAE - \angle AEC \]

In \(\Delta BDE\):

\[ \angle DBE = 180^\circ - \angle BDE - \angle BED \]

Since the two angles on the right are equal by Steps 1 and 2, the third angles are also equal:

\[ \angle ACE = \angle DBE \qquad (\angle\text{ sum of } \Delta) \]
Hint: Two pairs of equal angles force the third pair to be equal — a two-angle statement is already enough, but writing the third makes the A.A.A. complete.
Step 41A

All three pairs of corresponding angles are equal, therefore the triangles are similar:

\[ \boxed{\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE \qquad (\text{A.A.A.})} \]
Conclusion: \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\) (A.A.A.)

Part (b) — Is \(\Delta BDE\) right-angled? 3 marks

AE = 8 cm BE = 12 cm CE = 7 cm DE = 4.67 cm AE² + CE² = 113 AC² = 100

Move the slider: only when \(AE = 8\) do we get the given \(AC = 10,\ AB=20\) data. Notice the right angle does not appear — so \(\Delta ACE\) is not right-angled, and neither is \(\Delta BDE\).

Step 11M

By (a), \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\), so corresponding sides are proportional:

\[ \frac{BE}{BD} = \frac{AE}{AC} \]
Hint: Write the ratio with the correct matching vertices — \(A \leftrightarrow B\), \(C \leftrightarrow D\), \(E \leftrightarrow E\).
Step 21M

\(E\) lies on segment \(AB\), so \(BE = AB - AE = 20 - AE\). Substituting the data:

\[ \frac{20 - AE}{15} = \frac{AE}{10} \] \[ 10(20 - AE) = 15\,AE \] \[ 200 - 10\,AE = 15\,AE \] \[ 200 = 25\,AE \] \[ AE = 8 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: The key step is realising \(BE = AB - AE\); \(E\) is between \(A\) and \(B\).
Step 31M

To test whether \(\Delta ACE\) is right-angled, compare the square of the longest side with the sum of squares of the other two. Note \(AC > AE > CE\), so \(AC\) would be the hypotenuse if it were right-angled.

\[ AC^2 = 10^2 = 100 \] \[ AE^2 + CE^2 = 8^2 + 7^2 = 64 + 49 = 113 \]

Since

\[ AE^2 + CE^2 \neq AC^2, \]

by the converse of the Pythagoras’ Theorem, \(\Delta ACE\) is not a right-angled triangle.

Hint: \(113 \neq 100\) — the numbers are close but not equal, so the triangle is not right-angled. A common trap is to guess “yes” from the picture.
Step 41A

Since \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\) from part (a), similar triangles have the same shape — if one is not right-angled, the other is not either. Therefore:

\[ \boxed{\Delta BDE \text{ is \textbf{not} a right-angled triangle.}} \]
Answer: No — \(\Delta ACE\) is not right-angled (\(AE^2 + CE^2 \neq AC^2\)), so \(\Delta BDE \sim \Delta ACE\) is also not right-angled.

Summary of Answers

  • (a) \(\angle CAE = \angle BDE\) (alt. ∠s, \(AC /\!/ DB\)); \(\angle AEC = \angle BED\) (vert. opp. ∠s); therefore \(\Delta ACE \sim \Delta BDE\) (A.A.A.).
  • (b) \(\dfrac{BE}{BD} = \dfrac{AE}{AC} \Rightarrow \dfrac{20 - AE}{15} = \dfrac{AE}{10} \Rightarrow AE = 8\text{ cm}\).
  • (b) \(AC^2 = 100\), but \(AE^2 + CE^2 = 8^2 + 7^2 = 113 \neq 100\).
  • (b) By the converse of Pythagoras’ Theorem, \(\Delta ACE\) is not right-angled; hence by similarity, \(\Delta BDE\) is not a right-angled triangle.
