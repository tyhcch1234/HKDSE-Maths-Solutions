Circle $C_1$ Circle $C_2$ Centres $G_1,G_2$ Origin $O$ Common line $OG_1G_2$

Question

The equations of the circles $C_1$ and $C_2$ are $$x^{2}+y^{2}+8x-4y-5=0$$ and $$2x^{2}+2y^{2}+8x-4y-5=0$$ respectively. Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be the centres of $C_1$ and $C_2$ respectively. Denote the origin by $O$. Which of the following is/are true?
I.  $G_1$, $G_2$ and $O$ are collinear.
II. The radii of $C_1$ and $C_2$ are equal.
III. $O$ is equidistant from $G_1$ and $G_2$.
  • A.  I only
  • B.  II only
  • C.  I and III only
  • D.  II and III only

STEP 0 · UNDERSTAND THE FIGURE

Both circles come from the same template.
Writing each equation as $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$ will reveal the centre $(h,k)$ and the radius $r$,
so we can check each statement directly on the canvas.

STEP 1 · CENTRES $G_1$ AND $G_2$
Completing the Square for a Circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \left(x+\dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y+\dfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{D^{2}+E^{2}}{4}-F\) For \(C_{1}\) this gives \((x+4)^{2}+(y-2)^{2}=25\), so \(G_{1}=(-4,2)\) and \(r_{1}=5\).

For $C_1:$ completing the square,

$$(x^2+8x)+(y^2-4y)=5$$ $$(x+4)^2-16+(y-2)^2-4=5$$ $$(x+4)^2+(y-2)^2=25$$ $$\Rightarrow\; G_1=(-4,\;2),\qquad r_1=5$$

For $C_2:$ first divide every term by $2$:

$$x^{2}+y^{2}+4x-2y-\tfrac52=0$$ $$(x+2)^2-4+(y-1)^2-1=\tfrac52$$ $$(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=\tfrac{15}{2}$$ $$\Rightarrow\; G_2=(-2,\;1),\qquad r_2=\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}}$$ (Notice $G_2$ is the midpoint of $O$ and $G_1$ — a helpful check.)
STEP 2 · CHECK STATEMENT I

Are $G_1$, $G_2$ and $O$ collinear?
 Compare the slopes:

$$\text{slope }OG_1=\frac{2-0}{-4-0}=-\frac12$$ $$\text{slope }OG_2=\frac{1-0}{-2-0}=-\frac12$$

Both lines pass through $O$ and have the same slope,
so $O$, $G_2$ and $G_1$ lie on one straight line. I is TRUE ✓

STEP 3 · CHECK STATEMENT II
Radius from the General Form \(r=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\dfrac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}\) For \(C_{2}\) (after dividing by \(2\)) this gives \(r_{2}=\sqrt{4+1+\tfrac{5}{2}}=\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}}\neq5\).

Are the radii equal?
 For $x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$, $r=\sqrt{\left(\frac D2\right)^2+\left(\frac E2\right)^2-F}$.

$$r_1=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac82\right)^2+\left(\tfrac{-4}{2}\right)^2-(-5)}=\sqrt{16+4+5}=5$$ $$r_2=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac42\right)^2+\left(\tfrac{-2}{2}\right)^2-\left(-\tfrac52\right)}=\sqrt{4+1+\tfrac52}=\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}}\approx 2.74$$

$5\ne\sqrt{\tfrac{15}{2}}$, so the radii are not equal. II is FALSE ✗

STEP 4 · CHECK STATEMENT III
Distance Formula \(AB=\sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}\) \(OG_{1}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt{5}\) but \(OG_{2}=\sqrt{5}\), so \(O\) is not equidistant from the two centres.

Is $O$ equidistant from $G_1$ and $G_2$?
 Use the distance formula:

$$OG_1=\sqrt{(-4-0)^2+(2-0)^2}=\sqrt{16+4}=\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5\approx 4.47$$ $$OG_2=\sqrt{(-2-0)^2+(1-0)^2}=\sqrt{4+1}=\sqrt{5}\approx 2.24$$

$OG_1=2\,OG_2$,
so the distances differ. III is FALSE ✗

STEP 5 · CONCLUSION
I.True
II.False
III.False

Only statement I is true ⟹ the answer is A.

Answer: A (I only)
$G_1=(-4,2)$, $G_2=(-2,1)$; $O$, $G_2$, $G_1$ are collinear since both $OG_1$ and $OG_2$ have slope $-\frac12$. However $r_1=5$ while $r_2=\sqrt{\frac{15}{2}}$, and $OG_1\ne OG_2$.
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. The equations of two circles \(C_{1}\) and \(C_{2}\) are \(x^{2}+y^{2}+8x-12y-48=0\) and \(x^{2}+y^{2}+4x-6y-3=0\) respectively, and their centres are \(G_{1}\) and \(G_{2}\). \(O\) is the origin. Which of the following are true? I. \(O\), \(G_{2}\) and \(G_{1}\) are collinear. II. The radii of \(C_{1}\) and \(C_{2}\) are equal. III. \(O\) is equidistant from \(G_{1}\) and \(G_{2}\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Complete the square. \(C_{1}\): \((x+4)^{2}+(y-6)^{2}=48+16+36=100\), so \(G_{1}=(-4,\,6)\) and \(r_{1}=10\). \(C_{2}\): \((x+2)^{2}+(y-3)^{2}=3+4+9=16\),
so \(G_{2}=(-2,\,3)\) and \(r_{2}=4\).
Step 2. Slope of \(OG_{1}=\dfrac{6-0}{-4-0}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\) and slope of \(OG_{2}=\dfrac{3-0}{-2-0}=-\dfrac{3}{2}\). Both pass through \(O\) with the same slope,
so \(O\), \(G_{2}\), \(G_{1}\) are collinear: I is true.
Step 3. \(r_{1}=10\) but \(r_{2}=4\), so the radii are not equal: II is false.
Step 4. \(OG_{1}=\sqrt{16+36}=2\sqrt{13}\approx7.21\) and \(OG_{2}=\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}\approx3.61\), so the distances differ: III is false.
Step 5. Only statement I is true.
Answer: I only.
Exam tip: When a circle equation has a leading coefficient (like $2x^2+2y^2$), divide through by it first. For a circle, always make the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ equal to $1$ before reading off the centre and radius.
