Question & Solution

13. In Figure 1, \(O\) is the centre of circle \(ABCDE\). \(AC\) is a diameter of the circle. \(BD\) and \(OC\) intersect at the point \(F\). It is given that \(\angle AED = 115^\circ\).

(a) Find \(\angle CBF\). 3 marks

(b) Suppose that \(BC \parallel OD\) and \(OB = 18\text{ cm}\). Is the perimeter of the sector \(OBC\) less than \(60\text{ cm}\)? Explain your answer. 5 marks

💡 Diagram hint: points \(A,B,C,D,E\) lie on the circle in that order, with \(AC\) as a diameter and \(B\) on the major arc \(AC\). \(E\) is on the opposite side so that \(ABED\) is a cyclic quadrilateral — that is the key to part (a)!
Step 1 · Cyclic quadrilateral ABED

Since \(A,B,E,D\) all lie on the circle, \(ABED\) is a cyclic quadrilateral. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so \(\angle ABF + \angle AED = 180^\circ\).

\[ \angle ABF + 115^\circ = 180^\circ \quad\Rightarrow\quad \angle ABF = 65^\circ \]

The exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the opposite interior angle — same result, faster.

Step 2 · Angle in a semicircle

\(AC\) is a diameter, so the angle in the semicircle is a right angle:

\[ \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]

And since \(F\) lies on \(BD\), the ray \(BF\) coincides with the ray \(BD\), so \(\angle ABF\) and \(\angle CBF\) together make up \(\angle ABC\):

\[ \angle CBF + \angle ABF = 90^\circ \]
Step 3 · Answer to (a)
\[ \angle CBF = 90^\circ - 65^\circ = 25^\circ \]

(a) \(\angle CBF = 25^\circ\)

Step 4 · Angles in part (b)

Given \(BC \parallel OD\), the alternate angle \(\angle ODF = \angle CBF = 25^\circ\). Since \(OB = OD\) (radii), \(\triangle OBD\) is isosceles, so \(\angle OBF = \angle ODF = 25^\circ\).

Also, \(\angle DOF\) is the angle at the centre standing on the same arc \(CD\) as the angle \(\angle CBF = 25^\circ\) at the circumference:

\[ \angle DOF = 2\angle CBF = 2(25^\circ) = 50^\circ \]

Now \(F\) lies on \(BD\), so the angles in \(\triangle OBF\) and \(\triangle ODF\) at \(F\) are on a straight line:

\[ \begin{aligned} \angle BOC &= 180^\circ - \angle DOF - \angle OBF - \angle ODF\\ &= 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 25^\circ - 25^\circ = 80^\circ \end{aligned} \]
Step 5 · Perimeter of sector OBC

Arc \(BC\) (radius \(18\text{ cm}\), angle \(80^\circ\)):

\[ \text{arc }BC = \frac{80^\circ}{360^\circ}\times 2\pi(18) = 8\pi \text{ cm} \]

Perimeter of sector \(OBC\) (two radii + arc):

\[ P = 8\pi + 2(18) = 8\pi + 36 \text{ cm} \]
Step 6 · Conclusion (b)

Using \(\pi > 3\):

\[ P = 8\pi + 36 > 8(3) + 36 = 24 + 36 = 60 \text{ cm} \]

Hence \(P > 60\), so the perimeter of sector \(OBC\) is not less than \(60\text{ cm}\).

(a) \(\angle CBF = 25^\circ\)

(b) \(P = 8\pi + 36 > 60\), so the perimeter is not less than \(60\text{ cm}\).
