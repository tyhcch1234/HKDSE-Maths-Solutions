Circle Geometry — Angles in a Circle, Cyclic Quadrilateral & Sector Perimeter
Angles in a Circle Cyclic Quadrilateral Angle in Semicircle Sector Perimeter Estimation

Question 8 marks

In Figure 1, \(O\) is the centre of circle \(ABCDE\). \(AC\) is a diameter of the circle. \(BD\) and \(OC\) intersect at the point \(F\). It is given that \(\angle AED = 115^\circ\) and \(OB = 18\text{ cm}\).

(a) Find \(\angle CBF\). 3 marks

(b) Suppose that \(BC /\!/ OD\) and \(OB = 18\text{ cm}\). Is the perimeter of the sector \(OBC\) less than \(60\text{ cm}\)? Explain your answer. 5 marks

Part (a) — Finding \(\angle CBF\) 3 marks

115°

Slide to change \(\angle AED\) and watch \(\angle CBF = 90^\circ - (180^\circ - \angle AED)\) update live.

Step 11M

\(ABDE\) is a cyclic quadrilateral (all four vertices lie on the circle). Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary:

\[ \angle ABF + \angle AED = 180^\circ \] \[ \angle ABF + 115^\circ = 180^\circ \]
Use the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral property. Note \(\angle ABF = \angle ABD\).
\(\angle ABF = 65^\circ\)
Step 21M

Since \(AC\) is a diameter, the angle in a semicircle is a right angle:

\[ \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]
Angle in semicircle: an angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is \(90^\circ\).
\(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\)
Step 31A

Since \(F\) lies on \(BD\), the ray \(BF\) is along \(BD\), and \(B, F, D\) are collinear. Also \(BF\) lies inside \(\angle ABC\):

\[ \angle CBF + \angle ABF = \angle ABC = 90^\circ \] \[ \angle CBF + 65^\circ = 90^\circ \]
Split \(\angle ABC\) into two parts: \(\angle ABF\) (found in Step 1) and \(\angle CBF\).
\(\angle CBF = 25^\circ\)

Part (b) — Sector \(OBC\) Perimeter vs \(60\text{ cm}\) 5 marks

18 cm
∠BOC = 80° Perimeter ≈ 61.13 cm 61.13 > 60 ✓ not less
Step 11M

Because \(BC /\!/ OD\), alternate angles give:

\[ \angle ODF = \angle CBF = 25^\circ \]
\(OD /\!/ BC\) with transversal \(BD\) — alternate angles \(\angle ODF\) and \(\angle CBF\) are equal.
\(\angle ODF = 25^\circ\)
Step 21M

\(OB = OD\) (radii of the same circle), so \(\triangle OBD\) is isosceles and the base angles are equal:

\[ \angle OBF = \angle ODF = 25^\circ \]
Two radii form an isosceles triangle: base angles at \(B\) and \(D\) are equal.
\(\angle OBF = 25^\circ\)
Step 31M

\(\angle DOF\) is the central angle subtending the same arc as the inscribed angle \(\angle DCB\). By the angle-at-centre theorem:

\[ \angle DOF = 2\angle DCB = 2\angle CBF = 2(25^\circ) = 50^\circ \]
Angle at centre = twice angle at circumference (same arc \(DB\)).
\(\angle DOF = 50^\circ\)
Step 41M & 1A

In \(\triangle ODF\), the angles on the straight line \(BD\) at \(F\) sum to \(180^\circ\). Alternatively use the angles of \(\triangle ODF\):

\[ \angle BOC = 180^\circ - \angle DOF - \angle OBF - \angle ODF \] \[ = 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 25^\circ - 25^\circ = 80^\circ \]

Perimeter of sector \(OBC\) with \(r = 18\):

\[ \frac{80}{360}\left(2\pi(18)\right) + 2(18) = 8\pi + 36 \]
Sector perimeter = arc length + 2 radii. Then compare with 60 using \(\pi > 3\).
\(\angle BOC = 80^\circ\), Perimeter \(= 8\pi + 36\)
Step 51A

Since \(\pi > 3\):

\[ 8\pi + 36 > 8(3) + 36 = 60 \] \[ \therefore \text{perimeter} > 60\text{ cm} \]
Use the simple bound \(\pi > 3\) to give a rigorous comparison without a calculator.
No — the perimeter is not less than \(60\text{ cm}\) (it exceeds \(60\text{ cm}\)).
