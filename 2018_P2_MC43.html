The Question

John and Mary take turns to throw a fair die until one of them gets a number '1' or '6'. John throws the die first. Find the probability that John gets a number '6'.

A. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
B. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
C. $\dfrac{3}{10}$
D. $\dfrac{7}{10}$

🎲 The Die — Pause Conditions

The game stops as soon as a 1 or a 6 appears. The other four faces keep the game going.

Step-by-Step Solution

Players alternate: John → Mary → John → Mary → … The game continues only while the die shows 2, 3, 4, or 5. The moment a 1 or 6 appears, the game ends.

John throws on odd turns Mary throws on even turns

John throws on his $k$-th turn — which is the $(2k-1)$-th throw overall. For John to get a '6' on that throw:

  • All previous throws (the first $2(k-1)$ throws) must be 2, 3, 4, or 5 (game continues).
  • John's $(2k-1)$-th throw must be exactly 6.

💡 Hint:

"First $2(k-1)$ throws all continue" → probability $\\left(\\frac{4}{6}\\right)^{2(k-1)}$

"The $(2k-1)$-th throw is 6" → probability $\\frac{1}{6}$

So the probability for John winning on his $k$-th turn is:

$\left(\dfrac{4}{6}\right)^{2(k-1)} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6}$

John can win on his 1st, 2nd, 3rd, … turn. Sum all these possibilities:

$P = \dfrac{1}{6} + \left(\dfrac{4}{6}\right)^{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6} + \left(\dfrac{4}{6}\right)^{4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6} + \cdots$

📘 Formula — Sum of Infinite Geometric Series:

$S = \dfrac{a}{1 - r}$,   where $|r| < 1$

Here $a = \dfrac{1}{6}$ and $r = \left(\dfrac{4}{6}\right)^2$

$P = \dfrac{\frac{1}{6}}{1 - \left(\frac{4}{6}\right)^2}$ $= \dfrac{\frac{1}{6}}{1 - \frac{16}{36}}$ $= \dfrac{\frac{1}{6}}{\frac{20}{36}}$

$= \dfrac{1}{6} \times \dfrac{36}{20} = \dfrac{36}{120} = \boldsymbol{\dfrac{3}{10}}$

Answer

C. $\dfrac{3}{10}$

John has a first-mover advantage because he throws first. His probability should be greater than $\frac{1}{2}$ of the total stopping probability.

Total P(game stops eventually) $= 1$

John wins with '6' with prob $\frac{3}{10}$

Mary wins with '6' with prob $\frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{4}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{1-(4/6)^2} = \frac{1}{5}$

$P(\text{John gets } 1) = P(\text{Mary gets } 1) = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$

Check: $\frac{3}{10} + \frac{3}{10} + \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{5} = 1$ ✅
