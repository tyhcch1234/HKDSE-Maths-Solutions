📐 HKDSE Math 2020 Paper 1 Q2

Polynomials
Factorization Common Factor Quadratic Expressions

Question

Factorize

(a)

\( \alpha^2 + \alpha - 6 \)

(b)

\( \alpha^4 + \alpha^3 - 6\alpha^2 \)

(3 marks)

💡 Step-by-Step Solution

We need to factorize the quadratic expression:

\( \alpha^2 + \alpha - 6 \)

💡 Hint: Look for two numbers that multiply to give the constant term (\(-6\)) and add up to give the coefficient of the middle term (\(+1\)).

We need two numbers whose product is \(-6\) and sum is \(+1\). Let's list the factor pairs of \(-6\):

1 and -6

Sum: -5

-1 and 6

Sum: 5

2 and -3

Sum: -1

-2 and 3

Sum: 1 ✓

-3 and 2

Sum: -1

3 and -2

Sum: 1

The correct pair is \(-2\) and \(3\).

Using the numbers \(-2\) and \(3\), we can factorize the expression:

\( \alpha^2 + \alpha - 6 = (\alpha + 3)(\alpha - 2) \)

Check: Expand \( (\alpha + 3)(\alpha - 2) = \alpha^2 - 2\alpha + 3\alpha - 6 = \alpha^2 + \alpha - 6 \). ✓

Now consider Part (b):

\( \alpha^4 + \alpha^3 - 6\alpha^2 \)

Look at each term. Every term contains \(\alpha^2\). We can factor out the common factor \(\alpha^2\):

💡 Hint: Always check for a common factor first! Factoring out \(\alpha^2\) will reduce the expression to something we already know how to factorize from Part (a).

Divide each term by \(\alpha^2\):

  • \( \frac{\alpha^4}{\alpha^2} = \alpha^2 \)
  • \( \frac{\alpha^3}{\alpha^2} = \alpha \)
  • \( \frac{6\alpha^2}{\alpha^2} = 6 \)

So, the expression becomes:

\( \alpha^4 + \alpha^3 - 6\alpha^2 = \alpha^2(\alpha^2 + \alpha - 6) \)

From Part (a), we know that \( \alpha^2 + \alpha - 6 = (\alpha + 3)(\alpha - 2) \).

Substitute this into our expression from Step 5:

\( \alpha^2(\alpha^2 + \alpha - 6) = \alpha^2(\alpha + 3)(\alpha - 2) \)

✅ Final Answer (Part b): \( \alpha^2(\alpha + 3)(\alpha - 2) \)
