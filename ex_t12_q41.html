Coordinate Geometry (117) · Sub-Topic: Locus

T12 Q41

Points $A(2, 1)$ and $B(2, 5)$ are given. Describe the locus of a point $P$ that is equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

Solution

Let $P(x,y)$ be a point on the locus. Equidistant from $A$ and $B$ means $PA=PB$.
Using the distance formula: $\sqrt{(x-2)^2+(y-1)^2}=\sqrt{(x-2)^2+(y-5)^2}$.
Square both sides: $(x-2)^2+(y-1)^2=(x-2)^2+(y-5)^2$.
Cancel $(x-2)^2$: $(y-1)^2=(y-5)^2$.
Expand: $y^2-2y+1=y^2-10y+25$.
Simplify: $8y=24$, so $y=3$.
Thus the locus is the horizontal line through the midpoint $(2,3)$ of $AB$, perpendicular to $AB$.
The locus of $P$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$, given by the horizontal line $y=3$.
