Mensuration — cylinder, cone & sphere volumes

Cylinder Volume Cone Volume Sphere Volume Similar Triangles Immersed Object

Question 9 marks total

A right circular cylindrical container of base radius \(8\text{ cm}\) and height \(64\text{ cm}\) and an inverted right circular conical vessel of base radius \(20\text{ cm}\) and height \(60\text{ cm}\) are held vertically. The container is fully filled with water. The water in the container is now poured into the vessel.

(a) Find the volume of water in the vessel in terms of \(\pi\). [2 marks]

(b) Find the depth of water in the vessel. [4 marks]

(c) If a solid metal sphere of radius \(14\text{ cm}\) is then put into the vessel and the sphere is totally immersed in the water, will the water overflow? Explain your answer. [3 marks]

Part (a) — Volume of water 2 marks

The water comes from the FULL cylinder, so we compute the cylinder's volume.

Volume of cylinder  V = π r² h = 4096π cm³
Fixed vessel capacity  Vessel = ⅓π(20²)(60) = 8000π cm³
Step 11M

The container is a right circular cylinder with base radius \(8\text{ cm}\) and height \(64\text{ cm}\). Since it is fully filled, the volume of water equals the volume of the cylinder:

\[ V = \pi r^2 h = \pi (8)^2 (64) \]
Hint: The word “fully filled” guarantees that all water is poured out — the water volume is exactly the cylinder volume.
Step 21A

Evaluate the arithmetic:

\[ V = \pi (64)(64) = 4096\pi \text{ cm}^3 \]
Answer (a): The volume of water in the vessel is \(4096\pi\text{ cm}^3\).

Part (b) — Depth of water 4 marks

When water fills a cone, the water surface is a smaller similar cone. Drag the depth slider to watch the radius grow.

Water-surface radius  r = (h/3) = 16.00 cm
Water volume  V = ⅓π r² h = 4096.0π cm³
Target volume  4096π cm³
Step 11M

Let \(h\text{ cm}\) be the depth of water in the conical vessel. The vessel has base radius \(20\text{ cm}\) and height \(60\text{ cm}\), so the water-surface radius is proportional to depth by similar triangles:

\[ \frac{r}{h} = \frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{h}{3}\text{ cm} \]
Hint: The cone apex is at the bottom (inverted). As water rises to depth \(h\), the water surface is a circle similar to the cone’s base — use the ratio \(\tfrac{r}{h}=\tfrac{20}{60}\).
Step 21M1A

Volume of the water cone = volume of water poured in:

\[ \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = 4096\pi \] Substituting \(r = \dfrac{h}{3}\): \[ \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{h}{3}\right)^2 h = 4096\pi \]
Hint: Cancel \(\pi\) from both sides first — it makes the algebra much cleaner.
Step 31A

Simplify and solve for \(h\):

\[ \frac{h^3}{27} = 4096 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h^3 = 110592 \] \[ h = \sqrt[3]{110592} = 48 \]
Answer (b): The depth of water in the vessel is \(48\text{ cm}\).

Part (c) — Will the water overflow? 3 marks

Compare the sphere’s volume with the free (empty) space left in the vessel.

Water already in vessel  4096π ≈ 12 867 cm³
Empty space remaining  8000π − 4096π = 3904π ≈ 12 265 cm³
Sphere volume  V = ⁴⁄₃π(14³) = 10976π/3 ≈ 11 493 cm³
Step 11M

First compute the volume of the vessel (a full cone with \(r=20\), \(h=60\)):

\[ V_{\text{vessel}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi (20)^2 (60) = 8000\pi\text{ cm}^3 \] The water already occupies \(4096\pi\text{ cm}^3\), so the empty space is: \[ V_{\text{free}} = 8000\pi - 4096\pi = 3904\pi\text{ cm}^3 \]
Hint: The sphere must fit inside the water plus any free space — if the sphere volume is greater than the free space, the water overflows.
Step 21M

Compute the volume of the metal sphere with radius \(14\text{ cm}\):

\[ V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi (14)^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (2744) = \frac{10976}{3}\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx 11493\text{ cm}^3 \]
Step 31A

Compare:

\[ \frac{10976}{3}\pi \approx 11493\text{ cm}^3 \quad<\quad 3904\pi \approx 12265\text{ cm}^3 \] Since the sphere volume is less than the empty space, the water level rises but stays inside the vessel.
Answer (c): The water will not overflow.
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • (a) Volume of water = \(4096\pi\text{ cm}^3\)
  • (b) Depth of water = \(48\text{ cm}\)
  • (c) Sphere volume \(=\dfrac{10976}{3}\pi \approx 11493\text{ cm}^3 < 3904\pi \approx 12265\text{ cm}^3\) (free space), so the water will not overflow.
