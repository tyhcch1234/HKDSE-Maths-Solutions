QUESTION

In Figure 1, AB produced and CD produced meet at the point E. It is given that $\angle CAE=\angle BDE$.

(a) Prove that $\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE$.

(b) It is given that $AC=25\text{ cm}$, $AE=60\text{ cm}$, $CE=65\text{ cm}$ and $BD=15\text{ cm}$.
   (i) Is $\triangle ACE$ a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer.
   (ii) Find the area of $\triangle BDE$.  [5 marks]

Step 1 · Set up the two triangles

Lines $AC$ and $BD$ are cut by the two transversals $AE$ and $CE$, which meet at $E$. So we compare $\triangle ACE$ (the big triangle) with $\triangle DBE$ (the small one at $E$).

Hint: Look for shared vertices and the common angle at $E$ — that is the key to an A–A–A proof.
Step 2 · (a) Prove similarity — Angle A

$$\angle CAE=\angle BDE \qquad (\text{given})$$

This is the pair of equal angles highlighted in green in the diagram.
Step 3 · (a) Prove similarity — Angle at E

$$\angle AEC=\angle DEB \qquad (\text{common }\angle)$$

Both angles lie at the intersection point $E$, so they are literally the same angle.

Step 4 · (a) Conclude \(\triangle ACE\sim\triangle DBE\)

$$\angle ACE=\angle DBE \qquad (\angle\text{ sum of }\triangle)$$

Since the two triangles have three pairs of equal angles,

$$\boxed{\triangle ACE\sim\triangle DBE} \qquad (\text{A.A.A.})$$

Marking: correct proof with reasons = 2 marks; correct proof without reasons = 1 mark.
Step 5 · (b)(i) Test Pythagoras' theorem

The longest side is $CE=65\text{ cm}$. Check whether the two shorter sides satisfy $AC^2+AE^2=CE^2$:

$$AC^2+AE^2=25^2+60^2=625+3600=4225$$

$$CE^2=65^2=4225$$

Therefore $AC^2+AE^2=CE^2$, so

$$\boxed{\triangle ACE \text{ is a right-angled triangle, with } \angle CAE=90^\circ.}$$

(converse of Pythagoras' theorem — 1A)

Step 6 · (b)(ii) Find the scale factor

From the similarity in (a), corresponding sides are proportional:

$$\frac{DE}{AE}=\frac{BD}{AC} \qquad (\text{corresponding sides, } \sim\triangle s)$$

$$\frac{DE}{60}=\frac{15}{25}=\frac{3}{5}$$

$$DE=\frac{3}{5}\times 60 = 36\text{ cm}$$

(1M)

Step 7 · (b)(ii) Area of \(\triangle BDE\)

Since $\angle CAE=90^\circ$ and the triangles are similar, $\angle BDE=90^\circ$ as well — so $BD\perp DE$.

$$\text{Area of } \triangle BDE=\frac{1}{2}\times BD\times DE=\frac{15\times 36}{2}=270\text{ cm}^2$$

Answer: (a) A.A.A.  |  (b)(i) Yes — $25^2+60^2=65^2$  |  (b)(ii) $270\text{ cm}^2$
