Q Question 7 marks

In Figure 1, \(AB\) produced and \(CD\) produced meet at the point \(E\). It is given that \(\angle CAE = \angle BDE\).

(a) Prove that \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\). [2 marks]

(b) It is given that \(AC = 25\text{ cm}\), \(AE = 60\text{ cm}\), \(CE = 65\text{ cm}\) and \(BD = 15\text{ cm}\).

(i) Is \(\triangle ACE\) a right-angled triangle? Explain your answer. [1 mark]

(ii) Find the area of \(\triangle BDE\). [2 marks]

a Prove \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\) 2 marks

Figure 1 — Interactive diagram
\(\angle CAE =\) — \(\angle BDE =\) — \(\angle AEC = \angle DEB =\) —
STEP 1Given
\[ \angle CAE = \angle BDE \qquad (\text{given}) \]
Hint: Use the given information directly — no proof needed for the first angle.
STEP 2Reason: common angle
\[ \angle AEC = \angle DEB \qquad (\text{common angle}) \]
Hint: Angle \(E\) is shared by both triangles \(\triangle ACE\) and \(\triangle DBE\).
STEP 3Reason: \(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)
\[ \angle ACE = 180^\circ - \angle CAE - \angle AEC \] \[ \angle DBE = 180^\circ - \angle BDE - \angle DEB \] \[ \therefore \angle ACE = \angle DBE \qquad (\angle \text{ sum of } \triangle) \]
Hint: Once two pairs of angles are equal, the third pair must also be equal since angles in a triangle sum to \(180^\circ\).
STEP 41A
\[ \therefore \triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE \qquad (\text{AAA}) \]
Conclusion: \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\) by the AAA similarity criterion.

bi Is \(\triangle ACE\) right-angled? 1 mark

Figure 1 — Live Pythagoras check
\(AC^2 + AE^2 =\) — \(CE^2 =\) — Status: —
STEP 11M
Compute the two sides: \[ AC^2 + AE^2 = 25^2 + 60^2 = 625 + 3600 = 4225 \]
Hint: For a right-angled triangle, the converse of Pythagoras says \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) where \(c\) is the longest side.
STEP 21M
Compute the longest side's square: \[ CE^2 = 65^2 = 4225 \]
Hint: \(65\) cm is the longest side, so it must be the hypotenuse if the triangle is right-angled.
STEP 31A
\[ AC^2 + AE^2 = 4225 = CE^2 \] \[ \therefore \angle CAE = 90^\circ \]
Answer: Yes, \(\triangle ACE\) is a right-angled triangle (right angle at \(A\)), by the converse of Pythagoras' theorem.

bii Find the area of \(\triangle BDE\) 2 marks

Figure 1 — Similar triangles & area
\(DE =\) — \(BD =\) — Area \(= \frac{1}{2}\times BD \times DE =\) —
STEP 11M
Since \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\) (from part (a)): \[ \frac{DE}{AE} = \frac{BD}{AC} \] \[ \frac{DE}{60} = \frac{15}{25} \]
Hint: Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional. Match \(DE \leftrightarrow AE\) and \(BD \leftrightarrow AC\).
STEP 21M
\[ DE = 60 \times \frac{15}{25} = 60 \times \frac{3}{5} = 36 \text{ cm} \]
Hint: Simplify \(\frac{15}{25} = \frac{3}{5}\) first to make the multiplication easier.
STEP 3Reasoning
Since \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\) and \(\angle CAE = 90^\circ\), \[ \angle BDE = 90^\circ \]
Hint: Corresponding angles of similar triangles are equal — the right angle at \(A\) maps to the right angle at \(D\).
STEP 41A
\[ \text{Area of } \triangle BDE = \frac{1}{2} \times BD \times DE = \frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 36 \] \[ = 270 \text{ cm}^2 \]
Answer: The area of \(\triangle BDE\) is \(270 \text{ cm}^2\).

📋 Final Answers at a Glance

  • (a) \(\triangle ACE \sim \triangle DBE\) by AAA (given equal angle, common angle, third angle by \(\angle\) sum of \(\triangle\)).
  • (b)(i) Yes — \(\triangle ACE\) is right-angled at \(A\), since \(AC^2 + AE^2 = 25^2 + 60^2 = 4225 = 65^2 = CE^2\).
  • (b)(ii) \(DE = 36 \text{ cm}\), and the area of \(\triangle BDE = \frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 36 = \mathbf{270 \text{ cm}^2}\).
