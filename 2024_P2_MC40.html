3D Trigonometry in a Tetrahedron — finding \(\cos \angle PRS\)
3D Geometry Right-angled Triangles Pythagoras' Theorem Cosine Formula Angle Between Lines

QQuestion

In the figure, the base \(PQR\) of the tetrahedron \(PQRS\) lies on the horizontal ground. It is given that \(Q\) is vertically below \(S\). If \(\angle PQR = 90^\circ\), \(\angle QPS = 30^\circ\) and \(\angle QRS = 45^\circ\), then \(\cos\angle PRS = \text{?}\) Multiple Choice
A.  \(\dfrac{1}{2}\)
B.  \(\dfrac{3}{4}\)
C.  \(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\)
D.  \(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\)
Reading the figure: \(SQ\) is the vertical height of the tetrahedron (height \(h\)). The right angle at \(Q\) in the base and the right angles with vertical \(SQ\) give three right-angled triangles: \(\triangle SQR\), \(\triangle SQP\) and \(\triangle PQR\).

Step 11M

Let the length of \(SQ\) be \(h\). Since \(Q\) is vertically below \(S\), the line \(SQ\) is perpendicular to the horizontal ground. Hence \(SQ \perp RQ\) and \(SQ \perp PQ\), so both \(\triangle SQR\) and \(\triangle SQP\) are right-angled at \(Q\).

Hint: "Vertically below" is the key phrase — it forces two right angles into the figure at once.
Step 21M

In \(\triangle SQR\) (right-angled at \(Q\)):

\[ \tan\angle QRS = \frac{SQ}{RQ} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{h}{RQ} = \tan 45^\circ = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; RQ = h \]
Hint: \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\) is the simplest special angle — it makes \(RQ\) equal to the height.
Step 31M

In \(\triangle SQP\) (right-angled at \(Q\)):

\[ \tan\angle QPS = \frac{SQ}{PQ} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{h}{PQ} = \tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt3} \;\Rightarrow\; PQ = \sqrt3\,h \]
Hint: \(\tan 30^\circ = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\), so \(PQ\) is \(\sqrt3\) times larger than the height.
Step 41A

Now apply Pythagoras in the base \(\triangle PQR\) (right-angled at \(Q\)):

\[ PR^2 = PQ^2 + RQ^2 = (\sqrt3 h)^2 + h^2 = 3h^2 + h^2 = 4h^2 \;\Rightarrow\; PR = 2h \]
\(RQ = h,\quad PQ = \sqrt3\,h,\quad PR = 2h\)
Hint: Notice \(\triangle PQR\) is a \(30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ\text{–}90^\circ\) triangle with hypotenuse \(PR = 2h\).

Step 11M

In \(\triangle SQP\) (right-angled at \(Q\)):

\[ \sin\angle QPS = \frac{SQ}{PS} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{h}{PS} = \sin 30^\circ = \frac12 \;\Rightarrow\; PS = 2h \]
Hint: \(\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12\) — the hypotenuse is twice the opposite side. This is the classic \(30^\circ\text{–}60^\circ\text{–}90^\circ\) ratio.
Step 21M

In \(\triangle SQR\) (right-angled at \(Q\)):

\[ \sin\angle QRS = \frac{SQ}{RS} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{h}{RS} = \sin 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt2}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; RS = \sqrt2\,h \]
Hint: \(\sin 45^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\), so \(RS = h \div \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} = \sqrt2\,h\).
Step 31A

Collect the three sides of the target triangle \(PSR\):

\[ PS = 2h,\qquad RS = \sqrt2\,h,\qquad PR = 2h \]
\(PS = 2h,\quad RS = \sqrt2\,h,\quad PR = 2h\)
Hint: All three sides are now known in terms of \(h\) — the cosine formula will finish the job.

Step 11M

Apply the cosine formula in \(\triangle PRS\), with the angle at \(R\):

\[ PS^2 = PR^2 + RS^2 - 2(PR)(RS)\cos\angle PRS \] Substitute \(PS = 2h\), \(PR = 2h\), \(RS = \sqrt2\,h\): \[ (2h)^2 = (2h)^2 + (\sqrt2\,h)^2 - 2(2h)(\sqrt2\,h)\cos\angle PRS \]
Hint: Put the side opposite the angle (\(PS\)) alone on the left, then substitute carefully.
Step 21A

Simplify (divide every term by \(h^2\), \(h>0\)):

\[ 4 = 4 + 2 - 2(2)(\sqrt2)\cos\angle PRS \] \[ 4 = 6 - 4\sqrt2\,\cos\angle PRS \] \[ 4\sqrt2\,\cos\angle PRS = 2 \] \[ \cos\angle PRS = \frac{2}{4\sqrt2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt2} = \frac{\sqrt2}{4} \]
\(\boxed{\cos\angle PRS = \dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}}\)  →  Answer: C
Hint: Rationalise by multiplying numerator and denominator by \(\sqrt2\): \(\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\).

📌 Final Answers at a Glance

  • Height: \(SQ = h\), and \(Q\) is directly below \(S\), so \(SQ \perp\) ground.
  • Base edges: \(RQ = h\) (from \(\tan 45^\circ\)), \(PQ = \sqrt3\,h\) (from \(\tan 30^\circ\)).
  • Base diagonal: \(PR = \sqrt{PQ^2+RQ^2} = 2h\).
  • Slanted edges: \(PS = 2h\) (from \(\sin 30^\circ\)), \(RS = \sqrt2\,h\) (from \(\sin 45^\circ\)).
  • Cosine formula in \(\triangle PRS\): \[ \cos\angle PRS = \frac{PR^2+RS^2-PS^2}{2(PR)(RS)} = \frac{4h^2+2h^2-4h^2}{2(2h)(\sqrt2 h)} = \frac{2h^2}{4\sqrt2\,h^2} = \frac{\sqrt2}{4} \]
  • Correct option: C   \(\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\)
