Question

The base of the right pyramid \(VABCD\) is the square \(ABCD\). Let \(\theta\) be the angle between \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\). If \(AB:AV = 5:4\), then \(\cos\theta =\)

A. \(\dfrac{-25}{39}\)
B. \(\dfrac{-17}{33}\)
C. \(\dfrac{-9}{16}\)
D. \(0\)
Figure: right pyramid \(VABCD\) with square base \(ABCD\), \(M\) = midpoint of \(AB\), \(E\) = foot of perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BV\).
Step 1 · Set up the pyramid from the ratio

Let \(AB = 5k\) so that \(AV = 4k\). Since the base is a square,

\[AC = 5\sqrt{2}\,k,\qquad AO = \tfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}k \quad (O=\text{centre of } ABCD).\]

As a right pyramid, \(VO\perp\) base, so \[VO=\sqrt{AV^{2}-AO^{2}}=\sqrt{16k^{2}-\tfrac{50}{4}k^{2}} =\sqrt{\tfrac{14}{4}k^{2}}=\tfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}k .\]

Hint: everything is proportional to \(k\), so the answer is independent of \(k\) — keep the ratio \(AB:AV=5:4\).
Step 2 · Angle \(\angle ABV\) in the isosceles \(\triangle ABV\)

In \(\triangle ABV\), \(VA=VB=4k\) and \(AB=5k\). Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\), then \(VM\perp AB\) and

\[\cos\angle ABV=\frac{BM}{BV}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}AB}{BV}=\frac{5}{8}.\]

Hence \[\tan\angle ABV=\frac{\sqrt{8^{2}-5^{2}}}{5}=\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}.\]

By symmetry, \(\triangle BCV\cong\triangle ABV\), so \(\tan\angle CBV=\dfrac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\) as well.
Step 3 · Locate \(E\): the perpendicular \(AE\perp BV\)

Let \(E\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BV\). Since the faces \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\) both contain \(BV\), the angle between the two planes is \(\angle AEC\) — the angle between the perpendiculars to the common edge \(BV\).

In right \(\triangle ABE\) (right angle at \(E\)): \[BE=AB\cos\angle ABV=5k\cdot\tfrac{5}{8}=\tfrac{25}{8}k,\qquad AE=AB\sin\angle ABV=\tfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k.\]

Similarly, \(CE=\dfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k\).

Step 4 · Four distances, four equations

Apply the cosine rule in \(\triangle ACE\), and Pythagoras in \(\triangle ABC\), \(\triangle ABE\), \(\triangle BCE\):

  • (1) \(AE^{2}+CE^{2}-AC^{2}=2\,AE\cdot CE\cos\angle AEC\)
  • (2) \(AB^{2}+BC^{2}-AC^{2}=0\)
  • (3) \(AB^{2}-AE^{2}-BE^{2}=0\)
  • (4) \(BC^{2}-CE^{2}-BE^{2}=0\)

Adding \((1)+(2)+(3)+(4)\) the squared terms cancel:

\[-2BE^{2}=2\,AE\cdot CE\cos\angle AEC .\]

This is exactly the classic "twice use of Pythagoras" trick for the angle between two faces.
Step 5 · Evaluate \(\cos\angle AEC=\cos\theta\)

\[\cos\angle AEC=-\frac{BE^{2}}{AE\cdot CE} =-\left(\frac{AE}{BE}\cdot\frac{CE}{BE}\right)^{-1}\] \[ =-\left(\tan\angle ABV\cdot\tan\angle CBV\right)^{-1} =-\left(\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\right)^{-1} =-\frac{25}{39}.\]

Since \(\theta=\angle AEC\), we get \[\boxed{\;\cos\theta=-\frac{25}{39}\;}\]

The negative sign confirms the face angle is obtuse (the faces fold "inward" away from their front sides), which rules out option D and matches option A.
Final answer:
Option A

\(\cos\theta = -\dfrac{25}{39}\)
