Trigonometry · Angle between two triangular faces of a right pyramid

Q40 The Question

The base of the right pyramid \(VABCD\) is the square \(ABCD\). Let \(\theta\) be the angle between \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\). If \(AB:AV = 5:4\), then \(\cos\theta =\)

A. \(\dfrac{-25}{39}\)
B. \(\dfrac{-17}{33}\)
C. \(\dfrac{-9}{16}\)
D. \(0\)

Step Block 1 Setting up the pyramid and the ratio \(AB:AV=5:4\)

Step 1

Let \(AB=5k\) and \(AV=BV=CV=DV=4k\) (a right pyramid has equal slant edges).

Since \(ABCD\) is a square with side \(5k\), each face is an isosceles triangle.

Hint: A “right pyramid” means the apex is vertically above the centre of the base — so the four slant edges are equal in length.
Step 2

In \(\triangle ABC\): \(AC^2 = AB^2+BC^2 = 25k^2+25k^2 = 50k^2\).

In \(\triangle AVB\), with \(M\) the midpoint of \(AB\): \(AM = \tfrac{5k}{2}\).

The pyramid height \(h\) satisfies \(h^2 = AV^2 - \left(\dfrac{AC}{2}\right)^2 = 16k^2-\dfrac{50k^2}{4} = 16k^2-12.5k^2=3.5k^2\), so \(h=\sqrt{3.5}\,k\).

Hint: The apex \(V\) sits above the base centre, distance \(\frac{AC}{2}=\frac{5\sqrt2}{2}k\) from each base vertex.
0 / 2

Step Block 2 The angle between the two faces \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\)

Step 1

Drop perpendiculars from \(A\) to \(BV\) at \(E\), and from \(C\) to \(BV\) at \(F\). By symmetry \(AE = CF\).

The angle \(\theta\) between \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\) is the angle between their altitudes, i.e. \(\angle AEC = \theta\) (using the symmetry \(CE\perp BV\) too, since \(E \equiv F\) by symmetry — \(AE = CE\)).

Hint: The angle between two planes equals the angle between two lines, one in each plane, each perpendicular to the line of intersection \(BV\).
Step 2

In \(\triangle ABV\), \(BM=\tfrac{AB}{2}=\tfrac{5k}{2}\), \(AV=4k\): \[\cos\angle ABV=\frac{BM}{AV}=\frac{5k/2}{4k}=\frac{5}{8}\] hence \(\tan\angle ABV=\dfrac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\).

The perpendicular height from \(A\) to \(BV\) (altitude \(AE\)) satisfies \[AE = AB\sin\angle ABV = 5k\cdot\frac{\sqrt{39}}{8}=\frac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k.\] By symmetry \(CE=AE=\dfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k\).

Hint: \(\sin\angle ABV = \sqrt{1-(5/8)^2}=\frac{\sqrt{39}}{8}\). The triangle \(\triangle ABV\) is isosceles so the two base angles are equal.
Step 3

In \(\triangle ABC\): \(AC^2=AB^2+BC^2=50k^2\), so \(AC=5\sqrt2\,k\).

Apply the cosine rule in \(\triangle AEC\) (with sides \(AE=CE\)): \[\cos\theta=\frac{AE^2+CE^2-AC^2}{2\cdot AE\cdot CE} =\frac{2AE^2-AC^2}{2AE^2}=1-\frac{AC^2}{2AE^2}.\]

Hint: \(\triangle AEC\) is isosceles with \(AE=CE\), so the cosine rule simplifies beautifully.
Step 4

Substitute: \[\cos\theta=1-\frac{50k^2}{2\cdot\dfrac{25\cdot39}{64}k^2} =1-\frac{50}{2\cdot\frac{975}{64}} =1-\frac{50\cdot64}{1950} =1-\frac{64}{39} =\frac{39-64}{39}=-\frac{25}{39}.\]

Answer: \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{25}{39}\) — option A.
Note: \(CE\perp BV\) can be justified by symmetry (rotating the square by \(90^\circ\) about the vertical axis maps \(\triangle ABV\) to \(\triangle BCV\), preserving the foot \(E\)).

Summary Answers at a glance

  • Model: \(AB=5k\), \(AV=BV=CV=DV=4k\), square base of side \(5k\).
  • Face altitude (height from \(A\) to \(BV\)): \(AE=\dfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k\).
  • Base diagonal: \(AC=5\sqrt2\,k\).
  • Cosine rule in \(\triangle AEC\): \(\cos\theta = 1-\dfrac{AC^2}{2AE^2}\).
  • Final answer: \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{25}{39}\), i.e. option A.
