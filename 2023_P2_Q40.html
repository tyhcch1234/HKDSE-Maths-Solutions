Question

The base of the right pyramid \(VABCD\) is the square \(ABCD\). Let \(\theta\) be the angle between \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\). If \(AB:AV = 5:4\), then \(\cos\theta =\)

A. \(\dfrac{-25}{39}\)
B. \(\dfrac{-17}{33}\)
C. \(\dfrac{-9}{16}\)
D. \(0\)
Figure: right pyramid \(VABCD\) with square base \(ABCD\), \(M\) = midpoint of \(AB\), \(E\) = foot of perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BV\).
Step 1 · Set up the pyramid from the ratio
Pythagoras' Theorem \(VO^{2}=AV^{2}-AO^{2}\) The height of the pyramid is a leg of the right triangle with hypotenuse \(AV\) and other leg \(AO\).

Let \(AB = 5k\) so that \(AV = 4k\). Since the base is a square,

\[AC = 5\sqrt{2}\,k,\qquad AO = \tfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}k \quad (O=\text{centre of } ABCD).\]

As a right pyramid, \(VO\perp\) base,
so \[VO=\sqrt{AV^{2}-AO^{2}}=\sqrt{16k^{2}-\tfrac{50}{4}k^{2}} =\sqrt{\tfrac{14}{4}k^{2}}=\tfrac{\sqrt{14}}{2}k .\]

Hint: everything is proportional to \(k\), so the answer is independent of \(k\) — keep the ratio \(AB:AV=5:4\).
Step 2 · Angle \(\angle ABV\) in the isosceles \(\triangle ABV\)
Cosine Ratio \(\cos\angle ABV=\dfrac{BM}{BV}\) In the isosceles face \(ABV\), the right triangle \(BMV\) gives the cosine of the base angle.

In \(\triangle ABV\), \(VA=VB=4k\) and \(AB=5k\). Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\),
then \(VM\perp AB\) and

\[\cos\angle ABV=\frac{BM}{BV}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}AB}{BV}=\frac{5}{8}.\]

Hence \[\tan\angle ABV=\frac{\sqrt{8^{2}-5^{2}}}{5}=\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}.\]

By symmetry, \(\triangle BCV\cong\triangle ABV\), so \(\tan\angle CBV=\dfrac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\) as well.
Step 3 · Locate \(E\): the perpendicular \(AE\perp BV\)

Let \(E\) be the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BV\). Since the faces \(\triangle ABV\) and \(\triangle BCV\) both contain \(BV\),
the angle between the two planes is \(\angle AEC\) — the angle between the perpendiculars to the common edge \(BV\).

In right \(\triangle ABE\) (right angle at \(E\)): \[BE=AB\cos\angle ABV=5k\cdot\tfrac{5}{8}=\tfrac{25}{8}k,\qquad AE=AB\sin\angle ABV=\tfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k.\]

Similarly, \(CE=\dfrac{5\sqrt{39}}{8}k\).

Step 4 · Four distances, four equations
Cosine Formula \(AE^{2}+CE^{2}-AC^{2}=2\,AE\cdot CE\cos\angle AEC\) Adding the four distance relations cancels the squared terms and isolates the angle between the two faces.

Apply the cosine rule in \(\triangle ACE\), and Pythagoras in \(\triangle ABC\), \(\triangle ABE\), \(\triangle BCE\):

  • (1) \(AE^{2}+CE^{2}-AC^{2}=2\,AE\cdot CE\cos\angle AEC\)
  • (2) \(AB^{2}+BC^{2}-AC^{2}=0\)
  • (3) \(AB^{2}-AE^{2}-BE^{2}=0\)
  • (4) \(BC^{2}-CE^{2}-BE^{2}=0\)

Adding \((1)+(2)+(3)+(4)\) the squared terms cancel:

\[-2BE^{2}=2\,AE\cdot CE\cos\angle AEC .\]

This is exactly the classic "twice use of Pythagoras" trick for the angle between two faces.
Step 5 · Evaluate \(\cos\angle AEC=\cos\theta\)

\[\cos\angle AEC=-\frac{BE^{2}}{AE\cdot CE} =-\left(\frac{AE}{BE}\cdot\frac{CE}{BE}\right)^{-1}\] \[ =-\left(\tan\angle ABV\cdot\tan\angle CBV\right)^{-1} =-\left(\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{39}}{5}\right)^{-1} =-\frac{25}{39}.\]

Since \(\theta=\angle AEC\),
we get \[\boxed{\;\cos\theta=-\frac{25}{39}\;}\]

The negative sign confirms the face angle is obtuse (the faces fold "inward" away from their front sides), which rules out option D and matches option A.
Final answer:
Option A

\(\cos\theta = -\dfrac{25}{39}\)

Question. In the right pyramid \(VABCD\) with a square base, \(AB:AV=3:4\). Let \(\theta\) be the angle between the faces \(ABV\) and \(BCV\). Find \(\cos\theta\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Let \(AB=3k\) and \(AV=4k\). Then \(AC=3\sqrt{2}k\) and \(AO=\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}k\), so \(VO=\sqrt{16k^{2}-\dfrac{18}{4}k^{2}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{23}{2}}\,k\).
Step 2. In the isosceles \(\triangle ABV\), \(\cos\angle ABV=\dfrac{BM}{BV}=\dfrac{1.5k}{4k}=\dfrac{3}{8}\), so \(\tan\angle ABV=\dfrac{\sqrt{64-9}}{3}=\dfrac{\sqrt{55}}{3}\).
Step 3. With \(E\) the foot of the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BV\): \(BE=AB\cos\angle ABV=\dfrac{9k}{8}\) and \(AE=CE=AB\sin\angle ABV=\dfrac{3\sqrt{55}}{8}k\).
Step 4. From the four distance relations, \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{BE^{2}}{AE\cdot CE}=-\left(\tan\angle ABV\right)^{-2}\).
Step 5. \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{9}{55}\).
Answer: \(\cos\theta=-\dfrac{9}{55}\)
