Type 1

Simplifying Ratios and Ratio Notation · 7 questions

Reducing ratios, three-term ratios, unit ratios

Core idea: A ratio compares quantities of the same kind in the same unit. Multiplying or dividing every term by the same non-zero number leaves the ratio unchanged, so a ratio is in simplest form when the terms are integers with H.C.F. 1.

For a three-term ratio, extend the same idea term by term; for a unit ratio, divide so that one term becomes 1.

\(a:b=\frac{a}{b}\)\(a:b:c\)\(a:b=1:n\)
Q1★☆☆☆2 marks

Simplify the ratio \(36:48\).

Answer\(3:4\)
  1. Find the H.C.F. of 36 and 48, which is 12.
  2. Divide both terms by 12: \(36\div 12=3\) and \(48\div 12=4\).
  3. Hence \(36:48=3:4\).
  4. Check: \(3\times 12=36\) and \(4\times 12=48\), so the ratio is unchanged.
Common mistake

Subtracting the same number from both terms (for example \(36-12:48-12\)) does not keep a ratio — only multiplying or dividing does.

Q2★☆☆☆2 marks

Express the ratio \(\frac{2}{3}:\frac{5}{6}\) in simplest integer form.

Answer\(4:5\)
  1. Multiply both terms by the L.C.M. of the denominators 3 and 6, namely 6.
  2. \(\frac{2}{3}\times 6=4\) and \(\frac{5}{6}\times 6=5\).
  3. So \(\frac{2}{3}:\frac{5}{6}=4:5\).
  4. Check: \(\frac{4}{5}=0.8\) and \(\frac{2/3}{5/6}=\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{6}{5}=0.8\).
Q3★★☆☆3 marks

It is given that \(a:b=3:5\) and \(b:c=4:7\). Find \(a:b:c\).

Answer\(12:20:35\)
  1. The term \(b\) must be the same number in both ratios. The L.C.M. of 5 and 4 is 20.
  2. Write \(a:b=3:5=12:20\) (multiply by 4).
  3. Write \(b:c=4:7=20:35\) (multiply by 5).
  4. Now \(b=20\) in both, so \(a:b:c=12:20:35\).
  5. Check: 12, 20, 35 have no common factor greater than 1, so the ratio is in simplest form.
Common mistake

Writing \(a:b:c=3:5:7\) — you may not simply join the two ratios; the shared term \(b\) must first be made equal.

Q4★★☆☆3 marks

Simplify the ratio 45 minutes : 2 hours.

Answer\(3:8\)
  1. Convert to the same unit: 2 hours \(=2\times 60=120\) minutes.
  2. So the ratio is \(45:120\).
  3. H.C.F. of 45 and 120 is 15, so divide both terms by 15: \(45\div 15=3\), \(120\div 15=8\).
  4. Hence 45 minutes : 2 hours \(=3:8\).
Common mistake

Leaving the quantities in different units and writing \(45:2\) gives a meaningless ratio; a ratio of unlike units is not a pure number.

Q5★★☆☆3 marks

Express the ratio 25 g : 2 kg in the form \(1:n\).

Answer\(1:80\)
  1. Convert to the same unit: 2 kg \(=2000\) g.
  2. Ratio \(=25:2000\).
  3. Divide both terms by 25: \(25\div 25=1\) and \(2000\div 25=80\).
  4. Hence \(25\text{ g}:2\text{ kg}=1:80\).
  5. Check: \(1\times 80=80\) g per g, and \(25\times 80=2000\), correct.
Q6★★★☆5 marks

(a) If \(2x=7y\), find \(x:y\).
(b) If \(\frac{p}{4}=\frac{q}{9}\), find \(p:q\).
(c) If \(3m=4n=5p\), find \(m:n:p\).

Answer(a) \(7:2\)　(b) \(4:9\)　(c) \(20:15:12\)
  1. (a) From \(2x=7y\) we get \(\frac{x}{y}=\frac{7}{2}\), so \(x:y=7:2\).
  2. (b) \(\frac{p}{4}=\frac{q}{9}\) gives \(9p=4q\), so \(\frac{p}{q}=\frac{4}{9}\), i.e. \(p:q=4:9\).
  3. (c) Let \(3m=4n=5p=L\). Then \(m=\frac{L}{3}\), \(n=\frac{L}{4}\), \(p=\frac{L}{5}\).
  4. So \(m:n:p=\frac{1}{3}:\frac{1}{4}:\frac{1}{5}\). Multiply every term by 60 (L.C.M. of 3, 4, 5): \(20:15:12\).
  5. Check: \(3(20)=60\), \(4(15)=60\), \(5(12)=60\), all equal.
Common mistake

In (a), writing \(x:y=2:7\) — the coefficient of \(x\) gives the share of \(y\), not of \(x\). Test with numbers: \(x=7, y=2\) really does satisfy \(2x=7y\).

Q7★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2023 Paper 1 Q6 ↗

It is given that \(a:b=3:4\), \(b:c=6:5\) and \(c:d=2:9\). Find \(a:b:c:d\).

Answer\(9:12:10:45\)
  1. Combine \(a:b\) with \(b:c\) first. The L.C.M. of 4 and 6 is 12.
  2. \(a:b=3:4=9:12\) (multiply by 3) and \(b:c=6:5=12:10\) (multiply by 2).
  3. So \(a:b:c=9:12:10\) and in particular \(c=10\).
  4. Now bring in \(c:d=2:9\). The L.C.M. of 10 and 2 is 10, so \(c:d=10:45\) (multiply by 5).
  5. Hence \(a:b:c:d=9:12:10:45\).
  6. Check: divide each pair back — \(9:12=3:4\), \(12:10=6:5\), \(10:45=2:9\), all correct.

Type 2

Sharing a Quantity in a Given Ratio · 7 questions

Two-part and three-part sharing, difference given

Core idea: To share a quantity in the ratio \(a:b:c\), add the terms to get the total number of parts \(a+b+c\), then find the value of one part, and finally multiply.

If the question gives a difference instead of the total, that difference corresponds to the difference of the two terms only.

\(\text{one part}=\frac{\text{total}}{a+b+c}\)\(\text{difference}=(a-b)\times\text{one part}\)
Q8★☆☆☆2 marks

A sum of $240 is shared between Ivy and Jon in the ratio \(3:5\). Find the amount each receives.

AnswerIvy $90, Jon $150
  1. Total number of parts \(=3+5=8\).
  2. Value of one part \(=240\div 8=30\) dollars.
  3. Ivy: \(3\times 30=90\); Jon: \(5\times 30=150\).
  4. Check: \(90+150=240\), the whole sum, and \(90:150=3:5\).
Q9★☆☆☆2 marks

84 kg of fertiliser is divided among three gardens in the ratio \(1:2:4\). Find the mass given to each garden.

Answer12 kg, 24 kg and 48 kg
  1. Total parts \(=1+2+4=7\).
  2. One part \(=84\div 7=12\) kg.
  3. Shares: \(1\times 12=12\) kg, \(2\times 12=24\) kg, \(4\times 12=48\) kg.
  4. Check: \(12+24+48=84\) kg.
Common mistake

Counting only the distinct terms (here 3) instead of adding the terms — the number of parts is 7, not 3.

Q10★★☆☆3 marks

$560 is shared among a carpenter, a painter and a plumber in the ratio \(2:5:7\). Find the largest share.

Answer$280
  1. Total parts \(=2+5+7=14\).
  2. One part \(=560\div 14=40\) dollars.
  3. The largest share belongs to the term 7: \(7\times 40=280\) dollars.
  4. Check: the shares are $80, $200 and $280; \(80+200+280=560\).
Q11★★☆☆3 marks

In a quiz the points of three houses are in the ratio \(4:5:6\). The leading house has 96 points more than the last house. Find the total number of points scored by the three houses.

Answer720
  1. The difference between the largest and smallest terms is \(6-4=2\) parts.
  2. These 2 parts represent 96 points, so one part \(=96\div 2=48\) points.
  3. Total parts \(=4+5+6=15\), so the total is \(15\times 48=720\).
  4. Check: shares are 192, 240, 288; \(288-192=96\), and \(192+240+288=720\).
Common mistake

Dividing the 96 by the total number of parts (15) instead of by the difference of the two terms (2).

Q12★★☆☆4 marks

Two partners divide the profit of a stall in the ratio \(7:4\). The senior partner receives $72 more than the junior partner. Find (a) the total profit, (b) the junior partner's share.

Answer(a) $264　(b) $96
  1. The difference \(7-4=3\) parts corresponds to $72.
  2. One part \(=72\div 3=24\) dollars.
  3. (a) Total parts \(=7+4=11\), so total profit \(=11\times 24=264\) dollars.
  4. (b) Junior partner: \(4\times 24=96\) dollars.
  5. Check: senior partner \(=7\times 24=168\), and \(168-96=72\), as given.
Q13★★★☆5 marks

Ken, Leo and Max share a sum of money in the ratio \(3:4:5\). Ken and Leo together receive $672. Find (a) the total sum, (b) Max's share.

Answer(a) $1152　(b) $480
  1. Ken and Leo together hold \(3+4=7\) parts, and these 7 parts are worth $672.
  2. One part \(=672\div 7=96\) dollars.
  3. (a) Total parts \(=3+4+5=12\), so the sum is \(12\times 96=1152\) dollars.
  4. (b) Max receives \(5\times 96=480\) dollars.
  5. Check: Ken \(=288\), Leo \(=384\), Max \(=480\); \(288+384=672\) and the total is 1152.
Common mistake

Using the total 12 parts against $672 — the $672 covers only Ken and Leo, that is 7 parts.

Q14★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2019 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

A prize fund is divided among Amy, Ben and Chris in the ratio \(4:3:5\).
(a) If the fund is $1440, find Chris's share.
(b) If Chris's share is $300 more than Ben's, find the total fund and Amy's share.

Answer(a) $600　(b) fund $1800, Amy $600
  1. (a) Total parts \(=4+3+5=12\), so one part \(=1440\div 12=120\) dollars. Chris gets \(5\times 120=600\) dollars.
  2. (b) Chris exceeds Ben by \(5-3=2\) parts, and 2 parts \(=300\) dollars, so one part \(=150\) dollars.
  3. Total fund \(=12\times 150=1800\) dollars; Amy gets \(4\times 150=600\) dollars.
  4. Check (a): shares 480, 360, 600 sum to 1440. Check (b): 600, 450, 750 with difference \(750-450=300\).
  5. Note that in (a) and (b) one part has different values (120 and 150); each part of the question is a separate situation.

Type 3

Combining and Comparing Ratios · 7 questions

a:b with b:c, two-stage ratio changes

Core idea: Two ratios that share a term can be chained into one three-term ratio once the shared term is made equal in both (use the L.C.M.). To compare two ratios, compare the fractions they represent by cross-multiplying.

In a two-stage change, let the original quantities be \(ak\) and \(bk\), apply the change, then solve the resulting equation for \(k\).

\(a:b,\ b:c\ \Rightarrow\ a:b:c\)\(a:b>c:d\iff ad>bc\)\(ak\pm m\ :\ bk\pm n\)
Q15★☆☆☆2 marks

Given \(a:b=2:5\) and \(b:c=5:4\), find \(a:b:c\).

Answer\(2:5:4\)
  1. The shared term \(b\) is already 5 in both ratios, so no adjustment is needed.
  2. Read the numbers straight across: \(a=2\), \(b=5\), \(c=4\).
  3. Hence \(a:b:c=2:5:4\).
Q16★☆☆☆2 marks

Given \(a:b=3:4\) and \(b:c=8:9\), find \(a:b:c\) in simplest form.

Answer\(6:8:9\)
  1. The L.C.M. of the two values of \(b\) (4 and 8) is 8.
  2. Write \(a:b=3:4=6:8\) (multiply by 2).
  3. \(b:c\) is already \(8:9\), so \(a:b:c=6:8:9\).
  4. Check: \(6:8=3:4\) and \(8:9\) is unchanged, so the answer is consistent.
Common mistake

Multiplying the second ratio by 2 as well — only the ratio whose shared term needs changing is rescaled.

Q17★★☆☆3 marks

Which ratio is greater, \(5:8\) or \(7:11\)? Justify your answer.

Answer\(7:11\) is greater
  1. Compare the fractions \(\frac{5}{8}\) and \(\frac{7}{11}\) by cross-multiplying.
  2. \(5\times 11=55\) and \(7\times 8=56\).
  3. Since \(56>55\), we have \(\frac{7}{11}>\frac{5}{8}\).
  4. Check in decimals: \(\frac{5}{8}=0.625\) and \(\frac{7}{11}\approx 0.636\), so \(7:11\) is indeed greater.
Common mistake

Comparing only the first terms (7 > 5) or only the second terms (11 > 8) — a ratio must be compared as a single fraction.

Q18★★☆☆4 marks

In a drama club the ratio of boys to girls was \(4:5\). After 6 boys left the club, the ratio became \(3:5\). Find the number of boys and girls originally.

Answer24 boys and 30 girls
  1. Let the original numbers be \(4k\) boys and \(5k\) girls.
  2. After 6 boys leave: \(\frac{4k-6}{5k}=\frac{3}{5}\).
  3. Cross-multiply: \(5(4k-6)=3(5k)\), i.e. \(20k-30=15k\).
  4. So \(5k=30\), giving \(k=6\).
  5. Boys \(=4\times 6=24\), girls \(=5\times 6=30\).
  6. Check: after 6 boys leave there are 18 boys and 30 girls, and \(18:30=3:5\).
Common mistake

Writing \(\frac{4k}{5k-6}\) — only the boys changed, so only the numerator loses 6.

Q19★★☆☆3 marks

It is given that \(a:b=1:2\) and \(a:c=3:4\). Find \(a:b:c\).

Answer\(3:6:4\)
  1. The shared term here is \(a\), appearing as 1 and as 3. The L.C.M. of 1 and 3 is 3.
  2. Write \(a:b=1:2=3:6\) (multiply by 3).
  3. \(a:c=3:4\) already has \(a=3\).
  4. Hence \(a:b:c=3:6:4\).
  5. Check: \(3:6=1:2\) and \(3:4\) is unchanged.
Q20★★★☆5 marks

At a sports day the ratio of boys to girls taking part was \(7:5\). Later 6 more boys and 6 more girls joined, and the ratio became \(9:7\). Find the number of boys and girls who took part at first.

Answer21 boys and 15 girls
  1. Let the original numbers be \(7k\) boys and \(5k\) girls.
  2. After the change: \(\frac{7k+6}{5k+6}=\frac{9}{7}\).
  3. Cross-multiply: \(7(7k+6)=9(5k+6)\), i.e. \(49k+42=45k+54\).
  4. So \(4k=12\), giving \(k=3\).
  5. Boys \(=7\times 3=21\), girls \(=5\times 3=15\).
  6. Check: \(21+6=27\) and \(15+6=21\); \(27:21=9:7\), as required.
Common mistake

Forgetting to add the 6 to the denominator too — here both groups increased, so both terms change.

Q21★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2022 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

The ratio of the number of adults to the number of children at a concert was \(5:3\). After 240 adults and 240 children had left, the ratio became \(9:5\).
(a) Find the original number of adults and children.
(b) How many people were at the concert originally?

Answer(a) 2400 adults, 1440 children　(b) 3840
  1. (a) Let the original numbers be \(5k\) adults and \(3k\) children.
  2. After 240 of each leave: \(\frac{5k-240}{3k-240}=\frac{9}{5}\).
  3. Cross-multiply: \(5(5k-240)=9(3k-240)\), i.e. \(25k-1200=27k-2160\).
  4. Rearrange: \(2160-1200=27k-25k\), so \(960=2k\) and \(k=480\).
  5. Adults \(=5\times 480=2400\), children \(=3\times 480=1440\).
  6. (b) Total originally \(=2400+1440=3840\).
  7. Check: after 240 leave, 2160 : 1200 \(=9:5\) (divide both by 240), correct.
Common mistake

Cross-multiplying as \(5(5k-240)=9(3k-240)\) but then forgetting the minus signs — write both brackets out in full before simplifying.

Type 4

Rates: Speed, Density, Unit Price and Flow · 7 questions

Average speed, unit conversion, unit price, flow rate

Core idea: A rate is a ratio of two quantities with different units, so the unit of the answer is decided by the division: \(\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}\), \(\text{density}=\frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}\), \(\text{flow}=\frac{\text{volume}}{\text{time}}\).

Average speed for a whole journey is the total distance over the total time — never the average of the two speeds.

\(v=\frac{d}{t}\)\(1\text{ km/h}=\frac{1000}{3600}\text{ m/s}\)\(\rho=\frac{m}{V}\)
Q22★☆☆☆2 marks

A coach travels 180 km in 2.5 hours. Find its average speed in km/h.

Answer\(72\) km/h
  1. Use \(v=\frac{d}{t}\) with \(d=180\) km and \(t=2.5\) h.
  2. \(v=\frac{180}{2.5}=72\).
  3. Check: \(72\times 2.5=180\) km, the given distance.
Q23★☆☆☆2 marks

A shop sells 3.5 kg of rice for $42. Find the unit price in dollars per kg.

Answer$12 per kg
  1. Unit price \(=\frac{\text{total cost}}{\text{mass}}=\frac{42}{3.5}\).
  2. \(\frac{42}{3.5}=\frac{420}{35}=12\).
  3. Check: \(12\times 3.5=42\) dollars.
Q24★★☆☆3 marks

A train travels at a steady speed of 90 km/h. Express this speed in m/s.

Answer\(25\) m/s
  1. \(1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}\) and \(1\text{ h}=3600\text{ s}\).
  2. \(90\text{ km/h}=90\times\frac{1000}{3600}\text{ m/s}\).
  3. Simplify: \(\frac{1000}{3600}=\frac{5}{18}\), so the speed is \(90\times\frac{5}{18}=25\) m/s.
  4. Check: \(25\times 3.6=90\), and multiplying m/s by 3.6 converts back to km/h.
Common mistake

Multiplying by \(\frac{3600}{1000}\) by mistake — a speed in m/s must be a larger number than the same speed in km/h, and 25 < 90 confirms the correct direction of the conversion is a division by 3.6.

Q25★★☆☆3 marks

250 cm³ of a cleaning liquid has a mass of 210 g.
(a) Find its density in g/cm³.
(b) Find the mass of 400 cm³ of the liquid.

Answer(a) \(0.84\) g/cm³　(b) 336 g
  1. (a) Density \(=\frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}=\frac{210}{250}=0.84\) g/cm³.
  2. (b) Mass \(=\) density × volume \(=0.84\times 400\).
  3. \(0.84\times 400=336\) g.
  4. Check: 400 cm³ is 1.6 times 250 cm³, and \(210\times 1.6=336\).
Q26★★☆☆4 marks

A cyclist covers the first 60 km of a trip at 40 km/h and the next 60 km at 60 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole 120 km.

Answer\(48\) km/h
  1. Time for the first part \(=\frac{60}{40}=1.5\) h.
  2. Time for the second part \(=\frac{60}{60}=1\) h.
  3. Total time \(=1.5+1=2.5\) h; total distance \(=120\) km.
  4. Average speed \(=\frac{120}{2.5}=48\) km/h.
  5. Check: 48 lies between 40 and 60, and it is nearer 40 because the slower part took longer — exactly what we expect.
Common mistake

Averaging the speeds to get \(\frac{40+60}{2}=50\) km/h. That is only valid when the two times are equal, not when the two distances are equal.

Q27★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2015 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

An empty rectangular tank measures 1.8 m by 1.5 m by 0.6 m. A pump delivers water at 2.4 m³ per hour.
(a) Find the capacity of the tank in litres.
(b) Find the time needed to fill the tank completely, in minutes.

Answer(a) 1620 L　(b) 40.5 minutes
  1. (a) Volume \(=1.8\times 1.5\times 0.6=1.62\) m³.
  2. Since \(1\text{ m}^{3}=1000\text{ L}\), the capacity is \(1.62\times 1000=1620\) L.
  3. (b) Time \(=\frac{\text{volume}}{\text{flow rate}}=\frac{1.62}{2.4}\) hours.
  4. \(\frac{1.62}{2.4}=0.675\) h.
  5. In minutes: \(0.675\times 60=40.5\) minutes.
  6. Check: at 2.4 m³/h the pump delivers 40 L per minute, and \(1620\div 40=40.5\) minutes.
Q28★★★☆6 marks

A train makes a 240 km journey in two stages. It travels the first 150 km at 75 km/h, then the remaining 90 km at 90 km/h.
(a) Find the total journey time.
(b) Find the average speed for the whole journey.
(c) If the train had travelled the whole 240 km at the average speed found in (b), how would the journey time compare?

Answer(a) 3 hours　(b) 80 km/h　(c) exactly the same, 3 hours
  1. (a) First stage: \(\frac{150}{75}=2\) h. Second stage: \(\frac{90}{90}=1\) h. Total \(=3\) h.
  2. (b) Average speed \(=\frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}=\frac{240}{3}=80\) km/h.
  3. (c) At a constant 80 km/h the time would be \(\frac{240}{80}=3\) h, which is identical — that is exactly what an average speed means.
  4. Check: \(2\times 75=150\) km and \(1\times 90=90\) km, so the distances add to 240 km.

Type 5

Direct Variation · 7 questions

y = kx, y = kx², y = kx³, finding k

Core idea: \(y\propto x^{n}\) means \(y=kx^{n}\) for some non-zero constant \(k\). Always find \(k\) from the given pair of values first, then use the formula for the new value.

A useful ratio form follows from \(y=kx^{n}\): \(\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{n}\), which avoids finding \(k\) when only a change is described.

\(y\propto x^{2}\Rightarrow y=kx^{2}\)\(k=\frac{y}{x^{n}}\)\(\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}\right)^{n}\)
Q29★☆☆☆2 marks

It is given that \(y\) varies directly as \(x\), and \(y=12\) when \(x=4\). Find \(y\) when \(x=7\).

Answer\(y=21\)
  1. Write \(y=kx\).
  2. Substitute \(x=4\), \(y=12\): \(12=4k\), so \(k=3\).
  3. Hence \(y=3x\).
  4. When \(x=7\): \(y=3\times 7=21\).
  5. Check: \(x\) grew from 4 to 7 (a factor of 1.75), and \(12\times 1.75=21\).
Q30★☆☆☆2 marks

It is given that \(y\) varies directly as \(x^{2}\), and \(y=50\) when \(x=5\). Find \(y\) when \(x=9\).

Answer\(y=162\)
  1. Write \(y=kx^{2}\).
  2. Substitute: \(50=k(5)^{2}=25k\), so \(k=2\).
  3. Hence \(y=2x^{2}\).
  4. When \(x=9\): \(y=2\times 81=162\).
Common mistake

Squaring after finding \(k\) instead of before — the square applies to \(x\) only, never to \(k\).

Q31★★☆☆3 marks

The mass \(M\) grams of a metal cube varies directly as the cube of its side \(s\) cm. When \(s=2\), \(M=24\). Find \(M\) when \(s=5\).

Answer\(375\) g
  1. Write \(M=ks^{3}\).
  2. Substitute \(s=2\), \(M=24\): \(24=k(8)\), so \(k=3\).
  3. Hence \(M=3s^{3}\).
  4. When \(s=5\): \(M=3\times 125=375\) g.
  5. Check with the ratio form: \(\frac{M}{24}=\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{3}=15.625\), and \(24\times 15.625=375\).
Q32★★☆☆3 marks

The cost $C of printing \(n\) booklets varies directly as \(n\). Printing 250 booklets costs $1875.
(a) Express \(C\) in terms of \(n\).
(b) Find the cost of printing 480 booklets.

Answer(a) \(C=7.5n\)　(b) $3600
  1. Write \(C=kn\).
  2. Substitute: \(1875=250k\), so \(k=7.5\).
  3. (a) Hence \(C=7.5n\) (cost in dollars, \(n\) booklets).
  4. (b) \(C=7.5\times 480=3600\), i.e. $3600.
  5. Check: each booklet costs $7.50, and 480 booklets at that unit price give \(480\times 7.5=3600\).
Q33★★☆☆4 marks

It is given that \(y\) varies directly as \(x^{2}\), and \(y=45\) when \(x=3\).
(a) Find the constant of variation.
(b) Find the positive value of \(x\) when \(y=180\).

Answer(a) \(k=5\)　(b) \(x=6\)
  1. Write \(y=kx^{2}\).
  2. (a) Substitute \(x=3\), \(y=45\): \(45=9k\), so \(k=5\). Hence \(y=5x^{2}\).
  3. (b) Put \(y=180\): \(180=5x^{2}\), so \(x^{2}=36\).
  4. Then \(x=6\) or \(x=-6\); the question asks for the positive value, so \(x=6\).
  5. Check: \(5\times 6^{2}=5\times 36=180\).
Q34★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2016 Paper 1 Q8 ↗

It is given that \(y\) varies directly as \(x^{2}\). When \(x\) increases from 4 to 6, \(y\) increases by 100.
(a) Find the constant of variation.
(b) Find the value of \(y\) when \(x=4\).

Answer(a) \(k=5\)　(b) \(y=80\)
  1. Let \(y=kx^{2}\). At \(x=4\), \(y=16k\); at \(x=6\), \(y=36k\).
  2. The increase is \(36k-16k=20k\).
  3. (a) So \(20k=100\), giving \(k=5\).
  4. (b) At \(x=4\): \(y=5\times 16=80\).
  5. Check: at \(x=6\), \(y=5\times 36=180\), and \(180-80=100\), as stated.
Common mistake

Setting \(k(6^{2}-4^{2})=100\) but then dividing by 2 instead of by 20 — work out \(36k-16k\) fully before dividing.

Q35★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2017 Paper 1 Q8 ↗

The mass \(W\) grams of a solid sphere varies directly as the cube of its radius \(r\) cm. A sphere of radius 3 cm has mass 216 g.
(a) Express \(W\) in terms of \(r\).
(b) Find the radius of a sphere of the same material with mass 1000 g.
(c) A second sphere has twice the radius of the first. How many times is its mass greater?

Answer(a) \(W=8r^{3}\)　(b) 5 cm　(c) 8 times
  1. Write \(W=kr^{3}\).
  2. (a) Substitute \(r=3\), \(W=216\): \(216=k(27)\), so \(k=8\). Hence \(W=8r^{3}\).
  3. (b) Put \(W=1000\): \(8r^{3}=1000\), so \(r^{3}=125\) and \(r=5\) cm.
  4. (c) If \(r\) doubles, \(W\) is multiplied by \(2^{3}=8\), so the mass is 8 times greater.
  5. Check (b): \(8\times 5^{3}=8\times 125=1000\) g.

Type 6

Inverse and Joint Variation · 7 questions

y = k/x, y = k/x², z ∝ xy², combined variation

Core idea: \(y\) varies inversely as \(x\) means \(y=\frac{k}{x}\), so the product \(xy\) stays constant. In joint variation every stated factor multiplies into one constant: \(z\propto xy^{2}\) means \(z=kxy^{2}\).

For combined direct and inverse variation, the factors that vary directly go on top and the factors that vary inversely go underneath.

\(y=\frac{k}{x}\)\(xy=\text{constant}\)\(z=kxy^{2}\)
Q36★☆☆☆2 marks

It is given that \(y\) varies inversely as \(x\), and \(y=6\) when \(x=4\). Find \(y\) when \(x=8\).

Answer\(y=3\)
  1. Write \(y=\frac{k}{x}\), i.e. \(xy=k\).
  2. Substitute \(x=4\), \(y=6\): \(k=4\times 6=24\).
  3. So \(y=\frac{24}{x}\).
  4. When \(x=8\): \(y=\frac{24}{8}=3\).
  5. Check: \(x\) doubled from 4 to 8, so \(y\) halved from 6 to 3 — exactly what inverse variation predicts.
Q37★☆☆☆2 marks

It is given that \(z\) varies jointly as \(x\) and \(y\), and \(z=24\) when \(x=3\) and \(y=4\). Find \(z\) when \(x=5\) and \(y=6\).

Answer\(z=60\)
  1. Write \(z=kxy\).
  2. Substitute: \(24=k(3)(4)=12k\), so \(k=2\).
  3. Hence \(z=2xy\).
  4. When \(x=5\), \(y=6\): \(z=2\times 5\times 6=60\).
Q38★★☆☆3 marks

It is given that \(y\) varies inversely as \(x^{2}\), and \(y=9\) when \(x=2\). Find \(y\) when \(x=3\).

Answer\(y=4\)
  1. Write \(y=\frac{k}{x^{2}}\), i.e. \(yx^{2}=k\).
  2. Substitute: \(k=9\times 2^{2}=36\).
  3. So \(y=\frac{36}{x^{2}}\).
  4. When \(x=3\): \(y=\frac{36}{9}=4\).
  5. Check: \(4\times 3^{2}=36\), the same constant.
Common mistake

Writing \(y=\frac{k}{x^{2}}\) but then substituting \(k=9\times 2=18\) — the square must include the \(x\) value as well.

Q39★★☆☆3 marks

It is given that \(z\) varies directly as \(x\) and as the square of \(y\). When \(x=3\) and \(y=2\), \(z=48\). Find \(z\) when \(x=5\) and \(y=3\).

Answer\(z=180\)
  1. Write \(z=kxy^{2}\).
  2. Substitute: \(48=k(3)(2^{2})=12k\), so \(k=4\).
  3. Hence \(z=4xy^{2}\).
  4. When \(x=5\), \(y=3\): \(z=4\times 5\times 9=180\).
  5. Check: \(180=4\times 5\times 9\), and the constant \(\frac{z}{xy^{2}}=\frac{180}{45}=4\) is unchanged.
Q40★★☆☆4 marks

The time \(t\) minutes needed to fill a swimming pool varies inversely as the number \(n\) of pipes used. With 6 pipes the pool takes 40 minutes.
(a) Find \(t\) when 8 pipes are used.
(b) How many pipes are needed to fill the pool in 24 minutes?

Answer(a) 30 minutes　(b) 10 pipes
  1. Write \(t=\frac{k}{n}\), so \(nt=k\).
  2. Substitute \(n=6\), \(t=40\): \(k=240\). Hence \(t=\frac{240}{n}\).
  3. (a) \(n=8\): \(t=\frac{240}{8}=30\) minutes.
  4. (b) \(t=24\): \(24=\frac{240}{n}\), so \(n=10\) pipes.
  5. Check: \(10\times 24=240\) and \(8\times 30=240\), the same constant of variation.
Q41★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2020 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

It is given that \(z\) varies directly as \(x\) and inversely as \(y^{2}\). When \(x=8\) and \(y=2\), \(z=6\).
(a) Express \(z\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\).
(b) Find \(z\) when \(x=27\) and \(y=3\).

Answer(a) \(z=\frac{3x}{y^{2}}\)　(b) \(z=9\)
  1. Write \(z=\frac{kx}{y^{2}}\): \(x\) is on top because it varies directly, \(y^{2}\) underneath because it varies inversely.
  2. (a) Substitute \(x=8\), \(y=2\), \(z=6\): \(6=\frac{8k}{4}=2k\), so \(k=3\). Hence \(z=\frac{3x}{y^{2}}\).
  3. (b) Put \(x=27\), \(y=3\): \(z=\frac{3\times 27}{9}=\frac{81}{9}=9\).
  4. Check: with \(k=3\), \(z=\frac{3(27)}{3^{2}}=9\), consistent.
Q42★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2021 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

The time \(T\) hours needed to build a wall varies directly as the area \(A\) m² of the wall and inversely as the number \(n\) of workers. When \(A=120\) and \(n=5\), \(T=9\).
(a) Express \(T\) in terms of \(A\) and \(n\).
(b) Find \(T\) when \(A=200\) and \(n=15\).
(c) If 15 workers take 5 hours, how many workers are needed to finish a wall of area 320 m² in 4 hours?

Answer(a) \(T=\frac{0.375A}{n}\)　(b) 5 hours　(c) 30 workers
  1. Write \(T=\frac{kA}{n}\).
  2. (a) Substitute \(A=120\), \(n=5\), \(T=9\): \(9=\frac{120k}{5}=24k\), so \(k=\frac{9}{24}=0.375\). Hence \(T=\frac{0.375A}{n}\).
  3. (b) \(T=\frac{0.375\times 200}{15}=\frac{75}{15}=5\) hours.
  4. (c) Put \(T=4\), \(A=320\): \(4=\frac{0.375\times 320}{n}=\frac{120}{n}\), so \(n=30\).
  5. Check (c): \(30\) workers on 320 m² give \(T=\frac{120}{30}=4\) h, as required.
Common mistake

In (c), solving \(4=\frac{120}{n}\) as \(n=\frac{4}{120}\) — cross-multiply to get \(4n=120\) first.

Type 7

Maps, Scales, Mixtures and Multi-part Problems · 8 questions

Scale factors, mixtures, combined variation in context

Core idea: A scale of \(1:n\) means every length on the drawing is \(\frac{1}{n}\) of the real length, so real length \(=\) map length \(\times n\). Areas, however, scale by \(n^{2}\), because both dimensions are enlarged.

In mixture problems the amount of one ingredient is fixed while the other is added; write the new ratio as an equation and solve.

\(1:n\)\(\text{real}=n\times\text{map}\)\(\text{area factor}=n^{2}\)
Q43★☆☆☆3 marks

A recipe for 4 people needs 300 g of flour, 200 mL of milk and 3 eggs. Find the amount of each ingredient needed for 10 people.

Answer750 g flour, 500 mL milk, 7.5 eggs
  1. The amounts vary directly with the number of people: multiplier \(=\frac{10}{4}=2.5\).
  2. Flour: \(300\times 2.5=750\) g.
  3. Milk: \(200\times 2.5=500\) mL.
  4. Eggs: \(3\times 2.5=7.5\), so 7 whole eggs and half an egg (or scale a larger batch).
  5. Check: \(750:300=2.5\) and \(500:200=2.5\), the same multiplier for every ingredient.
Common mistake

Adding 6 to each quantity (the difference in people) instead of multiplying by the ratio \(\frac{10}{4}\) — direct proportion needs multiplication, not addition.

Q44★☆☆☆3 marks

A map has a scale of \(1:50\,000\). Two villages are 4 cm apart on the map. Find the actual distance between them in km.

Answer2 km
  1. Real distance \(=4\times 50\,000=200\,000\) cm.
  2. Convert: \(200\,000\text{ cm}=2000\text{ m}\) (divide by 100).
  3. \(2000\text{ m}=2\text{ km}\) (divide by 1000).
  4. Check: 2 km in cm is 200 000, and \(200\,000\div 50\,000=4\) cm on the map.
Q45★☆☆☆3 marks

On a plan, 1 cm represents 2.5 m.
(a) Write the scale as a ratio in the form \(1:n\).
(b) A corridor measures 6.4 cm on the plan. Find its actual length in metres.

Answer(a) \(1:250\)　(b) 16 m
  1. (a) \(2.5\text{ m}=250\text{ cm}\), so 1 cm on the plan represents 250 cm in reality: scale \(1:250\).
  2. (b) Actual length \(=6.4\times 2.5=16\) m (using 1 cm \(=\) 2.5 m).
  3. Check with the ratio: \(6.4\times 250=1600\) cm \(=16\) m.
Q46★★☆☆4 marks

On a map of scale \(1:25\,000\), a rectangular playing field measures 2 cm by 3 cm.
(a) Find the actual dimensions of the field in metres.
(b) Find the actual area of the field in m².

Answer(a) 500 m by 750 m　(b) \(375\,000\) m²
  1. 1 cm on the map represents 25 000 cm \(=250\) m.
  2. (a) \(2\text{ cm}\to 2\times 250=500\) m; \(3\text{ cm}\to 3\times 250=750\) m.
  3. (b) Area \(=500\times 750=375\,000\) m².
  4. Check with the area scale: \((25\,000)^{2}\) cm² per cm², so \(6\times 625\,000\,000=3.75\times 10^{9}\) cm², i.e. 375 000 m².
Common mistake

Multiplying the map area by 25 000 instead of \(25\,000^{2}\) — a scale factor applies to lengths, so areas grow by the square of it.

Q47★★☆☆4 marks

A 40 L container holds a fruit drink made of juice and water in the ratio \(3:5\). How much water must be added so that the ratio of juice to water becomes \(3:7\)?

Answer10 L of water
  1. Total parts \(=3+5=8\), so one part \(=40\div 8=5\) L.
  2. Juice \(=3\times 5=15\) L; water \(=5\times 5=25\) L.
  3. Let \(x\) litres of water be added. The juice is unchanged: \(\frac{15}{25+x}=\frac{3}{7}\).
  4. Cross-multiply: \(105=75+3x\), so \(3x=30\) and \(x=10\).
  5. Check: the new mixture is 50 L with 15 L juice and 35 L water, and \(15:35=3:7\).
Common mistake

Adding water to the total but also increasing the juice — only the water changes, so 15 L of juice stays fixed.

Q48★★☆☆5 marks

An alloy of mass 80 kg contains copper and zinc in the ratio \(7:3\). How much zinc must be added to make the ratio of copper to zinc \(7:5\)?

Answer16 kg of zinc
  1. Total parts \(=7+3=10\), so one part \(=80\div 10=8\) kg.
  2. Copper \(=7\times 8=56\) kg; zinc \(=3\times 8=24\) kg.
  3. Let \(x\) kg of zinc be added; the copper stays at 56 kg: \(\frac{56}{24+x}=\frac{7}{5}\).
  4. Cross-multiply: \(280=168+7x\), so \(7x=112\) and \(x=16\).
  5. Check: new zinc \(=40\) kg, and \(56:40=7:5\).
Q49★★★☆5 marksPast paper · 2023 Paper 1 Q12 ↗

A park is drawn on a map of scale \(1:20\,000\) as a rectangle measuring 7.5 cm by 4 cm.
(a) Find the actual dimensions of the park in metres.
(b) Find the actual area of the park in m², giving your answer in scientific notation.
(c) A path 1 km long crosses the park. How long is it on the map, in cm?

Answer(a) 1500 m by 800 m　(b) \(1.2\times 10^{6}\) m²　(c) 5 cm
  1. 1 cm on the map represents 20 000 cm \(=200\) m.
  2. (a) \(7.5\times 200=1500\) m and \(4\times 200=800\) m.
  3. (b) Area \(=1500\times 800=1\,200\,000=1.2\times 10^{6}\) m².
  4. (c) 1 km \(=1000\) m \(=100\,000\) cm, so the map length is \(100\,000\div 20\,000=5\) cm.
  5. Check (c): \(5\text{ cm}\times 200=1000\) m \(=1\) km, correct.
Q50★★★☆6 marksPast paper · 2024 Paper 1 Q10 ↗

The mass \(M\) kg of a metal wire varies directly as its length \(L\) m and as the square of its diameter \(d\) mm. A wire of length 20 m and diameter 2 mm has mass 1.2 kg.
(a) Express \(M\) in terms of \(L\) and \(d\).
(b) Find the mass of a wire of length 35 m and diameter 3 mm.
(c) Two wires of the same material have diameters 2 mm and 4 mm and the same mass. Find the ratio of their lengths.

Answer(a) \(M=0.015Ld^{2}\)　(b) 4.725 kg　(c) \(4:1\)
  1. Write \(M=kLd^{2}\).
  2. (a) Substitute \(L=20\), \(d=2\), \(M=1.2\): \(1.2=k(20)(4)=80k\), so \(k=0.015\). Hence \(M=0.015Ld^{2}\).
  3. (b) \(M=0.015\times 35\times 3^{2}=0.015\times 35\times 9\).
  4. \(0.015\times 35=0.525\) and \(0.525\times 9=4.725\) kg.
  5. (c) For equal masses, \(L_{1}d_{1}^{2}=L_{2}d_{2}^{2}\), so \(\frac{L_{1}}{L_{2}}=\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{d_{1}^{2}}=\frac{16}{4}=4\), i.e. \(4:1\).
  6. Check (c): the thicker wire is twice as thick, so it must be a quarter as long to have the same mass.
Common mistake

In (c), writing the ratio as \(2:1\) — length varies inversely as the square of the diameter, so doubling \(d\) cuts \(L\) to one quarter.
