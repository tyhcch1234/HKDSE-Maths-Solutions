Statistics — Standard Deviation & Standard Score under a Linear Adjustment

Standard Deviation Standard Score Linear Transformation Mean & Spread

Question Full Question Text

The standard deviation of the test scores obtained by a class of students in a Mathematics test is \(10\) marks. All the students fail in the test, so the test score of each student is adjusted such that each score is increased by \(20\%\) and then extra \(5\) marks are added.

(a) Find the standard deviation of the test scores after the score adjustment. [1 mark]

(b) Is there any change in the standard score of each student due to the score adjustment? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

Part (a) Standard deviation after adjustment

Each raw score \(x\) becomes the adjusted score \[y = 1.2x + 5.\] Drag the slider to change the raw spread and watch how the adjusted scores move — but keep your eye on how far apart the scores are.

Raw mean \(\mu_x\) = 60
Raw S.D. \(\sigma_x\) = 10 marks
Adjusted mean \(\mu_y=1.2\mu_x+5\) = 77.0
Adjusted S.D. \(\sigma_y=1.2\sigma_x\) = 12.0 marks
Step 11A

Increasing every score by the same percentage does not change the spread.

\[ \text{Raw S.D.}=10 \] \[ \text{After adding }20\%:\quad \sigma = 10\times(1+20\%) = 10\times 1.2 = 12 \]
Hint: Multiplying every score by the same positive constant \(k\) multiplies the S.D. by \(k\).
Step 21AAnswer

Adding the same extra \(5\) marks to every score is a translation, which shifts the mean but leaves the spread (and hence the S.D.) unchanged.

Standard deviation after adjustment \(=12\) marks.
Hint: Only the centre moved by \(+5\); the distances between scores are untouched.

Part (b) Change in the standard score?

The standard score (z-score) of a student is \[z=\frac{x-\mu_x}{\sigma_x}\qquad\text{before},\qquad z'=\frac{y-\mu_y}{\sigma_y}\qquad\text{after.}\] Use the slider to change the raw score \(x\); compare \(z\) and \(z'\\) for the same student.

\(z=\dfrac{x-\mu_x}{10}\) = -1.20
\(z'=\dfrac{(1.2x+5)-(1.2\mu_x+5)}{12}\) = -1.20
Change = 0.00
Step 11M

Let \(x\) be the test score and \(m\) the mean before adjustment. Before:

\[ z = \frac{x-m}{10} \] After the score becomes \(1.2x+5\), and the new mean is \(1.2m+5\): \[ z' = \frac{(1.2x+5)-(1.2m+5)}{12} \]
Hint: The mean also gets the same \(20\%\) increase and \(+5\), so it becomes \(1.2m+5\).
Step 21M

Simplify the numerator — the \(+5\) cancels:

\[ z' = \frac{1.2x+5-1.2m-5}{12} = \frac{1.2(x-m)}{12} \] \[ z' = \frac{1.2(x-m)}{1.2\times 10} = \frac{x-m}{10} = z \]
Hint: Note \(12 = 1.2\times 10\), so the factor \(1.2\) cancels completely.
Step 31AAnswer

Since \(z'=z\) for every student, the standard score is unchanged.

No, there is no change in the standard score of each student, because the adjustment is a linear transformation \(y=1.2x+5\) applied to both the scores and the mean, so the ratio \(z=\dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\) stays the same.
Hint: A standard score measures relative position. A linear transformation of every score preserves relative position.

Summary Answers at a glance

  • (a) Standard deviation after adjustment \(=\;10\times 1.2=\;\)12 marks.
  • (b) Let the raw score be \(x\) and the class mean be \(m\). Before: \(z=\dfrac{x-m}{10}\). After: the score is \(1.2x+5\), the mean is \(1.2m+5\), and the S.D. is \(12\): \[ z'=\frac{(1.2x+5)-(1.2m+5)}{12}=\frac{1.2(x-m)}{12}=\frac{x-m}{10}=z. \] So there is no change in the standard score of each student.
  • Key idea: \(y=kx+c\) with \(k>0\) gives \(\mu_y=k\mu_x+c\) and \(\sigma_y=k\sigma_x\), hence standard scores are invariant.
