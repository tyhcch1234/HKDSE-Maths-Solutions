HKDSE 2012 Paper 1 — Q15 (Statistics)

The standard deviation of the test scores obtained by a class of students in a Mathematics test is $10$ marks. All the students fail in the test, so the test score of each student is adjusted such that each score is increased by $20\%$ and then extra $5$ marks are added.

(a) Find the standard deviation of the test scores after the score adjustment. [1 mark]

(b) Is there any change in the standard score of each student due to the score adjustment? Explain your answer. [2 marks]

1Understand the linear transformation

Let $x$ be an original test score. After the adjustment each score becomes \[ x_{\text{new}} = x(1+20\%) + 5 = 1.2x + 5 .\] This is a linear transformation of every score with multiplier $1.2$ and shift $+5$.

Hint: write the adjustment as one formula before doing any statistics.
Effect of a Linear Transformation on Standard Deviation \(\sigma_{cx+k}=c\,\sigma_{x}\quad(c>0)\) Adding \(k\) shifts every score, so the spread is unchanged; multiplying by the positive constant \(c\) stretches every deviation by the factor \(c\).
2Effect of multiplying / adding on the standard deviation

For a set of data $\{x_i\}$ with standard deviation $\sigma$:

• Adding a constant $k$ to every score adds $k$ to the mean and leaves the standard deviation unchanged (all scores shift equally, so the spread is preserved).

• Multiplying every score by a positive constant $c$ multiplies the standard deviation by $c$: \[ \sigma_{\,cx} = c\,\sigma_x \qquad (c>0) .\]

Hint: the $+5$ only moves the whole distribution; the $\times 1.2$ stretches it.
3(a) New standard deviation

Here $c = 1.2 = 120\%$ and the original standard deviation is $10$ marks.

\[ \sigma_{\text{new}} = 1.2 \times 10 = 12 \text{ marks}. \]

(The $+5$ changes the mean only, not the standard deviation.)

Hint: answer with the unit "marks".
Standard Score \(z=\dfrac{x-\bar{x}}{\sigma}\) Measure how many standard deviations a score lies from the mean; the units cancel.
4(b) Set up the standard scores

Let $m$ be the original mean score. The standard score of a student with score $x$ before adjustment is \[ z_{\text{before}} = \frac{x-m}{10}. \]

After the adjustment: new score $= 1.2x+5$, new mean $= 1.2m+5$, new standard deviation $= 12$. So \[ z_{\text{after}} = \frac{(1.2x+5)-(1.2m+5)}{12} = \frac{1.2(x-m)}{12}. \]

Hint: subtract the new mean in the numerator first.
5(b) Compare and conclude

The constants $+5$ cancel in the numerator: \[ z_{\text{after}} = \frac{1.2(x-m)}{12} = \frac{x-m}{10} = z_{\text{before}}. \]

Hence each student's standard score is unchanged by the adjustment.

Hint: many marks are for the conclusion (1A) after the algebra (1M).
Answer:  (a) $12$ marks   |   (b) No. The standard score of every student stays the same.
PRACTICESimilar question — try it yourself

Question. In a test the scores have mean \(m\) and standard deviation \(15\) marks. Every score is first decreased by \(10\%\) and then \(4\) marks are added.
(a) Find the new standard deviation.
(b) A student claims that the standard score of every student is unchanged. Do you agree? Explain.

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Write the adjustment as one formula: \(x_{\text{new}}=0.9x+4\).
Step 2. Here \(c=0.9\) and \(k=4\), so \(\sigma_{\text{new}}=0.9\times15=13.5\) marks; the \(+4\) does not affect the spread.
Step 3. The new mean is \(0.9m+4\). Standard score after: \(z_{\text{after}}=\dfrac{(0.9x+4)-(0.9m+4)}{13.5}=\dfrac{0.9(x-m)}{13.5}\).
Step 4. \(\dfrac{0.9}{13.5}=\dfrac{1}{15}\), so \(z_{\text{after}}=\dfrac{x-m}{15}=z_{\text{before}}\).
Step 5. Hence every standard score is unchanged and the claim is correct.
Answer: (a) \(13.5\) marks. (b) Agree \u2014 \(z_{\text{after}}=\dfrac{x-m}{15}=z_{\text{before}}\).
