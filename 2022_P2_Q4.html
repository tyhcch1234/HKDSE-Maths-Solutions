Solve the equation for x (c is a constant)

Question

Let $c$ be a constant. Solve the equation \[ (x-c)(x-4c)=(3c-x)(x-4c). \]

  • A. $x=2c$
  • B. $x=3c$
  • C. $x=c$  or  $x=3c$
  • D. $x=2c$  or  $x=4c$
STEP 1Bring everything to one side.
\[ (x-c)(x-4c)-(3c-x)(x-4c)=0 \]
Hint: Both terms share the common factor \((x-4c)\) — never divide by it (it may be zero). Subtract instead.
STEP 2Take out the common factor \((x-4c)\).
\[ \big[(x-c)-(3c-x)\big](x-4c)=0 \]
Hint: Factorisation by grouping: \(AB-CB=(A-C)B\).
Factorisation by Grouping \(AB-CB=(A-C)B\) The two products share the factor \((x-4c)\), so subtracting brackets is safe even when \(x=4c\).
STEP 3Simplify the bracket.
\[ \big[x-c-3c+x\big](x-4c)=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; (2x-4c)(x-4c)=0 \]
Hint: Watch the double negative: \(-(-x)=+x\), so \(x+x=2x\) and \(-c-3c=-4c\).
STEP 4Extract the constant factor 2.
\[ 2(x-2c)(x-4c)=0 \]
Hint: \(2\neq 0\), so it can be divided out — the roots come from the two brackets only.
STEP 5Zero-product property — read off the roots.
\[ x-2c=0 \quad\text{or}\quad x-4c=0 \]
\[ x=2c \quad\text{or}\quad x=4c \]
Hint: If a product equals zero, at least one factor is zero. Always give both values for "or" answers.
Zero Product Property \(PQ=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P=0\ \text{ or }\ Q=0\) \((x-2c)(x-4c)=0\) therefore gives \(x=2c\) or \(x=4c\).
ANSWER D  —  \(x=2c\) or \(x=4c\)

Question. Let \(c\) be a constant. Solve the equation \((x-2c)(x-5c)=(4c-x)(x-5c)\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Bring everything to one side: \((x-2c)(x-5c)-(4c-x)(x-5c)=0\).
Step 2. Take out the common factor \((x-5c)\): \(\big[(x-2c)-(4c-x)\big](x-5c)=0\).
Step 3. Simplify the bracket: \(x-2c-4c+x=2x-6c\), so \((2x-6c)(x-5c)=0\).
Step 4. Extract the constant \(2\): \(2(x-3c)(x-5c)=0\), and \(2\neq0\).
Step 5. By the zero product property, \(x-3c=0\) or \(x-5c=0\), i.e. \(x=3c\) or \(x=5c\).
Answer: \(x=3c\) or \(x=5c\)
