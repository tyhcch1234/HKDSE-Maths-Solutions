Compound Inequalities

Question

Consider the compound inequality \(3x-4<5\le\frac{2-7x}{6}\) … (*).
(a) Solve (*).
(b) Write down the greatest integer satisfying (*). (4 marks)

Solution

STEP 1 — split into two inequalities
\[ 3x-4<5 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 5\le\frac{2-7x}{6} \]
STEP 2 — solve the first inequality
\[ 3x-4<5 \implies 3x<9 \implies x<3 \]
STEP 3 — solve the second inequality
Multiply through by 6 (positive, so the sign is unchanged): \[ 30\le 2-7x \implies 28\le -7x \] Divide by \(-7\) and reverse the inequality sign: \[ -4\ge x \implies x\le -4 \]
Reversing the Inequality Sign \(a-kb\quad(k>0)\) Dividing \(28\le-7x\) by \(-7\) gives \(-4\ge x\), i.e. \(x\le-4\).
STEP 4 — intersect the two ranges
Both must hold: \(x\le -4\) and \(x<3\). The intersection is \[ x\le -4 \]
Intersection of Solution Sets \(x\le-4\ \text{and}\ x<3\ \Rightarrow\ x\le-4\) The stricter upper bound \(x\le-4\) lies inside \(x<3\), so the overlap is \(x\le-4\).
PART (b)
The greatest integer satisfying \(x\le -4\) is \(-4\) itself (since \(x=-4\) satisfies both \(3(-4)-4=-16<5\) and \(5\le(2+28)/6=5\)).
(a) \(x\le -4\)    (b) \(-4\)
