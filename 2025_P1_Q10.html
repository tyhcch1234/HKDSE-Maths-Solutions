Polynomials Remainder Theorem Factor Theorem Rational Roots [2025 #10] · 6 marks

Let \(f(x) = 2x^{3} + hx^{2} + kx + 15\), where \(h\) and \(k\) are constants. When \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x+2\), the remainder is \(-45\). It is given that \(f(x)\) is divisible by \(2x-5\).

(a) Find \(h\) and \(k\). (3 marks)
(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation \(f(x)=0\) are rational numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer. (3 marks)

🧩 Step-by-Step Guided Solution

0 / 7 steps
1
Translate "divided by \(x+2\), remainder \(-45\)"
Use the Remainder Theorem

The Remainder Theorem says: when \(f(x)\) is divided by \(x-a\), the remainder equals \(f(a)\).

Here the divisor is \(x+2 = x-(-2)\), so the remainder is \(f(-2)\). Hence

\[ f(-2) = -45 \]
💡 Hint — Formula & Meaning
  • Remainder Theorem: \(f(x) = (x-a)Q(x) + R\) and \(R = f(a)\).
  • Divisor \(= x+2 \Rightarrow a = -2\) (set the divisor to zero: \(x+2=0\)).
  • \(f(-2)=-45\) means substituting \(x=-2\) into \(f\) gives \(-45\).
2
Substitute \(x=-2\) and simplify
Obtain the first linear equation in \(h\), \(k\)
\[ \begin{aligned} 2(-2)^3 + h(-2)^2 + k(-2) + 15 &= -45 \\[2pt] 2(-8) + h(4) - 2k + 15 &= -45 \\[2pt] -16 + 4h - 2k + 15 &= -45 \\[2pt] 4h - 2k - 1 &= -45 \\[2pt] 4h - 2k &= -44 \end{aligned} \]

Dividing every term by 2 gives a tidier equation:

\[ 2h - k = -22 \quad \text{......(1)} \]
💡 Hint — Watch the signs
  • \((-2)^3 = -8\), so \(2(-8) = -16\).
  • \((-2)^2 = +4\), so \(h(-2)^2 = 4h\).
  • \(k(-2) = -2k\).
  • Bring everything except \(h,k\) terms to the right side carefully.
3
Translate "divisible by \(2x-5\)"
Use the Factor Theorem

"Divisible by \(2x-5\)" means \(2x-5\) is a factor of \(f(x)\), so the remainder is \(0\).

By the Factor Theorem, solve \(2x-5 = 0\) to get \(x=\dfrac{5}{2}\), then

\[ f\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = 0 \]
💡 Hint — Do not confuse divisor with root
  • Divisor \(= 2x-5\); to use the Factor/Remainder Theorem, first solve the divisor \(=0\).
  • General form: if divisor is \(ax+b\), substitute \(x = -\dfrac{b}{a}\). Here \(a=2,\ b=-5\).
  • Divisible \(\Rightarrow\) remainder \(=0\), not "the coefficient \(=0\)".
4
Substitute \(x=\dfrac{5}{2}\) and simplify
Obtain the second linear equation in \(h\), \(k\)
\[ \begin{aligned} 2\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^{3} + h\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^{2} + k\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) + 15 &= 0 \\[4pt] 2\cdot\tfrac{125}{8} + h\cdot\tfrac{25}{4} + \tfrac{5k}{2} + 15 &= 0 \\[4pt] \tfrac{125}{4} + \tfrac{25h}{4} + \tfrac{5k}{2} + 15 &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

Multiply every term by \(4\) to clear the fractions:

\[ \begin{aligned} 125 + 25h + 10k + 60 &= 0 \\[2pt] 25h + 10k + 185 &= 0 \\[2pt] 5h + 2k &= -37 \quad \text{......(2)} \end{aligned} \]
💡 Hint — Clearing fractions
  • \(\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^3 = \dfrac{125}{8}\), and \(2 \times \dfrac{125}{8} = \dfrac{125}{4}\).
  • \(\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{25}{4}\).
  • The LCM of denominators \(4, 2\) is \(4\) — multiply the whole equation by 4.
5
Solve the simultaneous equations
Find \(h\) and \(k\) — answer to (a)
\[ \begin{cases} 2h - k = -22 \\ 5h + 2k = -37 \end{cases} \]
From \((1)\): \(k = 2h + 22\). Substitute into \((2)\).
\[ \begin{aligned} 5h + 2(2h + 22) &= -37 \\[2pt] 5h + 4h + 44 &= -37 \\[2pt] 9h &= -81 \\[2pt] h &= -9 \end{aligned} \]

Then back-substitute:

\[ k = 2(-9) + 22 = -18 + 22 = 4 \]
(a) \(\;h = -9,\quad k = 4\)
💡 Hint — Substitution or elimination
  • Either method works: make one variable the subject, or eliminate by adding multiples.
  • Always check your answer in both original equations.
6
Write \(f(x)\) and factorise completely
Start part (b) — find all roots

With \(h=-9\) and \(k=4\):

\[ f(x) = 2x^3 - 9x^2 + 4x + 15 \]

Since \(2x-5\) is a factor, divide \(f(x)\) by \(2x-5\) (long division or synthetic division) to get the quadratic quotient:

\[ f(x) = (2x-5)(x^2 - 2x - 3) \]

Now factorise the quadratic \(x^2 - 2x - 3 = (x+1)(x-3)\):

\[ f(x) = (2x-5)(x+1)(x-3) \]
💡 Hint — Getting the quotient
  • Dividing a cubic by a linear factor leaves a quadratic quotient.
  • Check: \((2x-5)(x^2-2x-3)\) should expand back to \(2x^3-9x^2+4x+15\).
  • Factorising \(x^2-2x-3\): find two numbers with product \(-3\) and sum \(-2\): they are \(+1\) and \(-3\).
7
Solve and conclude on the claim
Answer to (b) — with clear justification

Set each factor to zero:

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x - 5 &= 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \tfrac{5}{2} \\[2pt] x + 1 &= 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = -1 \\[2pt] x - 3 &= 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 3 \end{aligned} \]

So the roots are \(\dfrac{5}{2},\; -1\) and \(3\). Each of them can be written as a fraction of two integers (note \(-1 = \dfrac{-1}{1}\) and \(3 = \dfrac{3}{1}\)), so all three are rational numbers.

(b) Conclusion

\(f(x)=0 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{5}{2},\,-1\) or \(3\). All three roots are rational numbers, so the claim is agreed.

💡 Hint — What counts as a "rational number"?
  • A rational number can be written as \(\dfrac{p}{q}\) where \(p,q\) are integers and \(q \neq 0\).
  • Any integer (like \(-1\), \(3\)) is rational. Any terminating/repeating decimal is rational.
  • Irrational examples: \(\sqrt{2},\ \pi,\ e\).
  • Marks are awarded for stating the roots and explicitly saying they are rational.

📝 Summary & Exam Tips

Part (a) — 3 marks
  • 1M for using \(f(-2) = -45\).
  • 1M for using \(f\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = 0\).
  • 1A for both \(h=-9\) and \(k=4\) correct.
Part (b) — 3 marks
  • 1M for factorising \(f(x)\) using the known factor \(2x-5\).
  • 1M for finding the roots \(\dfrac{5}{2}, -1, 3\).
  • 1A for concluding "agreed" with a reason (roots are rational).
⚠️ Common pitfalls
  • Substituting \(x=5\) instead of \(x=\tfrac{5}{2}\) when using the divisor \(2x-5\).
  • Sign errors when expanding \(2(-2)^3\) or moving terms across the equals sign.
  • Finding the roots but forgetting to state that they are rational — the reason earns the mark.
