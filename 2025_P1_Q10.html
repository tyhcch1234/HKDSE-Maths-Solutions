HKDSE 2025 · Paper 1 Q10 · Polynomials

Let $f(x)=2x^{3}+hx^{2}+kx+15$, where $h$ and $k$ are constants. When $f(x)$ is divided by $x+2$, the remainder is $-45$. It is given that $f(x)$ is divisible by $2x-5$.

(a) Find $h$ and $k$. (3 marks)

(b) Someone claims that all the roots of the equation $f(x)=0$ are rational numbers. Do you agree? Explain your answer. (3 marks)

Part (a)

1Apply the Remainder Theorem

Dividing by $x+2$ gives remainder $-45$, i.e. $f(-2)=-45$.

$2(-2)^{3}+h(-2)^{2}+k(-2)+15=-45$

$-16+4h-2k+15=-45$

$4h-2k=-44 \;\Rightarrow\; 2h-k=-22$

Hint: remainder of $f(x)\div(x-a)$ equals $f(a)$.
2Apply the Factor Theorem

$f(x)$ is divisible by $2x-5$, so $f\!\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)=0$.

$2\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^{3}+h\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^{2}+k\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)+15=0$

$\dfrac{125}{4}+\dfrac{25}{4}h+\dfrac{5}{2}k+15=0$

Multiply by $4$: $\;125+25h+10k+60=0$

$5h+2k+37=0$

Hint: $x-a$ is a factor of $f(x)$ iff $f(a)=0$; here $2x-5=2\left(x-\tfrac52\right)$.
3Solve the simultaneous equations

$\begin{cases}2h-k=-22 & \cdots(1)\\ 5h+2k=-37 & \cdots(2)\end{cases}$

$(1)\times 2:\;4h-2k=-44 \quad\cdots(3)$

$(2)+(3):\;9h=-81 \;\Rightarrow\; h=-9$

Substitute into $(1)$: $2(-9)-k=-22 \Rightarrow k=4$

So $f(x)=2x^{3}-9x^{2}+4x+15$.

Hint: eliminate $k$ by doubling equation (1).

Part (b)

4Factorise using the known factor

Since $f(x)$ is divisible by $2x-5$:

$f(x)=(2x-5)(x^{2}-2x-3)$

Check: $(2x-5)(x^{2}-2x-3)=2x^{3}-4x^{2}-6x-5x^{2}+10x+15=2x^{3}-9x^{2}+4x+15$ ✓

Hint: divide $2x^{3}-9x^{2}+4x+15$ by $2x-5$, or compare coefficients of $x^{3}$ and constant term.
5Find all roots and conclude

$f(x)=0:\;(2x-5)(x+1)(x-3)=0$

Hence $\;x=\dfrac{5}{2},\;x=-1,\;x=3$.

All three are rational numbers, so the claim is agreed.

Hint: a rational root must be expressible as a fraction of integers — all three values qualify.
Answer: (a) $h=-9,\;k=4$.   (b) Yes — the roots are $\dfrac{5}{2},\,-1,\,3$, all rational; the claim is agreed.
