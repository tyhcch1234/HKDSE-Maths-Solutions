Visualising the Difference of Squares

Step 0 — Recognise: a square minus a square.

$4$ (big square side) $(2x-3y)$ (small square side) difference region
HKDSE 2016 · Paper 2 · Q3

Factorise: $16-(2x-3y)^2=$

  • A. $(4-2x-3y)(4+2x+3y)$
  • B. $(4-2x-3y)(4+2x-3y)$
  • C. $(4-2x+3y)(4+2x+3y)$
  • D. $(4-2x+3y)(4+2x-3y)$

Step-by-step Solution

STEP 1
Difference of Two Squares \(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a+b)(a-b)\) \\(16-(2x-3y)^{2}\\) is a square minus a square, with \\(a=4\\) and \\(b=2x-3y\\).
Recognise the pattern: it is a difference of two squares $$a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$$ Here $a^2=16$ and $b^2=(2x-3y)^2$,
i.e. $a=4$ and $b=2x-3y$.
Spot the form: (square) − (square).
STEP 2
Write $16$ as $4^2$. Then $$16-(2x-3y)^2=4^2-(2x-3y)^2$$
Make the big square explicit so the identity matches directly.
STEP 3
Apply the identity with $a=4$, $b=2x-3y$: $$=[\,4-(2x-3y)\,][\,4+(2x-3y)\,]$$
First bracket: $a-b$. Second bracket: $a+b$.
STEP 4
Removing a Bracket after a Minus Sign \(-(b-c)=-b+c\) \\(4-(2x-3y)=4-2x+3y\\), so both signs inside the bracket change.
Remove the inner brackets carefully (watch the minus sign): $$4-(2x-3y)=4-2x+3y$$ $$4+(2x-3y)=4+2x-3y$$
A minus before a bracket flips both signs inside.
STEP 5
Combine: $$\boxed{16-(2x-3y)^2=(4-2x+3y)(4+2x-3y)}$$ This matches option D.
Compare with the four options — only D has the $+3y$ with the minus-bracket and $-3y$ with the plus-bracket.
Answer: D  —  $(4-2x+3y)(4+2x-3y)$

Question. Factorise \\(25-(3p+4q)^{2}\\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. Recognise the difference of two squares with \\(a=5\\) and \\(b=3p+4q\\).
Step 2. Write \\(25=5^{2}\\), so \\(25-(3p+4q)^{2}=5^{2}-(3p+4q)^{2}\\).
Step 3. Apply \\(a^{2}-b^{2}=(a+b)(a-b)\\): \\([5+(3p+4q)][5-(3p+4q)]\\) .
Step 4. Remove the inner brackets: \\((5+3p+4q)(5-3p-4q)\\).
Answer: \((5+3p+4q)(5-3p-4q)\)
