HKDSE Math 2016 Paper 1 Q14

4. Polynomials
Remainder theorem Comparing coefficients Quadratic discriminant Real roots

Question:

Let \(p(x) = 6x^4 + 7x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c\), where \(a, b, c\) are constants. When \(p(x)\) is divided by \(x+2\) and when \(p(x)\) is divided by \(x-2\), the two remainders are equal.

It is given that \(p(x) = (lx^2 + 5x + 8)(2x^2 + mx + n)\), where \(l, m, n\) are constants.

(a) Find \(l, m, n\). [5 marks]
(b) How many real roots does the equation \(p(x)=0\) have? Explain your answer. [5 marks]

📘 Step-by-step Guided Solution

1. Use the remainder theorem to find \(b\) 1M ▾

Remainder theorem: When \(p(x)\) is divided by \(x - k\), the remainder is \(p(k)\).

Here we have remainders at \(x = 2\) and \(x = -2\):

\(p(2) = 6(2)^4 + 7(2)^3 + a(2)^2 + b(2) + c = 96 + 56 + 4a + 2b + c = 152 + 4a + 2b + c\)

\(p(-2) = 6(-2)^4 + 7(-2)^3 + a(-2)^2 + b(-2) + c = 96 - 56 + 4a - 2b + c = 40 + 4a - 2b + c\)

Given the remainders are equal: \(p(2) = p(-2)\)

\(152 + 4a + 2b + c = 40 + 4a - 2b + c\)

\(152 + 2b = 40 - 2b\)

\(4b = -112 \quad \Rightarrow \quad b = -28\)

💡 Hint: The remainder theorem states that the remainder of \(p(x)\) divided by \(x - k\) is \(p(k)\). Compare the two remainders directly to eliminate \(a\) and \(c\).

Thus \(b = -28\).

2. Find \(l\) by comparing coefficients of \(x^4\) 1A ▾

Expand the product \((lx^2+5x+8)(2x^2+mx+n)\). The coefficient of \(x^4\) is \(l \cdot 2 = 2l\).

But from \(p(x) = 6x^4 + 7x^3 + ax^2 + bx + c\), the coefficient of \(x^4\) is \(6\).

\(2l = 6 \quad \Rightarrow \quad l = 3\)

💡 Hint: When multiplying two polynomials, the highest degree term comes from the product of the leading terms. Here \(lx^2 \cdot 2x^2 = 2l x^4\).

Thus \(l = 3\).

3. Set up equations using coefficients of \(x^3\) and \(x\) 1M ▾

Now we know \(l=3\), so the factor is \((3x^2+5x+8)(2x^2+mx+n)\).

Compare coefficients of \(x^3\) and \(x\) with \(p(x)=6x^4+7x^3+ax^2+bx+c\).

Coefficient of \(x^3\):

From expansion: \(3x^2 \cdot m x + 5x \cdot 2x^2 = 3m x^3 + 10 x^3 = (3m+10)x^3\)

So \(3m + 10 = 7\)

Coefficient of \(x\):

From expansion: \(5x \cdot n + 8 \cdot m x = (5n + 8m)x\)

But coefficient of \(x\) in \(p(x)\) is \(b = -28\) (from Step 1).

So \(5n + 8m = -28\)

💡 Hint: Expand systematically, or use the fact that coefficient of \(x^3\) comes from \(x^2\cdot x\) and \(x\cdot x^2\) terms. Coefficient of \(x\) comes from \(x\cdot \text{const}\) and \(\text{const}\cdot x\).
4. Solve for \(m\) and \(n\) 1A+1A ▾

From \(3m + 10 = 7\):

\(3m = -3 \quad \Rightarrow \quad m = -1\)

Substitute \(m = -1\) into \(5n + 8m = -28\):

\(5n + 8(-1) = -28\)

\(5n - 8 = -28\)

\(5n = -20 \quad \Rightarrow \quad n = -4\)

Thus \(m = -1\) and \(n = -4\).

5. Factorise \(p(x)\) and set up \(p(x)=0\) 1M ▾

We have \(p(x) = (3x^2+5x+8)(2x^2 - x - 4)\).

\(p(x) = 0 \iff (3x^2+5x+8)(2x^2 - x - 4) = 0\)

\(\iff 3x^2+5x+8 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad 2x^2 - x - 4 = 0\)

💡 Hint: Once the product of two factors is zero, at least one factor must be zero. Solve each quadratic separately.
6. Check discriminant of \(3x^2+5x+8=0\) 1M 1A ▾

For a quadratic \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), discriminant \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\).

\(\Delta_1 = 5^2 - 4(3)(8) = 25 - 96 = -71\)

Since \(\Delta_1 < 0\), the equation \(3x^2+5x+8=0\) has no real roots.

💡 Hint: \(\Delta < 0\) means no real roots; \(\Delta = 0\) means one repeated real root; \(\Delta > 0\) means two distinct real roots.
7. Check discriminant of \(2x^2 - x - 4=0\) 1M+1A ▾

For \(2x^2 - x - 4 = 0\):

\(\Delta_2 = (-1)^2 - 4(2)(-4) = 1 + 32 = 33\)

Since \(\Delta_2 > 0\), the equation \(2x^2 - x - 4=0\) has 2 distinct real roots.

8. Conclusion: number of real roots of \(p(x)=0\) 1A ▾

The equation \(p(x)=0\) is equivalent to:

\((3x^2+5x+8)(2x^2 - x - 4)=0\)

The first factor gives no real roots. The second factor gives 2 distinct real roots.

Therefore, \(p(x)=0\) has 2 real roots.
