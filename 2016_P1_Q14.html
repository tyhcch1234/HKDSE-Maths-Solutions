HKDSE 2016 Paper 1 Q14 — Polynomials: Identities, Remainder Theorem & Real Roots

Step 1 — Compare $p(2)$ and $p(-2)$ to find $b$.

Given data Focused working Conclusion Coefficients of $x^3,x$

Question

Let $p(x)=6x^{4}+7x^{3}+ax^{2}+bx+c$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are constants. When $p(x)$ is divided by $x+2$ and when $p(x)$ is divided by $x-2$, the two remainders are equal.

It is given that $p(x)=(lx^{2}+5x+8)(2x^{2}+mx+n)$, where $l$, $m$ and $n$ are constants.

(a) Find $l$, $m$ and $n$.  [5 marks]

(b) How many real roots does the equation $p(x)=0$ have? Explain your answer.  [5 marks]

1

(a) Use equal remainders: $p(2)=p(-2)$

Remainder Theorem \(p(x)=(x-k)Q(x)+p(k)\) The remainder when \\(p(x)\\) is divided by \\(x-2\\) is \\(p(2)\\) and by \\(x+2\\) is \\(p(-2)\\); equating them kills \\(a\\) and \\(c\\).
$$\begin{aligned} p(2)&=6(16)+7(8)+4a+2b+c=152+4a+2b+c\\ p(-2)&=6(16)+7(-8)+4a-2b+c=40+4a-2b+c \end{aligned}$$ Setting them equal: $152+4a+2b+c=40+4a-2b+c$ $$4b=-112\;\Rightarrow\; b=-28$$
Hint: the "remainder of $p(x)$ divided by $x-k$" is just $p(k)$ (Remainder Theorem). Matching the two remainders kills $a$ and $c$.
2

(a) Match the leading coefficient to get $l$

The $x^{4}$ coefficient of $p(x)$ is $6$, and in the product it is $l\cdot 2$. $$2l=6\;\Rightarrow\; l=3$$
Hint: expand mentally — highest-degree terms only.
3

(a) Compare the $x^{3}$ and $x$ coefficients to get $m,n$

Comparing Coefficients \(a_{k}=b_{k}\ \text{for every power}\ x^{k}\) Expanding the product and matching the \\(x^{3}\\) coefficients gives \\(3m+10=7\\), and the \\(x\\) coefficients give \\(8m+5n=b\\).
Expanding $(3x^{2}+5x+8)(2x^{2}+mx+n)$: $$\text{coefficient of }x^{3}=3m+10,\qquad \text{coefficient of }x=8m+5n$$ Comparing with $p(x)$ (where $b=-28$): $$3m+10=7\;\Rightarrow\; m=-1$$ $$8m+5n=-28\;\Rightarrow\; 8(-1)+5n=-28\;\Rightarrow\; 5n=-20\;\Rightarrow\; n=-4$$
Hint: for the $x^{3}$ term pick $3x^{2}\cdot mx$ and $5x\cdot 2x^{2}$; for the $x$ term pick $5x\cdot n$ and $8\cdot mx$.
4

(a) Answer summary

$$l=3,\qquad m=-1,\qquad n=-4,\qquad b=-28$$
Hint: the two given facts (equal remainders + the factorised form) are independent — each gives part of the answer.
5

(b) Factorise $p(x)=0$ into two quadratic equations

$$p(x)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(3x^{2}+5x+8)(2x^{2}-x-4)=0$$ So the problem splits into two separate quadratic equations.
Hint: a product is zero only if one of the factors is zero.
6

(b) Test the discriminant of $3x^{2}+5x+8=0$

Discriminant \(\Delta=b^{2}-4ac\) For \\(3x^{2}+5x+8=0\\), \\(\Delta=25-96=-71<0\\), so this factor contributes no real roots.
$$\Delta=5^{2}-4(3)(8)=25-96=-71<0$$ No real roots from this factor.
Hint: a negative discriminant means the parabola never meets the $x$-axis.
7

(b) Test the discriminant of $2x^{2}-x-4=0$

$$\Delta=(-1)^{2}-4(2)(-4)=1+32=33>0$$ So this equation has $2$ distinct real roots.
Hint: a positive discriminant gives two distinct real roots of the quadratic.
8

(b) Combine the results

Total number of real roots of $p(x)=0$: $$0+2=2$$
Hint: the discriminant of the whole quartic is not enough — factorise first, then check each quadratic.
Answers (a) $l=3$, $\;m=-1$, $\;n=-4$ (and $b=-28$).
(b) The equation $p(x)=0$ has 2 real roots, because $(3x^{2}+5x+8)$ has $\Delta=-71<0$ (no real roots) while $(2x^{2}-x-4)$ has $\Delta=33>0$ (two real roots).
