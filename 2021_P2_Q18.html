HKDSE 2021 · Paper 2 · Q18

In the figure, $AB = BC$ and $AB \parallel CD$. Let $E$ be the point of intersection of $AD$ and $BC$. If $\angle ADC = 28^\circ$ and $\angle AEB = 94^\circ$, then $\angle CAD =$

A. 30°
B. 33°
C. 36°
D. 39°
Step 1 · Set up the unknown

Let $\angle CAD = x$.

Now look at the two key angle facts we can extract from the given parallel lines and the isosceles triangle.

Target: express every angle of $\triangle ACE$ in terms of $x$, then use the exterior angle $94^\circ$ at $E$.
Step 2 · Use $AB \parallel CD$

With $AB \parallel CD$ and transversal $AD$, the alternate angles are equal:

$$\angle BAD = \angle ADC = 28^\circ \quad (\text{alt. } \angle\text{s, } AB \parallel CD)$$

Therefore the whole angle at $A$ inside $\triangle ABC$ is

$$\angle BAC = \angle BAD + \angle DAC = 28^\circ + x.$$

Watch the figure: $\angle BAD$ and $\angle CAD$ are on opposite sides of $AD$, so they add to form $\angle BAC$.
Step 3 · Isosceles triangle $ABC$

Given $AB = BC$, triangle $ABC$ is isosceles with vertex at $B$. Hence base angles are equal:

$$\angle ACB = \angle BAC = 28^\circ + x \quad (\text{base } \angle\text{s, isos. } \triangle)$$

The base is $AC$; the equal sides are $AB$ and $BC$, so the equal angles sit at $A$ and $C$.
Step 4 · Exterior angle of $\triangle ACE$

In triangle $ACE$, the angle $\angle AEB = 94^\circ$ is an exterior angle, so it equals the sum of the two remote interior angles:

$$\angle CAE + \angle ACE = \angle AEB$$

$$x + (x + 28^\circ) = 94^\circ$$

$\angle CAE = \angle CAD = x$ and $\angle ACE = \angle ACB = x + 28^\circ$.
Step 5 · Solve

$$2x + 28^\circ = 94^\circ$$

$$2x = 66^\circ$$

$$x = 33^\circ$$

$\angle CAD = 33°$  —  Option B
