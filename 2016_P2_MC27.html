Equation of a Circle Centre & Radius Completing the Square Point–Circle Position

Q Question

The equation of the circle \(C\) is

\[ 3x^{2}+3y^{2}-12x+30y+65=0. \]

Which of the following are true?Multiple Choice

I.  The radius of \(C\) is \(14\).
II. The origin lies outside \(C\).
III. The coordinates of the centre of \(C\) are \((2,-5)\).

  • A.  I and II only
  • B.  I and III only
  • C.  II and III only  ✓
  • D.  I, II and III
Part (a) Simplify the equation into standard form

Divide throughout by \(3\) so that the coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) become \(1\).

Centre (2, −5) Radius² 22/3 ≈ 7.333 Radius r √(22/3) ≈ 2.708
1Divide the whole equation by 31M

\[ \frac{3x^{2}+3y^{2}-12x+30y+65}{3}=0 \]

\[ x^{2}+y^{2}-4x+10y+\frac{65}{3}=0 \]

Hint: A circle equation must have equal coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\). Always normalise first — here both are \(3\).
2Identify the coefficients1M

Comparing with \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\):

\[ D=-4,\qquad E=10,\qquad F=\frac{65}{3} \]

Hint: The general form \(x^{2}+y^{2}+Dx+Ey+F=0\) has centre \(\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)\) and radius \(r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F}\).
Normalised form: \(x^{2}+y^{2}-4x+10y+\dfrac{65}{3}=0\)

Part (b) Find the centre and the radius

Use the standard formulas to obtain the centre and radius of \(C\).

Centre G (2, −5) r √(22/3) ≈ 2.708 d(O, G) √29 ≈ 5.385 d − r ≈ 2.677 > 0
1Coordinates of the centre1A

\[ \text{Centre}=\left(-\frac{D}{2},-\frac{E}{2}\right)=\left(-\frac{-4}{2},-\frac{10}{2}\right)=(2,-5) \]

Hint: Note the double negative in the \(x\)-coordinate: \(-\frac{D}{2}\) with \(D=-4\) gives \(+2\).
Centre of \(C\) is \((2,-5)\).  ⇒ Statement III is TRUE.
2Length of the radius1A

\[ r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{E}{2}\right)^{2}-F} =\sqrt{(-2)^{2}+(5)^{2}-\frac{65}{3}} \]

\[ =\sqrt{4+25-\frac{65}{3}}=\sqrt{29-\frac{65}{3}}=\sqrt{\frac{87-65}{3}}=\sqrt{\frac{22}{3}} \]

\[ r=\frac{\sqrt{22}}{\sqrt{3}}\approx 2.708 \]

Hint: \(29=\frac{87}{3}\), so \(29-\frac{65}{3}=\frac{87-65}{3}=\frac{22}{3}\). Since \(\frac{22}{3}\approx 7.33\), \(r\approx 2.7\) — clearly not \(14\).
Radius \(=\sqrt{\dfrac{22}{3}}\approx 2.71\)  ⇒ Statement I (radius \(=14\)) is FALSE.

Part (c) Position of the origin relative to \(C\)

Compare the distance from the centre \(G(2,-5)\) to the origin \(O(0,0)\) with the radius \(r\).

d(O, G) √29 ≈ 5.385 r √(22/3) ≈ 2.708 d − r ≈ 2.677 Conclusion O is OUTSIDE C
1Distance from the centre to the origin1A

\[ d(O,G)=\sqrt{(0-2)^{2}+(0+5)^{2}}=\sqrt{4+25}=\sqrt{29} \]

\[ \sqrt{29}\approx 5.385 \]

Hint: Use the distance formula between the origin \(O(0,0)\) and the centre \(G(2,-5)\). Since \(5^2=25\), \(\sqrt{29}\) is just above \(5\).
2Compare \(d(O,G)\) with \(r\)1M

\[ d(O,G)=\sqrt{29}\approx 5.385, \qquad r=\sqrt{\frac{22}{3}}\approx 2.708 \]

Since \(5.385 > 2.708\), we have

\[ d(O,G) > r \]

3Conclusion and answer choice1A

Since the distance from the centre to the origin is greater than the radius, the origin lies outside \(C\).

Hint: Only II and III are true, so the answer is C.
Answer: C — II and III only.

✓ Summary of Answers

  • Normalised form: \(x^{2}+y^{2}-4x+10y+\dfrac{65}{3}=0\)
  • Centre: \((2,-5)\)
  • Radius: \(r=\sqrt{\dfrac{22}{3}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{22}}{\sqrt{3}}\approx 2.71\)
  • Distance \(d(O,G)\): \(\sqrt{29}\approx 5.39\)
  • Position of origin: \(d>r\Rightarrow\) the origin lies outside \(C\).
  • Statements: I FALSE  |  II TRUE  |  III TRUE
Final Answer: C (II and III only)
