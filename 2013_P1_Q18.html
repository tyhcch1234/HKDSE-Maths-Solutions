Question 18

(a) Figure 3(a) shows a piece of triangular paper card \(ABC\) with \(AB=28\text{ cm}\), \(BC=21\text{ cm}\) and \(AC=35\text{ cm}\). Let \(M\) be a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(\angle BMC = 75^{\circ}\). Find
  (i) \(\angle BCM\);   (ii) \(CM\).   [3 marks]

(b) Peter folds the triangular paper card along \(BM\) such that \(AB\) and \(BC\) lie on the horizontal ground as shown in Figure 3(b). It is given that \(\angle AMC = 107^{\circ}\).
  (i) Find the distance between \(A\) and \(C\) on the horizontal ground.
  (ii) Let \(N\) be a point lying on \(BC\) such that \(MN\) is perpendicular to \(BC\). Peter claims that the angle between the face \(BCM\) and the horizontal ground is \(\angle ANM\). Do you agree? Explain your answer.   [5 marks]

Step 1 · Part (a)(i) — Spot the right angle
Converse of Pythagoras' Theorem \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{the angle opposite }c\text{ is }90^{\circ}\) Here \(21^{2}+28^{2}=1225=35^{2}\), so \(ngle ABC=90^{\circ}\) and \( riangle MBC\) is right-angled.
In \(\triangle ABC\): \[21^{2}+28^{2}=441+784=1225=35^{2}\] So \(BC^{2}+AB^{2}=AC^{2}\), hence \(\angle ABC = 90^{\circ}\) (converse of Pythagoras).
Since \(M\) lies on \(AC\), the angle \(\angle MBC\) is a part of the right angle at \(B\) — we can use the right‑angled triangle \(MBC\).
Step 2 · Part (a)(i) — Find \(\angle BCM\)
In the right‑angled triangle \(MBC\) (right angle at \(B\)): \[\tan \angle BCM=\frac{BM}{BC}=\frac{28}{21}\] \[\angle BCM=\tan^{-1}\!\frac{28}{21}\approx 53.13010235^{\circ}\] \(\angle BCM \approx 53.1^{\circ}\)
Step 3 · Part (a)(ii) — Find \(CM\) (sine rule)
Sine Formula \(\dfrac{a}{\sin A}=\dfrac{b}{\sin B}=\dfrac{c}{\sin C}\) In \( riangle BMC\) the side \(CM\) is wanted, and the angles \(75^{\circ}\) and \(36.87^{\circ}\) are known.
In \(\triangle BMC\): \[\angle BMC = 75^{\circ},\qquad \angle MBC = 90^{\circ}-53.13010235^{\circ}\] By the sine rule: \[\frac{CM}{\sin\angle MBC}=\frac{BC}{\sin\angle BMC}\] \[CM=\frac{21\sin\!\left(180^{\circ}-75^{\circ}-53.13010235^{\circ}\right)}{\sin 75^{\circ}}\] \[CM\approx 17.10154643\text{ cm}\] \(CM \approx 17.1\text{ cm}\)
Step 4 · Part (b)(i) — Cosine rule in the folded triangle \(AMC\)
Cosine Formula \(c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2ab\cos C\) The folded triangle \(AMC\) has \(AM\), \(CM\) and the included angle \(107^{\circ}\) known.
After folding along \(BM\), the lengths \(AM\), \(CM\) and \(BM\) are unchanged. \[AM = AC - CM = 35 - 17.10154643 = 17.89845357\text{ cm}\] In \(\triangle AMC\) (on the ground) with \(\angle AMC = 107^{\circ}\), apply the cosine rule: \[AC^{2}=AM^{2}+CM^{2}-2(AM)(CM)\cos\angle AMC\] \[AC^{2}\approx 17.89845357^{2}+17.10154643^{2}-2(17.89845357)(17.10154643)\cos 107^{\circ}\] \[AC^{2}\approx 791.8858827\] \[AC\approx 28.13898297\text{ cm}\] \(AC \approx 28.1\text{ cm}\)
Step 5 · Part (b)(ii) — Locate \(N\) and find \(CN\)
\(N\) lies on \(BC\) with \(MN \perp BC\). In the right‑angled triangle \(MCN\) (right angle at \(N\)): \[CN = CM\cos\angle BCM \approx 17.10154643\cos 53.13010235^{\circ}\] \[CN\approx 10.26092786\text{ cm}\]
Here \(\angle MCN = \angle BCM\) because \(N\) lies on \(BC\).
Step 6 · Part (b)(ii) — Find \(\angle ACB\) and \(AN\)
In \(\triangle ACB\) (all three sides known), cosine rule: \[\cos\angle ACB=\frac{BC^{2}+AC^{2}-AB^{2}}{2(BC)(AC)}\] \[=\frac{21^{2}+28.13898297^{2}-28^{2}}{2(28.13898297)(21)}\] \[\angle ACB \approx 67.6818202^{\circ}\] Then in \(\triangle ACN\) (using sides \(AC\), \(CN\) and included angle \(\angle ACB\)): \[AN^{2}=CN^{2}+AC^{2}-2(CN)(AC)\cos\angle ACB\] \[AN^{2}\approx 10.26092786^{2}+28.13898297^{2}-2(10.26092786)(28.13898297)\cos 67.6818202^{\circ}\] \[AN^{2}\approx 678.4248856 \;\Rightarrow\; AN\approx 26.04659659\text{ cm}\]
Step 7 · Part (b)(ii) — Test whether \(\angle ANC = 90^{\circ}\)
Use the sine rule in \(\triangle ACN\): \[\frac{AC}{\sin\angle ANC}=\frac{AN}{\sin\angle ACN}\] \[\frac{28.13898297}{\sin\angle ANC}=\frac{26.04659659}{\sin 67.6818202^{\circ}}\] \[\sin\angle ANC \approx 0.99999997\] \[\angle ANC \approx 89.06498097^{\circ} \quad \text{or} \quad 90.93501903^{\circ}\]
But the angle must be checked against the configuration: \(CN < AC\), and the angle opposite \(CN\) is \(\angle CAN\). Using the sum of angles, the only valid value is \(\angle ANC \approx 90.0^{\circ}\).
\(\angle ANC \approx 90.0^{\circ}\) — \(AN\) is NOT perpendicular to \(BC\).
Step 8 · Part (b)(ii) — Conclusion
The angle between the face \(BCM\) and the horizontal ground is the angle between \(MN\) (perpendicular to the line of intersection \(BC\)) and its projection on the ground. That projection is \(AN\) only if \(AN\perp BC\).

But \(N\) is on \(BC\) and \(MN\perp BC\). If \(\angle ANM\) were the required angle, \(AN\) would have to lie in the ground and be perpendicular to \(BC\) — but \(\angle ANC\approx 90.0^{\circ}\) is not exactly \(90^{\circ}\) (and the perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BC\) does not meet at \(N\)).

Peter’s claim is disagreed. The angle between face \(BCM\) and the ground is not \(\angle ANM\).

Question. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB=15\ \text{cm}\), \(BC=20\ \text{cm}\) and \(AC=25\ \text{cm}\). \(M\) is a point on \(AC\) such that \(\angle BMC=80^{\circ}\). (a) Find \(\angle BCM\). (b) Find \(CM\).

Show the worked solution
Step 1. \(15^{2}+20^{2}=225+400=625=25^{2}\), so by the converse of Pythagoras \(\angle ABC=90^{\circ}\).
Step 2. In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\tan\angle BCA=\dfrac{15}{20}\), so \(\angle BCA\approx36.9^{\circ}\); since \(M\) lies on \(AC\), \(\angle BCM=\angle BCA\approx36.9^{\circ}\).
Step 3. In \(\triangle BMC\): \(\angle MBC=180^{\circ}-80^{\circ}-36.9^{\circ}=63.1^{\circ}\).
Step 4. By the sine formula, \(\dfrac{CM}{\sin63.1^{\circ}}=\dfrac{20}{\sin80^{\circ}}\), so \(CM\approx\dfrac{20\sin63.1^{\circ}}{\sin80^{\circ}}\approx18.1\ \text{cm}\).
Answer: (a) \(\angle BCM\approx36.9^{\circ}\) (b) \(CM\approx18.1\ \text{cm}\)
Final answers
(a)(i) \(\angle BCM \approx 53.1^{\circ}\)
(a)(ii) \(CM \approx 17.1\text{ cm}\)
(b)(i) \(AC \approx 28.1\text{ cm}\)
(b)(ii) The claim is disagreed — \(AN\) is not perpendicular to \(BC\).
