Sine & cosine rules, and the angle between a plane and the horizontal ground.

Sine Rule Cosine Rule Angle in 3D Folding a Triangle 8 marks

Q18 Question

(a) Figure 3(a) shows a piece of triangular paper card \(ABC\) with \(AB = 28\text{ cm}\), \(BC = 21\text{ cm}\) and \(AC = 35\text{ cm}\). Let \(M\) be a point lying on \(AC\) such that \(\angle BMC = 75^\circ\). Find

(i) \(\angle BCM\).
(ii) \(CM\).  [3 marks]

(b) Peter folds the triangular paper card described in (a) along \(BM\) such that \(AB\) and \(BC\) lie on the horizontal ground as shown in Figure 3(b). It is given that \(\angle AMC = 107^\circ\).

(i) Find the distance between \(A\) and \(C\) on the horizontal ground.
(ii) Let \(N\) be a point lying on \(BC\) such that \(MN\) is perpendicular to \(BC\). Peter claims that the angle between the face \(BCM\) and the horizontal ground is \(\angle ANM\). Do you agree? Explain your answer.  [5 marks]

(Note: since \(28^2 + 21^2 = 35^2\), triangle \(ABC\) is right-angled at \(B\).)

Original Figure

(a) Finding \(\angle BCM\) and \(CM\)

The triangle is right-angled at \(B\) since \(28^2 + 21^2 = 35^2\). Drag the slider to move the point \(M\) along \(AC\) and watch how \(\angle BMC\) changes — at exactly one position it equals \(75^\circ\).

\(\angle BCM = \) — \(\angle BMC = \) — \(CM = \) — \(AM = 35 - CM\)
Step 11MRecognise the right angle

In \(\triangle ABC\), check the converse of the Pythagoras' theorem:

\[ AB^2 + BC^2 = 28^2 + 21^2 = 784 + 441 = 1225 = 35^2 = AC^2 \] \[\therefore \angle ABC = 90^\circ \]
Hint: A \(3\text{–}4\text{–}5\) style triple multiplied by 7 gives \(21,28,35\). Whenever you see these lengths, suspect a right angle at the vertex between the two shorter sides.
Step 21APart (a)(i) — find \(\angle BCM\)

Apply the tangent ratio in the right-angled triangle \(ABC\) at vertex \(C\):

\[ \tan \angle BCM = \tan\angle BCA = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{28}{21} \] \[ \angle BCM = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{28}{21}\right) \approx 53.13010235^\circ \]
\(\angle BCM \approx 53.1^\circ\)   (to 3 s.f.)
Hint: \(M\) lies on \(AC\), so the line \(CM\) is the same as the line \(CA\); hence \(\angle BCM = \angle BCA\). Use the calculator answer (not the rounded value) in later steps to avoid rounding errors.
Step 31MPart (a)(ii) — set up the sine rule

In \(\triangle BMC\) the three angles are \(\angle BMC = 75^\circ\), \(\angle BCM \approx 53.1301^\circ\) and

\[ \angle MBC = 180^\circ - 75^\circ - 53.1301^\circ \approx 51.8699^\circ \]

By the sine rule (one pair of opposite side and angle is \((BC,\ \angle BMC)\)):

\[ \frac{CM}{\sin \angle MBC} = \frac{BC}{\sin \angle BMC} \]
Hint: Always write the rule in the form \(\dfrac{\text{side}}{\sin(\text{opposite angle})}\). The pair we know completely is \(BC = 21\) opposite \(\angle BMC = 75^\circ\).
Step 41ASolve for \(CM\)
\[ CM = \frac{21 \sin(180^\circ - 75^\circ - 53.13010235^\circ)}{\sin 75^\circ} \] \[ CM \approx \frac{21 \sin 51.86989765^\circ}{\sin 75^\circ} \approx 17.10154643 \]
\(CM \approx 17.1\text{ cm}\)   (to 3 s.f.)
Hint: Keep full calculator precision through the subtraction of angles; rounding \(\angle BCM\) to \(53.1^\circ\) first would shift the answer in the second decimal place.

(b)(i) Distance \(AC\) on the horizontal ground

After folding along \(BM\), the two triangles \(ABM\) and \(CBM\) lie flat on the ground and share the crease \(BM\). The angle \(\angle AMC\) becomes the angle between the two ground segments \(MA\) and \(MC\). Use the slider to change \(\angle AMC\) and see how the ground distance \(AC\) changes.

\(AM = \) — \(CM = \) — \(AC = \) —
Step 11MIdentify the two sides and the included angle

On the folded paper lay on the ground, \(M\) is common to both parts. From part (a):

\[ CM \approx 17.10154643\text{ cm}, \qquad AM = AC - CM \approx 35 - 17.10154643 = 17.89845357\text{ cm} \]

The included angle \(\angle AMC = 107^\circ\) (given). So \(\triangle AMC\) is determined by two sides and the included angle.

Hint: With side–angle–side given, the cosine rule is the direct tool; the sine rule would suffer from the ambiguous case.
Step 21MApply the cosine rule
\[ AC^2 = AM^2 + CM^2 - 2(AM)(CM)\cos\angle AMC \] \[ AC^2 = (17.89845357)^2 + (17.10154643)^2 - 2(17.89845357)(17.10154643)\cos 107^\circ \] \[ AC^2 \approx 791.9935562 \;(\text{cm}^2) \]
Hint: \(\cos 107^\circ\) is negative, so the last term is added (the minus sign cancels). This is why \(AC\) is longer than it would be if \(\angle AMC\) were \(90^\circ\) — a useful sanity check on the drawing.
Step 31ATake the square root
\[ AC = \sqrt{791.9935562} \approx 28.14238389 \]
\(AC \approx 28.1\text{ cm}\)   (to 3 s.f.)
Hint: Note \(AC \approx 28.14\text{ cm} > 28\text{ cm} = AB\): the diagonal across the folded card is longer than the original side \(AB\).

(b)(ii) Is \(\angle ANM\) the angle between face \(BCM\) and the ground?

The key test for “angle between a plane and the ground” is: the line where the two planes meet must be perpendicular to both the perpendicular foot-line in the plane and the vertical projection. Here the line of intersection is \(BC\). Use the sliders / buttons to tilt face \(BCM\) up and watch whether \(\angle ANM\) remains a right angle at \(N\).

\(MN = \) — \(CN = \) — \(\cos\angle ACB = \) — \(\angle ACB = \) — \(AN = \) — \(\angle ANC = \) —
Step 11MFind \(CN\) and \(MN\)

\(MN \perp BC\) at \(N\), so \(\triangle CMN\) is right-angled at \(N\). Using \(\angle MCN = \angle BCM \approx 53.13010235^\circ\):

\[ CN = CM\cos\angle BCM \approx 17.10154643 \times \cos 53.13010235^\circ \approx 10.26092786\text{ cm}\] \[ MN = CM\sin\angle BCM \approx 17.10154643 \times \sin 53.13010235^\circ \approx 13.68518207\text{ cm}\]
Hint: Alternatively \(CN\) can be obtained from \(\triangle ABC\): since \(\cos\angle ACB = \frac{21}{35} = 0.6\), the projection of \(CM\) on \(CB\) is \(0.6 \times 17.1015 \approx 10.2609\text{ cm}\).
Step 21MFind \(\angle ACB\) with the cosine rule

In \(\triangle ABC\) (with \(AB=28,\ BC=21,\ AC=28.13898297\)):

\[ \cos\angle ACB = \frac{BC^2 + AC^2 - AB^2}{2(BC)(AC)} = \frac{21^2 + (28.13898297)^2 - 28^2}{2(21)(28.13898297)} \] \[ \cos\angle ACB \approx 0.3797252 \;\Rightarrow\; \angle ACB \approx 67.6818202^\circ \]
Hint: Here \(AC\) is the ground distance from (b)(i), not the original \(35\text{ cm}\) edge — after folding the two edges \(AB\) and \(CB\) stay on the paper but \(A\) and \(C\) are now further apart in space.
Step 31MFind \(AN\) from \(\triangle ACN\)

In \(\triangle ACN\): \(CN \approx 10.26092786\), \(AC \approx 28.13898297\), included angle \(\angle ACB \approx 67.6818202^\circ\):

\[ AN^2 = CN^2 + AC^2 - 2(CN)(AC)\cos\angle ACB \] \[ AN^2 \approx (10.26092786)^2 + (28.13898297)^2 - 2(10.26092786)(28.13898297)(0.3797252) \] \[ AN \approx 26.03453787\text{ cm} \]
Hint: Keep every value to full calculator precision; rounding \(AC\) to \(28.1\) first can change \(AN\) by more than \(0.05\).
Step 41MTest whether \(\angle ANC = 90^\circ\)

Apply the sine rule in \(\triangle ACN\) to find \(\angle ANC\):

\[ \frac{AC}{\sin\angle ANC} = \frac{AN}{\sin\angle ACN} \] \[ \frac{28.13898297}{\sin\angle ANC} = \frac{26.03453787}{\sin 67.6818202^\circ} \] \[ \angle ANC \approx 89.06498097^\circ \quad\text{or}\quad 90.93501903^\circ \]

Because \(\angle ANC\) is not \(90^\circ\), the segment \(AN\) is not perpendicular to the intersection line \(BC\).

Hint: The “angle between a plane and a horizontal plane” must be measured along the line of greatest slope, i.e. \(\angle ANM\) is only valid if \(AN \perp BC\) and \(MN \perp BC\). Here only \(MN \perp BC\) is guaranteed; \(AN\) is not.
Step 51AConclusion

Since \(\angle ANC \ne 90^\circ\), the line \(AN\) is not perpendicular to the line of intersection \(BC\). Therefore \(\angle ANM\) is not the inclination of the face \(BCM\) to the horizontal ground.

Disagree. The angle between the face \(BCM\) and the horizontal ground must be measured between the line of greatest slope of \(BCM\) (perpendicular to \(BC\)) and its projection on the ground. Since \(AN \not\perp BC\), \(\angle ANM\) does not give that angle.
Hint: The true inclination angle would be \(\angle M N H\) where \(H\) is the foot of the perpendicular from \(M\) to the ground, and \(MN \perp BC\) is already satisfied — but \(AN\) is the wrong line to use.

Summary of Answers

  • (a)(i) \(\angle BCM \approx 53.1^\circ\)  (since \(\tan\angle BCM = \frac{28}{21}\) and \(\angle ABC = 90^\circ\))
  • (a)(ii) \(CM \approx 17.1\text{ cm}\)  (sine rule in \(\triangle BMC\) with \(\angle BMC = 75^\circ\))
  • (b)(i) \(AC \approx 28.1\text{ cm}\) on the ground  (cosine rule in \(\triangle AMC\), \(\angle AMC = 107^\circ\))
  • (b)(ii) Disagree. \(\angle ANC \approx 89.06^\circ \ne 90^\circ\), so \(AN \not\perp BC\); hence \(\angle ANM\) is not the angle between face \(BCM\) and the ground.
