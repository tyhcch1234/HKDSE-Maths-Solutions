QQuestion

In the figure, \(UVTSR\) is a cyclic quadrilateral (all vertices lie on a circle). It is given that \(RT /\!/ VU\) and \(TR\) is the angle bisector of \(\angle VRS\). If \(\angle VRT = 33^\circ\), then \(\angle RUT =\)

A. \(33^\circ\)    B. \(38^\circ\)    C. \(57^\circ\)    D. \(71^\circ\)

(1 mark — multiple choice)

AStep 1 — Set Up the Angles & Diagram

1M

Let \(\angle RUT = x\) and \(\angle URT = \angle SRT = y\). In the figure below, drag the slider to change the position of \(R\) on the circle and watch how the angle markings stay consistent with \(RT /\!/ VU\).

x = – y = – ∠VRT = 33° x + 2y = –
Step 11M

Let \(\angle RUT = x\) and \(\angle URT = \angle SRT = y\).

Since \(TR\) is the angle bisector of \(\angle VRS\), we have \(\angle VRT = \angle SRT = y\).

Given \(\angle VRT = 33^\circ\), it follows that \(y = 33^\circ\) only if the bisector condition is applied to the given angle. However, we keep \(y\) general and use the condition \(RT /\!/ VU\).

Hint: The key is to recognise that \(RT /\!/ VU\) gives alternate angles: \(\angle VUR = \angle URT = y\).

BStep 2 — Use Parallel Lines & Cyclic Quadrilateral

1M

Toggle the checkboxes to highlight the parallel lines and the cyclic quadrilateral property.

∠VUR = y ∠VUT + ∠VRT = 180°
Step 11M

Since \(RT /\!/ VU\), alternate angles give

\[ \angle VUR = \angle URT = y \]
Hint: \(VU\) and \(RT\) are parallel; \(UR\) is the transversal.
Step 21M

In the cyclic quadrilateral \(VUTS\) (or \(UVTS\)), opposite angles sum to \(180^\circ\):

\[ \angle VUT + \angle VRT = 180^\circ \]

But \(\angle VUT = \angle VUR + \angle RUT = y + x\), and \(\angle VRT = y + 33^\circ\).

Hint: Because \(TR\) bisects \(\angle VRS\), \(\angle VRT = \angle SRT = y\), and \(\angle VRS = 2y\). The given \(33^\circ\) is \(\angle VRT\) in the original figure — wait, the problem states \(\angle VRT = 33^\circ\), so \(y = 33^\circ\) is not forced; rather \(y\) is the bisected half and the given \(33^\circ\) is one of the angles at \(R\). Re-read: "If \(\angle VRT = 33^\circ\)" means the bisected angle is \(33^\circ\), so \(y = 33^\circ\). Then the cyclic condition gives a relation for \(x\).
RELATION \(x + y + y + 33^\circ = 180^\circ\) → \(x + 2y = 147^\circ\) … (1)

CStep 3 — Angle Sum of Triangle RST

1M

Use the cyclic quadrilateral again to find \(\angle RST\), then apply the angle sum of triangle \(RST\).

∠RST = 180° − x x − y = 33°
Step 11M

In cyclic quadrilateral \(RUTS\), opposite angles:

\[ \angle RST + \angle RUT = 180^\circ \] \[ \angle RST = 180^\circ - x \]
Hint: \(RUTS\) is cyclic, so the angle at \(S\) and the angle at \(U\) are supplementary.
Step 21A

In \(\triangle RST\): \(\angle RST + \angle SRT + \angle RTS = 180^\circ\).

Here \(\angle SRT = y\) and \(\angle RTS = 33^\circ\) (alternate angles with \(RT /\!/ VU\), or given). Substituting:

\[ (180^\circ - x) + y + 33^\circ = 180^\circ \] \[ x - y = 33^\circ \quad \text{… (2)} \]
EQUATION 2 \(x - y = 33^\circ\)

DStep 4 — Solve the Simultaneous Equations

1A

Solve (1) and (2) to find \(x\), and hence the answer.

x = – y = –
Step 11M

From (1): \(\;x + 2y = 147^\circ\)    (1)

From (2): \(\;x - y = 33^\circ\)    (2)

(1) − (2): \(\;3y = 114^\circ \Rightarrow y = 38^\circ\).

Step 21A

Substitute \(y = 38^\circ\) into (2):

\[ x = 33^\circ + 38^\circ = 71^\circ \]
ANSWER \(\angle RUT = x = 71^\circ\) → Option D
Hint: Always check: \(x + 2y = 71 + 76 = 147\) ✓ and \(x - y = 71 - 38 = 33\) ✓.

✓Summary of Answers

  • Let \(\angle RUT = x\) and \(\angle URT = \angle SRT = y\).
  • By alternate angles (\(RT /\!/ VU\)): \(\angle VUR = y\).
  • Cyclic quadrilateral \(VUTS\): \(\angle VUT + \angle VRT = 180^\circ\) gives \[ x + 2y + 33^\circ = 180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; x + 2y = 147^\circ \]
  • Cyclic quadrilateral \(RUTS\): \(\angle RST = 180^\circ - x\).
  • Triangle \(RST\): \((180^\circ - x) + y + 33^\circ = 180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; x - y = 33^\circ\).
  • Solving: \(y = 38^\circ\), \(x = 71^\circ\).
  • Final answer: \(\angle RUT = 71^\circ\) (Option D).
