HKDSE Math 2012 Paper 2 Q37

Topic 5: Formulas and Sequences

Arithmetic Sequence General Term Sum of Sequence Inequalities

Q Question

Let $a_n$ be the $n$th term of an arithmetic sequence. If $$a_{18} = 26 \quad \text{and} \quad a_{23} = 61,$$ which of the following are true?

I.   $a_{14} < 0$

II.   $a_1 - a_2 < 0$

III.   $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + \ldots + a_{27} > 0$

A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

📝 Step-by-Step Solution

💡 Hint: General term formula

The general term of an arithmetic sequence is:

$$a_n = a + (n-1)d$$

where $a$ is the first term and $d$ is the common difference.

Given:

$$a_{18} = a + 17d = 26 \quad \text{...(1)}$$

$$a_{23} = a + 22d = 61 \quad \text{...(2)}$$

Subtract (1) from (2):

$$5d = 35 \implies d = 7$$

Substitute into (1):

$$a + 17(7) = 26 \implies a + 119 = 26 \implies a = -93$$

✓ So, $a = -93$ and $d = 7$.

💡 Hint: Use $a_n = a + (n-1)d$

Substitute $n = 14$, $a = -93$, $d = 7$:

$$a_{14} = -93 + (14-1)(7)$$

$$= -93 + 13 \times 7 = -93 + 91 = -2$$

Since $-2 < 0$, statement I is true.

✓ Statement I is TRUE.

💡 Hint: Notice $a_1 - a_2 = -d$

First, find $a_1$ and $a_2$:

$$a_1 = -93$$

$$a_2 = -93 + 7 = -86$$

Then compute the difference:

$$a_1 - a_2 = -93 - (-86) = -93 + 86 = -7$$

Since $-7 < 0$, statement II is true.

✓ Statement II is TRUE.

💡 Hint: Sum formula $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$

The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence is:

$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$$

Substitute $n = 27$, $a = -93$, $d = 7$:

$$S_{27} = \frac{27}{2}[2(-93) + (27-1)(7)]$$

$$= \frac{27}{2}[-186 + 26 \times 7]$$

$$= \frac{27}{2}[-186 + 182]$$

$$= \frac{27}{2} \times (-4) = 27 \times (-2) = -54$$

Since $-54 < 0$ (not greater than $0$), statement III is false.

✗ Statement III is FALSE.

Final Answer: A. I and II only

Only statements I and II are true.
