Question
The figure shows the square \(ABCD\), the regular pentagon \(ADEFG\) and the regular hexagon \(AGHIJK\). Find \(\angle ABK\).
A. 69°
B. 72°
C. 74°
D. 75°
Step 1 · Angles at A of each polygon
Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon \(\dfrac{(n-2)\times180^{\circ}}{n}\) Square \(90^{\circ}\), pentagon \(108^{\circ}\) and hexagon \(120^{\circ}\) meet at \(A\).
All three shapes share vertex \(A\). Their interior angles at \(A\) are: $$\text{Square: }90^\circ,\qquad \text{Pentagon: }\frac{(5-2)180^\circ}{5}=108^\circ,\qquad \text{Hexagon: }\frac{(6-2)180^\circ}{6}=120^\circ.$$ These three angles completely surround point \(A\), so they must sum to \(360^\circ\): $$90^\circ+108^\circ+120^\circ=318^\circ.$$
Check: \(318^\circ\) leaves a gap of \(42^\circ\) around \(A\) — the shapes do not perfectly tile \(A\), the figure is drawn with them meeting at the single point \(A\).
Step 2 · The gap angle between AB and AK
Angles at a Point \(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{k}=360^{\circ}\) The three interior angles together leave a gap of \(360^{\circ}-318^{\circ}\) between \(AB\) and \(AK\).
Going around point \(A\), the square occupies \(90^\circ\) from \(AB\) to \(AD\), the pentagon \(108^\circ\) from \(AD\) to \(AG\), and the hexagon \(120^\circ\) from \(AG\) to \(AK\). $$\angle BAK = 360^\circ-\left(90^\circ+108^\circ+120^\circ\right)=360^\circ-318^\circ=42^\circ.$$
Step 3 · Lengths AB and AK In the square, \(AB=AD\) (side of the square).
In the regular pentagon, \(AD=AG\) (side of the pentagon).
In the regular hexagon, \(AG=AK\) (side of the hexagon).
All these side lengths are equal because the pentagon and hexagon share side \(AD\) and \(AG\) respectively, and the square shares its side with \(AD\). Hence $$AB=AD=AG=AK \;\Rightarrow\; \triangle ABK \text{ is isosceles with } AB=AK.$$
Step 4 · Base angles of triangle ABK
Base Angles of an Isosceles Triangle \(\angle ABK=\angle AKB=\dfrac{180^{\circ}-\angle BAK}{2}\) The shared side length makes \(AB=AK\), so \(\triangle ABK\) is isosceles.
In isosceles \(\triangle ABK\) with \(AB=AK\), the base angles at \(B\) and \(K\) are equal: $$\angle ABK=\angle AKB=\frac{180^\circ-\angle BAK}{2} =\frac{180^\circ-42^\circ}{2}=69^\circ.$$
The figure even shows the given \(90^\circ\) square corner at \(B\) — the marked arc in the figure at \(B\) is the right angle of the square, confirming the orientation.
Answer: A  ·  \(\angle ABK=\;\) 69°

Question. The figure shows the square \(ABCD\), the regular octagon \(ADEFGHIJ\) and the regular pentagon \(AJKLM\), all sharing the vertex \(A\). Find \(\angle ABM\).

Step 1. Interior angles at \(A\): square \(90^{\circ}\), octagon \(\dfrac{(8-2)180^{\circ}}{8}=135^{\circ}\), pentagon \(\dfrac{(5-2)180^{\circ}}{5}=108^{\circ}\).
Step 2. Their sum is \(90^{\circ}+135^{\circ}+108^{\circ}=333^{\circ}\), so the gap between \(AB\) and \(AM\) is \(360^{\circ}-333^{\circ}=27^{\circ}\).
Step 3. \(AB=AD=AH=AM\) (all sides of the three regular figures),
so \(\triangle ABM\) is isosceles with \(AB=AM\).
Step 4. \(\angle ABM=\dfrac{180^{\circ}-27^{\circ}}{2}=76.5^{\circ}\).
Answer: \(\angle ABM=76.5^{\circ}\)
Key facts used: interior angles of regular polygons; angles at a point sum to \(360^\circ\); isosceles triangle base angles.
