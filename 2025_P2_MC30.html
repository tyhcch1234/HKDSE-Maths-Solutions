Statistics — Mode, Median, Mean & Range of a Data Set

Mean Median Mode Range Ordered Data

Question Multiple Choice

Consider the following data:

\[ \alpha \quad \beta \quad -4 \quad -3 \quad 1 \quad 1 \quad 1 \quad 4 \]

Denote the mode and the median of the above data by \(s\) and \(t\) respectively. If the mean and the range of the above data are \(0\) and \(10\) respectively, which of the following are true?

  1. \(s = 1\)
  2. \(t = -1\)
  3. \(\alpha + \beta = 0\)
A. I and II only
B. I and III only ✓
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III

Part (a) — Find the mean condition & prove \(\alpha+\beta=0\)

-5
Sum of the 6 fixed numbers: -4-3+1+1+1+4 = 0
\(\alpha = -\beta\) → 5
Total sum: 0
Mean = 0
Step 11M
The data set has \(8\) values. Write the mean condition: \[ \frac{\alpha+\beta+(-4)+(-3)+1+1+1+4}{8} = 0 \]
Hint: The "mean is \(0\)" means the sum equals \(0\times 8 = 0\).
Step 21A
Compute the known part: \(-4-3+1+1+1+4 = 0\). So \[ \frac{\alpha+\beta}{8} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha+\beta = 0 \]
\(\alpha + \beta = 0\) — statement III is true.
Hint: The six fixed values already sum to zero, so all the "mean = 0" burden falls on \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).
0 / 2

Part (b) — Use the range to find \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)

10
Smallest fixed value: -4
Largest fixed value: 4
Range so far: 4 - (-4) = 8
Need range 10 → extend by 2 → \(\{5,\,-5\}\)
Step 11M
The fixed values span from \(-4\) to \(4\): a range of \(4-(-4)=8\). Since the given range is \(10 > 8\), the pair \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\) must supply the extreme values.
Hint: Range \(=\) largest \(-\) smallest. With \(\alpha=-\beta\), one of them is positive and one is negative.
Step 21A
Let the positive one be \(x>0\), so the other is \(-x\). Range: \[ x - (-x) = 2x = 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 5 \] Hence \(\{\alpha,\beta\} = \{5,\,-5\}\).
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) must be \(5\) and \(-5\) (in some order).
Hint: Watch out — this is a "must be" conclusion, so we cannot yet decide which is \(\alpha\) and which is \(\beta\).
0 / 2

Part (c) — Test statements I and II (mode & median)

ordered
Mode (most frequent): 1 appears 3 times → \(s=1\)
Median (4th & 5th values): 1 and 1
Median \(t = \frac{1+1}{2} = \) 1
Step 11A
With \(\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{5,-5\}\), the data are \[ 5,\; -5,\; -4,\; -3,\; 1,\; 1,\; 1,\; 4. \] The value \(1\) occurs three times; every other value occurs once. Hence the mode is \[ s = 1 \]
Statement I is true.
Hint: The mode is simply the most frequent value — here the repeated \(1\)'s dominate.
Step 21M
Rearrange in ascending order: \[ -5,\; -4,\; -3,\; \underbrace{1,\; 1}_{\text{4th, 5th}},\; 1,\; 4,\; 5 \] With \(8\) values (even), the median is the average of the 4th and 5th values.
Hint: For \(n=8\), the median sits between positions \(n/2=4\) and \(n/2+1=5\).
Step 31A
\[ t = \frac{1+1}{2} = 1 \neq -1 \]
Statement II is NOT true.
Hint: A common trap is assuming the median equals the mean (\(0\)) or thinking it must be negative. Always order and average the middle pair carefully.
0 / 3

Summary of Answers

  • Mean condition: \(\alpha+\beta = 0\) → III is true.
  • Range condition: \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) must be \(5\) and \(-5\).
  • Mode: \(s = 1\) (since \(1\) appears 3 times) → I is true.
  • Median: \(t = \dfrac{1+1}{2} = 1 \neq -1\) → II is false.
  • Conclusion: Only I and III are true → Answer: B.
